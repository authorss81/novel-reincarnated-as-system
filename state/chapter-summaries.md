## Batch 0004 (ch 37–46)

| Ch | Title | Summary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 37 | Stage Four | Laundry room and crew quarters, three days. She says nothing to him for three days to test whether the channel needs her: it does not. Fear comes off him as heat for the first time. In an unattempted argument about coal she keeps him in four lines — he is the only person who has lied to her in a way she could check. Account open, not closed: employed, not forgiven. She asks what he took, item by item, and he can only answer in weather. |
| 38 | The Scar | Furnace floor, night shift, week two. She asks what is under the scar. He describes the withdrawal as a thickness, a stack, a shelf. She finishes it: you did not take a thing, you took the order the thing was in. The tax takes order, not substance — practice rebuilds stages and cannot rebuild order. He does not understand the reading of himself. She tells him to stop describing things in images, because she is going to start reading what he reads. Intimacy in its worst form. |
| 39 | Eleven Days | Ash-flats, eleven days, stage 4 to 7. The yard refuses her the inner ring; Mei Jiu warns her once from the rail and goes inside. The leak through the crack costs her a fifth of everything she moves until she watches it go sideways into the Nine-Step pattern instead of out, and the pattern does work. Damage as technique, hers and unshared, and she tells nobody. Body-Forging 7 announced bound-wide to a terrace that has stopped being surprised. Four days left and the last technique is the one he cannot describe. |
| 40 | Feng Lushi Writes | Lowwater, the tower, a day. The Bureau has ordered Feng to omit her from the upward copy; he has disobeyed for eight days and has written her story upward four times in an unindexed column nobody reads, because he has never had a story where somebody was believed. She refuses to thank him and he says that is how it is done. She asks for the order of the sentences and he gives her eleven volumes without knowing it is a method. Stage 8 on the Lowwater road under forty-one volumes. The Watch is waiting at the foot of the terrace. |
| 41 | Lowwater | Held in the roundhouse on a Bureau note charging her with removal of kiln material, which is the wrong clause. Old Gao's hand on a young Watchman's arm stops a search that would have found the tablet. Captain Lu Tao walks her out in daylight. In the market square she reads a log page aloud and the crowd turns on the paper, not on her; part of it wants to know what she took out of the kiln and she refuses to read the name in public. She has leverage for the first time and gives the captain up instead of finishing him. It tastes like Yuan. |
| 42 | Three Counties | The copying floor at the fourth watch. Four sheets, one hour, four genuinely different sentences: Lowwater's plain report, Hanhui's *for discipline* with a county stamp, Shouyi's with her name removed, Wanzhou's *a servant of Qinghe*. The copyists reproduce faithfully; they were not in the room. The layer is written by somebody. She takes the three foreign sheets and puts them in the log. Her brother's sentence is now a piece of furniture in an office. She asks the voice who writes them and he says he has never known. |
| 43 | What the Voice Knows | Grain cart up the Lowwater road. He can see the sentences and cannot see where any of them is decided. She discovers he is counted once a day by something walking a row in the layer; he has assumed for six years he is a fault in the sky and now there are people in the room. He is more frightened than she is. She refuses to comfort him and tells him she needs him sharp, which is the first time she has needed him. She reaches stage 9 on a terrace step at dusk by opening herself on purpose, unaided. |
| 44 | The Terms of the Hearing | Corridor outside the Assembly Hall, day before the Hall closes the season. The Hall tries not to rule; clerk Xie Shuo forces it, because a non-ruling travels upward. He rules there is no precedent and then rules anyway, and rules that the Hall's own objection is the writ for her seal. Qiu offers his sponsor's writ and she refuses it in front of eleven witnesses, because a favour is revocable. She petitions for a public advancement hearing and for the Assembly floor and for nine hundred witnesses. Nine days to object. |
| 45 | Nine Days | The nine days on the flats, on her back. A list of four things going wrong and a stage nine that is a ceiling with a hole in it. Qiu's faction sends the second offer by servant-rank clerk in a corridor: inner rank, bed, stipend, account struck, Foundation, the tablet — for a signature that she has never entered sealed ground and never carried kiln clay. She does not burn it. She logs it word for word with the date, the clerk and the pin hole. Sealed for Condensing on the eighth day with three witnesses: the first servant-born in three hundred years. |
| 46 | The Petition | Assembly Hall, nine hundred and eleven people, the winter accounts take four hours and put her father's account and her name on the floor again. The Hall tries to defer her petition to the close of the session; she forces the reading on a rule she learned from the worst clerk she has ever met. An objection from Qiu's faction is read and disposed of on its own last line. Her name is read aloud for the second time in the volume. She petitions that the layer speak and wins it on the four county sentences. Trial set for the Assembly floor in four days, announced to the bound. |

## Batch 0005 (ch 47–58) — closes Volume 1

| Ch | Title | Summary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 47 | Waiting | Crew quarters, day one of the eleven-day ash fall. Ash in the lye trough, on the sill, on the washing crew's backs, in her lungs; her stair climb now costs her. Yuan tells her the real clock: a sealed member who does not attempt on the floor is recorded as a *refusal*, and a refusal is an intake, not a defeat. Mei Jiu comes to her door in the dark and hands her an unwritten inner compression for a narrow gate, for nothing, with no explanation, because she would rather the Hall be frightened of what happens if Ning Yan is refused than compete with her. Ning Yan refuses to write it in the log, so that a compression nobody has written down cannot be found in a Bureau hand. Xie Shuo asks to be one of the nine floor clerks. A folded sheet arrives from the Hall: the sponsorship terms, before the trial. |
| 48 | Terms | West corridor, day two. Lu Kang, Deng Chao and Pei An offer inner rank at first sitting, Foundation outside the furnace schedule, the account of Ning Bo struck, the Clay Tablet, and a clause barring her from ever mentioning the voice — with a penalty that reinstates everything rather than a fine. Third sheet reveals nine households struck in one brush stroke over eleven years: the faction has been running its own furnace out of Qiu's rooms and has never had to produce a word. She asks for one clause and they agree in four seconds, which is the horror. She nearly signs, stops on the thought that a bought truth is not a truth, and leaves the sheet on the table with the hour blank. Three days to decide. |
| 49 | Withheld | Anteroom, day three. She asks to sign after the trial; Lu Kang explains, without cruelty, that an advancement with no paper in front of it belongs to the sect, and one with paper belongs to the men who put her on the floor — the fourth hour and the ninth hour are different in law. She sits eight hours in a room where the waiting is the point, manages a cough in public, and says no. The offer dies at the last hour of the day, schedule of nine struck out with it; the minute-book entry about her brother survives because a minute book is not a schedule. At the second hour of the night the same servant-rank clerk walks six miles in the rain to bring the sheet back with no hour, no term and no deadline: they worked out in six minutes that a dated offer is an offer she is holding. She notices she has not said *please* once in eight hours. |
| 50 | Qiu's Fall | Assembly Hall, dawn of the trial, heaviest day of the ash fall, doors propped because the hinges have packed. Qiu tells her about a lamp shop in Lowwater and *the shape of a happy life*, and says he has been at the furnace forty-one years trying to buy it out of other people's children. The Hall vacates the entry of the eleventh day and eleven minutes as retrospective — and Qiu refuses to plead it, because pleading would void her rank with his. He is stripped in four minutes, pensioned, silent for the rest of the morning. On his way out he slows half a step beside her and says the sentence nobody understands, including the reader. She lets him go past and says nothing, and understands for the first time what it is to be inside a thing happening to a person with no way to touch it. Her name is called. |
| 51 | The Floor | Assembly Hall floor, ninth hour. She counts the room through Yuan: four people have decided something and one of them is the clerk. She starts with the Nine-Step and her body is four steps ahead of her head; she refuses, aloud, on a floor, to explain where it came from, and does not tell Yuan either. Mei Jiu's compression narrows the crack to a rim and works, and she states plainly that it is not hers and that she will look at Mei Jiu while she uses it so the room knows where to send the bill. Her right hand fails at the sixth form and she does not stop. Terror becomes the enormous specific relief of doing the one thing she is good at. Then the air falls out of the room and the ash goes straight down. |
| 52 | The Tribulation | The same room, immediately. The weather has a number in it — five — because a trial is a document and the bound executes it rather than reading it. The Speaker orders the floor cleared and cannot move her off it. She is struck five times: left forearm, right arm, a beam and the roof taken out, and the fourth out of a sky the building has not seen in three hundred years. She does not get up for two minutes and forty seconds. On the fifth she refuses to take a shape and opens the crack outward instead — Condensing stage one, the aperture stabilised at the crack as a mouth that will open and shut for the rest of her life. The Hall has no roof and never gets one again. |
| 53 | The Record of the Trial | Immediately after. Du Hong lifts her onto the Speaker's bench without asking and she lets him, knowing the report order he was sent with and that he does not know she knows. Xie Shuo writes for a minute and forty seconds and then stops, and four seconds pass in which the entire hall waits for a sound that does not come — the visible edge of a decision, and she looks for the cause in every corner of the room and finds nothing in it at all, then goes looking inside and finds him not saying anything. The announcement goes out and carries **servant-born**, which in eleven hundred years it has never once done; the word goes round the hall in about nine hundred mouths. Announcement wording fixed here: *Assembly Hall of Qinghe. Ning Yan of the Qinghe furnace crew, servant-born. What was held is let go. What was taken is held.* |
| 54 | What It Cost Him | Sixth step of the terrace, hours later, in the debris. He has been silent for nineteen minutes and she asks and he explains: a report is made and then released, and the release is the act of whoever is holding it, which is why no advancement has ever waited. If he had held it, the register says a sealed member did not attempt, and a furnace looks for exactly that. He chose the announcement over her safety because a servant-born who announces is too public to harvest quietly. He does not say how long he held it; she works out from the rule alone that it was the four seconds and neither of them says the number out loud. The layer has logged him and has a name for him: **Yuan Sheng**. Settlement passes two-thirds; Ledger is offered for ninety seconds and refused, and he loses the ability to read past her skin permanently. She does not forgive him, sets the rule that he decides nothing about her again without telling her, and stays. |
| 55 | Three Days Visible | Three days in the crew quarters, which she refuses to leave for a better room. Fever gone; right arm gone. Three factions inside a day want something from her. Her father's account is opened again in **four** county books, each of which adds *Credit. Condensing, stage one. Held* and bills the household twice more — being famous has cost her father eight more years. The layer enters **the first claim in the history of the bound against the Chosen One**, eight hundred jin, proof defined as a body, a place and a name; she cannot refuse it and he did not do it and he made her one. Three people come believing her and every one wants something; the laundress teaches her that being believed is the only one of the three resources that can be spent, and she spends it — eight words, her own hand, sent to the Lantern Register tower with nobody's name on it. Rage arrives over a Bureau copyist who cannot get *servant-born* right — the first straight thing she has felt in four months. **Then she spends it.** Du Hong finds her on the sixth step and she tells him the thing she has held since before the trial: he was sent to report her, he filed nothing, and she has never once mentioned it in any room, which is the only reason nobody has asked him why. He tells her it cannot be held open — with a price on her head he is the cheapest door in the bound. She says she will put the whole of it on the record in her own hand, and he tells her that will put her name in his file, and she says yes, because that is how she makes herself expensive. "Mostly at you." The protection and the danger are the same line in the same four books. |
| 56 | The Signature | Roofless Assembly Hall, fifth day after the trial, forty-one witnesses, the crew standing in the weather. She enters a schedule of the route to the Broken Kiln in her own readable hand and then states, under oath, that the article has not been in her possession since the hour she left sealed ground — a positive falsehood, said to their faces, witnessed, and the first thing she has ever done that is not strictly true. She reads her brother's record aloud in a room with no roof, twice, and the clerk enters one word. She signs the clause barring her from ever mentioning the voice, knowing it cannot bind a Chosen One and that it binds her by hand. Account of Ning Bo struck whole; the maintaining members' iron token given. **Then she uses the standing she has just bought.** She goes back in and has a second entry taken on the same sheet in her own hand: Du Hong advanced to inner rank at the winter session on her recommendation, directed by the Hall to report her, filed nothing, and the fact that she has held that since before the trial and entered nothing until this hour. Lu Kang lets it stand because a maintaining member may dictate from the hour she signs. The cost is immediate and she does it anyway — two files with her hand in them, so any man cashing eight hundred jin opens him first. She cannot afterwards tell whether she was angry or shopping. Relief enormous, and not the same as knowing. The Chosen One bounty and the word *reconciled* are now on the same line of the same four books. |
| 57 | The Shape of the Silence | Ninth Steps Terrace, ninth day of the ash, first grey snow. She goes up all nine steps for the first time since the winter. Yuan gives her what he has: the designation is a writ and not a reward; there have been **eleven** before her across six hundred years and he cannot see past any of the seams; and a designation can be *set aside* by a request from outside the bound, by somebody he cannot name. She stops him mid-sequence — *not tonight, you have spent enough of me tonight* — and for the first time in four months it works. She keeps him: employed, not forgiven, three rules, the work is her brother. She uses his name once, wrongly, and says she will be wrong again. He goes quiet for four hours without warning. |
| 58 | The Count | Same day, the terrace in the first snow. Du Hong brings her two lamp-menders' versions of her own announcement, both wrong in different ways, and she understands it is not carelessness but ears; she sends the four county sheets down to Lowwater in copies in her own hand. Two men are on the gate who were not on it when she came up, and one of them asked Du Hong what his file said before he had got to the fourth step: the entry she made in ch 56 has already made him somebody to stop. The pass closes and she never gets off the mountain. He comes back after three hours and gives her a number: **fifty-eight**, one a day since the first night — which means the row that walks past him has been coming down since before she ever existed and has been checking an empty slot, or something older than her. There is a place where the sentences are read after they go out, and it has a name he has never once been able to read, and it is the only thing in the layer he cannot see. She stands on the sixth step with the token she does not want and asks what it is counting. He does not answer. He is counting. |

## Volume 2 — The Chosen One (ch 59–118)

### Batch 0006 (ch 59–72) — opens Volume 2

| Ch | Title | Summary |
| --- | --- | --- |
| 59 | The Closed Pass | Tenth day of the fall, second hour. The pass at the fourth bridge iced over between the second and the fourth hour and Captain Lu Tao has the road barred; in his own hand, with no order on it, his barrier board reads *Ning Yan. Held.* He tells her why: a claim of eight hundred jin stands in the layer against the Chosen One and there is one person inside this wall who is that name. She counts to forty, finds nothing on the list the ice can fix, and turns round and walks back up all nine steps and takes the second bunk **on purpose, in front of the crew**, converting Volume 1's visibility into confinement. The barrier is a schedule, not a punishment. Power: none. |
| 60 | The Second Bunk | Eleventh and last day of the ash. She tells eleven men the true part — the dead arm, the crack as a door that does not shut properly, the eight hundred jin — and keeps the plate and the log to herself, because eleven men is not a lock. Cao Lei (back stair, autumn) and Xu An (oil ration, autumn) find the shape of it: **the roster takes a signature, any man of the crew can sign for a shift, and a signed shift is a legal fact regardless of who works it.** Stated once, here, by Cao Lei in the quarters. She lets them, after telling them aloud what a roster is, so the cover carries a price. Last day of the fall; it stops by evening. At the second night hour a clerk's folded sheet waits on the Hall table with her name on it in the wrong order. **Nothing on this page foretells what it costs.** |
| 61 | The Licence Clerk | Road opens for market. **She puts ch 60's folded sheet on the clerk's table first.** It came off the Hall table at the west end with the queue papers; his is a copyist's hand — *not a hand that writes names, a hand that writes down what it has been given.* She tells him to write her name and **he writes it in the wrong order**, because he has no other version. An office has her name down wrong and there is now a copy of the error in a building with no roof. Then the Hall's new clerk, eleven days at the table and more afraid of the three sponsors than of her, reads out **Clause Four of the Servant Advancement Settlement** and names the document: the **Licence to Practise Past Registration**, issued at the **Bureau of Records' county office at Lowwater**, registrar **Sun He** named once. Three parts and no part is the licence: **sponsors' seal, quota clearance, county countersignature.** She owns one and does not even have that yet. Quota clearance is counted in **hours**, not weeks, and the hours go into Shen Xiu's winter book. She copies the clause into the log in wash code and resolves to file. |
| 62 | The Price of Paper | The sponsors' corridor, sixth hour of the evening. Lu Kang, Deng Chao and Pei An seal the application — and the clause they drafted themselves in the second week of the autumn falls due at noon: **a fired plate the size of a hand returned to the maintaining members within the week of signature.** It cannot be late, because she swore in her own hand at the second hour and thirty-one minutes of the fifth day that she cannot produce it and does not undertake to. The paper demands an article the only person who could produce it has sworn she has not got. What they want is the schedule worked and her mouth shut, in that order, and she notices herself bargaining while it happens. Sealing enters her name in the county queue: **a public fact.** |
| 63 | The Lowwater Market | Old Gao holds her at the trestle by name, politely, *for her own safety* — the bounty made courteous — and will carry a letter up. It comes the next morning with the carts: **Du Hong delivered the four county copies to the tower, Feng read all four, walled them behind the fifth item, and entered none of them upward.** Feng's unindexed column stays at five and he has said *do not thank me for it.* The rest of the letter is the new fact: a man has been to **three county desks on the Lowwater square, not the tower**, asking which clerk keeps the copies of the Qinghe advancement, and has been paying for the answer. **The bounty has moved from wordings to records.** She writes back one line in her own unreadable hand — *copy nothing for him* — and works out that the third proof is a body, and therefore a room. **Pressed on the claim, Yuan fails to give her the arithmetic and says why: it does not come out of a ledger or a list, it comes out of a man asked in daylight who has had to answer in front of people who will remember it, and he will not say it from above a crowd.** *Ask me when there is one.* That is ch 68. |
| 64 | The Furnace Tithe | Night shift, one-handed. The floor does not know her after eleven days. The shift leader pities her onto the bench, which is worse than contempt, and she refuses and holds the channel instead. **The crack-as-channel is used, not explained** (spent at ch 39) and nobody on the floor sees a technique. The tithe table is four feet from the furnace mouth and the clerk counts slowly **on purpose**, because a fast number makes the book unbalanced at month's end. She signs the tithe book with her left hand for **four and a half hours**, entering herself as labour toward her own clearance. **New:** the sheet above the columns carries six unsigned lines saying the hours a furnace hand works are hours the county counts at the countersignature, delivered on the fourth day past, with eleven other unsigned sheets in a frame at the quota office. Held for Volume 7; not used again this volume. |
| 65 | Bunk by Bunk | Eighth hour of the morning. Two officers of the maintaining members come up the quarters stair with a genuine instrument for an examination of crew effects after the trial debris inventory. **The maintaining member's consent regulation, stated once and never explained again:** her effects may not be examined without her consent, and consent may be withheld without reason and cannot be challenged. She withholds it at the eighth hour. At the same hour Cao Lei and Xu An **stand up in front of eleven witnesses and claim her shift aloud**, which gives her a legal hour off the floor — and she spends it at the Hall getting the furnace schedule certified, unattached, filing nothing. **The clerk certifies her hours for the eleventh to the seventeenth as four and one-half**, the rest being the two men who sign for her. Gratitude with teeth: they have given the inspectors a line to write down, and she tells them so. **The roster rule is not restated here — it was stated once in the quarters at ch 60, and she remembers that room, not a book.** |
| 66 | The Inspectors' Paper | Tenth hour through midday; the doorway refusal is entered at **ten minutes past the tenth hour**. **The batch's irreversible decision.** She refuses the examination again, aloud, in the doorway, before eleven witnesses, and enters the hour twice. The seal holds. **The inspectors take the crew roster instead** — a board is sect property, an instrument silent on a board takes it, and they read it aloud against the certified schedule in front of the foreman, and in eleven seconds a short-handed man who says he is **short of two, and the two of them are standing on his stair** puts two names on the settling floor doubled from tomorrow to the close of the thaw. She has four moves available and uses none: she could not say *take me instead*, because of the plate. **She chose two lives that were not hers over one life that was.** A younger inspector, packing up, tells her kindly that they did not come for anything she is wearing, and that the maintaining members already hold the plate described in their own hand — **which tells her the clause at ch 62 was written for this, and by whom.** The option of surrendering the plate quietly closes. The refusal is in the minute book and retrievable in Volume 4. |
| 67 | Ash and Hours | Tithe table, seventh hour of the evening. The clerk is beginning to enjoy the counting and she notices and does not like it. Above her head Cao Lei and Xu An are on the settling floor, cheerful, arguing the second verse of the weighing song so the floor can hear. - **Ch 310 — *Half A Reach* (day 638, the twenty-fifth) — GOLDEN CORE 4, the batch's only bracket.** Taken at her own order at the fourth hour in the middle of a reach on Pei's field, bar in her left hand, nobody within two hundred yards, **where nothing is announced** — no one inside to be told, no county book to take it; the sound goes into the ground and nothing comes back. *[Ning Yan, past the line, servant-born, under no ceiling any county holds. Golden Core, stage four, is held.]* Then the hand tells her: **the flat of a second finger from longer to longest, permanently**, so every line she writes anywhere now costs its hour twice over, and **the yard work she can still price one-handed narrows to half a reach.** **There was no part of her that was slower, and that is the part that is frightening.** Half a reach, **two and a half coppers**, taken. *Emotion: not the fear she had at the third — the flat recognition that the stage cost exactly what she had already spent, and one plain sentence: she went into that reach able to price a day's work with one hand and came out able to price half of one, and there was nobody in the world she could tell.*

- **Ch 311 — *The Brick Money* (days 639–641, the twenty-sixth to the twenty-eighth).** The material shed past the mill is a lean-to with about nine things standing on it, no counter, no prices, and a man who does not come out from behind the stack. **Nine coppers for a full charge — said once.** And the second fact that is the whole chapter: **a full charge does not come in a size one arm can carry**, and a bar does not cut brick, and the flat of a second finger is now the longest it has ever been. The arithmetic in four lines: a reach and a half a day at five is two and a half a day, three days is seven and a half, a charge is nine — **so four days of clearing for one charge, and then three trips for the same one.** There are three things on that ground that could move a sack half a mile — a clearing paid in something other than money, a cart round, a shed that owed her — and **she names all three out loud in the yard and takes none of them**, because the one thing on that ground that can pay for brick in a size one arm can carry is not coppers, and that is Batch 0002's to take. A third of a sack, carried back in two loads, and the ring lit low on the night of the twenty-eighth. *Emotion, the new thing: a want with no shape in it — she wanted a thing she could carry in one trip that would make the ring hold.*

- **Ch 312 — *The Bundle Going Up* (days 642–643, the twenty-ninth and the thirtieth) — Feng, by report, once.** A loaded cart going west stops at Pei's gate, and a man with a loaded cart stops for a question and not for nothing else. **One question, one answer, and she never asks a second:** *is there anything that comes back down that road* — *no. There is a stair about nine days west of here. Men go up it carrying bundles and they come down with nothing in their hands, and they have been doing that since before I was driving.* He gives her the rest for nothing, as a fact about a road: two days east past the flat ground and then more carts going up than down, and a loaded cart out of that yard every month whatever is in it and a driver who will take a person going that way. **She sits down on a board under a rope with a bar across her knees — the first time she has sat down in daylight since the seventh — and says nothing for half an hour.** In the mill yard she sees the stack for the first time: trays under sacking, on the ground, in the open air, roped twice. She does not go near it and does not count it and does not ask. On the thirtieth there is nothing on that ground that falls on a date, and she catches herself doing the old pay-day arithmetic and laughs once, out loud, alone. *Emotion: she wanted to tell somebody; she told a carter nothing, and he goes on driving and never thinks about that stair again, which is the correct ending for him and the worst one there is for her.*

- **Ch 313 — *The Ground Past The Mill* (days 644–645, the first and the second) — the last chapter of the batch.** She walks past the mill in daylight at the fourth hour on the first of a month with the bar over her shoulder, because there is no book to shut, no plank, no season and nothing that will close on anything. **Going past the wheel is not a thing.** Past the mill the ground goes flat, farmed and hedged, about four houses in four miles, a track that goes on east and does not come back. From two miles west a stack of trays is under boards and sacking in a yard and somewhere in it is her column with **fifteen gaps in it now instead of twelve**, a name at the top, and *good* above the hours; **the twenty-second is not in it**, because it is under two feet of clay on the low side of a field and she is the only living thing that knows. She cannot get to any of it. The three boards and the roof she built are at the yard and she is not going back. On the second morning she finds she is not afraid, and that noticing it is what takes her nine steps. *His last line of the batch, on the ground on the first night: it has been twenty-three days since you walked out of that yard, and that is the longest you have gone in one direction without stopping. The human sentence, plain, about one person: she was eighteen years old and standing in a country where an office could read her and a wage could be set for her and nobody within nine days of road knew her name, and she had been afraid of all three for five months and had gone on walking.*
