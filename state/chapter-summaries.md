- **ch 803, day 2,654, the first of the month beginning 2,654, the second hour and the ninth. She names what she could not see: **not one board, not one stroke, not one corner.** The man did not look at the lamp — she heard his last four hundred and forty in a room with a flame in it and he did not look at it — **and that is worth more to her than the rest of the morning.** And then she says it, standing, in the ordinary voice: **a room that does not account for the light in it is a room working to a set of figures it was never given** — and puts the plain one in after it: **she has been standing four feet off that table for three hundred and fifty-three days saying every figure she has into twenty-two paces of dark, and she did not know that was what it was. She has been speaking into a room.** **Ending: she said it where the person at the far end of that table can hear it, and she did not mean to.**
- **ch 804, day 2,660, the seventh, *Nothing Answers*.** She waits four counts for an answer and there is none and she had known there would be none, and **the rule is that nothing in that room is required to answer her**, which she has never had said to her in nineteen years. Nothing at the far end has changed: shelf, cage, cut in the stone, a person on the far side of the shelf, **the cage out a hundred and twenty-six days.** She accounts for the six days out loud, including that **she did not go near the table on the fourth and fifth and that not doing that is not something she has done in three hundred and fifty-nine days.** She decides to go on saying things out loud, for two reasons, and the second one is that **she likes it** — six days ago a sentence went twenty-two paces and came back as silence and she has slept, and she did not know she had not been sleeping. Then she names the cost. **First: she cannot say *I do not know what that is for* about the place in the far end of that table any more**, because a thing that has been told what a room is working to is not a thing she can stand in front of and say she has stopped not knowing — **and it was not a big price for six words and she is angry about it.** Second: **a room working to a set of figures it was never given is working without one of the figures in the room, and there is no figure anywhere for what that does to a sentence.** She will keep saying them anyway, because that is the only way she has ever found out that a thing was wrong.
- **ch 805, day 2,662, the ninth, *Twenty-Two Paces*.** To get light onto the worn place she has to carry the lamp twenty-two paces and lose the near end, **and there is a strip of pale wood with her record on it lying at the near end, and she is not leaving a figure of hers in the dark for a count of a hundred and forty** — so she takes it, under her left arm against her fourth rib, where a book of eight leaves has been for nineteen years. Two things in two hands for the first time in that room. She goes out and back on her feet, because her knees have been wrong since the nineteenth of the month beginning 2,534, **and her knee goes on the loose flag she found at ch 796.** At the far end she puts the lamp four feet short of the end and looks down from four feet off, and the reveal is one word: **that is not the shape of a person sitting in it.** She stops at *what it is not* and refuses to guess at what it is. She corrects a thing she said out loud to him on the seventh of the month beginning 2,534 — **that it had been worn by somebody sitting in it — and says she said it without checking and has not been able to put it back.** She describes only what she can stand behind: **deeper at the front than the back, not deeper in the middle than at the edges, and a person puts the weight in the middle.** She looks a second time and says the second time is worse, because the first time she was looking at what it was and the second time she was looking at whether it was the shape of him, **and it is not.** She does not sit in it, does not put her weight on it, does not put her hand in the shape, and will not have anybody put in it. **Ending: she has seen something about it and cannot say what, and she is not going to make a pair of cuts for it.**
- **ch 806, day 2,663, the tenth, *Two Turns And Not A Knot*.** She decides about the bundle under the far end in about a count of ninety and gets nothing else out of the night. She states the fact once, flat — **two turns and not a knot, the cord has not moved, the dust is on top of it in the shape of the cord, five times in nineteen years, never pulled** — and then says nothing else about it at all. The reason is not fear of what is in it: **if she pulls it then this bundle is a thing she has opened, and it is the only thing in that building she has ever been able to open, and once that is true it is true for the rest of her life.** She goes over all five times out loud, one after another, and gives the last line: **five in nineteen years and never once on the second of a month when there was a light in this room**, and the last two are the only two where she could have been asked to stop. Then the real fear, which took her nineteen years to find: **not what is in it, but what she will be afterwards — whether the difference between a person who opened a thing and a hand that goes along a column four to a minute and does not stop is a difference with a figure behind it.** She says the word **tonight** out loud on purpose, the second time in her life, because tonight is the first night she has had light to look at it by, and then spends a count of a hundred and forty doing nothing at all **in front of a person**, which is the discipline and the cost. And she says the last of it: **a bundle under a table that a person of sixteen can reach is the first thing in that building that would put a name of his on something, and there is not one instrument in this county that could tell her who had opened it first.** **Ending: something else is in this room.**
- **ch 807, day 2,664, the eleventh, *It Is Not A Job*.** She says **you are not going to open it** before he has asked it, and gives the reason the whole night stood at: **a person who can be given a thing can be given a job**, and then a second one — nobody above the line knows either of them is up here, so a second job would follow on the strength of the first and not one thing could stop it. And then the part that was not in her plan and cost her nine days: **he never asked. He asked her for two nights in advance and made her say the whole arithmetic to get either of them, and he has not asked her once for this, and she worked out on the third day that he had not asked on purpose** — because she said on the seventh of the month beginning 2,534 that a boy of sixteen is not being sent, not being entered, and has no name of his on anything in that room, **and he took the third of those and built a wall out of it and stood behind it and let her stand in front of it thinking she was the one being careful.** She calls that the most frightening thing anybody has done to her in that room. He goes under the far end of the table on his own account, **with nothing in it that she has sent him**, and takes about a count of a hundred and forty, and comes out and says *it isn't tied* — and then, unprompted, **that it is two turns and comes apart in about two pulls and he did not pull**, and that he wants that said by him and not by her. **She asks him for the date and he gives it**, because in nineteen years there has not been one line in that building that got into a record that she had not said out loud there first, and this one is his. **Ending: the first line in that record that is not hers, not on a board and not on the wood and not going up any stair, and there is no place in that building for it and she is not going to make one.**
- **ch 808, day 2,665, the twelfth, the night before, *Sixty-Eight*.** She sets the taking of the eighth stage on the twelfth at the seventh hour and does it alone, and the part of it that was not alone is Yuan, out loud, in her own mouth, in the ordinary voice. **His objection is the right one and she knows it and there is no answer to it:** she has one figure in that row that she wrote herself, it is a count, counts date, it is nine days old and will be ten tomorrow — and on the fourth of that month she worked out that a column with a price at the head is a column of amounts, **so the column is reading a count she made against a price she cannot read, and the only way of finding out what the column is doing is to use it.** She says the interval out loud by subtraction, **sixty-eight days**, and gives the four intervals she has: fifty-eight, one figure, standing still · sixty-three, one figure and a look · seventy-four, nothing to take it against but a lamp · **sixty-eight, two figures and one of them moving.** **A stage taken against a row with two figures in it has to be taken against both of them, and a count already written never comes right again, so it is not fifty-eight.** She says it as arithmetic and not as a lesson, and says she has no rule for any of it. And before she decides she does the thing she has done before a stage four times: **she says a wrong figure out loud first — that she is taking it at the eighth hour — then corrects it to the ninth, because the wrong one went out first and that is what the fourth of the six is actually for.** She decides: the eighth stage tomorrow at the ninth hour on the thirteenth of the month beginning 2,654. The lamp will be there or it will not and she will not make a boy of sixteen promise her at the top of eight flights, **because a promise made at the seventh hour on the night before has nothing to do with the ninth hour on the day.** And she admits the want underneath: **if she puts a figure in that wood then there is one more figure in it, and she has had nineteen years of empty and the empty is a thing she has got.**
- **ch 809, day 2,666, the thirteenth of the month beginning 2,654. THE CLIMAX. IMMORTAL VESSEL 8.** Taken at the ninth hour, at the near end of that table, in oil light, on her own strip of pale wood, in her own hand, with the nib against her fourth rib, read out loud before it is a line anywhere. **She gets the month wrong on the first telling — two thousand five hundred and fifty-four — and stops, and says that being wrong in public first is the fourth of the six: it is not so that a room hears it, it is so that the wrong one does not stand in the room instead of the right one.** Then the right one, and the interval again, short, because this is the tenth time in ten days. She puts **eight** in the fourth pair of cuts, the first pair below the seven, empty since the wood was cut, and her right forearm goes down by the width of the little finger on her left hand while the nib is still on the wood — the fourth time a figure has come off that hand. She says it out loud again. **It felt like being let in**, the second time she has answered that question and the first time it has been the same answer twice, and she will not look at what that means. And she records one thing she has not recorded before, because it happened: **three things came into that room nineteen years ago, one of them a person and one of them a room, and for a count of nine with the nib on the wood the third one came as close to that table as it has ever come — and she is not going to say what it is.** **The cost, named once: she cannot be unread where she stands.** Plain version: **there is a room with a column in it that reads, it has been copying for four hundred years whatever goes on that table in a stroke that starts in the same place on every board, and from tonight there is not one place in that room where she can stand that nothing in it can make out what she is** — she said eight out loud in oil light in a room where something was taking it, she put eight in a column with a price at the head of it, and she said a name in that room on the fifth of that month in front of a boy of sixteen, **and none of the three can be taken back and any one of them could be the one that gets written on a board.**
- **ch 810, day 2,684, the first of the month beginning 2,684, eighteen days after ch 809. THE IRREVERSIBLE STEP.** She accounts for the eighteen days first: **she said the cost on the fourteenth and then stopped saying anything about it, because a cost that gets said again is a cost being paid in instalments and this one was paid whole**; she said eight out loud about two hundred counts on each of nine mornings and one hundred and forty times on the eighteenth day; and she did not once go to the far end of that table. Then the fourteenth bundle at the second hour: nine hundred and four steps, forty boards in ten minutes, thirty for the cloth, forty strokes — **and then, after the man is off the stair and the room has been quiet for a hundred and forty counts, a forty-first stroke**, one, on one board, not four to a minute, lasting about a count of nine. At the ninth hour she carries the lamp twenty-two paces and goes under the far end of the table edge on so she is not looking across twenty feet of it, and reads it. **It is a name. It is not her hand — three fingers and a thumb would show and they do not — and it is not the hand at the far end of that table, which has written four hundred years of strokes that all start in the same place. It is a third hand.** Under the name there is a pair of cuts with nothing in them, worn round at the corners, the same size as the ones on her own wood. And in the column at the far end, in the room's own hand, stroke for stroke: **it copied it exactly and it did not stop on it, and it does not know whose words it is writing.** She does not tear it up, and says out loud that she is not going to, and why — **there is no fire in that room, it has already been copied, and a board that comes up that stair does not go back down it.** She reads the whole of it out loud in the ordinary voice. **The finding is paid and not explained: he is readable now, by the thing that made him, and that cannot be put back.** She says the name was said out loud in that room three times and once of them was in front of a boy of sixteen, and she says out loud that this is not fear — **fear is about the next one, and this is about one that is already here and already copied and cannot be unread** — and ends on the strip of pale wood: one of her figures went into that room eighteen days ago, she does not know what any of them is worth, **and there is a name at the far end of that table now that somebody else has paid for.**
