# Volume 5 — Mortal Ash (chapters 239–298)

## Goal

> In this volume, Ning Yan must finish the world they came from, or leaving it costs everyone she left standing in it.

Fixed by `outline/series.md` §9. Not re-planned here.

## Central pressure

**Obligation at a distance.** Volume 4 was geography and distance — nine days of road to a line of work, and whose book an hour goes into. **Volume 5 is the first volume in the book where the distance cannot be walked back in time**, and it is the first one in which the books on both sides of it are kept by people who are right.

The opening pressure is not a line on the ground. It is a **figure on a board the far side keeps**:

> **She stood in the stretch nobody is paid to tend, and an announcement came out of her and went past the line, and the people on the far side wrote it in their own board because that is what the board is for. The bound did not notice, and cannot: a county book has no column for a thing heard past its own boundary, and nine counties that heard nine stages in order have heard nothing else. But the far side keeps a wage-board for a line it maintains by hand, and it now holds a figure in it that no county in nine counties can answer for — and a woman standing on their side of it whose licence carries a claim their side can act on.**

Write it as a fact on the page and not as a plan. **The question is not whether she can get back in. It is that everything she left standing inside the bound is now on a bill somebody else is keeping, and the mortar in it is loose.**

Both halves of that sentence are traps and both halves are true. Stay on the far side and work, and every hour she works proves the bound's books cannot close without her — and the people she left standing carry it. Go back inside to settle it in person, and she walks back into the execution range of a dismantling order that is now executable past the line as well, carrying a claim the far side has already read aloud. She cannot finish by staying and she cannot finish by returning. The whole volume is that scissor, and it closes from both sides at once.

**The antagonist is a standing obligation to people who cannot be reached** (`outline/series.md` §6 item 4, and not Volume 4 again): nine days of road, a bound she has left, a crew, a keeper of sixty-two, a carrier's round with no date on it, a girl of twenty-four at a wash house, a counting room with a date in it. **Every one of those is an institution or a person doing the only thing they are built to do, and that is worse.** Volume 4's antagonist was the Wall and the sects that live on it — a line of work maintained by hand by people with families. **Volume 5 must not re-run that and must not invent a named villain to replace it.** No new named villain. No new final enemy. The Scribe is fixed in `outline/ending.md` and belongs to Volume 15.

## The thing Volume 5 must not be

Volume 2 was confinement and paperwork. Volume 3 was lawful hunting by an officer who never lies — examination and exposure, audit and mercy, hunting with a calendar, cost and signature — and its paper was paper already stamped twice and now being read back. Volume 4 was ration and silence, hiring and the road and whose book an hour goes into, the line walked and the people who live on it, crossing and what it costs — and its paper was a wage-book kept by people who are not a county. **Volume 5's pressure is keeping and being kept, debt coming due, family paper, and ash and leaving**, and its paper is **a far-side wage-board that already holds her, a draft that goes west, and a family account that has been open since before she was born.** Nothing in this volume is a document being read back at her. Nothing in it is an officer with a calendar. Nothing in it is a road walked to find a book. Nothing in it is a crossing.

## The surface answer — owed, and paid here, and only this much

`outline/series.md` §3 is owed and is not yet paid: *the layer is the world's own bookkeeping… and the mortal bound simply never noticed the difference between being told and being charged.* `outline/volume-04.md` wrote it onto the desk explicitly: **when an announcement is heard past the line, the rule is not being broken — the drift is carrying it — and that is the surface answer the series owes the reader by Volume 5.**

**Volume 5 pays it.** Once, in a worker's mouth, on the page, and in short sentences: a stage announced inside the bound nine days west arrives on the far side as drift-carried sound and goes onto the far-side board as a reading, and a stage she takes on the far side goes into the ground and never reaches any county book at all — because being told is what happens inside, and being charged is what the ground carries, and the bound never noticed the difference. That is the whole of it.

**Do not pay the structural answer; that is Volume 6, and it is *the Wall is real and it is maintained*.** The ninety-parts account stands. The tenth part is not given. Nothing in this volume proves the crews wrong.

## Geography — the far side, and the road that does not come back

## The midpoint (ch 268)

**She goes back inside the bound once, on purpose, to settle what can only be settled where she can be heard — and finds out that being heard there now costs her the one safety the crossing bought her.**

Nine days west, a floor, nine men, a wash house, a tower with a light in it, a counting room with a date still in it. She stands one attendance she has already lost, in person, in her own hand, to put a paper for her brother's name where somebody has to look at it — knowing the referral is still pending, the dismantling executable past the line as well as inside it, and the far-side board already holding a figure no county can answer for. She does it in daylight, says her name out loud the way she has said it at every trestle, asks nothing, and is entered — and then she walks back out, because there is nothing left in there that is hers to carry.

**The irreversible decision of the volume is hers and it happens in this batch:** she authorises the far-side office to take the eight-hundred-jin claim onto its own book against hours worked past the line — closing the bound's bill by opening one the bound cannot read. It cannot be undone. From that hour leaving costs the far side instead of the bound, and that is what finishing means.

## Final chapter image (ch 298)

**A courtyard of ash on the far side, and the first thing she has to do in it is nothing.**

She stands past the last working point with a furnace of her own under her and a wage-book closed behind her, and the column on the near side of it is finished, and it is hers, and it stays. It took her five months. It took her three stages nobody heard. It took her a finger she lost two seasons back and a second finger that writes slower every month and will not get faster.

Nobody on the far side asks her a question. Nobody inside the bound asks her anything either, because no county in nine counties has a column for a woman past its own boundary, and nine counties that heard nine stages in order have heard nothing else since.

So she does the last mortal thing. She shakes the ash off her coat — the ash of a bound that burned its books to keep its ceiling — and it falls the way ash falls, and does not settle, and she leaves it where it falls.

Nine days' road behind her, and a floor, and nine men, and a tower with a light in it. **She will not walk it again in this book.**

**Everything that ever happened to her inside that boundary was announced. She is standing in a place where nothing is said when it happens, with a furnace of her own that nobody gave her, and it is the first silence she has ever stood in that is hers.**
