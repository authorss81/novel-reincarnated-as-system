## The Warden, and what he is and is not

`bible/characters.md` fixes him in two lines: *nine hundred years, keeps a list, some of the names on it are his
friends.* `outline/series.md` §6 and §10 fix the rest: *Warden Shuyin (Vol 9)*, and *nobody in the mortal world is
secretly good or secretly evil. Shuyin is honest.* `outline/ending.md` §1 fixes what this volume may not spend:
**he released the Anchor list in Volume 10 and is alive, old, and not thanked at the end of this one.**

**This outline therefore builds Shuyin out of one man and one kept thing, and builds nothing else.**

**The Warden is a man with a list and a rule and a body nine hundred years old.** He has a room he can be found in,
a way of standing, a thing he is careful about, and people he has outlived. He does not have a hall, a court, a rank
list, a wing, a committee, a uniform, or a hierarchy of subordinates with titles. **Volume 8 already found the shape
of this book's institutions — one building, one book, one rate, one rule, one lamp on every table — and Volume 9 must
not build a bigger version of it.** One man, one book, one rule, one room with a door that shuts.

- **What the list is:** a book, in his hand, of every ascension the bound has swallowed. A name goes in when a person
  reaches the top of a realm with no document to take them past it. A name stays. **What is on any given page of it is
  Volume 10's, and no card in Volume 9 reads a page of it out loud.**
- **What the rule is:** a person at the ceiling who opens the door from the inside must be written down, once, as kept
  or as lost. The rule is older than she is. It has cost him people he liked. He keeps going because he has decided
  what the rule is for and he does not think the decision is interesting.
- **What is NOT on the page in Volume 9: the Anchor.** The list is names. Where the names go, what holds them, and what
  releasing them costs are not stated once. **The Warden never explains why a bounded world needs readers.**
- **What may not be named:** no department, no floor, no rank list, no committee, no house of any office, no title held
  by anybody beyond the single fact that he is the Warden. **A drafter who wants to give him staff has begun inventing
  furniture, and the fiction is one room and one book.**

### Shuyin

**Nine hundred years old. Walks slowly and stands with his weight on one leg. Careful with doors — he shuts every one
behind him and waits for the latch before he speaks.** He has outlived the people who taught him the rule and two
people he put on the list himself. He buys nothing, threatens nothing, lies about nothing that matters, and he is not
a liar.

**His argument, in his own mouth, once, and it is not wrong:** *Somebody has to write down the ones the bound takes,
or there is no record that the bound takes any. I write them. I have written them for nine hundred years. The writing
does not save one of them and I have never said it does.* **What it actually is, said once, later, by Ning Yan and not
by him:** keeping is writing a name where somebody has to look at it, and the price of being written is that the real
realm can find you by it.

**He wins something in Volume 9 and it is on the page.** See §*What surviving him costs*.

**He is not a villain and no chapter makes him wrong.** He is right that the bound takes people, right that a ceiling
with no document is a real ceiling, right that an unrecorded taking is worse than a recorded one, and right that she is
at the ceiling. **He is wrong about one thing only, and he does not know he is wrong about it, and he says it once:
that a person on his list is better kept than a person off it.** Ning Yan survives that sentence and nothing else.

**He returns. He is not the final enemy and must not be built as one.**

## Central pressure

**She has bought her own anonymity and the only person in the world who cannot be stopped using it is the man who keeps
the list.**

Volume 8 made her the strongest person in every room and it turned out to be a qualification. Volume 9 makes her
nobody's reader, nobody's wage, nobody's line — and it turns out there is one book in the world that does not need
anybody to read her in order to write her down. **Nobody is offering her anything. Somebody has come to write her
down.** That is the volume's whole change of register, and `bible/themes.md` gives Volume 9 its temperature as
**long vigil, an old man's apology**.

**And the pressure is the only one she has never had.** In eight years five things have ever come for her and all five
came out of a document she could refuse to open, refuse to answer, or refuse to be read by. **The list cannot be
refused that way, because it is not read to you. It is written about you, after, whether or not you turn up.**

## The thing Volume 9 must not be

**Its pressure is being kept, and what a person is kept on.** **Volume 9 is the first volume in nine in which somebody

a change of air and not as the same book with an older building in it.**

### What Volume 9 takes — the first rung of *Door*, and it is his to take

`outline/series.md` §5 gives *Reacting* as Volumes 9–14: *the system finds him and the machinery starts answering
back*. `outline/series.md` §4 gives him **Door across Volumes 9–11**, and the cost of that stage is **every wall he
opens leaves someone permanently on the wrong side**. **Volume 9 is the volume where he opens one, or does not, and it
is the first volume in the run in which he has had nothing to say for eighty-four days and then has something.**

1. **She opens the paper herself, at ch 488, alone, without being read to.** Not because he tells her and not because
   Shuyin asks — because at ch 470 she worked out what it is to have a document read aloud to you by somebody qualified
   and she is not going to be read to again. **It cannot be un-opened.**
2. **He opens the wall from the inside, once, at ch 518, because she tells him to.** The mechanism is pinned below and
   no drafter invents another: a wall opened from the inside is a document read from the inside, and it leaves one
   person permanently on the wrong side of it. **He tells her the price before he spends it, in a number of one, and
   she says do it anyway.**
3. **She survives what it costs, at ch 519–520, and he has something to say for the first time in over two hundred
   days and it does not save anybody.**

**What breaks:** the assumption that a silence bought is a silence kept. **The list does not need her to answer in order
to keep her, and the door does not need anybody qualified in order to be opened — it needs her, alone, reading.**

### What he does that is the volume's real damage, and it is his oldest habit yet

**He treats one person as an entry in order to keep another person off a page, and he tells her before he does it, and
she lets him.** That is the flaw stated once in the outline and once on the page: `bible/characters.md` — *he treats
people as entries… he lies to her by omission and always calls the omission a kindness.* **In Volume 9 he does not lie
by omission. He names the entry, names the price, and asks. That is worse, because she says yes.**

### How she catches nothing and permits everything — pinned, and it is her own instrument

She does not catch him this volume. **There is no second column to read against him, because the list is not a sheet
with two dates on it and the wall is not a floor.** What she does instead is what she has done since the eighth of a
month seven volumes back: she counts. **She counts the days since he stopped, counts the days since the ninth stage,
counts the coppers, counts the lines on a rail she cannot take — and when the count runs out she tells him to spend
what she has been saving him from spending.** The permission is hers, out loud, once, in a shed with the door shut.

### The frame scene — yes, Volume 9 takes one, and it is pinned

**Ch 518. Away from her hearing, in the layer's own reading-space, a man who has had nothing to spend for over two
hundred days reads what opening from the inside would cost and finds out what a door is when it is not hers to walk
through.** It is the fifth such scene after ch 272, ch 338, ch 401 and ch 458. **It is not a description of him and it
is not a re-run of any of the four.** Every sentence he speaks in it is in the present tense and no other character is
ever in a marked present frame.

**He goes looking for one thing in it and finds it:** whether a wall can be opened from the inside without leaving
someone on the wrong side. **It cannot, and that is the answer, and he is the one who tells her so before she tells him
to do it anyway.**

### The refusal that is this volume's own — and it is not ch 474 again

Volume 6's refusal put something permanent into a book she cannot reach. Volume 7's refusal put **nothing** anywhere.
Volume 8's refusal at ch 474 put her name into the best book on nine days of road, on the record, read back before it
went in. **Volume 9's survival at ch 534–535 puts her own hand into her own book: she writes Shuyin's rule down in
words of her own, in a book of her own, where his hand cannot take it out — and she refuses to let him write her as
kept.** She is entered. She is not kept. The distinction is hers and it stands.

**The cost of surviving, and it is Volume 9's real ending:** Shuyin does not punish her. **He writes her as lost and
walks away.** The list keeps the name. The walls stay. And the reason for them is recorded where he cannot reach it,
which is the first time in nine hundred years that has happened.
