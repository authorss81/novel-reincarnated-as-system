## Batch goal

**She sends her column up in a box, finds out that being entered as good meant it would be read by offices
that never needed a bound, works a season that is not a season because there is no book to put it in, and
answers the question the reading produces — in daylight, out loud, in a room she has never stood in.**

**What the batch is, in one sentence of pressure:** the box circuit takes every book that closes clean on the
thirtieth up the stair to a floor where sheets are copied by hand, and hers was the only one in the cart
with a name at the top of it and the word *good* above the hours.

**This batch establishes that being entered as good is not a compliment. It is being read upward. And it
leaves her read.**

**What this batch buys, and what it pays for:** she buys a running column — a person, in a book, with a
date on it after the season closed — and she pays for it with the only thing she still had that nobody else
could certify, which is the truth of her own days. That trade is the batch's irreversible decision and it
happens at ch 308.

**What this batch does NOT buy:** a hiring. **A second vouching. GC5. GC6–9.** The Hand, spent at ch 325 in
Batch 0002. The sending, written in Batch 0003. The break in the box circuit at ch 344. The refusal at
ch 358. Nascent Soul at ch 360. A merit settlement. The tenth part. An answer to the Hunter's request. A
name for any office above the one whose name is known. **The sustained scene in which Yuan is on the page

### The box circuit — **new machinery, established in this batch, and it carries the volume**

**Nothing in Volume 5 set this down. It is written here once, at ch 300, and no chapter before 300 may state
it and no chapter after 300 may re-explain it.**

- **A far-side book that closes clean on the thirtieth is a finished book.** A finished book goes into a box.
  The box goes up to the mill **on the first cart after the book shuts.** At the mill a man with a rule and a
  paste-pot turns boxes over and sorts them, and **every box goes up the stair**, because up is where finished
  books are read against other finished books.
- **The box does not come back down.** It is not kept at the mill. **The mill is not the archive; it is the
  place boxes pass through.**
- **A book that does not close clean does not go up.** That rule exists and nobody past the mill has ever
  seen it broken, **and Volume 6 breaks it at ch 344.** **No card in this batch breaks it, bends it, hints
  at breaking it, or says in any mouth that a box could stay.**
- **Every reach on that ground sends a cart a month.** The reach she worked sent one box. **Other reaches
  send their own**, and hers was not special, and the sorter does not look twice at any of them.
- **A strip of cloth is what an office sends down**, the same cloth the keeper's board is made of, cut to a
  shape, and it is read by the counter and pasted into the lower book's margin or answered and sent back up.
  **This is the only channel between floors in this batch and it runs one way: down, with an answer, or up
  with nothing.**
