# Volume 3, Batch 0001 — Chapters 119–132

## Batch goal

She lives licensed for the first time in her life. County hours with nobody standing behind her,
paid work outside the crew by strangers who do not know what she is, a hirer's sheet that is not a
county sheet, and a first quarterly return five weeks out on a road that shuts in the weather. A man
who buys nothing and gives his name to nobody pays for a certified copy of her furnace schedule, and
he is right about the hole in her stage column, and that is what puts the relicensing in motion. She
works two schedules at once, gets three men who owe her nothing to sign for the thing their own writ
died under, and walks out of the county office relicensed to **Condensing nine in her own hand** with
seven conditions on the back and the eight hundred jin re-entered as the last of them.

**This batch buys a ceiling and not a body. Nothing gets stronger. Everything gets more expensive.**

## Chapter range

Chapters 119–132. Fourteen chapters. **Sized to its own arc** — licensed life, the first return coming
due, the Hunter's first purchase, double hours, relicensing granted — not to a fixed count. The arc
closes cleanly on ch 132; nothing was padded to reach fourteen and nothing was split to fit.

**In-world clock, canon for this batch.** **Day 0 is the last day of the thaw**, which is also where
the season's books close (ch 115). Ch 118 sits in the days immediately after it. The batch runs **day
2 to day 39 — five and a half weeks.**

**The return has two numbers on the page and they are not the same number.** Ch 116 fixes it: *that is
four weeks off the last day of the thaw, and the crate goes up on the day and not the one before, so the
working count is five weeks, and Sun He reads a return in the first week after it.* So **the crate
leaves the tower on day 28** — four weeks off, on the day, not the one before — and the **five weeks**
she has been counting since ch 118 is the *working count*, which is the crate day plus Sun He's reading
week. Ch 129 is the crate, on **day 28**. Nothing in this batch may move the crate off day 28 or move
it earlier, and nothing may treat the return as arriving before it.

**The week the return lands in is now the fifth week of summer.** Ch 116 and ch 118 each used to say
*the second week of summer*, which no version of the calendar supported — not the old day-35 plan and
not day 28. **Repaired word-level in the chapters on the review pass** (*second* → *fifth*, and ch 118's
*first week* → *fourth week*), so the page now agrees with ch 116's own four-weeks / five-weeks /
first-week-after arithmetic. Ch 116's separate line *five weeks to the quarter's return* was **left
alone deliberately**: it is the working count, which is the number she has been counting by since ch 118,
and it is not a claim about the crate day. Do not "correct" it. Full note in `state/continuity.md`
under *Repaired at the Volume 3 Batch 0001 planning pass*.

Chapter days: 119 = day 2 · 120 = 5 · 121 = 8 · 122 = 11 · 123 = 14 · 124 = 17 · 125 = 20 ·
126 = 23 · 127 = 25 · 128 = 27 · 129 = 28 · 130 = 31 · 131 = 35 · 132 = 39. Do not re-date these
without reconciling every elapsed figure in the chapter. **Ch 120 is the only card that spans two
days** — it opens on day 4 and closes on day 5, and its next-chapter pull (*a county hour in three
days*) is counted from the close, which is ch 121 on day 8. Every other chapter is a single day.

**The county hour is not weekly by rule.** Nothing on the page fixes its cadence. This batch has two
county-hour scenes, at day 8 and day 23, and a drafter must not invent a weekly rule, name a weekday,
or state one. **The road is open and the cleared cart days are counted** — the pass is a winter event —
so a chapter that uses the road must place its day inside a cleared day and must not claim to know
which weekday it was.

## Batch midpoint (ch 126)

**The Hunter buys his first thing.** A certified copy of her furnace schedule at the county office.
Coin on open wood, a receipt kept in his own hand, in daylight, at the public counter, while she is
eight feet away signing her own county hour in her own hand.

He reads the stage column — one, two, three, five — and says, to Sun He's clerk, in the ordinary
voice of a man making an observation in a room with a queue in it:

**the next stage must be announced or the paper fails its own terms.**

**He is right.** The reader has Sun He's ch 115 ruling and knows the county reads absence; the reader
also knows the Warden's office is a different reader and nothing stops the second reading.

He says the second true thing too, to the same clerk: **a licensee capped at five cannot be examined
past five.** That sentence is the relicensing. It goes from the man to the registrar to three sponsors
in six chapters, and the relicensing cannot begin until he has gone back to his room.

**Not a ch 131 beat.** The purchase, his being right, and the sponsors being told are one causal run.
Ch 126 is six chapters ahead of the climax and the cards must show the purchase *causing* the
relicensing — ch 127 is the chain moving, ch 128 is the sponsors' price, ch 129 is the record, ch 130
is the clearance that has to be level first, ch 131 is the seal, ch 132 is the stamp.

## Batch climax (ch 132)

**The relicensing granted.** Counter signed by Registrar Sun He at the county office on the square.
Ceiling at **Condensing nine in her own hand**, defect re-entered, Foundation barred again without a
second relicensing. **Seven conditions on the back** — the six carried forward with the amendment each
one actually requires, plus one new: the annex hours are certified furnace hours and return with the
quarter. **The eight hundred jin is re-entered as the last condition**, because Sun He is required to
enter it.

**The legs run in this order and the order is deliberate:**

1. **Clearance — Shen Xiu, ch 130.** A second quota clearance, the annex hours level with the asked
   figure at the close of the season.
2. **Seal — Lu Kang, Deng Chao, Pei An, all three on one sheet, ch 131.**
3. **Countersignature — Sun He, ch 132.**

**Inverted from Volume 2 on purpose. Do not tidy it back to seal → clearance → countersignature.** The
clearance has to be level *before* three men will put a seal on a ceiling above five: a member they
cannot account for in the winter book is the exact liability they have spent two volumes refusing to
create, and they know it because ch 69 put her hours in front of them. The seal then has something to
seal, and Sun He has a document worth his stamp. **A drafter who “corrects” this order destroys the
sponsors' motive and moves the ch 126 purchase out of causation.**

**No leg is skipped. No signature is forged, borrowed, or worked around.**

## Required character changes

She becomes, for the first time, **a person who is paid by strangers and described by them to each
other.** She learns that legal life is a wage, a lane and a column, and that the first thing it costs
is half a day's pay every time she has to be in a county room. She discovers that the man in her head
can feel her arm go before she can, and that this is not a favour. She finds the only route to a
ceiling and it goes through the three men she is least able to ask. She has one weapon against them
and does not fire it, and the not-firing is permanent. **She does not forgive him.**

## Required world reveals

1. **What paid work outside the crew actually is in Lowwater**: stone, oil, paper, water, one
   road-guard hire. Paid by the load and the day. **Their sheets are the only other schedules in
   reach** and they are not county sheets.
2. **A licensed woman's week is a wage and a column.** The licence's scope line — paid work outside
   the crew — is the whole of what four months bought, and it is worth exactly a hire.
3. **A relicensing is a second application down the same three legs**, carrying an hours record
   continuous **from the day the tithe book opened** (ch 115 reads that requirement aloud), and it
   buys a ceiling in her own hand and restores nothing.
4. **Paid work outside the crew is not on the Qinghe furnace schedule**, and the second condition
   says no other schedule counts. The annex is the way round it: hours certified onto the furnace
   schedule by the Hall clerk are furnace-schedule hours **by certification, not by place.**
5. **The county reads a licensee as paper, and a cap stops an examination as well as a body.** This
   is the same sentence twice, in two directions, and ch 132 is where they finally collide.
6. **A sponsor's seal on a ceiling above the one their writ died under is the hardest signature in the
   county — and they will give it.** A member examined inside their own schedule is the only thing on
   that schedule that ever worked; a member surrendered to a Warden's office is a book opened.
7. **The Hunter buys paper, not proof, and keeps receipts.** He is patient, he is not cleverer than
   her, and he never lies. He is more patient with paper.
