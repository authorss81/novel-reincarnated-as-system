## Batch goal

She breaks the one term she wrote in the spring, in public, on her own word, with the price named before
she says the word — and spends four and a half weeks of about ten to have a patient man read through her
eyes so that she does not have to take a stranger's paper on trust. Then she opens the paper, hears it
read, asks the two questions a competent person asks of a mercy, gets true answers to both, **refuses it
aloud in a county room in front of two witnesses, signs the refusal in her own hand, and has her own
refusal entered against her in a Warden's book where a clerk writes the name of the man who laid it.**
From that hour she is hunted in writing, monthly instead of quarterly, by a man who attends from the
square and buys nothing and writes everything down. She works a winter that has no work in it, loses the
hand she signs her hours with to a quarry sledge, and suppresses stage eight at her own order, and the
column in four counties takes its second hole — and a reader above this county reads an absence as
concealment, and he is right.

## Chapter 149 — Carried Down the Stair

Chapter: 149. POV: Ning Yan. Location/time: the tower loft in Lowwater at the fourth hour; the copying
floor in the same tower at the ninth hour; the lane behind a well. Day 133.

Immediate goal: Find out whether a paper that has been lying unopened on her mat for four days is true,
before she opens it and before she answers it.
Resistance: She cannot read it herself — she can read, she has been reading paper since the spring, but
she cannot know a man; she can only know a document. And the only way to know a man is to break the term
she wrote in the spring with her own hand, in front of a keeper who reports in full whether or not it is
entered, and pay for it out of a channel of about ten weeks.

Information revealed: The paper came to the loft on day 129 while she was in the bed with the fever, put
there by a man who knocked and did not come in, and it has been on the mat for four days. **It is not
addressed. It is not signed by any person.** The condition under which she would even consider it is the
one thing she cannot do herself.

Action or decision: **She says the order out loud on the copying floor, in front of Feng.** *Read him.*
**She names the price first, out loud, before the word** — four and a half weeks out of about ten — and
he says yes said. Then she goes up and says the word alone.
Emotional change: The terror is of her own handwriting, not of the paper. She has spent fourteen months
building a life out of documents that hold still, and she is about to spend four and a half weeks of the
only thing she has that does not hold still, in public, on her own signature.
Power or resource change: **Ledger taken and put down the same day. Channel about ten weeks → about five
and a half at the price, and about three weeks by ch 160.** **Ten less four and a half is five and a half;
the fall to three is ordinary rationed use and is accounted for on the page at ch 160.**
**No bracketed line.**

Continuity fact to preserve: She does not thank him and she does not forgive him. Do not re-list the ch
118 conditions or the seven licence conditions — reference the term as *the sentence she wrote in the
spring about the winter and about no version of it*. **Bai Zhu is not named.** Do not open the paper's
terms in dialogue — they are verified, not discussed, until ch 150. **Feng carries it down the stair as a
tower change.**

Ending type: irreversible decision, with its price already paid.

## Chapter 150 — What Stops Talking

hers and that she is the one it is about**, so that when it is read again he will know it is being read
about her and not about him.
