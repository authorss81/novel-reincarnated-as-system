## Central pressure

**She is read correctly and understood wrongly.** From ch 358 the country past the line has to deal with her where
she stands, and the offices that read her there have stopped sending for her and started reading her out loud.
The country east of the mill is farmed and lit, has rooms in it that Volume 6 never reached, and those rooms are
full of people who were never inside a bound — who carry furnaces, who lift dressed stone with one hand, who are
three or four stages above her and were never going to be otherwise.

**Every one of those rooms reads her column, her furnace and her holder, takes all three for granted, and prices
her at what she can lift. She cannot lift anything in them.** She is entered everywhere and understood nowhere.

**Nothing in this volume is an enemy. The pressure is a misreading that is not a lie.** Every office that reads a
line on this ground can also act on it, and the act it takes is to try to use her for what a column is, which is
to be *read out*. She has never once been the strongest thing in a room and Volume 7 is the volume where she
finds out what to do about that, and the answer is not to become stronger.

### What Volume 7 takes, stated here so that no drafter goes looking for it

**Volume 7 takes the second rung of *Breaking* (Volumes 6–8).** `outline/series.md` §5 gives that stage as

**The second rung is the direction of a sending.** Every instrument on that ground read downward in Volume 6: an
office copied her column upward, a strip came back down the mill road with a date on it, and she refused to be

**What breaks is the assumption that a line on this ground can only be read downward. What the offices can send

is the protagonist's act, not hers.

**It is not** an opening of the Wall, a statement about what is beyond it, a naming of any office above the
copying floor or the second office, or the Order anywhere on the page.

### The refusal that is this volume's own — and it is not ch 358 again

Volume 6's refusal put something permanent into a book she cannot reach. **Volume 7's refusal, at ch 417, puts
nothing anywhere.** She is offered the one thing she has wanted since a strip came down the works' road at the
seventeenth of a month: a man whose job it is can read the fine hand, and he will read it to her, once, in a
room, on the ordinary condition that she confirms or denies it. **She refuses.** A denial is an entry. So she goes
on not knowing what the fine hand says about her, forever, on purpose.

**Nothing in that building fails. No book will not shut. Nothing is written.** The bill is entirely hers and it is
enormous, and it is the volume's second irreversible decision: **she gives up a want she has carried for two
volumes to avoid becoming a person who can be entered twice.**

### The announcement rule, re-derived for Volume 7, on the page in the outline and not deferred to a batch

**The rule did not change. The listeners did.**

Inside the bound every stage was announced out loud, in a room, to people who had to write it down before she
finished saying it. Past the last working point the layer still announces, because that is what announcements are
for, but there is nobody inside to be told and no county book to take it — so the sound goes into the ground.
**Volume 6 established that what the offices above take is not the sound but the reading.**

**Volume 7's change is that the rooms have learned to look.** A stage taken in the open past the mill used to be
a thing a fine hand wrote down afterward, in a hand she cannot read, about three days later, at a gate post on a
cart. From **ch 371** it is a thing that two or three people on that ground have watched happen, in daylight, at
the seventh hour, on a flat with a mill in it — and after that it is a thing that is discussed in rooms she is not
in, at figures she cannot check.

**Who hears it:** still nobody hears a sound anywhere. **But the reading has a second kind of listener now, and
she is it.** A person who can read a column out loud in a room is not a clerk and not a cultivator and is not on
any board on that ground, and the offices cannot produce one, because a column is read by whoever has been doing
it for sixteen years and there is a post for that and there are not two.

**What that does to her, and it is the volume's engine:** from ch 371 every stage she takes is a public fact, and
from the moment the rooms can see what she is, **she is entered as a body and not only as a column** — which is
the last and worst thing an office on this ground can do with a line, and it is the reason she is the weakest thing
in every room she walks into. Nothing announces it. A man at a bar simply looks at her and works out where she
goes in the order.

Each batch carries its own goal, midpoint, escalation and climax, **at least one scene of physical risk or a real
deadline**, and at least one irreversible step. **Batch 0003 owns the break (ch 401–402) and Batch 0004 owns the
refusal (ch 417); neither batch restates the other's as its own**, and neither is Volume 6's break (ch 344, a book
that shuts clean) or Volume 6's refusal (ch 358, a permanent entry in a building she cannot reach). **The three
acts in three consecutive volumes are three different shapes: a circuit broken, an entry made, and an answer
withheld.**

**The batch-level action requirements, pinned before the cards are written:**

- **Batch 0001** — a loaded bar on a bank with nine men on it, in the open, on a real date, on the last day the
  furnace lights itself. **A load goes, she is under it, and the furnace in the shed is what stops it — and the
  next morning it does not.**
- **Batch 0002** — the furnace, a second pair of hands in it, and it goes wrong, and **the burn is on a man with a
  paste pot who was owed nothing and was not asked.**
- **Batch 0003** — the reach used deliberately, on a deadline, with people under a load, at ch 394 or 395, and the
  thing it moved does not go back.
- **Batch 0004** — the lifting floor, a bar, a chain, a man under a dressed stone, and a bell that has already
  gone. **She moves the stone the length of her own arm, which is all the reach can do, and it is not enough to
  put it back, and the floor is a block short for a week.**
