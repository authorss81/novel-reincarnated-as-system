## Goal

## Central pressure

**She is in a room where things are decided by a hand, and the hand belongs to somebody else, and she is the smallest thing in it.**

Volume 11 made her the person who knows. Volume 12 made her the person who has to be *seen*. **Volume 13 makes her the person who is
seen, and is entered by what the seeing produced, and does not get to write it.**

The whole machinery of the mortal world came up that stair on a bundle once a month for eleven hundred years, and every bundle went
onto a long table and was written down. **She is the first thing that has ever come up that stair that the room has not been built to
hold**, and the room has one instrument, and the instrument is a hand, and the deciding takes one look, and there is no second look and
no appeal.

`bible/themes.md` gives Volume 13 its temperature as **smallness, courtly cruelty**, and this outline takes both: **smallness is a
thing the reader has to see her be** — a woman who cannot reach a table made for longer arms, in a building where nobody counts days
because nobody up there loses them, carrying a record of what she cost that is invisible to every person making the decision — **and
courtly cruelty is procedural and bored, which is what `outline/series.md` §6 gives this volume: *the immortal world's ordinary cruelty,
which is procedural and bored*.** Nobody up there is a villain. They are correct, brief, and have four hundred years of practice at
not being interested.

**That is the whole change of air, and it is the first time in thirteen volumes that the thing standing between her and what she wants
is a set of people who are not trying to stop her.**

## What the volume must not be

Volume 12 ran being seen and what being seen costs. Volume 11 ran asking and what answering opens. Volume 10 ran asking a list. Volume 9
ran being kept. Volume 8 ran seduction and being wanted. Volume 7 ran rooms bigger than she is. Volume 6 ran reading and being read.

**Volume 13 is none of those, and none of its batches is a recruiting, a seduction, a tending, a ration, a hiring, a crossing of a wall,
an audit, an examination, a hunt, a signature, a confinement, a second demand in a room, a second asking about a list, a wage-book, a
debt, a family paper, an ash, or an audit of a budget.**

**Volume 7 ran rooms bigger than she is and Volume 13 is not that either, and the difference is the whole of it.** In Volume 7 nobody
looked at her. **Here everybody looks at her and there is one look and it decides.** A room that is too big is a nuisance. A room that
decides what you are in one look, four hundred years after it stopped asking, is the thing the volume is about.

**The one thing Volume 13 must not be is Volume 8.** Volume 8 was an institution that wants her as an instrument and has the shape of
one. **Volume 13 is an institution that does not want her at all.** Nobody in the room makes her an offer in Volume 13 and nobody
recruits her and nobody reads her a sheet. **The offer that has been on the table since Volume 8 is *stay small and unentered and alive*,
and nobody has to say it out loud, because the room is already doing it, and that is worse.**

### What he does that is the volume's damage, and it is his oldest habit again

**He keeps the record — and this time the record is the only way she can be entered.**

Volume 10 he named another name as an entry out loud. Volume 11 he named his own. Volume 12 he asked for the pen and she gave it to him.

in every room she has ever said them in, and **the seventh term is not available and is not asked for**, and the reader is left with the
fact that a record kept out loud with six terms was never a thing that could survive being read by somebody who was not one of the two.

## How she catches all of it and permits all of it — pinned, and it is still counting

She does not catch him. **There has never been a second voice to read him against and there is not one in Volume 13.** What she does is
what she has done for thirteen volumes: **she counts, and then she checks the count against the page**, and the whole of Volume 13 is
what happens when the page cannot be reached.

**And the volume's plainest sentence about one person, written once and not improved on:** *She has spent nineteen years being the only
person in every room who could do arithmetic, and she has gone up a stair to a room where nobody does arithmetic at all, and the one
thing she has is the thing they are looking for.*

## The structural decisions this outline makes, and why

**Recorded here because a close will audit against them and because a planning phase is allowed to decide a structural question by
recording the decision and its reason, and is not allowed to settle one by fiat or to leave a card needing a fact the outline does not
have. Nothing above the second stair exists on any page yet, and every one of these is a *shape*, not a population: **each card must earn
its people, its rules and its scale out of what she does with what she has, and no card may announce any of this as exposition.**

### D1 — What the Court is, and why it is a room and not a building

**Decision. The Court is the receiving end, and its whole function is that things arrive and a person writes down what arrived.** It is
Feng Lushi's job with the ceiling taken off: a mortal clerk in a tower four miles up a mountain, keeping a lamp in a cage and copying
upward to something he never named, for twenty-two years. **Above the low door there is the same room and there is a lamp in a cage at
the far end of a long table and no chairs, and ch 719 put the no chairs on the page two chapters before Volume 12 ended: *there are no
chairs. There have never been chairs in that room.***

**And the no chairs is a fact with a cause, and the cause is earned in Batch 0001 and not stated before it: nobody who has stood in this
room for four hundred years has ever stayed long enough to want to sit down.** It is not austerity and it is not reverence. It is what a
room looks like when the only work in it takes eleven seconds.

**Why this and not another reading.** Because it comes out of Volume 12 and not out of a fresh piece of furniture. The tower, the four
flights, the copying floor, the drawer, the boy, the lamp, the quarter, the bundle on a clerk's arm — all of it is already on the page,
and **the room at the top of the stair was drawn as a receiving room before it was drawn as anything else**, and the only reason it is a
Court is that the announcements she finally made arrive here and there is nothing in the mortal world that receives them.

### D2 — What *decides* means, stated before any card spends it

**The plan must say what decides means before any card spends it, and this is it:**

> Above the line, a thing is whatever the last hand at the table wrote in the row for it. **There is one instrument. The instrument is a
> hand.** Nothing arrives as itself; it arrives as a line somebody put in a column, and the column is the thing. **The deciding takes one
> look and there is no second look and there is no appeal, because in four hundred years there has never been anything to appeal with.**

**A thing that arrives with no row is not refused. It is administered.** Administration above the line is procedural and bored: there is
a form of arriving, there is a place a thing waits, and there is a procedure for a thing that has waited, and the procedure is run by
people who have run it four hundred times and will run it four hundred more. **Nobody in this room is obstructing her. Nobody in this
room is the antagonist. They are correct, brief, and not interested, and that is `outline/series.md` §6's line for Volumes 13–14 —
*the immortal world's ordinary cruelty, which is procedural and bored* — and the volume's whole job is to make the reader feel an
ordinary afternoon.**

**And the shape of it on the page is one sentence long and it is said by nobody:** *She arrived, and nothing was written, and the not
writing is not a decision anybody made.*

**`Ascendant` is 0 and `Choir` is 0 on the page in Volume 13.** Those are the series table's names for the pressure and they are not
anybody's word. **`Court` may be spoken at most once in the volume, in Batch 0004, by the one at the table, in the ordinary voice, as the
word for the building and not for a person, and no character in Volume 13 ever says it about themselves.**

### D3 — Being small, and the three ways the page shows it instead of saying it

**The outline does not describe scale. It fixes three measurable facts and requires every card to show at least one of them.**

1. **She cannot reach the table.** The long table is made for hands longer than hers. **Every chapter that has her in that room shows
   her get a hand on the edge of it or not get one, and no chapter explains that it is because she is small.**
2. **Above the line nobody counts days, because above the line nobody loses them.** She is the only person in the building — and by
   ch 750, in the whole volume — who knows what day it is. **She arrives on day 2,301 and the building does not mark it and there is
   nothing in the room she can count except a lamp and a person.** **The figure she has carried since she was nine does not stop above
   the line. It goes on, one hour and twelve minutes a day, whether or not she can see the day turn.** That is the volume's cruelty in
   its plainest form and it is never explained to her.
3. **Every price she has paid is invisible to everybody making the decision.** She is the first person this room has met whose arm does
   not close, whose hand has four digits on it, and who has been lighter every day for seven hundred and forty-one days and a half.
   **Not one of the three is on her face and not one of the three is in a row, and the only figure the room holds about her is the
   price.** That is `bible/themes.md`'s *mercy that costs nothing to the person granting it is not mercy* — except that there is no mercy
   here either. There is a number, and the number is getting more wrong every day, and nobody has done anything cruel.

### D4 — The lamp, and what it decides

**Decision: the lamp is the instrument, the clock, the currency and the cost, and it is the only one of the four that the reader can see.**

- **It is not an oil lamp.** There is no oil in the building four miles up a mountain, there has not been for nine years, and there is a
  day and a half of oil in a stone jar under a bench on the fourth landing that ran out on or about day 2,296. **The lamp in the cage at
  the top of the second stair burns, and there is nothing to put in it, and that is the first thing above the line she cannot count.**
- **It is fed by hand.** The one at the table turns it down to a mark and turns it back up, and something about the person is different
  afterwards, and **she measures the lamp and the person and works out that they are the same substance.** This is Batch 0001's climax
  and it is a *finding*, not an explanation: she does not know what the substance is, she knows what it costs, and she knows it is the
  same thing that has been coming off her every day since she was nine.
- **Feeding it is the whole of the ceremony and the whole of the price.** A stage above the line is entered by being written in the row,
  and the row is written by a hand, and the hand writes in the light of that lamp. **Every entry costs a feed. That is the rule, it is
  four hundred years old, nobody thinks it is remarkable, and it is the first rule in thirteen volumes that she is not the only person
  obeying.**
- **The interval between feeds is about thirty days and it is not a schedule.** It is fed when the lamp reaches a mark, and the mark is
  the same every time, and **it is not tied to the first of a month — the bundle comes up on the first and the lamp is the room's own and
  those are two different things, and she confuses them for two days before she works out that they are two different things.** The room
  counts the interval in **hands** and she counts it in **days**, and **the two have never agreed, because a hand turns a lamp as often as
  it has to and nobody has ever written down how often that is.** She measures it at twenty-nine days against thirty, and the discrepancy
  is the whole of what is wrong with the room's arithmetic and it is the first thing she has found that she can correct.

**And the line this volume may not cross, stated here so no card walks over it:** **she learns what feeding the lamp costs and who is
charged by it, in the single instance of herself. She does not learn what the lamp is paying for, and Volume 13 may not tell her, and no
mouth in Volume 13 may tell her.** **Volume 12 knew what the levy was for and did not know what it was paying for. Volume 13 gets one
step further and no further, and that step is the whole moral argument the ending owns and this volume may not touch.**

### D5 — The row, and what goes in it, and the figure at the head of it

**Decision. A row is a head, a hand, and a column. The head is a figure. The hand belongs to whoever is at the table. Nothing enters a
row that the person it is about did not put there, or that a hand at the table wrote there because a thing arrived.**

**The figure at the head of her row is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes.**

**It is the figure she wrote at ch 717 on a slate at the two posts, at the first hour of the first of the month beginning 2,294,
outdoors, in daylight, on an arm that was open, with a nib she did not lift — nineteen words with a date and a place on them, true,
because her hand cannot write a false line and because nobody writes one twice. It went up a stair at the second hour of that morning
in a bundle with about forty other boards, and a room at the top of it read it before it wrote anything, and the shape went up with it,
and there has never been a name in this building.**

**And here is what is in the head of that row, and it is the whole of the volume: the figure is still growing.** One in twenty of every
day a name has had in a column comes off it, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and it has been coming off her since the ninth hour
of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054. **She is the only person alive who knows it is growing. Above the line nobody loses
days, so there is nobody above the line who could ever notice that the figure at the head of her row has gone stale, and there is nobody
who could correct it, and that row is the only place in the world the figure exists except inside her own head.**

**So the room knows exactly what she is, in the sense the question was asked — how much she is worth, to the hour — and it does not know
her stage, and it does not know her name, and it is never going to know either, and the figure it is keeping for her is getting more
wrong every single day and there is nobody standing at the table who could tell.**

**This is the volume's thesis in one line and it is checkable by the reader from Volume 12 alone:** *a record of what she cost is not a
record of her, and the thing being kept for her is the price and not the person.*

**And her own figure is a stage, and she writes it herself, in her own hand, under the room's figure, in Batch 0003 — five, because she
is at Immortal Vessel 5 by then and there is no board up here to have said so.** That is Batch 0003's irreversible step: **a row with two
figures in it is a row that gets counted, because a figure that is a price can only be added up and a figure that is a person can be
counted, and being counted is the thing she has been escaping since she was nine years old.**

| **Immortal Vessel 4** | **She cannot be given a thing.** | New, and it is the ladder. | **Immortal Vessel 5** | **She cannot be measured with a person.** | New, and it is the room's instrument failing. The row measures a thing by comparison with the body at the table and has done for four hundred years. **It cannot measure her at all, and it writes down a figure anyway, and the figure is four stages stale** |
| **Immortal Vessel 6** | **She cannot be looked at again.** | New, and it is the rule from D2 arriving as a price. One look decided and the row said three and there is no second look, **and so whatever the row says after that is what she is and everything she does afterwards is done inside it** |

**And the three Volume 12 costs — she cannot be given a shape · she cannot be met where she is expected · she cannot be counted where she
stands — are applied at scale in this volume and are not re-explained.** They are the volume's obstacles and they are facts, not
revelations: a room that is expecting somebody cannot be met by her (Batch 0002, the one at the table has stood up and gone out of the
room four times while she was not there) · a harvest that takes a board cannot take a person, and she is one, and the room's whole
instrument takes a board (Batch 0003) · and a shape that cannot be given to her cannot be handed over either (Batch 0002).

### D7 — How she takes a stage with no board, no slate and no counter to take it on

**The prompt requires this and this is the answer, and it comes out of D4 and D5.**

**There is no board above the line, no slate, no counter and no ground. A stage is taken by being written in a row, by a hand, in the
light of a lamp that has to be fed to be lit, on a day the feeder chooses.**

**So the interval between two stages has to be counted twice: in days by her, and in feeds by the room — and the two have never agreed,
because the room's count is a count of hands and hers is a count of days, and hands are not days.** She is the first person who has ever
counted both, and the chapter that says so does not explain why it matters, and the chapter after it has to live inside a number that
does not fit.

**Every stage chapter states the interval by subtraction from the day number, names the month-begin it falls in under
`((day − 134) mod 30) + 1`, and says once why that interval and not the last one, in her mouth, in the ordinary voice, as arithmetic and
not as a lesson.**

### D8 — The second place at the table

**Decision: there are two places at the long table. One is occupied. The other is not a chair, is not a seat, is addressed, and is the
right size for him.**

**It is not a frame scene and it is not a vision and it is not put there by anybody in Volume 13.** It is a space in a room, found by a
woman who cannot reach the table and therefore has to look along it, and the ninth frame scene at ch 758 is a fragment looking at the
same room from the inside of the thing that keeps her and finding the second place and not getting into it.

**It is the whole of Volume 14 and this volume does not open it.** He does not say yes to it in Volume 13 and he does not say no, and
she cannot hear either, and **the only person in the volume who notices it is the boy of sixteen, in the last two chapters, and he
notices it because he is sixteen and he is standing in a doorway and he looks at a room the way a person looks at a room.**

**And what a first is, in this volume, is not what it was in Volume 12.** In Volume 12 a first was a date and not an event and she
worked that out at ch 707 in four days at a frame. **Here a first is the day the apparatus comes up the stair.** She does not re-derive
Volume 12's finding — she carries it — and what she has to learn is the other half of it: **a first is a date, and on this side of the
door a date has a bundle attached to it, and the bundle is the only thing that arrives in this room in a year.**
