## Central pressure

**She is keeping a record in a room with no light in it, and the room has found somebody else to keep it,
and the somebody else is him.**

Volume 11 made her the person who knows. Volume 12 made her the person who has to be seen. Volume 13
made her the person who is seen and entered and does not get to write it. **Volume 14 makes her the
person who is offered a keeper for the record she cannot read — a second place at the table, addressed,
the right size for the voice in her head — and the price of the keeper is one person bound to feed the
lamp, and she has to refuse it the way he once refused it.**

`bible/themes.md` gives Volume 14 its temperature as **temptation, and the first time he says yes** — and
this outline takes both literally and in order: **for three batches he does not say yes, and the boy, the
room, the dark and the arithmetic all press him toward it, and at the midpoint he says yes once, in the
present tense, where she cannot hear it, and the rest of the volume is what his yes costs her and what her
no then has to buy back.** The temptation is real, patient, and kind. Nobody in this volume is a villain.
The offer is correct, brief, and has four hundred years of practice at not being interested — and this
time it is interested, and that is worse.

## What the volume must not be

Volume 13 ran smallness in a room that decides in one look. Volume 12 ran being seen and what being seen
costs. Volume 11 ran asking and what answering opens. Volume 10 ran asking a list. Volume 9 ran being
kept. Volume 8 ran seduction and being wanted.

**Volume 14 is none of those, and none of its batches is a recruiting, a hiring, a wage-book, a debt, a
family paper, an ash, an audit, an examination, a hunt, a signature, a confinement, a second asking about
a list, or a second deciding look.** The look happened once at ch 776 and there is no second look and no
appeal, and no card spends one.

**The one thing Volume 14 must not be is Volume 8.** Volume 8 was an institution that wants her as an
instrument and has the shape of one. **Volume 14 is an institution that wants him as an instrument and has
the shape of a chair that is not a chair.** Nobody offers her anything in Volume 14. The offer is made to
the voice, in writing, where she can read it, and **she is the only person in the world who can refuse it
on his behalf by refusing to vacate the record it needs — and that is the whole of the volume.**

## The structural decisions this outline makes, and why

### D1 — What the apprenticeship is, stated before any card spends it

> Above the line, a keeper is a hand that writes in the light of a lamp somebody else feeds. The second
> place at the table has been empty for four hundred years because no hand has ever needed keeping: the
> rows arrived written and the lamp was fed by the writer. **She has brought the first row that arrived
> unwritten and the first dark the room has ever worked in. The room does not want her. It wants the
> voice that can read in the dark — him — to sit where the writing is done and keep what she says.**

**The offer, in its whole shape, and no card offers more:** be addressed, be archived, be remembered, be
given work that matters — keep her record, and the lamp fed, and the rows true. **The price, on the same
page and in the same hand:** one person bound to feed the lamp in place of the writer, as every row in
that building for four hundred years has been paid for out of a forearm. **One binding. Named once. That
is the thing Yuan was offered six hundred years ago and refused, and that is the thing she must refuse
here, or the whole bargain survives into Volume 15 with her consent on it.**

### D2 — The offer arrives in writing, never in speech

**Nobody in Volume 14 offers anybody anything out loud.** The one at the table has one speech in seven
hundred and eighty chapters, the amending offer at ch 772, and the volume's only other two are the ch 826
terms page and the ch 838 acceptance — both of which are **answers to her and not offers**, and neither of
which is out loud: she reads the first out loud in her own voice and the second happens at the end. See
*Who is in the room*, where this is flagged as the first it is. The offer comes up the stair on boards, on
firsts of months, in a fair hand: first his name (ch 810), then the place described as kept (ch 818),
then the terms with the price (ch 826). **She reads each one out loud in the dark in the ordinary voice,
standing, and that is how the reader hears them — in her mouth, not in a mouth.** The Scribe does not
speak in Volume 14. The address is Volume 15's.

### D3 — The dark is the volume's instrument, and the boy's lamp is its measure

The cage lamp is out and **no chapter of Volume 14 relights it** — any chapter that lights it owes the
reader an on-page reason and a cost, and no card in this outline spends that. What lights the room is the
boy's oil lamp, a cup and a half of oil, three lamps in rotation carried up eight hundred and eighty steps.
**Every chapter in the room states how far the light goes (a hand's width along the near end, twenty-two
paces of dark past it) and no chapter explains that it is because she is small.** The dark is not hiding
her: **at ch 793 she learns the dark does not hide her either, because a boy with oil can see her hands
and nothing in the room decides anything when he does.** That is Immortal Vessel 7's cost arriving as a
finding.

### D5 — How she takes a stage with no light, no board she can see, and no hand she can check

**A stage is taken by being written in a row, by a hand, in light.** There is no cage light. So every
stage chapter of Volume 14 is taken **in the oil light, on her own strip or a board out of the stack, in
her own hand with the nib against her fourth rib, read out loud before it is a line anywhere** (the fourth
term), and **every stage chapter states the interval by subtraction from the day number, names the
month-begin under `((day − 134) mod 30) + 1`, and says once why that interval and not the last one, in her
mouth, in the ordinary voice, as arithmetic and not as a lesson.**

### D6 — The bracket budget: zero in sixty, and the reason is Volume 13's carried forward

**Volume 14 spends nothing in square brackets anywhere in sixty chapters.** The reason is Volume 13's and
it still holds: above the line the layer is not in the room. The room is what the layer is for. The one
moment a batch will want it — the third hand's board arriving — is rendered as what a room does when a
bundle comes up a stair: it puts it on the table and moves on, and there is no line in brackets about it.
**A bracketed line at the finding would put the layer's voice back in the book at the exact moment the
book is about a thing that is not the layer deciding. The thing that decides in Volume 14 is a hand with
a nib in it, and the thing that reads is a boy with oil, and that is the whole of the volume.**

## The oil, as a clock — Batch 0001 runs on a deadline and not on a mood

**Batch 0001 is sixty chapters of pressure and the pressure has a date on it. Without this the batch is
fifteen variations on *she states arithmetic and he asks*. Everything below is already on the page; none
of it is new machinery.**

**The fare is the trip.** At ch 780 he is at the top of eight flights and eight hundred and eighty steps
with one lamp holding about a cup and a half, it was in his hand from the second hour of the morning, he
has three lamps in rotation, there is no oil in that building and there has not been for nine years, and
there is none to be had on that mountain. **So every lamp he lights in that room is a lamp he does not
light below the line, and the trip is what the oil pays for.** This is the batch's whole engine and it
costs him nothing to say and her everything to accept: he is spending his own store on her arithmetic.

**The date is a first of a month, and it is the room's own date.** A hand goes into that cage at the second
hour of the second of every month; the light comes down two of her own hand a day out of fifty-eight;
fifty-eight divided by two is twenty-nine; so the light comes down to that cut **on the first of a month**.
That machinery has run on a hand every twenty-nine days since before the building was a building. **She has
not put a hand in it since the first of the month beginning 2,534, and the lamp went out on that same
first, so the clock strikes on nothing.** Batch 0001 contains two firsts — **2,564 and 2,594** — and on both
the only light in that building is a boy's oil. **2,594 is four days after ch 792 and four days before
ch 793.** Whether he has oil left for it has one answer in oil and a different answer in days.

**What he wants is already on the page and is not restated here as a new motive.** He wants one small
thing, to know why the lamp at the top of that tower is lit, and he has been given an arithmetic and not
one sentence. **He has already watched a bearer put forty boards down (ch 741, ch 749), so no card may say
he has never seen forty go on — and the forty he counted at ch 750 came off that table on a copying floor
four miles down the mountain. Both facts are on the page and neither is this volume's discovery.** The
deadline needs a date, not a new want.

**Why the midpoint bites.** At ch 788 the two counts disagree by one day in twenty and both check out,
because oil burns by a wick and his wick is his, and a day goes by the count of it and the count is hers.
**Neither is converted into the other, and no card states a burn rate, a cups-per-day figure, or an hour the
oil runs out: none of those is on the page, and a figure with no derivation is the defect this manuscript
has been repairing since ch 764.**
**Stated with no consequence that is a curiosity; stated against a date it is the batch.** He cuts his
visits to make his own count true and the rotation shortens, and from ch 789 he is spending less time in
the room in order to be in the room later. Neither count is corrected, ever.

**Why the stair matters at ch 792.** The lamp goes **down** nine hundred and four steps and comes back up,
in her hands, four days out, because nothing has ever carried light to the bottom of that stair and the
bundle day cannot be reached with oil that has never been tested there. It is also the last time anything
is carried down that stair in this batch, and it is a rehearsal, and she does not call it one.

**Why ch 795 costs what it costs.** She trims the wick and fills the cup at the near end of that table on
day 2,600 out of **what he carried up and did not use**, and it goes down the stair with him when he goes.
So from the last page of this batch the light in that room is a light **she cannot hold except by being owed
it**, by a boy of sixteen, who is not bound, not entered, and not sent, and who may stop coming. **The dark
stops being hers alone and starts being hers and owed.** That is the price and it is a real one: it is not
paid by deferring the ch 793 cost, and it is not undone when the lamp goes out. **No card states when the
oil runs out, asserts an exhaustion date, or reconciles the cup at ch 795 against the lamp still burning at
ch 793 four days earlier — there is no figure on the page for a burn rate and none is invented here.** The
cage lamp is not relit and no store is replenished anywhere in the batch.

## Midpoint reversal (ch 810–812)

**A board comes up the stair with his name already on it in a hand that is not hers and not the room's —
and then, in the tenth frame scene, he says yes to the keeping where she cannot hear it.** The reversal
is double: she is not the only writer any more, and he is not refusing any more. Everything after it is

## Volume climax (ch 836–838)

The room offers the amendment it has never offered: enter him as keeper, feed the lamp with one bound
person, keep her rows true in the dark. Every part of it is true and it is the only kind thing the room
has ever done for the voice. **She refuses it, out loud, in the dark, standing, in the ordinary voice —
not because it is cruel but because there has not been one line in that building for three hundred days
that got into a row that she had not said out loud there first, and a keeper she cannot hear is a line
she cannot check.** She writes the refusal on a blank leaf out of the book against her ribs — the first
of the five she has ever written on — and sends it down the stair on the next bundle-day. **Immortal
Vessel 9 is taken in the same hour, against the refusal, and its cost is that she cannot be alone in the
room any more: the keeping is somebody's now, and the somebody is refused, and the refusal stays.**
