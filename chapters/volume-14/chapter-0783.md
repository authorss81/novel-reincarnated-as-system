# Stay Or Go

He came up on the sixteenth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, at the seventh hour, with a lamp in his hand, and he said it before she could get to the near end of that table.

He said, “This is not one of yours.”

She said, “It is not.”

He said, “Then I am not supposed to be here.”

She said, “No.”

And she stood there for a count of ninety and did not say anything else, because there were four things she could have said and all four of them would have been an invitation to a boy of sixteen to keep coming up eight hundred and eighty steps on his own oil, and she has been in this building two hundred and forty-eight days and she has not yet worked out whether she wants him to or not.

## Who Sent Him

He said, “Somebody sent you oil.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “Somebody sends you paper.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “Somebody sends a man up that stair once a month.”

She said, “A man comes up that stair once a month and he has been on that road a year and he does not look at anything and not one person in this building has ever asked him a question. He is not sent to me. He brings forty things to a table and goes back out. That is the whole of him and I have watched him every month since the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four.”

He said, “Then nobody above this line wants you to have light.”

She said, “There is nothing above this line. There is this room and the stair and the four miles of mountain and a boy of sixteen with three lamps, and none of that is above anything, and I have been up here long enough to know that there is no floor above this one to want anything.”

He said, “So you are asking me.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “You told me four nights out of seven.”

She said, “I told you what I was going to take. That is not asking. If I ask, you can say no, and there is not one thing in nineteen years that has ever happened where somebody said no to me, and that is not the same as a thing I have been given.”

He looked at her in the light of his own lamp and said, “That is a strange thing to say to somebody who is standing in front of you.”

She said, “I know what it is.”

## The Arithmetic Of Why He Is Here

And then she said the worst thing she has said in this building in two hundred and forty-eight days, and she said it out loud, standing up, in the ordinary voice, to a person, because she cannot let a thing stand in a room that has not been put into words where it exists, and because it was true.

She said, “You are not here because you want to be. You are here because I told you a day and you have worked out that the day is worth buying and that I am the reason any of the nights is worth buying.”

He did not say anything.

She said, “Forty-nine days from the twelfth to the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four. Four nights out of seven is about twenty-eight lamps across that. And you have three. And you know which end of that is going to hurt, and you came up tonight anyway, on a night that is not one of mine, which is not a thing a boy does because he has too much oil.”

She said, “That is all of it. That is why you are in this room. It is not welcome and it is not a kindness and it is not going to turn into one. It is arithmetic and I would rather say it than have you work it out on a stair.”

He said, “You could have let me work it out on the stair.”

She said, “I have been letting people work things out on stairs since I was nine years old and it has cost me everything I have got and I have decided to stop.”

And then she said the thing under it, which was the reason she had not slept on the night between the twelfth and the thirteenth.

She said, “And if you stop coming then I am standing in this room on the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four with no light in it, and there is not one instrument in this county that can tell me what a room does when it is worked and there is no one in it to watch it.”

She said, “And I do not want that. I want a person in this room on that day and I have no idea in the world what I am entitled to, and I am saying it out loud because I do not get to want a thing quietly in a room with a person in it.”

## Not Entered

He said, “What are you offering me.”

She said, “Nothing.”

He said, “Then why say it.”

She said, “Because you are going to go down that stair tonight and decide whether to come up again on a night that is not one of mine, and you are going to do it on an arithmetic, and the arithmetic has to be in this room in words or it will be in your head on a landing where I cannot check it.”

She said, “So here it is in words. You are not being sent. You are not being entered. There is no name of yours on anything in this room and there is not going to be, and there is not going to be a line anywhere that says where you go or what you do with an evening. If you come up that stair it is because you came up it.”

He said, “All right.”

She said, “And I am going to say that out loud again on the first of that month, in this room, standing up, so that on the day it matters there is a version of it in the air in this room that anybody standing here can hear.”

He said, “Why would you want that.”

She said, “Because I have kept a record in this building for nineteen years and there is not one line in it that I did not say out loud in this room first, and I am not going to start on the one day that matters.”

He said, “You said that about the lamp. That the arm it costs is somebody’s.”

She said, “A hand goes into that cage at the second hour of the second of every month and it costs whoever puts it there something I have never once been able to put a figure on, and it has been doing that for four hundred years, and I have watched it eight times, and it is not oil and it is not money and there is nothing anybody in this county has ever been able to put in a lamp in a cage.”

She said, “And I am not going to be the reason a person of sixteen pays that, and that is the whole of why I said nothing to you, and you can put it where you like in your own arithmetic.”

## Six Things At The Ninth Hour

He said, “What do you do in here.”

She said, “At the ninth hour I say six things out loud, standing up, in the ordinary voice, one at a time, and I have said them every day since I was nine years old.”

He said, “Every day.”

She said, “Every day, and in every room I have ever been in, and there are not many of those.”

He said, “Six things.”

She said, “Six, and there is not going to be a seventh, and nobody has ever asked me for them and I am not going to give them to you.”

He said, “All right.”

She said, “That is not all right, and you are not going to be all right about it, and I am telling you now so that it is not a surprise in about a week.”

She said, “The first one of them is one thing at a time. The second one is a thing about where I am standing, and I cannot keep the second one in this room any more, and I have not told anybody that until tonight.”

He said, “What has happened to it.”

She said, “The light went out on the first of that month and I put it out myself. I worked out on the twenty-seventh of that month what that does to the second one, standing in that light, with a person at the far end of this table, and I have not said one word of it out loud to anybody in the month since.”

He said, “So you will say it tonight.”

She said, “I will say all six tonight, and it will be the second one that costs, and I will be standing in the dark when I say it, and I have not worked out in what world that counts.”

He said, “It counts the same.”

She said, “You have been up here four nights and you have not worked out a single other thing about this room, and that is the first sensible thing anybody has said to me in two hundred and forty-eight days.”

## He Stays

And he said, “Then I am staying.”

She said, “You are staying.”

He said, “On my own count. Not on yours and not on anybody’s, because there is nobody else.”

She said, “No.”

And she stood there and did the last of it out loud at the ninth hour, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table, with her right arm open at her side and a boy of sixteen four feet off her holding the only light in the building.

She said, “Two thousand five hundred and forty-nine less two thousand three hundred and one is two hundred and forty-eight days that I have been in this room, and I have said nine hundred and eighty-two figures out loud into it, and not one of them has ever been for me. That is what I do with figures. I put them somewhere.”

She said, “And I have got one now and it is a person, and I do not know what the cost of it is, and I am going to find out in about thirty days whether he is still on that stair or not, and there is not one thing I can do in the meantime.”

He said, “Then I will be on it.”

She said, “You will be on it or you will not, and I will not ask, and I am not going to ask you tonight or on any of the other nights, and that is the deal and I have never made one before and I do not know whether it is a good one.”

He took the lamp and went to the low door, and she went down the first four hundred and forty with her left hand on the rail behind him because it is worse going down, and at the fourth landing he stopped and said one thing back up at her.

He said, “You did not have to tell me any of that.”

She said, “I know. I did it because a record is only worth something if somebody else has heard it, and I have been talking to a room for nineteen years, and I have got about a month left of being able to.”

And he went on down, and she stood on the fourth landing in the dark with her hand on a rail that stopped there, and she thought, with her hand on the iron and her breath at nine, that she had just found out she wanted him on that day more than she had wanted anything since a look at her own face, and that she had not the least idea in the world what it was going to cost him.
