# The Settling

Forty-four days since the ninth stage. Four hundred and seventy-five days in that room. The cage out two hundred and forty-two days. Right arm open four hundred and eighty-two days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and seventeen days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in the room at the seventh hour. She took an empty lamp in her left hand and went down the tower's own stair with her right arm open in front of her, and the light of the morning came in at four openings and stood on the wall opposite each one for about a count of forty as she passed it.

## Down With An Empty Lamp

She said out loud at the first opening, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“There is oil at the bottom of this stair and it is not mine and it came up here in a bag on the back of a boy who is not bound and not entered and not sent, and there is no line anywhere in this building that says where he goes in the evening.”

She said, “And I have taken his oil once, on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, and I said it out loud in front of him and he emptied a lamp into mine and went down eight hundred and eighty steps with an empty one in his hand.”

She said, “And I am going and getting it myself this morning and I am saying that out loud at the seventh hour on the third of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four so that there is no version of this in the future where anybody could say I did not say it.”

She went down.

She did not count it, and she said out loud at the foot of the first run that she was not going to, and that she had counted that stair in daylight thirty days ago and had got eight hundred and eighty and had put the figure in the record, and that a figure only has to be got once.

## Not Counting

She said it out loud on the first step of the second run, standing, in the ordinary voice, with the rail not there and her arm open in front of her:

“I am not counting this. That is the sentence and it is the whole of what I am doing differently and it took me about a count of a hundred and forty to work out why it is worth saying.”

She said, “Because a number is a thing you can hold, and a stair is a thing you cannot, and I have been holding numbers instead of stairs for nineteen years and there is not one of them that has ever come up those steps with me.”

She said, “And this morning I am going to go down eight hundred and eighty steps and come back up eight hundred and eighty steps and I am not going to have a figure at the end of it, and that is the first thing I have done in this building that is not arithmetic, and it is not a small thing and I am not going to make it one.”

She went down the second run on her feet with her left hand on nothing, and her knee went on the loose flag at the turn, and she said that out loud as well.

## A Place On The Fourth Landing

And then she filled the lamp at the bottom of that stair, and she came back up the first run of four hundred and forty with her left hand on the rail and the lamp full in her right hand with three fingers and a thumb on it and her forearm open at the elbow.

It took her about a count of a hundred and forty, and she said out loud that it was four times what it used to take, and that it was not getting easier and was not meant to.

And then she got to the fourth landing and she stopped, because she put her left hand flat on the stone floor of it to get her weight onto her left leg, and the stone was not cold.

She said, “Hold there.”

She said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, on that landing, with the lamp in her right hand and the light of the morning coming in at the opening beside her.

She said, “Hold there, and I am going to say what I have found before I stand up, because I am not going to stand up and then decide what it was.”

She stayed on her knees for about a count of ninety.

She said, “This landing is stone. I have been down this stair six times in nineteen years and I have put my hand on this landing floor every one of those times and it has been cold every one of those times, the way stone is cold in the morning.”

She said, “And it is not cold now. There is a place on it about the width of a step and the length of two of my hands, at the foot of the rail, and it goes across the landing and it stops. Six paces on it is the same as the rest of it. Under the opening it is the same as the rest of it.”

She got her hand up off it and sat down on the step above it, on the stone, with the lamp on the floor beside her, and she did not fill the count.

She said, “And I did not do it. I am going to say that first because I have said out loud more than once in this room that I am the only thing in this building that can pay for anything, and that came off the ground this room stands on and not out of anything in my hands.”

She said, “And I did not do it. I came up this stair this morning with an empty lamp. I am sitting on a landing with a full one. That is the whole of what I have touched.”

## What It Is Not

And then she said the rest of it out loud, sitting on that step in daylight with the lamp standing on the stone beside her, and it took her about a count of a hundred and forty and she said it in the order she uses.

She said, “I am not going to work out what paid for it. I have no instrument. I have been down here six times in nineteen years and I did not notice this the other five and I am not going to sit here and go back over the other five.”

She said, “And I am not going to say it is a person. It could be a person and it could be the stone and there is not one thing in this building that would tell me which, and I have said out loud that I cannot tell and I have said out loud that not knowing is a position and not a failure.”

She said, “And I am not going to say it is a reward. That is the one I want to be certain about, because on the second of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four I lay on the floor of that room in the dark and said out loud that something in this building had been paid for and I did not feel it go.”

She said, “That is the same thing. That is this landing, from the other end. And the difference is not that I know more now. The difference is that this time I put my hand on it while it was happening.”

She stood up, and picked the lamp up in her left hand, and went up the rest of that stair, the second run, with her hand on nothing, and she did not count it.

## What Merit Is And Is Not For

And then she was in that room again, and the light went a hand’s width along the near end of that table and stopped, and she put the lamp on the wood a foot in from the edge with the wick trimmed, and she said the last of that morning out loud, standing four feet off the near end of that table, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour.

She said, “Here is what a thing like that is not for, and I worked it out on a landing in daylight and I am putting it down in here while I still have the stone under my hand in my memory.”

She said, “It is not for anything. A person does not get it and spend it. It does not go into a column. It is not an amount and there is not a price at the head of it and it would not go under one.”

She said, “And it is not for the page. I want to say that plainly.”

She said, “I want it said on the third of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, two days after a piece of paper went down nine hundred and four steps in a cloth with forty boards on the top of them.”

She said, “If something was paid for down there on that morning, then it was paid for by somebody and not by me and not by the paper, and I am not going to stand here and hold a warm landing up against a refusal and call that a settling up.”

She said, “And there are no words for it. Words are a thing you say to a person and there is not one person in it that I can get at.”

She said, “And you cannot keep it. It is on the floor of a landing in a tower and it is not in my hand and it is not against my fourth rib and it is not on any wood in this room.”

She said, “So the whole of what a thing like that is for is this: it happens to somebody on the ground, and it goes off, and it does not come to you and it does not go into anything of yours, and the only part of it that is ever given back is that once in nineteen years a person puts her hand flat on a stone floor to get up off her left leg and finds out that it was warm.”

And then she said the figures out loud one more time in the ordinary voice, standing four feet off the near end of that table, and the light went a hand’s width along the wood and stopped.

There were four pieces of wood lying face up side by side about a foot in from the edge of it. There was a bar against the wall on the left that had been there four hundred and thirty-two days. There was a bundle under the far end with two turns of cord in it and not a knot.

And there was a room she could not be alone in, and nothing in that room said one word.
