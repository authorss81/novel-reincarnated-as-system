# Sixty-Six

Sixty-six days since the eighth stage, and it was the day and not the morning. Four hundred and thirty-one days in that room. The cage out a hundred and ninety-eight days. Right arm open four hundred and thirty-eight days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and seventy-three days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was a boy of sixteen in the room. The lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table with a trimmed wick and the light going a hand’s width along it and stopping, and it was his oil, and there was a strip of pale wood lying face up on the wood in front of her with four figures of hers in it and eight pairs of cuts under those with nothing in any of them and one pair below the eight.

## The Whole Of The Day Before It

She got up at the seventh hour and did not do anything until the ninth, and she said that out loud standing four feet off the near end of that table with the nib in her right hand at about the ninth hour, because she does not let a thing stand that has not been put into words where it exists.

She said, “I got up on the seventh and I did not do one single thing until the ninth and that is the longest I have gone in four hundred and thirty-one days without putting one of my own numbers against another one, and it was not rest.”

She said, “At about the eighth hour I worked out the interval. And at about the eighth hour and forty minutes I went and got the wood out and put my thumb on the pair of cuts under the eight and took it off again and put it back where it was, and then I did it a second time, and that is the whole of what I did with my hands between the eighth hour and the ninth.”

He said, “You did that yesterday.”

She said, “I did that yesterday and the day before and I have said out loud that I am not going to say anything about that pair, and I am not going to.”

## Said Before The Wood

And then she said the whole of it out loud before the nib touched the wood, standing, in the ordinary voice, because a figure gets said before it is a line anywhere.

She said, “Nine stages into the seventh realm, taken at the ninth hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen, at the near end of a table twenty-two paces long, in oil light, on a strip of pale wood a foot long and two inches across.”

She said, “It came up a stair in an armful on the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and it is in my own hand, with a nib that has not touched a column in this building in nine years.”

She said, “And that is the whole of it and there is not one word of it that is a row.”

She stopped.

She said, “And I am going to say what it is taken against, and I am saying it before the nib goes on the wood and not after, because if I say it after then it is something I did and not something I aimed.”

She said, “It is taken against a no I said out loud in this room at about the ninth hour and ten minutes on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen, standing, in the ordinary voice, into twenty-two paces of dark, that cannot be unsaid.”

She said, “And it is taken against a page I have not written. There is no paper in this room with a sentence on it and there is going to be one, and that is the whole of what this is taken against, and I am saying it at the ninth hour so that it is on the record that I took it against something that does not exist yet.”

## The Figure At The Head Of It

She said, “Two thousand seven hundred and thirty-two less two thousand six hundred and sixty-six is sixty-six days.”

She said, “It is not seventy-four. Seventy-four was a stage taken against nothing but a lamp that is not the room’s, and this one is taken against a refusal that a room has not answered. A refusal is not got in a hurry, and there has not been one in this room in four hundred years, and a room that has not answered has not finished.”

And then she said the head figure out loud, and she said it because she says that one before every stage, and it took about a count of forty.

She said, “There is a price at the head of that column and the same price at the head of that strip of pale wood. Ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes. A price does not date.”

She said, “And at this morning the figure at the head of it is out of date by twenty-one days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes, which I have checked twice this morning with my own hand, and that is the only figure in this building that is always wrong in the same direction and I am the only one who can work it out.”

## The Fifth Pair Of Cuts

She put the nib into the fifth pair of cuts, which is the first one below the pair with the eight in it, and that pair has been empty since the wood was cut.

She said, out loud, with the nib on the wood:

“An empty pair of cuts is for the night you need somewhere to put a figure that you have to write with your own hand on a piece of wood that is yours, in a room where there is no lamp in a cage and no look coming and nothing but oil. I said that once in this room and I am not going to say it again, and this is the fifth time I have done it.”

She said, “Nine.”

And she wrote it.

It is one stroke and a turn and it is a count and it is not a price, and it took a count of nine.

Her right forearm went down while the nib was still on the wood by the width of the little finger on her left hand, which is the cost of writing and has been the cost of writing for years, and it is the fifth time in her life that a figure has come off that hand.

She took the nib off and put it back against her fourth rib.

And then she said it out loud again, standing, in the ordinary voice, with the price on the wood and not coming off it.

She said, “Nine. And there are nine pairs under it now with nothing in any of them and nine pairs that had nothing in them yesterday.”

## The Part Of It That Was Easier

And then she stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty with the strip of pale wood face up in front of her and the lamp burning a hand’s width along it, and she did not expect what came next and she said so out loud before she said anything else about it.

She said, “That was easier than the last two and I am not going to dress that up.”

She said, “The seventh was the worst of them, and I have not said that out loud before.”

She said, “It took me about nine days to work out afterwards that the worst of them was the one I took against a look, and the eighth was the one I took against two figures with one of them moving.”

She said, “And this one was the shortest of the five, and it was the only one where I knew beforehand what the column was going to have to do with me.”

She said, “And it was easier because there is a person four feet off me with a lamp in his hand.”

She put her hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “I am saying that plainly because it is the part that matters and I would rather say it now than have it found later. I have taken four stages in this room alone and the fifth one is the first that went quicker with somebody in it, and I have noticed that, and I am not going to stand here pretending that is a coincidence.”

## What It Cost, Said Once

And then she said the cost, and she said it once, and she said it standing, in the ordinary voice, with the figure already on the wood and not coming off it.

She said, “It has cost me the right to be alone in this room.”

She said, “I cannot be alone in this room any more.”

She did not move while she was saying it.

She said, “And here is the plain version and I am saying it once.”

She said, “A place at the far side of that table has been kept for four hundred years and keeping it is being there when the figures are said out loud. That has not been anybody’s work this morning until this second. From tonight it is somebody’s. Not mine. I have refused it and a refusal I said in the dark does not put it back where it was.”

She said, “And I have said no to being kept, and I have said no to a lamp, and I have said no to a board coming up that stair with a place on it, and none of those three was about this. Every one of them was about what happens to a record and who is standing in a room when it is made. This is about the room itself.”

She said, “So from tonight, when I stand here at the seventh hour and the room is empty, I will not be standing in a room. I will be standing in a room that is waiting for somebody, and the somebody is not going to be chosen tonight and it is not going to be chosen by me, because I have not asked anybody for anything since I was nine years old and I am not starting on the night of a stage.”

He said, “You are shaking.”

She said, “I know. It is the writing. It has been the writing every time.”

## Who With

And then she said the last of the night out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour and twenty minutes, and it came out flat and she did not put anything on it.

She said, “So: a room I cannot be alone in, and no instrument in this county that can tell me who with.”

She said, “Not one. There is nothing in this building that asks a question and gets an answer about a person. There is a column that reads and a hand that copies and a fair hand that puts a day on my arithmetic and is right, and none of those four things has ever once wanted to know whether a person is in a room.”

She squared the strip of pale wood up with two fingers of her left hand, and the lamp went on burning a hand’s width along the near end of that table, and the boy took it and went down four hundred and forty and then four hundred and forty more with oil on his hands.

And nine was in the wood in her own hand, and there was a room at the top of eight hundred and eighty steps that she could not be alone in from that night on, and there was not one thing in this county that could tell her who with.
