# The Place Described As Kept

Forty-eight days since the eighth stage. Four hundred and thirteen days in that room. The cage out a hundred and eighty days. Right arm open four hundred and twenty days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and fifty-five days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

A bundle came up the second stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen at the second hour, and it was the fifteenth of them, and she was standing four feet off the near end of that table in the dark.

## Nine Hundred And Four Coming Up

Nine hundred and four steps coming up, and the step at the turn did not change.

Forty boards going on that table in ten minutes, four to a minute, and then thirty for the cloth, and then nine hundred and four steps going back down in the dark, and not one word out of any of it.

And then the room was quiet for about a count of a hundred and forty.

And then there was one more stroke.

She stood four feet off the near end of that table and did not move, and she said it out loud at about the second hour and ten minutes, standing, in the ordinary voice, into twenty-two paces of dark.

She said, “One. On one board. And it came after the man was off that stair and it was the same stroke and it started in the same place and it lasted about a count of nine.”

She said, “That is the second time in thirty days. I said out loud on the first of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four that whatever it was on, it was the last thing to be touched in this room at the second hour of a month for four hundred years. I am not taking that back and I am not going to get a second one out of it.”

## The Lamp Twenty-Two Paces, The Fourth Time

And then she went for the lamp, which was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end with a trimmed wick, and she carried it twenty-two paces along the near side of the table on her feet with her left hand on the leg of it, over the flag that moves at the nineteenth pace, and put it down about four feet short of the far end.

She said it out loud before she picked it up.

She said, “That is four times I have carried that lamp twenty-two paces. The ninth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four, the tenth of that month, the ninth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four, and this morning.”

She said, “And on the first two of those I had to carry my own record in my left hand with the lamp in that same hand, and on the other two I could leave it lying here, and I have only got one hand to spare either way.”

She said, “And tonight is the fourth time and the near end of this table has two pieces of wood on it a foot in from the edge with nothing of mine to carry away, because I put them there on the second of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four and left them, and I have not moved either of them since.”

The light came down flat onto the wood.

## What Was On It

She found the board by the writing, the way she found the last one, by running her thumb along the edge of it until the edge was not straight.

And then she said the whole of it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, because a thing gets said before it is a line anywhere and because it is the only thing in that room she cannot see from the near end.

She said, “The hand is fair. It is even and it is a little smaller than mine and there is not one place on it where the nib had to be lifted and set down again. It is not the hand at the far end of that table.”

She stopped for about a count of ninety.

She said, “I have seen that hand nineteen times before, on nineteen boards out of twenty in a stack under the middle of this table, and every time it was underneath my own arithmetic. I have never seen it at the end of a stroke on a board that a man carried up nine hundred and four steps in the dark, and I cannot tell you that it is the same hand and I am not going to guess in this room.”

She said, “And there is one pair of cuts under the writing and there is nothing in them, and they are worn round at the corners, and they are the same width as the pair of cuts under the name on the other board and the same width as the pairs on my own wood.”

## What It Says

And then she read it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, from about four feet off with the light coming up onto the wood, and it is what it says and she read every line of it twice because she said a thing before it was a line anywhere and this one had not been a line anywhere before this morning.

*The far side of this table is twenty-two paces long.*

*From the fifteenth pace to the seventeenth pace there is a place.*

*It is not a seat. Nobody has sat in it.*

*It is of the length of a forearm and of the breadth of a hand.*

*It is kept.*

*It has been kept for four hundred years.*

*It is not given to anybody.*

*It is kept by being there when the figures are said out loud.*

## A Place And Not A Thing To Be Done

And then she stood there and made herself say what it was, out loud, in the ordinary voice, at about the fourth hour, because she has said things in this room at length that turned out to be something else and she was not doing it twice.

She said, “That is a place.”

She said, “It is not a job. There is nothing in it that anybody could be told to do, and there is no second half of it anywhere on that board, and it does not say that anybody is to sit in it, or stand in it, or write in it, or carry anything to it, or be paid for it, or be given it.”

She said, “It describes where a place is. How long it is. How wide it is. That it is not a seat. That it has been kept for four hundred years. That it is not given to anybody. And what keeping it is.”

The light went a hand’s width along the wood and stopped.

She said, “That is all of it, and it took me about a count of a hundred and forty to be certain of that, and I have been certain of very little in four hundred and thirteen days.”

## The Cold

And then she did the thing she does when a thing in that room is bigger than the room, which is to name what she was feeling first, out loud, standing, before she said one word else about it.

She said, “I am going to say what this is before I say anything else about it, the way I said what it was on the first of this month, because it is going to look like fear to anybody who has not watched me and I am not going to have that in this room.”

She said, “It is not fear. Fear is about the next one. This is about one that is here and is written down and has been written down by a hand that puts a day on my arithmetic and is right.”

She stood there about a count of a hundred and forty with the light flat on the wood and the far end of that table twenty-two paces behind her in the dark.

She said, “It is the cold. It came up out of the stone when I put the lamp down and it has not gone off my ankles and it is not the cold of this room, which is the cold of a room with a dead lamp in a cage in it. It is the other thing and it is new and I have not had it before.”

She said, “And I am not frightened of it either. I want that said too, and it is not the same board as the cold, and I have got one day for getting that wrong.”

## What She Answers

And then she carried the lamp back twenty-two paces on the near side of that table and put it down on the wood a foot in from the near end where it had been, and the light went a hand’s width along it and stopped.

And she put the board down face up on the near end of that table beside the strip of pale wood and beside the other board, three pieces of wood lying side by side in a hand’s width of light, and she squared her own strip and the board with his name on it, which are the two she squares every morning, and left the third one where it fell.

And then she read the whole of it out loud again from four feet off the near end, in the ordinary voice and not in any other, and she let it go out across twenty-two paces of table.

And that is the whole of what she did between the fourth hour and the ninth hour.

She carried the lamp down twenty-two paces again once, at about the sixth hour, because those five hours were a woman deciding whether to say a thing in a room or only to a room. She put the lamp at the far end of that table and said it to that end of it as well, and then she carried the lamp back and said nothing else.

## Knowing Where It Went

And then she said the last of that morning out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, and she did it in the order she has used for nineteen years, which is figures first and then the reason, and she has never once done it the other way round.

She said, “The cage is out a hundred and eighty days and my right arm has been open four hundred and twenty of them and it is not going to close.”

She said, “And on the first of this month I said a sentence at about the seventh hour that went twenty-two paces and I did not know I had sent it. And I found out where it had gone at about the eighth hour and it went to the far end of this table, and I said out loud that I had told it, in its own room, what I know about what it is doing.”

She put her hand flat on the top of the table.

She said, “And this morning I read a board out loud in the ordinary voice from this end of this table, in front of a boy of sixteen and a lamp he carried up eight hundred and eighty steps, and I knew where it went while I was saying it. I chose the voice. I chose it for the length of that table.”

She said, “And nothing came back. Nothing has ever come back from that end of this table and it did not come back this morning and I did not expect it to and I am not going to stand here wanting it.”

She said, “That is two things I have said across twenty-two paces in this room. The first one I said by accident and found out afterwards. This one I said on purpose and knew about at the time.”

She did not move while she was saying it.

She said, “And I answered it with nothing. That is not the same as saying no. There is a difference between those two things and I am not going to muddle it at the fourth hour, and something is going to muddle it in this room, and when it does I am going to have to say which of the two I meant.”
