# No

Fifty-two days since the eighth stage. Four hundred and seventeen days in that room. The cage out a hundred and eighty-four days. Right arm open four hundred and twenty-four days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and fifty-nine days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in the room. The lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table and the light was going a hand’s width along it and stopping.

She had said her figures out loud at the seventh hour as she says them every morning, standing, in the ordinary voice, and then she had nothing to do for an hour and forty minutes, and then she did this.

## What The Keeping Is

She had gone over it once in her head before she opened her mouth and she wrote nothing down and she took a board out of the stack under the middle of the table and read it in the light and put it back.

Here is the whole of it.

There is a column with a price at the head of it. The price is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes. The column is a column of amounts and not a column of what happened. That was worked out at the near end of that table on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and it has not been taken back.

There is a strip of pale wood lying face up about a foot in from the edge of that table with the price at the head of it and the same price in the first pair of cuts and a five in the second, a seven in the third, an eight in the fourth, and eight pairs of cuts under that with nothing in them.

It came up the second stair in an armful on the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four. There is no second copy of it anywhere on this mountain.

There is a woman of nineteen who stands four feet off the near end of that table every morning and says every figure she has got out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice. She has done it since she was nine years old and she has never once not done it.

There is a fair hand that puts a day on her arithmetic about one morning in twenty and is right on every one of the fourteen mornings she can put her own mouth against.

There is a light. It is not the room’s light and it does not matter that it is not the room’s light. It comes up eight hundred and eighty steps in a bag on the back of a boy who has never been asked for it and has never been sent and is not bound and not entered.

There is a third hand that wrote a name on a board on the first of this month and a board that describes a place at the far side of the table with the words *it is kept* in it.

Every part of that works.

## What She Cannot Say

There are three reasons a person says no to a thing that works, and she had all three, and she said two of them out loud standing four feet off the near end of that table at about the ninth hour, and the third one she said at the fourth hour and did not get to the end of.

The first is that she cannot be given anything. That is nineteen years old and it is paid and it is not going to be argued about.

The second is that she cannot check it. That is the one she said at length.

A row of figures gets said out loud in a dark room by a woman standing four feet off a table, and a fair hand copies it, and the copy is a day on.

And the only thing in the building that could say whether the copy was right is at the far end of that table, in the dark, where the lamp does not reach. She has carried that lamp twenty-two paces four times in nine days and two of those four times cost her both hands.

The third one is that she is alone in that room at night and has been for four hundred and seventeen days and has never once been able to say that that is why.

She said the third one out loud at the fourth hour and she got nine words into it.

She said, “I am not saying this one. I have not said this one in nineteen years and I am not starting on a morning where it would be the reason.”

## The Calm

She checked it before she did anything else, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour and five minutes, because it was going to look like something else and she was not going to have that in this room, and because on the first of this month she had to do the same thing at about the ninth hour and got the answer wrong the first time.

It was not fear. It was not anger. It was not the cold that came up out of the stone when she put a lamp down at the far end of the table on the first of this month.

It was calm.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“I am going to say this next thing and I am not going to dress it and I am not going to take it back after it.”

She said, “I am calm. And that is the coldest thing I have ever been in this room and I have been in this room four hundred and seventeen days and I have been a great many things in it, and I have never once been this.”

She said, “I want it said in this room while I am standing here that I am not frightened and I am not brave. I have worked out what a thing that works looks like from the outside and it looks exactly like this, and I do not trust it, and I am going to use it anyway because the alternative is doing nothing and I have not done nothing in nineteen years.”

## The Word

And then she said it.

She put her left hand flat on the top of the near end of that table, four feet in from the edge, on the wood beside a strip of pale wood and beside two boards that were a foot long each, and she did not move anything, and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, into twenty-two paces of dark, at about the ninth hour and ten minutes on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen.

“No.”

## The Two Things She Is Not Doing

And then, because a word that size can go out into a room and be made into something it is not, she said what it was and what it was not, and she said it standing, in the ordinary voice, and she said the second half slower than the first.

She said, “That is a no to a person keeping what I say out loud in this room.”

She said, “That is what it is and it is all of it and there is one person in this building it could have been said to and it was not said to him because he was not in this room and I am not going to say it to him instead, and it is not said to him.”

She said, “And I am not moving this.”

She put two fingers of her left hand on the near end of the strip of pale wood and left it exactly where it was.

She said, “There is a strip of pale wood lying face up on this table with a price at the head of it and four figures of mine in it, and it came up that stair in an armful on the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four.”

She said, “And I am not moving it off the top of this table, and I am not moving it into a column, and I am not leaving it lying in the dark at the far end while I carry a lamp in two hands.”

She said, “I worked out on the eighteenth of this month that keeping would mean it stopped travelling, and that it would have to stop travelling, and that there is nowhere in this building to move it to. That is still true and I am not doing it.”

She did not move while she was saying it.

She said, “And I am not vacating the record. I am not stopping saying figures out loud at this table at the seventh hour in the ordinary voice. I said at this table on the second of this month that I am going to keep standing four feet off the near end of it and I said it at the ninth hour yesterday where twenty-two paces of that table could hear it, and I meant it then and I mean it now.”

She said, “So the no is a no to the keeping. It is not a no to the record.”

She said, “It is not a no to the place at the far side of this table, which is not mine and is not anybody’s and was not given to anybody when it was described to me on the first of this month.”

She did not fill the count with anything.

She said, “It is not a no to a boy of sixteen who is not bound and not entered and not sent. And it is not a no to a hand that puts a day on my arithmetic and is right, and I have not asked for that hand and I am not going to ask for it and I am not going to tell it to stop.”

## That It Cannot Be Unsaid

And then she said the last of it, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour and twenty minutes, and it is the sentence she has been walking towards since the fifth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four when she first said a name out loud in front of a person and could not put it back.

She said, “And I am going to say the last of it now while I am standing here, because I am not saying it out loud twice and I am not writing it on anything tonight.”

She said, “A no said in this room cannot be unsaid.”

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty with her hand on the wood and the light going a hand’s width along the near end of that table and twenty-two paces of dark behind the light.

She said, “Not because a thing hears it. I have said that three times now and I do not know that it does and it does not matter. Because there is a hand at the far end of this table that has been writing down what goes out across this table for four hundred years, and it took one stroke that lasted a count of nine on a board with a name on it, and it did not stop on it.”

She said, “So that is in the room now. It is the third thing that end of this table has had from me and it is the first one that is a no, and it is out loud and standing and in the ordinary voice, and there is nothing in this building I can do to put it back.”

She took her hand off the wood.

She said, “And I am not going to take it back tomorrow. I am going to stand four feet off the near end of this table at the seventh hour on the sixth of this month and say every figure I have got out loud in the ordinary voice, exactly as I have said them every morning for nineteen years, and that is what a person does the morning after she has said a thing that cannot be unsaid.”
