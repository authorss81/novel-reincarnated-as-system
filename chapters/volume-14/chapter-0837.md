# The Waiting

Twenty-eight days since the ninth stage. Four hundred and fifty-nine days in that room. The cage out two hundred and twenty-six days. Right arm open four hundred and sixty-six days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and one days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in the room. The lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table and the light went a hand’s width along it and stopped.

She said her figures out loud at the seventh hour, standing, in the ordinary voice, and then she said out loud, at about the seventh hour and ten minutes, standing, in the ordinary voice, that she was going to wait, and that she was saying it first.

## Forty Days Of Not Waiting

She said, “I am going to wait.”

She said, “And I want that said at about the seventh hour and ten minutes on the seventeenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four, standing, in the ordinary voice, before anything else, because on the sixth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen I said out loud at the seventh hour that I was not going to wait, and I said the reason, and the reason was good, and the reason has turned out to be the thing itself.”

She said, “This is the one time. That is what I am saying. Not once more in forty days and then again in another forty. Once.”

She put her hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “And here is how I have got here without noticing, and it took me about a count of a hundred and forty to put it in the right order.”

She said, “Forty-five days ago I stood four feet off the near end of that table at about the ninth hour and twenty and I let a count of a hundred and forty go past to see whether anything came back from the far end of that table. And nothing came back, and I said out loud that I had been measuring how long it does not take and calling it waiting for nineteen years.”

She said, “And on the sixth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen I stopped. I said I was not going to wait tomorrow either, because I was frightened of a morning when waiting had become the shape of my standing here.”

She said, “And I have been standing here every morning since and at about the ninth hour I have stopped and stood there for about a count of ninety doing nothing, and that is the same as what I said I was not going to do, and I have done it four days running, and I called it finding out whether I wanted to say something to a person.”

She said, “That was a name for it. It is the same thing. I have been waiting for forty days without waiting and that is the cleverest thing I have ever done in this room and I did it in order not to notice.”

## What I Am Actually Frightened Of

And then she said the fear out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the eighth hour, and she said it before she said one word else about it, because she has said since the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen that a thing bigger than the room has to be named first.

She said, “I am not frightened that it will refuse it. A refusal I can stand behind. I have said the shape of one out loud and I have got a page for it.”

She said, “I am frightened of nothing. That is the whole of it and it is specific and I am going to be exact.”

She stopped for about a count of ninety.

She said, “On the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, at about the second hour, a man is going to come up nine hundred and four steps with forty boards under one arm, and he is going to put them on that table.”

She said, “And there is going to be one more stroke at the end of it, the way there has been at the end of the last three, and it is not going to be on a board with anything on it. And then he is going to go back down nine hundred and four steps in the dark.”

She said, “And that is going to be the whole of the answer. A page that goes out of a room and a room that does not answer it. And then I am going to be standing four feet off the near end of that table at about the ninth hour on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, and I will not be able to tell whether a refusal was refused, or held, or never arrived.”

She said, “And that is the dread. Not the nothing. The not knowing what the nothing is. There is not one instrument in this building that turns a silence into a meaning, and I have known that since I was nine years old, and I have never before had something of mine where the difference between a silence and an answer was the only thing in it.”

She put two fingers of her left hand on the near end of the strip of pale wood.

She said, “And I have already done the morning after, in my head, about four times, and I am not going to pretend I have not, because I am going to be standing here on the second of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four at the seventh hour whatever has come up that stair or not, and I would rather have looked at it than be doing it for the first time.”

She said, “The morning after, if nothing comes back up this stair: I get up at the fifth hour. I say my figures out loud at the seventh, standing, in the ordinary voice, four feet off the near end of this table. I square my own strip of pale wood up and I square the board with the name on it and I leave the other two where they lie.”

She said, “And that is the whole of that morning and it does not require anything of me and it is exactly the same morning I have had four hundred and fifty-nine times.”

She said, “And that is what I am frightened of. Not an answer that says no. A morning in the second month after this one with nothing on that table but forty boards and a strip of wood and a bar on a wall, and me saying figures into it as I have done every morning since I was nine years old, and no way on this mountain of telling the difference between that and every other morning.”

She said, “And underneath that there is a second thing and it is worse and it is the reason I am saying all of this out loud on the seventeenth and not saving it.”

She said, “If nothing comes back up that stair, then the one page I have ever sent went into a building that has never once answered anybody. And I will still be standing here at the seventh hour the morning after, saying figures out loud in the ordinary voice, at a table twenty-two paces long, in a room with a dead lamp in a cage in it. And there is not one thing in that that would tell me I was wrong, and that is the part I cannot hold.”

## The One Time

And then she said the arrangement out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, and she said it because she has said it before in this room and a thing gets said before it is a line anywhere.

She said, “I am going to be standing four feet off the near end of that table at about the first hour on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four with a folded piece of paper in my left hand.”

She said, “At the second hour a man will come up that stair and I am not going to ask him to carry anything and not one person is going to be asked to carry anything, and the paper goes into the cloth with the forty boards on the top of them, and he will not know it is there and he has never known what is on any of them.”

She said, “And I am not going to be braced for it. I said out loud at about the ninth hour on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen that I was going to read out whatever stroke lands on whatever it lands on, and I meant it then and I have not gone back on it.”

She said, “And I am not going to say anything about what I think it is going to be before it happens. I have got no idea. That is the true position and I am putting it in this room.”

## A Quiet With Something In It

And then she stopped talking, and she did the rest of it standing, and the room was quiet, and about a count of ninety after she had stopped talking she heard something in it that she had not heard in four hundred and fifty-nine days.

She said it out loud at about the ninth hour and twenty minutes, standing, in the ordinary voice, without moving:

“There is a sound in that lamp.”

She said, “It has been making it since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, when the lamp on that table was filled out of what a boy carried up and did not use, and it makes it about four times in a count of a hundred and forty, and I have never once heard it.”

She said, “And I have heard it tonight because I have stood here for about a count of a hundred and forty doing nothing, which is what a room is for.”

She did not move while she was saying it.

She said, “And that is not an answer and it is not a message and it is not one thing out of this room that has come out to tell me anything. It is oil. And it is the first time in four hundred and fifty-nine days that this room has been quiet in a way you could hear something in, and it has been quiet in that way for forty days and I have been standing in it calling it something else.”

She left the lamp where it was. The light went a hand’s width along the near end of that table and stopped.

And she stood there and waited, having said out loud at about the seventh hour and ten minutes that it was the one time.

There were fourteen mornings in front of her, and at the end of them a man was going to come up nine hundred and four steps with forty boards under one arm, and either there was going to be one more stroke at the end of that or there was not.

She had not got a third thing to wait for and she was not going to invent one.
