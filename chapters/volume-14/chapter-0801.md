# The Head Figure

The first of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and twenty-four came and went the way the firsts have gone for four hundred years, which is that she was in this room and it came up nine hundred and four steps in the dark at the second hour and forty boards went on that table and a cloth went over them and a man went back down without looking at one of them.

She did not restage it. She said it out loud on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and twenty-four at about the seventh hour, standing at the near end of that table with a lamp standing a foot in from the edge and the light going a hand's width along the wood and no further, and she said it in about a count of forty and did not give it any more of her morning than that.

## Eleven

And on the second of that month she went in under that table and came out again, because she had said out loud on the thirtieth of the last one that she was going to, and she has never once in nineteen years said a thing out loud in this room and then not done it.

She said the number out loud from under it, on her knees, with her right arm out in front of her.

She said, “Eleven.”

He said, “It was ten on the thirtieth.”

She said, “It was ten on the thirtieth and it is eleven now and that is because I told you the number, and you are the reason it is a number that can go up while somebody is standing in the room.”

She said, “I have been putting my hand on those forty boards on the second of a month since I got into this room and I have never once had to say the number out loud to do it, and I have always known what it was, and knowing it quietly is not the same as saying it to a person, and I did not know that until four days ago.”

She said, “Thirty-eight and two. Unchanged. And the one with a paragraph of words in it is still in there on the top of the stack where I put it myself, and I have gone over all forty twice and I still cannot tell you which one it is, and I have stopped expecting to.”

## What She Did About The Fourth Board

And then she did the thing she had done on the second of every month for ten months and had never done once, which was to take the fourth board out of that stack and look at the order it came out in.

She put it back.

She said, out loud, from under that table, in the ordinary voice:

“Tenth was the fourth one. Eleventh was the first one on the other side.”

She said, “I have a hand for a board the way I have a hand for a step and I did not know that until I said it out loud, and I have never once checked it, and that is the second thing I have got that is a habit pretending to be a count.”

## Whether It Has Moved

And then she got herself out from under that table and stood at the near end of it and she picked the strip of pale wood up off the wood of the table and held it face up at eye height with the lamp standing on the table a foot in from the edge and the light going a hand's width along the wood and going up onto the underside of her own hand.

She said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, while she did it:

“Ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes.”

She said, “And it is not moved. Not by a stroke. Not by the width of a hair. I put that strip on this table face up on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four with seven in the third pair and it has not shifted since, and I have squared it up with two fingers of my left hand about nine times, and I would have known.”

She said, “So whatever is out of date, that is not.”

She said, “And I have been assuming since the sixth of that month that it was out of date, and I want to say where I got that.

I got it because there is a figure in it that is a number of something, and a number of something that stands still while I stand here and wait for it to change is a figure that is wrong. That has been my reasoning for nineteen years and this is the first time it has been in the room with me while I say it.”

She put the strip down on the table again and squared it.

And then she said, out loud, in the ordinary voice:

“I am going to find out what the top of that wood is for.”

He said, “You have looked at it every day for three hundred and thirty days.”

She said, “I have looked at it every day for three hundred and thirty days and I have never once asked what it is for, because you do not ask a figure what it is for. You use it. That is what I have been doing with it.”

She said, “And I am going to tell you the thing I cannot do about it first, because I want it said before I find out anything.”

She said, “I cannot write over it. On the sixth of that month I stood where you are standing and I told you that if I put my own figure at the head of that wood then it becomes a figure I chose on the sixth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and not one the hand at the far end of this table wrote, and I would have taken the only copy of a price out of this building that I can still read.”

She said, “And I have not worked out yet whether it is out of date. That is what I have got wrong and that is what I am going to try to get right today, and if it turns out that it is out of date then I am not going to be able to do anything about it.”

## What A Head Figure Is For

She worked it out in about two hundred counts and she worked it out standing, in the ordinary voice, with her hand flat on the wood, and she worked it out by looking at the arrangement of her own strip and not at any person.

She said, “There is a figure at the head of this wood. Under it, in the first pair of cuts, is the same figure. And under that there are pairs of cuts going down in a row, one under another, and every one of them is the same size.”

She said, “That is not a list of totals. If it were a list of totals the head would be a sum of the pairs under it, and it is not a sum of anything. It is the same figure twice, and then a column underneath it.”

She had her hand flat on the wood and she moved it half an inch along it without lifting it, which is the only way she has of thinking.

She said, “So the head is not counting anything.”

He said, “What is it doing.”

She said, “It is standing still while the column goes on underneath it.”

She said, “And a figure at the top of a column that everything under it is read against is a price. Not a count of anything. A price is what a thing costs and it does not date, and it does not add up, and it does not get written over by the people going along under it.”

And then she said the part that had been under all of it since the fourteenth of that month.

She said, “On the fourteenth I asked what it was counting from. It is not counting from anything at all. I have had that question standing in my head for twenty-four days and it does not have an answer because it is not a question.”

She said, “That is not comfortable, and I am not going to pretend it is, and I have been treating a price as if it were a count for nineteen years.”

## What That Does To The Column

And then she stood there and let the whole of it arrive, and it arrived fast, and she said it out loud standing in the ordinary voice with the lamp on the wood a foot in from her hand.

She said, “If that figure at the head is the price, then the pairs of cuts under it are not counts.”

She said, “A five in the second pair is not five of something. It is a figure that is read against ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes, and what it means is how much of that it took, and there is not one word in this building that says how much of a price is a thing.”

She said, “And the nine pairs under my seven are not nine nights I have not had. They are nine entries in a column of amounts, and I have been carrying them for nineteen years as a calendar and they are not a calendar.”

She stopped for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “That is the thing I worked out today and I am not going to soften it. A column with a price at the head is not a record of what happened. It is a record of what was paid. And everything I have written into that wood since I got into this building has gone into a column of payments, and I did not know that when I put the five in and I did not know it when I put the seven in.”

She said, “And I am not going to write to it.”

He said, “You are not going to do what.”

She said, “I am not going to put a figure under that price. Not tonight, not on the second of a month, not ever. Because the first time I do that I will have answered it, and I do not know what it asks, and I have never in nineteen years sent one word into a column that I could not read back.”

She picked the strip up off the wood and squared it with two fingers of her left hand and put it down again, face up, grain running up, in the same place.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“A column with a seven in it is now being read against a price of ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes, and I do not know what seven of that is.”
