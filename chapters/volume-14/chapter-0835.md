# Nothing Comes Back Up

Twenty days since the ninth stage. Four hundred and fifty-one days in that room. The cage out two hundred and eighteen days. Right arm open four hundred and fifty-eight days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and ninety-three days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in the room. The lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table and the light went a hand’s width along it and stopped, and there were three pieces of wood lying face up side by side about a foot in from the edge of it and one board lying face down about a foot further along.

She said her figures out loud at the seventh hour, standing, in the ordinary voice, and then she went and stood at the near end of that table with a folded piece of paper in the fold of her shirt against her fourth rib and she worked on it until the ninth hour without moving it once.

## The Second Stair And What Comes Up It

She said it out loud first, standing four feet off the near end of that table at about the seventh hour and twenty minutes, because she is not going to have three days in this room that cannot be read.

She said, “I am going to work out whether a piece of paper comes back up that stair. And I am going to say first that I know what the answer is going to be, and I am working it out anyway, because there are two different things and I do not want to have them in one.”

She said, “The first is whether it comes up. The second is whether I am going to find out. And the second one is the one that matters.”

## What Has Ever Come Up

She put two fingers of her left hand on the top of that table.

She said, “Here is everything that has ever gone down nine hundred and four steps in nineteen years and everything that has ever come up it, and I am going to say it in order because that is how I know a thing.”

She said, “A man comes up it once a month at the second hour with forty boards under one arm. It is the same stair going up and it is a different stair going down and he has never once said a word on it.”

She said, “Forty boards go up and a cloth goes down round them and a cord goes down twice round the cloth, and the boards come from four hundred years of a column with a price at the head of it, and they do not come back up.”

She said, “On the twenty-seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four I went down that stair in oil light with a lamp in my left hand. That lamp has not come back up. I have not gone down for it since and I have not looked.”

She said, “And I have gone down it myself six times in nineteen years and I have come back up it every time, because a person can always come back up a stair and a piece of paper cannot do that on its own.”

She said, “So: nothing has ever come back up that stair. Not once. In four hundred years there is no instance of it and I am not working it out from a book, I am working it out from a room.”

## Not Asking Twice

And then she got to the second thing, which was the one that mattered, and she did it quickly because she had been carrying it since the seventh hour.

She said, “So the finding is that a page goes down nine hundred and four steps and does not come up, and that is not a finding. That is a thing I already knew, and I have been carrying a folded piece of paper against my fourth rib for two days as though it were a question, and a question that has been answered before it is asked is not a question. It is a habit.”

She said, “And the question is not whether it comes up. The question is whether I am going to find out, and the way I find out is that a room puts something on that table at the second hour of a month, and it has not done it yet because the next one is twenty-two mornings away.”

She stopped for about a count of ninety.

She said, “And here is the other half of it and I want it said plainly and only once. If I ask again, on any morning before that first, then I am asking a question. And a question in this room is a person waiting for a thing that has never answered anything in four hundred years, and I have said out loud since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four that nothing in this room is required to answer me.”

She said, “And I have asked in this room before. Twice, on two mornings a long way apart, and I came away from both of those with nothing, and neither of those was a question that had not got an answer.”

She said, “I am not asking again. That is the decision and I am putting it in this room at about the eighth hour and twenty minutes on the ninth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four so that it is said before the twenty-sixth and not on it.”

## The Bundle Under The Far End

And then she went along that table, and she did not go the twenty-two paces, and she did not go down the second stair. She went as far as the thirteenth pace with her left hand on the leg of it and she stopped there and she stood in the dark with her arm open in front of her for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said it out loud where the table was in the dark on both sides of her.

She said, “There is a bundle of papers under the far end of this table. Two turns of cord and not a knot. I have had my arm into it five times in nineteen years and I have never once pulled anything out of it.”

She said, “And in the fold of my shirt against my fourth rib there is a folded piece of paper with six sentences on it in my own hand, written at the eighth hour two days ago, and it is going to go down that stair in a cloth with forty boards on the top of them on the morning a bundle comes up.”

She did not move while she was saying it.

She said, “And it is the first thing I have ever sent anywhere at all. Not out of that room. Anywhere. Nineteen years, and there has not been one line of mine past twenty-two paces of that table, and there is going to be one on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four.”

She said, “And the grief is not about the paper. I want to say that first because it would look like it was.”

She said, “The grief is that there are two things of mine that I have never looked inside. One has been under that table since before I came up that stair and I have had my arm into it five times. And the other one is folded in four against my fourth rib and I have not looked inside that either, because I wrote it, and a thing you have written yourself is not a thing you have looked inside.”

She said, “And the first of those two has been in this building for nineteen years and it is not mine to open. And the second one is mine, and I am going to open it in the only way there is, which is to let it go nine hundred and four steps in the dark and not come back up.”

She stood there in the dark at the thirteenth pace of a twenty-two-pace table.

She said, “It is not restraint. It is that I do not want to know.”

## A Room With A Page In It

And then she went back along that table to the near end with her left hand on the leg of it and stood four feet off the near end of it in the light, and she said the last of that morning out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour.

She said, “Here is what is different about a room with a page in it, and I worked it out on the way back along this table.”

She said, “Nothing. That is the honest answer and I am going to give it before anything else, because I am not going to have weeks of building toward a thing where a piece of paper changes a room.”

She said, “The lamp is where it goes. The cage is out. The bar is against the wall on the left. The bundle is under the far end. There are three pieces of wood face up at this end of that table and one face down about a foot along. There is a column at the far end of that table with a price at the head of it and four hundred years of amounts in it and not one person.”

She said, “And there is a sentence that was said out loud in this room at about the eighth hour on the seventh of this month, and it is folded against my fourth rib now, and there is one room in this building that has heard it, and it is the one I have been standing in.”

She put her hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “So the difference is not in the room. The difference is that a thing said out loud once in a room is only true in that room, and there is not one instrument in this building that can carry it out of the door, and I have said every figure I have got in this room every morning since I was nine years old and every one of them has stopped at twenty-two paces.”

She said, “And there is one sentence of mine that is going past twenty-two paces on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, and there is nothing I can do about it after that and there is nothing I want to do about it, and that is the first difference and it is not the one I expected.”

She said, “The second difference is that I took a stage on the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen and it cost me the right to be alone in this room, and I said at the ninth hour that night that there was not one instrument in this county that could tell me who with.”

She said, “And a page does not tell anybody anything either. It just goes.”

She left the lamp where it was. The light went a hand’s width along the near end of that table and stopped, and there was nothing on that table at the near end of it that had not been there the day before, and there was nothing in that room that had answered anything, and twenty-two mornings were in front of her.
