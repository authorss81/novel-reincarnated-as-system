# The Fourth Landing

She went down first, because a dark stair is worse going down and she was not going to let a boy of sixteen find that out on his own, and he came after her with the lamp in his right hand and his left out along the wall, and the wall was four inches off his hand on the one side and there was nothing at all on the other.

The low door was open. The first four hundred and forty had a rail on one side and she kept her left hand on it going down and told him at every hundred which side it was on, and he kept his left hand on the wall, and they came to the fourth landing together at about the seventh hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four.

## The Second Four Without A Rail

She said, “The rail stops here.”

He said, “I know. I have been down it four times.”

She said, “You have been down this one. You have not been down the second four.”

He said, “There is nothing down the second four that I do not know, and it is four hundred and forty more, and eight hundred and eighty is the whole of that stair from the low door to the bottom, and I am not going to count them tonight.”

She said, “All right.”

And he went down the second four with the lamp in his hand, and there is no rail on it, and she went down it after him with her left hand on the wall and her right arm out in front of her where it has been out since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and she counted his stops the way she counts everything.

He stopped once at the fifth flight, not to rest, because he was sixteen and had three flights left.

He stopped once at the sixth flight, and he put his free hand flat on the wall, and he went on.

He stopped once at the seventh flight, and this time he sat down on the step, and the lamp came down with him and the light went over the side of the stair and lay on the stone for a moment and then came back up when he got up.

She said, “That is three.”

He said, “That is not a count, that is a man sitting down.”

She said, “It is a count. It is the count of the times a body on that stair has stopped while I have been on it, and I have made that count in the dark four times in two hundred and fifty-one days and I have never once had anything to check it against.”

He said, “How many times have you stopped.”

She said, “Six.”

He said, “On the same stair.”

She said, “On the same four flights. On the first four, with the rail. And I do not think that is the same thing at all and I am not going to argue about it on a stair.”

## Oil On The Stone

And on the eighth flight he put his hand out for the wall and missed it, and went four inches further into the middle of that stair than he meant to, and the lamp went with him, and a little oil came over the side of it and went down the stone in the dark where neither of them could see it land.

He said nothing about that.

She said, “That was your oil.”

He said, “It was about a drop.”

She said, “It was about a drop on the last of eight flights that a person has to walk down holding a flame, and I am not going to be the one who says it does not matter, because if it does not matter to you then it will not matter to you at the two hundredth time and there will not be a two hundredth time when it matters.”

He said, “I have three lamps.”

She said, “I know you have three lamps. I have been costing them since the eighth of this month and I have never once said a number out loud about them, and I am not going to start at the bottom of a stair.”

He said, “Then say something else.”

She said, “No.”

And he went on down the last flight and she went on down behind him with her hand on the wall, and neither of them said anything for about a hundred and forty steps, and then they were at the bottom of that stair, which is eight hundred and eighty steps from the low door and the head of a second one going away under her feet.

## The Other Stair

And at the bottom of that stair she stood still for a count of ninety with her left hand on the wall where the stone stops and the second stair begins, and she did not put a foot on the second one, and she wants it on the page that she had been about to.

She said, “I am not going to walk you down.”

He said, “Why not.”

She said, “Because I have been down that stair twice tonight and it has taken my knees and I am going to have them for a week, and if I go down it a third time I will not get back up that first four hundred and forty without a hand on the rail, and then I will have to sit down on one of those steps in the dark with a person and I am not going to do that.”

He said, “That is a reason.”

She said, “That is a reason and it is not the reason.”

She said, “The reason is that I have walked exactly two hundred and fifty-one days of stair in this building and I have not once gone down any of it with anybody, and there is a version of tonight where I go all the way down the second one with a boy of sixteen and a lamp, and I know what that version is and I have known what it is since about the fifth flight.”

He said, “It is a woman walking somebody down a stair.”

She said, “It is four hundred and forty steps that I have counted more times than I can hold in my head, and you have just counted them with me in the dark with no rail on either side of us, and neither of us is going to be able to say what we counted, and that is not a thing I am going to do on the fifth night.”

He said, “I am counting the steps, not you.”

She said, “I know. That is why I have not told you to stop.”

## Four Stops

And then he said the number, which he had been carrying down four flights in his head.

He said, “Four stops.”

She said, “Four.”

He said, “You counted three on the way down.”

She said, “I counted three that were stops. There was one at the seventh flight where you sat down and one at the fifth flight where you did not, and one where you put your hand out for the wall and did not find it, and if you are going to count that as a stop then it is four and I will take your four.”

He said, “All right. Four.”

She said, “That is the first number we have agreed on since you came up this stair.”

## Her Knees

And she went back up the eight hundred and eighty of it alone, and the last four hundred and forty of it took her more than the first four hundred and forty took her, and she was slower on that stair than she has ever been on it, and she knew exactly why and said it out loud in the dark at the top of the second four with her left hand flat on the wall.

She said, “There is a rail on the first four of those flights and there is not one on the other four, and I can walk that second four up and down every day of the two hundred and fifty-one days I have been in this building with a hand on the wall, and tonight I walked it down with a hand on the wall and my breath at nine and a lamp on the flight below me that I could hear and not see, and then I walked it up with no lamp anywhere on it at all.”

She said, “And it has taken something out of my knees that I am going to have for about a week, and that is the fare for one night of a lamp being lit at the near end of that table, and I did not know the figure before tonight and I know it now.”

## Twelve Days

And at the low door she stopped and said the thing out loud that she had been carrying since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four.

She said, “Twelve days.”

He said, “To what.”

She said, “To the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four, which is the next day of that month on which a man comes up that stair at the second hour with about forty boards under one arm, and puts them down on that table in front of one lamp, and I am going to have to be standing in this room for it.”

She said, “And I have watched that man do that every month since the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four, in lamplight and in daylight and once in the dark when I told him to take them back down, and I have never once watched it with no light at all, and there is no light in this building and none on this mountain.”

She said, “And I have been doing sums all day with one arm open and a floor under my knees and I keep arriving at the same one, which is that I am going to have to ask a boy of sixteen for his oil, and I have not asked anybody for anything in nineteen years.”

And she went in out of the low door into that dark room and stood four feet off the near end of that table, and her knees were the whole of what she had learned that night, and she said so out loud to a room with nothing in it, and then she went and sat down on the floor with her back against the leg of that table until her breath was back to four.
