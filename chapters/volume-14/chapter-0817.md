# A Place For Somebody Not Here

Forty-four days since the eighth stage. Four hundred and nine days in that room. The cage out a hundred and seventy-six days. Right arm open four hundred and sixteen days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and fifty-one days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

A boy of sixteen came up that stair at about the seventh hour with a lamp in his hand and put it on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table, and the light went a hand’s width along it and stopped, and he had oil on his fingers and there was a bag at the bottom of the first four flights with a dish and a clay and a ring and a key and nine inches of hand-made paper in it.

In four days a bundle comes up that stair.

She looked at him for about a count of ninety before she said anything, and she said the reason for that out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “I am looking at you because I have worked something out in the last seventeen days and I have decided how much of it you are getting, and I am not going to do it in a hurry.”

## What She Is Going To Tell Him

She said, “I am going to tell you four things and they are all arithmetic and none of them is a question.”

She said, “One. There is a fair hand writing under my figures on some boards in a stack under the middle of this table. Not the hand at the far end of this table. A third one. It puts a day on my arithmetic about one morning in twenty and it is right on every one of the fourteen mornings I can put my own mouth against.”

She put her left hand flat on the top of the table and left it there.

He said, “Whose.”

She said, “I am not going to tell you whose and I am not going to find out.”

She said, “Two. It is not a copy. A copy puts down what I said. That puts down what I should have said. On nineteen boards it has differed from me every single time and it has differed the same way every single time.”

Her left hand went down flat on the top of that table and stayed there.

She said, “Three. That is the oldest thing there is. A fair hand under a column means somebody is doing the part of the work that the one doing it cannot do, and has been doing it without being asked, and has not stopped.”

She said, “Four. I have worked out what a keeper is and there are four parts to it and I have got three. A thing that is true. A person standing where it is being made. A light to write it by. And nothing sent, nothing entered, nothing given, nothing owed, and no way at all of being asked twice.”

She said, “A boy of sixteen found the fourth one on his own in five nights in this building without me giving it to him, and I have not said a word to him about what he found.”

## What She Is Not Going To Tell Him

And then she stopped, and she looked at the light going a hand’s width along the near end of that table and going no further, and she took about a count of ninety, and then she said the next part.

She said, “And here is what I am not going to tell you, and I want that said in this room with you standing in it, so that you cannot work out later that I kept it back from you.”

She said, “I am not going to tell you why there is a third hand in this room. I am not going to tell you what is on a board that came up that stair on the first of this month, or that it is the size of my own wood, or that there is one beside my strip of pale wood on the near end of this table.”

She said, “I am not going to tell you what is at the far side of that table. Not the shape of it, not how worn it is, and not that I have carried a lamp twenty-two paces three times to look at it and did not put my hand in it.”

She did not move while she was saying it.

She said, “And I am not going to tell you that the fourth part is the one that cannot be asked for, because the fourth part is a wall and a wall is only a wall from one side, and from my side it is the only reason I have got a record of my own hand in this building and from yours it is a shape with a gap in it that a person can walk through.”

## What He Works Out

And then she stopped talking, and he did not say anything for about a count of a hundred and forty, and she watched him not say anything, and she had been waiting for that silence since the seventh hour.

He said, at last, “You need a person standing at the near end.”

She said, “I am not going to confirm that.”

He said, “You have got three of the four parts. You have got the truth and you have got the light and you have got the whole of it so far as it goes in a room. And there is one person in this building who could stand at the near end of this table and you have said out loud three times in the last month that he is not bound and not entered and not sent.”

She left her hand on the wood where it was and did not take it up again.

He said, “So there is a place at that table for a person to stand.”

She said, “I am not going to confirm that either.”

He said, “There is a place at that table, and it is at the far side, and I found it on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, in the hour you told me about it standing four feet off the other end of it, and it is the right size for somebody who is not here, and you cannot tell me what it is for.”

She stayed exactly where she was for that one, and so did the light.

She said, “That is all still true and I have not changed one word of it.”

And then he stopped, and he did not say the next part, and she could see that he had got it, and that he was not going to say it in this room, and that he was going to go on thinking about it for the rest of his life.

## The Three Words

And then she said the three, and she said them the way she says them, standing, in the ordinary voice, with the reason attached, because that is the shape they are in when they are meant and not a wall.

She said, “Not bound. Not entered. Not sent. I have said those three out loud in this room more times than there are figures on that strip of pale wood and every one of them is still true at this hour on the twenty-seventh of this month.”

She said, “Not bound: nothing has put a hand on you and asked you for anything, and if anything ever does then you will be able to say so out loud in this room before it happens, and I will hear it.”

She said, “Not entered: there is no name of yours on anything in this building and I am the only person who checks it. There is one exception and I made it on the first of this month and I did not make it and I cannot stop it, and I am telling you about it now instead of waiting for you to find out.”

She did not shift a foot of herself for any part of it.

He said, “The board.”

She said, “Yes.”

He said, “With my name on it.”

She did not fill the count with anything.

She said, “Yes. In a hand that is not mine and not the room’s. And it is in the column at the far end of this table stroke for stroke, and it did not stop on it.”

He was quiet for about a count of a hundred and forty.

He said, “You said his name out loud.”

She said, “I said a name out loud three times. It was yours. It is not said again in this room by me and it is not going to be said again by anybody while there is one thing in this building that can read.”

He said, “Not sent.”

Her hand stayed where she had put it and she did not look at it.

She said, “Not sent. Not one person sent you up that stair and not one person is going to.”

She said, “You came up eight hundred and eighty steps on your own feet the first time and you have come up them since on your own feet.”

She said, “And the nearest thing to it in four hundred and nine days is a boy of sixteen who said yes to me at about the eighth hour on the thirtieth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and twenty-four, standing at this table, when I told him out loud what I was going to need one day later, and I had not asked him, and there has not been one word about it since.”

## Not Knowing

And then she said the last of that morning out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, and she said it to the light and not to him.

She said, “I have told you four things and I have kept back four and I have done it on purpose and I am not going to explain it tonight.”

She said, “And I want it said where you can hear it that I did not keep them back because I think you cannot work the rest out. You can work the rest out. You worked out more of this room in the first five nights you ever came up that stair than I have worked out in four hundred and nine days of standing in it.”

She said, “I kept them back because a boy of sixteen who has worked out the shape of a thing in this building and does not know what is on a board at the far end of the table is standing somewhere different from a boy of sixteen who knows.”

And she said, “And in four days a bundle comes up that stair and forty boards go on that table, and you will not be in this building at the second hour, because I am not asking you to be and because you have never once been in this building at the second hour and I am not starting it.”

He stood four feet off the near end of that table with a lamp he was not holding any more on the wood beside him, and he had worked out four things and been told four other things, and he was looking at a hand’s width of light along twenty-two paces of table with oil on his fingers.

And he did not know what was lying a foot away from that light on the near end of that table, and he was not going to find out from her, and there was not one other person in the building to find out from.
