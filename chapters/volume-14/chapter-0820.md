# The Lamp And The Person

Fifty days since the eighth stage. Four hundred and fifteen days in that room. The cage out a hundred and eighty-two days. Right arm open at four hundred and twenty-two and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and fifty-seven days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

Fifty-four days back she carried that lamp the whole length of that table and put it down at the far end of that table and looked at a thing in that wood from about four feet off, and she has not been able to arrange the doing of it since, and this morning she arranged it.

## The Problem With Two Hands

She said it out loud at about the seventh hour, standing four feet off the near end of that table, with a boy of sixteen standing eight feet off her and oil on his fingers.

She said, “The light goes a hand’s width along the near end of this table and stops, and to look at anything at the far end of it I have to carry this lamp twenty-two paces and put it down there, and then this end of the table is a dark table with three pieces of wood on it and a figure of mine in two of them.”

She said, “On the ninth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four I solved that once. I took the strip of pale wood off this table and put it under my left arm against my fourth rib, and carried the lamp in my left hand as well, and walked twenty-two paces on the stones with my right arm open in front of me.”

She said, “And that is the only time in nineteen years that I have looked at anything at the far end of that table and had my own record with me.”

She held up her left hand in the light.

She said, “And it took both hands. Both of them. And that is not a figure of speech, that is the whole of what I have, and I do not have a third hand and the other one does not close.”

She said, “So the arrangement that has worked one time in nineteen years is not available to me tonight and has not been available to me for fifty-four days.”

## What She Did Instead

She said, “And I am going anyway.”

Her right arm was open at her side and she did not put it behind her.

She said, “And I am going to leave it.”

She put her hand flat on the strip of pale wood and then on the board with the name on it and left both of them lying face up about a foot in from the edge of that table.

She said, “There is a strip of pale wood on this table with a price at the head of it and a five and a seven and an eight in it in my own hand.”

She said, “And there is a board with a name on it in a third hand that came up this stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four and has not gone back down it, and a third board a foot long lying beside them that came up the first of this month and says where a place is.”

She said, “And I am going to walk twenty-two paces along the near side of this table and leave all three of them in the dark for about a count of a hundred and forty, and I have not done that in four hundred and fifteen days, and I did not think I would ever do it.”

She said, “That is the whole of what this costs and I am paying it out loud.”

## Going Down Onto The Stone

The boy came with her. That was the arrangement and neither of them said one word about it: she walked twenty-two paces along the near side of that table on her feet with her left hand on the leg of it, and he came behind her with the lamp in his hand, and she put it down about four feet short of the far end and he stayed where he was with his hands at his sides.

The light came down flat onto the wood. And the near end of that table went out behind her, and the strip of pale wood and the two boards lying beside it went out, and a boy with oil on his fingers went out, and she could not see one line of him.

She did not do it from above this time.

She got down onto the stone at the far end of that table, on her hip and then onto her side with her right arm out in front of her, and she put her own face about a hand’s width off the floor and looked along the surface of that stretch of wood instead of down onto it.

## From The Other Side Of It

And the light came in across it at an angle instead of straight down, and that is the whole of the difference, and it took her about a count of a hundred and forty to understand why she had not done this fifty-four days ago.

She was not looking down at a shape. She was looking along the edge of one.

What came up out of the wood under a light that came in across it was the line where the wear stops, and that line has a shape, and the shape is not the shape of anything being put down in the middle.

She lay still on the stone for about a count of a hundred and forty.

And here is the thing she did not say, and she has not said it in this room and she is not going to say it now, and there is no line anywhere in that stack of forty boards that says any of it, because nothing was written and nothing was said.

## The One More Thing

Deeper at the front than at the back. She has said that out loud once, in this room, and she will not say it again.

And not deeper in the middle than at the edges. She said that one out loud in this room as well, and it is the second and last of the two things about that wear that this room is getting.

And in the middle there is no depth at all. There is a place about two fingers wide, in the middle of the length of that wear, where the wood is not worn down at all, and it is not worn on either side of the gap either, so the gap does not have edges to it, and there is no way at all of looking at that which makes it an edge.

She has been carrying that one about since the ninth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four and she has told no one, and there is no instrument in this county that could read it off a piece of wood, and she was not going to say it out loud standing in the dark twenty-two paces from a boy of sixteen with a lamp at his feet.

## Not Sitting In It

And then she got up off the stone and stood about four feet back from the lamp with her right arm open at her side and her breath at nine, and she said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“That is where I am.”

He said, “Where.”

She said, “Four feet off, standing, in the ordinary voice, telling you where I am and not telling you what I have been looking at for about a count of a hundred and forty, and both of those are true and you are sixteen and I am not going to say the second one in this room.”

He said, “All right.”

She put her hand flat on the wood and left it there.

She said, “I am not going to say it because a boy of sixteen who knows what a place in that wood is doing is a boy of sixteen who can be asked to do it, and I said that out loud to you on the eleventh of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four about a bundle of papers and I meant every word of it.”

She said, “And I am not sitting in it and not one person has put me in it. Not tonight, not on any night, and not you and not me. I have found three things in that wood in fifty-four days and I have not put my hand in it and I have not put my weight on it and I am not going to, and there is not one instruction in this building that has ever put a person in it.”

## Coming Back Along The Near Side

And then she carried the lamp back twenty-two paces herself and put it down on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table, and the light went a hand’s width along it and stopped.

And the three pieces of wood were where she had left them, face up, side by side, and she squared her own two of them with two fingers of her left hand and left the third one where it fell, and she had said out loud on the second of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four that she was not going to turn that one over.

And she said the last of that morning out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, and she said it about the light and not about the wood.

She said, “I have been trying for fifty-four days to get that place into light without giving this end of this table up, and I have failed every time, and this morning I gave this end up on purpose and put the lamp down at the far end and left a strip of pale wood and a board with a name on it lying in the dark for about a count of a hundred and forty.”

She said, “And when I put the lamp back down here this morning the light went a hand’s width along this table and stopped, and it went exactly as far as it goes on every other morning, and not one inch of it came back with me.”

She said, “And that is the thing I keep not understanding. That lamp travels twenty-two paces and comes back and the light it makes is the same width it always was and it is not wider for having been to the far end of that table, and there is no figure anywhere in this building that says it should be.”

She did not fill the count with anything.

She said, “I am not going to work out what a hand’s width is about. I said on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four that it was the width of my own hand and that is the whole of the instrument I have and it is not going to get better than that.”

She said, “And I am not calling that solving it. I have had fifty-four days of not solving it and about two hours of not solving it with a strip of pale wood in the dark, and the second one is not better than the first one except that I know what it costs now.”

She said, “And there is one more thing in that wood that I can stand behind, and I am going to keep it, and I am not going to say it out loud to anybody in this building, and that is the arrangement and I am not going to improve on it.”
