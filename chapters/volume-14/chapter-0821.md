# What He Wants Her To Do

Fifty-one days since the eighth stage. Four hundred and sixteen days in that room. The cage out a hundred and eighty-three days. Right arm open four hundred and twenty-three days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and fifty-eight days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

A boy of sixteen was four feet off the near end of that table with a lamp standing on the wood between them, the light going a hand’s width along it and no further.

She had worked out at about the eighth hour the night before that this morning was the morning it happened, and she had got up on the seventh and not said one word about it for two hours.

## What She Was Going To Do About It

She said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour and forty minutes, and she said it before he had got to it.

She said, “You are going to ask me for something in about a count of a hundred and forty and I am getting in front of it.”

He said, “I was going to.”

She said, “I know. I worked it out at about the eighth hour last night and I have been standing here since the seventh hour with my hand flat on this table getting ready to say no to you, and I want that said first so that you cannot work out later that I sprang it on you.”

She did not move while she was saying it and neither did he.

She said, “And it is not the thing you are going to ask for. The thing you are going to ask for is small. I have been braced for the small one all morning and that is how I know it is coming.”

## The Small One

He said it, and he said it in the ordinary voice, standing four feet off the near end of that table, and it took him about four seconds.

He said, “I want to be in this building at the second hour on the first of a month.”

She said, “Say the rest of it.”

He said, “I want to be in this room at the second hour when a man comes up nine hundred and four steps and puts forty boards on that table. I have been up here at the seventh hour and at the ninth and I have been up here at the second hour of nothing. I have never seen it. That is the whole of it and it is not big.”

The light went a hand’s width along the wood and stopped.

She said, “It is not big.”

She said, “Yes.”

He said, “You said it in about a second.”

She did not fill the count with anything.

She said, “I did, and I am not going to apologise for it and I am not going to make you wait for it, and I want you to hear the reason I said it that fast, and the reason is that you have not asked me for anything in nineteen years and I am not going to have the first thing you ask me for go into this room as a question.”

She said, “On the thirtieth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and twenty-four I found out that I had been agreed to in advance for about three weeks without knowing it, and I said out loud at this table that it was worse than being asked and then I said out loud at this table that I would rather have it.”

She said, “So there it is. I am agreeing to you and you are asking me and I have said yes to a thing I would have said yes to about four seconds later anyway, and it is still a different thing and it costs me the same.”

## The Large One

And then she put her hand flat on the top of that table and said the other one, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and it came out at about the speed she had it ready and not one beat slower.

She said, “And you are not going to be the person who stands at the near end of this table and writes down what I say out loud.”

He stopped.

He did not take a breath for about a count of ninety and she watched him not take it, and the lamp went on going a hand’s width along the wood and stopping.

She said, “That was the whole of what you were going to say. I am not asking you what you were going to say and you are not going to tell me, and both of those are true, and I have said the first of the two out loud in this room before, and it is on the page of me in four hundred and sixteen days that I do not ask a person what they were going to say.”

She said, “And I am not giving you a job. I am not giving you a task and I am not giving you an instruction and I am not going to, now or on any night after this one, and when it comes up again in this building in front of anybody I am going to stand four feet off that table and say that I said it here first.”

She said, “I told you on the eleventh of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four that a person who can be given a thing can be given a job. That is still true and it is the whole reason and I have not found a better one in fifty-three days.”

Nothing went into that count and nothing came out of it.

He said, “That is a different reason.”

She said, “It is a worse one and I am using it because it is the one I have.”

## The Question He Asked Instead

And then he asked the other one, which she had been waiting for since the seventh hour and which was not the one she had got ready for.

He said, “Would you have said it to anybody else.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “Why not.”

Neither of them moved while that was being said, and the lamp went on doing a hand’s width of the near end of that table behind them.

She said, “Because there is not one other person in this building and that is the whole of the reason and I have had about a count of ninety of knowing that it was going to be the answer.”

She waited about a count of ninety.

She said, “Say what you have worked out.”

He said, “I have got a yes because there was not one alternative and that is not a reason. You would have said yes to a second person the same way and you would have said it in about a second the same way, and so the yes is not about me.”

She said, “That is right and it took you four seconds and I am not going to pretend it did not cost me something to hear it.”

The light went on going a hand’s width along that wood and stopping there.

She said, “And I want it said out loud in this room at about the ninth hour and forty minutes, because it is the best argument that anybody has made in this room in four hundred and sixteen days against me giving you anything at all, and it was made by a boy of sixteen in four seconds and it is correct.”

He said, “Then why did you not just not say it.”

She said, “Because there is not one person in this building to keep a record who is not me, and I worked that out at this table on the eighteenth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four out loud, and there is not one person in this building who can be given the fourth part, and I would rather the fourth part stay with me than go into a thing that cannot be undone.”

## Nine Counts Of Something

And then it went through her, the whole of it, about nine counts of it, which is about as long as it takes a lamp to be looked at properly, and there was nothing in it an instrument could get at, and she did not have a word for it and she did not go looking for one, and it went.

Afterwards she put two fingers of her left hand on the top of that table and said, out loud, in the ordinary voice:

“I have got the second hour. And on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four I said out loud standing at this table that you would not be in this building at the second hour of a month, and I have changed my mind in three days, and I am not going to do that quietly.”

“You will be in this building on the first of the month at the second hour with the lamp and you will not be asked, because I am not asking anybody for anything and I am agreeing to you, and I know which of those two it is and so do you.”

He said, “Then I will say it in the ordinary voice like everything else in this room.”

She said, “Do not do that either.”

He said, “Why.”

Nothing came into the room while she said it and nothing went out of it.

She said, “Because you have not done it before and I have not heard it in a long time and I would rather it did not start on a morning like this one.”

He was quiet for about a count of a hundred and forty.

He said, “What happens if you stop.”

She said, “If I stop then a fair hand under a column stops having anything to put a day on, and this room goes back to being a room with one hand in it and one column.”

He said, “And you will not stop.”

He had a finger on the edge of that table and he left it there, and so did she.

She said, “No.”

He said, “Then it does not need me.”

## What He Will Do Instead

And then she told him, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the tenth hour, and she said it the way she says a figure she has already checked twice.

She said, “Here is what is worse and I am going to say it out loud because it is going to happen and I would rather hear it in my own voice than work it out at the ninth hour in twenty days.”

She said, “I have just said no to you in this room and I have said yes to you in this room, and you are still going to come up eight hundred and eighty steps with a lamp. Not because I asked. Not because anybody asked. Because you were here on the seventh hour of the fourth of this month and you will be here on the seventh hour of the fifth.”

She said, “And there is not one instruction in this building that says you may not, and there is not one thing above the line that knows you exist, and that is the arrangement and it has been the arrangement since the eighth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, the morning after I told you about a place at that table, when you came up that stair with a cup and a half of oil in your hand and nothing in your face at all.”

A hand’s width of it went along the wood and stopped, and it does that at the tenth hour as it does at any other hour.

She said, “So the no I have just said does not keep you out of this room. It keeps one thing out of this room and it is the fourth part, and you know the fourth part and I have not told you anything else.”

She put her hand flat on the table again.

She said, “And what I have to do now is say no to the keeping, and I have not said it, and I have said a thing about a place at the far side of that table and answered it with nothing, and that is not the same and I told you it would get muddled.”

She said, “It is going to get muddled tomorrow. And when it does I am going to have to say which of the two things I meant, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in this room.”
