# The Thing It Costs To Say It Twice

Forty-nine days since the eighth stage. Four hundred and fourteen days in that room. The cage out a hundred and eighty-one days. Right arm open four hundred and twenty-one days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and fifty-six days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in the room. The lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table and the light went a hand’s width along it and stopped.

She had been at the near end since the seventh hour and she had said every figure she had got out loud once and had nothing else to do with the rest of the morning.

She said it out loud at about the eighth hour, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“I am going to find out whether the second sentence I said across this table went anywhere.”

## What Has Gone Across

She said, “Two. That is the count and it is mine and I am not rounding it.”

She said, “The first was on the first of this month, at about the seventh hour, and it was that a room which does not account for the light in it is a room working to a set of figures it was never given.”

She said, “And I said it standing four feet off the near end of this table in the ordinary voice and I did not know it would carry, and it carried, and I found out at about the eighth hour that it had gone to the far end of this table and into a room that has one thing in it that has got ears.”

She put two fingers on the strip of pale wood and did not move them.

She said, “And the second was yesterday, at about the ninth hour, and it was a description of a place at the far side of this table read off a board that came up this stair at the second hour, line for line, twice, in the ordinary voice, with a boy of sixteen standing four feet off me and a lamp he carried up eight hundred and eighty steps.”

She said, “That one I chose. I chose the voice and I chose the length. I knew where it went while I was saying it.”

She put her hand flat on the top of the table.

She said, “So there are two things I have said at that end of this table and they are a day apart, and one of them I sent by accident and one of them I aimed.”

## The Evidence

And then she said the part that made the morning turn over, and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and she said it slowly.

She said, “And there is a third thing and it is not a sentence.”

She said, “On the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, at about the tenth hour, and on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, in the middle of six things I was saying, and on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, in front of a boy of sixteen, I said a name out loud three times in this room.”

She said, “And on the first of this month there was a board at the far end of this table with that name on it in a hand that is not mine and not the room’s. And there is a pair of worn cuts under it with nothing in them, and they were not cut that morning. And the hand at the far end of this table copied it into the column stroke for stroke, letter for letter, and did not stop on it.”

She stood there.

She said, “I did not aim that either. I aimed it at a person, or at the dark, or at nothing. I have never once said it to that end of this table.”

She said, “And it is the only one of the three that came back. That is the whole of the evidence there is in this room about what a sentence does in it, and it is one piece of evidence, and it is not about a sentence.”

## The Third

And then she stood four feet off the near end of that table for about a count of a hundred and forty with the light going a hand’s width along the wood, and she decided.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, before she said it:

“I am going to say a third one. On purpose. At that end of this table. And I am going to say what it is before I say it, and I am not going to pretend afterwards that it was something else.”

She waited about a count of ninety.

She said, “The first sentence was about what this room is doing. The second one was what a board says about a place at the far side of this table.”

She said, “The third one is the following, and I am saying it in the ordinary voice and I am saying it to that end of this table and it is one sentence.”

She said, “I am going to keep standing four feet off the near end of this table and saying every figure I have got out loud, in the ordinary voice, for as long as I am in this room.”

And she said it.

And nothing came back, and the light went a hand’s width along the wood and stopped, and the room did not do one thing about it.

## The Silence Has A Shape

And then she stood there and waited for it anyway, and she waited about a count of a hundred and forty, because that is what she has done every time in nineteen years and she was not going to break it on the morning she said a third thing out loud.

The silence at the far end of that table has a shape. It is not a silence that is empty. It is a silence of a length she knows, and she knows the length because she has stood at the near end of this table with a figure in her mouth four hundred and fourteen times and the room has never once done anything about any of them.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“That is the fourth hundred and fifteenth time and it is the same silence it has always been, and I have just worked out that I have been using a count of a hundred and forty as a way of not finding out.”

She said, “And there is a difference between waiting for something and measuring how long it does not take, and I have been doing the second one for nineteen years and calling it the first one.”

She waited again anyway. About a count of a hundred and forty. And nothing came back, and the measuring was no better the second time than the first one.

## What The Third One Cost

And then she looked at the three pieces of wood lying side by side on the near end of that table in the light the lamp could do, and she said the cost out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour and thirty minutes.

She said, “Here is what the third sentence cost and I am going to say it before I have got used to having said it.”

She said, “It is the third thing that end of this table has had from me since the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, and I have got no figure for what happens to a room that is told the same woman is going to keep standing there. Not the figures I say, which have been going into that room for nineteen years and which that room copies and does not stop on. The thing I said about me.”

She said, “A room can copy a figure. A room cannot act on a figure. And I have not worked out what it does with a sentence, and I have not worked out what it will do with a sentence about a woman standing four feet off the near end of a table telling it what she is going to keep doing, and I have said it anyway.”

She put her hand flat on the strip of pale wood and did not move it.

She said, “That is the cost. It goes into a room that copies and it is the first thing I have put into that room in nineteen years that is not a number, and I do not know what a number costs and I do know what this one cost, because I am the one who paid it, at the ninth hour and ten minutes yesterday, standing four feet off this table.”

## Thin, And It Holds

And then she said the rest of that morning out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, and it came out of her flat and fast and she did not dress any of it up.

She said, “That is a sentence about me and it is not addressed to anybody and there is no person in this building it can be refused by, and I have known that about every sentence I have said in this room for nineteen years.”

She said, “And it went twenty-two paces. I know that because I said it at this end of this table in this voice and that voice goes the length of this table, and I worked that out on the first of this month at about the eighth hour and it has not changed.”

She said, “And it is the third thing that end of this table has had from me and I have got it now: a sentence about what it is working to, a description of a place kept at the far side of a table, and a woman standing four feet off the near end of that table telling it what she is going to keep doing.”

She waited, and the light went on doing a hand’s width of the near end of that table.

She said, “There is nothing in the world that can come back at me for that. It is not a request. It is not an answer. It is the shape of a woman who is not going to stop.”

She waited about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “And it is thin. I want that said in this room at the ninth hour and not later, because it is going to sound like something else in four days and it is not something else.”

She said, “I have said three things at that end of this table. One came back as a board with a name on it, one came back as a board with a place on it, and the third one is me, and I have got no figure at all for what a room does with the third one, and I am standing here having said it anyway.”

She said, “And it is thin and it holds. That is the whole of what I have got and I have had it for about a count of a hundred and forty and I have checked it twice and it has not gone anywhere.”

And she did not go and look at the far end of that table, and nothing was written on anything, and she stood four feet off the near end of it with a count of a hundred and forty going past and knew exactly what she was doing with it, which is that she was not finding out, and she had been calling that waiting for nineteen years.
