# The Third Pair Of Cuts

She went to look at what she had written on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four on the fourteenth of that month at the seventh hour, with a lamp standing a foot in from the near end of that table and the light going a hand's width along the wood and no further, and she stood over it for a long time and did not touch it.

## Nine Days

Seven was in the third pair of cuts, in her own hand, in one stroke and a turn, and it had been there nine days.

She said it out loud before she did anything else, standing, in the ordinary voice, because the fourth of the six is that a figure gets said before it is a line anywhere.

She said, “Seven stages into the seventh realm, taken at the ninth hour of the fifth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, at the near end of this table, on this strip of pale wood, in my own hand, with the nib against my fourth rib.”

She said, “That figure is nine days old. Two thousand six hundred and seven less two thousand five hundred and ninety-eight is nine.”

And then she stood there and let the other half of it arrive, which took her a count of a hundred and forty and did not arrive the way she expected.

She said, “That is the first figure of mine that has ever been wrong.”

## Two Kinds Of Figure

Because there are two kinds of figure and she had them mixed up for nine days and had not known there were two kinds.

She said, “A price does not date. I have been in this building nineteen years and a price is what a thing costs and it is the same tomorrow and the day after, and it is standing there with ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes at the head of this wood and it has not moved by a count since the wood was cut.”

She said, “A count dates. Seven was a count. I took seven stages and that was on the fifth of that month and it is the fourteenth of that month and it is still seven, and it is not still seven, it is seven nine days ago.”

She put two fingers of her left hand on the third pair of cuts without touching the strokes.

She said, “And I cannot check it. That is the part I have not been able to say out loud for nine days and I am saying it now.”

She said, “The five in the pair above it and the seven in this one are the only two figures on this wood I can swear to, because they are mine, and there is not one thing in this building or in this county that could come at either of them and tell me whether it was true.”

She said, “And there is not one thing that could tell me whether a count is out of date except the count, and the count is me, and I have never before had a figure of my own on this wood that I could check against anything at all.”

## The One She Could Check

And she got down onto the stone and put the lamp on the floor with its face up on the wood so that the light came down on the strip, and she lay on her back under the near end of that table with her head at the level of the boards and looked at the top of that strip from underneath, and she had done that twice in nineteen years and it is the only way she has ever seen wood in light.

And she did the one thing on that wood she could check.

She said, out loud, on her back under that table:

“Seven stages on the fifth of this month. Two hundred and ninety-seven days in this room on the fifth of this month. Three hundred and six days in this room today. Those are the same number nine days apart, and both of them are mine, and I have checked one against the other, and they agree.”

She said, “So the date on this row is right. That is the part I have got and I have got it the only way a person can have it, by putting one of my own counts against another of my own counts, and both of them being nine days out of date together.”

She said, “And a price at the head of that wood has been standing still for however long it has been there and I cannot check it against anything at all, and I have never tried.”

## The Nine Empty Pairs

And then she went along the whole of that strip with two fingers of her left hand, out loud, counting them, because she has carried nine empty pairs for nineteen years and has never once run her hand along them and said where one ended.

She said, standing over the wood with the light a hand's width along it:

“One. Two. Three. Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Nine.”

She said, “That is what is under the seven. And I said out loud on the fifth of this month that an empty pair is for the night I need somewhere to put a figure, and I said it as though there were nine nights ahead of me.”

She said, “There are not nine nights ahead of me in those nine pairs. Nine is a number of nights and I have never worked out where the nights stop and the pairs begin, and I have been carrying them as the same thing for nineteen years.”

She said, “A night is a day I have lived. A pair of cuts is a place a figure goes. And the reason there are nine of them is not that there are nine nights coming. It is that somebody cut nine pairs of cuts into that piece of wood before it came off the tree, and cut them the same day as the twelve that were under them, and I have never been able to work out why.”

He said, “Then what is the count of them.”

She said, “I do not know. And that is the second thing I cannot answer tonight and it is a different thing from the first one.”

## The Row Again

And she said the whole row out loud, standing, at about the eighth hour, in the ordinary voice, with her arm open at her side and her breath at four counts.

She said, “At the head of this strip, ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes. The same figure in the first pair of cuts under the head. A five in the second pair. A seven in the third pair. Nine pairs of cuts under that with nothing in any of them, and two of the nine were cut before the wood came off the tree and the other seven were cut the same day as the others and she could not work that out either.”

She said it, and she stopped there, and the stopping was the decision.

She said, “I am not going to add a figure to it.”

He said, “Why not.”

She said, “Because a column with a figure added to it because I felt like having one in it is a column that cannot be read back. There are nine empty pairs and every one of them is a night I have not had yet, and I am not going to be the woman who filled one to see what it looked like.”

He said, “That is a rule.”

She said, “That is not a rule, that is a preference, and I am the only one in this building who gets to have preferences about her own arithmetic.”

He said, “Then what happens to the ones that are already wrong.”

She said, “The seven goes on being seven, and it will be twenty-six days old on the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, and no one in this building but me is ever going to know how old it is, including the hand at the far end of this table.”

## The Question She Cannot Answer

And then she got to the part she had been carrying since the seventh hour, and it came out in the wrong order, the way a thing does when it has been held for nine days.

She said, “The seven is out of date and I can see exactly how far, and the five in the pair above it, if that was a count, is out of date by four hundred years and change, and I have never once gone back and looked at it.”

He said, “Then the five is out of date.”

She said, “The five is out of date by an amount I have not got and I am not going to do the subtraction on, because it is not mine to do and it is not a figure of mine to spend a night on.”

She said, “Here is the one I cannot answer and I have had it since the seventh hour of the eighth and I am putting it out loud before I go down that stair tonight.”

She put her hand flat on the wood with the lamp on the far side of it.

“If the figure at the head is a count, then it is counting from something, and if it is a price then it is not counting from anything at all, it is standing still. And I have been reading it as a count for as long as I have had this wood in my hands, and I have read it as a count because a count is the only kind of figure I have ever known what to do with.”

She said, “I do not know what it is counting from.”

She said, “And that is the first question I have ever had about a figure that I have got no way of even guessing at.”

She said, “And I am going to walk down that stair tonight with both of them unanswered, and I have walked down that stair about a hundred and forty times and I have never once gone down it with a question I could not have answered standing at the top of it.”

She put her left hand flat on the wood with the lamp on the far side of it, and the light went a hand's width along the near end of that table and stopped, and the other twenty-two paces of it were exactly as dark as they had been when she came up.

She said, “Ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes. What it is counting from. And nine pairs of cuts and nine of nothing.”

She said, “Goodnight.”
