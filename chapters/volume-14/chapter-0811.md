# The Morning After The Name

Nineteen days since the eighth stage. Three hundred and eighty-four days in that room. The cage out a hundred and fifty-one days. Right arm open three hundred and ninety-one days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and twenty-six days lighter at this morning and not one of them going back.

The lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end with a trimmed wick, and the light went a hand’s width along it and stopped, and it went that way whether a person was in the room or not.

There was no person in the room.

## Nothing Done About It

She said it out loud at about the seventh hour, standing four feet off the near end of that table, in the ordinary voice, into twenty-two paces of dark.

She said, “There is a board at the far end of this table with a name on it in a hand that is not mine, and it has been there since the second hour of yesterday, and I have not done one thing about it.”

She said, “That is the first morning in nineteen years that I have stood in this room and not done the thing I do every morning.”

She said, “I am going to do it now. It is not late. It is one day late and I am going to do it before I do anything else and I am going to say that out loud so that I cannot come back up here in four days and pretend it was later.”

## What The Thing Is

And she said what the thing is, because she does not let a thing stand in this room that has not been put into words where it exists.

She said, “I say every figure I have got out loud once, standing, in the ordinary voice, before I do anything else. Not to anybody. To the room.”

She said, “That is the one thing I have never not done. Not once, not for a figure, not for a day, not the day my knees went. And I have a piece of wood at the far end of this table with a name on it that I read out loud yesterday at about the ninth hour and it is not in the record and it is not anywhere.”

And then she stopped, because she had got to that and had been standing on it for about a count of ninety without noticing she had stopped.

## Nowhere To Put It

She said, “There is no instrument in this building I can write it on.”

She said, “There is a strip of pale wood lying face up about a foot in from this end of this table and it is a foot long and two inches across and it came up that stair in an armful on the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and there is not one other copy of any of it in this world.”

She put her left hand flat on the wood beside it.

She said, “There is a price at the head of it. Ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes. The same price in the first pair of cuts. A five in the second pair, a seven in the third, an eight in the fourth. Eight pairs under that with nothing in any of them. All of it in my own hand with three fingers and a thumb on the nib.”

She said, “And a column with a price at the head of it is a column of amounts. I worked that out at the near end of this table on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and I said it out loud and I have not taken it back.”

She said, “So if I put a name on that wood then a name is an amount. And if I put a pair of cuts on that wood then a pair of cuts is an amount. And I have had nineteen years of nothing on it that was not a figure I intended to be worth something.”

She took her hand off it.

She said, “I am not going to do that. I would rather have it in the room unrecorded than have it in the record as a price.”

## Twenty-Two Paces Without A Lamp

And then she went and got it, and she went without the lamp, and that was the first thing about that morning that she had not planned.

She said it out loud before she moved.

She said, “I cannot see it and I do not need to see it. I know where it is and I know what is on it and I have known both since yesterday at about the ninth hour. I am going to fetch it with my hands.”

She went down onto the stone at the near end of that table and went along the near side of it on her feet, with her left hand on the leg of the table, the whole twenty-two paces, counting them out loud because there is not one person in this room to hear how long twenty-two paces takes and she says figures into empty rooms on purpose.

The nineteenth pace is a flag that moves. She put her left hand flat against the table leg and waited about a count of ninety and went the last three.

At the far end she stood about four feet short of it and put her right hand up onto the top of the wood, and she had to go along it twice with the flat of her hand before she was where the boards were.

And then she found it by the writing.

She said, out loud, from four feet off, in the ordinary voice:

“You are the easiest thing in this room to find in the dark. You have a name cut into you and a name is the only thing on this table that a hand can feel that was not cut by the room.”

## Carrying It Back

She picked it up in her left hand and stood there about a count of ninety and then went back twenty-two paces with it, on her feet, on the stones, with her right arm open in front of her, and the nineteenth pace moved again and she went over it.

And when she got back to the near end she put it down on the top of that table face up, a foot in from the edge, beside the strip of pale wood and not touching it.

And that was the whole of the errand and it had taken her about a count of a hundred and forty twice over and she had not needed the light for any of it, and she stood four feet back and looked at two pieces of wood lying side by side in a hand’s width of flame.

## What Is On It, In Words

And then she read it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, the way she reads everything in this room, with her left hand flat on the top of the board and not on her own strip.

She said, “A name. The name I have been carrying against my ribs for ten years. It is not written by three fingers and a thumb. It is not written by the hand at the far end of this table, which writes four hundred years of the same stroke starting in the same place.”

She said, “Under it there is one pair of cuts and there is nothing in them, and they are not new. They are worn round at the corners and they are the same size as the ones on my own wood, and they have been empty longer than the name has been on that board.”

She said, “And the hand at the far end of this table copied the whole of it into the column at the far side of the shelf, stroke for stroke, letter for letter, after the man had gone off that stair, with one stroke that took a count of nine. It did not stop on it.”

She put two fingers of her left hand on the near corner of her own strip and squared it.

She said, “So it is said twice. Once in a hand I do not know and once in the room’s own hand, and it went up that stair on the first of this month and nothing came down that stair with it.”

## Two Pieces Of Wood

And she stood over them and she looked at the two of them side by side, and it took her about a count of a hundred and forty, and then she said out loud the thing she had worked out and had not wanted.

She said, “They are the same size. A foot long and two inches across, both of them, and there is nothing in this building that made them and no reason on this earth that they should be.”

She said, “Mine has a price at the head of it and four figures and eight empty pairs under them. That one has a name and one worn pair of cuts with nothing in it. And the pair of cuts under it is the same width as the pair of cuts on mine and there is not one instrument in this county that could tell me why.”

She put two fingers of her left hand on the near corner of his board and squared it up the way she squares her own strip.

She said, “I am not going to turn it over.”

She said, “That is the decision and I am saying it out loud standing here. Every board that has come up that stair in nineteen years has gone on this table face up and has gone down this table face up, and a board that is face down on this table is a board I have made on my own account out of the one thing in this building that copies what it is not given.”

She said, “So it stays face up, next to mine, and I am not going to touch it again today.”

## The Length Of Her Own Forearm

And then she stopped, and she looked at the two pieces of wood again, and she said the last thing of that morning out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and it came out slower than the rest of it.

She said, “There is a stretch of the far side of this table about the length of my own forearm, paler than the rest of the wood, with a shape worn into it that no tool made.”

She said, “I saw it on the ninth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four at about the seventh hour with a lamp I carried twenty-two paces for, and it is not the shape of a person sitting in it, and I have not said it to a person and I am not going to say it to a person today either.”

She said, “And a board is a foot long. And I am not going to think about that in this room.”

She left the lamp standing on the wood where it was, and she left the two pieces of wood lying side by side in the light it could do, and the strip and the board were the same length and she had said out loud that she was not going to think about it and she was going to think about it every morning for the rest of the time she had.
