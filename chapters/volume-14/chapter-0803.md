# The Same Stroke

The thirteenth bundle came up that stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four at the second hour, in the dark, and she was standing four feet off the near end of that table with a lamp standing on the wood a foot in from the edge and the light going a hand's width along it and no further.

That is the whole of what she had of it. A hand's width of light at the near end and twenty-two paces of nothing.

## At The Second Hour

He came up nine hundred and four steps in the dark with forty boards under one arm and she has never once heard him hurry and she did not hear him hurry that morning either.

She could not see him. That is the whole of what that bundle is: forty boards going on a table twenty-two paces off in a room where the light goes a hand's width along the near end of it, and a man she has watched twelve times putting down something he has never once looked at, and not one figure of him available to her at all this morning.

He put them on that table. Ten minutes, from the near end to the far end of it, four to a minute, and she counted every one of them out loud in the dark because she says figures before they are anything else and because there was no one there to say them to.

Then there was a second ten minutes.

She said, out loud, to the dark, in the ordinary voice:

“There is a hand going along that table.”

## What She Was Standing There For

And she said out loud, standing four feet off the near end of that table with the lamp burning a hand's width along the wood and nothing else lit anywhere in the room, what she was standing there for, because she says a thing before it is a line anywhere and because there was a version of this morning in which she stood here and said nothing at all and that version was hers until the second hour.

She said, “I am standing here to watch a room be worked in front of a flame that not one thing in this building asked to be here.”

She said, “I have watched twelve bundles come up that stair and eight of them were in the light of that cage and one of them was in the dark on the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and three of them were with a lamp in the room that came up eight hundred and eighty steps in the hand of a boy of sixteen.”

She said, “And in twelve of them the working was the working. Ten minutes on, thirty for the cloth, forty strokes, four to a minute, same stroke, nothing stops. And I have never once been in this room with both things at once, a bundle and a light that the room did not put there.”

She said, “So I have got no instrument for it. Not because it is hard. Because I have never seen it.”

## Two Tens Of Strokes

She stood four feet off the near end of that table and listened to a hand go along twenty-two paces of wood in the dark, one board at a time, four to a minute, and she counted it.

It came to forty.

And every one of the forty was the same stroke. Not the same loudness, which varied with how near he was along the table, but the same stroke, starting at the same place on each board, going the same way, and taking the same count of time, and she could tell because a sound that begins in the same place on forty different pieces of wood in the dark sounds the same forty times and anything else sounds different at least once.

And it did not stop.

She said, out loud, into the dark:

“Forty. None of them stopped. Not one of them came off a board any slower than any other one, and there was not one of them where the hand went back and did it again.”

And then, at about the second hour and forty minutes, the cloth, which is thirty, and then the stair, and then the nine hundred and four of it going back down in the dark, and the room.

And she did not move for about a count of a hundred and forty after the stair stopped, because she was waiting for the sound of the low door from the inside, which is how a person who has been in a room for three hundred and fifty-three days knows a man has gone out of it.

He had not looked at the lamp.

That is not a figure she can put a number to. It is the whole of what she has got about him and it is worth more than any of the rest of the morning, and it took her about a count of ninety to say it out loud and she said it twice because she could not get it right the first time.

She said, “He came up nine hundred and four steps in the dark with forty boards under one arm and a lamp was burning at the top of that stair and he came up the last four hundred and forty in a room with a flame in it.”

She said, “And he did not look at it. Not at the top of the stair, not in the doorway, not while he was putting forty boards on that table in the dark where a lamp was burning a foot in from his own hand as he walked past it.”

She said, “He has been carrying a thing through this building for four hundred years without a light on it and he has never once been in this room with a light on it, and this morning was the first, and he did not look, and I do not know what that means and I am not going to guess.”

## What She Could Not See

And she stood there and named the limits of what she had, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, because she does not let a thing stand in that room that has not been put into words where it exists.

She said, “I could not see one board. Not one stroke. Not one corner. That lamp went a hand's width along this table and stopped, and everything I have just said is what I heard and not one thing of it is a thing I saw.”

She said, “So I am going to say out loud what I do not know, and it is the corner, and I am not going to stand here and work out which corner it is and then say it as though I had.”

She said, “I do not know what corner. I know the stroke and I know where it starts on the wood and I know it does not stop, and that is the whole of what I have got about forty hands.”

## The Thing She Said Standing

And then she said the thing, and she said it standing, and in the ordinary voice, and she said it to the room.

She said, “A room that does not account for the light in it is a room working to a set of figures it was never given.”

She said it out loud and she heard it go out across twenty-two paces of table and she did not know it would carry that far and she had not thought about the far end of that table when she opened her mouth.

She said, “That is what I have just worked out about this morning. Eighty strokes at four to a minute, the same stroke on forty boards in a row, none of them stopping, and a flame standing a foot in from the near end of this table that I did not put there and he did not put there, that came up eight hundred and eighty steps in the hand of a boy who was not asked.”

She said, “And nothing in what I heard changed. Not the count. Not the pace. Not where the stroke starts. Not one thing in twenty minutes of that room being worked was different because there was a light in it.”

She said, “So either the light is not part of the working, and then the working is being done to a set of figures that know nothing about a lamp, or the light is part of it and I have got the wrong number of things in this room.”

And she waited for about a count of a hundred and forty before she went on, and what she said next came out of her at about half the speed of everything else in that room, and she said it slowly on purpose.

She said, “And I want to put the plain one in after that one, because a room only holds one version of a thing and I have been getting the second one into these sentences all morning.”

She said, “I have been standing four feet off this table since the second hour. That is where I stand. Every night for three hundred and fifty-three days I have stood four feet off the near end of that table and said every figure I have out loud into twenty-two paces of dark, and not one of them has ever gone anywhere, because there was nothing in this room to take them.”

She said, “Tonight there was a flame here and I have been standing four feet off this table with my own light behind me for seven hours, and I said a sentence that went all the way to the end of it.”

She said, “I have been speaking into a dark room for nineteen years. I did not know that was what it was. I have been speaking into a room.”

## The Place She Said It

And then she found out where she had said it.

She stood four feet off the near end of that table with her hand flat on the wood and she looked along twenty-two paces of dark towards the far end of it, where the shelf is and the cage is and the cut in the stone is, and where a person stands on the far side of that shelf and has stood for four hundred years.

She said, out loud, to the dark:

“There is a person at the far end of this table.”

She said, “And I have just said, standing, in the ordinary voice, that this room is working to a set of figures it was never given, and I said it loud enough to go twenty-two paces, and that is where it went.”

She waited for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “Nothing came back. Nothing has ever come back from that end of this table and I did not expect anything to and I want that said because I am about to want something.”

She said, “And I did not mean to say it there.”

She said, “I said it standing four feet off the near end of this table and I said it to a room, and a room has no ears, and there is one thing in this building that has got them, and it is the thing I have never once got a figure of and cannot be looked at again.”

She said, “And I have told it, in its own room, that I know what it is doing.”
