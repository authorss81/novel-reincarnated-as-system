# The Second One About A Stair

Eight days since the ninth stage. Four hundred and thirty-nine days in that room. The cage out two hundred and six days. Right arm open four hundred and forty-six days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and eighty-one days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

She went down the tower's own stair at about the seventh hour of the twenty-seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen, with an empty lamp in her left hand and her right arm open in front of her, and it was in daylight, and it was the first daylight there had been in that stair in four hundred and thirty-nine days.

## Four Openings And The Sun On Them

There are four openings in that stair and at the seventh hour of that morning the sun was in all four of them at an angle that put a long shape of light on the wall opposite each one, and she had seen it from the top of that stair about four times in nineteen years and never once going down.

She said out loud at the first opening, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“There is light on this wall. There has been light on this wall at this hour every morning for nineteen years and I have come up and down eight hundred and eighty steps on four hundred and thirty-nine of them in the dark, and the light has been here the whole time.”

She said, “And I am not going to make anything out of that this morning. I have a lamp to fill and I have said what I came down for.”

She went on down.

## Eight Hundred And Eighty In Daylight

She counted it, and she said out loud that she counted it, and the reason was that she had gone down that stair in the dark twice in nineteen years and not counted either time and had been lighter both times and she was not going to lose a figure on a stair in daylight to get at that again.

She said it out loud at the foot of the first run:

“Eight hundred and eighty. Two runs of four hundred and forty. The first four flights have a rail on one side and the second four have not got one, because the wall is the wall and there was never room for a second one.”

She said, “And that is one stair. The other one is nine hundred and four and it is under that room and it goes down out of a place in the wall behind a shelf and comes back up into the floor of the same room, and the two of them have never been added together in four hundred years and they are not going to be added together on this stair in daylight.”

She went up the second run with her left hand on nothing, because there is nothing on that half, and her knee went on the loose flag at the turn, and she said that out loud too.

## Six, One At A Time

And then she stopped on the fourth landing, where there is a lamp in a cage on the wall that has been out since the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and she put the empty lamp down on the stone, and she said the six terms out loud, standing up, in daylight, one at a time, the way she has said them in every room she has ever said them in since she was nine years old.

She said, “One thing at a time.”

She said, “In daylight.”

She said, “Standing up.”

She said, “You say it out loud as you go. Every line. Before it is a line anywhere else.”

She said, “If there is one I have not heard first then it is not a record, it is a thing you did on your own, and there is no version of this where I find out about it later.”

She said, “And if you put down one word that is not true, I will know it inside a day, and there is no road back for either of us.”

She said, “And it is a record. It is not a hand. You do not decide anything. I decide everything, and the only thing you have is that it is written down.”

And then she said the second one again, on its own, and it came out differently than it had gone in.

## One Thing On A Landing

She said, “In daylight.”

She said that twice and then she stopped talking for about a count of a hundred and forty, standing on a stone landing four hundred and forty steps below a room with a lamp on a table in it.

She said, out loud, into the light coming in at the opening beside her:

“There is no version of a binding that gets done in the dark. A person is put into a column standing up, in front of another person, in daylight, with both of them able to see, because that is the only arrangement in which the person being put there can afterwards say it out loud and have it be a fact about them and not a thing that happened in a room.”

She said, “And I have never worked that out before this morning and it took me one hundred and forty counts on a landing, and I have been saying the second of those six terms every morning since I was nine years old and it has never once been about anything except light.”

She did not move while she was saying it.

She said, “A board came up a stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four with a line on it that says one person is bound to feed the lamp in place of the writer. And it has no name on it and no date on it. And I said out loud in this room this month that a binding that does not name the person can be put on anybody.”

She said, “A binding can only be done in daylight. That is the whole of what that board is and it is not hiding.”

## The Asking

And then she went down the rest of that stair and she found him on the floor at the bottom of the first run with two lamps standing on the boards by his feet and the third one in her hand, and she said the asking out loud before she did it, standing, in the ordinary voice, because a thing gets said before it is a line anywhere.

She said, “I am going to ask you for oil.”

She said, “And I want it said here, on this floor, in daylight, before I open my mouth, that I know what I am doing. On the eleventh of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four I told you that a person who can be given a thing can be given a job, and I said it standing at the top of that stair, and I have not taken it back and I am not taking it back now.”

He said, “There is oil.”

She said, “There is oil.”

She said, “And that is it. That is the whole asking. I have never once asked you for anything in nineteen years and there is not one line in this building that says I am to, and I am asking for it because there is a lamp on a table at the top of eight hundred and eighty steps and it is not the room’s.”

He put oil in it without asking her a single question, and she noticed that he did not ask her a single question, and she said nothing at all about it.

The floor he works on is boards and there is a bench along the wall with the two lamps standing on it and the bag he carries them up in is on the floor by his knee, and there is no window in that tower at that height and the light comes down from four flights above and lands on the boards in one long shape about the length of the bench.

She said, out loud, standing on that floor with the lamp full in her left hand, “I have been in this building four hundred and thirty-nine days and I have been on this floor twice, and both of those times I was going somewhere else.”

She said one thing, at about the ninth hour, on the landing with the dead cage:

“A person who is not asked is a person who gives, and I have been refusing the keeping of a person in a room, and you have not been asked once in nineteen years and you have come up eight hundred and eighty steps in daylight every time the lamp has wanted it.”

She said, “And I am not going to look at those two things next to each other this morning. I have got an empty lamp to take up eight hundred and eighty steps and I have got a morning’s work at the top of it and I have not got anything else.”

## What The Second One Is

And then she went up, with the lamp full and her left hand on the rail for the first four hundred and forty and on nothing for the four hundred and forty after that, and she said the last of it out loud at about the ninth hour of that morning on a stair with the sun on the wall opposite the second opening.

She said, “Here is what the second one is, and I worked it out on a landing and I am saying it going up.”

She said, “It is not a window. It is a witness. It is the arrangement where the thing that is being recorded says it out loud to somebody who can hear it, in a place where both of them can see what is happening, before either of them can say afterwards that it happened in the dark.”

She said, “And there is a stair in this tower of eight hundred and eighty steps in two runs of four hundred and forty and the first four have a rail and the second four have not, and somebody comes up it every time that lamp wants filling, and nobody has asked him to, and he is not bound and not entered and not sent, and there is no line anywhere in this building that says where he goes in the evening.”

She said, “And that is the second one about a stair. I have said it every morning for nineteen years and I have never once said it about that.”

And then she said the last sentence of that morning, standing at the top of it in the dark at the low door, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and she said it into the room and not to the stair:

“And that is the last time any of those six goes out in daylight. There is not one morning left in this building where I say them on a stair, and I am not going to miss it and I am not going to be able to help it.”
