# The Near End

Thirty-nine days since the eighth stage. Four hundred and four days in that room. The cage out a hundred and seventy-one days. Right arm open four hundred and eleven days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and forty-six days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

Nine days to the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen.

There was no person in the room. The lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table with a trimmed wick and the light going a hand’s width along it and no further, and it had been standing there for a night and a morning and would go on standing there until somebody came up eight hundred and eighty steps with oil.

She had been at the near end of that table since the seventh hour and had got nothing done.

## What She Said On The Thirtieth

She said it out loud at about the eighth hour, standing four feet off the near end, in the ordinary voice, and she said it the way she says a thing she has to hear herself say.

She said, “On the thirtieth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four I stood at the near end of this table and I was going to clear it before a bundle came up the stair, and I stopped with my hand on the wood and I did not clear it.”

She said, “I said out loud that I was not clearing it because I did not want a figure of mine in the room when forty of somebody else’s went through it. And I said out loud, standing right here, that that was not a reason. That it was me not wanting a figure of mine to be in a room when forty of somebody else’s went through it.”

She said, “And that was eighty-two days ago and I have not cleared this end of this table on any day since, and I have not said the second half of it once.”

## The Second Half

And then she was quiet for about a count of a hundred and forty, and the light went on doing a hand’s width of the near end of that table, and then she said it, and she said it slowly.

She said, “The second half is this. I do not want to be standing four feet off this table on the morning a bundle comes up that stair.”

She said, “Not because of a man with forty boards under his arm. He came up nine hundred and four steps in the dark on the first of this month and put forty boards on this table in the dark and went back down and he did not look at the lamp, and I have said out loud that I do not know what that means and I am not going to guess.”

She said, “I do not want to be standing here because of what the hand at the far end of this table did on the first of this month, about a count of a hundred and forty after that man was off that stair, when the room had been quiet and there was one stroke that took a count of nine.”

She did not move while she was saying any of that.

She said, “Because that was a room doing arithmetic about a name I said out loud three times, and I was in the room when it did it, standing four feet off this table with a lamp burning a foot in from my own hand, and I had a figure of my own on this table at the time.”

She put her hand flat on the wood.

She said, “That is the whole of it. It is not about the bundle. It is that I did not want to be a thing in a room while a room was working out what to do about me.”

## The Shame

And then she said the part that was worse, and it came out of her at about the speed of the rest of it, and she did not stop it.

She said, “I have got about nineteen years of not wanting to be in rooms. I did not notice that was what I was doing. I thought I was working.”

She said, “And the reason I did not want to be in the room is that it is easier to be outside a thing than inside it, and I have never once in this building been inside anything, and there has never been anything here to be inside, and I have got very good at the outside of a thing over nineteen years.”

She waited about a count of ninety.

She said, “And I am not going to stand here and decide what this building makes of a woman who does not want to be in a room. That is not a thing I do and it is not a thing I have a standing to do at four hundred and four days.”

She said, “What I can do is this table, this morning, this end of it. And it is a thing I can do differently on the first of a month, and I have said that out loud, and the saying is the part that is going to have to hold.”

## Not Clearing It

And then she said what she is not clearing it for, and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and the sentence was worse than the habit had been.

She said, “I am not clearing the near end of this table.”

She said, “And here is the reason and I have been keeping it since the eighteenth of this month and it is the worst thing I have said out loud in this room.”

She said, “If I clear this end of this table then forty boards will go down on bare wood with nothing of mine on it, and then this strip of pale wood goes on the stone or under the table or against my fourth rib, and then I am a person who got it off her own table before a bundle came, and I will have moved it.”

She put two fingers of her left hand on the near end of the strip and did not lift it.

She said, “And there is nowhere in this building to move it to. There is no second copy of it anywhere on this mountain. There is a bag at the bottom of the first four flights with a dish and a clay and a ring and a key and nine inches of hand-made paper in it, and I have not used one of them in four hundred days, because a piece of paper with a sentence on it has nowhere to go.”

She said, “So if I move that strip of pale wood then it is gone out of this room and it does not come back, and I have known that since the eighteenth of this month and I have not said it out loud until now and I am saying it now because on the first of a month I want it said in the room before it is a thing I have done.”

## The Nine Days

And then she said the nine days out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour and ten minutes, because she says a figure before it is anything else and because a woman who has just worked out a shame ought to have a plan against it or the shame is all there is.

She said, “There are nine days between this morning and the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen, and I am not going to spend nine days being ashamed of a habit.”

She said, “The second of this month I put a board face up on this table and said out loud that I was not going to turn it over.”

She said, “The tenth I found a row on a board in that stack, in my own hand, that I did not write, and a second hand underneath it that was right. The fourteenth I checked fourteen mornings of mine against my own mouth and six that I could not, and I wrote nothing.”

The light went a hand’s width along the wood and stopped, and behind it twenty-two paces of the room was gone.

She said, “The fifteenth, the sixteenth and the seventeenth I did the thing I do every morning and nothing else, and I want that said in this room because three days out of nine of nothing is not a failure. It is a woman letting a thing go down on its own.”

She said, “The eighteenth of this month I worked out what a keeper is, in four parts, out loud, standing at this table, and found out that I have got three of them and cannot get the fourth.”

She said, “The nineteenth, the twentieth and the twenty-first I did not work out how a no said in this room becomes a thing that leaves this room. And I am not going to say out loud tonight whether I got as far as wanting to work it out, because if I say that out loud I will work it out in the morning.”

She waited about a count of ninety.

She said, “And the twenty-second is this morning, and this morning I found out why I have not cleared this end of this table since the thirtieth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and twenty-four, and the answer is that I do not want to be a thing in a room while a room is working out what to do about me.”

She said, “And on the first of this month I am going to stand four feet off that table at the second hour and watch forty boards go on it, and I am not going to clear the near end of it first, and that is said in this room nine days early and it is said early on purpose.”

## The Low Doorway

And then she walked twenty-two paces along the near side of that table on her feet with her left hand on the leg of it, over the flag that moves at the nineteenth pace, and went past the far end of it and up to the place in the wall behind the shelf where the low door is, and stood in it.

The stair goes down from there. Nine hundred and four of it, and she is not counting, because she counted it four ways in the light on the twenty-sixth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and the number has not changed and she is not going to stand in that doorway and do a fifth.

The dark went down off her feet and there was nothing in it to see.

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty and said the last of that morning out loud, into the doorway, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “I am not going down here tonight.”

She said, “I have been down here once since the twenty-sixth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, which was eighty-six days ago, and it was with a lamp in my left hand, and I counted the whole of it four ways, and I came back up it with nothing in either hand, and I am not doing it again until I have a reason that is a reason.”

She said, “And in nine days a bundle comes up that stair at the second hour and forty boards go on the top of that table, and the near end of that table is where the lamp stands and where two pieces of wood are lying side by side that a foot long.”

She did not move while she was saying any of that.

She said, “So that is what forty boards are going to go down beside.”

She said, “A lamp a boy of sixteen carried up eight hundred and eighty steps, and a strip of pale wood with a price at the head of it and a five and a seven and an eight in it and eight pairs of cuts with nothing in them, and a board with a name on it in a third hand that came up this same stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four and has not gone back down.”

She said, “And I am not going to clear them off, and I am going to be standing four feet off that table when the forty go on, and I said that out loud at the near end at about the ninth hour on the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four, and it is said in this room now and there is nothing anybody can do with it.”
