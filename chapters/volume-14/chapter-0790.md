# The Far End Of The Table

She told him on the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four at the seventh hour that she was going to carry his lamp to the far end of that table, and he said that was twenty-two paces, and she said it was, and then neither of them said anything for about a count of a hundred and forty.

## Twenty-Two Paces With A Lamp

Twenty-two paces is not a long way to walk in a room with a lamp in your hand on a floor you know.

She had not done it in forty-one days and neither had anybody else, and the difficulty was not the twenty-two paces. It was that she had three fingers and a thumb on the hand that holds things and one arm open at her side since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and it was going to have to hang straight down the whole way or the oil goes out of it.

She said, out loud, standing, “If that lamp goes out on that floor I am going to be standing at the far end of a twenty-two-pace table in the dark with no light in this building and no oil on this mountain, and you are going to be at the near end of it with nothing in your hands. That is the whole of what is at stake and I am saying it before I do it and not after.”

He said, “Then I will hold it.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “Why not.”

She said, “Because then it is your hand and not mine, and if it goes out then it went out on you, and I have already got a figure in this room that is somebody else’s oil.”

And then she took it and went.

The light went along the stone ahead of her and up the leg of that table and stopped at the leg, and the top of that table stayed dark above it, and the twenty-two paces went past her one at a time in the dark with a bar nine feet nine flat against the wall on the left of her and the lamp in her left hand and her right arm out in front of her where it has been out for two hundred and eighty-one days.

And it took her a long time, and she counted the paces out loud as she went because she says figures before they are anything else and because there was a boy of sixteen at the other end of that table who was entitled to hear how long twenty-two paces takes in the dark.

She got to the far end at about the two hundredth count and she put the lamp up on the top of that table, on the shelf in front of the cage, and the light came off the wood and went out into the room and she stood at the far end of it in her own light for the first time since the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four.

## Forty-One Days

The lamp in that cage had been lit every night of four hundred years and she had put the last of it out herself on the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and the light had gone under the cut in the stone and had not come back.

Forty-one days.

That was the whole of what that end of the table had got, and she had spent two hundred and seventy-four days standing four feet off the near end of it describing it out loud to a room, and she had got every single thing about it right.

The shelf was where the shelf was. The cage was where the cage was. The cut in the stone was where the cut was. The table went on past it, which she had said, and there was no second lamp and no second shelf, and the person was standing at the far side of that shelf where that person has stood for four hundred years.

She said out loud, standing in her own light with her arm open at her side:

“Two hundred and seventy-four days and every figure in that room has been right and I have never once stood here to find that out.”

## Corded Twice And Not Knotted

And then she got down onto the stone and went under the far end of that table, which she had done five times in two hundred and seventy-four days, and the lamp standing up on the shelf above her lit the underside of it and the bundle that was under there.

A bundle of papers, corded round twice, and not knotted.

She had said that out loud on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, in the dark, standing at the other end of twenty-two paces of table, and she had said the whole of the rest of that room, and she had been right about all of it.

What she had not said, because she had never seen it, was the dust.

It was on the top of that bundle, a grey film of it with the shape of the cord lying in it, and the cord was under the dust and the dust was over the cord, and she could see that from where she was lying on the stone without moving anything at all.

She said it out loud to the underside of that table, because he was twenty-two paces off at the near end of it and could not have heard a word of it, and because she has never let a thing stand that has not been put into words where it exists.

She said, “The dust is on top of the cord.”

She said, “That means the cord has not moved since the dust went down, and I do not know when the dust went down, and it is the first thing in this room that I have been able to say anything true about that I could not have said standing at the near end in the dark.”

She said, “Four hundred years of a table being worked and a bundle going up and coming down, and the one thing under this end of it has not been touched.”

## Five Times

And she put her arm into it five times in two hundred and seventy-four days, and on the fifth of them her hand had gone as far as the cord.

She said, “I have had my arm into this bundle five times and never pulled on it.”

She said, “And the reason is not that I was afraid of what is in it. I have not worked out what is in it and I am not going to stand here at the seventh hour of the twelfth of a month and make up a reason for nineteen years of my own hands.”

She said, “The reason is that the cord is not knotted. Two turns and not a knot, and that comes apart in two pulls, and if I pull it then this bundle is a thing I have opened, and it is the only thing in this building I have ever been able to open.”

She lay on the stone under that table for a count of a hundred and forty with her right arm out in front of her and the dust on the top of that bundle with the shape of the cord in it.

She said, “And I am not going to do it tonight, and that is a decision and not a refusal, and I want it said out loud in here because the last thing I decided on a stone floor I did not put a time on and it has cost me two days of thinking.”

## Less Than She Wanted

And then she got herself out from under that table, which took her about as long as getting in had, and she picked the lamp up off the shelf and stood at the far end of twenty-two paces of table holding it, and she said the thing that had been going on the whole of the way down there.

She said, “I wanted it to be bigger.”

He said, “Bigger than what.”

She said, “This room. I have got that table down to a bar, a bundle, a strip of pale wood, a lamp in a cage, a shelf and a person, and I have said all of it out loud to a person, and I have been right about all of it, and I have been standing four feet off the near end of it for two hundred and seventy-four days.”

She said, “And I thought that if I put a lamp on the far end of it I would find one more thing. Not a figure. Just one more thing. That is all I went down there for.”

She said, “And there is not one more thing. There is a bundle with a cord on it that comes apart in two pulls and a shelf and a person standing at it, and the far end of this room is exactly the size I have been telling you it is, and I have been telling you the truth about a small place for two hundred and seventy-four days and I did not know until tonight that that was what I was doing.”

And she carried the lamp back.

Twenty-two paces with it, in the dark, one arm open at her side, and the bar against the wall on the left of her the whole way, and she got to the near end of that table and put it down where he had been standing and her knees went while she was putting it down and she sat down on the stone at the near end with the lamp on the wood.

She said, “Four days.”

He said, “To what.”

She said, “To the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four. And then four days past it there is a thing I have to take with a nib in oil light at the near end of this table, and I have never done that either, and I have had two hundred and eighty-one days of my arm open to think about it and I have not got a figure.”
