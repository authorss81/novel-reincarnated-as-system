# What A Lamp Does On A Stair

She took the lamp down the second stair on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four at the seventh hour, four days out from the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, and she told him she was taking it before she took it, and she told him what it was for, and he gave her the lamp without saying anything.

“There is no rail on it.”

“I know there is no rail on it. I have been down it twice. I am telling you because there is a flame in my hand for the first time in my life on a stair that goes down under a mountain.”

“I am coming with you.”

“No.”

“You said no before I had finished.”

“I did, and I have said no to this before tonight and I am not going to be a person who changes it four days out. It takes about a long time and there is a turn in it and I want to do it once.”

## Nine Hundred And Four In Light

She went down the low door and took the first four hundred and forty with her left hand on the rail, and the last four hundred and forty with her hand on the wall, and her knees were still not right from the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and they were worse in the second four than they had been in the first.

And then she was at the head of the second stair with the lamp in her left hand.

Nine hundred and four. She had counted it three times in two hundred and eighty-nine days and she was not going to count it again tonight, because a number you have counted three times does not get truer and it does not get false, and there is nothing on that stair to check it against.

What she was going for was the light.

She got the lamp in front of her and put her right arm out in front of her, open, where it has been out since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and she went down.

The light comes off a lamp on a stair and it goes up onto the ceiling above you and down onto the step under you and it does not go very far in either direction, and what it does is put a hand’s width of stone around her feet and take it away again behind her.

That is the whole of what a lamp does on a stair.

And the thing she found out, going down nine hundred and four steps in oil light for the first time in her life, was that it is enough.

## The Thirty-Second Turn

There is a turn in that stair at the thirty-second step from the bottom.

She had counted it three times and it had always been thirty-two, and at the top of it in the dark on a day in the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fifty-four she had worked out that a turn is where a person walking down with a lamp stops being able to see what is in front of them, because at a turn the flame goes along the wall beside her instead of down the steps under her.

So she stopped at the top of that turn and held the lamp out at the height of her own chest with her left arm straight, so that the light went round the corner before she put her weight on it.

And it went round.

And she went round after it, and the flame leaned over once on the corner and stood up straight on the other side of it, and there is no draught in that stair and there never has been.

And she said out loud, on the other side of it, standing, in the ordinary voice, to a stair:

“There it is.”

## The Bottom

And she got to the bottom.

It is stone, and it is flat, and there is a wall at the end of it, and she did not go past the wall. She stood on it with the lamp held up at about the height of her own chin, and the light went along that floor and up that wall and out of sight, and it was the first light that floor has had since she came up that stair.

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty.

And she said, out loud, to the wall at the foot of that stair, because she has never let a thing stand that has not been put into words where it exists:

“A man comes up this stair at the second hour of the first of every month with forty boards under one arm and a bundle of papers in a cloth under the other, and he has been coming up it for four hundred years, and I have watched him eight times at the top of it.”

She said, “And I have never once been at the bottom of it in light.”

She said, “And he comes up it in the dark, every time, every month, because there has not been a lamp lit in that cage since the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and before that the light did not go down there, it went down to a cut in the stone at the top of it, and he has never once walked up his own stair in front of anything but the dark.”

And the voice in her head said one thing, and it was the first thing he had said in four days that was not about a stage.

He said, “He knows how many steps it is.”

She said, “Yes.”

He said, “He has been coming up it since before you were born and he has never once counted them, and you have counted them three times, and neither of those is a thing you would think of when you are looking at a person with his arm full.”

She said, “No. I would think about the arm.”

And she said, out loud, standing at the bottom of that stair in oil light:

“He carries forty of them up nine hundred and four steps once a month and it has never been in his hands in light and it never will be, and there is not one instrument in this county that would tell him what is on them, and he has never looked at one. I have watched him not look at one eight times.”

## What She Did Not Count

And she went back up.

Nine hundred and four again, and she did not count.

She had gone down that stair three times in two hundred and eighty-nine days and counted it every single one of those, in the dark, on the way down and on the way up, and she had a number and it was a number she trusted, and on the night of the twenty-seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four she carried a lit lamp down nine hundred and four steps and back up nine hundred and four steps and did not count one of them.

She stopped on the second flight of that stair with the lamp on the step beside her and said it out loud to a wall, because she has never let a thing stand that has not been put into words where it exists.

She said, “Nine hundred and four is the number I have and it is the number I will go on having, because I counted it three times in the dark and I checked it against nothing and the nothing did not change it.”

She said, “And tonight there was light on every step of it and I did not count, and that is not because I did not want to. It is because I did not want to find out that the number in the light is a different number, because if it was then I would have to hold two, and I have got one.”

She said, “He counted four stops on that other stair in the dark on the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and I counted three and we agreed on four, and that is the only time in nineteen years that a count of mine has been met by another one and both of them came out.”

She said, “I am not going to have a boy of sixteen count nine hundred and four steps for me in oil and give me a figure, because I do not need one and I have not needed one since I was nine years old, and a second number in my head is a thing I have never once survived.”

## The Same Length

And then she went back up.

Nine hundred and four again, and she did not count, and her right arm was out in front of her the whole way, and at the thirty-second turn from the top she stopped and held the lamp round the corner before she put her weight on it and it went round again.

And she came up out of the top of that stair into the low door with the lamp still burning in her left hand, four flights and eight hundred and eighty steps above that, four miles above a mountain, with her knees gone and her breath at nine.

He was standing at the near end of that table.

He said, “It is still lit.”

She said, “It is still lit.”

He said, “How long was it.”

She said, “Nine hundred and four steps.”

He said, “Then the oil and the stair are the same length.”

She said, “Yes.”

And she put the lamp down on the near end of that table, two inches in from the edge, and sat down on the stone at the near end of it with her arm open at her side and her breath at nine, and she said the rest of that night sitting down, which is the only time she has ever said anything to that room sitting down.

She said, “That is the first fact I have had in this room that came out of something other than counting, and it took nine hundred and four steps to get it, and it is a fact about a lamp and not about me.”

She said, “And it means the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four is reachable. A lamp can go down that stair and come back up. That is all it means and I have had four days of it sitting in my chest.”

She said, “And on that day the man will come up it at the second hour in the dark with forty boards under his arm, and there will be an oil lamp burning at the top of that table with me standing four feet off it, and I will hear the whole of that stair coming up and I will not be able to see one step of it.”

She said, “That is the thing I have not got a figure for, and it is not about oil and it never was.”
