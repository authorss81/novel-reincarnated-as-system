# Four Feet Off The Near End

Twenty-four days since the ninth stage. Four hundred and fifty-five days in that room. Right arm open four hundred and sixty-two days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and ninety-seven days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in the room. The lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table and the light went a hand’s width along it and stopped, and three pieces of wood lay face up about a foot in from the edge of it and one board lay face down about a foot further along.

She said her figures out loud at the seventh hour, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the order she has used since she was nine years old, which is the figures first and the reason after, and she said out loud that she had gone back to that order on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen and had not left it since.

## What The Method Is Made Of

And then she said, at about the seventh hour and twenty minutes, standing four feet off the near end of that table with her left hand on the top of it, what that method is made of, because she had taken it apart in her head three times since the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen and this was the morning it came out.

She said, “Here is the whole of it and it is short and it has held for nineteen years.”

She said, “I stand here. I say a figure out loud, standing up, in the ordinary voice. I say the next figure out loud. And then, after, I say why.”

She said, “And the reason it has held for nineteen years is that there has not been one single thing in it that depends on a person. Not one. I have said every figure I have got in this room at the seventh hour every morning since I was nine years old and if there had not been one other person in this building for the whole nineteen years then not one of those mornings would have gone differently.”

She said, “That is not a lonely thing. I have never found it lonely. It is a thing that does not require an audience, and that is the best thing about it, and it is the only thing in my life that has ever not required anything from me.”

She stood there for about a count of ninety.

She said, “And I took a stage on the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen and it cost me the right to be alone in this room. I said that out loud once, that night, in the ordinary voice, and I am not going to say it again.”

## Three Mornings Of The Doorway

She said, “And here is what is different, and it took me four days to get to it and it is not about the stair and it is not about the light.”

She said, “On the ninth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four, at about the ninth hour, standing exactly where I am standing now, I looked at that doorway.”

She said, “That is the doorway the tower stair comes in at, at the near side of that room, and I have been in this room four hundred and fifty-five days and I have not once looked at it while I was standing at the near end of that table. I have looked at it four times in nineteen years and three of those were when I was coming in or going out.”

She said, “And I looked at it on the ninth, and then on the tenth, and then this morning, and there has not been a person in it on any of those three mornings, and there was not one on the first of them a second sooner than on any other morning.”

She said, “And I caught myself doing it and I have not been able to stop doing it, and that is what I want said at the eighth hour on the thirteenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four: I have started managing a room I am in by myself, and I did not decide to, and it is the third time in nineteen years that something has come up out of this room and rearranged the way I stand at this table.”

She said, “The first two were a fair hand under my own arithmetic and a place at the far side of that table. And this one is smaller than either of them and it is going slower, and it is the same shape.”

## A Second Voice

And then she got to the part that actually frightened her, and she said the fear out loud before she said one word else about it, because she has said since the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen that a thing that is bigger than the room has to be named first.

She said, “I am not frightened of the doorway. I am frightened of what happened on the eleventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four, and I am going to tell you exactly what happened and then tell you what it is and then stop.”

She said, “I said out loud at the ninth hour that morning: *if there were a person in this room right now, I would say that again, differently, and I would want to know whether it was right.*”

She said, “That is what happened. It took about four seconds and then I stopped in the middle of it, standing here, with my hand on that table.”

She said, “And that is not a figure. That is the beginning of a second record.”

She said, “A record kept for somebody is a record with an audience in it, and the whole of what I refused on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen was a person keeping what I say out loud in this room.”

She said, “And if I start saying what I would say to a person then there are two of them in here, one at this end of the table and one nowhere.”

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “And I have caught myself at it four times since. On the eleventh. On the twelfth. This morning at the seventh hour, before I said a single figure. And on the twelfth I got eleven words into it and then stopped and said the figures out loud instead.”

She said, “That is the vertigo. That is the word and I have had it three times in this room and I have got it for it and I am not going to invent a new one.”

She said, “A method that has worked for nineteen years and has never once needed anything from me has gone over, and there is not one instrument in this building that can tell me the difference between me talking to a room and me keeping something for a person, and those are the same act done on purpose and by accident and I cannot see the seam.”

## Four Feet Off, Anyway

And then she said the decision out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, and she said it before she did anything else about the rest of the morning, which is her order and has been her order for nineteen years.

She said, “And the decision is that I go on standing four feet off the near end of that table.”

She put her hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “Not four feet off it somewhere else. This end. In the light. At the seventh hour. Figures first and the reason after. In the ordinary voice. For as long as I am in this room.”

She said, “And I am not going to change the order again. I changed it once, on the sixth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen, because a record is only worth something if somebody else has heard it, and there had not been one other person in this room since I came into it. And I said at the seventh hour on the seventh that I was going back to figures first, and I am not going to take it back.”

She said, “That one day is all I get for changing how I stand at this table. A rule that can be broken once for a reason can be broken twice for the same reason, and I have worked out what that is called and I am not doing it.”

She said, “And I am not going to go down that stair and look at the doorway from the other side. I have been down nine hundred and four steps twice in nineteen years and I am not going down it to have a look at a door.”

## Eighteen Mornings

And then she said the last of that morning out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour and twenty minutes, and she did not fill the count after it.

She said, “Here is the one thing in my morning that is different now and I am going to say it and it is a small thing.”

She said, “At about the ninth hour I stop. That is what I have done every morning since I was nine years old. I say the figures and the reason and then I stop, because there was nothing left to say and I was tired.”

She said, “And these past four days I have stopped at about the ninth hour and then stood there for about a count of ninety doing nothing, and the reason is that I have been finding out whether I want to say something to a person, and there is no person, and that is a different thing from finding out whether I want to say something.”

She said, “And I have not said anything in the count of ninety. Not once in four days. And I am going to keep standing in that count of ninety every morning for as long as it lasts, because the alternative is that I fill it, and I know exactly what I would put in it.”

She left the lamp where it was. The light went a hand’s width along the near end of that table and stopped.

And the doorway at the near side of that room was the same doorway it had been for four hundred and fifty-five days, and there was no light coming through it, and there were eighteen mornings in front of her, and something might come up that stair at the second hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four and she had no idea at all what she would do when it did.
