# Nothing Answers

She stood four feet off the near end of that table on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four at about the seventh hour and waited for something, and there was nothing, and she had known there would be nothing, and she said so out loud at about the fourth count of waiting, which is the rule she has kept since she was nine years old.

The lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the edge and the light went a hand's width along it and stopped, and that hand's width was the same hand's width it had been on the first of that month, and everything else in that room was in the same place it had been in six days ago.

## Six Days

She said the count out loud before she did anything else with it, because she says figures before they are anything else.

She said, “Two thousand six hundred and sixty less two thousand three hundred and one is three hundred and fifty-nine days. That is how long I have been standing in this room. It is sixty-two days since I took the seventh stage, and the lamp has been standing on that table since the seventh of the month before last.”

She said, “And I said a sentence out loud in this room six days ago that I have not been able to put down since, and I am going to go and find out what it cost.”

## What The Six Days Went On Doing

And then she told him what the six days had gone on doing, because he had been up that stair twice in them and had not asked and she wanted it in the room before she said the rest of it.

She said, “I said the thing on the first of that month at about the second hour and forty, and then I lay down on this stone at the near end of this table for about an hour, and then I went down nine hundred and four steps and back up them, and then I did not do anything at all for the rest of that night.”

She said, “And on the second I got under that table with the lamp and went over the stack, and on the third I said the six terms standing in the dark with no lamp in the room at all, which is the last time I have said them with no lamp.”

She said, “And on the fourth and the fifth I did not go near that table and I am not going to tell you why, because the reason is that I did not want to be in the room when it did whatever it was going to do, and not doing that is not something I have done in three hundred and fifty-nine days.”

She said, “And then on the sixth I came up here at the seventh hour and stood four feet off this table and waited for something, and there was nothing, and I said so out loud at about the fourth count of waiting.”

## Nothing Answered

She went and looked at the far end of that table from four feet off the near end of it, which meant she looked into twenty-two paces of dark at a shelf and a cage she could not see.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“There is no change at the far end of this table.”

She said, “There is a shelf and a cage on it and a cut in the stone and a person on the far side of the shelf, and that is what was there six days ago and that is what is there now, and the lamp in that cage has been out for a hundred and twenty-six days and it is out, and there is nothing in this room that has been fed or lit or opened or moved since the first of that month.”

She said, “And there has not been a sound out of that end of this table. Not a word and not a mark and not a change in the pace of anything. I said a sentence across twenty-two paces of table to a working thing and it has taken no notice, and I want that said out loud on the same morning as the waiting, because I am nineteen years old and I waited four counts before I let myself admit I was waiting.”

He said, “What did you expect.”

She said, “Nothing. That is the answer and it is why I did it anyway.”

She said, “And I want the rule said out loud with it, because it is the rule that has run this room for nineteen years and not one person has ever said it to me.

Nothing in this room is required to answer me. Not the person at the far end, not the cage, not the column, not the stair. I said a sentence to a working thing and a working thing does not answer a person, and I knew that before I said it and I knew it while I was saying it.”

## She Goes On Saying It

And then she said what she was going to do about it, standing, in the ordinary voice, and it took her about a hundred and forty counts to get to and she did not dress it up.

She said, “I am going to keep saying things out loud.”

He said, “That is what you already do.”

She said, “It is not. It is what I have always done and this is different and I can tell you the difference and it is about four days old.”

She said, “Everything I have said out loud in this room for nineteen years has been said to a room that had no light and no person in it. I said figures into the dark because the fourth of the six is that a thing gets said before it is a line anywhere, and I have never once been in a position to know whether it was heard.”

She said, “Six days ago there was a flame in this room when I said a sentence. That is the whole difference and it is not a small one, and it means that for the first time in nineteen years a thing I said out loud was said in front of something that could have stopped me.”

She said, “And it did not stop me, and I am still here, and I am going on saying them. Because the other version of this is that I stop, and that I work it out quietly, and that the only record of it is on a strip of pale wood in a room with no light in it that one person on two occasions has been able to read.”

She said, “And a record that has not been heard is not a record. It is a thing that happened.”

She said, “And there is a second reason and I am not proud of it. I like it. I have been in this room three hundred and fifty-nine days saying figures into a dark room and not one thing in this county could have told me whether one of them was a mistake. Six days ago I said something and it went twenty-two paces and came back as silence, and I have been better for it. I have slept. I did not know that I was not sleeping before.”

She said, “That is what a room that has heard you does to a person who has been talking to a wall, and I do not know yet whether it is a good thing, and I am going to keep saying things out loud either way.”

## What It Cost Her

And then she said the cost out loud, and it was short, and she said it standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, with her hand flat on the wood.

She said, “Here is what it cost and I have had six days to get it right.”

She said, “I have lost the right to say one sentence in this room.”

He said, “What sentence.”

She said, “I cannot say out loud, standing in this room, *I do not know what that is for*.”

She said, “I have said that sentence about the place in the far end of this table out loud to a boy of sixteen on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, and again on the seventh of the month before last, and both of those times I said it standing four feet off the near end of a dark table, and there was nothing in the room that had been told anything.”

She said, “Six days ago I told the far end of this table what I know about what it is working to. And a thing that has been told what a room is working to is not a thing I can stand in front of and say *I do not know what that is for*, because it does not know whether I have stopped not knowing.”

She looked along twenty-two paces of dark to where she could not see anything.

She said, “So the one place in this room I could still be honest about in the dark is the one place I can no longer be honest about in the dark. And that is what it cost, and it is not a big price to pay for six words, and I have paid it, and it was cheap, and I am angry about it.”

She said, “And there is a second half of the cost and it is the one I am going to have to live in the dark with.”

She said, “I told that room out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, that I know what it is working to. And I have worked out since, standing on this floor on the fourth night of those six days, that a thing which is working to a set of figures it was never given is a thing that is working without one of the figures in the room.”

She said, “I do not know what that does to a sentence. I do not know whether a thing that is working blind gets a sentence wrong. I have got no figure and I have got no way of getting one and I am not going to stand here at the ninth hour and work out what a room does with a sentence by thinking about it.”

She said, “All I have is this. Every figure I have said out loud in this building for nineteen years went into a room in the dark, and the room was doing whatever it does with a figure in the dark, and that was the whole arrangement, and the arrangement worked, because nothing in this room has ever once been out of date that I could see.”

She said, “And as of six days ago the light in this room is mine and hers, a boy’s, and the arrangement has changed, and I have no figure for what it does to a figure when the light is standing a foot away from where I am saying it.”

She said, “So I am going to keep saying things out loud. In the dark, on a floor, on a landing, four flights down, and in front of a person. All of them. And if one of them is wrong then it is wrong and I will have said it, and that is the only way I have ever found out that a thing was wrong.”

And she put her hand flat on the top of that table beside the lamp, and the light went a hand's width along the wood and stopped, and the strip of pale wood was lying face up on it with a five and a seven in it and nine empty pairs under them.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour:

“And that is the whole of what six days has cost me, and I have been standing here about a count of a hundred and forty trying to make it cost less than that, and it will not.”
