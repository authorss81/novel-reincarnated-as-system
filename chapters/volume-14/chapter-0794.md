# After The Writing

She did not go down that stair on the sixth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and she did not go down it on the fifth either, and that is the first night of the two hundred and ninety-eight days she has been in this room that she has spent every hour of standing.

## The Width Of A Little Finger

She measured her own forearm at the fourth hour, standing at the near end of that table with the lamp on the wood beside her, and she measured it the only way she has ever been able to measure a thing on herself.

She put her left hand flat on her right forearm, palm down, and she put her left little finger across it where the muscle is under the skin, and she looked down at how much of her own finger was still there.

She said it out loud, because she says figures before they are anything else, and it came out in four seconds.

She said, “The width of the little finger on my left hand.”

She said, “That is what a figure costs me. It is not the arm. The arm has been open since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and it is not going to close and it is not the price. The price is that the writing comes off the hand that writes and it comes off it while the nib is still on the wood, and it has come off my hand three times.”

He said, “Three.”

She said, “The twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four, and last night. And it came off a fourth time on the seventeenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four, on a foot of pale wood lying on the top of that table, when a head came down over it and a stroke and a figure went onto it.”

He said, “That was not you.”

She said, “That was not me and my arm went down by the width of the little finger on my left hand anyway, and the hand it came off was not mine, and I have had a hundred and nine days to put a figure on that one and I am not going to tonight.”

## The Figure She Will Not Give Him

And then she went down onto the stone at the near end of that table and put her hand on the strip of pale wood where it was lying face up on the top of the wood, and the lamp was burning a hand’s width along it and there was a seven in the third pair of cuts in her own hand.

And she said the other figure, out loud, in the ordinary voice, standing up.

She said, “Two thousand five hundred and ninety-nine less two thousand two hundred and ninety-four is three hundred and five days, and at seventy-two minutes a day that is fifteen days and six hours, and that is how far out of date the figure at the head of this wood is.”

He said, “You are not going to fix it.”

She said, “I am not going to fix it.”

He said, “You could write it again.”

She said, “I could write the head of that wood again tonight with the nib and the ink and my right hand, and it would be the third time I have put a figure of mine in that column with my own hand and not one of them was a price.”

She said, “And when I do that the figure at the head is one I chose tonight and not one the hand at the far end of this table wrote, and I would have taken the only copy of the price out of this building that I can still read.”

## The Cup

And then she looked at the lamp.

It was standing on the top of that table about a foot in from the near end with the wick turned down, and she looked at it for about a count of ninety and did not say anything.

He said, “You are looking at it.”

She said, “I am.”

He said, “Say what you see.”

She said, “That is a lamp and about a cup and a half was in it when you filled it, and some of it has gone into that room, and I can see what is left of it and that is the whole of what I am going to say about it.”

He said, “How many nights is that.”

She said, “I am not going to tell you.”

He said, “You told me four out of seven.”

She said, “I told you four out of seven on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and that was a number out of my own mouth and not a figure about your oil, and those are two different things and I have worked out which one is which since the twelfth.”

She said, “And the number of nights that lamp is worth is your count and I gave my count away on the twelfth of that month and I am not going to take yours in exchange, and that is why there is no figure.”

He said, “That is a rule.”

She said, “That is a rule and I have not got another one, and a rule that keeps me out of your arithmetic is worth more to me tonight than a correct figure would be.”

## The Question He Asked

And then he asked her a thing that was not a figure and not a term and not a count, and she had it standing in the middle of that room at about the eighth hour with nothing to do with it.

He said, “What did it feel like.”

She said, “I have a figure for it.”

He said, “I did not ask for a figure.”

And she stood there for about a count of ninety and tried three ways of doing it and got a different answer each time and put all three away, because the first one was wrong and the second one was about the cost and the third one was about the room.

Then she said it, standing, in the ordinary voice, with the strip of pale wood lying face up on the top of that table in front of her.

She said, “It felt like being let in.”

And then she said the rest of it, because she cannot let a thing stand in that room that has not been put into words where it exists.

She said, “And that is a word I have not got a use for in nineteen years, and I have been in this building two hundred and ninety-eight days and I have not been let into one thing in it, and last night somebody came up that stair with oil in his hand and turned a wick down with his own fingers and stood four feet off me while I wrote, and that is all that happened.”

She said, “And I do not know who let me in and I have not got a figure for who it was and I am not going to spend a night looking for one.”

## Two Things

And then she sat down on the stone at the near end of that table with her back against the leg of it, and her knees went, and she stayed there, and she said the rest of that morning sitting down.

She said, “There are two things in this room I cannot be hidden from now and I want both of them said out loud on the same morning so that I have got them in one place.”

She said, “The first one is the one I said last night. A person in this building with a flame in his hand can find me anywhere in this room, on any night he chooses, and I have no arrangement of my own body that puts me outside it. That is the cost of the seventh stage and it is paid and it is not going to be softened.”

She said, “The second one is not a cost of the stage. It is a cost of last night being the ninth hour with a person in it.”

He said, “Say it.”

She said, “A figure of mine went out of this room in your voice at about the eleventh hour last night and it is in your head and there is not one thing I can do about it, and it is seven, and I wrote it on a piece of wood that is mine on the top of a table that is not, and I said it out loud twice.”

She said, “And there is not one instrument in this building or in this county that can find out what you are going to do with it, and I am not going to ask you, and I have not got a right to ask you.”

He said, “You said that about the oil.”

She said, “I did. And it is the same sentence and it is a different thing, and I have been in this room two hundred and ninety-eight days and I have never once had a figure leave it, and I do not know which of the two of us is going to care about that more in five years.”

## She Does Not Put It Out

And at about the ninth hour she got herself up off that stone on her left hand and stood four feet off the near end of that table and looked at the lamp on it.

He said, “Are you going to put that out.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “You have never had one of those on this table.”

She said, “I have had one on this table since the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four. It has not stayed on it. It goes down that stair with whoever brought it, every night, and it has been off this table every night since, and on the nights you are not here the near end of this table is a dark room with a strip of pale wood lying face up on it and a seven in the third pair of cuts in it.”

She said, “So the lamp going out is not a thing I have done lately. The lamp going out is what this table does. It is what it has done since the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and it is going to do it in about four minutes.”

He said, “Then leave it.”

And she put her left hand flat on the top of that table, about a foot in from the edge, on the cleared place where the strip of pale wood had been lying and was not lying any more, and she stood with her arm open at her side and her breath at nine and she did not pick the lamp up.

She said, “I am going to have to decide where that lamp lives, and I have not got a figure for it, and I am not going to decide it tonight.”
