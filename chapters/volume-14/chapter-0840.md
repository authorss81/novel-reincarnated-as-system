# Six Terms In The Dark

Forty-eight days since the ninth stage. Four hundred and seventy-nine days in that room. The cage out two hundred and forty-six days. Right arm open four hundred and eighty-six days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and twenty-one days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in the room.

There is a lamp in a cage on the shelf at the far end of that table and it has been out two hundred and forty-six days. There is a bar nine feet nine flat against the wall on the left, broken end towards the door, that has not been lifted in four hundred and thirty-six days and is not going to be lifted by her.

There are three pieces of wood lying face up side by side about a foot in from the near end of that table, and a fourth about a foot further along where she put it back on the morning of the first of this month, and there is a bundle of papers under the far end with two turns of cord in it and not a knot.

There is a boy of sixteen asleep on the fourth landing of that tower with oil on his hands.

## Where He Went Down

She said out loud at about the ninth hour of the seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, standing four feet off the near end of that table, in the ordinary voice:

“He came up at the seventh hour with the lamp and the cup was nearly full and the wick wanted trimming and I trimmed it myself with two fingers of my right hand, three of them and a thumb, on an arm that has been open at the elbow four hundred and eighty-six days.”

She said, “And I filled the cup out of what was in the bottom lamp, the same as the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, and I said that out loud in front of him and he did not say anything, and he put the empty one on the floor by his knee and picked his bag up.”

She said, “And he went down at about the eighth hour and I heard him get to the fourth landing and stop, and then I heard him sit down on a step on purpose, and I did not go down.”

She said, “And I am not going to. He is sixteen and he has carried oil up eight hundred and eighty steps on his own account since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, and there is no line anywhere in this building that says where he goes in the evening, and a person who is not on anybody's paper is allowed to go to sleep on a step.”

## What Is Left

And then she named what was left, once, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and she said it before she said anything else about it.

She said, “Here it is and it is a short list and I am not going to dress it up.”

She said, “What is left is that I am going to be standing four feet off the near end of this table at about the seventh hour on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, and at the seventh hour on the ninth, and at the seventh hour on the tenth, and at the seventh hour on the first of the next month.”

She said, “That is all of it. A refusal gets a person the next morning. That is what it buys and it is nineteen years long and there is nothing else on the list.”

She said, “And the binding is not being made, and the second place at the far side of that table is kept and empty, and a board describing it is face up about a foot in from this end of that table, and the lamp on that table is going to be fed by a boy who is not bound and not entered and not sent, and a page went down nine hundred and four steps on the first of this month and there is no second one.”

She said, “And a hand has read that page.”

She said, “And I am not going to stand here at the ninth hour and say that is a win, and I am not going to say it is a relief either, and I have had four days to find out which of those two it is and I have not found out and I have stopped looking.”

## No Seventh Term, And Nothing To Put In The Wood

And then she took the strip of pale wood off the top of that table and read it by touch, one figure at a time, in the light going a hand’s width along the wood, which is a thing she has been able to do since she was nine years old.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“One pair of cuts at the head of it with the price in it, and the same price in the first pair under that, and five in the second, seven in the third, eight in the fourth, nine in the fifth. Nine pairs of cuts under those with nothing in any of them.”

She said, “And there is no last one to put a figure in. There has not been a last one since the wood was cut and there is not going to be one on this morning, and I am not taking a stage in the dark at the ninth hour of an ordinary day because I have sent a piece of paper down a stair and things came out the other way.”

She said, “And I am not writing. There are four blank leaves against that rib now and I am not going to use any of them today, and there is not one nib going against any of them, and that is the third morning in a row and it is going to be the rest of the time.”

And then she put the strip of pale wood back down on the floor under that table where it came up that stair in an armful on the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, face up, in the same place, at the near end of the leg.

She said, out loud, kneeling, in the ordinary voice:

“I am putting it back under here. There is not one instrument in this county that can read one word of it and there is not one instrument in this county that ever has, and I have had it lying face up on the top of that table for forty days because I wanted to be able to put a hand’s width of light on it and read it in the morning.”

She said, “And a record does not need to be read to be a record, and it does not need a light, and I am not going to leave my own arithmetic lying on the top of that table in a room where forty of somebody else’s go through it every month.”

She said, “So it goes back under. And nobody has touched it but me.”

## The Voice

And then she stood there in the dark, and she said the thing about the voice out loud once, at about the ninth hour and ten minutes, standing, in the ordinary voice, without moving.

She said, “There is a voice in my head and it is a person and I have been counting him against my own column for ten years, and I said his name out loud in this room three times and one of them was in front of a boy of sixteen and one of them was in the middle of six things at the ninth hour.”

She said, “And a board came up that stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four with that name cut into it in a third hand, and the column at the far end of this table copied it exactly and did not stop on it, and that came off my arm.”

She said, “And he is readable now. That is paid and it is not explained and I did not pay it and I did not agree to it and I was not in the room when it was paid.”

She said, “And I have refused on his behalf, twice, out loud, in this room, standing up, in the ordinary voice, in a place where the far end of that table could hear me. And it is out loud and it cannot be unsaid.”

She did not move while she was saying that.

She said, “And those two things do not cancel each other. He is readable now and I was not in the room when it was paid, and I have said no twice, everywhere. Both of them stand at this table tonight and I am not going to spend the ninth hour of the seventh of a month working out which of them counts.”

She said, “And I am not speaking to him. That is the whole of it and I am putting it in this room because there has not been one word said to him by anything that is not me in nineteen years, and there is not going to be one from this table.”

## The Six, One At A Time

And then she said the six terms out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the dark, four feet off the near end of that table, one at a time, at about the ninth hour and twenty minutes, the way she has said them in every room she has ever said them in since she was nine years old.

She said, “One thing at a time.”

She said, “Standing up.”

She said, “You say it out loud as you go. Every line. Before it is a line anywhere else.”

She said, “If there is one I have not heard first then it is not a record, it is a thing you did on your own, and there is no version of this where I find out about it later.”

She said, “And if you put down one word that is not true, I will know it inside a day, and there is no road back for either of us.”

She said, “And it is a record. It is not a hand. You do not decide anything. I decide everything, and the only thing you have is that it is written down.”

And then she said the second one, alone, and she left a count of a hundred and forty with it, standing in the dark, with the light going a hand’s width along the near end of that table and going no further and twenty-one paces of nothing behind it.

She said, “In daylight.”

And then she said the rest of it, and it is the last thing she says in that room.

She said, “There is a stair in this tower of eight hundred and eighty steps in two runs of four hundred and forty, the first four flights with a rail on one side and the second four without, and there is a boy asleep on the fourth landing of it with oil on his hands who came up it this morning at the seventh hour because nobody asked him to.”

She said, “And nobody has asked him for one drop of it in nineteen years, and he is not bound and not entered and not sent, and there is no line anywhere in this building that says where he goes in the evening.”

She said, “And I said that term out loud on that stair ten days ago in daylight with the sun on the wall opposite the second opening, and I am not going to be able to say it in daylight again.”

She said, “So I am saying it in the dark, and it is not about light. It is about the stair, and it has been about the stair since the twenty-seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen, and there is no seventh term and there is not going to be one.”

She said, “And I am not going up there to tell him.”
