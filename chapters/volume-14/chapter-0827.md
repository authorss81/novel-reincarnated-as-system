# The Work Before The Writing

Four hundred and twenty-nine days in that room. The cage out a hundred and ninety-six days. Right arm open four hundred and thirty-six days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and seventy-one days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in the room. The lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table and the light went a hand’s width along it and stopped, and the four pieces of wood lay face up side by side about a foot in from the edge, and one of the four had no name on it at all.

She said her figures out loud at the seventh hour, standing, in the ordinary voice, and then she stood there and did not do one single thing for about a count of ninety, and then she started.

## Four Things She Is Not Going To Do

She said them out loud first, standing four feet off the near end of that table at about the seventh hour and twenty minutes, because she is not going to have a day in this room where somebody watching it could not tell what she did and did not do.

She said, “I am not writing on a leaf today.”

She said, “I am not writing on my own wood.”

She said, “I am not going to answer that board out loud. I said it out loud once and a no said in this room cannot be unsaid and I am not giving it a second answer.”

She said, “And I am not going down that stair with anything in my hand.”

She put her left hand against her fourth rib, where a book of eight leaves has been against that rib for nineteen years, and left it there for about a count of forty and took it off again.

She said, “That is the whole of what I have been doing since I stood up and it has taken me twenty minutes and none of it is the work.”

## What A Refusal Costs Before It Is Refused

And then she got to the work, and it was the thing she had been standing there for since the eighth hour, and it came out of her fast because she had been carrying it since the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four.

She said, “Here is the arithmetic and it took me two days to get it and it is four lines.”

She said, “A refusal costs what the thing costs, and it can only cost that after the thing is on the table in a form you can put your hand on. Until then what you have is a refusal of a thing you have imagined, and an imagined thing cannot be priced, and that is why a person says no out loud to a room before anything has been offered and is heard making a noise.”

She said, “And on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen, at about the ninth hour and ten minutes, I said no in this room to a person keeping what I say out loud in this room. There was no board then. There was a place described as kept and a pair of cuts and a boy of sixteen and a fair hand under my own arithmetic. That was a no to a noise.”

She said, “And then a board came up that stair with a price on it and no name on it and no date on it, and it is thirty days old, and I have not refused it.”

She stopped for about a count of ninety.

She said, “So the thing I am going to have to do is refuse something that has not been refused yet, and that is a different piece of work and I have not done one in nineteen years.”

## What That Costs

She put her hand flat on the top of that table and kept it there.

She said, “And here is what it costs and I worked it out standing here at about the seventh hour and forty minutes and it is one figure with nothing on the other side of it.”

She said, “Refusing in advance means being wrong for a month. It means that from this morning until the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four I will have said no to a thing in my own head, in the dark, standing up, where nothing can check it, and there is not one instrument in this building or in this county that can tell me on any morning in between whether I was right.”

She said, “And if the room comes up that stair on the first of that month with a second board and a second price, then I have been wrong for thirty days and I will have said so out loud in here every morning at the seventh hour, in the ordinary voice, and the ordinary voice is the part that does not come off it.”

She said, “That is the whole of what it costs. It is not courage and it is not the cold. It is thirty days of being the only person in the world who knows what I have refused and cannot prove it.”

## The Intervals She Has

And then she stopped for about a count of a hundred and forty, and then she went and got the wood.

She carried it no distance at all, because it was lying face up on the near end of that table about a foot in from the edge with a price at the head of it and four figures of hers in four pairs of cuts under that and eight pairs of cuts under them with nothing in any of them.

She read it with her thumb rather than her eyes, because she has been able to do that for nineteen years and she did it standing.

She said, out loud, in the ordinary voice:

“One pair of cuts at the head of it with the price in it, and the same price in the first pair under that, and five in the second, seven in the third, eight in the fourth. Eight pairs under those with nothing in any of them.”

She said, “And there is one pair below the eight that I put my thumb on while I was saying that, and it has been empty since the wood was cut.”

And then she stood there and did not say anything else about it, and that was deliberate, and she said out loud that it was deliberate, because she had said a thing about an empty pair of cuts in this room once and had said she would not say it again.

She said, “I am not going to say anything about that pair. Not here, not today, not to a person. It said itself once and that was enough.”

## Sixty-Six And Not Seventy-Four

And then she did the last part of the day, which was arithmetic, and she did it standing up with the wood face up in front of her on the table and the light coming a hand’s width along both of them.

She said, “Four intervals. Fifty-eight, which is a row with one figure in it that stands still. Sixty-three, which is one figure and a look. Seventy-four, which is a row with nothing to take it against but a lamp that is not the room’s, taken on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-eight with the cost of not being able to be hidden by the dark.”

She said, “Sixty-eight, which is two figures and one of them a count that dates, taken on the thirteenth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four.”

She said, “And this one is a fifth thing and I am going to say what it is in the same order I have said the other four, which is as arithmetic and not as a lesson, because if I make it a lesson then I will start choosing intervals out of it instead of out of the room.”

She said, “The price of this one is a refusal.”

She stopped.

She said, “And a refusal is not got in a hurry. I have watched myself refuse things in nineteen years. I refused a lamp on the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and it took me three days. I refused to clear the near end of that table and it took me eighty-two. And I said no to the keeping on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen and I had known for fifty-three days before I said it.”

She said, “And there has not been one in this room in four hundred years. That is not a figure I have out of a book.”

She said, “I have been standing four feet off the near end of that table for four hundred and twenty-nine days and there has not been one. And there is a column at the far end of it with a price at the head of it, and every row in it for four hundred years is a row of amounts, and not one of them is a refusal.”

She put her thumb on the empty pair of cuts again and took it off.

She said, “So it is sixty-six and it is not seventy-four. Seventy-four was for one taken against nothing but a lamp. This one is taken against a refusal that a room has not answered, and a room that has not answered has not finished, and you do not take the figure out of the middle of that.”

## The Interval She Has Had And The One She Is Choosing

And then she said the last of the day out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, and she said the two figures side by side and left them there.

She said, “Sixty-four days is what I have had this morning.”

She said, “Sixty-six is what I am choosing, and it is two days on from this, and it is not going to be taken early and it is not going to be taken late, and I am not going to stand here at the ninth hour on a morning in between and talk myself into a different one.”

She squared the strip of pale wood up with two fingers of her left hand and left the other three pieces of wood where they lay.

She said, “And I am not going to write the decision down anywhere, because a decision that goes on a board in this building is a row under a price, and that is a column of amounts, and I have said that out loud in this room twice and I have said it on purpose.”

She left the lamp where it was. The light went a hand’s width along the near end of that table and stopped.

And there were five blank leaves against her fourth rib that were still blank, and there was a piece of paper with no name and no date on it lying face up about a foot in from the edge of that table, and there was one pair of cuts with nothing in it that she had put her thumb on twice and said nothing about, and the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen was two days away.
