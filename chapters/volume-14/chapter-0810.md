# The Third Hand

A bundle came up the second stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four at the second hour, and it was the fourteenth of them, and she was standing four feet off the near end of that table with a lamp standing on the wood a foot in from the edge and the light going a hand's width along it and no further, and she heard the whole of it in the dark.

Eighteen days since the eighth stage. Three hundred and eighty-three days in that room. The cage out a hundred and fifty days. Right arm open three hundred and ninety days, and it was not going to close.

## The Second Hour

Nine hundred and four steps coming up, and the step at the turn did not change, and forty boards going on that table in ten minutes at four to a minute, and then thirty for the cloth.

And then a hand went along the wood, forty strokes, four to a minute, the same stroke, starting in the same place on every board, and not one of them stopped.

And then it went back down nine hundred and four steps in the dark and did not say one word.

And then the room was quiet for about a count of a hundred and forty.

And then there was a forty-first stroke.

## The Forty-First

She stood four feet off the near end of that table and did not move for about a count of ninety and then she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, into twenty-two paces of dark.

She said, “There was one more.”

She said, “One stroke, on one board, and it did not come up that stair and it did not go down it. It came after the man was off the stair and it was the same stroke and it started in the same place and it was not four to a minute, it was one, and it lasted about a count of nine.”

She said, “And whatever it was on, it was the last thing to be touched in that room at the second hour of a month for four hundred years, and the only thing in this room that touched it was a room.”

## What The Eighteen Days Went On Doing

And she said that out loud at about the fourth hour, standing four feet off the near end of that table with the lamp on the wood and the light going a hand's width along it and no further, because she has eighteen days of it to put into words and she is going to put it in while it is still exactly eighteen.

She said, “I did nothing for nine days that I am not going to account for, and then I did one thing.”

She said, “On the fourteenth I said the cost out loud. I cannot be read where I stand. And then I stopped saying anything about it, because a cost that gets said again is a cost that is being paid in instalments, and this one was paid whole.”

She said, “And for nine days I did the thing I do every morning in this room, which is say every figure I have got out loud once so that none of them is only in my head. And I said eight out loud about two hundred counts on every one of those nine mornings, and the price out loud, and the days, and the number of empty pairs, and on the eighteenth day I said eight one hundred and forty times.”

She said, “And on the same nine days I did not once go and look at the far end of that table.”

She said, “That is not discipline. I have been calling it discipline for nine days. It is that I do not go to the far end of this table with a lamp in my hand and find out what it is doing, and I am not going to find out tonight either until I have to.”

## Walking It With A Lamp

And at about the ninth hour she carried the lamp twenty-two paces and put it down about four feet short of the far end, and the light came down flat onto the wood, and she looked at the boards that had come up that morning.

She said, out loud, in the ordinary voice:

“There is a board on this table that has not got cuts on it.”

She said, “Thirty-nine of the forty have got cuts on them. And this one has got a hand on it, and the hand is not the hand at the far end of this table and it is not mine.”

## What Is On It

And she got down onto the stone first, and went under the far end of that table on her hands and her knees, so that she would be looking at the wood of that table edge on and not across twenty feet of it, and she came back up and picked the lamp up again and stood over it.

She said the whole of it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, because the fourth of the six is that a thing gets said before it is a line anywhere, and because it is the only thing in that room she cannot see from the near end and she was not going to look at it once and let it be looked at and never heard.

She said, “It is a name.”

She said, “It is the name I have been carrying against my ribs for ten years and it is on a board a foot long and two inches across in a hand that has never been in this room.”

She said, “It is not my hand. My hand has three fingers and a thumb on it and the writing on this is not written by three fingers and a thumb. It is not the hand at the far end of this table either. That hand has written four hundred years of strokes into that column and every one of them starts in the same place and goes the same way and this is not one of them.”

She said, “It is a third hand.”

And under the name there was a pair of cuts, and nothing in them.

She said that out loud too, standing, at the far end of that table with the light a foot in from her hand.

She said, “There is a pair of cuts under the name and there is nothing in them, and they were not cut today. They are worn round at the corners and they are the same size as the ones on my own wood.”

She said, “And they are not empty because there is nothing to put in them yet. They are empty because they have been empty longer than the name has been on this board.”

## What The Room Did To It

And she stood there and looked along the top of that table at about four feet off, into the light, and the column was there at the far side of the shelf where the column has always been.

She said, out loud, in the ordinary voice:

“It has copied it.”

She said, “There is the name in the column at the far end of this table in the room's own hand, stroke for stroke, letter for letter, and the hand at the far end of this table put it there at about the second hour and twenty of the morning with one stroke that took a count of nine, and it did not stop on it.”

She said, “It did not stop on it. That is what I want said first and I want it said out loud, because everything I have ever been told about a thing that works to a set of figures is that it stops on the figures that are wrong.”

She said, “It copied it exactly and it does not know whose words it is writing.”

## What She Did Not Do

And she stood at the far end of that table for about a count of a hundred and forty and she did not tear it up, and she said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, that she was not going to tear it up.

She said, “I am not tearing it up. I want that said in this room while I am standing over it, because I have had my arm into that bundle five times in nineteen years and never pulled on it, and there was a version of this morning in which I picked this board up off that table and put it in the fire, and that version is still available to me and I am not going to take it.”

She said, “I am not tearing it up because there is no fire in this room, and because it has already been copied, and because a board that comes up that stair does not go back down it. That is the arrangement. Forty boards and a cloth go down and nothing goes down that came up, and I have watched that a dozen times and once more tonight, and I have never once had a reason to be glad of it.”

And then she read the whole of it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, from four feet off, with the light a hand's width along the wood and the name already in the column at the far end of the room.

## What Eighteen Days Bought

She said, out loud, to the dark past the light:

“I said that name out loud in this room three times. On the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, at the tenth hour. On the fifth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, in the middle of six things I was saying. And on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, in front of a boy of sixteen, when I told him there was a voice in my head and gave him its name.”

She said, “It has been written down. Not by me and not by the hand at the far end of this table. By a third, eighteen days after the eighth stage, on a board a foot long and two inches across, with a pair of cuts under it that have been worn round and are the same size as the ones on my own wood.”

She said, “He is readable now. By the thing that made him. And that cannot be put back.”

She said, “And I am not going to work out tonight what a thing that reads does with a name. I have got no figure for it and I am not going to make one up standing in the dark at the far end of a table with a lamp I cannot hold, and I have been in this room three hundred and eighty-three days and I have never yet been wrong about a thing in it in a way that mattered.”

She said, “And I am not frightened. I want that said too, because it is going to look like fear to anybody who has not watched me and I would rather it was on the page.”

She said, “This is not fear. Fear is about the next one. This is about one that is already here and already copied and cannot be unread.”

She said, “And I have got a strip of pale wood lying face up at the near end of this table with a price at the head of it and a five and a seven and an eight in three pairs of cuts, and one of those figures went into this room eighteen days ago, and I do not know what any of them is worth, and there is a name at the far end of this table now that somebody else has paid for.”
