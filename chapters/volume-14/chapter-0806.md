# Two Turns And Not A Knot

She went to the far end of that table on the tenth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four at about the eighth hour to decide about the bundle, and she decided, and the deciding took her about a count of ninety and the rest of the night she did not get back.

She carried the lamp twenty-two paces and put it down about four feet short of the far end and lay down on the stone under that end of the table with the light coming up onto the underside of the boards, and there was the bundle where it has been for three hundred and sixty-two days.

## What Is There

She said it out loud once, flat, in the ordinary voice, to the underside of that table, because she says a thing before it is a line anywhere and because she is not going to say it five more times over five more nights.

She said, “Two turns and not a knot. The cord has not moved and the dust is on top of it in the shape of the cord. I have had my arm in to the cord five times in nineteen years and I have not pulled on it.”

And then she lay there on the stone with her right arm out in front of her and said nothing else about it at all.

## What Pulling Would Mean

She said, out loud, after about a count of a hundred and forty:

“The reason is not that I am afraid of what is in it. I have not worked out what is in it and I am not going to stand on a stone floor at the eighth hour of a night and make up a reason for nineteen years of my own hands.”

She said, “The reason is two turns and not a knot. That comes apart in two pulls. And if I pull it then this bundle is a thing I have opened, and it is the only thing in this building I have ever been able to open, and once that is true it is true for the rest of my life and I cannot get back to before it.”

She said, “And I have not been able to work out why I want it badly enough to have thought about it four hundred times and never once on the second of a month when there was a lamp in this room.”

## The Fifth Time

And while she lay there she went over the five times, out loud, one after another, because she does not let a thing stand that has not been put into words where it exists and because she had four hundred and forty-one days of not saying it.

She said, “The first was the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four and my hand went to the cord and came back.”

She said, “The second was the third of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fifty-four and my hand went past the cord because I was feeling for the bottom of it.”

She said, “The third was the ninth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and sixty-four and I had a figure to put in that bundle and I did not put it in and I went away and I have not been back able to think about that one.”

She said, “The fourth was the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and I had my arm in to the cord when the lamp went out, which is the only time I have ever been in this room with my arm in it and nothing to see by.”

She said, “And the fifth was the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four, on this floor, with a lamp I had carried twenty-two paces, with the dust on the top of it and the shape of the cord in the dust, and I said the word tonight out loud on purpose.”

She said, “Five, in nineteen years, and every one of them on a different day of a different month, and I have never once done it on the second of a month when there was a light in this room.”

She said, “And I have done all five of them with no one standing at this end of this table except on the last two, and the last two are the only two where I could have been asked to stop.”

## What She Was Afraid Was Not Fear

And lying there on the stone with her right arm out in front of her and the underside of that table above her, she worked out one more thing, out loud, and it took her about two hundred counts and it is the only thing she said on that floor that was not about the bundle.

She said, “I want it said that the thing I am frightened of is not what is in it.”

She said, “It is what I will be afterwards. In nineteen years I have not been a person who has opened anything. I have been a person who counts things and says them out loud and does not touch them, and that has been a life, and it has been mine, and I have got the whole of it in a strip of pale wood a foot long.”

She said, “And if I open it then I am a person who opened a thing in this building, and there is not one figure anywhere in this county that can tell me what the difference is between that and a hand that goes along a column four to a minute and does not stop.”

She said, “And I would rather not be the first one in four hundred years who found out by doing it.”

She said, “That is the whole reason and it has been the whole reason the whole time and it took me nineteen years to say it out loud, and it is not about the bundle at all.”

## Tonight

And then she said the word out loud, standing, at about the ninth hour, in the ordinary voice, with her hand flat on the underside of that table.

She said, “Tonight.”

He said, “You said that before.”

She said, “I said it on the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four on this floor with a lamp I had carried twenty-two paces, and I said it because I had decided a thing with no date on it and it had cost me two days of thinking.”

She said, “So I am going to say it again tonight, because tonight is the tenth of this month and a bundle came up this table at the second hour yesterday with a flame standing a foot in from the near end of it, and I want the word in the room on the night when I have had light to look at this by.”

She said, “And I am not opening it.”

He said, “Then why did you come down here.”

She said, “To say the word.”

She said, “And then to lie here for about a count of a hundred and forty doing nothing, which is the part that costs, and which I am not going to do in front of a person, and I am doing it in front of a person.”

And she got herself out from under that table on her hands and her knees and went and sat down on the stone with her back against the leg of it at the far end, eight feet from where he was standing, and she put her right arm across her knees and she left the lamp on the top of the table where she had put it and the light went a hand's width along the wood there and stopped.

And she did nothing at all for about a count of a hundred and forty, which is the discipline part and which costs her every time and which she has never once said out loud before tonight.

She said, from the stone, without getting up:

“That is the whole of what I came down here for. A hundred and forty counts of not doing it. And I have never in nineteen years spent a hundred and forty counts on not doing a thing, and I have got very good at spending a hundred and forty counts on doing a thing, and those are not the same skill and I did not know I had the second one until tonight.”

## What It Costs

She said it out loud from the stone floor, to the underside of the table, in the ordinary voice, and it was the shortest thing she said all night.

She said, “It costs me the night.”

She said, “That is all it costs. It costs me the night I was going to use, and there is one of those every night and I am standing in one of them choosing not to do the one thing in this building I could do.”

She said, “And a boy of sixteen is standing at the near end of this table eight feet above my head with oil on his fingers and there is a lamp in this building and he is the only person above the line, and there is not one instruction in this room that says he may not come to this end of this table.”

She said, “And I have not asked him not to.”

She lay there and let it sit for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “And that is what is underneath it, and I am not going to say it any plainer than that, because I said a thing on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four about a boy of sixteen not being sent and not being entered and not having a name of his on anything in this room, and I meant all three of those, and I am the only person who checks that they are still true.”

She said, “And a bundle under a table that a person of sixteen can reach is the first thing in this building that would put a name of his on something, and it would not be his name, it would be a handprint of his on a piece of cord, and there is not one instrument in this county that could tell me who had opened it first.”

And that was the end of the decision, and she lay there and let it sit, and then she said the last of that night out loud from the stone, with her hand flat on the underside of that table, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “Something else is in this room.”
