# Words And Not Cuts

She went down the first four hundred and forty of the tower's own stair on the twentieth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four at the seventh hour with a lamp in her left hand, because she had got to the end of what she knew about a room and the room was only the top of eight flights, and she wanted to know whether there was anywhere in the whole of that building a lamp could be put down on and left.

The light went along the stone about her own feet and went away again behind her as she came down, which she had worked out once already on the second stair and had said out loud and was not going to say a second time.

## Nothing To Put It Down On

She got down the first two hundred and twenty on her left hand on the rail and then she let go of the rail for the next two hundred and twenty, because a lamp in a hand and a rail in a hand is one too many hands.

Her knees went twice on that half and once of them was on the landing where the rail stops, and she sat down on the step for about a count of a hundred and forty with the lamp beside her on the stone and the light going down the stair away from her.

She got to the fourth landing where the rail stops and she went the rest of it with her hand on the wall, and at the bottom of the first four flights there is a floor and a bag and that is all there is at the bottom of the first four flights.

She put the lamp down on the stone and it stood there on its own base and the light went out from it about as far as it had gone on the table and it did not go any further for being on a floor instead of on wood.

She looked at it.

She said, out loud, to the floor, in the ordinary voice:

“There is nowhere.”

She said, “There is nothing on this floor. There is a bag with a dish in it and a clay and a ring and a key to a drawer I have measured and not opened and nine inches of hand-made paper that I have carried since I was nine years old. And there is not one ledge, not one hook, not one shelf, not one nail in this wall, and I have been up and down it since I got into this building and I would have known.”

She picked the lamp up again and stood with it.

She said, “That is the answer and I did not want it in this shape. I wanted to come down four flights and find something that had been there the whole time that a lamp could go on, and instead there is a floor.”

## The Bag

And then she got down onto the floor at the bottom of those four flights with the lamp and put it down on the stone where it would stand, and she took out the only thing in that bag that could have held a sentence.

There is a bag at the bottom of the first four flights and it has a dish in it and a clay and a ring and a key to a drawer in another building that she measured on the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fifty-four and did not open, and nine inches of hand-made paper that she has carried since she was nine years old.

She took the nine inches out and held it up into the light and the light went a hand's width and the paper is longer than that and the far end of it was not lit.

She said, out loud, crouching on the stone floor of the first four flights with the paper in her left hand:

“There are nine inches of paper in this bag and about six of them are in the light and I have carried it since I was nine years old and I have used about an inch of it on a shelf that has had nothing on it since I got in.”

She said, “And I am not going to use any more of it tonight, because if I write this arrangement on this paper then I have a piece of paper with an arrangement on it, and where does a piece of paper with an arrangement on it go. There is no one above the line who has told me to produce one. There is a stack of forty boards under a table and there is nothing in this building that a piece of paper could be handed to.”

She folded it in three along the folds that were already in it and put it back in the bag and pushed the bag under the leg of the stair with her foot.

She said, “There is a board under that table. That is worse and it is the only place there is. And I am going to go up four flights to use it, which is the most ridiculous thing I have done in this building.”

## What She Said On The Seventh

And at about the eighth hour on the fourth landing she said the thing out loud that had been going down the stair in front of her, because she says a thing before it is a line anywhere and because she cannot let a thing stand that has not been put into words where it exists.

She said, “There is not one thing in this building that makes light out of anything that is not already burning. Not a lamp in a cage, not a candle, not a tinder, not a flint, not a fire, nothing. There has not been one thing in this county that could put the light back in that cage and I have known that since the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four.”

She said, “And I said it out loud to a boy of sixteen on the seventh of that month, standing, in the ordinary voice, with the oil going from one lamp into another, and I said *there is not one instrument in this building that can make light out of anything that is not already burning*, and I said it to be true, and it was true, and it is the reason there is a flame on that table at all.”

She said, “And I cannot unsay it.”

She said, “So the answer to the question I went down those four flights with is that the lamp in that room is kept alive by a person, and there is one person above the line in that building, and he is sixteen, and he is not bound and not entered and not sent, and there is not one word in this building with any authority over what he does with an evening.”

She said, “And I cannot ask him for oil.”

She stopped on the landing for about a count of ninety.

She said, “That is the whole of it and it took me four flights to say and it could have taken me a count of nine standing at the near end of that table. I do not have a way of asking for a thing and I have known that since I was nine years old and I have never tested it on a person.”

## Words And Not Cuts

And then she went back up the first four hundred and forty with the lamp and went in under the middle of that table with it held low so that the light came off the stone and up the underside of the boards and not onto the tops of them.

There were forty boards face up in that stack and she had put her hand on all forty of them on the second of a month since the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four.

She took the top one out.

It is a board about a foot long and two inches across and about the thickness of two fingers, and on the wood it is cuts and not letters, and she found that out on a stone floor on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four with a boy reading it off the end of her own hand.

And she turned the nib over in her right hand, three fingers and a thumb on an arm that has been open at the elbow since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and she put the point of it on that wood and she did not cut.

She wrote.

## What She Wrote On It

She wrote words. That is what she wants said first, because a thing made of cuts cannot carry a sentence and she had known that for five days and had not yet done anything about it.

She wrote, with the nib and the ink against her fourth rib, on a board out of that stack, in her own hand:

*Three lamps. He is on three nights out of six. Two hours on each. What is standing on the table from the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four came out of the lamp he carried up that night and the lamp he carried up is going back down it empty. There is no oil in that building and none on this mountain. I did not ask for it. I took it.*

And her right forearm went down by the width of the little finger on her left hand while the nib was still on the wood, which is what a figure costs and is not a price and is not the arm, and the arm has been open for three hundred and nineteen days.

She put the nib back against her fourth rib and read the whole of it out loud in the ordinary voice before she turned the board over.

## She Cannot Tell Which One

And then she sat on the stone at the near end of that table with that board in her left hand for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, out loud, to the dark:

“There is a board in this building with my own handwriting on it, in words, not cuts, and I am not going to send it up that stair.”

She said, “I have thought about it for about a count of a hundred and forty and I am not going to send it, and here is why. If I send it up that stair then it goes with the next bundle, and the next bundle is forty boards and a cloth and it comes up at the second hour and a man puts it on that table and goes down without looking at one of them.”

She said, “And if I send it up then I have said one thing to that room in nineteen years that it did not already know, and it will write it into the column the same as the other two, and I will not know which of the three it was.”

She said, “And there is a thing I have not said out loud in this building and I am saying it at about the ninth hour on the twentieth of that month with a lamp on a table in a room that is four feet deep in the dark.

I cannot be given a thing. That has been true since I was nine years old and it has never once been tested on a person, and I have been standing in this room for three hundred and twelve days wanting to hand somebody something and there has not been one thing in it I was allowed to hand over.”

She put the board back into the stack, face up, on the top of it, in the same order it was in.

And she put her hand on all forty of them, three fingers and a thumb on the edges, from the near end to the far end of that stack, twice, and she could not tell which one it was, and she did not expect to, and she said that out loud.
