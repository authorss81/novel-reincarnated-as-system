# One Day In Twenty

Thirty-one days since the eighth stage. Three hundred and ninety-six days in that room. The cage out a hundred and sixty-three days. Right arm open four hundred and three days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and thirty-eight days lighter at this morning and not one going back. And the figures at the far end of that table are twenty days, three hours and thirty-six minutes old, which is the only figure she has never once got wrong.

She said all of it out loud at about the seventh hour, standing four feet off the near end of that table, with the lamp on the wood a foot in from the edge and the light going a hand’s width along it and no further.

And then she went and did the thing that cost her, which was to find out whether a correction she cannot check is a correction.

## How She Found Out

She took twenty boards off the top of that stack over four mornings, five a morning, and she carried them to the near end one at a time and read them in the light, and she put every one of them back the way she had found it, and then she wrote out nothing at all and remembered.

She said out loud how it worked, standing, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “I say a figure every morning at about the seventh hour. Most mornings it is the price at the head of that column and the days and the number of empty pairs, and the price is the same this morning and this evening.”

She said, “One morning in twenty I say what the price ought to be today. Ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes, and however many days it has been since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four. And I have said that figure out loud at that table nineteen years and not one instrument in this building has ever taken it.”

She said, “And one morning in twenty I am a day behind. Not wrong about anything. A day behind. There is a day in that number that I have lost between the seventh hour and the column, and I have never known which morning it was, because a count that is out of date is out of date and there is no day on which it comes right again.”

She waited about a count of ninety.

She said, “And that is the figure the fair hand has got under mine.”

## Why It Is A Correction And Not A Copy

She said, “A copy is the easy thing. A copy puts down what I said and that is what a room does, and I have watched a room do it four hundred years’ worth of one stroke starting in the same place.”

She said, “This is not that. On every one of the twenty boards I have read this month there is my row and there is a second row under it, and on nineteen of the twenty the second row is not the same figure as mine. One of the twenty came back with a second row under mine that says the figure I said, and that is the one board out of twenty where I cannot tell a correction from a copy.”

The light went a hand’s width along the wood and stopped.

She said, “And it does not begin at the end of my row. On each of those nineteen it begins four figures back from the head of that board and runs with the rest of it to the end, and under the four figures before that there is nothing but my own hand going back as far as it goes.”

She said, “So it is not old and it is not four hundred years of somebody tidying up after me. There is one place on each of those boards where it starts and the place is four figures from the head, and I put the first of those boards back on the top of that stack on the tenth of this month.”

She said, “It differs from mine every time and it differs the same way. Every time it is one day on from what I said. Not a correction of a figure that is wrong by a lot, not a figure rebuilt. One day. On nineteen out of nineteen.”

She said, “And I said that out loud because a thing you have only seen once is a thing you do not know. Fourteen of those twenty I have been able to check and it is right on all fourteen, and I am going to tell you how I checked them and it is not with an instrument.”

## Fourteen And Six

She said, “I checked them against my own mouth.”

The light went a hand’s width along that wood and stopped there, and twenty-two paces of the room behind it was gone.

She said, “I said a figure out loud at this table on a morning and it went twenty-two paces and I could not see where it went. And then some days afterwards a fair hand has written under it the figure I should have said that morning, which is the figure I say out loud the next morning when I get it right, and I have said it right the next morning, and both of those are in my head and not one of them is in this building.”

She said, “Fourteen boards. Fourteen mornings where I can stand at the near end of this table and say the figure I said that morning and the figure the fair hand put under it, and they are the same figure.”

She said, “And the one that came back the same as me is not evidence either way. There are two ways of writing a figure somebody else has said and I cannot tell them apart from the top of a stack in a dark room with a lamp a foot in from my own hand.”

She did not fill the count with anything.

She said, “And six of the twenty I cannot check. Six mornings back, and I can tell you what day each one was and I cannot tell you what I said, because saying it is not the same as keeping it and I have found out that in nineteen years the only figure I have ever kept is one I have written on my own wood.”

She said, “So it is a correction. It is not a copy and it is not a coincidence and it is right on every one of the fourteen I can put my mouth against, and I cannot put my mouth against the other six and I am not going to build an instrument out of the first one that has ever been right about me.”

## Writing Nothing

And then she had to decide what to do about it, and she decided it standing four feet off the near end of that table with the lamp burning a hand’s width along the wood, and she said the decision out loud before she did it, which is the rule and the rule is older than anything in this room.

She said, “I am not going to write anything about this.”

She said, “There is a strip of pale wood on this table with a price at the head of it and a five and a seven and an eight in it and eight pairs of cuts under that with nothing in them, and it came up that stair in an armful on the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four. It is the only record of my own hand in this building and it is the only one there will be.”

She put her left hand flat on the wood beside it.

She said, “I could put a figure against this. I could put a figure in an empty pair and say out loud first that I was putting it there, and I would have obeyed the rule exactly and there would be a line in that wood about a fair hand under a column.”

She said, “And it would be an amount. A column with a price at the head of it is a column of amounts and not a column of what happened, and I worked that out at this table on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, and I do not know what a fair hand correcting my arithmetic is worth, and I am not going to find out by putting a number on it.”

She took her hand off it.

She said, “So there is nothing written. There is a fair row under my row on some boards in a stack under that table and there is no line anywhere in this building that says so.”

## What She Felt

And then she was still standing there and she did not go and that was the whole of what was wrong with the morning.

She said, out loud, to herself, in the ordinary voice, and she said it at about the eighth hour and forty minutes and she said it once:

“I want it.”

She stood there.

She said, “I have wanted somebody to make that figure right since I was nine years old. I have never once had it. I have had a hand that copies and does not stop and a column that is out of date for four hundred years and a strip of wood two inches across that is the only place on this earth where a figure of mine is written down at all.”

She said, “And I do not want it.”

She said, “I want it and I do not want it and both of those are in this room at the same time and they are about the same thing.”

She did not move while she was working through them.

She said, “There is a name on a board at the far end of this table in a third hand and that hand has been in this room thirteen days and the fair hand is the hand that has been putting a day on my arithmetic, and I do not know that they are the same and I have not asked and I am not going to ask.”

She said, “And I am not going to enjoy a thing I cannot account for. That is not a virtue. It is what I have got instead of a person to ask.”

## The Thing She Would Have To Give Up

And then she worked out the last thing about it, and she said it out loud standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, and it is the sentence she has been walking towards for four days and had no idea of that when she said it.

She said, “Here is what I would have to give up for it to stop.”

She said, “Every morning at about the seventh hour I stand four feet off the near end of this table and I say a figure out loud in the ordinary voice. That is the whole of it. There is no board, no instrument, no hand, no room, no person. It is a woman of nineteen standing in the dark saying a number so that the number exists somewhere.”

She said, “And that is the only thing I have ever done in this room that is mine. I have not written it down, I have not given it to anybody, and it goes into a room with a column in it, and on the thirteenth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four I took the eighth stage, and the cost of that was named out loud in this room the same day, and it has not been taken back.”

The light went on doing a hand’s width of the near end of that table and stopping where it always stopped.

She said, “So there is no version of me standing four feet off that table that is not a line in a column.”

She said, “And if I stop, the rows stop. And if the rows stop then there is nothing for a fair hand to put a day on, and in about a count of a hundred and forty the fair hand stops too, and this room goes back to being a room with one hand in it and one column and nothing of mine in it anywhere.”

She said, “And I have just worked out that the whole of what I have got in this building is the standing up and the saying, and I have been saying it out loud as a rule since I was nine years old and I did not know until this morning that it was the only thing.”

She left the lamp standing where it was.

She said, “I am not giving it up.”
