# How A Room Names A Person

Thirteen days since the ninth stage. Four hundred and forty-four days in that room. The cage out two hundred and eleven days. Right arm open four hundred and fifty-one days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and eighty-six days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in the room. The lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table and the light went a hand’s width along it and stopped.

She said her figures out loud at the seventh hour, standing, in the ordinary voice, and then she went to work on it, and it was the thing she had been carrying since the ninth hour on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four.

## Four Hundred And Forty-Four Days Without One

She said it out loud first, standing four feet off the near end of that table at about the seventh hour and twenty minutes, and she said it in the plain version, because she is at the stage of a thing where the long version stops working.

She said, “I have been in this room four hundred and forty-four days and nothing in it has named me.”

She said, “That is the whole of it and it has taken me a day and a night to say it and it is not a complaint.”

She put two fingers of her left hand on the near end of the strip of pale wood.

She said, “There is a bar against the wall on the left and it is not dated. There is a lamp in a cage on the shelf at the far end, and it is not dated either, and it has not been lit since I got here.”

She said, “There is a bundle of papers under the far end of that table, corded round twice and not knotted, and it has not been opened. There is a column at the far end with a price at the head of it and a figure in it that stops every two months.”

She said, “And there is a place worn into that table of the length of a forearm and the breadth of a hand.”

She said, “And there is me.”

She said, “And in four hundred and forty-four days not one thing in this room has said what I am. I am the one who says figures out loud at this table. That is a use and it is not a name, and I have known that since I was nine years old and I have never once minded it until this morning.”

## What A Binding Does

And then she took the second half of it, which was the part she had been working on since the eighth hour, and she did it in the order she uses, which is the plain part and then the part that costs.

She said, “A binding is a room’s way of naming a person it has already decided about.”

She stopped for about a count of ninety.

She said, “And I am going to say the rest of that sentence slowly, because it took me a day and a night and it is the whole of what I have got on this.”

She said, “A room with a column in it has four hundred years of rows. Every row is an amount, and every amount is somebody, and not one of those rows has a name against it. It does not need one. It uses people the way a column uses a figure and it does not have to know what to call them.”

She said, “And then a board comes up a stair with a line on it that says one person is bound to feed the lamp in place of the writer, and there is no name on it, and no date, and not one person in it is spoken to.”

She said, “And the whole meaning of that board is that the column is now going to have to hold a person. It has four hundred years of amounts in it and it has never had a person, and it has just been told one is coming.”

She said, “So the room decides first. It decides somewhere between the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen and the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four, and then it writes a board, and the board is how it says a thing it has already settled.”

She put her hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “And the word on that board is not *you* and it is not *the writer*. It is *one person*. And a board that says *one person* is a board that is waiting for the room to have somebody standing in it, and I have been standing in it for four hundred and forty-four days.”

## The Part That Was Not About The Lamp

And then she got to the part that she had not expected, and she said out loud that she had not expected it, because it is the rule and because she was not going to have it in that room as a feeling without a name.

She said, “I am not frightened of the lamp and I want that said first.”

She said, “The lamp is a forearm and I have known what a forearm costs in this building since the twenty-sixth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, and a price is the easiest thing in the world to refuse.”

She said, “What I did not expect is that the one thing on that board that I have wanted since I was nine years old is a name.”

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty with the light going a hand’s width along the near end of that table and twenty-two paces of dark behind it.

She said, “I have said my figures out loud in this room for nineteen years and not one thing has ever been written down that I am the one who says them. I have said a name out loud in here three times and it is not mine.”

She said, “There is a hand at the far end of this table that has been writing down whatever goes across this table for four hundred years, and it wrote a name that was not mine, and it wrote it exactly, and it did not stop on it.”

She said, “And there is no version of me in this building. Not on a board, not in the column, not on the wood under that table. I have checked and I checked it again this morning and there is not one instrument in this county that would tell me what I am to a room that reads.”

## The Word For It

And then she did the last part of the morning, which was working out what she is going to have to write on a leaf, and it took her about a count of ninety and she said every step of it out loud.

She said, “A page refuses a thing by saying what it will not be put in the room as. That is the only shape a refusal has when it is on paper and not in a mouth.”

She said, “And a binding begins with a name. Not with a name it is given, with a name it is entered under. And so the page has to say the name it is refusing, and it has to say it exactly, and there is not one word of slack in it.”

She said, “So here is the word. Not a name. A description, and the shortest description of a person that can be put down on a board, and it is on that board already in those words.”

She said, “*The one who feeds the lamp.*”

She said it out loud and she stopped dead after it and stood there.

She said, “And those are five words and they are not a job, and they are not a sentence addressed to me, and they are not anybody’s name. And if they go into that column then I am those five words in that room for as long as the column stands, and there is a no said in that room that cannot be unsaid.”

She said, “I have been here four hundred and forty-four days without being named by anything in this room. That is the whole of what has happened to me in this room. And the thing on that board that I cannot have is the only thing that would ever have fixed it.”

She put her left hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “And I am not going to put those five words on a leaf of my own hand and send it down nine hundred and four steps. And I have said that out loud, and that is not the same as saying them out loud, and I am aware of the difference and I have not got a better way of refusing a thing than the only way that works.”

## Oil

And then she said the last of it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, and it came out of her the way the rest of that morning came out of her, which is to say all at once and without any dressing.

She said, “The lamp on this table is not the room’s and there is no oil in this building and there has not been for nine years and there is none on this mountain, and it comes up eight hundred and eighty steps in a bag on the back of a boy, and there is not one line anywhere in this building that says where he goes in the evening.”

She said, “And it will want filling. And I have never once gone and got it.”

She said, “Every time that lamp has been filled I have been standing at this table with a strip of pale wood in one hand and a lamp in the other, and he has filled it. And I have not asked him for it once in nineteen years, and I took what was left in his lamp on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and said that out loud in front of him.”

She said, “And I worked out this morning that going and getting it is the same shape as asking for it, only with one more stair in it, and I would rather walk down eight hundred and eighty steps in daylight with an empty lamp than stand at the top of them and open my mouth.”

She said, “So I am going down for it tomorrow morning. In daylight, on my own feet, with my left hand on the rail.”

She left the lamp where it was. The light went a hand’s width along the near end of that table and stopped.

And there was a board lying face up about a foot in from the edge of that table with no name on it and no date on it, and a column at the far end of that table with four hundred years of amounts in it and not one person, and a woman four feet off the near end of it who had just found out which of the two she wanted more.
