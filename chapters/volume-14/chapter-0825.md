# The Bundle Before The Bundle

Fifty-five days since the eighth stage. Four hundred and twenty days in that room. The cage out a hundred and eighty-seven days. Right arm open four hundred and twenty-seven days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and sixty-two days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in the room. The lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table and the light went a hand’s width along it and stopped.

She had said her figures out loud at the seventh hour and she had said out loud at the ninth hour the previous evening what she was going to do and when she was going to do it, and on this morning she went and checked the arrangement.

## Getting To The Doorway

It is twenty-two paces from the near end of that table to the far end of it, and then it is a little way past the far end and up against the wall behind the shelf, where the lamp in the cage stands that has been out a hundred and eighty-seven days, and there is a low door in that wall.

She went the twenty-two paces on the near side of that table on her feet with her left hand on the leg of it, over the flag that moves at the nineteenth pace, and she went past the far end and up to the wall and she did not go through the door.

She said it out loud first, standing in the doorway, in the ordinary voice, at about the eighth hour.

She said, “I have been down that stair in the light once. Once. On the twenty-sixth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, which was a hundred and two days ago, and I counted it four ways and the number is nine hundred and four and there is no nine hundred and fifth.”

She said, “I am not counting it tonight. I want that said first because everything I have done in nineteen years has been counting and I am about to go down nine hundred and four steps and do not do it.”

## Why No Lamp

She said, “I am not taking the lamp and I have worked out why, and it is not careful.”

She did not move while she was saying it.

She said, “A lamp goes down that stair and then it is a lamp in the dark at the bottom of it, and nothing in this building has ever carried light to the bottom of that stair.”

She said, “On the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four I had a lamp in my hand down there and I laid it on a step and it was the only light in that stair in nineteen years and it did not help, because a lamp that goes down with me is a lamp that is not on the table, and the table is where the figures are said.”

She put her right hand out and found the edge of the low door in the dark with the flat of it.

She said, “And I am not taking a lamp because a leaf of paper in my left hand and a lamp in my left hand do not go down nine hundred and four steps together, and there are five blank leaves against my fourth rib that are going to go down that stair in a hand on a morning a bundle comes up, and I have got exactly two hands and one of them does not close.”

She said, “So: no lamp. And no counting. Those are the two arrangements and I am saying both of them out loud standing in this doorway at about the eighth hour on the eighth of this month.”

## Going Down Without Counting

And she went down.

The dark off that doorway is not twenty-two paces of a room. It is a stair going down and turning once, and she went down it with her left hand on the wall and her right arm open in front of her, and she did not count, and that was the whole of the work and it took her everything she had.

She said out loud, at the turn, in the ordinary voice, into the dark:

“I am not counting it. That is the sentence. I am going down nine hundred and four steps that I know the number of and I am not counting them and that is the first time in nineteen years I have gone down a stair and not turned it into a figure.”

She stood on the turn for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “I know the number is nine hundred and four. I am carrying that. It came up out of me without my doing anything and I am not checking it against anything and I am not adding it to anything.”

She said, “And there is a stair in this tower of eight hundred and eighty steps in two runs of four hundred and forty with a rail on the first four, and the two of those are two stairs and they have never been one stair and I am not going to make them one tonight.”

And then she went down the rest of it, and the bottom of that stair is a low door in a floor and she came out of it on the near side of that table into a room with a lamp standing a foot in from the near end with a trimmed wick, and the light went a hand’s width along the wood and stopped.

And she was in the room again in a whole number of counts, and she had not counted one of them.

## Back Up

And then she went back up, which is the part that has never been the work, and she did it in about a count of ninety, and she found that about that too.

She said, out loud, standing four feet off the near end of that table, in the ordinary voice:

“That is the second time I have gone all the way down that stair without a light. The first was the sixth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, which was a hundred and eighty-two days ago, and I came back up it and found that the bundle under the far end of this table was against a different leg of it than the one I had put it against.”

She said, “And the first time I came up it afterwards I could not have done it for about a count of a hundred and forty and I stood at the near end of this table breathing at nine and counting the days since the light came down to that cut in the stone.”

She said, “And tonight it was lighter. Not easier. There is no version of that stair that is easy and I have been down it in the dark twice in nineteen years and I know exactly what it is. Lighter.”

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “That is the second thing that has come out of this room in eight days that I did not go looking for. The first was a fair hand putting a day on my arithmetic that was right. This one is a stair I went down and came back up without counting and did not fall over.”

She said, “And I am not going to work out what either of those two means, because I have a refusal to get down nine hundred and four steps in a hand and it takes everything I have got and I am not spending the rest of it on working out why a stair felt lighter.”

## What She Is Going To Do At The Second Hour

And then she said the last of it, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, at the near end of that table, with the lamp burning a hand’s width along the wood and three pieces of wood lying face up side by side about a foot in from the edge.

She said, “Here is what is said and what is going to be done and I am putting the two of them in this room together so that neither of them can be changed on its own.”

She said, “What is said: a no, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour and ten minutes on the fifth of this month, to a person keeping what I say out loud in this room. It cannot be unsaid. It is said. I am not working on it any more.”

She said, “What is going to be done: five blank leaves against my fourth rib, one of them, in my own hand, with a nib and ink that have not touched anything for nineteen years, and the sentence said out loud standing here at about the first hour before the nib goes on the paper, and the leaf folded, and it put in the hand that goes down that stair, on the morning a bundle comes up.”

She put two fingers of her left hand on the near end of the strip of pale wood.

She said, “And at the second hour on that morning I am going to be standing four feet off the near end of this table, and forty boards are going on it, and a hand is going along it four to a minute, and then the room is going to be quiet for about a count of a hundred and forty, and then there is going to be one more stroke.”

She said, “And whenever that stroke lands on whatever it lands on, I am going to read it out loud in this room in the ordinary voice, standing, and I am not going to be braced for it and I am not going to have said anything first about what I think it is.”

She said, “That is what I am going to do at the second hour and I have said it out loud standing at the near end of this table on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen, and there is nothing in that room that is going to answer it, and I have not asked for an answer, and I have known there would not be one since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four.”

She left the lamp where it was.

And the light went a hand’s width along the near end of that table and stopped, and there were three pieces of wood lying side by side in it, and five blank leaves against her fourth rib that were still blank, and nine hundred and four steps under the floor going down in the dark, and she had been down them and back up them and not counted one.
