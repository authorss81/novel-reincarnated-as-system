# Twenty-Two Paces

She went to look at the worn place in the far end of that table on the ninth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four at about the seventh hour, and she took two things with her, and the second one is the whole reason this time is not the last time she did it.

The lamp goes a hand's width along the near end of that table and no further. To get light onto the worn place she has to carry the lamp twenty-two paces and put it down there, and then the near end of that table is a dark table again with nothing on it.

## Two Things In Two Hands

And there was a strip of pale wood lying face up on that near end of that table with a price at the head of it and the same price in the first pair of cuts and a five in the second pair and a seven in the third pair, all of it in her own hand, and that strip was the whole of her record and there is not one other copy of any of it in this building.

She said it out loud, standing, before she picked anything up, because the fourth of the six is that a thing gets said before it is a line anywhere.

She said, “There is a strip of pale wood on this table and I am going to take it with me.”

He said, “You could leave it.”

She said, “If I leave it then it is in the dark for about a count of a hundred and forty while I am at the far end of this table with the only light in this building in my hand, and there is a figure of mine on it, and there is not one instrument in this county that can find out what is written on it.”

She said, “And I am not spending that on a strip of wood about two inches across. I would rather carry it.”

And she put it under her left arm against her fourth rib, where a book of eight leaves has been against that rib for nineteen years and five of those eight leaves are still blank, and she took the lamp in her left hand.

She said, “That is two things in two hands and it is the first time in this room I have needed both.”

## The Nineteenth Pace

And then she went down onto the stone at the near end of that table with the strip of pale wood under her left arm against her fourth rib and the lamp in her left hand and her right arm open in front of her.

She went along the near side of that table on the stones, which takes longer than going along on her knees, and she chose it anyway, because her knees had been wrong since the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and she has been leaving them alone.

The light went along the floor ahead of her and stopped at the leg of the table and the top of the table stayed dark above it the whole way, and the bar nine feet nine flat against the wall on the left of her the whole way, and she counted the paces out loud because there is a boy of sixteen at the other end of that table who is entitled to hear how long twenty-two paces takes in the dark.

She got to about the nineteenth pace and her knee went on the loose flag and she put her left hand out flat against the leg of the table, with the lamp still in it, and waited about a count of ninety, and then she went the last three.

And she put the lamp down on the top of that table about four feet short of the far end, and the light came down onto the wood and spread out flat on it.

And the near end of that table went out behind her the moment the lamp was down, and so did the strip of pale wood and the forty boards under the middle of it and the boy standing four feet off the near end, and everything she had said out loud to that boy in the last half hour was still in the room and she could not see one line of him.

## Four Feet Off

And she got up onto her feet and stood about four feet back from the lamp, looking down at the wood the light could do, and she saw the worn place from about four feet off for the first time.

She said, out loud, to herself, in the ordinary voice:

“That is not the shape of a person sitting in it.”

And then she stopped and made herself say the rest of it, because she does not let a thing stand that has not been put into words where it exists, and because a thing seen at four feet in a hand's width of light is a thing half seen.

She said, “That is the whole of what I have got. Not what the shape is. What it is not.”

She said, “I told a boy of sixteen on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four that this place had been worn by somebody sitting in it. That is a thing I said out loud and I did not check it first, and I have not been able to put it back since, and it was wrong.”

She stood over it for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “It is worn deeper at the front than at the back and it is not deeper in the middle than at the edges, and a person sitting in a place puts the weight in the middle and takes it off at the front and the back, and this has not done that.”

She said, “And I am not going to work out what shape it is out here on a table with a lamp a foot away and my own two eyes, and I am not going to say one word about it to anybody, because I would be guessing and I have never in nineteen years put a guess in this room.”

## The Second Look

And she looked at it once more, standing about four feet back from the lamp, and the second time was worse and she wants that said as loud as the first.

She said, out loud, to the wood:

“The second time is worse because the first time I was looking at it to see what it was and the second time I was looking at it to see whether it was the shape of him.”

She said, “It is not. I do not know what the shape is and I am not going to look for it any harder tonight. There is nothing in this room that would tell me what shape a thing is that has been worn by four hundred years of something I have never once seen happen.”

She said, “And I want it said that I looked. That is the whole of what I have done at the far end of this table in three hundred and sixty-one days. I looked at it twice, at about four feet, in a hand's width of light I carried there myself, and I did not sit in it and I did not put my weight on it and I did not put my hand in the shape.”

## Back Up The Near Side

And she carried the lamp back twenty-two paces and put it on the wood a foot in from the near end, and the light went a hand's width along it and stopped, and nothing about the far end of that table had changed by her having gone to it.

The last three paces with the lamp in her left hand were the same three as the first three and she took them the same way, and she did not find anything on the way back that she had not walked past on the way out.

She put the strip of pale wood down where she had left it, face up, grain running up, and squared it with two fingers of her left hand.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“I have been carrying a thing I cannot say for about two hundred counts and I have not put it anywhere, and that is new. I have put figures into that wood for nineteen years and every one of them went in. I have not had anything before that did not go in.”

She said, “And I am not going to make a pair of cuts for it. That is the rule I made on the fourteenth of that month and I have not broken it once in nineteen years and I am not starting with something I got by looking at a worn place in the dark.”

## She Does Not Sit In It

And he said the thing from the near end of that table that he is allowed to say.

He said, “Then what do you do with it.”

She said, “I do not sit in it.”

She said, “I have never sat in it. I found it and I have walked past it two hundred times and I have put my hand flat on the wood beside it and I have not once put my weight on it, and no instruction in this building has ever put anybody in it, and I am not going to be the one.”

She said, “And I am not going to have anybody put in it either. That is the second thing I have said out loud about it in nineteen years and it is not going to change tonight.”

And then she squared the strip of pale wood up again with two fingers of her left hand where it was lying face up about a foot in from the edge of that table, and stood there with the light a hand's width along the wood and twenty-two paces of dark back to the place she had just looked at.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“I have seen something about that place and I cannot say what it is, and I am going to be carrying the not-saying about for a long time, and I have never done that before and I do not know what a person does with one.”
