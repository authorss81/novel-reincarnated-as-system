# What Came Back In Two Hands

Twenty-seven days since the eighth stage. Three hundred and ninety-two days in that room. The cage out a hundred and fifty-nine days. Right arm open three hundred and ninety-nine days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and thirty-four days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

She said every figure she had out loud at about the seventh hour, standing four feet off the near end of that table, with the lamp on the wood a foot in from the edge and the light going a hand’s width along it and no further.

And then, at about the eighth hour, she did a thing she has done on one day out of thirty and no other day in nineteen years.

## Going Along The Table

She went along the near side of that table on her feet with her left hand on the leg of it and she stopped about eleven paces out, which is the middle, and she got down onto the stone and put her hand flat on the underside of the top where the stack is.

She said it out loud before she moved it.

She said, “There are forty boards face up in a stack under the middle of this table and I put all forty hands on them on the second of every month and I have not put one hand on them on any other day in nineteen years.”

She said, “It is the tenth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four and I have not done this since the second. I am going to do it now and I am not going to say out loud that I am doing it because of yesterday, and I am going to say out loud that I am doing it, which is better than not.”

## The Top Board

She got the top board out of the stack by touch and carried it back along eleven paces to the near end and put it down on the wood face up in the light, and then she went back for three more, one at a time, because she could carry one in her left hand and she could not carry four.

The first three had nothing on them. Not a mark. Thirty-eight of the forty had nothing on them. The thirty-ninth is a board with a paragraph of words on it that I put there myself on the twentieth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and cannot tell from any of the others.

The fourth one had a row on it.

## Her Own Hand

She stood four feet off the near end of that table and looked at it in a hand’s width of light and it took her about a count of a hundred and forty to be able to say anything at all.

Then she said, out loud, in the ordinary voice:

“That is my hand.”

She said, “Three fingers and a thumb and the way the five comes down and round and stops before it is a whole, and the way I have written every figure on my own wood for nineteen years, and there is not one other thing in this room that writes like that.”

She said, “And I did not write it.”

She put her left hand flat on the table on the other side of the board and did not touch the board itself.

She said, “I have never written a row of figures on one of those forty. Not one, ever. There is one board in that stack with words on it that I put there myself on the twentieth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and it was a paragraph, and I cannot tell which of the forty it is, and I have not been able to find out whether this room reads words or only cuts.”

She said, “So it reads words.”

## The Second Hand

And under that row, about a finger’s width below it and running the whole length of the board, there was a second row.

It was not the hand at the far end of that table. That hand has written four hundred years of one stroke starting in the same place on every board and this was not that.

It was fair. Even, and a little smaller than hers, and it went on without a single place where the nib had to be lifted and set down again.

And it was not the same figure.

She said, standing four feet off, in the ordinary voice:

“Under every figure of mine on that board there is the same figure one day further on than mine is.”

She said, “Not different. Further on. The one I said out loud at about the seventh hour this morning is out of date by a day by the time anybody could use it, and the fair hand has it as it stands this evening. It has done the one thing I cannot do standing in a dark room, which is to be a day old.”

She waited about a count of ninety.

She said, “And it is right.”

## Why It Is On One Morning In Twenty

And then she did the arithmetic, and she did it out loud, standing, because she says figures before they are anything else and because she had been standing there for about a count of a hundred and forty with nothing coming out of her mouth.

She said, “One morning in twenty. That is the figure and it is mine and not anybody’s.”

She said, “Most mornings I stand at the near end of this table and say out loud what I have, and most mornings what I have is the price at the head of that column and the days and the number of empty pairs. And a price does not date. A price is the same this morning and this evening and in a year. I worked that out on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and said it out loud.”

She said, “One morning in twenty I say a count instead. A count of days, or a count of boards, or a count of anything that was true when I said it. And a count is out of date by a day for every day I stand in this room, and there is no day on which a count I have already said comes right again.”

Her right arm was open at her side the whole time and it did not get any better.

She said, “And it is on the count mornings that there is a row on one of those forty. So it is not an accident and it is not a mystery and it is somebody doing the one thing I have wanted done to that wood for nineteen years.”

## What She Cannot Check

And then she said the part that made her get down onto the stone, out loud, in the ordinary voice, with her back against the leg of that table.

She said, “I cannot check it.”

She said, “That board is a foot in front of me and I can read every figure on it in this light, both rows, and I have just told you that both rows are in hands I know. And it is not the row that is the problem.”

She said, “The problem is where the thing being corrected lives. It lives in the column at the far end of that table, at the far side of the shelf, twenty-two paces off in the dark where a lamp in a cage used to put light on it, and I cannot see one figure of it.”

She put her left hand flat on the wood beside the strip and left it there.

She said, “To check that correction I would have to carry that lamp twenty-two paces and stand at the far end of this table and I did that on the ninth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four and it took both my hands and it put the near end of this table into the dark with my own record lying on it.”

She said, “So I can read the correction and I cannot check it, and that is the whole of where I am standing.”

## Not Asking

And she stayed on the stone with her back against the leg of that table for about a count of a hundred and forty, and she said the two things out loud that she had decided to say, in the ordinary voice, in that order.

She said, “I am not going to ask who is doing it.”

She said, “Not tonight and not by asking a person and not by standing at that end of this table with a lamp in my hand at the ninth hour making a face at the dark until something gives. I have had a name written down in this room that I did not write and I did not ask about that either, and asking is how you find out, and finding out is not the same as being able to do anything.”

She said, “And I am not going to stop putting them down.”

Her right arm was open at her side the whole time and it did not get any better.

She said, “That is the half I am not going to lose. I said every figure out loud this morning at the seventh hour and I am going to say every figure out loud tomorrow morning at the seventh hour.”

She said, “And I am not going to let a fair hand under a column stop me standing four feet off the near end of this table in the ordinary voice, because the day I stop is the day that row stops and I am not giving that away to somebody I cannot see.”

She got up off the stone and put the board back on the top of the stack under the middle of that table, face up, with the row of mine on it and the fair row under it, and she put it back the way it was, which she knows because she counted the stack from the top at the seventh hour.

And the last thing she said about it, standing four feet off the near end with the lamp burning a hand’s width along the wood, was out loud and in the ordinary voice and it was about a person and not about a column.

She said, “There is a boy of sixteen in this building who is not bound and not entered and not sent, and I say those three words out loud in this room so that they are in it, and that is still true, and a fair hand writing under my figures does not make it less true.”

She put the strip of pale wood square with two fingers of her left hand, and the board with his name on it beside it, and then she stood four feet back and looked at the two of them in the light the lamp could do.

And what was on the table in front of her was not a name and a price any more. It was a price and a name and a row in her own hand with a day already added to it by somebody who had never met her, and she could read all of it and check none of it, and she could not refuse it, and she did not know how a person is supposed to do either of those two things.
