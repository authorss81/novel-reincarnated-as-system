# Sixty-Eight

She set the taking of the eighth stage on the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four at about the seventh hour, with a lamp standing on that table a foot in from the edge and the light going a hand's width along it and no further, and she said it out loud where she was standing, four feet off the near end.

And she did it alone, and the part of it that was not alone she said out loud standing, in the ordinary voice, because the fourth of the six is that a thing gets said before it is a line anywhere.

## The Whole Of The Day Before

And she had spent the whole of that day not saying it, and she said that out loud too, standing, at about the seventh hour, before she got to any of the arithmetic.

She said, “I have not said the word stage out loud in this room today and it is the twelfth of the month and I have been standing here since the seventh hour.”

She said, “I got as far as *the thirteenth* at about the eighth hour and then I stopped and I went and stood at the near end of that table and looked at a lamp for about a count of a hundred and forty.”

She said, “The reason is that saying it out loud in this room makes it a thing that this room has heard, and this room wrote a figure of mine into a column on the fifth of the month before last and I have not worked out what that column is doing, and tomorrow I am going to put another one in it.”

He said, “Then do not say it.”

She said, “No. I am saying it. That is the rule and the rule does not have an exception in it for a thing I am frightened of, and if it did then I would make the exception on the first night and then on the ninth, and I have watched myself do that with a date once already.”

## The Objection

He said, “You have not written anything in that wood since the fifth of the month before last.”

She said, “I have not.” He said, “Then there is one figure in that row that you wrote yourself and it is a count, and counts date, and it is sixty-seven days old and it will be sixty-eight tomorrow morning.”

She said, “Yes.” He said, “And you worked out on the eighth of the month before last that a column with a price at the head is a column of amounts and not a column of counts. And you have a seven in the third pair of that column and it went in as a count.”

She said, “Yes.” He said, “So the column is reading a count you made against a price you cannot read, and you do not know what seven of that is, and you told a boy of sixteen on the eighth of the month before last that you did not know, out loud, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table.”

She said, “I did.”

He said, “Then you are about to put eight in the fourth pair, and the column will read eight of something, and it will be right, because you wrote it. And the only way of finding out what that column is doing is to use it, and you are going to find out by doing it.”

She stood there.

She said, out loud, to a person, “That is the objection and I have known since the eighth of the month before last that it was coming and I have not got an answer to it and I am going to take the eighth stage anyway.”

## The Interval By Subtraction

She said the interval out loud standing, in the ordinary voice, and she did it by subtraction, and she made herself do the whole thing without stopping in it.

She said, “Two thousand six hundred and sixty-six less two thousand five hundred and ninety-eight is sixty-eight days. The fifth was at fifty-eight days and the sixth was at sixty-three days and the seventh was at seventy-four days. There is no rule in this room that puts a stage at any interval. There are four figures and they are four different intervals and I have had them out loud in this building four times.”

Fifty-eight is when there was one figure in that row and it was a price and it was not mine. Sixty-three is when there was one figure in that row and a look had come off the foot of that wood on the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four and there was going to be no second one.

Seventy-four is when there was nothing in that row to take it against except a lamp a boy carried up that stair for me. Sixty-eight is tomorrow, and it is between the first two and the fourth, and there is nothing in the middle of anything.

She said, “And that is the whole of the arithmetic and I am going to say the part underneath it, because the part underneath it is the part I have never said out loud in nineteen years.”

## Why Sixty-Eight And Not Fifty-Eight

She said, “Here is the arithmetic and I want it said as arithmetic and not as a lesson, because I have said a lesson out loud in this room twice in nineteen years and both of them were a mistake.”

She said, “A stage taken against a row with one figure in it can be taken at fifty-eight days and there is nothing to be out of date. A stage taken against a row with two figures in it has to be taken against both of them, because the row does not know which of them the stage is for.”

She said, “And one of those two figures is a count I wrote on the fifth of the month before last and it is out of date by a day for every day I stand in this room, and it was out of date by one count when I woke and it will be out of date by two when I write tomorrow.”

And she could not wait for it to come right either, and she said that too, standing, because there is no day on which a count she has already written comes right again, and that is the whole of what is wrong with it.

She said, “And so it is not fifty-eight. Fifty-eight is what you use when the row holds one thing and the thing is not moving. It holds two things and one of them is moving, and you take it anyway, and that is sixty-eight.”

She stopped for about a count of ninety.

And she said out loud, then, standing, in the ordinary voice, that she had been working this interval out in her own head since the thirtieth of the month before last and had not once said aloud that she had no rule for it, and that she was saying it now, because the objection she had just heard in this room was the right one and she would rather say that than find out tomorrow that she had taken a stage against a figure that was already wrong.

## The Decision

And then she decided, and she decided out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, with her right arm open at her side and her breath at nine.

She said, “Before I say it I am going to do the thing I have done before a stage four times now, which is to be wrong in public first.”

She said, “I am going to take it at the eighth hour.”

She waited.

She said, “It is not at the eighth hour. It is at the ninth hour, because the sixth was at the ninth and the seventh was at the ninth and there is no reason on this earth to move it, and I reached for the eighth hour because it was nearer and I wanted it nearer.”

She said, “I have now said out loud, standing in this room, a figure that is wrong, and the wrong figure went out first, and that is what the fourth of the six is actually for. It is not so that a room hears it. It is so that the wrong one does not stand in the room instead of the right one.”

She said, “And now the right one. I am taking the eighth stage tomorrow, at the ninth hour, on the thirteenth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four. At the near end of this table, in oil light, on this strip of pale wood, in my own hand, with the nib against my fourth rib, read out loud before it is a line anywhere, the same as the last one.”

He said, “That is decided.”

She said, “That is decided and I said it out loud so that it is decided. There is nothing left about tomorrow except whether the lamp comes up that stair.”

He said, “It will come.”

She said, “You cannot say that. There is no oil in that building and there has not been for nine years and there is none on this mountain, and there is no figure anywhere in this county that says what is left in the lamp on your landing.

And I am not going to make you stand at the top of that stair and promise me. A promise made at the top of eight flights is a thing you say at the seventh hour on the night before and it has nothing to do with the ninth hour on the day.”

She said, “So the only thing I have is the ninth hour tomorrow and the light coming a hand's width along that wood, and if it does not come then there is a room with a column of amounts in it and no lamp in it, and I have decided to take a stage in there anyway.”

And that was what she wanted. It was the whole of what she had wanted for four days, and she had not said it out loud because saying it out loud makes it a thing that can be taken away from her.

She said, “And I have wanted it since the seventh of that month and it has not been the lamp. It has been that if I put a figure in that wood then there is one more figure in it, and I have nine empty pairs underneath it and I have had nineteen years of empty and the empty is a thing I have got.”

He said, “Then it is only a count.”

She said, “It is only a count and it is eight, and on the eighth of the month before last a woman worked out that a column with a price at the head of it is a column of amounts, and I do not know what eight of ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes is, and I am going to find out.”

She put her left hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “I am going to want it tomorrow at the ninth hour and I am frightened underneath that, and both of those are true and I have put them in the room in that order so that the wanting is on top.”
