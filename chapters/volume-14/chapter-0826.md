# One Binding

Four hundred and forty-three days in that room. The cage out two hundred and ten days and not one of them going to be put right by anything anybody does. Right arm open four hundred and fifty days and it is not going to close. The bar against the wall on the left four hundred days. One thousand one hundred and eighty-five days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was a boy of sixteen in the room at the second hour, because he asked for the second hour of a first of a month on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen and she said yes in about a second, and the lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table with a trimmed wick and the light going a hand’s width along it and stopping.

## Nine Hundred And Four Going Up

She counted it going up. She said out loud that she counted it, and that she was going to count it, and that the reason was that the last time she had gone up and down it she had not counted and she had got her figures from that for a day and a half.

Nine hundred and four coming up, and the step at the turn did not change.

Forty boards going on that table in ten minutes, four to a minute, and then thirty counts for the cloth and the cord going round them, and then nine hundred and four going back down in the dark.

It was the sixteenth bundle and she did not say so out loud until after, because it had come up fifteen times before that morning and each time she had named it and this one she had better things to do with her mouth.

And then the room was quiet for about a count of a hundred and forty.

And then there was one more stroke.

## The Stroke That Came After

It lasted about a count of nine and it started in the same place it has started in twice before, which is the same place forty of the other strokes start, and there is not one instrument in this building that can tell the difference between a stroke that begins there because it is a hand doing its work and a stroke that begins there because it is doing something else.

She said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, into twenty-two paces of dark:

“That is three. The first of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four, the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen, and this morning. Sixty days between the first and this one and thirty between the second and this one, and I have not worked out whether that is a figure or a coincidence and I am not going to work it out in the next hour.”

She said, “And I said out loud on that first morning that whatever it was on, it was the last thing to be touched in this room at the second hour of a month for four hundred years. I have said that twice since. It is still true and I am still not taking it back and I am still not going to get a second thing out of it.”

## What The Board Is And How Long She Has Had It

And then she said, at about the eighth hour and forty minutes, standing four feet off the near end of that table with the boy three feet off her and the light doing a hand’s width along the wood:

“Now I am going to do the other thing, and I am going to say why I have not done it, and I am not doing the why first.”

She said, “There is a board lying face up on this table about a foot in from the edge that came up with that bundle this morning on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four, and it has been lying on this table since the second hour.”

She put two fingers of her left hand on the near edge of that board and did not turn it over.

She said, “I have read it through twice this morning before I read my own strip and after I have said my figures out loud and I have read it standing, and I have read it in the ordinary voice of a woman reading a thing to herself.”

She said, “And I have not said it out loud. Once. Not to this room and not to a person and not to myself standing here.”

She stopped for about a count of ninety.

She said, “That is keeping the fourth of the six this morning and not breaking it. A thing gets said out loud before it is a line anywhere. I have said that term every day since I was nine years old and I have never once had a thing written on a board in this building and not said it here first, and I have one this morning and I am saying it.”

She said, “And I want that said in this room at about the eighth hour and forty-five minutes on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four, because a person who has a new board needs it on the record that she knows what rule it goes under, and because I am going to keep it in about a count of forty.”

## Every Line Of It

And then she said it out loud, in the ordinary voice, standing four feet off the near end of that table with the lamp burning a hand’s width along the wood and the boy beside her and twenty-two paces of dark past the light, and she read every line of it twice because it had not been a line anywhere until this moment.

She said, “The hand is fair. It is even and it is a little smaller than mine and there is not one place on it where the nib had to be lifted and set down again.”

She said, “And there is one pair of cuts under it and nothing in them, worn round at the corners, and they are the same width as the pair under the name on the other board and the same width as the pairs on my own wood.”

She said, “Here it is. Line for line.”

She said, “The place at the far side of this table is kept by a person who is there when the figures are said out loud.”

She said, “That person does not write them.”

She said, “That person is not named on this board.”

She said, “The lamp is fed in place of the writer.”

She said, “One person is bound to feed it.”

She said, “One binding.”

She said, “And the record is not vacated, and the rows are true, and what is said out loud at this table is kept.”

And then she read the whole of it a second time, the same way, at the same distance from it, and the light went a hand’s width along the near end of that table and stopped, and she did not move anything while she was doing either of those.

## No Name On It

And then she said the thing about it out loud, and she said it before anything else about it, because she has learned twice in this room that a thing that is bigger than the room has to be named before it is argued with.

She said, “It does not have a name on it.”

She said, “There is a board on this table with a name cut into it in a third hand, a foot long and two inches across, and it has been lying face up here since the first of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four. This is not that board and there is not one character of a name on it anywhere, and I have looked at it four times in the last two days.”

She put her hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “And there is not one word of it that says when it begins. There is no first of a month on it and there is no second hour on it and there is no day on it of any kind.”

She said, “So here is what it is: a thing with a price on it and no name on it and no date on it, which means it can go on anybody who is standing in this room, and it has been sitting on my table since the second hour this morning waiting for one of us to be the person it is for.”

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “And I am not going to say a word out loud in this room about which one of us it was for, and I want that said before I say anything else about it.”

She said, “Because I have said a name out loud in this room three times and one of them was in front of a boy of sixteen, and a thing that reads does not stop on a name, and I am not putting a fourth one next to a board like that.”

## What The Price Is

And then she got to the price, and she got to it last, and she had got about nine words into it and stopped and started again.

She said, “The price is one person.”

She said, “One person bound to feed that lamp in place of the writer. In place of me. It is not oil and it is not money and it is not a night’s sleep and there is not one thing anybody has ever been able to put in a lamp in a cage in this county, and what goes in it is a person’s arm.”

She said, “And every row in that column at the far end of this table for four hundred years has been paid for out of a forearm, and I worked that out on the twenty-sixth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and I have not taken it back since.”

She stopped.

She said, “And I am going to say the plain version, once, and I am not going to say it again in this building.”

She said, “A binding takes a person and makes them a standing item in a room’s arithmetic. It is not a job. It is not being sent anywhere. It is a person written into a column so that the column can use them, and the person does not get to take themselves back out of it afterwards, and there is one of them and there is only one.”

## The Cold That Is Not New

And then she said what she was feeling, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, because she is not going to have a thing in that room be mistaken for something else.

She said, “It is the cold. It came up out of the stone out of my ankles when the lamp was put down on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen and it lasted about a count of a hundred and forty.”

She said, “It is not new this time. That is the whole of what I have to say about it and I want it said plainly, because the first time I said a thing about that cold in this room I had to explain what it was not, and I am not going to spend a count of a hundred and forty doing that again.”

She said, “It came back this morning at about the second hour and it is the same cold and it did not take me by anything at all. And I know exactly what is different, and it is that the first time it came I did not know what the thing was. Now I know what the thing is, and a cold that has to surprise you only works on a person who does not know yet.”

## What She Is Not Going To Say

And then she said the sentence, which was the sentence she was not going to say out loud that day, and she said the shape of it out loud as a description and not the words, which is a distinction she is aware of and is not proud of.

She said, “Here it is, and I have had it since the second hour. It is the refusal of the place and the price on it. And I am not vacating this record.”

She said, “And I am not going to say it out loud standing at this table today.”

She said, “And it is not courage, and it is not the cold, and it is not that I have not got it.”

She said, “The reason is that I said a no in this room on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen that cannot be unsaid, and I said out loud on the day after it that I was not going to say it out loud a second time.”

She said, “A refusal said twice is not a refusal. It is a person standing in a room asking a question she already knows the answer to and waiting to be disagreed with.”

She put her left hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “And the answer is going to come off this table. That is the only thing that has ever left this room and it goes down nine hundred and four steps on the morning a bundle comes up, and the next morning anything comes up that stair is the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four.”

She said, “Thirty days. And I have got the sentence and I have not got the paper and the paper is the half of it that is not me.”

And then she squared her own strip of pale wood and the board with the name on it, which are the two she squares every morning, and left the third one where it fell, and the boy took the lamp down to him and stood with it at the near end of the room and did not say anything, and she did not ask him to.

And the light went a hand’s width along the near end of that table and stopped, and there were four pieces of wood lying face up side by side about a foot in from the edge, and the fourth one had no name on it at all.

Under her own strip there were eight pairs of cuts with nothing in any of them and one pair below them that had been empty since the wood was cut, and she had not put her thumb on it that day and she was not going to.
