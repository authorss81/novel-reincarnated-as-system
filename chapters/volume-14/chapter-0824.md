# Five Blanks And None Of Them

Fifty-four days since the eighth stage. Four hundred and nineteen days in that room. The cage out a hundred and eighty-six days. Right arm open four hundred and twenty-six days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and sixty-one days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in the room. The lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table and the light went a hand’s width along it and stopped.

She got to work on it at about the eighth hour of the seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen and she worked on it until the ninth hour and she did not write a word.

## What A Refusal Has To Be Made Of

She said it out loud, standing four feet off the near end of that table, in the ordinary voice, because a thing gets said before it is a line anywhere and she is not going to start on the one morning when that stops being true.

She said, “I said a no in this room yesterday at about the ninth hour and ten minutes, standing, in the ordinary voice, into twenty-two paces of dark, and it cannot be unsaid, and I said that out loud too.”

She said, “And I have worked out what that is worth, standing here this morning, and here is the whole of it.”

She said, “A no said out loud in a room with one hand in it is not a no to that hand. It is a noise.”

She put two fingers of her left hand on the near end of the strip of pale wood.

She said, “That hand writes down what goes across this table. It does not answer. And I have known that since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four and I said it out loud on that day and once since, and there has been no speech out of the far end of this table in four hundred years and there is not going to be one now.”

She put her hand flat on the wood.

She said, “So for it to be a refusal instead of a noise, it has to be a thing that leaves this room.”

## The Only Way Anything Leaves

She said, “And here is the whole of what I have got, and it took me about a count of a hundred and forty to say it in that order.”

She said, “There is one door out of this room that anything has ever gone out of. There is a place in the wall behind the shelf at the far end of this table and a stair goes down off it and it is nine hundred and four steps and it comes back up into this same room out of a low door in the floor on the near side.”

She did not move while she was saying it.

She said, “And I counted it four ways in the light on the twenty-sixth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and the number has not changed.”

She said, “Things have gone down that stair. I have gone down it. A lamp has gone down it once, on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four, and I have never found out what it was for. Nine hundred and four steps is not a distance, it is a way of getting something out of this room and nowhere else.”

She said, “And a man comes up it on the first of a month with forty boards under one arm, and he has done that since before this building was a building, and the two of those are the same stair going the same way at the same hour on the same morning, and that is not a coincidence I have worked out. That is how this building works. Something goes down that stair on the morning something comes up it, or it does not go at all.”

## The Sentence She Is Not Going To Spend

And then she got to the part she had been working on since the eighth hour, and she said it out loud standing, in the ordinary voice, and it came out of her fast.

She said, “I am not going to give you the sentence I have given you before about there being no way of making one of these.”

She said, “I have said a sentence like that in this room before, about other things, about other mornings, and I am not going to spend it here. I have got one of those and it has been true more than once and it has never once helped, and I am not going to use it on the one thing in nineteen years that I actually have to do.”

She said, “So I am going to say the other one, which is that I know what a refusal is made of and have known it since I was nine years old, and it is a piece of paper with a sentence on it and a hand that put it there, and that is it. That is the whole of it and there is nothing else in this building that can carry one.”

## Five Blanks

And then she took her left hand up against her fourth rib, where a book of eight leaves has been against that rib for nineteen years, and she had it out of the fold of her shirt and she held it in the light going a hand’s width along the near end of that table.

She said, “Eight leaves. Three of them have been written on in nineteen years. The last one was written on when I was nine years old and it was a date and it was in my own hand and it was the fourth of a month in a year I am not going to say out loud in this room.”

She said, “Five of them are blank and they have been blank since I was nine.”

She said, “And I have had them against my fourth rib for nineteen years and I have carried them up nine hundred and four steps and eight hundred and eighty steps and down both of them a great many times, and in nineteen years I have taken them out about twice a year and looked at them and put them back.”

She was quiet for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “I have not written on one since I was nine. That is the whole of it and I am not going to dress it up as anything else. There is a no said in this room that cannot be unsaid and there are five blank pieces of paper against my fourth rib and there is a nib and ink against my ribs and I have not put one of the two anywhere near the other.”

## Not Tonight

And then she put the book back against her fourth rib, and she did it slowly, and she put her right arm open at her side the whole time, and she said the decision out loud before she did it, which is the rule.

She said, “I am not going to write on one.”

She said, “And I want that said in this room at about the ninth hour on the seventh of this month, by me, standing, in the ordinary voice, and I want it said before I say the rest of it, because the rest of it is that I know what I am going to do and when I am going to do it and I have been standing here since the eighth hour working out that the two of those can be got to fit each other and they cannot.”

She said, “I am not going to write on one tonight.”

She waited about a count of ninety.

She said, “And I am not going to write on one tomorrow, and I am not going to write on one on any morning between tomorrow and the morning I have said, and that is not me being careful. That is me finding out that I have not got the sentence yet and that a leaf is for the sentence and not for the wanting to have said it.”

She put her hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “I said a no yesterday and it is said and it cannot be unsaid. That is finished and I am not working on it any more. What I have not got is the thing that goes down nine hundred and four steps on a morning a bundle comes up that stair, and that is a different piece of work and it has been four hundred and nineteen days of work and it takes however long it takes.”

## The Morning It Is Going To Be Used On

And then she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, and she said it plainly, because a date that is not a date is not a date.

She said, “I am going to write it on one of those five blank leaves, in my own hand, with the nib and the ink that have been against my fourth rib for nineteen years, and I am going to fold it and I am going to put it in the hand that goes down that stair, and I am going to do it on the morning a bundle comes up.”

She said, “Not tomorrow. Not the first of this month, because that first is six days gone and it went past while I was working out what a refusal is made of. The next morning anything comes up that stair.”

She said, “And that is a morning and not a day, and a bundle comes up at the second hour, and I am going to have the leaf in my hand at about the first hour, and I am going to say the sentence out loud standing four feet off the near end of that table before the nib goes on the paper, because a thing gets said before it is a line anywhere and I am not going to break that on this.”

She did not fill the count with anything.

She said, “And that is the whole of the arrangement and it is said in this room and not one person has been asked to carry it and not one person is going to be.”

She left the lamp standing where it was. The light went a hand’s width along the near end of that table and stopped.

And against her fourth rib there were five blank leaves that were still blank, and they were going to stay blank through that night and through the next one and through however many mornings came before the morning a bundle came up, and there was nothing in that room that was going to hurry her, and nothing in that room was going to answer her either, and she had stopped expecting either and had not told anybody she had stopped expecting it.
