# The Board That Is Already There

Sixteen days since the ninth stage. Four hundred and forty-seven days in that room. The cage out two hundred and fourteen days. Right arm open four hundred and fifty-four days and it is not going to close. One thousand one hundred and eighty-nine days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in the room. The lamp was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table and the light went a hand’s width along it and stopped, and there were four pieces of wood lying face up side by side about a foot in from the edge of it.

She said her figures out loud at the seventh hour, standing, in the ordinary voice, and then she did the boring half of that morning, and it took her until the ninth hour and she said at about the eighth hour that she was going to do it.

## Nowhere To Put A Thing That Is Not A Figure

She said it out loud first, standing four feet off the near end of that table with her left hand on the top of it, because she has been here before and she knows what it costs to forget.

She said, “I am going to do the thing I did on the fourteenth of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four, which is put there in the wrong place. On that day a fair hand had been putting a day on my arithmetic underneath my own figures for nineteen years and it was right, and I wanted to write something against it, and I did not, because there is no column on this wood for anything that is not a figure.”

She said, “That was a correction under a figure. This is a price under a person. And they are the same mistake.”

She put two fingers of her left hand on the near end of the strip of pale wood.

She said, “A column with a price at the head of it is a column of amounts. I worked that out at the near end of this table on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and I have not taken it back since.”

She said, “So the first thing about that board is that I am not writing it on this wood, and I am not going to write it on a board out of the stack under the middle of this table either.”

She said, “Because there is one paragraph of words in my own hand in that stack that I put there on the twentieth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and cannot tell from the other thirty-nine.”

She said, “And I am not going to do that twice.”

## The Boring Half

And then she sat down, which she has not done in that room in nineteen years, on the floor with her back against the leg of that table and her right arm open in front of her, and she put the board down on the wood a foot in from the edge where the light could get it, and she worked on it line by line until the ninth hour.

She said out loud, at about the eighth hour and twenty minutes, from the floor:

“I am going to do this the way a person does a job they have been given and do not want. One line at a time, and out loud, and the same order every time, and I am not going to get a feeling out of it.”

She said, “Line one: the place at the far side of this table is kept by a person who is there when the figures are said out loud. That obliges a person to be in this room when I speak. It does not say who. It does not say how long. It does not say what they are to do with their hands.”

She said, “Line two: that person does not write them. That obliges somebody to stand here and do nothing at all for nineteen years. That is the whole of the job and there is not one thing to do in it.”

She said, “Line three: that person is not named on this board. That is not an obligation at all. That is a door left open.”

She said, “Line four: the lamp is fed in place of the writer. That obliges somebody to go out of this building and come back with oil, and it does not say how, and it does not say how often, and it does not say what happens on a morning when there is none.”

She said, “Line five: one person is bound to feed it. That is the price and I have said it out loud in this room once and I am not going to say it again.”

She said, “Line six: one binding.”

She said, “Line seven: the record is not vacated, and the rows are true, and what is said out loud at this table is kept.”

And then she stopped and said, “That is the whole of it and I have got it right and it took about a count of a hundred and forty and there is nothing in that board I have not looked at.”

## The Whole Of It, Twice

And then she read the whole of it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, from four feet off the near end of that table with the light coming up off the wood onto it.

She read the first three lines, and she read the last four, and she read all seven again from the top without stopping.

And then she read it a second time.

She said, out loud, before she started the second one, in the ordinary voice:

“I am going to read it again and it is going to take me about a count of ninety, and I am doing it because I read a board out loud in this room on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four and got one thing out of it, and I want to know whether there is a second thing in it.”

And she read it again, and it was the same seven lines, and the light went a hand’s width along the near end of that table and stopped while she did it.

And then she said, out loud, standing:

“That is twice and the board has not changed and I have not changed anything about how I read it, and I got a different thing out of it.”

## What Changed Between The Two Readings

She said, “Here it is and it took me about a count of forty to say and it is the only thing I have got today.”

She said, “The first time I read that board out loud it was the ninth hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four, at the end of a bundle morning, with a boy of sixteen three feet off me and a lamp he had carried up eight hundred and eighty steps, and I heard seven conditions.”

She said, “And this morning I read the same seven lines and there is not one condition in them.”

She put her hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “Every line on that board has a room in it and not a person. The place keeps. The person does not write. The lamp is fed. One person is bound. The record is not vacated. The rows are true. What is said out loud is kept. Every one of those is a thing that is true of a room that is already arranged that way.”

She said, “There is no verb in that board with a person standing behind it. Not one. It does not ask. It does not say you will. It does not say anyone is to. It does not even say that this is being offered, and I have read it every morning since it came up that stair and I could not tell you this morning which of two things it is, because both of them are made of the same seven lines.”

She said, “So it is not an offer. It is a schedule. It is a room saying out loud how it is already built, and the only thing a person can do about a schedule is stop, or be in it.”

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “And that is what I said in this room at the eighth hour yesterday from the middle of that table, and I said it there because I did not want it to go one way only. And I got it out of a different mouth at the same table this morning and I have not been in the middle of anything.”

## The Difference, Plainly

And then she said the plain version, which is the part that costs her, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour.

She said, “I read it wrong the first time.”

She said, “I read it as seven conditions because I have been reading boards in this building as conditions since I was nine years old, and because a thing that says one person is bound to feed the lamp is not a thing a person reads as a description of a room.”

She said, “And it is a description of a room. And a description of a room cannot be refused, because there is nothing in it to say no to. You can say no to *you will*. You cannot say no to *is*.”

She said, “And I have spent four days holding a sentence against a board that cannot hear it, and the sentence is right, and it is exactly the right sentence, and it is aimed at a thing that does not ask for anything.”

She said, “Which means there is nothing on that board for a refusal to push against. It does not want me to stop standing here. It does not want me to go into the place. It wants nothing. It is a room being a room and putting it in writing so that there is a copy.”

She said, “And the only way to refuse a thing that wants nothing is to put something of your own into it.”

And she said that last sentence out loud and then stood there and did not fill the count, and the lamp burned a hand’s width along the near end of that table and twenty-two paces of the rest of it were dark, and she did not go down the second stair that morning and she did not go down the tower’s stair and she did not take the book of eight leaves out of the fold of her shirt.

She left the board lying face up where it lay, and she squared her own strip of pale wood up and squared the board with the name on it, and left the other two where they lay.

And against her fourth rib there were five blank leaves that were still blank, and there were twenty-six days in front of her, and the morning a bundle came up that stair was going to be on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four.
