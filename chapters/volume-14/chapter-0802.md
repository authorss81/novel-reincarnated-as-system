# Already Agreed

She stood four feet off the near end of that table on the thirtieth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four at about the seventh hour and looked at a lamp that has been standing on the top of the wood a foot in from the edge since the seventh of the month before this one.

And she did arithmetic about a morning instead of doing arithmetic about a day, because the day after tomorrow was the first of a month and a man was going to come up nine hundred and four steps in the dark.

The light went a hand's width along the wood and no further.

## Twelve Bundles

She said the count out loud standing, in the ordinary voice, because she says figures before they are anything else.

She said, “Twelve. Eight of them in the light of that cage, one in the dark on the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, and three since with a lamp in this room that is not the room's. Tomorrow is the thirteenth.”

She said, “Forty boards, ten minutes going on to that table from the near end to the far end of it, and thirty for the cloth, and then it goes back down. Four to a minute. That has not changed once in four hundred years and it is not going to change tomorrow and there is no figure anywhere that says it could.”

She said, “And of the three since, the first two I asked a boy of sixteen to bring up that stair, and I asked him out loud and it took me a hundred and forty counts the first time and four seconds the second.”

She said, “And the third one came without me asking anybody anything at all, and it was the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, and the lamp was already standing there, and forty boards went on a table with a flame a foot in from the near end of it.”

She put her hand flat on the wood.

She said, “And tomorrow is the first one where I have not had to ask for anything. That is not the same as being ready. Those are two different things and I have been standing here for about a count of a hundred and forty working out whether I can tell the difference.”

## What She Did Not Clear

And then she stood at the near end of that table and started to do what she has done before every one of the twelve times a bundle has come up that stair she was here for, which is to clear the near end of it, so that whatever comes off that man's arm lands on bare wood.

And she stopped, with her hand on the wood.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“There is a strip of pale wood lying face up on this table with a price at the head of it and a five and a seven in it, in my own hand, and I have cleared that end of this table ten times in this building and I am not clearing it tonight.”

She said, “Because the first thing I did the last time there was a bundle coming was to take that strip off this table and put it on the stone, and the reason I did it was so that forty boards would go down beside a clear piece of wood and I would not have to be looking at a figure of mine while a man walked past me in the dark.”

She said, “And that is not a reason. That is me not wanting a figure of mine to be in a room when forty of somebody else's go through it.”

She put her hand flat on the wood beside the strip.

She said, “So it stays where it is. That is the whole of the preparation and it is not very much and I have made it out loud so that it is in here before it happens.”

## The Second Asking

He came up at about the eighth hour and she asked him standing, in the ordinary voice, before he had got the lamp on the table.

She said, “I am going to ask you for a night again.”

He said, “You are.”

She said, “The first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four. I want you here at the seventh hour with a lamp in your hand, and I am asking you for it in advance and not on the morning.”

He said, “Why in advance.”

She said, “Because there is nothing to be ready for. That is the honest reason and it took me nine days to get to it.”

She said, “In the old dark I knew to the day when a board was going on that table. I did not need to be told and I did not need to have anybody in the room.

And there has been a lamp on this table for fifty-three days and I still do not know what a morning looks like when it is going to matter. The only honest way to deal with not knowing that is to have a person in the room who can put a flame on the wood.”

She waited.

He said, “That is the second night you have ever asked me for.”

She said, “It is.”

He said, “You are not going to tell me what it costs.”

She said, “I am not, because I have already had that out loud on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and I do not have a second version of it.”

She said, “And the other one is the stage, and it is the fifteenth of that month, four days after, and I have not said that out loud to anybody and I am saying it to you at about the eighth hour on the thirtieth of a month because a rule I have is that a thing gets said nine days before it is a thing.”

He said, “You have said the first and not the second.”

She said, “I have said the first and I have worked out the second and they are not the same decision and I am not going to put them in one sentence tonight.”

She waited for about a count of ninety.

She said, “That is nine days early and I am not apologising for it and you can do what you like with it.”

## Worse Than Being Asked

And he said the thing, and she had not thought of it once in nine days.

He said, “You do not have to ask me twice.”

She stood there.

He said, “You asked me on the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and I came. You asked me in the doorway on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four and I came before the fifth hour. And you have asked me twice and both times I have said yes, and so you did not have to ask this one, and you asked it anyway, which means you have asked me twice for one night.”

She said, “Say it the other way.”

He said, “You did not ask me for tomorrow night. You told me what you are going to need and I agreed to it about three weeks ago and you have only now found out.”

She said, “That is the other way and it is worse than being asked.”

She said, “Being asked is a thing that happens once. Being agreed to in advance is a thing that has been happening for a month and I have not known about it and I cannot get back to the day it started.”

He said, “You did not have to find out.”

She said, “That is not a comfort and you know it is not, and I have been in this room three hundred and fifty-two days and I have not once been comfortable and being comfortable is not what I am asking about.”

She put her hand flat on the wood beside the lamp.

She said, “I have got a person in this building who has agreed in advance to do a thing for me and I did not know, and there is nothing in the world I can do about it, and it is exactly the shape of a debt and I have not got a name for it that makes it smaller.”

He said, “You could stop asking me.”

She said, “I could.”

He said, “Then do it.”

She said, “That is a rule I have never had and I am not having it on the thirtieth of a month at the ninth hour because a boy of sixteen has made me feel awkward.

Asking is the only door out of this room. I cannot be given a thing and I can only ask for one, and the day I stop asking is the day that lamp on that table becomes a thing with no name on it and no arrangement behind it, and that is worse than being agreed to.”

She put her hand flat on the wood beside the lamp.

She said, “And I would rather be agreed to than asked. I did not expect that and I have had about a count of a hundred and forty to sit with it and it has not gone the other way.”

## At The Ninth Hour

And at about the ninth hour she said the last of that day out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, with the lamp on the wood and the strip of pale wood face up beside it and twenty-two paces of dark past the end of what the light could do.

She said, “Tomorrow at the second hour forty boards come up that stair and go on that table and a cloth goes over them and a man goes back down without looking at one of them, and he has done that for four hundred years, and he will do it tomorrow.”

She said, “And on the last three of those mornings there was a flame in this room that came up eight hundred and eighty steps in the hand of a person, and not one thing at the far end of that table has ever had anything to do about it.”

She said, “I am not going to find out tonight whether that matters. I am going to stand four feet off this table at the second hour tomorrow and watch, and I am not going to have an opinion about it before I have watched.”

And at about the ninth hour she heard a man coming up the turn of that stair in the dark, on the outside of the low door, with nothing in his hands, coming up to a room with a lamp already burning at the near end of that table.
