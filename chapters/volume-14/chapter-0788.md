# Oil Is Not Days

She did the sum again on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four and got the same answer she had got on the second and on the first, and the answer was that one of them was wrong, and she went and got the boy of sixteen to come up and stand in the doorway of that room with his lamp in his hand so that she could say it out loud to somebody.

Three days since the bundle. Twenty-seven days to the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four.

## Two Counts

She said, “You carried a lamp up that stair on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and I have had light in this room on every night since except eleven, and I have got eleven because I wrote them down at the time and I do not lose one.”

He said, “How many is that.”

She said, “That is not the question I have got and you are going to be annoyed about the question I have got.”

She said, “I have got the question of how many you have been up here, and you have got the number, because you are the one who carried them, and I have got the number of nights, because I am the one who was here. And they are not the same number and they have not been the same number since about the fourteenth and we are both right.”

He said, “Say mine.”

She said, “I am not going to say yours. That was the arrangement on the twelfth and I have not broken it once in twenty-two days and I am not starting at the seventh hour of a night with a lamp in your hand.”

He said, “Say the difference, then. That is not the count.”

She said, “That I will say. One in twenty.”

And then she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table with the light going a hand's width along the wood and no further, and it took her about forty seconds, and she did it in figures and not in a lesson, because that is what she is.

She said, “Over twenty nights, you have been up that stair one time more than I have had light in this room. One. Not one night out of two and not one lamp out of three. One in twenty, and it has been that since the fourteenth, and it was not that before the fourteenth and it is going to be worse and not better by the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four.”

## Neither Of Them Is Wrong

He said, “Then one of us is counting badly.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “That is what a difference is.”

She said, “A difference is a difference and I have had a difference in a figure before and that is when a thing is wrong. You are not counting a night. You are counting a lamp. And I am not counting a lamp. I am counting a night, and a night goes by the count of it, and the count of it is mine, and a lamp goes by a wick, and the wick is yours.”

He said, “I did not know that was how you thought about it.”

She said, “I have not said it out loud to anybody in nineteen years, because there has not been anybody to say it out loud to, and because it is not the sort of thing anybody says.”

He said, “Say the rest.”

She said, “The rest is that a lamp on the seventh of that month was in this room from the seventh hour to about the tenth. The next one was here from the seventh hour to the ninth and a half, because I was saying things out loud and you stayed. And the one after that you put down on the floor at the fourth hour of a first of a month and did not stop once, and that is the lamp that has cost me the most and it is not in the number.”

She said, “So it is not one number in twenty. It is one time in twenty that the thing I do with you takes longer than you had it figured, and you have been figuring it out the only way anybody figures out a lamp, and I have been counting the nights on the other side of the same twenty.”

He said, “So the one in twenty is me.”

She said, “The one in twenty is me. And I am not going to be the one who tells you that, because you have known it longer than I have.”

## What It Decides

And he put the lamp down on the near end of that table, the way he had done on the twelfth, and stood looking at it.

He said, “There is a thing I have to say and I am going to say it once.”

She said, “Say it.”

He said, “On my count I get to the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four.”

She said, “All right.”

He said, “And on your count I do not.”

She said, “I did not say that.”

He said, “You did not say it. I did. And I am telling you what my count is and not asking you anything about yours, and I have not come up that stair tonight to ask you.”

And then he said the second thing, which was the one he had come up for, and she heard it happen in his voice before he had finished it and she went cold and stayed cold.

He said, “And the way my count comes out right is if I am in this room for less time each time.”

She did not say anything.

He said, “Not less often. Less time. The lamp does not care how many nights I bring it up. It cares how long it is lit.”

She said, “Say that again.”

He said, “You wanted me to say it again.”

She said, “I wanted to hear whether you would say it twice without changing it. I have been in rooms where a thing gets said once and then it is said differently for the rest of your life.”

## The Bill For It

And she stood there at the near end of that table and let the whole of what he had just said go past, because she could not stop it and because it was true.

She said, “You are going to be here for less time.”

He said, “I am going to be here for less time.”

She said, “And on the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four I am going to be standing in this room at the fourth hour with about nine hundred and four steps of a stair going away underneath me and forty boards on that table, and I will not know whether you have got oil for it until you are either in the doorway or not in the doorway.”

He said, “That is the arrangement.”

She said, “It is not the arrangement. It has never been the arrangement. The arrangement was four nights out of seven and I gave you that number out loud on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and you took it because you said I had no right to give it. And it is not that number any more and you have not told me what it is now and I am not going to ask you.”

He said, “It is two hours.”

She said, “Two hours.”

He said, “Some nights I can do more. On a night when you are not talking I can do four.”

She said, “And you will do two.”

He said, “I will do two when you are talking.”

And she said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, the thing that went through her, and it was not the two hours.

She said, “You have cut three nights in twenty-three days and you have cut them out of the part of the evening I was using, and you did it because I said a figure out loud to you in a doorway, and you have not said one word about it being my fault.”

He said, “It is not your fault.”

She said, “It is. And I have been doing this for nineteen years and I have never once had anybody spend anything on a figure of mine, and I do not know what to do with it, and I have been standing in the dark with my hand on this table for about a count of ninety trying to work out whether I am allowed to say thank you for it.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “No.”

## Two True Counts

And at the ninth hour she said the thing that had been sitting under all of it since the twelfth, which was the part of the date that the drift had got into.

She said, “The first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four is the day the room does what it does, and I have not put a hand in that cage since the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, so it will strike on nothing again.”

She said, “And there is something I am going to have to do in this room four days after that, and I have not told you about it, and I am telling you about it tonight because the two hours you have just given up are the reason I cannot keep it to myself.”

He said, “Say it.”

She said, “No. Not tonight. I will say it when I have said it out loud in this room on my own for about nine days, because that is the rule I keep, and I have not been in this room two hundred and sixty-six days to break it.”

And she said the last of it standing, at the near end of that table, with the light on the wood a hand's width deep and twenty-two paces of dark past the end of it.

She said, “Two counts. Both of them true. I am not going to correct yours and you are not going to correct mine, and neither of us is going to turn one of them into the other, because a wick is not a night and there is no figure on this mountain that does that.”

She said, “And I have gone nineteen years without one person in the world who had a count that was not mine, and I did not know what that was worth until a boy of sixteen told me a lamp goes by a wick at the seventh hour of a night, and the trouble is that it is now worth something every single night, and it is going to go on being worth something on the nights you are not here.”

She said, “Twenty-seven days. And I have a lamp that does one thing and a boy who has decided to make it do less of it, and neither of those is a night, and I have to stand in this dark on the first of that month anyway.”
