# Thirty-Eight

She went in under that table on the thirtieth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four at about the eighth hour with a lamp in her left hand held low so that the light came off the stone and up the undersides and not onto the tops, and she counted the stack of forty boards face up under the middle of that table, and she did it twice.

The lamp was standing on the top of that table a foot in from the near end, and it had been standing there since the seventh of that month, and the light went a hand's width along the wood and no further, and there was no other light anywhere in that room.

## Thirty-Eight Again

Getting under that table takes her about a count of ninety on her hands and knees and she cannot do it standing up at all. There is a cross-beam under that table about the height of her chest and she goes under it the way she has gone under it every month for ten months.

There is stone dust on her left forearm to the elbow when she comes out the other side, and that dust is the same dust that is on the top of the bundle at the far end of that table, and she has never worked out whether that is one room's dust or two.

Thirty-eight of the forty carried nothing that any hand in this building had put on them.

She said the figure out loud, standing at the near end of that table with her hand wet with stone dust, before she did anything else with it, because the fourth of the six is that a figure gets said before it is a line anywhere.

She said, “Thirty-eight. The same as it was on the twenty-sixth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, which is sixty-four days ago, when I said that figure out loud to a person for the first time in nineteen years.”

She said, “And the other two are still two, and I still cannot tell which two, and I did not expect to, and I am not going to pretend tonight that I looked for it properly.”

She had not looked for it. There is not one instrument in this building or in this county that will take one board out of that stack and say which one it is, and she has said that out loud before and she is not going to say it again tonight.

## The Order Of Them

And then she went over them a third time and this time she was not looking at the faces of the boards, she was looking at the order they are in, which is face up, and then edge on edge, and then face up again, and which is the order they came off that table in the first place.

She said, out loud, from under that table:

“They go on face up in the order they come off. Not the order they are bound in. The order they are walked up that stair in.”

He said, “You have said that.”

She said, “I have said that I did not know it. I have never said that I knew it and never checked it, and there is a difference between those two, and I have been living in the first one since I got into this room.”

She said, “If the order is the walking order then I have been reading that stack as if it were an inventory, and it is not an inventory. It is a list of what came up a stair and when, and I have been turning over forty boards looking for a figure instead of reading the order they are standing in.”

She lay back on her left elbow under that table.

She said, “And I have not worked that out before tonight, and it took a lamp that has been on this table for twenty-three days, and not one thing in this room has been written on in twenty-three days.”

## A Board With Words On It

And then she put the lamp down on the stone and she went over that stack again from the near end to the far end of it, three fingers and a thumb on the edges, and this time she was not looking for cuts.

She was looking for the board she had written on four days before.

She said, out loud, on her knees under that table with her right arm out in front of her:

“There is a board in this stack with my own handwriting on it in words and not cuts, and I put it in on the twentieth of that month on the top of the stack in the same order it was in, and it has been in there ten days.”

She got to the far end of that stack and started again.

“There are forty boards. Thirty-eight of them carry nothing. Two of them carry something of mine and I cannot tell which two. And one of those two carries a paragraph that a boy of sixteen could read out loud in about a count of forty, and it is the one I put in last, and I have gone over all forty twice tonight and I still cannot tell you which one it is.”

She put the lamp down on the stone beside her and stayed on her knees with her right arm out in front of her, and she did not come out from under that table again until she had said all of it.

She said, “And it is worse than that, and I am going to say the worse of it out loud because I have said the other half of it and it would be dishonest to stop there.”

She said, “On the twentieth of that month I sat on this floor at about the ninth hour and asked myself whether that room can read. And I have known since the eighth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four that on the wood it is cuts and not letters, and I have known it for fifty-two days, and I have never once gone and asked what a room does with a sentence.”

And she had put a sentence in there. Out loud, in words, in her own hand, about three lamps and a boy and what is standing on that table and that she did not ask for it. And it was in a room that had been copying figures into a column for four hundred years.

## Nothing Else Has Moved

She said, “And nothing else in here has changed.”

She said, “That lamp has been standing on the top of this table since the seventh of this month and not one thing in this room has been written on while it has been standing there. No figure, no cut, no stroke in the column at the far end of that table. Twenty-three days of a flame a foot in from my own hand and this room has not put one mark anywhere.”

She said, “So either it cannot see it or it does not count it, and I have got no way of telling those two apart, and both of them are worse than the other one.”

She got herself up out from under that table on her left hand and stood at the near end of it with the lamp a foot in from the edge, and she looked along twenty-two paces of that table at a shelf and a cage she could not see.

She said, “And there is a third thing and I want it said out loud and I have been standing here about a count of a hundred and forty deciding whether to say it.”

She said, “On the twentieth of this month I put a paragraph of words into that stack. If that room reads words then a paragraph of words has been sitting in it for ten days. If it does not read words then it is carrying about forty more strokes of nothing than it was carrying before, in a room that is under the middle of a table that has had the same number of boards in it for four hundred years.”

She said, “And neither of those two is a thing I can check. That is the answer to the count of thirty-eight. I have counted it and it is thirty-eight and I have got no way of finding out whether that is the right number, because the only instrument in this county that could find out is standing four feet off me with oil on his fingers.”

She put the top board of that stack back in the same order it was in, face up, and squared it with two fingers of her left hand.

## Ten

And then she told him the number, standing, in the ordinary voice, and it is the number she had been carrying since the twenty-sixth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and had not said.

She waited for about a count of a hundred and forty first, and he waited with her, and neither of them said anything in it, and then she said it.

She said, “I am going to give you a figure and it is one I have never said out loud to anybody, and I want it said before I say it, which is that I have never said it.”

He said, “Then say it.”

She said, “Ten.”

She said, “I have put my hand on every one of those forty boards on the second of a month in this room since the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four, which is the twenty-fourth of the whole three hundred and twenty-two I have been standing in here. I have done it ten times. Once a month, on the second, for ten months running, and there has not one time of the ten been in front of anybody.”

She said, “I have never said that out loud. Not to you in the last four days and not in this room in nineteen years, and the reason is that it is the only count I have ever had that has never been checked by a second person, and I did not know until this morning that it was the only one.”

And she could say ten right now and get it exactly right, because she had said it in her own head on every one of those ten nights. What she could not do was say it out loud to a person and have it be true, and that turned out to be a different kind of true, and she had been treating it as one thing for nineteen years.

He looked at the stack under that table for a while.

He said, “That is every second of a month for ten months.”

She said, “That is every second of a month for ten months, and the first of the month is tomorrow, and a man is going to come up nine hundred and four steps in the dark and put forty boards on this table and go back down without looking at one of them, and on the morning after that I am going to put my hand on all forty of them for the eleventh time.”

She said, “And I have never once been in this room on the second of a month when a bundle was coming.”

She said, “And I have told you that number and I cannot take it back, and I have not got a right to have a figure out of me that I do not hand over on purpose.”
