# It Is Not A Job

She said it out loud on the eleventh of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four at about the seventh hour, before he had asked it once, because she was not going to let him ask it. She was four feet off the near end of that table when she said it and she did not move while she was saying it.

She said, “You are not going to open it.”

He said, “I did not say that.”

She said, “You were going to. You have been at that end of this table twice now and you have looked at the underside of it twice and there is one thing under it and you know there is.”

He said, “You told me there is.”

She said, “I told you there is and I told you it comes apart in two pulls on the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four, and you have been carrying that since the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four, and you have been down here twice in two nights.”

## The Wall She Built Out Of It

And then she said the thing she had been standing at that table with since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and it came out faster than she had it ready, and she let it come out fast.

She said, “On the seventh of that month I told you that there is no name of yours on anything in this room.”

She said, “And I meant it, and it is still true, and I want to know what it is like to be the only person in this building who can be blamed for what it does with a piece of paper, because I have been that person for three hundred and sixty-three days and I did not choose it and I would not give it up.”

He said, “Then let me do it.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “Why not.”

And that is the question the whole night was standing at, and she had known it was coming since the seventh hour and she had it ready and it came out in four seconds.

She said, “Because a person who can be given a thing can be given a job.”

## A Person Who Can Be Given A Thing

She said it standing, in the ordinary voice, with the lamp on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table and the light going a hand's width along it and no further.

She said, “That is the whole of the reason and it has been the whole of the reason since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and you have not heard it in this shape before.”

She said, “If I hand you one job in this room then you have one job in this room. And then somebody could come and ask you to do a second one, and the second one would be on the strength of the first, and there is not one thing above the line that could stop it because there is not one thing above the line that knows either of us is up here.”

She said, “I am not bound and not entered and not sent, and that was said out loud on the sixteenth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and it has not been withdrawn. And I have not asked you for one single thing in nineteen years except two nights and a lamp, and I am not starting it with the only thing in this building I could have somebody else do for me.”

He said, “You asked me for a lamp.”

She said, “I asked you for two nights and I told you I was taking your oil in front of you, and neither of those was a job, and the difference is that I told you what it was going to cost before I did it.”

He said, “This would cost you nothing.”

She said, “It would cost you about the rest of your life and you are sixteen, and I am not going to be the woman who starts it.”

## Five Nights Of Not Asking

And then she told him what she had done with herself for the five nights since the sixth of that month, standing, in the ordinary voice, because she says a thing before it is a line anywhere and because she wanted it in the room before she gave him the answer.

She said, “I have not slept properly since the sixth of that month, when I worked out that you had not asked me on purpose.”

She said, “And the reason is not the bundle. It is that you did not ask me. You have been asking me something every night since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, about a lamp, about a number, about what a thing felt like, about what a record is, about why a lamp at the top of a tower is lit, and you have been down here twice in two nights on the one thing that you have not asked me about.”

She said, “And I worked it out on the third day of those three.”

She said, “Because you asked me for two nights. You asked me twice, in advance, and I had to say the whole arithmetic out loud to get either of them. And you have not asked me once for that.”

He said, “You told me not to.”

She said, “I did not tell you not to. There has never been one sentence in this building in which I told you not to do anything. I said a boy of sixteen is not being sent, and is not being entered, and there is no name of his on anything in this room. And you took the third of those and built a wall out of it and stood behind it and let me stand in front of it for five nights thinking I was the one being careful.”

She stood there.

She said, “That is the most frightening thing anybody has done to me in this room and nothing has ever been done to me in this room.”

He said, “You are not angry.”

She said, “I am not angry at you. I have been angry at you for three days and it stopped on the ninth, on a floor, at about the seventh hour, when I worked out that you had built a wall out of a sentence I said to keep you out and you had built it out of the sentence that was in it for me.”

He said, “That was careless.”

She said, “It was the most careful thing anybody has ever done to me. You found the one line in nineteen years of a woman saying a thing out loud that would hold, and you stood behind it, and you did not take anything.”

## He Does It Himself

And he said, “All right,” and went down onto the stone.

“What are you doing,” she said.

“Nothing you asked me to do.”

And he went along the near side of that table on his hands and his knees, the way she had done twice, and got under the far end of it on his own account with no one in the room who had sent him, and she stood four feet off the near end of that table and did not follow him and did not stop him.

She could not see it. The light went a hand's width along the near end of that table and the far end of it was twenty-two paces of dark, and she heard him go down and she heard the stone under him and she stood there and listened.

It took him about a count of a hundred and forty.

And then he said, from under the far end of that table, in the ordinary voice, and it was not a question and it was not for her:

“It isn’t tied.”

She said nothing.

He said, “It’s two turns. It comes apart in about two pulls. I could feel it.”

And then he came out from under that table on his hands and his knees and stood up at the far end of it and said, “I am not going to do it.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “You did not ask me to.”

She said, “That is what I am telling you. You did not ask me and I did not do it, and both of those are the same thing, and you did not give me a job and I am not standing here holding one.”

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“That is the difference between you and a hand at the far end of this table and it took you five nights to arrive at it, and I have been trying to say it to you since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, and I have never once been able to say it in fewer than about a hundred and forty counts.”

He said, “And I want to say the other half of it. I did not do that because you told me not to. I did it because there was a thing under a table and I am sixteen and no one else was going to, and if I had waited for you to give me leave then it would have been a job and I would have had to think of it as one, and it is not one.”

He said, “It is a bundle. And I put my hand on it and it came apart in about two pulls and I did not pull, and I want that said by me and not by you, because it was mine and you had no part in it.”

She looked at him.

She said, “Say the date.”

He said, “Why.”

She said, “Because in nineteen years there has not been one line in this building that got into a record that I had not said out loud there first. And you have just put one in. And it is yours, and I am not entitled to it, and I am going to ask you for it anyway, and I have not got a right to ask you.”

He said, “The eleventh of this month.”

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“There is a record in this room of a hand that went into that bundle on the eleventh of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and fifty-four and came out of it without pulling, and it is the first line in this record that is not mine, and it is not on a board and it is not in that strip of pale wood and it is not going up any stair.”

She said, “And there is no place in this building for it and I am not going to make one.

I am not going to say anything else about it tonight, because there is nothing in this building that can carry a person being given something they did not ask for, and the nearest thing to it is a debt. I told a boy of sixteen what happens when a person in this building describes a debt, on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, and he said he was not going to call it one.”
