# The Acceptance

Forty-two days since the ninth stage. Four hundred and seventy-three days in that room. The cage out two hundred and forty days. Right arm open four hundred and eighty days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and fifteen days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was a boy of sixteen in the room from about the first hour, because he asked for the second hour of a first of a month on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen and she said yes in about a second, and he had the lamp in his hand and the light was going a hand’s width along the near end of that table and stopping.

She had a folded piece of paper in her left hand from about the first hour.

## The Seventeenth Bundle

Nine hundred and four coming up, and the step at the turn did not change.

Forty boards going on that table in ten minutes, four to a minute. Thirty counts for the cloth, and the cord going round them twice.

And she stood four feet off the near end of that table the whole of that and she did not move while it was going on, and she said out loud at about the second hour that she was not going to read anything out of it while it happened because a thing gets said before it is a line anywhere and not after.

Then the room was quiet for about a count of a hundred and forty.

And then she did the thing she had said out loud at about the ninth hour on the seventeenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four that she was going to do.

## Into The Cloth

She said the sentence out loud first, standing, in the ordinary voice, four feet off the near end of that table, into twenty-two paces of dark, and it was the second time she had said it and she had said it once before the nib went on the paper and once after, and this was the fourth time and she said every word of it the same way.

She said, “I refuse the place at the far side of this table.”

She said, “I refuse a binding.”

She said, “I refuse to be named in the column at the far end of it.”

She said, “No person is going to feed that lamp in place of me.”

She said, “And I am going to keep standing four feet off the near end of this table at the seventh hour and saying every figure I have got out loud in the ordinary voice.”

She said, “And you may do what you like with the paper.”

And then she turned the fourth piece of wood over onto its own face and slid it back about a foot along the wood, into the light, where she had put it on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four.

She said, out loud, standing, “I put that one face down so that a sentence would not go out past it. It is face up now, before the sentence goes, and it is going to be lying face up on that table for as long as I am in this room, and I am not taking the twenty-four days back.”

And then she went to the near end of that table where the cloth and the cord were and she put the folded leaf into the cloth under the forty boards with her left hand, and her right arm was open in front of her the whole time and she could not see what her hand was doing.

He said, “What is that.”

She said, “A piece of paper. It goes down with the boards.”

He said, “I can carry it down.”

She said, “No.”

She said, “And I am not going to tell you not to offer again, because you offered once and I said no, and that is the arrangement and it is said in this room at about the second hour and ten minutes on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four.”

She said, “And I am not going to say anything to you about being in this room this morning, because you have not done anything for me except stand four feet off me with a lamp, and I have been saying the six terms at this table since I was nine years old and I do not say those to a person and I am not going to start.”

## Nine Hundred And Four Going Down

And then the man came back up off that stair, which she heard and did not see, and he took forty boards under his arm in a cloth with a folded piece of paper in it, and he went down.

Nine hundred and four going down in the dark, and not one word out of any of it.

She said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the second hour and twenty minutes:

“That is the first thing I have ever sent out of that room. Nineteen years, and there has not been one line of mine past twenty-two paces of that table, and there is one now at the bottom of nine hundred and four steps, in the hand of a person who has never once been asked to carry anything in his life and has not been asked this morning.”

She said, “And he does not know it is there. He has been coming up that stair with forty boards for longer than I have been in this room and he has never once looked at what is on them, and that is the only thing I know about him, and that is why it went in the cloth and not in his hand.”

And then the room was quiet again.

## And No Stroke

She waited a count of a hundred and forty.

She had said out loud that she would, and she did, and nothing came.

And then she waited about forty counts more, which she had not said out loud she was going to do.

And then there was nothing at all.

She said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, into twenty-two paces of dark, at about the second hour and twenty-five minutes:

“There is no stroke.”

She said, “And I want that said at the second hour and twenty-five minutes on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, standing four feet off the near end of this table, because I am going to be standing here about six hours from now and I am going to want to have said it while it was still fresh.”

She said, “Forty boards went on this table. A cloth went round them and a cord went round the cloth twice. And then nine hundred and four steps went down in the dark. And after the count of a hundred and forty there has been nothing, and I have waited a count of a hundred and forty and about forty counts on top of that, and there is not one stroke.”

She said, “There have been three of those in ninety days. On the first of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four, on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen, and on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four. One each time. Not four to a minute. One, on one board, lasting about a count of nine, starting in the same place the other forty start.”

She said, “And there is not one this morning.”

## What Is Not There

And then she went and stood against the leg of that table with her back to the dark, and she said what she had got out of it, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and it took her about a count of a hundred and forty and she said it in the order she uses, which is figures first and reason after.

She said, “The binding is not being made.”

She said, “And here is how I know, and it is not because a room agreed with me. It is because of how this room writes.”

She said, “The hand at the far end of that table copies what goes across it. That is all it does. It has done that for four hundred years and it does not decide anything, and I have said that out loud in this room on two mornings and it has not changed since.”

She said, “And when it has decided something about a person, it writes.”

She said, “On the first of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four a board came up that stair with a name cut into it in a third hand, and the column at the far end copied it exactly, stroke for stroke, and did not stop on it.”

She said, “That board has never gone back down. It is lying face up about a foot in from the near end of this table, and it is the reason a man can be found now.”

She said, “So this room puts a person into its arithmetic by writing. That is the only road it has. And this morning, forty boards went on that table and there was not one stroke after them, and that means there is no figure to enter, because a refusal that has been granted has nothing to record.”

She stopped for about a count of ninety.

She said, “And I am not going to stand here at the second hour and thirty minutes and tell you it is glad. I do not know that anything in that room is glad and I am not going to give it a face.”

She said, “What I know is what is not there. There is no binding. There is no name in that column that is mine. And the second place at the far side of that table is exactly where it was at the fourth hour on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four, and it is kept, and nobody is in it, and the board that describes it is face up on this table about a foot in from the near end.”

She said, “And the lamp on this table is going to be fed by a boy of sixteen who has not been asked for it once in nineteen years and who is not bound and not entered and not sent, and there is no line anywhere in this building that says where he goes in the evening, and nothing that happened this morning changed any of those three things.”

## Twenty-Four Days Exactly

And then she stopped, and went and stood four feet off the near end of that table, and picked up the strip of pale wood off the wood and read it by touch in a hand’s width of light, which is a thing she has been able to do since she was nine years old.

And then she said out loud, at about the ninth hour, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“There is a price at the head of that column and the same price at the head of that strip of pale wood. Ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes. A price does not date.”

She said, “And I worked out the other figure this morning at about the seventh hour, before he came, with the wood in my left hand, and it came out wrong.”

She said, “Twenty-four days. Exactly twenty-four days, and not an hour over and not an hour under, for the first time in nineteen years that figure at the head of that column has been right on the morning I read it.”

She said, “And I am saying it out loud in a dark room at the ninth hour to a person who has three fingers and a thumb on the hand he writes with and knows nothing about any of it, and there is not one instrument in this building that would have seen it, and the row at the far end of that table is exactly what it was at the seventh hour.”

She said, “A price does not date. That is what I have been saying out loud in this room for four hundred and seventy-three days and today it came out exactly right and nothing came of it.”

## The Cold, And Then The Rest Of It

And then she said what she was feeling, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour and ten minutes, and it took her a count of forty to get to the plain part and she got there.

She said, “It is the cold.”

She said, “It came up out of the stone out of my ankles on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen and it has been there on and off since and it is not new and I have said that.”

She said, “And it is not the cold of a person who has won. I want that said before anything else about it. I do not know what it is the cold of.”

She put her hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “It is the cold of a person standing in a room at the ninth hour with a piece of paper nine hundred and four steps below her that she cannot get back and was not asked to get back and would not if she could.”

She said, “I said no in this room on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen and it could not be unsaid.”

She said, “And on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four I wrote the refusal on a leaf of a book that has been against my fourth rib for nineteen years, and it has gone down that stair this morning in a cloth with forty boards on the top of them.”

She said, “And it is the first thing I have ever sent anywhere at all.”

She said, “And it cannot be called back.”

She said, “And I would not call it back. That is the second half of that sentence and I am saying it out loud in this room standing up in the ordinary voice, and there is nobody here to hear it but a boy who is not bound and not entered and not sent, and a room that has not said a word in four hundred years.”

And the boy took the lamp down to him and stood with it at the near end of that room and did not say anything, and she did not ask him to.

And the light went a hand’s width along the near end of that table and stopped.

There were three pieces of wood lying face up side by side about a foot in from the edge of it, and there was a fourth lying face up about a foot further along where she had put it back that morning.

There were four empty pairs of cuts under a strip of pale wood that nobody had put a figure in that morning, and there was a room at the top of eight hundred and eighty steps that she could not be alone in, and there was nothing on that table that had come back up the stair.
