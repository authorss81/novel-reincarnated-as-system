# Four Feet From A Place

She said all three of them out loud on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, twenty-two paces of dark away from the thing she had been talking about, and she came back up the first four hundred and forty at the seventh hour of the eighth and nothing about any of the three had changed.

She said that out loud before she did anything else, because the fourth of the six is that a thing gets said before it is a line anywhere.

## The Light Is The Exception

The lamp was standing where it had been standing since the seventh of that month, on the top of that table about a foot in from the near end, and the light went a hand's width along the wood and stopped.

There was a strip of pale wood face up on that wood beside it with a figure at the head and the same figure in the first pair of cuts under the head and a five in the second and a seven in the third, all in her own hand, and there were nine pairs of cuts under that one with nothing in any of them.

Forty boards face up in a stack under the middle of that table. A bundle of papers under the far end, corded round twice and not knotted. A bar nine feet nine flat against the wall on the left, broken end towards the door. A cage on a shelf at the far end, out for sixty-seven days.

And one flame, about a foot in from the edge of that table, belonging to a boy of sixteen, put there by her, filled by him, and standing on a table that is four hundred years old.

She looked at it for about a count of ninety.

She said, out loud, “That is the only thing in this room that is not mine.”

## What A Lamp Is For

She had not worked that out yet on the seventh and she worked it out on the eighth, standing, with the strip of pale wood in front of her and her right arm open at her side, and it took her about a hundred and forty counts and she said the whole of it out loud when she had it.

She said, “A lamp does not make a thing visible. I have been treating that lamp as if it did, since last night, and it does not, and that is why it has not answered anything.”

She said, “What it does is smaller than that and it is this. It puts one piece of one thing where my hand is. That is all it has ever done. On the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four you put it on this table and I moved it two inches in and I told you that was the first thing anybody had done to this table in two hundred and forty-four days that was not taking something off it, and I was proud of that, and two inches.”

She put her left hand flat on the wood and put her fingers on the edge of the lamp's base.

She said, “The rest of this table is exactly as dark as it was before you came up that stair. Twenty-two paces of it. A hand's width of it is lit and the other twenty-one paces and the rest of that foot is not, and it is not a little less dark than it was. It is the same dark.”

And that was the thing, and she said it last, because it had taken the longest and she wanted the other two first.

She said, “What the lamp is for is this: it makes a place where I can put my hand and know what my hand is on. That is all. I have not got a use for one that is any bigger than that and I have been asking it for one every day since the seventh.”

## The Procedure

And then she laid out the procedure, once, in the shape of a procedure, because she says a thing before it is a line anywhere and because a procedure that has not been said is not a procedure.

She said, “Here is what I am going to do and I am going to say all of it before I do any of it.”

She said, “You are going to light it at the seventh hour on a night you are here. I am going to stand at the near end. You are going to pick it up in your right hand and walk it along the top of that table to the middle of it, which is half of the whole thing, and put it down.

And I am going to walk behind you the whole way with my left hand on the wood, because if it goes out on that table then this room has no light in it until you are next, and I am not going to have that happen twice.”

He said, “Then I will walk in front and you will hold the lamp.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “Why not.”

She said, “Because then the oil that is not there comes out of a hand I cannot see, and I have been in that room long enough to know what I am looking at.”

## The Floor In The Dark First

And then she went and learned the floor before he brought the lamp to it, which took her about two hundred counts and which she did not tell him about until after.

She went down onto the stone at the near end of that table and went along the near side of it on her hands and knees in the dark, with her right arm out in front of her and the bar nine feet nine flat against the wall on the left of her the whole way, and she put her left hand out every third pace to find the leg of the table.

It is the same leg she put her left hand on every night for three hundred days and she found it every time, and the floor between the near end and the middle of that table has a flag in it about nine paces along that is loose and comes up under a knee if you go on your hands and knees and do not know about it, and she found it tonight for the first time because she had never gone that far with her knees on the ground.

She said, out loud, on her knees on the stone with her right arm out in front of her:

“There is a loose flag nine paces in and it is going to put me on the floor on the night you carry that lamp down this side of the table, and I would rather have found it tonight.”

She came back to the near end and got herself up on her left hand and stood four feet off that table and put her hand flat on the wood.

She said, “And the lamp goes on the top of the table and not in your hand while we walk. That is the other half of the procedure and I want it said before we do it. Set it down at the eleventh pace, both hands off, and stand there while I get round to it.”

He said, “Then neither of us has it.”

She said, “Then neither of us has it. And if it goes out at the eleventh pace then we are both standing at the middle of a dark table with a dead lamp on it and I am going to want to be able to say that was the plan.”

## What She Asked For

He came up at about the eighth hour and she said the request out loud standing, in the ordinary voice, before he had put anything down.

She said, “I am going to ask you for one night.”

He said, “You are.”

She said, “I want the lamp carried to the middle of that table. One night. Not to stay there, because I cannot hold it there and if it stops where I put it then it burns down on the wood until there is nothing left in it. To be carried there, put down, looked at, and carried back.”

She waited. She had expected to have to say why, and she had it ready, and the not having to say it was the thing she had not expected.

He said, “Which night.”

She said, “Any night you are here.”

He said, “That is two nights then, because I am here on this one and the next one is not mine.”

She said, “Then it is two.”

And that was the whole of the asking. It took four seconds, the second one did, and the first one had taken about a hundred and forty counts on the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, and she said that out loud too, standing, with her hand on the wood, because she does not let a thing stand in that room that has not been put into words where it exists.

She said, “That is the second time I have asked a person for something in nineteen years and the first time it has taken four seconds, and I do not know what to do with that and I am not going to work it out standing here.”

He said, “You are not frightened of asking.”

She said, “No. I have not been frightened of asking once in nineteen years and I have had four seconds tonight to find out that the thing I have been frightened of was not that.”

## The Width Of My Own Hand

And at about the ninth hour she did the thing she had been standing there wanting to do since the seventh hour, which was to find out how far the light actually went, in a figure, because she says figures before they are anything else and she had been saying a hand's width out loud since last night without ever once checking what a hand was.

She turned her left hand over flat on the wood with the lamp on the far side of it, and she put the edge of the lamp's base against the first joint of her little finger, and she moved her hand away a count at a time and watched her own hand out of the light.

And when her hand went out of it, that was the end of it, and the wood after that was the same as the wood twenty-two paces off.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“It is the width of my hand. From the knuckle of the little finger to the second joint of the thumb. That is a hand's width and it has been a hand's width since last night and it is the width of my hand and it is the first figure I have had in this room that has got a person in it.”

She said, “And I have been measuring this room with myself since I got into it, and this is the first time one of those measurements has been a figure instead of a habit.”

She put her hand flat on the wood one last time and left it there.

She said, “And it is the width of my hand, and I am going to be carrying this lamp twenty-two paces before I have found out what else it is the width of.”
