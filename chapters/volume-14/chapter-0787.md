# The Thirtieth

She said the six terms out loud on the thirtieth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four at the ninth hour, standing up, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table, one at a time, the way she has said them in every room she has ever said them in, and there was no daylight in that room and there was no lamp in it either.

## The Second One Again

She said the first one and it came out the way it has come out every day since she was nine years old.

One thing at a time.

And she got as far as the second one and stopped, and she stood there in the dark for a count of ninety before she said it, which she has never once done at any point in the six terms in nineteen years.

In daylight.

And then she said it out loud, to a room with nothing in it, standing up, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “There is no daylight in this room. There is a lamp in a cage at the far end of that table and it went out on the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and it is not coming back on its own, and there is not one window in this building that I have ever been able to see from the inside, and tonight there is not even a lamp in my hand.”

She said, “On the twenty-ninth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four I said out loud that I would say this one on the thirtieth as well, and that it would be the last time I said it in this room. I said it on the thirtieth. It was not the last time, and the reason it was not the last time is that on the first of that month I put the lamp out myself.”

She said, “And tonight it is not a mistake either. It was a mistake for a long time and I worked that out standing in that light with a person at the far end of this table, and I have had it in front of me ever since. Tonight there is nothing in here for the term to be true in. I am going to say it anyway, and that is a worse thing and a different thing, and I am not going to stand in the dark and pretend to myself that the two of them are the same.”

## What She Did With The Other Five

And then she said the third one, standing up, in the ordinary voice, in the dark.

Standing up.

And the fourth one.

You say it out loud as you go. Every line. Before it is a line anywhere else.

And then she got to the fifth one and did not say it out loud, and she stood there for a count of a hundred and forty and worked out why, and said the working out as well, standing, because that is the shape of her and she does not get to stop being it on the nights it costs her.

She said, “The fifth one says that if there is one in here that I have not heard first then it is not a record, it is a thing I did on my own, and there is no version of this where I find out about it later. And the sixth one says that if I put down one word that is not true I will know it inside a day and there is no road back for either of us.”

She said, “I have said those two in this room every day for nineteen years and I know every word of them tonight, and I am not going to say them out loud on a night when I have not got the second one, because I would be standing on four of them and not being able to check whether I have heard the other two.”

She said, “That is me, on my own account, on the thirtieth of a month, at the ninth hour, with no light in this room and no one else in it.”

And then she said the last of it, which was the thing she had actually come to the end of the month for.

She said, “Six terms. There is no seventh and nobody has ever asked me for them. I have said four of them out loud tonight, and one of those four is one I cannot keep, and I did not say the other two at all. That is the worst night I have ever had on them and I am not going to put it anywhere.”

## The Second Hour Of The First

And she did not go down either stair that night and she did not lie down, and at the second hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four she heard it.

Not twenty-nine days off. The night before.

She said it out loud to the dark as it came, standing at the near end of that table, because she cannot let a thing stand in a room that has not been put into words where it exists.

She said, “He is coming up the turn.”

And she stood there and listened to a loaded floor come up out of the wall behind that lamp’s shelf, and she said, “I cannot hear the stair. I can hear the shelf.”

And she said, “The shelf came off the frame at the middle of that table and there are hands going along the wood from the near end to the far end of it at about four to a minute, and that table has not been worked in front of me with nothing to see it by at any hour in two hundred and sixty-three days, and I am hearing it now.”

And she stood four feet off the near end of that table in the dark and let it happen, and she counted, because she counts.

Ten minutes for the boards. She counted that on a first of a month in daylight once with a person watching her do it, and she counted it now, and it was ten minutes again.

Thirty for the cloth.

And then the shelf settled and the footsteps went back into the wall and the room was a dark room again, and not once in any of that had anything in it come near her or stopped at the end of that table.

## The Fourth Hour

And at the fourth hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and sixty-four she stood at the near end of that table and said out loud the thing she had been counting down to since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four.

She said, “That is the hour the light comes down to the cut.”

She said, “Fifty-eight of my own hand from the top of that cage to the line cut in the stone and two of my own hand a day and fifty-eight divided by two is twenty-nine, and twenty-nine days from the second of a month is the first of the next one. That has been true every month since before this building was a building, and it is true tonight, and the light is not coming down to that cut tonight, because there is nothing in that cage and there is nothing in this room to light it.”

She said, “And I am the one who put the last one out and I did not put one in, and I have not put one in since, and so the clock in that room has struck on nothing every month since the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and tonight it struck on nothing with a person standing in it.”

And then he came.

## What He Came At Four For

He came in out of the low door at somewhere about the fourth hour, which is five hours before the hour he had told her at the near end of that table on the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four that he would be here, and he came in with a lamp and the light went along the stone and up the leg of that table and there were forty boards lying face up in the middle of it.

She said, “It is the first.”

He said, “It is.”

She said, “You said the ninth hour.”

He said, “I said the ninth hour. I did not say I would not be here at four.”

And she stood in that light and did not say a single word for about a count of a hundred and forty, because there are forty things on that table in that building for the first time in nine years and the only light in the world that has ever been on them is in the hand of a boy of sixteen who came up four hundred and forty steps with no rail on them five hours early, because he worked out somewhere on that stair that a day he had promised her was going to come round whether he was standing on it or not.

She said, “That is the whole of what that oil is for, and I have understood it on my own for seven nights and I have had it wrong.”

He said, “What is the whole of what it is for.”

She said, “Light in this room on a first of a month. That is all it has ever been for and it took a boy with three lamps to show me.”

And then she went along that table with him in the light, ten paces to the middle, and put her left hand flat on the top board and looked down the face of it.

And the top board was blank.

And so was the one under it, and the one under that, and she said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, with the light on the wood and a person four feet off her, that she had been putting her hand into that stack for nineteen years and it had taken her until the twenty-sixth to find out that two out of forty were written on, and that she could not tell which two.

And then she went back to the near end with the lamp and got down onto the stone and went along the floor to the middle of that table and put the light under it, which is a thing she had promised herself she would do on the night of the twenty-sixth and had not expected to do with a person standing at the end of it.

And it took her about a count of ninety, and the top board of that stack was blank in light, and she did not go any further than the two she knew were in there, because she could not tell which two they were and there is not one instrument in this building or in this county that would take one board out of that stack and tell her.

And the man had gone back down eight hundred and eighty steps with a cloth under his arm, and the lamp in the cage at the far end of that room had been out for thirty days, and nothing in that building had said one word to either of them.
