# A Question With Two Halves

She asked it at the sixth hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 2,054, standing, in daylight, at the east end of the bench in a copying room with a lamp burning in a cage on the fourth landing overhead and a boy of sixteen four feet away at the rack.

She said the terms out loud first, because she has said them out loud since the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,024 and she will not put a question into the air in this building without them.

She said, “One at a time. In daylight. Standing.”

The boy said, “I know. I have been in the room.”

She said, “You are not the one I am saying them to.”

He said, “All right.”

## Eight Years Of Not Asking

She said, “I have counted with you for eight years.”

She said, “In eight years I have asked you about yourself perhaps a hundred times and I have asked you about the world once, and the once was at a turn in the dark about whether you were something that had been made, and you told me you were made to do one thing and that you were not any good at it.”

She said, “And I have not asked you one question about what is in a building with a lamp in it. Not once. In five years of sitting on that end of that bench.”

He said, “Why not.”

She said, “Because every time I got to the edge of it I found that the question I wanted was two questions, and I was not going to put two into the air, and so I put none, and then the next day it was a day further off, and that is the whole of eight years and it has cost me a room.”

She said, “And I am going to put one into the air this afternoon, and I have had it in my head since the eighth hour of the tenth, and it has a first half and a second half and I am going to give you the first half and I am going to want the second half.”

He said, “Ask the first.”

## What She Put Into The Room

She said, standing, at the east end of the bench:

“Can a person be announced without being designated.”

The boy put his brush down. He did not do that at anything else all morning. He put it down square with the edge of the bench and he came round the end of it, which he has not done in front of her in five years.

He said, “Say the second half.”

She said, “That is the question. The question has two halves and the second half is the same question, and the whole of it is one sentence, and I am going to say it as one sentence because I have said the terms and one sentence is one thing.”

He said, “All right.”

She said it again, the whole of it, once, in the room, with a witness she had not asked for, because a thing said once to one man in a head is not a thing that has happened and she has been told that in a yard and did not like it and he was right.

She said, “Can a person be announced without being designated.”

## One Half And A Stop

He said, “I have got half of it.”

She said, “Say the half you have got.”

He said, “Not yet. Say it once more in a minute, because a question said once has not been said, and I want to have it twice before I answer it.”

She said, “That is the second time you have asked me to say a thing twice.”

He said, “I am aware of that.”

She said, “All right.” And she said it a third time, in the room, in daylight, standing, at the sixth hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 2,054: “Can a person be announced without being designated.”

And then he said the first half.

He said, “An announcement goes where there is something to receive it.”

She waited. He did not go on.

She said, “That is not half of an answer. That is the beginning of one.”

He said, “It is half.”

She said, “Then give me the other half.”

He said, “I cannot get to the other half.”

## What She Asked Him And Did Not Ask Him

She said, “Try.”

He said, “I have been trying since the eighth hour of the tenth. I can get to a room in a tower and I cannot get past the door of it, and that is not a thing I am doing to you. I have been trying for two days and I get to the door and there is a second half on the other side of it and I cannot find out what it is.”

She said, “Then you have told me that a thing goes to a place and not what the place is for.”

He said, “No. I have told you the half that is about the going. The half that is about the arriving is not in me.”

She said, “Why is it not in you.”

He said, “Because the arriving is not a thing anybody can do on their own. It needs a room and a person in the room and a hand, and I have none of the three, and you have two of them and not a hand.”

She said, “That is not an answer.”

He said, “It is the end of the first half and it is where the first half stops, and I have been carrying the second half for thirty-one years and I do not have it, and if I had it I would have given it to you at the turn in the dark without being asked, because that is what I have wanted to do since you said you were going up.”

## What The Boy Heard

The boy said, from the rack, after a while: “You said designated.”

She said, “I did.”

He said, “I have copied nine announcements in two years and every one of them names a place and a day and a thing to be done there on that day, and not one of them names a person. Not one. I have been looking at the word in a column for two years and I could not have told you until this afternoon what the word was for.”

She said, “Say what you think it is for.”

He said, “It is the word that goes on a board with a number against it.”

She said, “That is right.”

He said, “Then you have just asked, out loud, standing up, in my room, whether a thing can happen to a person without their number going on a board.”

She said, “Yes.”

He said, “And you cannot take it back.”

She said, “I cannot take it back.”

He said, “All right,” and picked the brush up again, and then put it down again, and said, “I am sorry. I want to say one thing and then I will not say it.”

She said, “Say it.”

He said, “I have been in this room two years and a man has died in it and there has not been one person on this hill but him and me, and I have made up my days into columns and gone down with bundles and come back up empty, and if you can be announced without being put on a board then I could have asked the carter what my bundle was for and got an answer, and if you cannot then every day I have written down is only ever a day.”

She said, “Your days are a day.”

He said, “Are they.”

She said, “They are a day in a column in a bundle that a carter of sixty-four carried up a hill four times in thirty-one years, and that is a day and not nothing, and I am not going to let anybody tell you it is nothing.”

## The Second Question She Did Not Put

She stood at the end of the bench and felt the second question come up her chest the way it came up on the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,024 and on the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,034, which is the thing she has never done and has said out loud twice that she will not do.

It was the right question. It was standing there ready and it was the better one, and asking it would not have been asking two things at the same time, because the first question had been asked and answered and was in the room.

That is the shape of it and she has thought about it since. The lever does not look like the lever from a distance. It looks exactly like being efficient.

She said, out loud, in the room, to him and not to the boy, so that he would know the refusal was a refusal and not an oversight:

“I am not going to ask you a second thing. You have told me the half you have got and I am going to leave it there and I am not going to ask you again until you have got the other half or until I have been up the stair myself, and one of those two is going to happen first and I do not know which.”

He said, “You have said that before.”

She said, “I have said it at least four times and two of them were about you.”

He said, “Then I will hold it.”

She said, “Hold it where.”

He said, “I do not know. I hold things and then they are somewhere and then I am asked about them.”

She said, “Then I will hold it with you. A question said out loud, standing, in daylight, once, with a witness in the room, cannot be un-asked, and I have said it in this building and the boy has heard it, and it is on me now and not on you and that is the whole of what I came up the hill for.”

## What She Said To The Room Afterwards

She did not go down the stair. She sat on the bench under the window with her hands flat on it, and the boy went back to the rack, and she sat there for a count and a half and worked out what had happened, which is what she does instead of feeling things, and it does not work as well and she has never stopped doing it.

She said, out loud, at the bench, in daylight: “I asked a question about the world. Not about him. About the world.”

She said, “In eight years. And it took nine days of road and a hill and a boy and a carter to get me to the place where I could ask it, and I have asked about a hundred things about myself and about a boy and about a man who is dead, and it is the first one that has ever been about the thing I am actually here for.”

She said, “And I have got the half that is about the going, and the half that is about the arriving is up four flights of stair that nobody in this building has gone up in nine years, and he has told me he cannot finish it, and he has told me that the reason is that the arriving needs a room and a person in the room and a hand.”

She said, “He does not have a hand. He has not had a hand since he was nineteen at a fire in the east.”

She said, “I have got a hand. Four fingers and a thumb on the right one and the index gone, and an arm that is open four days in five, and a hand that cannot write a false line, and it is the only instrument of the three that exists in nine days of road.”

She said, out loud, to the bench and the window and the lamp in the cage above her, at the seventh hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 2,054: “So the second half of it is up the stair, and it is the same shape as the whole of it, and I asked the question standing up because I did not want there to be a day between the asking and the going.”

He said, “You are not going today.”

She said, “No. I am going when I have stopped wanting it to be there.”
