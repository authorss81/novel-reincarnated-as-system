# The Woman Who Was Right

She sat on the bench at the second hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 2,054 and she was on it through the whole day, and the woman of sixty was on it through the whole day too, because it is the same bench and there is only one of them.

She had said on the eighteenth that she would sit on it for a day first, and she was on it, and the woman of sixty said nothing about that at all, which was correct.

The bar was on the ground against the bench leg. There are nine men and two carts in the yard some days and four of them on others. A cart went west at the seventh hour with stone in it.

A woman she did not know went east at the ninth hour with a basket and did not look at the two posts, and did not look at her either, which is the ordinary traffic of a turn and is the only thing that has ever happened on this road in nine years.

## What She Was Trying To Work Out

She had one thing to work out and she had been carrying it since the sixteenth of the month beginning 2,054 at the top of a stair, and it was this.

She can read. She has read boards since she was twelve. She is faster than anybody she has ever stood next to and she does not misread and she has caught four men in three counties in a false line and had them go back and do it again.

And there is a board four hundred yards east of this bench that has had nothing on it since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,814, and there are four hundred people in this county who cannot read, and one of them is the woman on the bench next to her.

She had not worked that out in thirteen days of road and four days of tower and nine years of boards, and it took her until the fifth hour of the twentieth to get to the bottom of why she had not, and the reason was that she has spent nine years thinking of a board as a thing a person is put on, and she had never once in her life thought of it as a thing that goes out to everybody else.

## What A Board Is Not For

She said, at the fifth hour, to the grit and the two posts and the woman of sixty, out loud, on a turn on a store road:

“A board is not a roof. I have known that for years and I have never once said it out loud and it is the reason I have never been on one.”

She said, “It is not wages, either. A board is the thing that says where the wages came from and to whom they went.”

She said, “It is not a hand on your back, and not a keeper, and not a place to keep a place by staying in it. It is a line in a hand on a piece of wood that goes up on the first of a month.”

The woman of sixty said, “Go on.”

She said, “And it is not for the person on it. I have been saying for years that it is the only thing anybody has ever paid me for, and that is true, and it is the wrong end of it.”

She said, “It is not for the person on it at all. It is for everybody else.”

## What Everybody Else Is For

She said it out loud and it took about three seconds and then she had to sit there.

She said, “A board goes up on the first. It stands in the open. Every person who comes along that road reads it, and not one of them is there for me and not one of them knows my name, and they all find out it.”

She said, “That is what it is for. It is a thing that tells a whole road where I am, four hundred yards away, at a cost of one sixth of a penny in ink, and not one person on the road has to do anything about it.”

She said, “And I have been off every board in the world for nine years and have not been able to be paid, or given a place, or given anything, and I have walked a hundred and fifty miles in thirteen days because none of those things happens to a person that four hundred people cannot see.”

The woman of sixty said, “That is a long way round for a thing that a child could tell you.”

She said, “It is. And I have not got a child on any road I have ever walked.”

## Saying It Out Loud To A Bench

She said, “Can I say a thing out loud here that is only for me.”

The woman of sixty said, “You have been saying things out loud to me for nine days.”

She said, “Not like this one.”

And then she said it, at the sixth hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 2,054, sitting on a bench on a store road with four hundred yards of grit in front of her and a woman of sixty beside her:

“My name is Ning Yan.”

She said it the way she would say it into a slate, which is flat, and even, and without anything on the end of it, and she said it three times, because three is what it takes to find out whether a name sounds the same the fourth time.

She said, “That is how it goes on a board. Not loud. Not with anything on it. Twice is enough for anybody and three is enough for me.”

She said, “And it is four sounds, and it takes about the length of one breath at four counts, and there is nothing in it.”

The woman of sixty said, “You have not said what you are called after.”

She said, “No.”

The woman of sixty said, “I have known you nine days and I have not known what to call you, and I have not asked twice, because you told me on the first day that you were not going to be asked twice, and I do not ask twice.”

She said, “You are right and I am not going to tell you today.”

The woman of sixty said, “All right.”

She said, “I have said my own name out loud four hundred times to a bar in a yard and to a wall and to a boy in a tower, and I have said it in order to hear it. And I have never once said it on a road to a person, because on a road a name is a thing you say to a person and it means you know them, and you do not know me.”

The woman of sixty said, “I have known where you are for nine days.”

She said, “That is not the same and I do not want it to be the same, and that is what I have been sitting here all day working out.”

## The Last Name That Went Up

The woman of sixty said, “Say the number.”

She said, “What number.”

The woman of sixty said, “The last day there was a name on the two posts. You have said it four times in two days and you have said it as though it were a fact about a road. It is not a fact about a road. It is a fact about me.”

She sat on a bench on a store road and did not move for about ten seconds.

She said, “Say it as a fact about you.”

The woman of sixty said, “My name went up on the second of a month beginning 1,806. I worked on the road out of Lowwater for nineteen years and I was on that board for four of them and I was paid at the rate on the seventh, the seventeenth and the twenty-seventh like a person.”

She said, “And?”

The woman of sixty said, “And then there was a closure on this ground and the west road stopped and there was no work to send anybody on and a board is not a wage, it is a notice, and a notice about a person who is not doing anything is a notice that gets walked past.”

She said, “How long ago.”

The woman of sixty said, “Nine years. The eighteenth of the month beginning 1,814 was the last first of a month with anything on it and I watched a man of forty take the slate out of the frame and carry it away under his arm.”

She said, “You have been on that bench nine years.”

The woman of sixty said, “I have been on that bench nine years because it is where the board is and I have not got anywhere else that I would be recognised in, and that is the whole of it, and I am not going to tell you not to go on it.”

## What She Was Told That She Had Not Worked Out

The woman of sixty said, at the ninth hour, without moving, on a bench on a store road:

“I was seen for four years and I did not know it was a kindness and I have had nine years to work out that it was not.”

She said, “It was not what?”

The woman of sixty said, “A kindness goes both ways. A notice does not. When my name was on that board, four hundred people knew where I was, and not one of them came and not one of them wrote to me, and when the road stopped they all went back to not knowing in one morning, and the last of them stopped knowing on the first.”

She said, “And here is the thing you have not worked out and it is the thing I have wanted to tell you for nine days.”

She said, “Say it.”

The woman of sixty said, “You are going to go and be seen. And being seen is what you have wanted since you were about fourteen years old and you are right to want it. And it will last until the first of the next month, and then it will be over, and you will still be standing at the bottom of the same hill with a bar on your shoulder.”

She said, “And the ones that are on it are not taken off the board and put somewhere else. The ones that are on it are on it and everybody who comes along the road knows, and the day the notice stops, they stop.”

The woman of sixty said, “I am not going to tell you what happens to a notice after the first.”

She said, “I am not going to ask you,” and she did not ask, and the woman of sixty did not say one word more about it, and the two of them sat on a bench on a store road through the whole of the nineteenth of the month beginning 2,054 while a cart went west and a woman went east with a basket and twelve people went past the two posts four hundred yards east without one of them looking at them.
