# No Way Of Knowing Where To Come

Seven days with a lamp burning in a room she cannot get into, and the whole of what happened in them was that nothing happened again.

The days were the third, the fourth, the fifth, the sixth, the seventh, the eighth and the ninth of the month beginning 2,204.

## The Seven Days

She went up four flights every one of the seven days, at the second hour, and put her eye to the grille, and the lamp at the far end of the long table was burning every one of the seven days.

The bar was across the outside of the door every one of the seven days and the dust on the top of it was not broken anywhere, and the door did not move, and there was no sound on the other side of it of anything at all, and there were no chairs in that room because there have never been chairs in that room.

She sat on the top step for a count of forty each time. That is where you sit. Her breath goes to nine at the top of that stair and she had it at seven and it came down to four on the fourth flight and stayed there.

And she did not try to find out what it costs, because she had worked that out at the second hour of the day before in nine seconds and saying it out loud does not make it smaller.

What she did instead was get up on the fourth of the month beginning 2,204 and copy nine sheets with the boy of sixteen, and on the sixth she read the sixth shelf from the left out of four hundred volumes, and on the eighth she sat on the copying floor and did nothing at all for two hours and he did not come and ask her why.

## What A Person In A Room Is For

The thing that came at her in the five days was not the lamp. It was the arithmetic of it, and she did it standing up at the second hour on the seventh of the month beginning 2,204, at the bottom of the stair, with the boy of sixteen in front of her and the lamp over his head.

She said, “How does anybody come up this hill.”

He said, “They come up the mountain road.”

She said, “Who.”

He said, “The carter. Once a month, on his own time, and he has done it for thirty-one years.”

She said, “And if a message came up here about me.”

He said, “It would come up with him, and I would come and tell you, and that is all there is to it.”

She said, “And if I was not in the building.”

He thought about that for a while, and he is sixteen and he has been in that building two years and he has not had a person in it in nine years.

He said, “Then he would come and there would be no one, and he would take the message back down, and he has not got anywhere to take it to, because the only people in this county who have ever asked about anybody are the four clerks at the seat and they have never had anybody to ask about.”

## Two Records That Do Not Meet

And she asked him one more thing, one question at a time, in daylight, standing up, because she cannot help it and because he is the only person in nine days of road she is allowed to ask.

She said, “When I am gone from here, how does anybody know there was a woman in this building.”

He said, “They do not.”

She said, “Is there a column.”

He said, “There is a column on the board by the door and it is days and not names. We are nine of us on it and the days go in every night and I do mine and the four of the others do theirs and one does two and it has been that way for two years.”

She said, “And no one comes and reads it.”

He said, “Not one person comes at all.”

She stood there at the bottom of a stair in a tower four miles up a mountain and worked out two records, standing, in daylight, with the lamp in a cage over a boy of sixteen’s head.

His record has nine names in it that are not in it and he knows all nine and she knows seven of them. Hers has two hundred people on a ground who have found her twice in nine days and a woman of sixty on a bench four hundred yards from a frame and a man of forty-nine with a stick of white chalk and a line in her own hand on a board that four carts a day go past.

Neither of those two records is on top of the other, and there is nothing in this county that joins them, and the joining thing is a person walking up a mountain road once a month with a rope over his shoulder and he takes what he is given to a cart.

And she said, out loud, at the bottom of that stair:

“So if I am not here on the day he comes, then on the day he comes I am nowhere. That is not a figure. That is the absence of a place.”

## What That Is

That is the whole of what a stage does to a person this time.

It is not a wound and it is not a mark and there is nothing on any board anywhere in this county. She has gone up three stages and there is not one thing about any of them that a person could put a hand on.

And the third one has left her in a position where there is no place on this ground that knows she is in it.

Not the rows. There are two hundred people at the north end of a ground two days of road east and a made road with four carts a day on it and a bar nine feet nine goes past that ground four times a month. There is a line in her own hand on a board there that says she is nineteen, and there is a slate at a turn with her name on it and a figure in the third column in a county hand, and there is a frame at the turn that a woman of sixty sits beside.

Not this building. A boy of sixteen and four hundred volumes and a drawer with a lock on it and a carter who comes up once a month on his own time and goes back down.

And not the room at the top of the stair, which is where she went to be received, and which has a lamp in it now, and which does not know anything else about her and has no way of being told and would not come down to her if it could.

That is the first stage in nine years that a person could not come and find her, and there is no way of knowing where to come, and she is the only one in this county who knows where she is.

## What She Said About It

She said it out loud at the ninth hour on the ninth of the month beginning 2,204, standing, in daylight, on the copying floor with the boy of sixteen and the empty frame on the wall and the lamp in a cage over his head.

She said, “I came up this mountain for thirty days to be met in a room, and there is a lamp burning at the far end of the long table and not one person in the building but him and me, and I am not going to ask what it is for, because I asked a question like that once on a floor and I cannot un-ask it and I cannot ask it twice.”

He said, “There is a lamp in there.”

She said, “There is a lamp in there.”

She said, “And I am going to go down that mountain in the morning and walk east for about a day and a half and be at the north end of a ground of two hundred people, and I am not going to be able to tell any of them that there is a room with a lamp in it, and I am not going to try.”

## Going Down The Mountain

She went down at the first hour on the tenth of the month beginning 2,204 with the bar on her left shoulder and no strap on it and the strip with eighty-one lines on it in the front of her bag.

He walked down with her to the yard because it is four miles up and there is no other way down, and they did not say one word for twenty minutes, and at the bottom he said, “Will you be at the top of the stair again.”

She said, “Not this year.”

He said, “The lamp.”

She said, “I will leave the oil in it. There is oil in the building and a boy who knows where it is kept, and you have been keeping one lamp going for a hundred and sixty-four days and you are the only person in this county who is keeping anything, and that is not a small thing and I am not going to be the one who says so.”

And then she said the other half of it, standing, in daylight, at the top of the yard, with her back to a tower wall:

“I am going to leave the lamp in that room burning too, and I am not going to be able to feed it, and there is oil in this building and you know where it is kept, and I am going to leave that with you and not with anybody else, and it is not fair and I am doing it anyway.”

She said, “Because a lamp that ought not to be burning is going to be burning on my account in a room that has been shut nine years, and if a man comes out of that door in nine years and sees it, he is going to think somebody is there.”

She said, “And that is the nearest thing to a person being met that this world has offered me, and I am nineteen years old and I am not going to be able to do better than a lamp, and I would like it said out loud that I said so on the way down four flights of stairs.”

Then she went east up the mountain road with the bar on her shoulder and her arm open and four counts of breath on the flat, and she did not count the road, and there was nothing behind her at the top of it but a tower with a lamp in it burning on the fourth landing that would go out in the winter if no one fed it.
