# In Her Own Hand And Only Once

She left a building with the shutters up at the fourth hour of the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 2,264 with a bar on her left shoulder and a bag on the other side with a stub of a nib and a cake of ink in it that has not been let dry since the seventh of the month beginning 1,874, and she was at the turn at the sixth hour of the afternoon of the twenty-ninth.

## What She Did With The Day Before

She did the whole of the thirtieth of the month beginning 2,264 and she wrote nothing.

She sat in the grit four feet back from the frame where a cart can go past without touching her, with her arm open and the bar along the ground at her feet, and she did the sum out loud four times standing up in daylight, because a sum done at nine hours and a day before is a different sum from a sum done at the first hour of the day.

And the four times were the same four times and it is the plainest arithmetic in this county.

Two thousand two hundred and ninety-four less two thousand and seventy-seven is two hundred and seventeen, and counted with the first day as the first, which is how she counts everything, it is two hundred and eighteen days.

Two hundred and eighteen out of twenty is ten and nine tenths of a day. Nine tenths of a day is twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes.

Ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes.

And then she sat down on the butt of the post and did the last half of it, which is what she does to herself and has done since she was nine.

She said, out loud, in daylight, to a frame, on the thirtieth of the month beginning 2,264:

“Nineteen years old. Ten days and twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes out of me, and not one hour of it has ever been late and not one day of it has ever gone back.

And there is no instrument that anybody in this county has ever heard of that puts any of it back, and I am writing the whole of it on a piece of slate at the first hour of tomorrow because I would like there to be one place in this world where it is written down outside of me.”

## Three People Inside A Hundred Yards

The eighth first came at the first hour and she was four feet back in the grit.

There was not enough light to see a slate by, and the light comes up on that frame last, and a woman of sixty told her that nine years ago and was right, so she stood there in the dark for twenty minutes with a bag against her chest and her right arm open and a bar on the ground at her feet and she waited for it, and it came.

There were three people inside a hundred yards of that frame at the first hour and she counted all three of them before she took anything out of the bag, and a fourth person four hundred yards west was sitting on a bench.

The man of forty-nine was not there. There was a carter thirty yards east with a horse that did not need him to be holding it, and a woman of about fifty sitting in the road with her back to a wall, and a boy of nine on a gatepost at the far end of the grit with a basket on his arm.

Three inside a hundred yards and a fourth four hundred yards west. She had not asked for one of them and she never would.

## What She Wrote

She took the nib out and the cake of ink out and unscrewed the cake and put the nib in it and drew the charge through, and it was wet, and it has not been let dry since the seventh of the month beginning 1,874.

Then she did the thing she had decided to do on the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,234 at the ninth hour in a gravel turn.

She did not lift the nib.

A nib laid on cold slate and not lifted will take, and a nib laid on cold slate and lifted and put back does not, and a person who does this has one go at it and does not get a second one, and that is why one is never written twice.

She put the nib on the slate about four inches above her name in the county hand and she wrote with her right hand on an arm that was open, outdoors, in daylight, in the grit, at the first hour, at the two posts on a store road, and she did not lift the nib, and she wrote:

“Ning Yan, the two posts. Two hundred and eighteen days. One in twenty. Ten days, twenty-one hours, thirty-six minutes.”

Nineteen words. She counted them out loud afterward, standing, in daylight, with a hand that was not the hand she had started with.

And then, underneath it, because a person who cannot do the arithmetic has to be able to check it without doing the arithmetic, she put the same sum in figures and then she put it in words.

Both halves are the same number. That is what not a false line means.

## What It Cost Her Hand

And the arm did not close afterwards and there is no closing it.

She does not know the exact moment. She has been back over it nine times and the only thing she can put a date to is that the nib was still moving, because a nib that has stopped moving writes the same mark whether a hand is on it or not.

What she knows is this.

The little finger of her right hand stopped being part of her hand somewhere in the middle of that line. It did not come off with anything. There is no mark on it and there is no blood and she can move it and it does not go where she puts it.

Three fingers and a thumb.

She has carried a bar nine feet nine for two days of road on a hand that is open four days in five and a finger fewer than it was at the first hour, and she picked it up out of the grit in a count of twenty-nine, which is the same twenty-nine it has always been, which is the part she found unbearable.

## The Second Hour

The cart came at the second hour and the man got off and took the slate out of the frame and put it on the cart flat and put four small wedges under the corners.

She stood four feet back and looked at the ground.

Thirty days of hers went up a road in a county hand. A fifth came off the days, the way a fifth has come off them every month since the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054, and it did not say so on the board and it did not come off the name, and a man told her that once and was right and she has not worked out since why she did not think to ask him what it would look like.

And underneath her name, in a hand that was not a county hand, nineteen words went up a road with a figure on them that had been true since a man with a stick of chalk wrote her name in the grit, and a copy of that figure was in a pencil column on the other side of the same slate in a county hand that had been running for nineteen years, and both of them were right.

The cart went west. She watched it go for about a count of sixty until it was round the shoulder of the hill.

## What Went Up And What Cannot Come Down

And then she stood in the grit at a frame in a turn in the first light of a morning with an empty frame and a bar on her left shoulder and three fingers and a thumb on her right hand, and she said the whole of it out loud, standing, in daylight, to the two posts, and there were three people inside a hundred yards and a fourth four hundred yards west.

She said, “That is the eighth first of a month and there has been something of mine on a board on all eight of them, and on the first one I asked a man with a stick of white chalk for my name and on the last one I wrote a figure in my own hand and it is true and I did not lift the nib.”

She said, “And it is not a name. That is the whole of what I have got. A figure is not a person and there is nothing on that board a person can be collected off.”

She said, “And a man of sixty-five in a room with a hundred people reading behind him told me on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 2,264, once, in the ordinary voice, that I cannot be counted where I stand, and he did not soften it, and he was right, and I have not said that sentence in my own mouth and I am not going to.”

And then something happened, and it is the last of it, and she has not been able to write it down.

## What Came Down

There was a sound in the air at that frame for about nine seconds.

It was not loud. It was not inside her head and it was not outside her head. It was at the two posts and on the grit and it was there for a carter thirty yards off, for a woman of fifty sitting with her back to a wall, for a boy of nine with a basket at the end of the grit, and for a woman of sixty on a bench four hundred yards west.

She has tried to put down what it was and she cannot, and she is not going to try again, and the only thing she will say about it is this: it was not nine announcements. It was one, and it was short, and it had something in it that she recognised, and she was standing four feet back in the grit with an empty frame and a bar at her feet and her own handwriting going west on a cart at four miles an hour.

And there were four people within four hundred yards of that frame who were not asked for it and there is not one of the four of them who will say the same thing about it, and she has not asked any of them and she is not going to, because the day she asks is the day there is one account.

She is not going to be one of the four.

She put the bag back on her left shoulder with her right hand, which has three fingers and a thumb on it, and she stayed in the grit at the frame, and she did not go east and she did not go west, because a woman who has just written a thing that cannot be taken back does not go anywhere for a day.

And then she worked out what the line was going to do, standing in the grit in daylight, out loud, to a slate with nothing on it, and it took her a count of nine.

It is going to go up a stair and into a room and be read by somebody, and she will not be there and will not be named and will not know, and there is not one instrument in this county that will let her find out who read it or when or what they did with it.

And the four people inside four hundred yards of that frame are going to go on saying four different things about the same nine seconds for the rest of their lives, and not one of the four of them is ever going to be able to correct the other three.
