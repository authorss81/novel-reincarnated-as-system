# The Board At The North End

The first of the month beginning 2,114 came at the first hour and she was in a ground with two hundred people in it and a bar leaning against a stone at the head of a row, and she stood there and worked out at about the third hour that a cart had gone east off a turn twenty miles west with a slate under a piece of sacking.

That is the second first of eight and the eighth is the first of the month beginning 2,294, and there is no work left in her that a board can get at.

## Fourteen Days

She stayed in the rows for fourteen days, and the whole of the reason she stayed there is that there is a board at the north end of that ground and four men who write on it.

She watched four men for fourteen days.

Four men carry stones. Every one of them is in that ground on the second hour and off it at the seventh, and every evening the four of them go to the north end and one of the others writes a stroke and a number under the stroke, and the numbers are days and the strokes are a day, and they have been four lines for eight years.

She got it from a woman of about forty in the ninth row on the fourth day, and she asked the one question she was going to ask, standing up, in daylight, in a ground with two hundred people in it:

“Who writes it.”

The woman said, “One of the four of us takes a turn at it. It is a quarter of an hour. You do it on your day and you do the day before yours as well if he is the one who is slow.”

She said, “And it is not a thing anybody checks.”

The woman said, “It is not a thing you check. It is stones.”

## The Thing She Could Not Name

From the fourth day of the month beginning 2,114 something was happening to her that she could not name and could not stop and would not describe to a person if she had one.

She has taken nine stages. Every one of them came on its own, on a schedule she could not argue with, and every one of them had been announced, and every one of the nine announcements went into the ground, because there has never been anything above the line in this county to receive one.

Nine times she has gone up through a thing that has no top and nothing has heard her.

This one would not come.

Not because it would not come. Because there was nothing for it to come into, and she worked that out standing at the north end of a ground on the ninth day and said it out loud to a board, which is the fourth time in nine days she has said something true to a board:

“A thing has to look like something before anything can recognise it. And there is nothing in this world that can make me look like something.”

A shape is not a body. She has a body; it has been open and broken and repaired and opened again for nine years and it carries a bar nine feet nine.

A shape is the form in which a thing is presented to whatever is going to be looking at it. It is a name in a book. It is a roll and a rate and a room and a shelf. It is a title on a piece of paper over a door.

It is a set of true statements about a person, written down, in a hand, with a date and a place on it, so that a thing with a record in it can find the person the statements are about.

And every single one of those is a thing that has to be given.

## Why A Hundred And Thirty-One And Not Seventy

On the eleventh day of the month beginning 2,114 she worked the interval out standing at the north end of the rows, and she had it wrong twice before she had it right, and she has put the two wrong ones down here because the second one nearly cost her the stage.

She took the ninth stage on the fourth of the month beginning 1,994, which is day one thousand nine hundred and ninety-seven.

She took this one on the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114, which is day two thousand one hundred and twenty-eight.

Two thousand one hundred and twenty-eight less one thousand nine hundred and ninety-seven is one hundred and thirty-one.

What she had been carrying since the second of the month beginning 2,114 was seventy.

Where seventy came from: she had counted it on her fingers off the day she came off the store road onto the turn at the first hour of the sixth of the month beginning 2,054, which is day two thousand and fifty-nine, because that was a day she could stand on and see and put her hand on.

Two thousand one hundred and twenty-eight less two thousand and fifty-nine is sixty-nine.

Sixty-nine, counted on her fingers, came out as seventy.

And sixty-nine is the interval from the turn to the stage, and it is not the interval at all. The interval is measured from the day she took the ninth stage, and the day she took the ninth stage is sixty-two days further back than the day she reached the turn, and sixty-two and sixty-nine make a hundred and thirty-one.

She said that out loud, standing, in daylight, at the north end of a ground with two hundred people in it, on the eleventh of the month beginning 2,114:

“An ordinal and the interval that reaches it are two different figures. I have been counting off the day I can stand on, and the interval has to be counted off the day the thing happened on, and I have been carrying a number about my own body that was never about my body.”

## The Fifteenth

The fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114 is day two thousand one hundred and twenty-eight.

She was at the north end of the rows at the second hour, in daylight, with about two hundred people in the ground doing what they do in a ground, and the four men had not come to the board yet because it is not their hour.

There was a lamp in a cage in her coat and there has been one in it since a yard four miles up a mountain where a boy of sixteen kept it going for somebody else.

She has never known what it was for.

It came at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and it took the length of nine seconds, and there was no announcement, and the lamp did what a lamp does.

She felt the whole of it go out of her at once. That is the part she has never been able to describe to anybody and has not tried since she was twelve years old.

Afterwards there was one place left where she was still doing it, and she went and stood at the north end of the board.

## What It Costs, Named Once

A stage does not give you a new hand. It gives you the reach, and the reach needs a shape, and a shape is a thing that has to be held.

Here is the whole of it and it is the first new thing this has ever cost her at scale, and she is naming it once, standing, in daylight, in a ground with two hundred people in it, and then she is not going to talk about it again:

She cannot be given a shape.

Not by the building, not by the county, not by a book with four hundred and seven names in it, not by a board, not by a sheet, not by anything that has ever been made for the purpose of making a person into a thing that can be found.

Every shape that has ever existed in this world has been a shape somebody else gave you. A roll is a name somebody else wrote. A rate is a figure somebody else set. A room is a door somebody else opened.

And a shape is the only thing that has ever let anything above the line see a person, and she has spent nine years refusing the only two that were ever on offer to her, and neither of them was refused out of principle: one was the fifth line of a sheet and one was a question she asked standing up on a floor.

And what she has instead, on the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114, at the fourth hour of the afternoon, at the north end of two hundred rows of stones, is that she can make one.

It cannot be given to her. It has to be made, and it has to be made in her own hand, and her hand cannot write a false line.

## The Line On The Board

There is a board at the north end of the rows and there has been a stroke and a number under it on that board every day of it for nine years, and on the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114 a person with four fingers and a thumb and an arm that was open wrote on it, at the fourth hour of the afternoon, in daylight, in front of two hundred people, the first line on that board that is not a stroke and a number.

NING YAN. The fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114, at the north end of the rows. I am nineteen. I am not on a roll and I am not under a name above this ground and I am not going to be given one. I have written this one myself and it is true.

She wrote it with her right hand because her right hand was the one that could do it and because her right arm was open and she did it anyway.

She has a book of eight leaves against her ribs with a date and a place on the first and four sentences on the second and four capital strokes on the third and five that are hers and that are blank.

She did not write it in the book. She has not written anything in those five leaves since the day she made them and she is not going to start on the day she takes her first stage past the last working point in the world.

A shape goes where a shape goes. It goes where it can be found by a thing that keeps records, and a book in a coat lining is not found by anything, and a board nailed up at the north end of a ground that four carts go past a day is.

She stood back and looked at it from six feet.

It is a bad line. It is the first thing she has ever written for a person to find and it is four sentences with a date and a place on it and it is on a board at the north end of a ground with two hundred people in it, and she read it from six feet off and she could not improve one word of it, because a hand that cannot write a false line cannot write a better one either. It can only write a true one.

The first of the month beginning 2,144 is sixteen days off.
