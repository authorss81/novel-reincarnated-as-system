# Nothing On The Other Side Of The Grille

She went west at the fourth hour of the third of the month beginning 2,294 with a bar nine feet nine on her left shoulder and her right arm open, and it had been open since the first hour of the first of that month, and it was not going to close.

## Nine Miles Paid For And Four Miles That Are Not

She was at the post at the end of the paid road at the eighth hour of the fourth of the month beginning 2,294, and there has been no road under her feet for four hundred yards from that post in one direction, and she stood at it for a count of sixty the way she stands at things.

Then four miles of road that is not paid for, and a cottage at the far side of it that is not the one at the stopped lime bed, and then four miles up.

And she did all thirteen miles of that on an arm that was open, with three fingers and a thumb on the hand that has been carrying the bag, and she put the bar down four times on the way.

The last of them cost her fifty-four counts, which is more than it has ever cost her and is four times what it cost a fortnight ago, and she said that out loud at the bottom of the hill at the second hour of the sixth of the month beginning 2,294 to a boy of sixteen who was standing in a yard with a lamp in a cage on the fourth landing over his head.

She said, “Four flights to the copying floor and four more above it. Eight. On this.”

He said, “You have done eight flights in thirty days.”

She said, “I have done eight flights in thirty days with an arm that shut twice.”

## The Third Landing

She went up at the third hour of the sixth of the month beginning 2,294.

The first four flights are four hundred and forty steps and she has counted them thirty times and her left hand is on the rail the whole way, and her breath is four counts on a flat flight and seven on anything that rises, and the lamp in a cage on the fourth landing goes out of her sight about halfway up.

The next four are the same four hundred and forty and there is not one rail on the second four, and she has said that out loud twice in nine years and both times to a stair.

And the short breath is at the third landing of the second four, which is a landing four feet across with a window in it that has not been opened since before either of them was born, and every time she has come up here her breath goes to nine on it and holds there for about a count of forty.

She stood on it and had it at nine and she was nineteen years old and she had carried a bar nine feet nine up eight flights that afternoon and she did not sit down.

## The Thing Going Up Ahead Of Her

And then she said the other thing out loud on that landing, in daylight through a window about two feet across, in the ordinary voice, to a stair.

She said, “There is a bundle going up a stair in this building that is four days old and I cannot see it and it is a long way above me.”

She said, “There is a figure in it in my own hand with a date and a place on it, and it is true, and it is about six days higher up than I am, and there is not one thing I can do about where it is.”

She said, “And not one person in this county will ever be able to tell me how high it has got, and I am not going to be able to find out, and that is the arrangement. That is what I bought at a frame in a turn with a nib in my right hand.”

## The Bar Across The Door

And then she went up the last flight and put her eye to the grille of the low door.

The bar is across the outside of it. Nine feet of ash, four inches by six, with a hold-iron at each end, and the dust on the top of it is not broken anywhere, and the nine feet between that bar and the floor has not been walked on by anything in nine years.

She put her hand flat on the top of the bar and it was cold and it did not move, and she did not put her weight on it, and she is not going to.

And behind the grille there is a room with a long table down the middle of it and a lamp in a cage at the far end on a shelf, and beside the lamp there is the stub of a candle in a dish with one grey bloom of cold wax in the bottom of it.

And there are no chairs. There have never been chairs in that room.

She stood at the grille for a count of a hundred with the bar lying along the floor behind her and her right arm open and nine counts of breath standing still, and she looked at a table and a lamp and nine feet of floor.

And then she said, out loud, to the door, in the ordinary voice, the thing she has been carrying up four miles of mountain since the twelfth of the month beginning 2,264:

“I cannot describe what is in that room and I am not going to try.”

She said, “It is a table and a lamp and a dish and a floor and there is nothing else in it, and I have put my eye to that grille nine times in thirty days and there is no person and no rule and no sound in it, and the whole of what is on the other side of that grille is that it is warm and it is lit and not one person has ever said anything in it.”

She said, “And I am not met. And I am expected. And there is no instrument in the world that will tell a room what to do with a person standing on the wrong side of a low door, and I have said that sentence once already this month and I am not going to say it again.”

## The Lamp And The Boy

And then she went down the second four and the first four and a boy of sixteen was at the bottom of the stair with a lamp in a cage on the fourth landing over his head that he has been keeping going, and she had asked him about the oil on the twelfth of the month beginning 2,264 and he had said there was enough in the jar under the bench until the weather turned.

She said, “How is the weather.”

He said, “It has turned.”

She said, “Then how much is in the jar.”

He said, “A day and a half, near enough.”

And then she stood in a copying room four miles up a mountain at the sixth hour of the sixth of the month beginning 2,294 with four hundred volumes on the shelves behind her and said out loud, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, the whole of what she is, one item at a time, because she has never in her life let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

She said, “I am paying for a lamp in a room I cannot get into with oil out of a jar in a building I can get into, and I am nineteen, and I have two coppers I cannot count, and I cannot be paid, and there is no work here for me, and I am going to be able to keep that lamp going for a day and a half and not one day after that.”

He said, “I will keep it going.”

She said, “You have been keeping it going.”

He said, “Yes. But I will keep it going after you stop asking.”

And then she went back up eight flights, which took her two hours and fifty-four counts, and stood at the outside of a low door that was still barred.

## The Thing She Would Do In The Morning

And then she took her eye off the grille and sat down on the top step, which is where you sit, and her breath came down off nine in a count of sixty and went to seven and stayed at seven, and she sat there with the bar along the floor behind her for about four minutes without thinking about anything.

Then she said the decision out loud, at the ninth hour of the sixth of the month beginning 2,294, sitting on the top step of a stair with her back against a cold door and a bag on her left and an arm that has been open for six days.

“I am not going to lift this bar tonight.”

She said, “I have had eight flights and a landing in the morning, and I have got the bar at the bottom of them, and if I lift that bar I will be standing in a room at the sixth hour of a day with nothing in my hands, and there is not one thing in this county I could have put on a table.”

She said, “And I am not going to go down eight flights tonight and carry that bar back up them again, because I have been up and down them forty times in thirty days and I am not going to be able to do it once more and get anything out of it.”

She said, “And there is one thing I have not done in nine years and I am going to do it, and it is this.

I am going to leave the bag at the bottom of the first four and come up these eight flights at the first hour tomorrow morning with an empty hand, and I am not going to lift the bar either, and I am going to stand on the outside of this door and find out what is on the other side of it in the light.”

She said, “And whatever is on the other side of it, I am not going to put a single word of it in five blank leaves against my ribs, and there is not one person in this county who is going to be told.”

And then she went down eight flights, the first four with her left hand on the rail and her breath at seven on the third one and four on the rest and the second four with no rail at all, and the boy of sixteen was in the copying room in the dark with a lamp in a cage on the fourth landing over his head that he has been keeping going, and he had heard her come down.
