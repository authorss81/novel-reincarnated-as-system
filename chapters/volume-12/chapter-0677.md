# Every Cart Goes East

She went back up off the flat at the fourth hour of the ninth of the month beginning 2,084 and it took her a count of thirty-one, and she has said twenty-eight on a hard surface and fifty-four at a gate, and thirty-one on a flat at the fourth hour of the morning is a good number and she took it anyway.

Then she went through the wall and east.

## A Made Road With Carts On It

Past the wall the ground runs out flat for about two hundred yards and then there is a road, and it is a made road, and it goes east.

She had never walked it. She had seen the turn from it once, in the eighth year, and she had seen a cart on it go east with four bundles on it, and that was the whole of what she knew about this road, and she had been putting a great deal under it for two years.

It is made the same as the store road. Two ruts, gravel between them, a crown in the middle that is a foot high, and a ditch on the south side with water in it four days in seven. There are stakes along the north side every twenty yards or so and some of them have paint on them.

Carts go on it.

That is the first thing and it took her about a mile to take it in properly, because she had spent six days on a road where the only thing that came past was a man going west with a load and a woman going west with a basket.

At the fourth hour she counted seven carts in one hour going east. Two went west. One of the two that went west was empty.

She said that out loud at the fifth hour, standing at the side of the made road with the bar on her shoulder and not one person within nine feet:

“In one hour, seven east and two west, and one of the two that went west was empty, which means nine carts came off this road and seven of them came back with the same load on them.”

A woman going east with a basket went past her and did not look at her, which she has decided not to read anything into, because the alternative reading is that she is a person with a bar on her shoulder and there are three of those in every county in the world.

## Being Seen

No one on that road was afraid of her and no one on that road was looking for her either, and she had expected one or the other and got neither, and it took most of a mile for her to understand what had happened to her.

She has been off every board in the world for years. She has been a name on a wall once and a name on a list a boy of nineteen has never seen and a figure of hours in a woman’s own handwriting that a room full of strangers did not know was hers.

This morning four people looked at her and one of them looked at her hand.

That is the whole of it. A made road with carts on it at the fifth hour, and a girl with a bar on her shoulder and four fingers on her right hand and an arm that hangs, and the hand is what people looked at, and the hand is not new and is not a secret and does not have anybody’s name on it.

She stopped at a stake at about the ninth hour and put the bar down and did the twenty counts and then stood in the ditch with the bar at her feet and worked out that she had not once in six days wanted to be somewhere else, and that she has wanted to be somewhere else every hour of every day for years.

Then she picked it up again in twenty counts.

## Counting The Road

She counted the road because she cannot help it and because she had nothing else to put in the day.

She counts in her own paces, with the bar on the left shoulder and the right hand swinging, and she has counted that way since she was fourteen and she has never had to check it against anybody’s figure, and on the store road in the eighth year she counted four thousand paces from the turn to a certain stone and got the same answer three years running.

It is a bad unit. A made road goes up and down and her pace is shorter going east than going west with the bar on the left. So the number she arrives at is not a distance and she has never pretended that it is.

She had a figure for the store road at the end of it: from the turn to the wall, in her own paces, with the bar on the left shoulder.

She went and looked.

What she wanted to know was whether this road and that road are the same road. She knew they were not, in the ordinary way, because the store road goes west and she had walked it. But she wanted the number, because she has never in her life been on a piece of ground she could not put a figure on, and the whole of the last six days has been the feeling of being on ground she could not put a figure on.

She got the figure at the sixth hour, at a point where the road came up on a rise and she could see a long way east, and then she stopped, because the figure was wrong and she could see why it was wrong before she had done the arithmetic.

It said six miles past the wall. There were not six miles past the wall. She could see the end of what was left of it from where she was standing, and there were lamps in it.

There are stakes along the north side every twenty yards, and a man does not set one at twenty yards and then set the next at forty. She counted them from the wall, four at a time, and did the fours twice.

Two hundred and sixty-four stakes at twenty yards each is three miles of made road east of the wall. Three miles east of a wall that is thirteen miles east of the turn is sixteen miles east of the turn, and that is a figure the road itself says and not one her shoulder says.

## Sixteen Miles East

So she worked it the other way, which is the way she works everything.

She counted the stakes and she counted her own paces on a stretch of road with the bar and did it twice, and the two figures did not agree by about a ninth, which she allowed for, because she is counting her own paces and not the road.

Both of them put her three miles in, and she is standing in a ditch with the bar at her feet, six days on a made road, with a figure.

She stood in the ditch at sixteen miles at the seventh hour of the ninth of the month beginning 2,084 with a bar at her feet and worked out the rest of it as a sum, standing up, out loud, because she says sums out loud:

“Four miles is two days at two miles and a bit a day, and I have got sixteen days to the twenty-fourth and twenty-three to the first of the month beginning 2,114, and I can do it and be standing on the twenty-fourth with two days to spare.”

And then she did the second half of the sum, which is the half that is the reason she is on this road:

“On the first of the month beginning 2,084 a man took a slate with my name on it off a frame at a turn twenty miles west of here, put it on his cart with four bundles and a length of pipe, put a piece of sacking over it and took the whole thing east.”

She said, “And this morning there have been seven carts going east in one hour and one empty going west, and every cart on this road that goes east is going east with something on it that is made to be collected.”

She said, “And I have been walking for six days to get to the road.”

## Lamps At The End Of It

Three miles past the wall, at about sixteen miles east of the turn, the made road turns.

It turns north off the line of the wall and goes a little way and there is a gate on the north side of it, which is not the gate she came in by, and a lane, and a lane going back east, and a building at the end of the lane.

The building is one storey. The roof sits low. It is a hundred and forty feet front, she can tell, because she has stood at the front of it twice.

There is a row of windows and every one of them has a lamp in it, and it is the seventh hour and the light is going and every lamp is lit.

There are about forty people at that gate. There is a man at it. There is a queue of four carts on the lane.

She stood in the ditch at the far end of a made road for about a count of forty with the bar on the ground at her feet, and this is what she took in, in this order, and it took her forty counts and she did not miss one item of it:

A gate with a man at it. A lane. A building with a lamp in every window. About forty people. Four carts.

And nothing else. No wall round it, no bar across the road at the near end of it, no water in a ditch, no second building, nothing at all standing between a made road and a room with a lamp in every window.

She has walked sixteen miles and six days to get to a thing that is standing in the open at the end of a made road with forty people in front of it.

## The Part That Is Not In The Sum

Then she worked out the other half, and it took her the rest of the daylight and the dusk.

That road does not know she is coming. There is not a person on it who knows she exists.

She has been in this county thirty-three days and the only person on nine days of road who has written her name anywhere is a man of forty-nine who writes four columns a month, and he wrote hers at the sixth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054, and she has been off that board ever since except for one morning when it went east under a piece of sacking.

She has built fourteen miles of road into a plan because she is the only person who can do the arithmetic, and she came onto this made road at the fourth hour of the morning with a bar and no name on anything except a slate that is twenty miles west of here.

And she has been walking past about two hundred people a day for six days and she has been seen by all of them, and not one of them has any idea that there is a figure in her head that she could not get to them if she tried.

She stood at the far end of a made road at the ninth hour with the bar on her shoulder and she said one sentence out loud, alone, in daylight, to no one:

“I am going to walk up to that gate in the morning and there is not one person in this world who knows that I am going to.”
