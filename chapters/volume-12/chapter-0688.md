# The Bill Left Unpaid

She spent the nineteenth of the month beginning 2,114 at the made road two miles west of a ground with two hundred people in it, watching people get found, and that is the whole of that day.

## What Findable Looks Like

There is a made road two miles west of the rows and carts come off it twice a day and about forty people walk the half mile between the gate and the north end of the ground every day because there is nowhere else to be.

She stood in the ditch at the fourth hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 2,114 and watched from the first count until the light went and counted how many times a person on that ground was looked for.

She counted thirty-four.

Not thirty-four searches. Thirty-four times that a person in that ground was found by somebody who was looking, in one day, and they are not the same and she kept them apart.

A woman of about sixty found a boy of about six in four seconds flat. A man of about forty found a woman he had been looking for since the second hour. A man of thirty-two found a set of shoulders he knew from nine feet off and did not speak to and stood next to for a count of twenty.

Thirty-four times, in one day, by thirty-four different people, and it was ordinary, and not one of the thirty-four was remarkable, and every one of them had done it before and would do it again the next day.

She worked out standing in a ditch at the ninth hour that this is what a ground of two hundred people is, and that it is made entirely of being looked for, and that a person in a ground like that is found about once a day and does not notice and is not grateful for it and has no way of being un-found for long.

And then she stood there and did the other half of it, out loud, to the ditch:

“I have been on this ground four days and I have been found twice. Once by a carter with a cart and once by a man with a book four miles west, and before that by a man of forty-nine with a stick of white chalk, and that is four times in nine days, and the four times have come faster.”

## The Bill

She asked it at the tenth hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 2,114, standing at the north end of the rows with her back to the board and the bar on the ground and about a hundred people lying down in the dark about forty feet off.

One question. One at a time, in daylight, standing. She has kept those three terms since the ninth year and she is not going to start breaking them at the end of the hardest month she has ever had.

She said, “What does it cost.”

He said, “I do not know what it costs. I know what it is, and the two are not the same, and I am not going to do the thing I did when I gave you a number in a mill at a yard in a valley and put the whole of it on your foundation without telling you what it was.”

She said, “Then tell me what it is and let me decide what it costs.”

He said, “There is a thing that reads records.”

And then he stopped, and she waited, and he went on, because he does not soften anything and that is the whole of what changed in him and it happened a long time ago.

“It has read every record in this county since before either of us. It reads them and it does not do anything about them. It has never had to do anything about them, because there has not been one in eleven hundred years with anything in it that it wanted.”

He said, “There is one now. It is not a large one. It is a shape held by somebody and it is held by you and it is on a board at the north end of a ground with two hundred people in it, and the name is on that board, and I am standing four feet off that board.”

He said, “That is the cost. Not what it takes from me. That a thing which reads records will be able to read one with me in it, and that it will read it because I asked to be found, and that I asked for it in the morning knowing what I was asking for.”

## Why It Was Not Answered

And she did not answer it.

That is the whole of what happened at the tenth hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 2,114 at the north end of two hundred rows of stones, and she has not been able to do anything about it since, and she has thought about it every hour of every day since.

He did not ask her to. That is the part she keeps coming back to. He named it, he did not soften it, he did not say it was a small thing, and he did not put a question at the end of it, and he stopped.

And she stood there in the dark with a bar on the ground and a hundred people lying down forty feet off and she worked out four things in about nine seconds and she came out of them with a bill in her hand and nobody owing anything.

That a thing that reads records has not wanted one in eleven hundred years and there is one now, and that it is on a board she wrote four days ago, and that a carter has said his name at it.

That she could go east and take the board down and scrub it and put the strokes back over it and there is no hand in nine days of road that would stop her, and the two men who wrote numbers either side of it would not know and would not ask.

That she cannot, because she cannot write a false line, and a board with a line scrubbed off it is not a true board, and the two men would keep writing their days next to a blank and not know.

That he said *that is the cost* and not *that is what it will take*, and the difference between the two is the whole of the difference between a bill and a price, and a bill is not payable by a person who has not agreed that it is owed.

So she stood there and said, at about the eleventh hour, out loud, in the dark, standing up, to a person who cannot be answered by anybody else:

“I am not paying that tonight and I am not refusing it either. I am going to go and work out what a record is and I am going to be in that building tomorrow, and if the answer is that I have to take it down then I will take it down and it will not be because a voice in my head asked me to.”

And he said, “That is yours,” and that is the last thing either of them said about it for the rest of that night.

## The Rest Of The Nineteenth

She went back out to the made road at about the eleventh hour and sat in the ditch with the bar across her knees and did the only arithmetic that was available to her, which was what the bill was in the only money she has ever been able to earn.

She cannot be paid. Her hand does not close on money given for reading and has not closed on one since a shed at a mill when she was fifteen. Eight coppers a day is about four loaves and she has not been hungry in years and does not need to, and there is nothing in this world she could buy with eight coppers a day that she would want.

So it is not a bill that can be paid with money, and it is not a bill that can be paid with work, because she cannot be given a place and cannot be paid and cannot be given anything.

There is one thing she has and there has only ever been one thing, and it is days, and days are the only thing anybody has ever taken off her, on a board, one in twenty, since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544.

She sat in a ditch two miles west of a ground with two hundred people in it and worked that out and it took her about nine seconds and she said it out loud, sitting, in the dark, to a ditch:

“If that thing ever prices this, it will price it in days, because it will not be able to price it in anything else, and days are the only thing I have ever had that was worth taking.”

She said, “And I have got five hundred and seventy-two of them and I have had them since I was nine years old and not one of them has ever been in a fold in the inside of a coat or on a slate or in a book, and I have never been able to put them anywhere.”

And then she stopped, because she had worked out at about the sixth hour of that day that she was pricing a thing for a person who has never been allowed to be given anything, and that a person who is given nothing cannot be made to pay anything, and that the whole of the bill may be that it lands on a shape on a board and takes the writing.

She did not say that last part out loud. She has not said it out loud since.

## The Twentieth

On the twentieth of the month beginning 2,114, at about the ninth hour, a man in the fourth row asked her a question.

He was about thirty and he had been in that ground six years and he had noticed, apparently, that a line had appeared on the board at the north end that was not a stroke and a number, and he had been carrying the question about for two days and had worked out that the only person who would know was the one standing at the board every morning at the first hour.

He said, “Is your name the one on the board at the north end.”

She said, “It is.”

He said, “Right,” and went back to his stone.

That is the whole of it. It took nine seconds and it cost her something she has not been able to name, because she has been in that ground four days and two hundred people have now been told by somebody that there is a person on it, and before the seventeenth of the month beginning 2,054 there had not been one in eleven years.

And at the eleventh hour of the same day a second cart came off the made road and stopped at the north end of the rows and the man on it did not get down and did not read the board and said one word to a boy of about nine standing next to it, and then went on east.

The boy came and asked her what the word meant.

She said, “It is a name. It is not yours and it is not mine. Go to your work.”

And she stood at the north end of two hundred rows with the bar on the ground at her feet and fourteen strokes gone in beside her line since the morning, and worked out standing up, in daylight, at the eleventh hour of the twentieth of the month beginning 2,114, what the rate of arrival is.

Two days. Two mouths, and one of them was in a ground with two hundred people in it.

The first of the month beginning 2,144 is eleven days off and the bill is open and nothing has been said about it by anybody except her, and she said it to a person who cannot be answered by anybody else, in a field, in the dark, at the tenth hour of a night.
