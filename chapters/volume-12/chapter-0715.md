# The Count She Wrote Herself

She came down four miles of mountain road at the first hour of the seventeenth of the month beginning 2,264 with a boy of sixteen walking beside her for the first mile because he walked her to the yard the first time she ever came and has done it every time since.

He stopped at the yard. She went on west on her own with a bar nine feet nine on her left shoulder and her arm open and four counts of breath on anything flat.

## The Question She Took Down The Mountain With

She put it the same way three days running so that she could not move the terms.

How is she going to be counted at a first of a month.

And the first thing that came out of it, on the fourth mile at the second hour of the seventeenth, standing in the road with not one house within two miles of her, is the sentence she already knew and has known since she was ten years old.

A count takes a board and not a person.

She said it out loud, in daylight, to a mountain road, because she has never in her life let a sentence stand that she has not put into words where it exists, and it stood there and it was the same.

And then she said the next part, which is the part that took the rest of the day.

“In nineteen years there has not been one count of me made by anything except a piece of wood. A county does not count a person. A county counts a line, and a line is on a board, and the board is four feet off a road, and that is the entire distance between me and being a number.”

## What Has Been Counted And What Has Not

She went through all of it walking, at four miles an hour, in the ordinary voice.

A ground of two hundred people. She was found there twice in nine days by people who were looking, and a man of fifty counted thirty-four finds in one day in a ditch and it was ordinary, and that is not a count. That is people looking for their own.

A frame at a turn with a man of forty-nine and a stick of white chalk. Her name in a county hand and thirty days in a column beside it and a twentieth of every one of those days coming off it, and a figure in a pencil column on the other side of that same slate that has been running for nineteen years and that a man puts in four times a month without knowing what it is.

A board at the north end of a ground with four sentences in her own hand on it and a date, and a carter who could not read it and said a name off it out loud on a made road.

And not one count of her anywhere in this county has ever been made by anything that looked at her.

She said, out loud, at the fifth hour of the seventeenth of the month beginning 2,264, standing at the post at the end of the paid road where there has been no road under her feet for four hundred yards:

“I have never in my life been counted where I stand. Not once. And everything that has ever come off me has come off me in a place I was not in, from a hand that was not mine, on a day I did not choose.”

## The Thing She Did Not Count

And then, on the store road between the ninth mile and the turn, on the eighteenth, something happened that she could not put a number to and has not been able to put down since.

Something has been settled.

She does not know when it started. She does not know what work it was on, and she went through the whole of the last thirty days looking for it and found a copying floor with four hundred volumes on it and nine sheets a day and a ground of two hundred people with four men putting strokes on a board and a boy with a lamp.

What she knows is where it comes from.

It comes out of the ground under her feet.

She stopped on the road at the third hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,264, stood still in the grit with a bar on her shoulder, and said out loud, in daylight, to a store road:

“It is not off my hands. I have not lost anything. I have two coppers I cannot count and a bag and a bar and I have had them for a month, and it has not come off any of that, and it has not come off my arm, and it has not come off the place behind my fourth rib, and it has not come off my breath, which is four counts and has been four counts since I was sixteen.”

She said, “And it comes out of the ground, on no day I can point to, and I have no instrument for it and I am not going to ask anybody for one, and I want it said out loud on a road that this is the first thing in nineteen years I have decided not to count.”

She picked up the ground under her feet with a bar and looked at the two hands and said, “I have decided not to count it and I am not going to find out about it and I am not going to protect anybody with it, and if it ever comes off my hands I will know that day because there will be nothing in them.”

## What She Is Going To Write And Why She Cannot Say So Here

And she worked out the shape of it on the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,264, standing at the end of the paid road with the post at her back, and she has not said any of it to anybody and is not going to.

She said, out loud, in daylight, to a road with nine miles of it in front of her, that the line has four things on it and no more.

A date, and the date has to be a first of a month because a board goes up on a first and there is not one other date in this county a board will travel on.

A place, and the place is going to be a frame in a gravel turn at a junction of a store road and nine miles of paid road with four carts a day going past it, because a thing that keeps records has to be able to get to the place it happened and look at the ground.

A figure, in her own hand, because a county hand writes down that a person exists and she is not writing down that she exists.

And the same figure twice, once in numbers and once in words, so that a person who cannot do the arithmetic is able to check it without doing the arithmetic.

She said, “And that is nineteen words and not one of them is about me except the true part of one thing about me, and I am not going to be able to take it off with a cloth and I am not going to want to, and I have known that for a month and I have only just said it out loud.”

She said, “And I am going to write it at the first hour of the first of a month beginning 2,294, and the reason for the first hour is that the light comes up on that frame last and I want to be able to see what I am doing.”

She said, “And I have said this out loud four times in four days on a road, to a road, which is a thing I do and which does not stop me finding it out and does not stop it being true.”

## The Count She Cannot Escape

And she came into the turn at the sixth hour of the eighteenth and stood in the grit at the foot of two posts with a bar on the ground at her feet and worked out the last of it, standing, in daylight, out loud, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “The only thing that can be counted at a first of a month is a line.”

She said, “A first of a month is the day a board goes up. A board is wood and slate and chalk and a name and a column of days. What a first counts is what is on it. And what can be on it is a column, or a name, or a line in a hand.”

She said, “And there is going to be a line on this one on the first of the month beginning 2,294, in my own hand, and it is going to have a figure on it, and a figure is not a person, and that is the only reason it can go up at all.”

And then she said the thing that is the whole of it, and it took her a count of nine.

She said, “My name being on that slate is a thing I chose. I asked for it. I stood in this grit at the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054 and I asked a man with a stick of white chalk to put my name on a board and he wrote it and wiped the chalk off his thumb on his coat, and I said out loud that I could not be un-written off it.”

She said, “And four sentences on a board at the north end of a ground of two hundred people on the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114, in my own hand, and a carter said a name off it three days later on a made road and did not know what it was.”

She said, “And a line on this slate in thirteen days in my own hand with a date and a place on it, and a board that will go up a stair.”

She said, “Every single one of those three was mine and not one of them was made for me. And I have spent nineteen years being counted by things and I have never once been counted by a thing that did not have my name on it first.”

## Three Things She Chose Herself

And she stood in the grit with the light going off the slate and worked out what it came to, and it came to one sentence, and she said it once.

She said, “The count I cannot escape is the one I wrote myself. Not the one in the column and not the one in the building and not the one four men write strokes for. Mine.”

And then she picked the bar up in a count of twenty-nine and put it on her left shoulder and stood there for a count of a hundred, because there is nothing at that turn for a person to do but stand.

She is going to be standing at it at the first hour of the first of a month beginning 2,294 with her hand open and a nib in it and thirteen days of this in her, and she has not got one word of the line and she is not going to get it by standing here.
