# An Old Man And A Shut Book

She went west out of that ground at the first hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 2,264 with a bar on her left shoulder and did not stop at the wall, and two days later she was at the ninth-mile post in the evening, and she slept in a field four hundred yards short of it and was at the bottom of the mountain at the second hour of the fifteenth.

## The Building

The lamp in a cage on the fourth landing was burning.

She went up the first four flights with her left hand on the rail and her breath at seven on the third one and four on the other three, and she was under the cage four minutes after she started, and a boy of sixteen was on the copying floor with four hundred volumes on the shelves behind him and a board by the door with days and not names on it.

He said, “You came off the west road.”

She said, “I came off the store road and I have been on it for two days.”

He said, “For what.”

She said, “To stand at a door.”

## Four Hundred Volumes And A Drawer

She copied nine sheets with him on the first afternoon and read the third shelf from the left out of four hundred volumes on the second, and she did not ask him one question about the boy of nineteen or about the frame or about a third of a name, and he did not ask her about any of it.

The drawer is at the west end of the floor under the rack. It has a lock on it. The key has been in the front of her bag a foot from her hand every day since the eleventh of the month beginning 2,054, and she was thirty feet from that drawer for thirty days on her last visit and did not use it, and she is not going to use it now.

He saw her looking at it on the second day and said, “It is all right. I have never opened it. I do not know what is in it and I am not going to.”

She said, “It is a dead clerk’s and it is four sheets in his hand and I have not read the margin and I am not going to.”

He said, “All right.”

And that was all that was said about it in two days, and she has not been able to work out since what it cost him not to ask.

## The Oil

And she asked him about the lamp in the room at the top of the stair, standing up, in daylight, one question at a time, on the copying floor with four hundred volumes behind him.

She said, “How much oil is left.”

He said, “Enough until the weather turns.”

She said, “And after the weather turns.”

He said, “There is a stone jar under the bench with about four days in it and after that there is nothing in this building.”

She said, “Where does the oil come from.”

He said, “The carter brings it up with his rope once a month and he does not charge for it because he is not a carter for this building, he is a carter for the square, and he has never once asked who the lamp is for, and I have never once told him.”

She stood there for a count of twenty with that.

Then she said, “I am nineteen years old and I have two coppers I cannot count and I cannot be paid, and there is no work anybody will give me here, and I have left you the oil for a lamp in a room I cannot get into and I am not going to be able to feed it after the weather turns, and I have known that since the tenth of the month beginning 2,204.”

He said, “I know.”

She said, “You have not asked me for it back.”

He said, “No.”

## What Was On The Shelves

And she read the third shelf out of four hundred volumes on the second afternoon and a boy of sixteen copied beside her and did not ask her one question about any of it for two days.

There are four hundred volumes in that room and they are the whole of what this county has written down that anybody has kept, and she has been through a fifth of them in thirty days and has not found one column of days with a name under it in any of them, and she has not said that out loud to anybody because she does not know what it means.

She said one other thing on the second afternoon, standing at the shelves, in daylight, one question at a time, and he answered it without putting the pen down.

She said, “Is there anything in this building that has ever come up that stair.”

He said, “Four bundles in thirty-one years. I have been here two years and I have seen one of them go up and I did not see who took it.”

She said, “And you asked the carter about the last one that went up.”

He said, “He said it goes up and comes down empty and there is a man at the top of it who has been there thirty-one years and takes it and puts the door bar back across the outside.”

She said, “And what is in it.”

He said, “Boards.”

## Eight Flights

She went up the second four at the second hour of the sixteenth of the month beginning 2,264.

Four flights to the copying floor and four more above it, and the lamp in the cage on the fourth landing goes out of her sight halfway up the second four, and there is a landing at the top of them with a bar across the outside of a low door.

The dust on the top of that bar was not broken anywhere.

She put her eye to the grille and the lamp at the far end of the long table was burning, and it was burning the same way it had been burning the last time she stood here, and the dish beside it still had one grey bloom of cold wax in the bottom of it, and there were still no chairs, because there have never been chairs in that room.

She stood at the grille for a count of a hundred with the bar lying along the floor behind her, and her right arm was open, and she had seven counts of breath standing still at the top of eight flights and she let them go to nine.

And she did not lift the bar.

She has not lifted it and she is not going to, and she has told herself four different reasons in two years and two of them are the truth and the third one is that she is afraid of a room with a lamp in it and the fourth one is that there is a bar across a door on purpose.

## The Man On The Mountain Road

And she came down eight flights with her left hand on the rail and her breath at seven on the last of them and four on the other seven, and she was in the yard at the sixth hour of the sixteenth of the month beginning 2,264 with a bar nine feet nine on her left shoulder.

And an old man was on the mountain road below the yard going up, with a book shut under his arm.

She said, standing in the yard, in daylight, one question, in the ordinary voice, because she has never in her life let a person go past without asking one thing if there is a thing to ask.

She said, “Have you been up here before.”

He did not stop. He did not look at her and he did not slow down and he went up the mountain road past a tower four miles above a square with a shut book under his arm and the road is wide enough for both, and he did not answer her and he did not say that he had not.

And she stood in a yard and watched an old man go up a hill and worked out, out loud, in daylight, to a tower, standing still, that he was not going to the tower, because the road goes on past it.

He was alive. He was old in a way she has not got an instrument for. He was carrying a book and he had somewhere to be and it was not up that hill, and he passed a woman with a bar on her shoulder on a road and it was not to her and it was not about her, and that is the whole of him and there was not one thing about it that she was owed.

## What She Said Down The Mountain

And she went down four miles of mountain road with her arm open and four counts of breath on the flat parts and she said the last of it out loud, at the bottom of the hill at the ninth hour of the sixteenth of the month beginning 2,264, to a post at the end of the paid road.

She said, “I came up that mountain to stand at a door and I stood at it and I did not go through it and I am not going to, and it took three days to get here and I am going to have to do it again in three, and there is a stone jar under a bench with four days of oil in it and I cannot pay for any more of it.”

She said, “And I have worked out where I am going to be, and it is at the bottom of it, on the fifteenth day from now, at the first hour, at a frame in a gravel turn two days of road east of this hill, with a nib and a cake of ink that has not been let dry since the seventh of the month beginning 1,874.”

She said, “And after that I am going to come back up this hill. Fifteen days from today it is the first hour of the first of a month beginning 2,294, and a line I have not written yet is going onto a slate at a frame in a turn, and two days after that I am going to be standing on this yard again with a bar nine feet nine and an arm that has been open since the morning I write it.”

She said, “And there is a bar across a door nine years old with the dust not broken on it and a lamp burning behind it that I am paying for out of a jar I did not fill, and I am nineteen, and I do not have one word of the line yet, and there are fifteen days.”
