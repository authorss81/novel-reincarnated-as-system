# Back Under The Two Posts

She was at the broken wall at the ninth hour of the seventh of the month beginning 2,234 and she went west out of it at the first hour of the morning after, and it took her the whole of that first morning to turn round in her own head.

## Thirteen Miles The Other Way

She put the bar down four times on the store road and the last of them cost her thirty-four counts, and she was at the ninth-mile post at the eighth hour of the eighth with the sun behind her and her right arm open.

Everything she has ever known about that road she learned going east.

The fence on the north side that goes on for a mile and then stops. The cottage at the stopped lime bed where a woman stands in a yard with her hands behind her and has said nothing at all. The mill at the fifth mile with a wheel on the stream out of the mill race that has not turned in nine years.

She went past all three and not one of them looked up, and she found that she has been counting them as landmarks for a person going the other way, and that she is not that person today.

Then four miles of road that is not paid for, and a cottage at the far side of it that is not the one at the lime bed.

And then the turn, at the sixth hour of the afternoon of the ninth, and the grit, and two posts with a rail on them and a slate in a frame four feet off the ground.

## What The Slate Said

It had been wiped and put back. That is what the first carter west does every morning of his working life, with the flat of his thumb, in the time it takes a man to turn round twice.

Her name was on it in a county hand in the first column. The days of the month beginning 2,234 were in beside it in the second column, thirty of them, and she counted them out loud anyway, all thirty, twice, standing in the grit with her thumb on the rail, and got thirty both times.

And there was nothing on that board she could check a figure against.

She had known that the last time she stood in this grit and she had stood there for a count of a hundred working out whether she was right about it, and she was, and knowing it twice does not make it worse or better. It is just there, the way a wall is there.

## Four Hundred Yards West

She walked four hundred yards west down the grit to the bench and the woman of sixty was on it with her hands in her lap, and she did not get up and she did not say a word, and Ning Yan stood nine feet off for a count of twenty and then walked back.

That is three times in nine years. She has never worked out why she stopped and has stopped trying, which is the only thing in this county she has ever decided not to finish.

The light comes up on that frame last, and the woman told her so once, and it was true then. There was an hour and a bit of light left in it and there was nothing at all for her to do in this county except stand in a gravel turn and be looked up by two people who do not get up.

## What She Does Not Have To Do

The thing she is worst at in this world is being in a place where not one person needs anything off her, and she has just done it for six days on purpose and liked it, and she has not been able to explain to herself since why she liked it.

There is a frame in that ground with a queue at it every afternoon and nine or ten people in the queue and half of them under thirty, and she can do a sum in a count of twenty, and there has not been one in front of her since the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,204.

She does not eat. She has not been hungry since a year she can remember and being light for six hundred and eighty-one days has taken the wanting out of food and out of sleep and out of four other things, and what it has not taken out is standing at a frame being useful, because that is not a thing a person wants because of a body. That is a thing a person wants because of who she is.

And there is not one person in this county who needs a sum off her, and she put a queue of nine behind her when she walked out of that ground and she has not been able to think of anything else since, and it is a bad thing to have done and she would do it again.

## What Went On Writing Without Her

And that was the thing she had carried two days of road without getting out from under, and she got it out at the ninth hour of the ninth, standing at the frame with her arm open and the bar on the ground at her feet.

Her line went onto a board at the north end of a ground of two hundred people on the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114, and four men write one stroke and one number each in the evening, four a night, and that has not stopped for one night in the whole time she has been off this road.

Two thousand two hundred and forty-two less two thousand one hundred and twenty-eight is a hundred and fourteen nights, and a hundred and fourteen times four is four hundred and fifty-six, and she said that number out loud in an empty turn at the ninth hour on the ninth of a month to a slate four feet off her hand.

There will be a stroke at the top that the man with the second hour puts in early. She has left it every time she has counted, and she is going to leave it four hundred and fifty-six times over of it.

And there are fourteen people at a frame in a ground two days of road east of this one who can do a sum like that in their heads, and nine of them were not doing it the last time she was in that ground, and she has told none of them anything, and that did not change while she was gone. Two days on a store road does not stop a thing she did not do.

She has three reasons. Two of them are good. She has said them out loud in that ground and they have not worn.

## What She Went Back For

She stood at the foot of the two posts from the ninth hour until the light went off them and worked out, standing, in daylight, out loud, why she had walked thirteen miles to get back to a piece of slate.

A first of a month is the day a board goes up. A board goes up whether she is standing in the grit or two days of road east with a bar on her shoulder, and there is not one thing she can do to a first of a month from anywhere at all. That is what a first is. It is a date and it is a door that opens whether or not anybody comes.

And what a person can do to a date is arrive on it with the right thing in her hand, and she has not got the right thing in her hand, and she has not known for four days what the right thing is, which is the first time in nine years she has stood at this frame with a thing to say that is not about a board.

## The Nineteen Days

On the tenth she counted the posts, because there are two of them and she has counted them four hundred times. On the eleventh she counted the rail. On the twelfth of the month beginning 2,234 she was at the frame at the ninth hour and did the sum out loud, standing, in daylight, because she cannot help it and she cannot stop it and she has stopped expecting to.

Two thousand two hundred and forty-five less two thousand and seventy-six is a hundred and sixty-nine days counted with the first day as the first. A hundred and sixty-nine out of twenty is eight and forty-five hundredths of a day, and that is eight days and ten hours and forty-eight minutes, and that is what is under her name in the third column on the other side of that slate in a county hand.

She has been lighter for six hundred and eighty-five days and the world has taken eight days and ten hours and forty-eight minutes out of her, and both numbers are correct, and they are about two different things, and she has never once been able to make them meet, and she is not going to be able to on the twelfth of a month.

And then she stood in the grit with the light going and worked out the other figure, which is the one she has been carrying since the ninth hour of the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,144, in front of two hundred people, where it cannot be un-said.

The first of the month beginning 2,264 is nineteen days on from here. She worked it out three times standing in the grit and got nineteen every time and did not say it out loud the third time.

She is going to be at this frame at the first hour of it. She is going to be four feet back in the grit where a cart can go past without touching her, which is the place she asked for on the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054 and was given by a man with a stick of white chalk who said it is not a thing anybody does.

And the board will go up. And a fifth will come off the days in her column exactly as a fifth has come off it every month since she asked to be on it, and there is no mark on the slate that says so, and it does not come off the name.

And nothing of hers will be on that board that she did not put there with her own hand, and there is not one thing in her hand.

She has not worked out what she is going to write. That was true at the ninth hour of the twelfth, and she stood in the grit and let it be true for a count of forty, which is the longest she has ever let a thing be true that she could have started on.

Then she picked the bar up off the ground in a count of twenty-nine and put it on her left shoulder, because there is no strap on it and there has not been one since the month beginning 2,084, and she went four hundred yards west along the grit to a bench where a woman of sixty had been sitting for nine years and asked her one question, standing up, in daylight, one at a time, the way she asks everything.

She had worked out three reasons for not asking it and she walked the four hundred yards anyway, and the reason she asked was that a woman who has been on a board and come off it is the only person in this county who has ever stood where she is going to stand.
