# What Thirty Days Costs

She had twenty-six days from the twenty-second of the month beginning 2,144 to the first hour of the first of the month beginning 2,174 and she spent six of them finding out whether she could get to the frame, which is a thing she had never had to find out before in her life.

Planning is not counting. She wants that understood. Counting is what she does and it is what she is, and it takes nine seconds and it does not cost anything.

Planning is a different thing and it took her six days and it is the first time she has ever done one.

## The Six Days

The twenty-second, the twenty-third, the twenty-fourth, the twenty-fifth, the twenty-sixth and the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 2,144.

The ground is four miles long from the south end of it to the north end, and after the north end it is the gate and two miles of flat with nothing at all to lie down on, and then the wall standing open four feet and a bit, and she went over that leg in thirteen days with a strap on the bar and she did not count the leg between the wall and this ground then and she is not going to count it now. And then there are thirteen miles of store road from the wall to a turn, and she has been over every yard of that road and she knows it to the fence on the north side that goes on for a mile and then stops.

The strap is gone. It parted at the second hour of the fifth of the month beginning 2,084, six miles east of the turn, and there has been no strap since, and every lift and every set-down has cost her between twenty-two and fifty-four counts, and she has done it four hundred times in a month without counting them.

So she counted them. That is what the six days were.

## Each Of The Six Days

The twenty-second was the half mile and it came out at twelve minutes and two hundred and six counts over the four-count baseline, and she set the bar down twice.

The twenty-third was the same half mile at the second hour of the afternoon instead of the morning, because heat is not the only thing that is different in the afternoon and she wanted to know about the other one. It came out at thirteen minutes and two hundred and sixty counts, and she set the bar down three times.

The twenty-fourth she walked it backwards, which no one does and which took fourteen minutes and two hundred and ninety counts, and she learned on that day that her breath is worse going into a rise than out of one, which she already knew and had not wanted written down.

The twenty-fifth was the first day her arm was open and she got nine minutes forty on the half mile with two set-downs, and she had to sit down on a stone at the end of it for a count of ninety, and she worked out standing up that a mile on an open arm is not a mile.

The twenty-sixth she did nothing at all with the bar and read the four volumes on the third shelf of the counting floor, and she has not read a volume for anybody since she was nineteen.

And the twenty-seventh she did a whole mile, out and back, and got four hundred and twenty counts of breath over the baseline, which is nearly exactly twice the half, and she sat down on a stone and found that she could stand up.

And she said, out loud, at the ninth hour of the twenty-seventh, standing, in daylight, at the north end of the rows, with a queue four feet behind her:

“Four hundred and twenty is twice two hundred and six and it is four more, and the four is the two set-downs, and a set-down costs twenty-nine counts every time whatever the length of the day is.”

She said, “And thirteen miles is twenty-six half miles, and if I set it down nine times on that I lose two hundred and sixty counts that I cannot spare, and I have not worked out the ninth set-down yet, and that is the one at the frame.”

## What A Mile Costs

On the twenty-second she walked half a mile west along the made road with the bar on her left shoulder and no strap and her right arm open, and she counted.

Half a mile is four hundred and forty yards. Four hundred and forty steps at a pace of twenty-eight inches is four hundred and forty, and a bar nine feet nine is heavier than it looks in the way that everything gets heavier by the fourth hour, and her breath is four counts on the flat and seven on anything that rises.

Half a mile took her twelve minutes and cost her two hundred and six counts of breath over the four-count baseline, and she set the bar down twice, and each set-down cost twenty-nine.

She did that half mile on six days and then she did a whole mile on the twenty-seventh and got four hundred and twenty for the breath, which is nearly exactly double, and she sat down on a stone at the end of it for a count of ninety and found that she could stand up.

Then she did the sum out loud at the ninth hour of the twenty-seventh, standing, at the north end of the rows, with a queue of small sums four feet behind her that she had stopped working for twenty minutes:

“Thirteen miles. Nine seconds of breath and a half mile is a hundred and three counts. Thirteen miles is twenty-six hundred and forty seconds over that baseline, and it is four counts a step and the step is three to the second on the flat and two on anything that rises.”

She said, “And I have to set it down. Four days of flat with nothing to lie down on and then thirteen miles of store road, and I cannot hold it in one hand for a mile at a time on that arm, and I do not know how many times I will have to put it down until I am standing at the frame and the first hour is still an hour away.”

## What She Decided

Three things, and she said all three out loud in daylight on the twenty-seventh, standing up, at the ninth hour.

One. She leaves at the first hour of the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 2,144, which is day two thousand one hundred and seventy-one, and the first is at the first hour of the twenty-eighth day after that, which is day two thousand one hundred and seventy-four.

Two. She stops at the wall, because there is a stone trough at the broken wall that takes water out of the sky and has not been dry in her experience, and she has done that leg twice and she did it once with a strap and once without.

Three. The bar does not go up on the frame with her name on it, because the frame is a frame and a slate goes into a frame and a bar nine feet nine does not, and if she leaves it lying at the two posts it will be found in about a day by somebody who will take it for a thing left, and that is correct, and she is not going to be a person who leaves things.

And then she said the fourth one, which is the one she had not expected to have.

Four. She does not tell anybody in that ground that she is going, and not the man of forty in the ninth row and not the woman of thirty-four, and if anybody asks her where she is going she says east, which is a lie, and she is going to have to say it four times in three days.

She said that last one at the ninth hour, standing, in daylight, to a queue of ten people, and she did not soften it and she did not say why, and the man of forty in the ninth row heard all of it and did not ask her about the lie, which is the correct thing to do and cost him something anyway.

## What It Costs That Is Not A Mile

And here is the part that is not in the six days and that she did not find out by counting.

At the ninth hour on the twenty-seventh she went back to the frame and there were ten people at it.

She has had a queue of small sums at this frame every day since the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,144, and it has grown, and on the twenty-seventh there were ten of them, and about half of them were under thirty years old.

She stood at the frame and looked at them for a count of forty and she found something she had not expected to find and it took her a moment to see what it was.

Three of them were not waiting for her.

They were waiting behind each other. And the man of forty in the ninth row, who said to her on the fourteenth that it was a very fine way of saying she had found it first, was doing two sums for two other people while she was doing none, and he had got them right, and the woman of thirty-four was checking his working before she gave the answer on.

That is all it is. It is a man who can add and a woman who can check him, and there are forty people in this ground who can count to eighty, and there are two hundred people in this ground who have sums.

She stood there and worked out what that is, standing, in daylight, in front of ten people, and it took her nine seconds.

She said, “That is the whole of it. That is what a figure is. It is not mine and it is not a secret and it is not a thing I am keeping, and it is two days old and there are three of them doing it now who were not doing it nine days ago.”

She said, “And a boy of nineteen in this ground told me once that if one of them knew there would be not one of them left on the rows in a month, and he was not saying it as a warning, he was saying it as arithmetic, and he was right.”

She said, “And I am going to leave at the first hour of the twenty-eighth and be twenty miles west at the first hour of the first, and I am not going to stop them, because I have never once in nine years had a thing to stop anybody with, and this is not that.”

She said it and then she did four sums for the people at the frame, and she got them right, and the light went.
