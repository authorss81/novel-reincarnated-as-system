# The Question She Asked Was Wrong

Seven days of it, the fifteenth to the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,144, and the seventh of them is the day she found out she had been asking the wrong question for two months.

Nothing was taken off her. That is what a first is for and she has known it since she was nine, and the fourteen days between the fourteenth and the twenty-first went past at the rate that days go past.

## What Changed In The Ground

On the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,144 a man of fifty in the eleventh row came and asked her whether his two rows were counted right.

That is all it took.

He had two rows and he had been in that ground a long time and he had never had a reason to check them, and he said he could count them himself but he did not trust himself, and she counted them in nine seconds and told him.

And then a woman of thirty-four asked her the same question about a store she had run for four years, and a boy of seventeen asked her whether four thousand and ninety stones at two stones to a cart is a whole number of carts, and she did it standing in the dirt with her arm hanging and thirty people watching, and none of them were grateful and none of them were not.

She has been unable to be given anything for nine years and unable to be paid and unable to be kept, and on the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,144 a man of fifty in the eleventh row asked her for a thing she can give, and she gave it, and it was nine seconds.

And by the nineteenth there were nine people a day coming to the north end of the rows at some hour or other, and by the twenty-first there was a queue of small sums at the frame at the second hour of the afternoon, and forty feet of it, and she worked them from the ninth hour to the seventh.

One of them on the twentieth was a woman of fifty with a stone tally she has kept for six years, four marks on a flat stone for every cart of stone that came into that ground, and she wanted to know how many she had, and there were two hundred and forty-one marks on it, and she put the stone down afterwards and looked at it for a while and then put it in her apron and went back to her row.

That is what it looks like. There is no ceremony and there is no rate and there is no hand that is paid and not one of them is grateful and not one of them is not.

That is what being findable looks like from the inside of a ground of two hundred people, and she did not enjoy it and she did not stop, and she has thought about both of those facts every day since.

## The Figure Moves

And here is the part no one has ever had to think about, because no one has ever been told.

The figure moves.

One in twenty of a day is an hour and twelve minutes. That is what comes off every day she stands on a slate two days of road west, and so at the ninth hour on the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,144 her total was four days and two hours and twenty-four minutes, and by the twenty-first it was four days and nine hours and thirty-six minutes, and the number she said out loud in front of two hundred people on the fourteenth was already wrong.

There is no instrument in the world that keeps a total still.

## What She Found Out On The Seventeenth

There is one question that came out of seven days of it that she did not expect and it came on the seventeenth of the month beginning 2,144 at about the fourth hour, from a man of thirty-three in the second row who was doing a sum for a woman of fifty.

He said, “Is it the same for everybody.”

She said, “The fraction is. One in twenty is one in twenty on every ground in this county and it has been since before your mother was born.”

He said, “Then it is not about how long you have been here.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “Then what is it about.”

And she stood at the frame with nine people behind her and worked out the answer in nine seconds, and the answer is that a figure about a person is made of two things and she has had both of them in her hand since she was nine years old.

It is the day it starts and the fraction. And the day it starts is the day a name goes on a board, and that is the only thing in it that is not the same for everybody, because it is a thing that happens to a person and not to a ground.

She said, “It is about the day your name goes on a board. Not the day you arrived and not how many stones you have carried and not how many years. One day. And then one in twenty of every day since, and it does not stop.”

The man of thirty-three put the pen down.

He said, “Then it is not the ground.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “Then what have I got to do with it.”

And she had no answer to that at all, and she has had seven days since and has not found one, and it is the first time in nine days of road that she has been asked a question by a person in a ground of two hundred people that she could not do.

She said it out loud at the ninth hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,144, standing, in daylight, at the north end of two hundred rows of stones, to nine people and a board.

She said, “I asked a question on a floor two months ago in front of a person I did not ask for, and I cannot un-ask it and I cannot ask it twice, and I have been carrying it about every day since, and it is a bad question and I am telling you the answer to it so it stops being in my head.”

She said, “Can a person be announced without being designated.”

She said, “No. You cannot. Every announcement that has ever gone anywhere in this county has gone with a name on it, and if a thing is announced then it is designated, and if it is designated then there is a person to collect, and the only person to collect is on a board.”

And then she said the second half, which is the one that took her two months and which she says out loud once and is not going to improve on:

“And the question was wrong, and I want the nine people standing here to hear the reason, because it is the only interesting thing anybody has said in this ground in nine years.”

She said, “I did not want an announcement without a name. I wanted a figure without a name. And a figure with no name on it cannot be found by anything, and it is not found, and that is exactly what happened on the fourteenth.”

She said, “I said four days and one hour and twelve minutes in this ground on the fourteenth and I did not say whose, and forty people heard it and forty people can count to eighty, and not one of them will be able to say whose it was tomorrow.”

## What She Would Not Say

There was one question she refused all seven days and she refused it four times, and the fourth refusal is the one she has thought about most.

A woman of sixty asked her on the fifteenth, at the twelfth hour, what is mine.

A woman of sixty asked her again on the sixteenth.

A man of twenty-five asked her on the nineteenth what hers was, meaning the woman of sixty’s.

And on the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,144, at the eighth hour, at the frame, in daylight, with nine people at a queue of small sums and the four men putting their strokes in for the night, she said:

“No. And I want the reason said out loud, because I am not refusing any of you and I am not above the ground. A figure about a person is a thing that gets found, and I have been found twice in nine days, and I know what it costs the person it happens to. I am not going to be the reason there is a second one.”

And a man of forty in the ninth row said, “You have told us there is one.”

And she said, “I have. And I have not told you how, and that is the whole of what I am able to do about it, and it is not enough, and I knew that when I said it.”

## The Thirty-Six Nights

And on the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,144, at the ninth hour, she counted the board, because that is what she does.

Her line went in on the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114. There have been thirty-six nights since that day. Four men write a stroke and a number under it in the evening, one each, and they have done it for nine years, and thirty-six times four is a hundred and forty-four.

She counted them at the ninth hour with the light going and got a hundred and forty-four, and there was one stroke at the top that the man who has the second hour had put in early again, and she left it, because it is the same man and the same habit and it is not a thing she is going to spend a day correcting.

A hundred and forty-four lines on a board, and one of them is hers, and four men cannot read it.

And on the twenty-second of the month beginning 2,144 she is going to start planning how to be two days of road west at the first hour of the first of the month beginning 2,174, and she has not slept eight hours in one place since she was nine years old and she has not slept eight hours in this ground once.
