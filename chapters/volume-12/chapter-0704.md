# What Two Hundred People Can Do

She walked east for a day and a half and she did not tell anybody where she had been and she did not stop at the bench, and both of those are the same decision.

She went down four miles of mountain road and nine miles of store road and past the mill with the wheel that has not turned in nine years and past the cottage at the stopped lime bed, where the woman was in the yard with her hands behind her, at the third hour on the eleventh of the month beginning 2,204, saying nothing, as she has said nothing in nine days of road.

Then thirteen miles east to a turn, and the woman of sixty was on the bench at the ninth hour of the eleventh, and she looked at the bar going past and she did not say one word, and Ning Yan did not stop, and there is a name in the fourth column of a slate that no one on this road has ever read and she has read it and she is carrying it thirteen miles east and she is not going to be able to put it down.

## The Ground She Came Back To

She was at the north end of the rows at the second hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 2,204.

Six hundred and fifty-five days lighter, not one of them gone back. A bar nine feet nine with no strap on it. An arm that had been shut for two days and was open on the second day of the walk. Four fingers and a thumb.

And a queue at the frame.

Not four long. Nine or ten at the third hour of the afternoon, and about half of them under thirty, and she stood at the south end of the ground for a count of forty before she came up, and she counted what she needed to count.

Nine of them were doing it without her.

Nine people in a ground of two hundred who were doing sums for other people and who had not been doing sums on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 2,144, which is nine days ago, and which is the last day she was here and counted them.

The man of forty in the ninth row was doing four. The woman of thirty-four was checking his working before she gave an answer on. A boy of seventeen she had never spoken to was doing two, badly, and correcting himself, which is what she was doing at his age in a shed at a mill.

## What She Found Out About The Nine

She spent the first two days of the fourth watching, and not counting, which is the hardest thing she does.

On the twelfth she stood at the south end from the first hour to the seventh and did not go near the frame. She watched four of them working. It is not that they are clever and it is not that they are fast.

A figure is a subtraction. You take the days and you take a twentieth of them and you take it off the days and what is left is a total. There is no key to that. She said it at the south end on the sixteenth in nine words and a woman of sixty in the eleventh row finished the sentence for her before she had got to the end of it, and got it right, and did not realise she had done it.

The second day she worked out how many there were.

On the twelfth there were nine people doing it who were not doing it on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 2,144. On the sixteenth there were fourteen. And on the thirteenth she counted the number of people in that ground who can count to eighty, and got forty, and she stood there with that figure for a count of forty and then stopped counting, because the number was not going to change and there is nothing else in this world to count.

Then she worked out the other half of it, and the other half is what she has not been able to put down since.

Four of the nine are under thirty. Three of them are women. The man of forty in the ninth row has a brother on the sixth row who has done nothing but carry stones for nine years and who came to the frame on the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,144, on his own, and stood behind six people, and did not ask for anything at all.

She watched him go away at the seventh hour and she could not work out what he had come for.

## The Whole Of It

She worked it out standing at the south end in daylight with her thumb on the last stone in the last row, and it took nine seconds and it is the plainest arithmetic anybody has ever done in this ground.

There is no craft in it.

A total is a date, a day and a fraction, and the fraction is one in twenty, and the fraction is the same on every ground in this county and has been since before anybody alive was born. She has not found it. She has not kept it. She stood at a frame on the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,144 and said that she had told two hundred people there was a figure and had not told them how, and she thought at the time that that was the whole of what she could do about it.

She thought that nine days ago.

There are forty people in this ground who can count to eighty. There are two hundred people in this ground who have a slate with their name on it twenty miles west or a figure in a pencil column on a frame, or a set of days in a column somewhere, or nothing at all and a number of years they can count back on their own two hands.

And not one of them has to be told how. Not one of them has to be told anything. It is subtraction. She said the sentence at the south end of the ground, out loud, to nine people at the frame, because she does not let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists:

“I did not keep it. I never kept anything. There is nothing on this ground that was hidden and there is nothing anywhere that I am the only one who knows, and there is a boy of nineteen in this ground who told me once that if one of them knew there would be not one of them left on the rows in a month.”

She said, “He was not warning me. He was doing arithmetic. He was fifteen when he said it and he is not wrong, and I have had nine days to work out what it is he was right about and I have not worked it out.”

And she said the one that has been sitting under all of it, at the fourth hour, in daylight, to nine people at a frame:

“And I am not going to find out by asking, because there is not one person in this ground I could stand in front of and ask that I am entitled to ask, and that is not modesty. That is a wall and I have been up against one of them at every turn of the last four years.”

She said, “And I am going to go on not having it, and a boy of nineteen told me once what the not-having is worth, and it was not one of them left on the rows in a month, and I have a figure in my head that proves him right and I am not going to be able to put it down.”

## What She Did Not Do

She did not go and tell them.

That is the whole of the decision and she has gone over it every day since and she has not found a way round it.

There was a frame at the north end and two hundred people in a ground and a queue at it of nine or ten a day, and she could have stood there at the ninth hour, in daylight, standing up, in the ordinary voice, the way she has said everything for nine years, and said: a figure about a person in this county is a subtraction of one in twenty off the days in a column, and it begins the day your name goes on a board and it does not stop, and that is what I said out loud on the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,144.

And she did not do it, and she is not going to, and she has three reasons and two of them are good.

The first is that she said, at the ninth hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,144, in front of nine people, that she was not going to be the reason there is a second one, and she has been found twice in nine days and she knows what that does to the person it happens to.

The second is that she does not know what it does. She has ruled out four things about a figure on a wall in a building in this county and found the fifth, and she is nineteen and she has one good arm and she does not know what comes off a ground of two hundred people when two hundred people know what they are being counted for.

A boy of nineteen told her at the fifth of the month beginning 2,054 that there would be not one of them left on the rows in a month, and he said it as arithmetic out of nine years of his own and there is no how in it she could hand to anybody.

And the third reason is not good and she has said it out loud because she does not do anything else.

She cannot stop counting. She has been counting since she was nine years old and it is not a habit and it is not a skill and it is the only thing that has ever been hers, and she could no more stop counting in this ground than she could stop having four counts of breath in her chest. She has been in this ground nine days and she has counted the strokes on the board every night of it and she counted the number of people at the frame before she counted anything else, and she will do it tomorrow, and it is not going to get better.

She said, at the ninth hour of the sixteenth of the month beginning 2,204, standing, in daylight, at the north end of two hundred rows of stones, with a queue of small sums four feet behind her:

“I am not going to tell them, and I am not going to stop counting, and I am not going to be able to tell anybody which of those two is the reason.”

And she did four sums for the people at the frame and she got them right.

## What It Is Going To Cost

And that is the part she has not been able to put anywhere, and it is the last of the sixteenth of the month beginning 2,204.

She is nineteen years old. On the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,144 she stood at this frame in the daylight and said a true figure about herself out loud, in front of two hundred people, and it was the first one that has ever been said, and she meant it when she said it, and she would say it again.

And for nine days she has been the only person in this ground who knows that a figure like that exists and where it comes from.

And now there are fourteen, and there were nine on the day I came back, and it is not going to stop there.

And she said her own total out loud in this ground nine days ago and meant it, and she said she would not do it to anybody else, and the whole world is going to do it to everybody on its own in about a year, and she did not do that and she cannot stop it and she cannot tell one person in this ground that it is coming, because telling them is the thing she decided at the ninth hour of the eighteenth, standing up, in the daylight, in front of nine people, and she has never in her life gone back on a thing she said out loud.

She counted the board at the ninth hour with the light going, because that is what she does.

Her line went in on the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114 and there have been ninety-one nights since that day and four men write one each, and ninety-one times four is three hundred and sixty-four.

She counted them and got three hundred and sixty-four and there was one stroke at the top that the man with the second hour had put in early, and she left it.
