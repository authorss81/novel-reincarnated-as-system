# What The Levy Can Reach

The man with the board was at the two posts at the sixth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054 and she was at the two posts at the sixth hour with the bar on her left shoulder, because the arm was open and it is open four days in five.

He had a slate out of the frame and a rag in his back pocket and a short stick of white chalk in his right hand, and he wiped the slate from the left corner to the right corner with the flat of his thumb in the time it takes a man to turn round twice, which is what he had done on the twenty-first, and put the slate back into the frame.

Then he said, “Say your name.”

She said, “I am not going to say it yet. I have one question and I am going to ask it standing up in daylight, and after that you can have whatever you want from me.”

He said, “I have got four columns to write before the light goes.”

She said, “Then it will be one question.”

## The Question She Held Back

She said, “When my name is in that column and the days are in beside it, what goes on that board on the first of the month beginning 2,084.”

He said, “All of it.”

She said, “That is not an answer, that is a fact. I have asked about the going.”

He said, “Then here is the answer and it is short. The name goes on it and the days go on it and the days are yours and the name is yours and the first of the month is the day it goes up, and from the first of the month until the next first, anybody who comes along this road can find out where you are and what you are counted as.”

He said, “That is the whole of what a column is. There is not one more thing on it.”

She said, “And the fifth.”

He said, “The fifth comes off before it goes up. It comes off the days and it does not say so on the board and it does not come off the name.”

She said, “Then the board on the first says thirty days of mine and thirty days of mine are on it and the board is not a true statement.”

He said, “It is a true statement about what went up.”

She said, “Yes.”

The three hours were three columns, a rag, and a woman of sixty on a bench four hundred yards west who did not come down the road. The sun came over the shoulder of the hill and the slate stayed clean.

She stood there for a count of twenty with her left hand on the strap of the bar and the open arm hanging and the light hard on the two posts, and then she said, standing, in daylight, at the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054, to a man of forty-nine with a stick of white chalk in his hand:

“My name is Ning Yan.”

## The Name In A County Hand

He said it back once, flat, the way she had said it three times into the grit three days ago, and he got it right the first time, and she has never worked out how and has stopped trying.

Then he wrote it.

He wrote it with the chalk, not with a nib, and he wrote it in one line, and the hand was not like hers and was not like the clerk’s. It was upright and level and every letter was the same size as every other letter, and he did not look at it while he wrote it, and he did not write slowly.

She watched his hand for the whole of it.

She has watched four hundred hands write on four hundred boards since she was twelve and there is not one thing about this that she did not already know, and she found out anyway that it is different to watch a hand put your name on a board.

The difference is that the hand is not hers and it is doing it for a living.

He wrote N I N G, a space, Y A N. He put a stroke in front of it, in the first column, the way a stroke goes in front of everything, and he wrote a number in the second column.

She said, “Thirty.”

He said, “Say it as a month.”

She said, “Thirty days of the month beginning 2,084, and the first of that month is seven days after today, and the twenty-fourth of this month is the day you take in what goes on the slate.”

He said, “That is right.”

He wrote it, and he wrote the month against it, and then he wiped the chalk off his thumb on his coat and put the chalk in his pocket, and the board was done.

## Thirty Days And A Day And A Half

She did the sum out loud at the frame, standing, in daylight, with a man of forty-nine standing four feet off and the chalk still in his pocket.

She said, “Thirty days. One in twenty. A day and a half.”

She said, “And on the first of the month beginning 2,084 that day and a half comes off the thirty before it goes up, somewhere between here and a room I have never been in, off a piece of slate that is the only copy of me in the world, and there is no line on the board that says it happened.”

He said, “That is right.”

She said, “Say it once more for the road.”

He said, “I am not going to say it again. I have four columns to write and the light is going.”

She said, “Then say it once for me, because you are a person and I am not.”

He said, “The name goes on it and the days go on it and the fifth comes off the days before it goes up and the person does not know what is left. That is all a column is and I have written four of them a month for nineteen years.”

She said, “All right.”

## What Four Hundred Yards West Saw

The woman of sixty came down the road at the ninth hour and did not hurry and did not stop at the bench, which she has not done in nine years, and she came across the grit to the two posts and stood about ten feet off and looked at the slate.

She said, “That is a name.”

She said, “Yes.”

The woman of sixty said, “That is your name.”

She said, “It is.”

The woman of sixty said, “I did not know you were going to do that on the day he comes with the chalk. I had nine days of guessing what you were going to do at this frame and I had it wrong every one of them.”

She said, “I am. I said on a bench on the nineteenth that I was going to and I have said it out loud to you and to two posts and to four people and to a man of forty-nine, and there is no way of taking any of it back and I did not want there to be.”

She said, “It is the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054 and there is nothing else to know about the day.”

The woman of sixty said, “It is the day he comes with the chalk.”

She said, “It is.”

## What Four Hundred People Did Not Do

And then nothing happened, and she had expected something to happen, and she had said on a bench on the nineteenth that she was going to stand in the open and be seen, and she had expected to feel it when the hand did it.

What she felt was this.

She stood in the grit at the foot of the two posts with a bar nine feet nine on her left shoulder and an open right arm and a slate four feet away with four strokes and a number and a month on it, and there was a woman of sixty at ten feet and a man of forty-nine at four, and a cart had gone west at the seventh hour and two men with a handcart had gone east at the eighth, and not one living person had stopped.

She said, out loud, standing, at the ninth hour, “It is a piece of wood four hundred yards from my hand.”

She said, “And it is the first time in my life that there is a line in a hand anywhere in the world with my name at the end of it that is not in a book against my ribs.”

She said, “And I have been lighter for five hundred and seventeen days and I have never once been found out, and there is a stroke in front of my name now and the finding out is the first of the month beginning 2,084, and from that day there is a fifth of whatever I put on it that can be taken off me by a person who has never met me.”

She said, “It cannot be put back. There is no solvent for chalk and there is no hand in this county that will take a line out of a slate, and I have said that out loud four hundred times in nine years about boards and I have never once said it about my own.”

The woman of sixty said, “Say the last part again.”

She said, “I cannot be un-written off that board. It is done. It was done at the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054 by a man of forty-nine with a stick of white chalk, and I asked him for it standing up in daylight as one question, and he wrote it and wiped the chalk off his thumb on his coat, and that is the whole of how it happened.”

## The Decision She Made Afterwards

The man put the slate back into the frame and put the frame square with the two posts by the eye he used for that and nothing else.

She said, “What happens to the slate on the first.”

He said, “It goes up.”

She said, “And then.”

He said, “And then there is a man with a cart comes along on the first at the second hour and the board goes up on the cart and comes back down and the slate is wiped and it goes in the frame empty for the next month.”

She said, “So there is one first at which four hundred people can find out where I am.”

He said, “One.”

She said, “Then I want to be at this frame at the first hour of the first of the month beginning 2,084, and I want to be standing in the grit when the cart comes, and I am not going to be nine miles off on a made road.”

He said, “That is not a thing anybody does.”

She said, “It is a thing a person does, and I am going to do it, and you are going to be here, and that is the whole of what I have got out of nine days of road and a tower and a stair and a question asked standing up in a room with a boy in it.”

He said, “Stay off the road.”

She said, “I will stand in the grit at the foot of the posts, four feet back, where a cart can go past without touching me and where a man carrying a board can get the slate out.”

She said, “And the arm is open and the bar is on my left and I am going to be standing here on the first of the month beginning 2,084 at the first hour, and I am telling you now, in daylight, at the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054, so that you can say afterwards that you were told.”
