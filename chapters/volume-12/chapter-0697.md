# The Only Person Who Can Say It

She wrote the eighty-first line in the dark with the stub of a nib and the cake of ink, standing at the board at the north end of the rows with the strip on her palm and her right arm open, and it took a count of forty, and the ink was cold, and the hand it came out of was a hand of four fingers and a thumb and it is the hand of a girl of nine.

Two thousand one hundred and fifty-seven less two thousand and seventy-seven is eighty, and eighty less eighty is nothing, and eighty-one is eighty-one.

Eighty-one divided by twenty is four and a twentieth. A twentieth of a day is an hour and twelve minutes.

Four days, one hour and twelve minutes. That is what is on the paper and her hand cannot write a false line and no one writes one twice.

She stood there in the dark and looked at it for a long time and then she said it out loud, once, to herself, at the frame, because she has never in her life let a sentence stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

Then she waited for the light, because she does not do this in the dark, and the terms do not allow it.

## What The Eighty-First Line Was

Here is what she did in the dark, because it is the whole of it and she has been over it every hour since.

The first eighty lines on that strip end at the thirteenth of the month beginning 2,144 and the figure at the bottom of them is four days. Four is eighty out of twenty and eighty is the number of days from the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054 to the thirteenth of this month with the first day as the first.

The eighty-first line is the fourteenth of this month. One more day, and a day out of twenty is a twentieth of a day, and a twentieth of a day is an hour and twelve minutes.

So she wrote one more line and the figure at the bottom of it is four days and one hour and twelve minutes, and she wrote the hours out in words as well as in figures, because a figure that is written in two places is a figure that can be checked by a person who cannot do the arithmetic.

She did that on purpose. She wrote the sum on the strip and then she wrote the same sum underneath it in words, in a big hand, four fingers and a thumb, on a strip of hand-made paper nine inches long that came down off the top of a stair with nothing on it.

And then she folded it once in the middle, the way it had been folded when it arrived, and put it in the front of her bag on top of the dish and the clay and the ring and the key.

And she stood at the board in the dark with the bag in her hand and found that she had written it twice, and that there is a rule in this world about writing one twice, and that the rule is about a thing being true, and that this was not that.

There is no line anywhere in this county that says a figure about a person may not be written on two pieces of paper by the person it is about. She checked, standing up, in the dark, at the frame, at about the third count of that hour.

There is nothing at all.

## The Saying Of It

At the first hour on the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,144 the four men came with the barrows and went past and one of them looked at her and said good morning and she said good morning, and about a hundred and sixty people came out of the ground into the light and started the day.

She stood at the board with the bar on the ground at her feet and the strip in her coat and the sun coming up over the rise behind her, the way it comes up over that rise every day of her life.

One thing at a time. In daylight. Standing up.

She said, at the ninth hour, standing, in daylight, at the north end of two hundred rows of stones, in the ordinary voice, so that it would arrive:

“My name is Ning Yan. I am nineteen. I have been on a slate at a turn two days of road west of this ground since the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054, and that is eighty-one days counted with the first day as the first. A fifth of a month comes off the days in a column before a board goes up and this county takes one in twenty.”

She said, “And it has taken four days and one hour and twelve minutes off me, and that is my own total, and it is the first one that has ever been said out loud in front of anybody, and I am saying it standing up in the daylight because that is the only way a thing gets to exist in a place where two hundred people can hear it.”

## What Happened

Nothing happened.

That is what she has thought about every hour since. Two hundred people were in that ground and forty of them were inside forty feet of her, and not one of them said a word, and the day went on, and the barrows went out and came in and the four men did not leave their work.

A woman of sixty in the seventh row put a stone down and picked up another one nine seconds later and did not look round. A man of thirty in the fourth row, who is the man she lied to about the light on the seventh of the month beginning 2,144, was forty feet off and carrying a barrow and he did not stop.

It took her nine seconds to stand there and understand that she had done the thing.

The thing she has wanted to do since she was nine years old is to have one figure about herself in the open, said, true, and heard. And she has got it, and the whole of the price is that it was cheap, and there is not one instrument in nine days of road that can take it back now, because two hundred people have heard it and forty of them can count to eighty.

And she was not light for another moment after she said it. She was light before and she was light after, and she stood there and understood that the figure she had just said out loud was not something that happened to her.

It is a number somebody else has been keeping since the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054, whether she says it or not. She had not made it. She had only read it.

## The First Question

The first question came from a man of forty in the ninth row at a count of a minute after, and he asked it well, which she had not expected.

He said, “How do you know that is the number.”

She said, “I counted the days.”

He said, “Counted them off what.”

She said, “Off the date the slate went on the road. That is all I am going to say about it.”

He said, “Why.”

And she said, standing, in daylight, at the north end of the rows, to a man of forty in the ninth row: “Because if I tell you how, then there are two hundred people in this ground who can do it, and I am not the only one who ever will, and that is a thing that belongs to whoever finds it and not to me.”

And the man of forty said, “That is a very fine way of saying you found it first.”

And she said, “Yes,” and he went back to his stone, and she has thought about that sentence every hour since and it is the one that is going to cost.

And then, a count of ten after he had gone, the same man said, over his shoulder, without turning round:

“There is four hundred and ninety people in this county who can count to eighty. I have been one of them for thirty years and I have never once counted anything about myself, because there was nothing to count. You have just told me there is.”

And she said, “Yes,” again, and he did not turn round, and she stood there with two yeses in a count of ten and she could not improve on either of them.

## What He Said Afterwards

He did not say anything.

He said it once, at the tenth hour, in the ordinary voice, in a place where two hundred people could hear it, and she did not stop him and she did not tell him to be quiet, because the fourth term is that he says it out loud before it is a line anywhere else and there is no line anywhere else and he is inside her head and he is not saving her anything.

He said, “You have just told two hundred people that there is a figure, and you have not told them how to get it, and there are forty of them who can count to eighty.”

He said, “I am not going to say anything else about it.”

She said, “You are not.”

And then the other three happened, and they all happened before the eleventh hour, and all three of them were the same question in different mouths.

A woman of sixty at the twelfth hour: what is mine.

A man of twenty-five at the second hour of the afternoon: how long is it.

A boy of ten at the third hour, from the fourth row, who was sent by somebody and did not admit it: how much of it is left.

## The Human Part

And she stood at the board at the ninth hour of the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,144, at the end of the day, in daylight that had gone long and flat, with the ground lying down behind her and about a hundred people lying down forty feet off, and she put down the only part of the day she is going to be able to remember in a year.

She said, out loud, to the rows and the board and the dark and the one person inside her head:

“I am nineteen years old and I have told two hundred strangers this afternoon the price of me, and not one of them knows what it was for, and not one of them is going to find out from me, and I am not going to tell them, and I did it in the daylight in front of everybody because the terms I gave on the twenty-first say that is how a thing gets to exist.”

She said, “And I would do it again at the first hour tomorrow, and that is the part I am going to have to look at.”

She picked the bar up off the ground in a count of twenty-two and put it on her shoulder with her left hand, because the right one is open and there is no strap, and she went down the length of the rows to the south end and stood there a while, and the strip with eighty-one lines on it was in her coat against her ribs, and it was heavier than paper that size has any business being.
