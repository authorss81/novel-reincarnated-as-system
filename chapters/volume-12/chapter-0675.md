# Counting The Days She Can See

She was at the two posts at the first hour of the first of the month beginning 2,084 and she had been in this county for thirteen days and had not slept in a building in any of them.

## Thirteen Days On A Store Road

The thirteen days are the whole of the middle of this and there is not a great deal to say about them.

She stood at the frame in the mornings. She read whatever was on the slate, because she had said out loud in front of a man of forty-nine that she would, there was nothing on it, and she read the nothing. A carter going west stopped on the twenty-eighth, looked at it, said, “That is not much of a board,” and went on.

She ate a loaf and a half a day. She did not sleep. She washed a cloth in the trough by the bench at the ninth hour and she did not have a place to put it at night so she carried it.

On the twenty-eighth she went back up the mountain, four miles up and four flights to a copying floor with four hundred volumes on the shelves, and a boy of sixteen was at the rack and a lamp was burning in a cage on the fourth landing and he had been keeping it for another eighteen days.

He said, “There is a name on a board at the turn.”

She said, “There is.”

He said, “Whose.”

She said, “Mine. On the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054, in a county hand, with thirty days of the month beginning 2,084 beside it.”

He said, “Then the first is in three days.”

She said, “The twenty-ninth, the thirtieth, and then the first, and the first is not in the month that is written on the slate. That is three.”

He said, “I had six. I counted off today.”

She said, “You counted off today. That is how a boy of sixteen gets six out of three.”

## Coming Down On A Date

She came down the mountain at the second hour of the thirtieth of the month beginning 2,054, which is the day before the first, and she walked the four miles and stood at the frame and did not sit down.

She has never in her life arranged to be the night before anything that was going to be said at the first hour, and she did it because the alternative was that she would be nine miles off on a made road and the board would go up and come back down empty and she would not have been there.

She said it standing at the two posts, at the fourth hour of the thirtieth, in daylight, with the bar on the ground against her boot:

“I have done the sum. One day is not enough for a mountain and four miles of made road if anything goes wrong. It is enough if nothing goes wrong. And there is nothing I can do about anything going wrong, so I have done the sum and I have come down and there is no second version of it.”

## The Woman Of Sixty On The Day Before

On the thirtieth of the month beginning 2,054 the woman of sixty said, at the ninth hour, sitting on the bench with a blanket over her shoulders:

“I have watched that frame for nine years and there have been three things on it in nine years and I want to tell you what I saw and you will not like it.”

She said, “Say it.”

The woman of sixty said, “Nothing on a board does not read. I have known that for nine years. I sat on that bench nine years watching four hundred people walk past a slate and not one of them ever learned how to read one.”

She said, “Then how did I do it.”

The woman of sixty said, “You stood at it. You stood in front of it in daylight for thirteen days with a bar on your shoulder and a thing in your face, and anybody who came along that road knew in about two hundred yards that somebody was standing there who wanted something, and about a fifth of them stopped and looked at the frame because the frame was what you were looking at.”

She said, “It was not the writing. It was you wanting it.”

She stood in the grit and took that.

She said, “Then the first was a waste and I made it a waste on purpose.”

The woman of sixty said, “The first was correct and you will work that out in about nine days. The first was the one nobody looked at and you were standing in the grit for it anyway, and that is the whole of what is worth having and it does not pay.”

She said, “Then what pays.”

The woman of sixty said, “Not one thing. Do not wait for that.”

## The First Hour Of The First

At the first hour of the first of the month beginning 2,084 a man with a cart came along the store road from the east and stopped at the frame and did not get off the cart.

She could see that he had done it before, in the way he put the cart parallel without being told and in the way he reached out with his right hand and did not look at what he was taking.

He took the slate out of the frame. He put it on the cart on its edge against a board with four bundles on it and a length of pipe, put a piece of sacking over it, and took the whole thing — slate, frame, two posts, all of it, because the posts go into sockets in a flat cart and he had four sockets and four bolts — and the cart stood there.

She stood four feet back, in the grit, where she had said on the twenty-fourth that she would stand, with the bar on her left shoulder and the arm open and the light coming grey off the east.

At the first hour and twenty minutes a woman came east with a basket and went past four feet of empty grit where two posts had been.

Then a carter going west. Then a boy of about nine with a dog. Then two men with a handcart, who slowed down and then did not. Then a woman with a bucket. Then a man on foot with a stick. Then three people together, who were talking. Then a boy with a load of straw. Then a man with a barrow of stone. Then a woman with a child.

Eleven people in an hour and ten minutes and not one of them looked at the grit.

## One First

She said it out loud, standing in the grit at the foot of two sockets, at the second hour of the first of the month beginning 2,084:

“Eleven people went past this frame in an hour and not one of them looked down.”

She said, “And I have been on it for one first out of the whole of my life, and the whole of what it cost me was a stroke, a number and a month, and a man with a cart has taken it east, and my name is not in this county any more until he brings it back on a wet evening and wipes it.”

She said, “That is not a failure. I want that said plainly, because I have been standing here for thirteen days with a bar on my shoulder waiting to feel something enormous and there was nothing enormous and there was a carter and a piece of sacking.”

## Thirty Days

The man with the board came back at the ninth hour of the first with the frame and the slate and the four bolts, put it back into the sockets, squared it by the eye, took the slate out of the sacking and wiped it from the left corner to the right corner with the flat of his thumb, left to right, without hurrying.

She said, “Is it empty.”

He said, “It is empty.”

She said, “When is the next one.”

He said, “The first of the month beginning 2,084 was this morning. The next is the first of the month beginning 2,114.”

She did the sum standing at the frame, out loud, in daylight, and it took her the length of one breath and she got it right first time.

She said, “Thirty days.”

He said, “It is thirty days. It has been thirty days all my working life.”

## The Eight Of Them

And then she counted them, standing in the grit at the foot of two posts, at the ninth hour of the first of the month beginning 2,084, with a man of forty-nine and a woman of sixty and a bar nine feet nine, and she did not hurry and she did not skip one and she did not count any of them twice:

She said, “The first of the month beginning 2,114. That is the second.”

She said, “The first of the month beginning 2,144. That is the third.”

She said, “The first of the month beginning 2,174. That is the fourth.”

She said, “The first of the month beginning 2,204. That is the fifth.”

She said, “The first of the month beginning 2,234. That is the sixth.”

She said, “The first of the month beginning 2,264. That is the seventh.”

She said, “The first of the month beginning 2,294. That is the eighth.”

She said, “And between now and the eighth of them there is one day and a half of me that will be taken off a column on a cart in the east under a piece of sacking, and not one of the eight will say so to me, and I will stand in this grit eight times and eleven people will go past and look at nothing eight times, and at the eighth something is going to happen that I cannot see from here.”

The man of forty-nine said, “Nothing is going to happen that you cannot see from here.”

She said, “Something is going to happen that I cannot see from here.”

## What She Is Going To Do With The Thirty Days

The woman of sixty said, from the bench, at four hundred yards, “You have counted eight firsts and you have been alive for none of them. Say what a person does with that.”

She said, “I am going to find out what takes a fifth.”

The woman of sixty said, “That is not a thing anybody can ask about.”

She said, “I know. I am not going to ask anybody. I have said out loud at this frame on the twenty-fourth and on the first that a fifth comes off the days before the board goes up, and a man of forty-nine confirmed it, and that is all I have got.”

She said, “And it comes off somewhere. It does not come off at this frame and it does not come off in Lowwater and it comes off between here and wherever a column is finally read, and that is east of here, because that is the way the cart went this morning.”

The man of forty-nine said, “You have been west for thirteen days and you were east for nine days before that and you were on a made road all the way.”

She said, “I know. And I have been counting things since I was nine years old and I have never once counted in the direction a cart went.”

She said, “A board goes west up a road. And the thing that takes a fifth off a board does not go west, because there is no fifth of me west of this turn, and there has not been since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,814, and this morning it started again.”

She said, “So the harvest is not in this county. It is east. And I am going east in the morning with a bar on my shoulder, and I have got thirty days, and I have got a name in a county hand, and I have got a fifth of a day of me already spoken for.”

She said, “And I have not got one single person who knows I am coming.”
