# A Record Belongs To Whoever Keeps It

He came east along the store road on the afternoon of the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,234 with a shut notebook under his arm, which is the way he has been for four years, and she was at the frame and saw him at the ninth mile and counted him in for about four miles.

He has never lied to her and has never threatened her and has never touched her and has never come into a room she was in and has never paid anybody for force, and he has never asked her a question, and he is not going to start now.

She had one.

## One At A Time

She let him get to the rail before she said anything, because a man with a notebook under his arm who is put at from forty yards does not answer the same as one who is waited for, and she wanted the ordinary voice.

She said, standing up, in daylight, at the frame, with the bar on the ground at her feet:

“I am going to ask you one thing and you are going to answer it or not and I am not going to ask you anything else today.”

He said, “Ask.”

She said, “Is a thing that is written in my own hand and sent up a stair still mine.”

He turned the notebook over once and did not open it.

## What He Said About Nine Seconds

He stood nine feet off and she watched him not answer for about a count of sixty, which is longer than any answer he has ever given her.

He said, “No.”

She said, “Say the rest of it.”

He said, “A record belongs to whoever keeps it. That is not a thing about records, that is a thing about hands. You write a line, and the line is yours, and the moment it is true and it is down and it is readable, it is a thing somebody else is keeping, and it does not come back to you. Not because anyone took it. Because it is not the shape of a thing that comes back.”

He said, “And I have kept one for nine years.”

She said, “You have.”

He said, “I have. And I have never once been able to take a line out of it. Not in nine years. There is no instrument for it. You can go back and change what you meant, which everybody does every day, and what is down does not know that you meant something else.”

## What She Asked For

And then he said the other part, and it is the part she did not expect and she has gone back over it more than once since.

He said, “That is the only question anybody has ever asked me that I would rather had been asked earlier.”

She said, “Earlier than what.”

He said, “Earlier than nine years ago.”

He did not say what the question was or what his line said or who wrote it, and she did not ask, because she has one question a day and she had spent hers, and because she could hear that a man does not volunteer the content of a thing he has kept shut for nine years.

He said, “You are the second person who has asked me something about a line instead of about a person. The first one is not somebody you will meet.”

And he put the notebook under his other arm and looked down the store road east, at thirteen miles of it and a broken wall at the end of it, and she understood that he had come a long way to be nine feet off a woman with a bar and was about to go on and had never intended anything by it, and there was nothing in it for anybody, and neither of them said so.

## The Thing She Did Not Do

There was a thing he wanted and has wanted for nine years, and she has known what it is since a gate on a made road two years back and forty-four volumes ago, and she could have asked for it. She had asked him one question and she could have used the count of sixty after his answer to put it to him, and he was standing nine feet off in daylight and would have had to answer her.

She did not.

She said, “I have got what I came for.”

He said, “Then I will walk on.”

And he went east past the frame and past the bench and up the road without saying another word, and a woman of sixty on that bench watched him go and did not comment on it, and she has never commented on anything.

## Nine Feet Off, Going East

He goes at the pace a man with a book goes at, which is not slow and does not vary, and he had come four miles along a store road in the afternoon and she got nine words out of him and they were the nine she came for.

She stood in the grit and watched him go and noticed four things, which is what she does with a person instead of talking to them, because talking to people has never once got her a figure.

He did not stop when she stepped out. She stepped out at the rail and he came on at the same pace and stopped about nine feet off, and the nine feet were the same nine feet as at a gate on a made road four years back and he has never once varied it.

He did not sit down. There was a wall two hundred yards on that a man of any age would have sat on, and he did not, and it is not because he is tired, because he walks that road every month and she has watched him do it twice.

He turned the notebook over once, in his hand, and did not open it, and that is the whole of what he did with it, and she has seen him do it four times in nine years and it has never once been a threat and it has never once been an accident.

And he said her name back to her once, flat, and got it right the first time, the way a man of forty-nine gets it right the first time at a frame, and she has never worked out how either of them does it and has stopped trying about both.

## What It Did To The Plan

And that is the part of the day that she has not put down since.

She went back and stood at the frame with the light going and worked out what she had just been told, standing, in daylight, out loud, to a slate with her name on it in a county hand.

If a line does not come back, then there is no version of the thing she has been getting ready for in which she changes her mind on the twenty-fourth of a month and scrubs it off with a cloth, and there is no version in which she gets to the eighth first and stands in the grit and discovers she has nothing to put there.

There is no version in which she puts down a thing she does not mean.

She said, out loud, at the ninth hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,234, standing with the light gone off the slate and her arm open:

“Then I have got to have it before the twenty-fourth and I have got to have it exactly, and once it is on a piece of slate it is not a decision any more, it is a fact about a county, and I will not be able to stop it, and there is not one person in nine days of road who can be asked whether to stop it.”

## The Nineteen Days Before It

And then she sat down on the butt of the post for the second time in four days, which is twice in nineteen years, and worked out the other thing.

If it is true that a line cannot be taken out of a book, then it is true of the line she agreed to at a board at the north end of a ground two days of road east, four sentences in her own hand with a date on them, read out loud by a carter who could not read it and said a name off it anyway.

That has been true since the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,114 and she has known it since the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,114 and she has never once gone and tried to take it off, and the reason is not that she could not.

The reason is that she would have to write something over it, and anything she writes over it has to be true, and a board with a line scrubbed off it and a true line written back on it is still a board with a scrubbed line on it.

She said, out loud, to the two posts, sitting on one of them:

“So it goes up or it does not go up, and the difference between those two is decided before the day and not on it.”

She came off the post and picked up the bar in a count of twenty-nine and stood there a moment longer than she needed to, which is not a thing she does, and then she said out loud, in daylight, to an empty turn, at the ninth hour on the twentieth of the month beginning 2,234:

“I have eleven days and I have not got one word of it and I am going to get one.”

And a cart came east at four miles an hour and went past two posts with a slate on them, and there was not one thing written on that slate that was going to go up a stair, and she was going to have to fix that in eleven days, and there was not one person in nine days of road she was entitled to ask.
