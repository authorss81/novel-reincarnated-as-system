# Going West Past The Wall

She left the rows at the first hour of the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 2,144 with the bar on her left shoulder and a strip of paper with eighty-one lines on it in the front of her bag and two days of walking in front of her, and she did not say one word to anybody about where she was going, which is the correct thing to do and cost her something anyway.

She had never in nine years gone west out of that ground on purpose.

## The First Day

The ground is four miles from the south end of it to the north end and she walked it at the second hour of the morning with the bar on her shoulder and about a hundred people lying down about it, and she got to the gate and out of it by the fourth hour.

Then two miles of flat with nothing on it to lie down on and the sun coming up on her left over the whole of it and a broken wall at the end of it.

She put the bar down twice on the flat and once in the first mile and once at the wall, and every set-down cost her between twenty-nine and forty-one counts, and she was at the wall at the seventh hour of the twenty-eighth with her arm open and four counts of breath where she should have had four and nine where she had not asked for nine.

Two miles of flat with nothing on it to lie down on, and thirteen miles of store road after that, and a woman of sixty said something to her at a turn she has not got to yet.

The wall stands open four feet and a bit. She went through it at the seventh hour and looked west and there was a made road and the sun on it, and she did not count the leg between the wall and the ground behind her and she noticed herself not counting it, which she has done once before and did not enjoy then either.

## The Store Road

Thirteen miles, and she knows it to the yard in three places.

A fence on the north side that goes on for a mile and then stops. A cottage at a stopped lime bed at the fourth hour with a woman in the yard who has been standing with her hands behind her looking at the bed since before she was nine years old. A mill at the fifth mile with a wheel on the stream out of the mill race that has not turned in nine years.

She went past all three and the woman at the cottage did not say one word and neither did she, and she has been past that cottage eleven times in nine days of road and neither of them has ever said a word that was not about the hour and the weather and the bed.

And she stopped at the ninth mile, which is the end of the paid road, and stood at the post there for a count of sixty.

There is a post at the end of nine miles and after that it is not a road anybody pays for, and the thing about a paid road is that it is about how it is kept and not about where it goes. She stood at that post and worked out, out loud, in daylight, alone, that the paid part of that road is nine miles out of thirteen and she is standing at the end of the nine, and that there has been no road under her feet for four hundred yards.

And she said, out loud, at the ninth mile, to a post:

“I do not know what is up there and I am going to find out in three days and there is no person in this county I could ask and I have not got anybody.”

Then the four miles of road that is not paid for, and a cottage at the far side of it that is not the one at the lime bed, and then the turn.

## The Bench

The woman of sixty was on the bench.

She has sat on that bench for nine years and she was the last name on that board for four years before that, and she was sitting on it at the ninth hour of the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 2,144 with her hands in her lap, and she watched a woman with a bar come down the road from the east, and she did not say one word.

Ning Yan stopped.

She stood nine feet off the bench for a count of twenty, in daylight, with the bar on the ground at her feet, and there was nothing to say, and she said it anyway, because she has never in her life let a thing stand that she has not put into words.

She said, “I am going to stand at the frame for a first hour tomorrow and I have not been at one in nine years, and I am not asking you for anything.”

The woman said, “I know. There is not one person at that turn but the two of us and the carter, and I have been the last name on it for four years.”

And then the woman said, “You will be there at the first hour or you will not, and the light comes up over that shoulder of the hill at the first hour and it comes up on the frame last.”

That is all. It took nine seconds and it cost the woman of sixty something and she did not say what.

## The Two Posts

She got to the two posts at the fifth hour of the afternoon on the thirtieth of the month beginning 2,144, which is day two thousand one hundred and seventy-three, and the first hour of the first of the month beginning 2,174 is eight hours away.

The slate was in the frame.

Her name was on it, in a county hand, in the first column, and the days of the month beginning 2,144 were in beside it in the second column, thirty of them, all of them there, and the slate had been wiped and put back and it was clean as a wet board and it was four feet off the ground on two posts with a rail.

She stood in front of it and looked at it for a count of sixty.

It had been wiped and put back. That is what the first carter west does every day of his working life, with the flat of his thumb, in the time it takes a man to turn round twice.

And her name was on it. And the days were on it. And there was nothing taken off it that she could see.

And then she did the thing that took the rest of the hour.

She counted the days in her own column, out loud, standing, in daylight, one at a time, with her thumb going down the slate, and she got to thirty, and she counted them again and got to thirty, and she looked at the column beside her name the way she would look at a hand in a county seat and she found the same thirty.

She looked at the column above hers, which is a man of thirty-four who came on the fourth of a month, and there are thirty in that one too, and the one above that, and that one.

Every column on that slate has thirty days in it and every one of them is full and there is not one mark anywhere on that board that says a fifth of anything has gone.

She said, out loud, at the frame, at the seventh hour of the thirtieth of the month beginning 2,144:

“He told me it does not say so on the board.”

She said, “And he was right, and I have spent my whole life being right about boards, and I did not think to ask him what it would look like.”

## The Reversal

That is what she came for, and she stood at a frame in the last of the daylight and found that she could not get it.

A man of forty-nine told her at the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054 that the fifth comes off the days and does not say so on the board and does not come off the name.

So of course there is nothing to see. There is no mark. There is no line saying it and no figure on that slate that is different from any other month’s slate in this county, and a woman who has been lighter every single day for six hundred and thirteen days and who has told two hundred people a figure that is true was standing at a frame at the fifth hour of the afternoon and could not tell from it whether anything had been taken off her or not.

She said, out loud, in daylight, to the two posts and the slate and the empty road, with not one person inside a hundred yards:

“I have got a figure in my head that came off me off that thing and I cannot check it against it, and there is no mark, and that is on purpose, and whoever set it up that way is not hiding it, because a thing you cannot see does not have to be hidden.”

She said, “And I have been able to check everything I have ever had in my life against something. Every number since I was nine years old. And there is nothing on this slate to check this one against, and that is the first time that has ever been true.”

She stood there and then she did the thing she does, which is to look at the frame properly, all the way round it, every edge of it, the top rail and the two posts and the inside of the frame where the slate sits, because the man of forty-nine takes that slate out of the frame every month and puts it back the other way up.

And she looked at the inside of the frame for a count of twenty and the daylight was going and she could not see anything at all, and she came back to it at the first hour tomorrow with the light coming up on the frame last, because a woman of sixty who has sat on a bench for nine years told her so and told her true.
