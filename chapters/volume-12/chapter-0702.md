# The Room That Expected Somebody

She was at the frame at the first hour of the first of the month beginning 2,174 and she was at the bottom of a mountain on the third of that month, and she had not said one word to the woman on the bench in between and she has not said one since.

The store road goes west out of the turn for nine miles and there is a post at the end of the nine, and after that it stops being a road anybody pays for, and then it is four miles up.

The bar went up and came down twelve times on the road and nine times on the mountain, and the last set-down cost her fifty-four counts, which is the most it has ever cost her, and she stood at the bottom of the hill at the eighth hour of the second of the month beginning 2,174 with her arm open and the yard thirty feet across in front of her and the tower four flights and a stair above her head.

## Staying Thirty Days

She stayed in that building for thirty days, and it is the longest she has been in one place since she was nine years old, and the boy of sixteen asked her on the fourth day why and she told him, one question at a time, in daylight, standing up, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “Because the next one is on the third of the month beginning 2,204 and I have worked out that it is seventy-eight days and not seventy, and seventy-eight is longer than seventy, and I know why it is longer, and the why is the whole of the thirty days.”

He said, “Say the why.”

She said, “The first one past the last working point took a hundred and thirty-one days and I made it myself, in my own hand, on a board, because there was nothing above the line to give me one. This one has to be waited for, because a thing that is going to be sent has to have somewhere to arrive, and I have to find the somewhere first, and finding one takes longer than making one.”

He said, “Have you found it.”

She said, “No. And I have been to the top of the stair fourteen times in thirty days and there is a room up there and there is not one thing in that room for anybody, and I do not know if it is a somewhere or if it is only a room.”

And then she told him what she had not told anybody, on the eleventh of those thirty days, at the second hour, in daylight, standing up, on the copying floor with four hundred volumes on the shelves behind her.

She said, “I am looking for a place where there is something to receive it. That is the whole of the thirty days. There is a receiver in this world and I have been trying to find one since the twelfth of the month beginning 2,054, where I asked whether a person can be announced without being designated, and there is a man who has carried four bundles up that stair in thirty-one years and on each of the four a man came out of that door and took his hat off.”

He said, “That man is nine years dead.”

She said, “Yes.”

He said, “Then the room is not expecting anybody.”

She said, “The room is expecting somebody. It has a long table down the middle of it and a lamp at the far end and no chairs. Rooms are made for people. That is not the same as a person being there.”

## The Work She Did Not Do

She copied nine sheets a day with him on the copying floor for four of the thirty days, and she read the four hundred volumes on the shelves about a fifth of what is in them, and she slept on the floor against the end of the bench where she has slept before.

The drawer is at the west end of the floor under the rack, and it has a lock on it, and the key has been in the front of her bag every one of those thirty days about a foot from her hand.

She did not use it. She has no reason and she knows why she has no reason, and the reason is that the four sheets in that drawer are a clerk’s and not hers, and she has said for four volumes that she is not going to find out what is in them, and a drawer that is a foot from her hand for thirty days does not change that.

He saw her looking at it twice and said nothing about it, which is correct, and she has not thanked him for that and is not going to.

## The Fifth First

The first of the month beginning 2,204 came at the first hour and she was at the top of the stair, four flights above a copying floor, sitting on the top step in the dark with her back to the wall and the bar lying along the floor behind her.

She counted it going past.

It is thirty days from the first of the month beginning 2,174, which is the fourth first, and that one she was standing at the frame for at the first hour with the light coming up on the frame last. And somewhere a long way east, at a turn, a slate came out of a frame at the first hour and went on a cart and the fifth came off the days before it went up and it did not say so on the board.

And she is on that board. Her name, her days, and a figure in pencil in the third column of a slate that is lying face-up in a frame somewhere with the light on it that she cannot see.

And she did not go and watch. She sat on the top step of a stair in the dark at the first hour of a first of a month and counted it going past, and that is the first first of a month she has ever let go by without wanting to be at it.

## Seventy-Eight

Two thousand two hundred and six less two thousand one hundred and twenty-eight is seventy-eight.

Seventy days is what a stage takes when there is a person standing in the way of it. Seventy-eight is what it takes when the person has to go and stand somewhere else first.

She worked that out on the first of the month beginning 2,204, sitting on the top step, in the dark, out loud, to a stair, and she said it into the space where the door is, and then she said the other half, which is the half that has been standing in her since the third of the month beginning 2,054:

“An announcement is not made to be heard. It is made to arrive. And it arrives where there is something to receive it.”

She said, “And I have found the room. And the room has been standing empty for nine years and there has not been a person in it, and I have been up fourteen times in thirty days and there is still not one thing in it for anybody.”

She said, “And I am going up in about an hour and I am going to be standing at the outside of that door when it comes, and that is the whole of the preparation I have got, and it is the first time in eleven years that the preparation for a stage has been standing outside a door.”

## What It Costs

It came at the second hour of the third of the month beginning 2,204 and it took nine seconds, the same as the last one, and she was standing at the low door with her eye at the grille and her left hand on the rail and her right arm open.

There was no announcement. There is no voice and there is nothing in the air and there is no line anywhere.

Afterwards she stood on the top step with her back against the cold iron and worked out what had changed, standing, in the dark, out loud, and it took her nine seconds, and it is a thing she has carried every day since and she is not going to explain it twice.

She cannot be met where she is expected.

Not by a person. The room at the top of that stair has been built for somebody who comes when there is something to receive, and everything in it is arranged for that person: a long table down the middle of it, a lamp on a shelf at the far end, and nothing on the table and no chairs anywhere, because a person who is met is somebody who is expected, and a room is a thing that expects.

And she is not met. She is expected and she is not met, and there is no instrument in the world that will tell a room what to do about a person standing on the wrong side of a low door, and she is the wrong person by nine years of arriving on her own and not on anybody’s errand.

Then she put her eye back to the grille.

The lamp at the far end of the long table was burning.

It had been out when a boy of sixteen and a woman of nineteen looked at it through the bars nine years ago and it had been out since before he was born, and beside it on the shelf there is the stub of a candle that went into the dish long enough ago that the dish has one grey bloom of cold wax in the bottom of it.

The bar is still across the outside of the door. The dust on the top of it is not broken anywhere and the door has not moved and the nine feet of it between the bar and the floor has not been walked on by anything.

Something in that building had gone up four flights, opened the door, lifted the bar, put a lamp in a cage at the far end of a long table and put the bar back and shut the door, in about the length of nine seconds, and had not opened the door.

She stood at the grille for a count of a hundred with a bar lying on the floor behind her.

And then she said, into the dark, to the door, in the ordinary voice:

“You lit a lamp.”

She said, “Nothing in this world has ever lit a lamp for me before, and I am nineteen years old and I have been met four times in thirty-one years by a man in a doorway with his hat off, and the lamp is the first thing in eleven hundred years that met me, and it did not say one word.”

And she went down four flights with her left hand on the rail and her breath at seven on the third one and four on the rest, and the boy of sixteen was at the bottom of the stair in the dark with a lamp in a cage on the fourth landing over his head that he has been keeping going for four months now, and he had heard her come down.
