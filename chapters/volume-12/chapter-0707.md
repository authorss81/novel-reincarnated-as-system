# The Firsts Are Only Dates

She stood at the frame for four days and asked one question out loud in daylight every day of it, standing up, in the ordinary voice, to a slate, because that is the only instrument she has ever had.

## The Question

Can the first of a month do anything to her that thirty ordinary days have not already done.

She put it the same way four times so that she could not move the terms, which is what she does with a thing she is frightened of.

And then on the second of those four days she worked out how to answer it, standing, with her thumb on the rail of a frame, and the answer took her a count of nine and it is the plainest arithmetic anybody has ever done at that turn.

## What Has Actually Happened At One

She went through them. That is what she does with a thing. She goes through it from the top and down, fast, the way she reads a column, and she counted six of them out loud and got six every time.

She said, standing, in daylight, on the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,234:

“What happens on a first. The man of forty-nine takes the slate out. He wipes the frame. He wipes the road side with his thumb and turns it over and puts two columns in on the other side. He puts the slate back with the chalk side out. A man with a cart comes at the second hour and the board goes up on the cart and comes back down before the twenty-fourth and the slate is empty for a month.”

She said, “That is the whole of it. That is what a first is. There have been six of them since my name went on that slate and I stood at two of them and watched the other four from a distance, and not one of them has done one single thing to me that the twenty-eighth of a month has not also done.”

## The Thing That Was Always Happening

And then she said the other half of it, and this is the half that took the rest of the day.

“A first is not a day that does something. It is the day the thing that has been happening quietly gets counted in front of other people.”

She said, “A column has been losing a twentieth of a day since the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054. That is the ninth hour of a day and it has not stopped on one first or thirty firsts or three hundred firsts.

It goes on while I am asleep. It is the only continuous thing in this county that has my name anywhere in it, and I could read it two days of road west of here on the other side of that slate in a man’s hand and he does not know he is writing it.”

She said, “So a first is not a door and it is not a wall. It is a date. It is the day a sum is shown to somebody who was not shown it on any of the other days.”

## Six Of Them, And What She Expected

She sat down on the butt of the post on the fourteenth and did the other half of it, which is that she went through the firsts she has lived through since her name went on a slate and asked herself what she was standing at each one for.

That is six of them. She did all six out loud, standing, in the rain, one at a time, to a frame, because she does not let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

The first of the month beginning 2,084: she stood in the grit and watched a cart go up the road with her name on it and she was not afraid of it. She was watching to see whether the world would end, and it did not, and she thought that was the whole of that morning.

The first of the month beginning 2,114: she was at the north end of a ground of two hundred people and had said her own total out loud four days before and was standing at a board at the end of the day watching people get found.

The first of the month beginning 2,144: she was at the north end of the rows and she had said a true figure about herself in front of two hundred people and could not say any of it again.

The first of the month beginning 2,174: she was at this frame for the first time in nine years, and she had read a figure in a pencil column that was right.

The first of the month beginning 2,204: she was on the top step of a stair in the dark, four miles up a mountain, and she let it go by on purpose.

The first of the month beginning 2,234: she was at a broken wall thirteen miles east of here and watched the whole of it happen at a distance.

She said, standing, in the rain, on the fourteenth: “And every one of those was a day. And on four of the six I was somewhere else, and on two of them I was here, and nothing whatever came to me on any of them.”

## The Thing That Has Never Happened

And then she did the second half of the count, which took her the better part of an hour standing in the grit in the rain, and she did not get it wrong.

She has been announced nine times. Nine times the thing above the last working point has said something out loud into a county with not one person in it to be told, and nine times it went into the ground.

And she has been at two of the firsts inside the last hundred days and she stood at this frame for one of them and at a broken wall thirteen miles east of here for the other, and at not one of them did anything arrive.

Not a person. Not a message. Not a carter with something in his hand. Not a bundle. Not a boy.

She said it out loud, standing, in daylight, on the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,234, with a bar on the ground at her feet and a woman of sixty four hundred yards west who did not come:

“Not one thing has ever arrived at a first of a month. Not once in nineteen years. And I have been waiting nine times for one and there has been nothing nine times.”

## What That Does To The Plan

And then she sat down on the butt of the post, which she has never done at that frame, and worked out what that does to a plan she has been carrying since a tower four miles up a mountain.

She has been standing at this frame on firsts for nine years, and she has been standing at this frame on the firsts inside the last hundred days, and the whole of what she has been doing is standing at a date.

She said, out loud, to the two posts, at the seventh hour of the fifteenth:

“If a first is only a date, then I do not have to be here on one. I have to be here on the day the slate is loaded, which is the twenty-fourth, because that is the day a man with a stick of chalk puts thirty days in beside my name, and the day a cart takes it, which is the second hour, and the day the whole county can find out where I am.”

She said, “And everything else about a first is a thing I have been standing in the grit watching happen to me.”

She had not written it down anywhere. It went out of her standing up in the rain in a gravel turn and it is true and there is a count of nine of her holding it, and there is not one other person in this county holding it.

## What She Wants Now

And then, because she does not let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists, she said the last of it at the ninth hour on the sixteenth of the month beginning 2,234, standing with the light going off the slate and her arm open and the bar across her knees.

She wants to stop standing at dates.

She has been a person who stands at things for nineteen years. She stood at a window and at a board and at a frame and at a wall and at a door at the top of a stair, and every one of those was a place where something was supposed to come, and at every one of them something came eventually except the door.

And the finding of four days in a gravel turn is that she has been standing at a first of a month as if a first were a place where a thing comes to her, and it is not, and it never has been, and the reason she has kept standing there is that she did not have anywhere else to stand.

She said, “And I am not going to be able to tell anybody I have worked that out, and I am not going to be able to put it in five blank leaves against my ribs, and there is not one person in this county who would understand a word of it, and I am saying it out loud to a slate because the slate is the only thing at this turn that is going to still be here tomorrow.”

She put her hand flat on the frame, about a foot above the ground, the way she did on the fourth first.

“And I have got a first of a month in fifteen days that I have been standing at for nine years in my own head, and it turns out it is a day, and I have got something to put on it, or I have not, and I have not worked out which.”

She picked the bar up in a count of twenty-two and put it on her left shoulder and stood in the grit with the rain coming off the top rail onto the back of her neck, and she did not go west, and she did not go east, and she stayed at the frame with the light gone off it for a count of a hundred, which is the longest she has stood at anything in nine years without wanting a thing to arrive.
