# A Name In Another Mouth

It came to the north end of the rows at about the seventh hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,114, on the back of a cart that was going east with nothing on it that mattered.

## The Cart

There is a made road two miles west of a ground with two hundred people in it and carts come off that road four times a day, and for nine years they have come off it and gone past, because there is no gate on that ground and nothing at the north end of it worth stopping for.

The man on this cart stopped.

He stopped because there was writing on a board at the north end of the rows, which there has not been in nine years except for four strokes and four numbers, and he came about thirty feet up the rise, stopped the horse, got down, and walked over to look at it.

She was at the board. She had been at the board since the first hour.

He stood four feet off it with his hat off and read it.

He could not read it. That is established and it is not a small thing; it took her four days at a counter and a walk east to work out that it was not a small thing.

He looked at it for about twenty seconds and then he opened his mouth to say something ordinary to a woman standing next to a board on a made road, which is what a man does.

What came out of him was a name.

“Yuan Sheng.”

## Two Times

He said it once and then he said it again, and the second time he said it slowly, the way you say a thing you are trying to place.

“Yuan Sheng. That is what it says.”

She said, “Say the whole line.”

He said, “I cannot read the rest of it. I have got the name because a name is a name and anybody can read a name. It is not on any roll and it is not on any board that comes down this road and I have been driving this road for nineteen years and I have never heard it.”

There are about two hundred people in that ground.

There is a man of about fifty standing four feet off a board with a hat in his hand, saying a name, in daylight, at the north end of two hundred rows of stones, on the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,114.

Nobody in that ground moved.

And she stood there with her right arm hanging and four fingers and a thumb and the small open place low behind her fourth rib the size of a coin, and she heard a name that is written on the third leaf of a book against her own ribs in four capital strokes in her own hand, said out loud, in daylight, by a carter who has never been in this ground before, at a board she wrote three days ago.

Two people in the world have that name. One of them wrote it down on a leaf, in her own hand, when she was nine years old. The other has carried it for nineteen years without a body.

And a man with a cart has just said it to a field.

## What She Did About It

She did not say one word.

She said, out loud, standing, in daylight, to a man with a hat in his hand: “You have stopped for nothing. Go on east.”

And then she stood at the north end of a ground with two hundred people in it and she did not run, and she did not go up a road, and she did not put her hand on the board, and she stood still for a count of sixty while a carter got back on his cart and went east, and she watched him go and she could not tell whether the horse was going faster than it had come.

Then she said one sentence to the board, out loud, in daylight, standing up, which is how she has said every true thing since she was nine years old:

“That is not a thing that came down this road.”

## The First Hour Afterwards

She stood at the north end of the rows for an hour after the cart went and did not move, because the thing she had to do next was work and she could not do it while she was walking.

She counted the strokes on the board. There were twelve. Three nights of four men since the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114, and her one line in among them, and she looked at the twelve and worked out that four men a night for nine years is about thirteen thousand lines on that board and not one of them has ever been any use to anybody.

Then she did the only thing she could do with what had happened, which was put it in order.

What has arrived on this ground since the seventeenth of the month beginning 2,054, in the order it arrived:

A shape, on the fifteenth of this month, in her own hand, on a board.

A carter, on the eighteenth of this month, saying a name he had never heard, at about four feet off the board.

That is all. That is the whole list. She counted it twice and it is two items long.

And then she worked out where the second item came from, standing in the light with two hundred people carrying stones around her, and it took her about nine seconds and it is the finding of the whole day, and she said it standing, in daylight, at the north end of the rows:

“He made himself findable and the thing that found him found the line I wrote three days ago. It did not find a person. It did not find a ground. It found a line with a name at the top of it and it came here because of that line.”

She said, “And I wrote the line.”

That is the part that stayed with her and it is the part she has not put into a sentence since.

She went east to find out what takes a fifth. She has found out, in four days, that the thing above the line looks for a person where a person has written themselves down, and that she has written herself down on a board at the north end of a ground of two hundred people because nothing in the world would give her a form.

The two facts are the same fact and she made them both happen.

## The One Question

She asked it at the ninth hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,114, standing at the north end of the rows with her back to the board and two hundred people about ten feet apart and a bar lying along the ground at her feet.

She asked him one question. The terms have not moved in nine years: one thing at a time, in daylight, standing.

“What have you done.”

And she got an answer in the ordinary voice, and it was not a long one, and she has gone over it a great many times.

“I took it this morning,” he said. “Before you stood up. I did not decide to and I was not asked and there was nothing to do with it.”

He said, “A thing that cannot be found cannot be sent for. I have been that for nineteen years. You found out about that a long time before I did and you have never once tried it, and I have watched you not try it, and I would rather be found than sent for.”

He said, “That is what it is. I am addressable. A person in a room can be spoken to. And a thing above the line can put a name in a mouth I do not have.”

And then he said the last part, and he said it the way he says everything, which is as a fact and not as an apology, and she had asked for one question and had got one answer and the answer had two halves in it and she has never been able to work out whether that was fair:

“And a record with me in it can be read.”

## What She Did With That

She stood at the north end of the rows and worked it out, in daylight, standing, in about nine seconds, and the working out took her voice because it took her voice in her head too and she cannot help it.

“He has been sendable for nineteen years and addressable not at all, and addressable is what a thing above the line can do with a person, and it has never had him and it has had every other person on nine days of road without asking.”

She said, “And a carter on a made road has just said his name at a board I wrote, in a ground with two hundred people in it, and the thing that put it in that mouth does not put things in mouths by accident. It puts them in mouths and it does not say a thing about the ground it lands in.”

She said, “He has made himself findable. And the first thing that arrived at the finding was a name, and the second thing that is going to arrive is going to arrive faster.”

Then she stopped, because the third thing had arrived while she was saying the second one, which is the thing she was afraid of, and she said it at the ninth hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,114, standing up, in daylight, at the north end of a ground with two hundred people in it:

“It does not care what ground it lands in. It landed here. There are two hundred people here and a board and a woman standing at it and it has not asked any of them whether it may.”

She picked the bar up off the ground in a count of twenty-two and put it on her shoulder, and the strap is not on it and she has to do the twenty-two counts every time, and she has done it about four hundred times in a month.

And the first of the month beginning 2,144 is thirteen days off, and the third first of eight, and there is a board at the north end of this ground with a line in her own hand on it, and it has now been read out loud once by a stranger, and she did not ask him to read it.
