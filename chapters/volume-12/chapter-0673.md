# A Hand And No Reader

She was at the two posts at the sixth hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,054 and again at the sixth hour of the twenty-second and again at the sixth hour of the twenty-third, which is three days in front of a frame with a slate in it, and on the third of those days the man with the board was already there.

## What She Did With Three Days

On the first day she watched a hand. A carter of about fifty came west at the seventh hour, stopped at the frame, took his left hand out of his coat, and wiped the slate from the left corner to the right corner with the flat of his thumb, in the time it takes a man to turn round twice, and put his hand back in his coat and went on west.

That is the whole of a board being maintained and she had never once in nine years of reading boards seen it done, because boards are maintained by the people whose boards they are and she had not had one.

On the second day she did not speak to anybody. She stood four hundred yards from the turn and watched a stone go west and a woman come east with a basket and thirteen people pass the frame, and not one of them looked at it.

On the third day the man was there at the sixth hour and had been there since the fourth, with his hand flat on the top of the frame, the way a man stands at something he is responsible for.

## The Man Whose Job Is The Frame

He is forty-nine. He has come out on the twenty-fourth of a month for nineteen years and he goes out on the twenty-fourth of the next one, and he does not live at this turn and has never once said where he lives.

He looked at her for about ten seconds and then he said, in the ordinary voice, to a girl with a bar on her shoulder standing four feet off:

“You have been stood here three days.”

She said, “I have.”

He said, “You are not a man of this ground.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “You are not paid by this ground and you are not sent by it and you have no business at this frame and everybody on this road can see it.”

She said, “Yes.”

## What She Said Before Anybody Offered It

And then she said the second part out loud, standing, in daylight, four feet from a man with his hand on a frame, before he had said one word about it and before she had asked him a question, which she did afterwards on purpose, and it took her three days to get the ordering right.

She said, “There is a hand on this board and there is not one person west of Lowwater who can read it.”

He said, “I can read it.”

She said, “You are not west of Lowwater, you are on it.”

He said, “That is a thing you have worked out about me in ten seconds and it is right.”

She said, “And there are four hundred people on this ground and not one of them can read what a man writes on a slate, and there has been nothing on this slate for nine years, so no one has found out. On the first of next month there is going to be a name on it and not one of them will be able to read it.”

She said, “So here is the second part and I am saying it now, out loud, standing, before anybody has offered it to me, so that not one person in this county has to be generous to me or has to think about it.”

She said, “I will read it. I will stand at this frame and read whatever is on that slate out loud to anybody who comes along this road, and I will not be paid for it, and I am telling you now so that not one person in this county can say afterwards that I asked for money.”

## The Cost Of Saying It First

He said, “Why are you telling me first.”

She said, “Because if I wait to be asked then I am a person who was offered something and said yes, and I have been offered things nine times in my life and I have said no nine times and I am not going to build a place for myself out of the first one that anybody asks me for.”

He said, “You are nineteen.”

She said, “I am nineteen.”

He said, “Then you have not done a day’s work in your life and I do not know why you are standing at my frame.”

She said, “I have read boards since I was twelve. There is not a false line in this county I could not catch, and I have caught four men in three counties, and I have been paid a rate for it about nine hundred times, and I have not been paid in four years and cannot be, and there is not one hand in this world that could pay me and choose to instead.”

She said, “So there is a hand in this county that can read and a woman who can read and neither can do anything for the other one. That is not a tragedy and it is not an injustice, it is the shape of this ground, and it is the same shape on every ground I have ever stood on.”

He said, “You have just told me you can read and cannot be paid, in that order.”

She said, “Yes.”

He said, “Why in that order.”

She said, “Because the other order is a person asking for something, and I have been a person asking for something for nine years and it has got me a bar and two coppers and a brother who walked west past me.”

## The Three Days Before She Asked Anything

She worked out on the first of the three days that she had no idea what a name looked like in a county hand, and that this was the only gap in nine years of reading. A date has a shape anybody can check. A number has a shape anybody can check. A name is four sounds and a person has to be used to hearing them.

So she sat down in the grit with her back against the left post and she said a name out loud, and she listened to it, and she found that she had been listening to it wrong.

She said, “Ning Yan.”

And then she said it the way she said it to a bar in a yard at the fourth hour, which is slower, and lower, and with the second sound held a little longer because that is what she does when she is alone and she has never had to do it for anybody else.

A boy of sixteen at a rack four miles up a mountain had said *Ning Yan* back to her on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 2,024, once, and got it right the first time, and she had worked out since that a person can do that at sixteen because he had listened to her say it four hundred times.

She said, out loud, in the grit, on the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,054: “That is not a county hand. That is nine years of road.”

## What He Said About The Column

He said, “What are you going to read.”

She said, “Whatever is on it.”

He said, “There is nothing on it.”

She said, “There is going to be something on it on the first of the next month.”

He said, “Is there.”

She said, “There is. I have said that out loud to a woman of sixty on a bench and to two posts and to four people on a road, and I cannot take it back. You are the first person I have said it to who can put a name in a column, and I am telling you I know that.”

He took his hand off the frame.

He said, “A name goes in a column and the days go in beside it and the days are what a person is paid for and the board goes up on the first.”

She said, “Yes.”

He said, “Say the rest of it.”

She said, “The rest of it is that a fifth of a day comes off before the board goes up, and it comes off the days in the column, and it has come off the days in the column of this ground since before I was born, and I have been off this board for nine years and I want to be on it.”

He said, “That is not a thing you want.”

She said, “It is the only thing I have wanted for nine years and the only thing in this world that makes me visible, and I have said both of those out loud to you at the seventh hour of the twenty-third of the month beginning 2,054, in daylight, standing four feet from you. Take it to the woman of sixty this afternoon and she will say I said the whole of it in the same words.”

He said, “You have asked me one question in ten minutes and it was not the question you came here with.”

She said, “You have noticed that.”

He said, “I write four times a month. I notice.”

She said, “Then I will ask the one I came here with. It is one question and I am asking it standing in daylight, and I am not going to ask it twice whatever you say.”

## What He Asked Her Instead

He said, “I am not going to answer that yet.”

She said, “Say why.”

He said, “Because there is one thing I have to put in that column tomorrow and it is not going to be a name and it is not going to be days, and I am not going to get an answer out of a girl of nineteen at the seventh hour in front of a road.”

She said, “Then ask me the thing you have to ask.”

He said, “You said a fifth of a day comes off the days in the column.”

She said, “I did. On the seventeenth of a month four years ago I said it in front of thirteen people and I have said it eleven times since and it is not mine, it is a figure, and it comes off a board before the board goes up, and it is one in twenty.”

He said, “Then you already know what is going to happen to you if I put your name in that column and your days in beside it, and you have told me you want it anyway, and I am going to make you say the number out loud.”

She said, “Say the number.”

He said, “How many days do you want in it.”

She did not answer straight away, and she did not have to do any arithmetic, because she had done it two days ago at the foot of these two posts and she had it.

She said, “Thirty.”

He said, “Thirty days of the month beginning 2,084.”

She said, “Thirty days of the month beginning 2,084, which is the month whose first is seven days after the twenty-fourth.”

He said, “That is thirty days and a fifth of thirty is a day and a half.”

She said, “A day and a half is the unit. It comes off the column and not off me, and I know that, and I said the whole of it out loud at the foot of these two posts on the twentieth to four people going past. The woman of sixty on a bench four hundred yards west will confirm every word of it.”

He said, “You are not frightened.”

She said, “I am frightened and it is not stopping me, and those are two different things and I have had four years to find out that they are two different things.”

He said, “All right.”

She said, “Say it back to me. You have been writing columns for nineteen years and I am not going to have my days in one on the word of a person I have spoken to for ten minutes.”

He said, “A name in one column and the days of a month in the next, and a fifth of a month is not asked about, and the board goes up on the first.”

She said, “Say the part about the fifth again.”

He said, “The fifth comes off the days before it goes up. It comes off the column and not off the person, and the person does not know what is left.”

She stood in the grit at the foot of the two posts and heard the whole of it said back to her in the ordinary voice by a man of forty-nine who has done it since he was thirty. It was exactly what she had worked out. Being told it by somebody who does it four times a month went into her in a way that nine years of road had not managed.

She said, “You have said it back to me exactly, and I have had it for two days off two posts and now I have had it off a man who writes columns four times a month.”

He said, “Then do not make a ceremony of it.”

She said, “I am not. I am putting the date on it. The twenty-third of the month beginning 2,054, at the seventh hour, at this frame, off a man of forty-nine who does not live at this turn.”

He said, “That is the correct way to keep it.”

## The One Question At The End Of Three Days

She said, “Will you put a name in that column tomorrow morning with days beside it.”
