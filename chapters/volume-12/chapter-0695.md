# Three Days Of Not Saying It

She carried a number about inside her for three days in a ground with two hundred people in it and did not say it once, and by the third day the not-saying was a thing she was doing.

The three days were the sixth, the seventh and the eighth of the month beginning 2,144.

## What She Had

What she had was four days and it was not finished.

The span is from the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054, which is day two thousand and seventy-seven, and a fifth of a month comes off the days in a column before a board goes up, and one in twenty is the fraction this county uses on every ground.

Two thousand one hundred and forty-nine less two thousand and seventy-seven is seventy-two, and seventy-two is not the number, and she had it twice and once wrong, and the wrong one had to do with whether the day she got on the slate was counted as the first, and she has never in her life been sure of that and cannot find out, because there is not one person in this county who has ever had a total before and so there is not one person to ask how they counted it.

So she had a figure with a hole in it, and a figure with a hole in it is the most dangerous object she owns, because her hand cannot write a false line and her mouth has no such arrangement.

Here is the whole of what she could do in her head, and she did it standing at the frame on the sixth, out loud, twice.

Day two thousand one hundred and forty-nine less day two thousand and seventy-seven is seventy-two. Seventy-two divided by twenty is three days and fourteen hours and twenty-four minutes.

Counted the other way, with the first day as the first, it is seventy-three days, and seventy-three out of twenty is three days and fifteen hours and thirty-six minutes.

And one of those two answers is exactly one hour and twelve minutes more than the other one, and one hour and twelve minutes is one twentieth of a day.

That is the whole of what is riding on it.

And there is a third figure, which is the one she has been carrying since the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114, and it is five hundred and eighty-nine days, and it is hers in a way the other two are not, because she has been lighter every single day for that long and not one of them has gone back.

Three numbers about herself at one frame, and two of them are one day apart by nothing at all but a convention, and there is not one person in this county to settle the convention.

## The Sixth

She worked on it for the whole of the sixth, standing at the board at the north end with her thumb on the frame and the bar on the ground at her feet, and she got it to three days and fifteen hours and she did not say it to the board either, and she noticed that she had not said it to the board and that was new.

In nine days of road she has said true things to boards, at the fourth hour, in daylight, alone. She has never had anything in her she was keeping off a board.

## The Seventh

On the seventh of the month beginning 2,144 a man of thirty in the fourth row put his barrow down at the second hour and came up to the frame, because he has done that before, twice, and because she is the only person on this ground whose business is a frame.

He said, “You have got a thing you are not saying.”

She said, “I have.”

He said, “Say it.”

And she got four words out.

That is all. Four words, at the second hour, in daylight, in front of a ground of two hundred people, and then she stopped in the middle of them the way a person stops when they have heard themselves.

He waited a count of twenty.

Then she said, “The light is going.”

He said, “It is the second hour.”

She said, “It will be.”

And he went back to his barrow, and forty feet off two hundred people had seen a woman at a frame start a sentence and not finish it, and one of them laughed, once, briefly, the way people do.

## The Shame Of It

She got nine seconds past it and then had to go and stand by herself at the south end, at the end of the last row of stones, with the ground going on behind her and her arm hanging.

She had lied to a man of thirty in the fourth row. Out loud, in daylight, about the light.

And she has never in nine years put a false line in a record of anything, and she has never in nine years said a false thing to a person who has come four feet to her to be spoken to, and she did it in a count of nine seconds because she wanted to stop talking.

Then she thought about what she was doing and it went round and came back.

She was using a false thing to get out of saying a true one. And a false thing in daylight, in a ground of two hundred people, is a thing that is now in two hundred people and not in her.

She came back up the rows at the seventh hour and stood at the board and said it out loud to the frame, to make it exist in a place where it existed:

“I told a man in the fourth row a false thing at the second hour today on purpose, to get out of saying a true one, and he is thirty and he has a barrow and he is not a keeper of anything, and that is the first time in nine years I have put a false thing into a ground.”

She said, “And I am not going to do it again, and I am not going to take it back out of him, and I do not know how you take a thing out of a man of thirty in the fourth row who believed you about the light.”

## The Crack In It

And on the eighth of the month beginning 2,144, at the first hour, at the frame, he said the thing that made those three days what they were.

He said, “You have said less out loud in three days than in any three days since the twenty-first, and I have put down the fact that you have said less and not the reason, because the reason is not mine to take at the first hour.”

She said, “It is not yours to take at any hour. You do not decide anything. It is a record and not a hand.”

He said, “Then I will say the other half of it once, and then I will not say it again. A record of what you said has a hole in it the moment you stop saying things, and you have put that hole in it and not me, and I have known since the twenty-ninth and I have said nothing for three days, because you gave me six terms and one of them is that you decide everything.”

She stood at a frame and looked at him for a count of twenty.

He was right, and she knew he was right in nine seconds, and it is the same hole as the fifth term. She found that one in the record at the ninth hour of the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 2,114 and she did not see the shape of it until the eighth of the month beginning 2,144, because the first one was in a thing that was already broken and this one is in a thing that has not broken yet.

A record that cannot be checked cannot be forced to be complete. And a mouth cannot be cross-examined by a person who is not allowed to ask.

She said, “I am carrying a figure.”

He said, “I know that you are.”

She said, “You do not know that. Not one person knows that. I am telling you that there is a figure and not what it is, and you are the only person I am permitted to say anything to at all, and I have said less to you in four days than I said in one hour on the twenty-first.”

He said, “That is yours.”

## The Question That Cannot Be Asked Again

She stood there and worked out why she had stopped, and it took her about a minute, and the answer was not the number.

It was the question she asked standing up on a floor.

Can a person be announced without being designated. She asked it at the sixth hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 2,054 with a witness she had not asked for, and it cannot be un-asked, and she is not permitted to ask it again, and every single thing she has ever done in public for nine years has had to be run past it.

And here is what she worked out on the seventh of the month beginning 2,144 at the ninth hour, standing, in the dark, at the north end of the rows, which is the plainest thing she has thought in a month:

She has been using the question as a reason.

The number in her is a figure about herself, and a figure about a person in this county is the first half of being found, and she does not know the answer to her question, and so she has been telling herself that she cannot speak until she does.

There is no rule in this county that stops a woman from standing up in a field and saying a number about her own arm.

She can say it. She said no first and she said yes after it, at the third hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,114, standing up, in daylight, in front of two hundred people, and the reason she gave was true, and she is not going to be one of those people who counts the have and lets the have-not go.

## What She Would Say And When

So she decided on the eighth of the month beginning 2,144, at the first hour, standing, in daylight, at the north end of two hundred rows of stones, not to say it until she can say it to the minute.

And that was the first free thing she had done in nine years and it cost her something, because a free decision about a number is a decision not to let two hundred people have it for another day, and she does not know yet what they would do with it, and she has not asked, and she is not going to ask.

She said, “Not until it is right.”

And then she went and found paper to do the sum on, because she is not going to do eighty-one days of counting in her head on an arm that is open four days in five, and there is one piece of paper in this ground and it is nine inches long and it came down off the top of a stair with nothing written on it.
