# A Job Description

She came in at the fourth hour of the thirteenth of the month beginning 2,084 and she came in at the fourth hour of the fourteenth, and she stood at the end of the counter both times with the bar leaning against the outside of the wall in the yard where she had left it, and she spent about nine hours in a room with the shutters up on those two days and she did not sit down once.

## Two Days Of Counting

Here is what she counted on the first day, standing up, in daylight, with a lamp on every table that was worth nothing at that hour.

Tables: twenty-two.

People at them on the two days: about a hundred, and it was between ninety and a hundred and ten both days and she does not pretend to a figure she did not get.

Sheets read on the two days: she did not count these, because the room does not carry a sheet back to a table, it carries it to a shelf, and a woman at the far end takes them off a table and puts them on a shelf and a shelf goes out at the fourth hour and a shelf comes back at the sixth, and there is no way to see a number.

How many times a person was called: twice in two days. Both times at the seventh hour. Both times it was the same man at the second table from the door, and both times it was one word and a hand across the room, and both times a woman got up and went and came back.

She has read that line on the sheet two years running. Standing. If you are in a room and a thing cannot be read, you are a person they can call, and a reader is not to be called twice in a day.

She watched it happen and it is exactly what it says on the paper, and she wrote nothing down, and she did not have to.

## Where Four Hundred And Seven Came From

She asked four people where the readers in that room come from.

She did not ask the question as a question. She asked it the way she has asked everything since she was nine years old, standing up, in daylight, one thing at a time, and not two, and never two.

The man with the brush was about fifty and had been in the room nineteen years. She said, “Where did you come from.” He said, “A village up the north road. I was nineteen.” Then he squared a sheet and went back to it, and that was the whole of that conversation.

The young reader at the desk on the far side of the counter is thirty now and was twenty-six when she was here last, and he read at a desk from the far side of a counter, about a person who is never in the room. He said, “My mother’s village. I was nineteen and I could read and there was a woman in it and she had a sheet.”

She said, “Was there another way.”

He said, “There was no other way. I did not know there was a reading and then I knew there was a reading. There was not a stretch of anything in between.”

The third was a woman of about forty at the ninth table who had been in nine years. She said, “Nineteen.” And then, because she is a person who says the rest of it when it is asked once, she said, “And I had not thought about it before the woman said it, and afterwards I thought about it for about a year and then I stopped, and it is not a bad life and I am not telling you it is a good one.”

And the fourth was the woman of sixty-three at the end of the counter, and she said one sentence and would not say another on it.

“I was nineteen in a village and there was a sheet, and that is how four hundred and seven of us got here, and it has never once been anything else.”

## What She Works Out On The Second Day

She worked it out standing at the end of the counter at the fourth hour of the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,084 and it took her the length of four sheets being squared, and what she worked out was this and she said it once, to the boards, in daylight, in an empty part of a room with a hundred people in it.

She had been treating that sheet as an offer for two years.

An offer is a thing that is put in front of a person in order to be taken. A description of a job is a thing that says what the work is, in the words of the work, so that the person reading it knows whether they are the kind of person the work is done by.

It is not asking her for anything.

The first line is not a promise made to her. *A paper that says a person whose reading has been confirmed in this room by another person in this room is a person whose reading may be relied on inside a set of walls.* That is not about her. That is a definition of the word recruited, and it is written the way you write down what a rope is when you want a person to be able to tell whether they can hold one.

The second line is a rate. The third is a room. The fourth is the word standing, which is not a favour and not a debt, and the woman of sixty-three said it and it is true and it is a rule about how many times in a day a person may be made to stop what they are doing for someone else.

None of it is asking her to be a person.

It is asking her to be a pair of hands that can be found.

## What She Did About The Fourth Line

She tried to be called.

The fourth line is standing. If you are in a room and a thing cannot be read, you are a person they can call.

She stood at the counter at the fourth hour of the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,084 for the length of four sheets being squared and she put her hands on the wood in front of her, the way a person waits at a counter, and she waited.

On the two days she was in that building and she was in it from the fourth hour to the ninth, and a person was called twice on each of them, and both times it was one word and a hand across the room, and neither of the two women who got up was her.

She worked it out at the fifth hour and said it out loud, standing, in daylight, to the boards:

“The call is a rule about the book. It is not a rule about the room. You cannot call a person who is not in it, and I have proved that twice in nine hours, standing up, with my hands on the counter, and the man with the brush has not looked at me once.”

And that is the fourth of the six lines, and the fourth of the six lines is the shortest one on the sheet, and it is the only one of the five that has ever cost her anything, and it turns out to be the same as the first one, because both of them are about being in a book, and there is no way of being in a book that does not go through the fifth line.

## The First Line

And then she read the first line properly, for the first time in two years, standing up, at the fourth hour of the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,084.

*Confirmed in this room by another person in this room.*

She stood still for a count of nine.

To confirm a reading you have to be able to read it. That is not a rule anybody in that room had to make. It is what the word means.

There are a hundred people in that room and a rate board on the wall behind the counter with three lines on it, and the third line is *a hand nobody in this building can read*, at twice the first rate, and it has been on that board since before she was born, and it is a real line on a real board, and there is not one person in that building who can do it.

She has been saying for two years that she is the only person on nine days of road who can read a hand the people around her cannot.

There is not one person in that building who can read hers.

Not one. Twenty-two tables and a hundred people and forty-four years of readers and there is not a hand in that room that can stand behind her and look at a page she has read and say *yes, that is what it says*, because confirming it means reading it and reading it means being able to do the thing that is being confirmed.

## What She Has Been Refusing

So she stood at the end of a counter at the fourth hour of the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,084 and worked out the whole of two years in the length of nine counts, and here is what it came to, and she is not going to soften it because she has been wrong and she has been wrong in the most expensive way a person can be wrong, which is by being right.

She could not have been recruited.

Not on the fourteenth. Not at the fourth hour of the third of a month two years and two months ago with the sheet in front of her. Not on the twelfth of this month with a figure in her hand about being nineteen. Any day at all.

The first line has said it since the day it was written and every person in that room for forty-four years has known it without knowing that they knew it, because a hand that cannot be read is a hand that cannot be confirmed, and a hand that cannot be confirmed is a hand that never goes in a book, and there has never been a question put to her in that building in two years because there has never been a possibility of one.

And she refused twice. Out loud. Standing. In daylight. With a man writing.

She said it out loud into a room with the shutters up, at the fourth hour of the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,084:

“For two years I have been standing in this room refusing something that was never on the table, and I have been doing it with a hand in front of me, and I want it understood that I did not know, and that knowing now has not made it into a different thing, and that a man wrote two lines in a book about a refusal that could not have happened and both of those lines are going to go up a stair.”

She stood there for a while with her hand flat on the wood of the counter, with four fingers and a thumb, and did not take it away this time.

And then she said the part that is the finding, and she said it to an empty room with a hundred people in it:

“So there is a trade in here that a hand like mine is not allowed to do, and I have been refusing it, and I would have gone on refusing it for the rest of my life without finding out, and the only reason I have found out is that I came east to find out what takes a fifth of a board.”
