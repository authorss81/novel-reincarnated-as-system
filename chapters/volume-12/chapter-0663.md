# Where A Bundle Goes

They went down four flights at the eighth hour of the tenth of the month beginning 2,054 with the boy carrying the empty can in one hand and his other hand on the rail, and she went down beside him and counted his steps because her own are counted whether she wants them or not.

She was at seven on the third landing and at four on the flat below it and back to four on the second, and she let it go the way she had learned to let it go at fourteen, which is to stop arguing with the number once the number has been established.

The yard at the bottom of the hill is thirty feet across and has a well in it and a stack of broken pallets and the cart, and the boy sat down on the pallet stack with his back to the tower wall because he had clearly decided where he was going to say it and had chosen it nine days ago.

## What The Boy Said On The Ninth

He said, “The carter came up on the ninth. He has worked this road thirty-one years and he came up on his own time, and he stood at the bottom of the stair with his hat in his hand and he said it once and then he went back down.”

She said, “He said it to you.”

He said, “He said it to me because I asked him on the first.”

She said, “Say the asking.”

He said, “I said, where does it go when it goes up.”

She said, “And what does he do with a question like that.”

He said, “He looked at the stair for a while. And then he said, that is the correct question, and I have never had it asked of me, and then he told me.”

She sat down on the bottom step of the tower, on the stone, with her elbows on her knees, because she has learned that a person who is about to say a thing that has been sitting on him for nine days will say it better to somebody who is lower than he is.

## Eight Bundles And A Door

The boy said, “He said he has carried four bundles up that stair in the thirty-one years he has worked the road, and he can remember all four because a bundle is not a heavy thing and it is not a dull thing, it is a square of oilcloth with a weight in the middle of it, and he could tell anybody what was in each one.”

She said, “Say what he said about them.”

He said, “He said a bundle is not county paper. He said he has carried county paper down that hill two hundred times and it goes down to the county seat and it is read by four clerks and it is put in a shelf, and he said, and this is the part I have not been able to put down, he said, that is not what this stair is for.”

She said, “What is it for.”

He said, “He said a bundle is what a thing is put in when it is going to be said. Not read. Said. He said there is a difference and he has been carrying both for thirty-one years and the ones that come down are the ones that get read, and the ones that go up are the ones that get said.”

She said, “To whom.”

He said, “That is what I asked him and he said he does not know, and that he has never been told, and that he has worked out over thirty-one years that the answer to that question is not written on a sheet anywhere because the man who would know it does not write it down.”

## The Door Above The Floor

She said, “Where does it go up to.”

He said, “A door on the landing above the floor. Low. With bars in the top half of it. And he said he has carried four through it and put them down where he was told to put them down, and he said that on each of the four occasions a man came out of that door and took his hat off and said something to him that made him stand there, and he said that on none of the four occasions did he see one inch past the man into the room.”

She said, “What year was the last one.”

He said, “Nine years.”

The yard was quiet for a while after that. The well rope moved a little in a wind that was not doing anything to anything else.

She said, “Nine years is eight quarters.”

He said, “It is four bundles and not eight and I asked him the same and he said he has carried four and it is not every quarter and he does not know why it is not every quarter, and that this has been the shape of his working life and he has never once been told.”

She said, “And his hat came off four times.”

He said, “Four times.”

She said, “You said that out loud.”

He said, “I did not mean anything by it.”

She said, “You meant it exactly. His hat came off four times on a stair that goes up out of a room and he has carried for thirty-one years and not one person in that time ever asked him where it went. That is the whole of what he wanted to tell me and he has been carrying it down the hill with him since the ninth.”

The boy said, “You are angry.”

She said, “I am angry at a carter who has been correct for thirty-one years.”

She said, “He did the only thing there was, which was to carry what he was given up a hill and to hold the door, and he did it well, and four men said something kind to him for it, and not one of them ever asked him what the door was for. And now he is sixty-four and he comes up a hill on his own time to tell a boy of sixteen the answer to the correct question.”

## Nine Days With It In His Mouth

She said, “You have had that for nine days.”

He said, “Since the first.”

She said, “Why the first and not the second or the sixth.”

He said, “Because a bundle goes up on the first. And I asked him on the morning of the first, before it went, and I have been in this building nine days with it and there has not been one person to hand it to except a woman who walked in off the hill yesterday with nine days of road on her.”

She said, “You could have written it on a leaf.”

He said, “There is no paper in this building that is mine to write on, and I did not ask him for any, and if I had asked him for a leaf then the first thing in it would have been his name and I was not going to do that.”

She looked at the boy for a moment. He was sixteen and he had a can of oil under a lamp that ought not to be burning and a nine-day answer and he had not once put it on paper, and the reason he gave was not modesty and it was not loyalty, it was that he understood something about writing things down that she has understood for ten years and has been alone with.

She said, “What did you think it was for.”

He said, “I thought it was for you.”

She said, “It is not for me.”

He said, “Then it was worth the nine days anyway, because now you know it.”

## One Fact And Not Two

She got up off the step and stood in the yard and looked up at the tower, four miles up which is nothing from here and four flights of which is everything she has got.

Then she said the thing out loud, in a yard, in daylight, to a boy of sixteen sitting on a stack of broken pallets.

“For nine years a bundle has gone up four flights in that building and gone through a low door on the landing above the floor, and a man has come out of the door and taken his hat off, and the bundle was going to be said to somebody. And for nine years there has not been a bundle, and there has not been a man coming out of the door, and there is a boy of sixteen on the fourth floor copying announcements that are going to be said into a building with one person in it.”

He said, “There was you.”

She said, “Say that again.”

He said, “You were in the building twice. And you read four sheets out loud on the twenty-eighth and I was in the room and you had not asked me to be.”

She said, “And after that?”

He said, “After that you went down the stair and west and I have not seen anybody in this building since.”

She said, “Then there is no one in this building.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “And a thing that is going to be said, goes where there is somebody to say it to. And if there is not one person in the room, it goes to the ground, and it goes to the ground without anybody knowing that it did, and there is not one person on any ground who has been told their own total.”

He said, “You have been up nine days of road to work that out and I worked it out in four days.”

She said, “You are right and that is the second time in ten years that a person has got there before me and both times he was sixteen.”

He said, “What was the first time.”

She said, “A clerk of sixty-seven, at a bench, in four days, with a brush in his hand.”

The cart came up the track from below at the ninth hour, slow, with the shafts up, and the carter came past the bottom of the yard without stopping, and he put two fingers up off the rein to a boy of sixteen and looked at her for about a second and a half.

He said, as he went by, “It goes up.”

She said, “I know it does.”

He said, “Not one person has ever come down for it.”

He did not stop. The cart went up the track and round the side of the hill and there was nothing on it but two sacks and a length of pipe.

She stood in the yard of a tower four miles up a mountain and listened to a carter of sixty-four who had gone up a hill on his own time that morning to hand a boy of sixteen the only sentence anybody in thirty-one years had wanted, and had gone away down the hill again with not one thing for it.
