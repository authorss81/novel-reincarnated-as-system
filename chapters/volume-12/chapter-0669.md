# The Instrument In Her Coat

She left the book of eight leaves on the bench at the sixth hour of the seventeenth of the month beginning 2,054 and did not open it for half an hour, which is the longest she has gone in ten years without opening it.

The lamp in the cage was burning. The pile on the floor was lower by nine sheets. The drawer under the shelves was locked and had four sheets in it and the key was in the front of her bag. The boy was at the rack and the bench was clear and the window was open nine inches because it had been open on the twelfth when she came up with two days of road on her.

## The Book On The Bench

It is a book of eight leaves made out of a blanket, and it is about the size of two hands together, and the thread that holds it is a piece of the bar’s own strap.

The first leaf has a date on it and a place above the date. The second has four sentences on it that she said once into the air when she was a girl of eight and has not repeated since and is not going to repeat here. The third has four capital strokes on it that are the worst four strokes in this world and are hers.

The fifth, the sixth, the seventh and the eighth are blank.

She had said once, on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 2,024, at a bench in this room with a boy of sixteen four feet away, that she was not going to write on them. She had said it twice since. She said it here a third time and then she went and worked out whether she still meant it.

She meant it. And the reason she still meant it was not the reason she had given at the bench, which was that one leaf of eight was enough, and that was true, and it was not the whole of it.

The whole of it is that she does not know what an announcement looks like.

## Three Things In Her Hand And None Of Them Is The Thing

She laid them out on the bench, one at a time, the way she lays everything out, from left to right, in the order she would use them.

A stub of a nib. A cake of ink she opened on the seventh of the month beginning 1,874 and has never let dry and has carried through nine years and three places that had walls. And a hand.

She said, out loud, to the bench, at the seventh hour of the seventeenth of the month beginning 2,054:

“That is the whole of the instrument. A piece of a pen, a bit of ink, and three fingers and a thumb on the right hand with the index gone, and the arm is open today and it is four days in five.”

She said, “And it is all of it in her coat, and none of it is in this building.”

The boy said, “You could ask me to write.”

She said, “I know. You have been waiting nine days for me to ask you to do something and I have watched you do it.”

He said, “I have been in this room two years and the only hand in it that has ever written a county line is his and it stopped on the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,024, and I would write it well. I have had two years to learn what a county hand looks like from copying it and I am better at it than you are.”

She said, “You are. That is not the reason.”

He said, “Then give me the reason and I will hold it.”

## The Reason A County Hand Is Not The Hand

She said, “Because a thing that is going to be said to a person is said by somebody. And if you write it, then the boy of sixteen on the fourth floor of a tower in Lowwater has said it, and there will be one of him in it for as long as the paper is in a room, and a boy of sixteen is the last thing in this world that ought to be in a thing like that.”

He said, “I am going to be seventeen in a month.”

She said, “I know. That is the reason and it is not a good one and it is true.”

She said, “And here is the other one, and it is the real one, and I would rather say it to you than find it out later. If you write it, then I will have got somebody else to do the one thing in this life that I have not been able to get anybody else to do, and I have been alone on every one of them since I was twelve.”

She said, “I am not going to spend that on a piece of paper. There is not enough of it left.”

## The Rule About A False Line

Then she said the other thing, out loud, at the bench, in daylight, with the nib and the ink lying on it and neither of them touched.

She said, “There is a rule on this hand and I have never once been able to get round it in ten years and I am going to say it out loud in this room because in two days I am going to have to use it.”

She said, “This hand cannot write a line that is not true. Not a lie. A line that is not true.”

He said, “Say the difference.”

She said, “A lie is when the writer knows. This is not that. This is when a thing comes to the end of the hand that is not what the hand would have put down, and the hand stops, and there is a place on the paper where a line ought to be and there is not one on it.”

She said, “I have done it twice. Both times in my own book. Both times I rubbed it out afterwards and both times I could not make the paper be as though it had not been there.”

She said, “And I have never once tried to write something in order to find out what it would do, because I do not want to know whether I can, and because the day I find out I will not be able to stop finding out.”

The boy said, “That is a strange thing to have and a strange thing to tell a person about.”

She said, “I have had it since I was ten and there is one person in the world who could do something about it and he is three feet from the back wall of this building, and so I have said it in daylight to a boy of sixteen instead, and he is the only person it has ever been said to in daylight with his hands in front of him.”

## What The Boy Does With An Answer He Was Not Asked For

He said, “Then I will not ask you.”

She said, “You have not asked me anything.”

He said, “I have been not asking you things for nine days and I have got very good at it and I would like you to know that I got good at it because you taught me how on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 2,024 and you did not mean to.”

She said, “I did not know I had taught you anything.”

He said, “You told me four things in an order. You did not have to give them to me in an order. There was no reason on that bench for an order.”

She said, “There was a reason. I was afraid I would lose one.”

He said, “That is the reason. I lost one, the fourth one, for about a day and a half, and I had it in my own head afterwards because you said it out loud to me, and I have not lost a thing since, and I do not know what to do with that.”

She sat at the end of the bench and let a boy of sixteen tell her what her own method had done to him, and she did not say that it had been done to her first and for longer, and she did not say that she had lost the sum of six men’s days that morning in a tower four miles up a mountain with a stack of nine announcements in front of her.

She said, “You have your own column.”

He said, “I have my own column.”

She said, “Then the two of us are the only two people in nine days of road who keep a count of ourselves in a hand that is ours, and neither of us has ever been told what the other one has.”

He said, “Will you tell me.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “Then I will not tell you.”

She said, “That is correct and I am not going to say why, and you are not going to ask me why, and that is the whole of what we have got.”

He said, “It is a small thing to have.”

She said, “It is the first thing I have had with another person in eight years that has not been about the past.”

## Five Blank And Going West

She put the nib back in the bag and the ink back beside it and shut the book and put it against her ribs on the side where four sheets of a fair hand had lain for a fortnight and had not lain for four.

She was lighter at the eighth hour of the seventeenth of the month beginning 2,054 than she was at the sixth hour of the day before, which is five hundred and ten days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back.

She said, at the eighth hour, to the shelves and the locked drawer and the boy at the rack:

“Everything I own is in this coat and on this shoulder and it all walks. There is not one thing in this world that I have that will still be mine if I stand still, and I have been told for four years that I cannot keep a place by staying in it, and I believed that about places, and I did not know until this morning that it went on things as well.”

She said, “And the instrument that can put an announcement into a room is a bit of a pen and a bit of ink and a hand with three fingers on it, and it is going to have to be my hand, because it cannot be his and it cannot be the boy’s and there is no third pair of hands in nine days of road.”

She said, “So that is settled and it cost me four days and a locked drawer to settle, and now I have thirteen days of road in front of me with an unwritten book and a question that has two halves and one of them is in a room with a table in it and the other half is somewhere I have not been.”

He said, “You are going down to the turn.”

She said, “I am going down to the turn.”

He said, “Why.”

She said, “Because I have been standing on a store road for thirteen days not knowing what a board is for, and I have spent a day finding out that the thing I was looking for is a table in an empty room, and there is one more thing before I go back up anything, and it is a bar on two posts four hundred yards east of a turn with nothing written on it.”
