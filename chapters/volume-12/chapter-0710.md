# The Fourth Time Past The Bench

The woman of sixty took the rest of that night about it and answered her at the second hour of the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 2,234, standing up, in daylight, one question, in the ordinary voice, and she took her time about it the way she takes her time about everything.

She said, “Yes.”

She said, “I was on that board for four years and my name came off it and there was not one person to tell and the man with the chalk did not say anything and he has not said anything about it in five years.”

She said, “And I have sat on this bench nine years and I have watched about forty people go on that board since and about thirty come off, and I have never once known which was which.”

## The Half Of It She Was Not Going To Say

And Ning Yan stood there and found that she was about to say a thing, and that she had nine words of it ready and the first one was a date.

She did not say it.

She stood four feet off a bench in the grit with the bar on her left shoulder and a light going off the frame behind her and she worked out in about nine seconds why she was not going to say it, and then she worked out the other half of why, which took longer.

The first half is standing. The second half is that a woman who has been on a board and come off it would ask her what else is on it, and there are three hundred and one names in that column, and Ning Yan is not entitled to read three hundred and one names out loud in a turn to a woman with a bench, and she has not got one reason that would survive being said out loud.

And the third thing, which is not a reason and which she said to herself in the ordinary voice because she does not let anything stand that she has not put into words where it exists: she is going to write a line on a board in seven days with a date and a place on it, and anybody who walks up to that frame on a good day will be able to read it.

So the whole of a thing about a person in this county is going to be on a stick in public for the first time in eleven hundred years.

And she is carrying the name of a woman on a bench in her head where it cannot be reached.

## What She Carries

She worked the rest of it out over two days, standing, in daylight, at the frame and on the flat two hundred yards south of it where there is nothing to sit on.

A name in the fourth column of that slate is a name that came off that board. That is the whole finding and she had it at the first hour of the fourth first of a month two days of road west of here and it has not changed.

There are three hundred and one of them, counted twice. The man of forty-nine does four a month and has done it for nineteen years, so there have been nine hundred and twelve names on that board since he put it there.

Three hundred and one out of nine hundred and twelve is very nearly a third, and not a fifth, and not a tenth.

She does not know what a third of a name is. She has known that since the first of a month beginning 2,174 and she has not worked it out, and she has not asked, and she is not going to ask, and she said so out loud on the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 2,234 to a gatepost that has no opinion.

## What It Would Cost The Woman

And then she did the thing she does with a problem, which is that she worked out what it would cost to be told.

A woman of sixty has sat on a bench for nine years and has not asked anybody for anything. She told a stranger with a bar that the light comes up on a frame last, and that was nine years of sitting there with nothing to say to anybody who passed, and she gave it away for nothing, twice, to two people.

If Ning Yan tells her she is in a column on the other side of a slate, then the woman of sixty knows.

And a woman who knows that there is a third of a column of names she has been part of for nineteen years is a woman who will think about the other three hundred.

And there is one person in nine days of road who can read that column and there is one person in nine days of road who is walking away from a bench with a bar on her shoulder.

She said, out loud, on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 2,234, standing in the grit at the ninth hour with the light going:

“I would be handing one woman a thing that only I can carry and that I cannot put down, and she would have to carry it too, and she is sixty, and she has carried a bench for nine years and she is not mine to add a column to.”

She said, “And that is the correct decision and I have made it and I am not going to take it back, and I want it said out loud here that I made it on purpose and that it cost me something and that the something is going to be still there in nine years.”

## Four Days Of Carrying It

And she carried it for four days, which is not a long time, and she has been carrying things for nineteen years and has never once tried the weight of one against a day.

On the twenty-fifth she counted the posts again because there are two of them and counting things she already knows is the only cheap way she has of not counting the other thing.

On the twenty-sixth she went down to the mill four hundred yards west at the stopped lime bed and stood in a yard with a woman who has been standing there with her hands behind her since before she was nine, and said nothing at all for about a count of sixty, and the woman did not look up, and it was the fourth time in nine years she has gone and stood in that yard and been given nothing at all.

On the twenty-seventh she went east nine miles out of thirteen to the post at the end of the paid road and stood at it, and there has been no road under her feet for four hundred yards from that post in one direction, and she said out loud, to a post, that she had come to a county where a third of the names on a board are names that came off it and she was the only person in it who knew, and the post did not care.

On the twenty-eighth she stayed at the frame.

That is the whole of what she did with it and she got nowhere with any of it, and she said so, standing, at the ninth hour, in daylight, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “It does not get lighter and it does not get heavier and it does not come out in a number I can put against something else, and it is the only thing I have ever carried that has no figure on it anywhere in the world, and I have been carrying it since the first hour of the month beginning 2,174 and I am not putting it down.”

## Four Times

And on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 2,234 she walked past the bench and the woman of sixty was on it and she did not get up and she did not say a word, and that is the fourth time in nine years that Ning Yan has walked past that bench knowing a thing about the woman sitting on it.

The first time she stopped and said one true sentence about herself for nothing.

The second time she did not stop.

The third time she stood nine feet off for a count of twenty.

And this time she walked past at four miles an hour with a bar nine feet nine on her left shoulder and an arm open four days in five, and she was lighter for seven hundred and one days, and she did not say a word, and she is not going to.

She did not say a word because she has something to say to that woman that would change the shape of her next nine years, and she has got no standing to say it, and there is not one other person in this county she could say it to who would be able to act on it, and a thing that cannot be acted on is not information, it is weight, and she has already got enough of her own.

## What She Is Carrying Into The First

And that night, in a field four hundred yards south of the turn with the bar across her and the rain coming through the skin she has not used since the month beginning 2,084, she worked out the other half of the thing, which is the half that has a first of a month in it.

In thirty-three days she is going to write a figure on a slate at that frame in her own hand and it is going to go up a stair.

And there is a name in a column four feet off where she is going to write it, and the woman it belongs to came off that board five years ago, and that name is going to stay exactly where it is.

She said, out loud, lying on her back in the wet, out loud, to the grass, which is the ordinary way she says the things that cost:

“In thirty-three days I am going to put one true thing about myself where any person in this county can read it, and I am going to be allowed to, because it is mine and it is a figure and it is not a name.”

She said, “And four hundred yards from where I am going to do it there is a woman who has got a thing about herself that I know and she cannot and I am not going to be able to put it anywhere for her, and on the morning I do it I am going to be doing the easy half of a thing I have not done for anybody at all.”

She said, “And I have not worked out which of the two is worse and I have got four days in which to work it out.”

And she lay there and counted the first hour of the first of the month beginning 2,264 to the first hour of the first of the month beginning 2,294 and it is thirty days, and she is going to spend one of them standing in a gravel turn in the rain watching a board go up with nothing of hers on it that she did not put there.
