# The Line Between Two Numbers

She found it on the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,084 at about the sixth hour, standing at the north end of a room with the shutters up, and it took her two days to work out and it has taken her the rest of her life to be sure she had it right.

## The Sheet At The North End Of The Room

It is nailed to the wall at the end of the counter, over the shelves the folders go on, at the height of a person’s eyes, in a plain upright hand, and it is a keeper’s sheet, and anybody who comes down that lane can read it in the time it takes to walk past it.

At the top, in the middle, in a larger hand:

The twelve months ending on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 2,084.

Under that, a total.

Ninety-eight thousand coppers.

Under that, seven lines, in this order, with a figure against each of them.

Paper, quills and ink, four thousand five hundred and sixteen.

Lamp oil and wick, one thousand six hundred and eighty.

Firewood for the stove at the back, two thousand four hundred.

Food, for the room, three thousand five hundred and sixty.

The counter and the four doors, three hundred and sixty.

The rate, at two coppers the page, eighty-one thousand nine hundred and sixty.

The rate, at four coppers the page, six hundred and four.

Three rooms, furnished, nothing at all.

She read it standing up at six feet off it at the sixth hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,084 with a hundred people reading under it at tables with a lamp on every one of them, and the sun was on the wall through the shutters, and there is not one word of it that is not a plain hand on a plain wall.

She had walked past it twice.

## The Line That Is Hers

She went straight to the second-from-last line because of the four words printed beside it, and those four words are the whole trade.

*The rate, at four coppers the page. A hand not one reader in this building can make out.*

That is the third rate off the board behind the counter. Two coppers a page. A second rate by the difficulty of the hand. A third rate at twice the first, for a hand not one reader in this room can match.

Six hundred and four coppers.

She stood under that line at the sixth hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,084 and did the only division that mattered, standing up, in daylight, out loud, at the end of a counter, to an empty stretch of floor:

“Six hundred and four at four coppers a page is one hundred and fifty-one pages. One hundred and fifty-one pages in twelve months.”

She said, “That is about a page every two and a half days, to the one hand in this building that cannot be read in it.”

She said, “And I have not read one of the hundred and fifty-one in two years, and I am not going to read one, and that line is a figure about a kind of person, and the kind of person is standing under it with her hand open.”

## Seven Lines, Four Ways

She added the seven lines four times.

The first time she did it straight down, in order, without stopping, and it took her about nine seconds. The second time she did it backwards. The third time she did it in pairs and then added the pairs. The fourth time she did it with the three rooms line left out entirely, on the argument that a line with nothing in it is not a line.

All four came to ninety-five thousand and eighty.

She did not believe the fourth one and did a fifth, which was to subtract each line in turn from the total on the wall.

Ninety-eight thousand less four thousand five hundred and sixteen is ninety-three thousand four hundred and eighty-four.

Less one thousand six hundred and eighty is ninety-one thousand eight hundred and four.

Less two thousand four hundred is eighty-nine thousand four hundred and four.

Less three thousand five hundred and sixty is eighty-five thousand eight hundred and forty-four.

Less three hundred and sixty is eighty-five thousand four hundred and eighty-four.

Less eighty-one thousand nine hundred and sixty is three thousand five hundred and twenty-four.

Less six hundred and four is two thousand nine hundred and twenty.

She stood in front of that wall at the seventh hour and looked at a total and a set of lines and worked out that they do not make the same number.

Ninety-eight thousand.

Ninety-five thousand and eighty.

Two thousand nine hundred and twenty.

## What Two Thousand Nine Hundred And Twenty Is Not

Then she spent the rest of the day on that figure, standing in the room and sitting on the end of the counter with her feet on the boards, and this is everything she ruled out, and she ruled each one out against a sheet or a person and not in her head.

It did not come off a page.

Eighty-one thousand nine hundred and sixty at two coppers is forty thousand nine hundred and eighty pages. Six hundred and four at four coppers is a hundred and fifty-one pages.

Forty-one thousand one hundred and thirty-one pages in twelve months across about a hundred readers is about a hundred and thirteen pages a day for this room and rather more than a page a reader a day, and she does not know whether a page is a fair day’s work for a hand being careful, because she has never in her life done one page as a day’s work for anybody.

Both rate lines come out in whole pages. If a copper had come off the end of a line, or off the end of a page, or off the end of a reader, one of those two figures would not divide. Both of them divide.

Not one reader in that room was paid short.

It is not a fraction.

She had been looking for a fifth all the way east and she looked for one here and there was not one. Ninety-eight thousand divided by five is nineteen thousand six hundred, and two thousand nine hundred and twenty is not nineteen thousand six hundred.

She said that out loud, standing, at the eighth hour, to a wall: “The levy is not in this building and has never been in this building. Whatever takes a fifth of a board takes it somewhere else.”

It is not spread.

The rate is paid on the seventh, the seventeenth and the twenty-seventh. That is thirty-six times in twelve months. Two thousand nine hundred and twenty divided by thirty-six is eighty-one and four thirty-sixths. A thing taken off every payday comes out even. This does not.

So it did not come off the rate at all, and it did not come off evenly, and it fell somewhere, once, or a few times, or in one month.

## What Two Thousand Nine Hundred And Twenty Is

Eight coppers a day.

Three hundred and sixty-five days of a year, and eight coppers on every one of them, and that is the whole of the figure and there is nothing clever about it.

And this is the sentence she said out loud at the ninth hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,084, standing under a sheet nailed to a wall at the height of a person’s eyes, in daylight, in a room with a hundred people in it:

“A loaf is two coppers on nine days of road. There is somebody in this county who has had bread for a year out of this building, and there is no line on that wall with his name against it, and there is no hand in this room that could tell me who it is.”

And then she said the other half of it, out loud, standing, in daylight:

“Forty-five years that wall has had a total on it and a set of lines under the total, and a hundred people have worked under it every day of it, and not one of them has ever added the lines to the total, because adding them is not a job, and because a person does not add up the room they are standing in.”

## The Twenty-Fourth Of This Month

And then the twenty-fourth came on its own.

It came on the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,084, which is day two thousand one hundred and seven, three days after she found the number. She was in a building with the shutters up, sixteen miles east of a turn, reading a sheet on a wall.

At the sixth hour of that day a man of forty-nine came out on the store road from somewhere he does not live and stopped at a frame in four hundred yards of grit and put a number of days in a column against a stroke, and then put thirty days of the month beginning 2,114 in chalk against the name in it, which is Ning Yan.

She was sixteen miles east and she had been on that board for two months and she missed the day it was taken in.

That is the first time in nine years she has missed a day of a column and she has never missed one, and there is no consequence available to her at all, because a man of forty-nine does not know where she is and could not find out and has never once asked, and the whole of what a board is, is that it does not need to know.

## Six Days Carrying It

She carried it for six days, from the twenty-first to the twenty-seventh.

Past a hundred people a day. Past a man of sixty-five with a book. Past a man with a brush. Past a woman of sixty-three who has not got smaller. Past a man of thirty at a desk, reading at a desk, about a person who is never in the room.

She did not say it.

Not to protect it. The second time she kept a figure in her head in daylight, at the north end of a ground with two hundred people in it, she kept it because of a question she asked standing up on a floor with a boy of sixteen in it. This one was a different figure about a different thing.

The reason she did not say it was simpler and worse than that.

She was the only person in that room who could do it.

On the twenty-second she counted the bodies doing addition under a lamp at four different tables in one hour — four of them, every one of them working with other people’s figures all day long, not one of them their own. Forty-five years of this room and forty-five years of that wall and four bodies under a lamp every hour of it, and not one of them has ever once added the lines to the total.

She had been doing that arithmetic in her head, on a bar, since she was a child. Every day of her life. With no hand to put it in.

Two thousand nine hundred and twenty coppers.

It was on the thirtieth of the month beginning 2,084 that she walked out of that building with the number still in her head, and it went with her, and it is the first figure of her own she has ever been standing in a place where she could say it out loud.
