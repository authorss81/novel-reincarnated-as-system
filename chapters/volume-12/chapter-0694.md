# A Shape Is A Thing Kept

There is no floor in this place and there is nothing to put a hand on and he checks both of those before he lets anything arrive, because he has been in one of these eight times and he has never once been in a hurry.

There is no light that comes from anywhere and there is no shadow. That is not new and it is not what he came for.

What is in it this time is a fortnight.

## The Days He Reads

On the twenty-second of the month beginning 2,114 a carter of fifty could not read a line in a woman’s own hand at the north end of a ground of two hundred people and said a name out loud twice and did not know that he had said it.

On the first of the month beginning 2,144 a cart came east off a turn two days of road west, having put a slate on another cart at the first hour, and it stopped at the north end of that ground and a man put down a stone and off the stone a strip of paper and kept his hand out until she had taken it.

On the second, the third and the fourth of that month she took a strip of blank paper apart with a thumb and the daylight and came to the end of what could be known about it without asking anybody.

And on the twenty-eighth, the twenty-ninth and the thirtieth of the month beginning 2,114 she told him a thing that was not true on purpose, in daylight, standing, in front of two hundred people, and he wrote it down, because it was what she said.

That is the fortnight. He reads it the way he has always read it, which is from the outside and in order.

## What He Is Looking For

He has one question and it is not the one he had the last four times.

He is not asking whether a fragment can be known without being spent by the knowing. He settled that years ago in a place with no floor in it and the answer was that a fragment is not an entry and an entry cannot be lost, only filed.

He is asking what it costs to keep one.

Because on the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,114 a thing happened to him in a ground with two hundred people in it and not one person spoke to him, and what it was is that he can hold a shape. Not a body. A shape. A person can be spoken to, and a mouth he does not have can be given his name, and that is not a finding, that is a piece of furniture.

The finding is that a shape is not held. A shape is kept.

And he has been nineteen years old in the world and has never kept one, and he worked out in nine seconds on the first of the month beginning 2,144 why that is: because the only things that have ever been kept in this county have been kept by people who were paid to keep them, and there is not one person in nine days of road who is entitled to be paid, and there is not one person in nine days of road who can be given anything.

She is both of those. She cannot be paid and she cannot be given a thing, and she has been both since she was nine years old.

## What A Shape Turns Out To Be

He goes looking for it the way he goes looking for everything, which is by taking one thing apart at a time until the thing he wants is the only thing left in the room.

A shape, in this world, is a set of true statements about a person, written in a hand, with a date and a place on it, so that a thing which keeps records can find the person the statements are about.

Written.

That is the whole difficulty and it took him a count of forty to get to it and it has taken him nineteen years to arrive at the end of it.

On the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114 she made one. She stood at the north end of a ground of two hundred people in the open at the fourth hour of the afternoon and wrote four sentences in her own hand on a board, with a date and a place on it, and it is true, because her hand cannot write a false line.

And then she went on counting.

Nothing has happened to the board since. Four men write a stroke and a number in beside it every night and not one of the four can read it, and a cart passes four times a day, and one carter in a year stops.

He looks at what she wrote and asks the only question in it that matters, and the question is not about her.

The question is: who is keeping it.

## The Answer He Finds

He works it out in nine minutes.

Not one of them.

Not the four men, who cannot read it. Not the county, which does not know the ground exists. Not the building with the lamps, which has a book with four hundred and seven names in it and not one of them is a woman of nineteen standing in a field.

And not her.

That is the part he does not like and he does not go round it. She wrote a shape in her own hand and she put it on a thing that four men write on and that a cart passes, and a shape that is not kept is not a shape. It is a line. It is four true sentences lying in the weather, and in nine years a board that carries something true gets wiped on the twenty-fourth by a rag in a man’s back pocket and the statements go into a rag and the rag goes into a coat.

So the only person in the world who has ever been able to keep a shape is the one who cannot be given one.

And he asks himself, in a place with no floor in it, what exactly that means, and it means this: there is no instrument in this world that will give a thing to her. Not a rate, not a roll, not a room, not a name. She has been offered two shapes in nine years and refused both and neither of them was refused out of principle.

One was the fifth line of a sheet read out loud in a room at the fourth hour of the third of the month beginning 1,304, and a man of sixty-one wrote one line and the date in the ordinary hand, and no hand in that building has taken it out since. The other was a question asked standing up on a floor, and she asked that one herself.

And so the only shape that has ever been kept for anybody in eleven hundred years is one somebody wrote for themselves and then carried.

## The Price

He is holding one now.

He knows what it is. It is the thing that was made to record her, asked at the third hour of the afternoon on the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,114, in daylight, standing, in front of two hundred people, and given an answer with six terms in it, and the six terms are standing and cannot be added to.

He is holding it and he is the only person who is holding it and he cannot put it anywhere.

Here is the price and he looks at it for about an hour and it is not what he expected.

She cannot keep what she finds by holding it. She told him that a long time ago, in the ordinary voice, standing up, on a floor, and she has not taken it back and he has not heard her take it back, and it is the reason there is no shape in this world with her in it that anybody else is holding.

And he cannot be the one who holds it, because a thing he holds is findable, and findable is what she agreed to at the third hour of the twenty-first and what a thing that reads records will find inside a year, and she priced that bill in days at the tenth hour of a night on the nineteenth and did not answer it.

So the only way to keep a shape is to ask her to keep it. And asking her is ch 609’s rule turned round and pointed at him for the first time in nineteen years.

One thing at a time. In daylight. Standing up. Said out loud before it is a line anywhere else.

There is no version of that where she can hear the asking and go back to before she heard it.

And there is no version where he asks it and does not mean it, and that is the part he goes round four times before he lets himself look at it.

Nine years he has been the thing that speaks in her head without anything written down, and she took that away on the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,114, and she was right to, and he has not missed it. What he has missed is the other half of it, which is that a thing which is not written down cannot be kept by anybody but her, and there is a version of her that does not exist tomorrow.

That is not the finding. The finding is that the asking is the keeping and he cannot have one without the other.

## What He Is Going To Do

He is going to ask her.

He knows what it costs now and he has known it for an hour and he is not going to do anything else about it, and he does not put it in a book and he does not put it on a board, because he does not decide anything. She decides everything.

And the part he has not got, coming out of it, is the one thing a person would want out of a thing like this, which is a date.

She has a first of a month in thirty days and there are five of them left in this year and every one of them is a day a board goes up on a stair, and there is exactly one of those days she has never been away from, and he has no way of knowing which one it is until she is standing in it, and she does not tell a person things a day in advance.

He has been in this place eight times and he has never once left it with something he wanted to be able to do and not been able to do.

This time he is going to ask her to keep a shape for him, and he cannot say the day, and he is going to do it anyway.
