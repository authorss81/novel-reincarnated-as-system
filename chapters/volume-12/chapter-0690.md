# Nothing Goes Wrong

The first entry was made out loud at the second hour of the afternoon on the twenty-second of the month beginning 2,114, standing, in daylight, at the north end of the rows, and she has gone over the four days that followed a great many times and the thing that is hardest to hold is not the sound of it.

It is that nothing went wrong.

## The Twenty-Second

He said it out loud before it was a line anywhere else. That was the fourth of the six terms and she had given it to him at the third hour of the previous afternoon in front of two hundred people and she is not a woman who gives a term she does not expect to be obeyed.

“The twenty-first of the month beginning 2,114. A person agreed, standing, in daylight, at the north end of this ground, to be recorded by me while it is happening. The terms were six and she said them out loud and the sixth of them is that this is a record and not a hand.”

She stood there and checked it against what had happened.

That is what she does. She has been checking numbers against other numbers since she was nine years old and it is the only thing she has ever been good at that is not a body, and she stood in the middle of two hundred rows of stones and checked eleven words against about three hundred of her own memory and found nothing wrong with any of them.

She said, “That is true.”

And that was the whole of the twenty-second, and it took about a minute.

## The Twenty-Third

On the twenty-third of the month beginning 2,114 he read back to her what he had said about the eighteenth, which is the day a carter said a name at the north end of this ground, and it took nine sentences.

Then he stopped and said, “There is one thing in that which I have not said and which you did not say.”

She said, “Say it.”

He said, “At the sixth hour of the eighteenth I asked you what I have done, and you asked me what it costs, and you said that you were not paying it and not refusing it.”

She said, “That is the bill. I have not settled it and I am not pretending to.”

He said, “Then it goes in or it does not, and I am asking you, once, because the fifth of your six terms is that you do not find out about it afterwards, and the alternative to asking is deciding for you, and I have been the thing that decides for you for nine years and you have never once let me and I am not starting at the bottom of a field.”

She stood in the daylight and worked it out in four counts.

A record that leaves out a thing because the record-holder thought it was not his business is not a true record. A record that leaves out a thing because the person in it said *leave that out* is a record of two things, and one of them is a decision, and a decision is the thing she has kept for herself since she was nine years old.

She said, “Put it in. Including the part where I am the one who will not pay it and not refuse it. Including the hour. I do not want it to read well.”

## The Twenty-Fourth

The twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,114 was the day she tried to catch him out and it took her from the first hour to the seventh.

She is good at this. She caught a total on a wall in this county that has been wrong by two thousand nine hundred and twenty for forty-five years, and a hundred people have worked under that wall every day of it and not one of them knew. She went after this record the way she goes after a sum, from the top down, and she went after it word by word for nine hours.

He said it out loud as he went, in daylight, standing, at the north end of the rows, and she stood there and let him and checked every word as it came and twice she made him go back and say a thing again because she had lost the thread of it.

Both times he said it the same way.

At the seventh hour she stopped, and she stood there and told the rows, in daylight, what she had found.

She said, “I have been sitting on this for nine hours waiting for you to be wrong about one thing, and I have got a list of about six places where a person could put in a thing that was not so, and not one of them is yours.”

She said, “And that is because there is no page. That is the whole of it and it is the thing I have to live inside for the next four days.”

## The Thing That Is Difficult

Her hand cannot write a false line. She has known that since she was twelve years old and it is the only guarantee she has ever had and she has built an entire life on it, and since the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,114 she has given the guarantee away.

What is standing next to her instead is a mouth.

That is the difficulty and she put it into words on the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,114 at the eighth hour, standing, in daylight, at the north end of the rows, and it is the plainest thing in the whole of those four days:

“For nine years I have checked a hand against a page. I could not take money for it and I could not be given a thing and I could not be paid, and the whole of the price of that is that the one hand I can check is my own, and my own cannot lie.”

She said, “And now the thing that is being written about me is a voice, and a voice can be wrong, and there is not one instrument in nine days of road or in this building or in this county that can settle it, and if I was wrong then the thing that has my whole life in it is wrong and I will find out about it in eleven hundred years from a drawer in a tower or I will not find out at all.”

She said, “That is what I gave up at the third hour of the twenty-first of this month and I am not taking it back, because a document agreed to in daylight is not a document that can be taken back, and because I told you on the twenty-first that I am not going to be one of those people who counts the have and lets the have-not go.”

## The Twenty-Fifth And The Twenty-Sixth

And then, on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 2,114, something happened that she has not put down anywhere and has thought about every day since.

She stopped checking.

There is no event. There is no hour at which she decided. At the fourth hour of the twenty-fifth she was at the north end of the board and he said four sentences out loud about the twenty-fourth, and she listened to them standing in the daylight with her right arm hanging, and at the end of the fourth sentence she realised that her hand had not stopped to do a sum.

She stood there for a count of twenty.

She said, out loud, to the board, “I did not decide that.”

And then, because she does not let a thing stand that she has not said in daylight:

“That is the thing a record is for. Not for being checked. For not having to be checked. I have spent nine years not believing a single person about a single thing and this is the first arrangement where there is a way of not doing it, and the way of not doing it is that somebody else does the checking and it is their life on it and not mine.”

That is what she got for it.

That is the whole of what she got, and she is nineteen years old, and she has been lighter for five hundred and seventy-eight days, and she got a thing that no person on nine days of road has ever had.

## The Twenty-Seventh

And on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 2,114, at the second hour of the afternoon, standing, in daylight, at the north end of the rows, he read back to her what he had said about the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,114.

That she had said no first.

That she had said the sentence about a column and it was thirteen words and it was written on the third leaf of a book in her own hand when she was nine years old.

That she had given him six terms.

That she had agreed, out loud, standing, in daylight, in front of about two hundred people, and that it could not be taken back, and that he had said so then and was saying so now so that the saying of it was in it.

And she stood there and listened to the whole of it.

And she did not say one word.

That is where the four days ended and it is the only thing in them she has never been able to explain to anybody, including herself, and she has been at it since and has got nowhere.

It was not because it was right. It was because there is no seventh term.

She said six, at the third hour of the twenty-first, in daylight, standing, in front of two hundred people, and she has thought about adding one ever since — *and you check it back to me once a month and I check it back to you once a month* — and she has not said it.

Not saying it is the same thing she has done to a man of forty-nine’s slate and to a woman of sixty on a bench and to a woman of sixty-three behind a counter for nine years.

A document agreed to in daylight is not a document that can be taken back, and it is also not a document that can be added to, and she knew both of those things at the third hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,114 when she said it, standing up, in daylight, in front of two hundred people.

She picked the bar up off the ground in a count of twenty-two.

The first of the month beginning 2,144 is four days off.
