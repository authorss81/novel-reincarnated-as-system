# Four Accounts That Do Not Agree

She stayed at the frame for two days after the eighth first and she did not go anywhere, and it is the first time in nineteen years she has been in a place for two days with nothing to be useful for that she did not arrange on purpose.

She counted the grit for nine hundred paces on the second of the month beginning 2,294. She counted the posts. She slept in a field four hundred yards south of the turn.

She is lighter for seven hundred and thirty-six days and not one of them has gone back and she is not hungry, and she was not hungry before and she was not hungry after, and the whole of what the eighth first did to her body happened in about nine seconds in the middle of a line in her own hand.

## What Four People Said

And the accounts came back into the turn over about a day and a half, and she did not ask for any of them, and every one of them arrived at four feet off a person standing at a frame.

The man of forty-nine came past at the sixth hour of the second of the month beginning 2,294 with the rag in his back pocket and the chalk in his right hand, and he stopped about nine feet off the frame, which he has never done, and said:

“There was a sound at this frame on the first and I have been doing this for nineteen years and there has not been one before.”

She said, “Say the sound.”

He said, “I will not. I am not going to say it for the road.”

She said, “Why not.”

He said, “Because I have got four columns to write and the light is going, and because I have not got it right yet, and a man who says a thing wrong once at a turn does not get to say it again.”

And he went and did his four columns and she stood in the grit and did not press him, and she has never pressed anybody in her life.

The woman of sixty said nothing at all about it on the second of the month beginning 2,294, and she said one sentence from a bench four hundred yards west on the third of it, and it was this:

“It was a number.”

She said, “How do you know.”

She said, “Because I have sat on this bench nine years and I have heard men say carts and horses and the post and the mill and a boy crying, and I have never once heard anybody at this turn say a single figure out loud in the open, and that is what it was.”

A carter of about fifty stopped at the frame on the second of the month beginning 2,294 with his horse and said:

“There was not anything at all at this frame and I was three hundred yards off it and I heard my own axle.”

She said, “You were three hundred yards off.”

He said, “I was, and I heard my own axle, and I have driven this road for thirty-one years and I know what my axle sounds like at two hundred yards and at three hundred and this was not it.”

And then he said the last of it.

He said, “But my boy was sat at the end of your grit and he is nine and he has a basket on his arm all day, and he came home and told my wife that the turn made a noise like a man reading out loud, and my wife said nobody reads out loud at a turn.”

The boy was at the end of the grit with a basket on his arm on the second of the month beginning 2,294 and he was there again on the third, and she asked him one question on the third of it, standing, in daylight, one at a time, the way she asks everything.

She said, “What did you hear.”

He said, “Me.”

She said, “Say that again.”

He said, “It was me. It said me.”

And his father came and took him away at the fourth hour and he did not say a word about it after that, and she did not follow it up, and she has not been able to forget it, and she is not going to find out what it was.

## The Four Of Them

And there it is, and she wrote it out on the flat of the frame rail with a stick of chalk so that she would not be able to lose it.

A man of forty-nine who will not say what he heard and has not got it right.

A woman of sixty who says it was a number.

A carter of fifty who says it was nothing at all, and that the boy with him heard something else entirely.

And a boy of nine with a basket on his arm who says it was him.

Four people. Four accounts. No two of them agree, not one of the four can repeat what they heard, and three of the four have said out loud that they are not going to try.

She is not one of the four and she heard it too, and she has told nobody what she heard and she is not going to, and the day she tells it is the day there is an account that agrees with hers and that is the day it becomes a thing that was said instead of a thing that happened.

She said that out loud, standing, in daylight, on the third of the month beginning 2,294, at a frame in a gravel turn, to a stick of chalk and a slate with her name on it.

## What The Harvest Took

And that is where the bill came in, and it came in the way bills do, which is that you are standing in a place not thinking about it and then it is in the room.

She was lighter for seven hundred and thirty-six days and she worked it out on the third of the month beginning 2,294, standing, in daylight, out loud, and it took her a count of nine.

The levy is one in twenty of every day a name has had in a column. It has been coming off her since the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054, at the rate of a day and a half a month, and it does not stop on a first, and it is not something that happens to a board.

And she wrote a figure on a board on the first of the month beginning 2,294, and the figure is what the levy has already taken, and it is on the wood now in her own hand with a date and a place on it.

She said, standing, in daylight, at the frame, on the third of the month beginning 2,294:

“So it has come off the writing and it has not come off me again, because I did not put anything on that board that had not already been taken off me a day at a time for two hundred and eighteen days.”

She said, “That is the harvest. That is the whole of it. It has taken a board and it has not taken a person, and there is no instrument in this county that knows how to take a person off a piece of wood, and there never has been one, and there is not going to be one.”

She said, “And the price of that is that it is in a book. And a figure that is only in my head can be doubted by a person and has been doubted by two hundred people in the last forty days, and a figure in a book cannot be doubted at all unless somebody comes and does the arithmetic, and not one person in this county is going to come and do the arithmetic.”

## The Bill Came In

And then she said the thing out loud that she had not said out loud anywhere since the tenth hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 2,114 at the north end of a ground of two hundred people, when a voice in her head named a price and she refused to pay it and did not refuse it either.

She said, “It is paid.”

She said, “On the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114 there is a board at the north end of a ground with four sentences in my own hand on it and a name at the end of it, and a carter read that name out loud on a made road three days later and did not know what it was.

And a thing that reads records has never wanted one in eleven hundred years and there is one now, and the bill was that it would be able to read it, and that it would read it because somebody asked to be found.”

She said, “It has read it. Not that one. That is what I thought it wanted and it is not that.

What it wanted was a shape with a hand on it, and it has got one, and it went up a stair at the second hour of the first of the month beginning 2,294 in a bundle with forty other boards in it, and four people in the seat have never in their lives looked at one of those boards, and a room at the top of a stair four miles up a mountain from the square has.”

She said, “And I did not agree to it. I priced it in days once and did not say out loud what I worked out, and I have not said it out loud now either, except that I am telling you it is paid, at a frame, in daylight, on the third of the month beginning 2,294, and I am not going to be able to write a line that does not have my name at the end of it ever again.”

She said, “And I would have written it anyway.”

## What She Took West

And she put the chalk down on the rail and wiped it off with her thumb and put the thumb in her coat and picked the bar up off the ground in a count of twenty-nine.

She put it on her left shoulder, because there is no strap on it and there has not been one since the month beginning 2,084, and she is nineteen years old and she has three fingers and a thumb and an arm that is not going to close and seven hundred and thirty-six days behind her and a figure in a book that is above her and not here.

She went west at the fourth hour of the third of the month beginning 2,294.

Nine miles of store road that is paid for, then four miles of it that is not paid for, then a cottage at the far side of that that is not the one at the stopped lime bed, and then four miles up.

And four miles down the mountain and a long way east, on a store road, at a turn, there were people standing about who could not say what they had heard, and one of them is going to say it wrong for the rest of their life, and she is not going to be standing four hundred yards from a bench when it happens.
