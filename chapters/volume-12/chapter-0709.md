# What Has To Be On A Board

She worked on it for four days at a frame in a gravel turn with a bar on the ground at her feet, standing, in daylight, out loud, one condition at a time, the way she has done every hard thing since she was nine.

A thing that keeps records can find has to have three things on it, and she found them by taking a shape she already had apart.

The shape she took apart was four sentences and a date, written on a board at the north end of a ground of two hundred people that four carts go past a day.

## The Three Things

She wrote them out with a stick of chalk on the flat of the frame rail where they come off in the rain, because she has four hundred volumes in her head and no paper that is hers.

A date. So that a thing that reads records can put it in order.

A place. So that a thing that reads records can get to the place it happened and look at the ground.

And true statements about a person, in a hand, in words that are the ordinary words of this county.

She said, standing, on the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,234, reading down the rail:

“That is a shape. That is all a shape is. And I have got one of those already and I did not make it and I have not been able to stop it since the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,114.”

## The Third Thing Is Not Hers

And then she got to the third condition, which is the one that took her four days.

A shape has to be findable. Findable means a thing can pick it out of everything else it has read and go and look at it. And the way a thing does that is with a name, because a name is the handle.

She has a name on a slate at this frame in a county hand and has had one since the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054, and the county hand is a man of forty-nine doing his working life and it went on a board because she asked him to, standing up, in daylight.

And that is the one the levy takes. That is a name with thirty days in a column beside it and a twentieth of every one of those days going off it whether the board is on a frame or in a cart or in a room.

She said, out loud, to the rail, on the twenty-second:

“So the third thing has to be not her.”

And then she stood in the grit for about an hour working out what not her means when the handle of the whole thing is a name, and she came back to it with the answer and the answer was a figure.

“A name is a person. You can put a name on a board and a twentieth of every day since can come off it. And a figure is not a person. There is no one to come off. You cannot collect a number off a piece of slate, because it has never belonged to a living person.”

## What The Figure Is Made Of

She took the strip out of the front of her bag on the twenty-second, unfolded it once, and put it flat on the top rail of the frame.

Nine inches by two. Long rag fibre every way at once. Two edges cut with a knife and one torn for half an inch and stopped. A square the size of the flat of her hand pressed into the middle of it, darker than the paper and not wet and not ink and not grease, and eighty-one lines on it in a small hand, and under the lines the same sum written out in words.

She looked at it for a long time.

She said, out loud, standing, in daylight, with the light going off the rail:

“This is a figure and it is true and there is no name on it and I put the sum in twice, once in numbers and once in words, because a person who cannot do the arithmetic has to be able to check it without doing the arithmetic. That is not a false line. Both halves are the same number.”

She said, “And I have been carrying it in the front of my bag for thirty days and it is not a shape, because a thing with no name on it is not a thing anybody is on. I worked that out on a frame two days of road east and I have not got a better answer than that now.”

## The Strip Cannot Go

And then she tried it properly, which is the only way she has ever tested a thing, by trying to put it in the wrong place and seeing whether it would go.

The strip is nine inches by two and it came off the top of a stair four miles up a mountain on a cart from a man of forty who put his hand out flat and kept it out until she took it and did not ask where it came from and did not look at it again.

A bundle is a square of oilcloth with a weight in the middle of it, and there is a square the size of the flat of her hand pressed into the middle of that strip, darker than the paper, and not wet, and not ink, and not grease.

She has carried it for thirty days and it is the only object in this county that was made to be sent somewhere and went nowhere, and she has thought about putting a line on it every single day of those thirty.

She worked it out standing at the frame on the twenty-third.

A thing that keeps records can find has to be a thing somebody can hold up to something. That is the whole of findable. It is not a kind of paper, it is not a hand, it is not a county. It is that a person can pick it up and walk it to where it is going, and put it where somebody else is going to look.

And she can carry a strip of hand-made paper in the front of a bag to a frame four feet off a road. What she cannot do is make the road and the cart and the stair at the far end of it go somewhere a person at a frame will find a strip of paper, because there is no machinery in this county for a strip of paper and there has never been.

She said, out loud, to the top rail of a frame, on the twenty-third of the month beginning 2,234:

“So it is slate or it is nothing, and the slate is four feet off a road where four carts go past a day, and that is why it has to be slate.”

## The Date Is Not A Choice

And that is when she found the thing that decided the whole of it, and it took her a count of nine and she got it in the rain standing up.

She wrote the conditions on the rail and then she wrote the word that goes in the first space, and then she sat on the butt of the post and worked out what dates a board goes up on.

A board goes up on the first of a month.

Not on a day a person picks. Not on a day a person could choose if there were two to choose from. On the first, and the firsts in this county are thirty days apart and they have always been thirty days apart and there is not one person alive who decides them.

She said, out loud, to the two posts, on the twenty-third of the month beginning 2,234:

“So the date on it is a first. And a first is the only date a board goes up on, and there is not one other date in this county a line can travel on, and that is why it is not sooner.”

She said, “The seventh first is seven days from the morning after this. And the eighth is thirty days on from the seventh, and that is the whole of why it is thirty days on from the seventh and not sooner, and there is not one thing I can do about it and there is not one thing I want to do about it.”

## What The Twenty-Fourth Is

And on the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,234 the man of forty-nine came at the sixth hour with a rag in his back pocket and a short stick of white chalk in his right hand, and she stood in the grit four feet back where a cart can go past without touching her and watched the whole of it.

He took the slate out and wiped the frame and wiped the road side with the flat of his thumb in the time it takes a man to turn round twice, and he wrote four columns and her name went in the first of them with thirty days in beside it, and he did not look at her while he wrote it, and he did not write slowly.

And then he went east on foot toward a bench and she stood there and looked at a slate with her name on it in a county hand and thirty days of a month that has not started yet beside it.

## The Last First She Gets For Nothing

And then she said it out loud, standing, in daylight, at the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,234, to a slate four feet off her hand, one thing at a time.

“I am not going to write it today.”

She said, “I am going to write it at the first hour of the first of the month beginning 2,294, in the grit, in daylight, with my own hand, and it is going to be a date and a place and a figure and it is going to be true, and there is not going to be one word of it that is about me except the truth about one thing about me.”

She said, “And the reason I am not writing it today is that a line on a board is not a decision. It stops being a decision the second it is down, and I want there to be thirty days of it still being a decision, because I have not got one and I would rather find out on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 2,264 than on the first of the month beginning 2,294.”

She said, “And the second reason is that the seventh first is in seven days and I am going to stand in this grit and watch that board go up with nothing of mine on it that I did not put there, and I am going to do that on purpose, and that is the last first of a month I am ever going to get for nothing.”

She picked the bar up in a count of twenty-nine, and her hand was the hand that had done the writing of four columns a man of forty-nine does not know anything about, and she has four fingers and a thumb on it, and she put the bar on her left shoulder and went four hundred yards west and asked the woman of sixty whether she had ever had anything of hers carried anywhere, one question, standing up, in daylight.

She had not worked out what she was going to do with the answer, and she had not worked out what she was going to do about the fourth name from the bottom of that column either, and a bench four hundred yards west is the only place in this county where either of those two things can be looked at.
