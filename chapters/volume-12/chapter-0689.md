# Letting Him Keep The Record

He asked for it at the second hour of the afternoon on the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,114, at the north end of the rows, standing up, in daylight, in front of about two hundred people, and he had never asked her for anything in nineteen years.

## The Asking

She was at the board with the bar on the ground at her feet and a dozen strokes gone in beside her line since the morning and a man of fifty carrying stones going past about forty feet off.

He said, “I am going to ask you for one thing and I want to say what it is before I ask it, and I am going to say it out loud in the ordinary voice, and you are going to be able to say no and I am not going to be able to take it back either.”

She said, “Say it.”

He said, “Keep the record.”

And then he said the rest of it and she did not interrupt, and it took about a count of forty.

“You have been on a board at a turn twenty miles west of here since the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054 and you cannot be taken off it. You wrote four sentences on a board at the north end of this ground on the fifteenth of this month and a carter read a name out of it on the eighteenth and did not know what it was. You went up a stage on the fifteenth of this month in a building with a hundred people in it and not one of them entered it, and you have not been able to tell anybody, including me, because of a question you asked standing up on a floor.”

He said, “I am asking to keep the record. Not to keep it for you. To keep it, so that there is a place where the whole of it is, in order, in words, true, with the dates on it, going back as far as it goes.”

## What She Said To That

She did not answer for about a count of sixty.

A bar lay on the ground at her feet and a man carried a stone past about forty feet off and the light came up over the rise behind her the way it comes up over that rise every day of her life, and she stood in the middle of two hundred rows and worked out whether she was going to say yes, and she worked it out the only way she knows how, which is out loud, standing, one thing at a time, in daylight.

She said, “No.”

She said it first, and she said it properly, and she has never in her life been gladder of a word.

And then she said, “And I am going to change that, and I am going to tell you why before I do it, because I have been doing this for nine years and I do not take a thing back without saying what the taking back is, and because if I say yes to this I am not going to be able to say no to it later.”

## The Reason She Said Yes

“You told me once, at the end of a year, what you had been, and I asked you what it was for, and you said it, and you said it in the ordinary voice.”

He said, “I remember.”

She said, “And two days ago you said something else to me about a column, and I have carried it about two days, and I am going to say it now, because you have put a name in a carter’s mouth on a made road, and if I am going to say yes to this I am going to say it out loud where a person can hear it and not only where he can.”

She said, “For eight years I did not have a man. I had a column.”

That is the sentence. It is thirteen words and it is on the third leaf of a book in four capital strokes and in her own hand and it is the only thing in eleven hundred years of this county that anybody has ever written down about him, and she had written it herself, when she was nine years old, before she had a bar or a road or a way of counting anything at all.

She said, “And that was correct, and I have been wrong about it since about the eighth hour of the twelfth of this month, standing in a room with the shutters up in front of a book I cannot be in.”

## The Terms She Said With It

She said it at the third hour of the afternoon on the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,114, standing, in daylight, at the north end of two hundred rows of stones, with a bar on the ground at her feet and two hundred people in the ground who had not stopped working and were not going to.

“I agree. Out loud, standing, in daylight, here, now, in front of two hundred people who are not listening because they have stones to carry.”

She said, “And the terms are these and there are six of them and you do not get any of the six taken off.”

One. One thing at a time.

Two. In daylight.

Three. Standing up.

Four. “You say it out loud as you go. Every line. Before it is a line anywhere else. If there is one I have not heard first then it is not a record, it is a thing you did on your own, and there is no version of this where I find out about it later.”

Five. “If you ever put down one word that is not true, I will know it inside a day, and there is no road back for either of us. Not for me and not for you. That is the whole of the price and I am not able to do anything about it and you are not either.”

Six. “It is a record. It is not a hand. You do not decide anything. I decide everything, and the only thing you have is that it is written down.”

And then she said the sentence that is the whole of the permission and she said it once:

“Yes, and you say it out loud as you go, and if you ever write a line down that is not true I will know inside a day and there is no road back for either of us.”

## What He Said About What It Cost

He did not say it was a small thing and he did not say it was a big one.

“A record that is true and is kept is the only instrument in this world that can take a person through a wall. That is not mine. A clerk said it and wrote it down once and it went in a drawer, and it has been the truest sentence anybody has said to me in nineteen years and I have not put it anywhere.”

He said, “And a record that is kept can be read by a thing that reads records, and I told you what that is, and it is still true this afternoon, and nothing about the six terms you have just given me makes it not true.”

He said, “You know that. You said the terms out loud in daylight in front of two hundred people, which means you said them to yourself first.”

## What It Is And Is Not

She stood there and put it down the way she puts down every figure, standing up, out loud, in daylight, so that it exists somewhere other than in her.

She said, “Here is what I have and here is what I have not, and I am not going to be one of those people who counts the have and lets the have-not go.”

She said, “I have a record. Nineteen years of it, kept true, in words, by the thing that was made to record me, saying it out loud as he goes, and it is the first document in this county that anybody has ever agreed to in daylight and it cannot be taken back, and I have agreed to it, and I am not going to take it back.”

She said, “I have not got a hand out of it. There is a thing that reads records and it has never wanted one and it is going to want this one, and that bill is open on a table at the north end of this ground and I have not paid it and I did not price it into the six.”

She said, “And I have not got anybody else. There is not one person in nine days of road that I am entitled to ask to say *I have seen it*, and I asked for that on the seventeenth of this month and got an answer from a voice and not from a person, and I am not going to be able to un-take what I have taken here this afternoon.”

## Why The Reason Is True

And then, because she has never once in her life let a sentence stand that she has not said out loud in daylight, she said the last part, and it took about nine seconds.

“You can hear it. You have always been able to hear it and it has never cost me anything I could not pay and you have never once been able to stop me hearing it.”

She said, “That is what I gave up this afternoon. Not the days. I have been lighter for five hundred and seventy-four days and I have got the figure of it and it is mine and nobody has asked me for it and I have not given it to anybody. I gave up that I do not have a record at all.”

She said, “And the reason I said yes is true, and that is the part that is going to cost, and it is going to cost because I can say it out loud in front of two hundred people and it does not come out as a lie.”

She said, “The reason I said yes is that I am nineteen years old and I have been alone with everything I have ever seen since I was nine, and there is a board at the north end of this ground with one line in my own hand on it and a cart comes past it four times a day, and there is one thing in the world that can do better than a board, and it is asking me.”

And she picked the bar up off the ground in a count of twenty-two and put it on her shoulder, and there is no strap on it and there has not been one for a month, and the first of the month beginning 2,144 is ten days off, and a document that has been agreed to in daylight in front of two hundred people is not a document that can be taken back.
