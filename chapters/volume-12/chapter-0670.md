# The Same Four Miles

She went down the mountain at the second hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,054 with the can of oil on her shoulder, because a boy of sixteen had carried it four times in two days and she had counted, and because she had noticed him counting her watching him and decided to do the one thing that was in reach.

She went down the long way round the tanner’s, because the short way goes past a door she has not gone past in five years and the size of this life does not need one more thing she has not been asked about.

Then she went west on the store road with the bar on her shoulder and the light behind her and nine miles of it in front of her.

## What A Day Of Road Is For

There is a thing she does when she has been in a room four days and she does not know how to do it, which is that she stops counting for about half of a day.

The breath went to seven on the cart track and to five on the made road and to four on the long flat where the made road comes down off the shoulder of the hill and runs out along the valley, and the bar is nine feet nine and the strap is out of her own coat lining and she has carried it since the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,904.

She ate half a loaf at the fourth hour, sitting on a wall, and the other half was for the evening, and she is not hungry, and has not been hungry since she was ten years old and has never once worked out what that means.

The arm was open. It is open four days in five and this was one of them, and the bar was on her left shoulder by the ninth hour and stayed there, and she does the swap at a wall or a gate without stopping and without deciding, and it has been the same for ten years.

## Nine Miles And A Wheel That Has Not Turned

She came to the mill at the sixth hour and stopped, which she has not done in five years.

The wheel is on the stream out of the mill race and it is nine feet and it is built of oak and the arms are still square and it has not turned in nine years, and the stream under it has cut a channel and gone round the bottom of it. The miller is dead. She has not been inside the mill since she was fourteen.

She stood in the failing light in front of a wheel for about four minutes.

Then she said it out loud, on made road, to the wheel, because she had been carrying the number for seven days and it wanted company:

“Nine years is nine years. The room at the top of a tower has been shut for nine years and a wheel at a mill has not turned for nine years, and I have been walking past both of them and I have not put them in the same count until this evening on a road with an empty bar case.”

She said, “I do not know what it means. I have said that out loud and it is still true, and I am not going to build something on two numbers that happen to be the same, because I have watched four people in my life do that and every one of them was wrong inside a month.”

She said, “But I have said it out loud, which is the first part of a thing being real, and the second part is that I am walking another four miles to stand in front of a board.”

## Two Posts Seen From The Turn

She came to the turn at the ninth hour on made road from the east with the light going, and there were nine men and two carts at the yard and the woman of sixty was on the bench, and the bench has been the same bench for nine years and she has been on it every morning of them.

She could see the two posts from the turn. Four hundred yards east, and they are black against the last of the light, and the bar between them is a bar and there was nothing on it, and she looked at them for about a count of ten from where she was standing with the bar on her shoulder and did not go near them.

She worked out the number standing where she stood, because she was going to be asked about it later and she does not want to be the sort of person who has not done the sum.

The eighteenth of the month beginning 2,054. The twenty-fourth is six days off. The first of the next month is thirteen days off, and the board goes up on the first, and a thing that is going to be on a board has to be on it before the first goes up.

That is not a rule. She has not got a rule. It is what a board does and she has watched boards since she was twelve, and she is going to find out at the posts in a day or two whether she has it right.

Six days is not long. She has walked a hundred and fifty miles in thirteen days and eaten half a loaf a day and slept on stones and in a stairwell and she is not hungry and she has been lighter every day since she was ten.

Five days is more than she has ever needed to decide anything that mattered.

She came to the turn at the ninth hour on made road from the east with the light going, and there were nine men and two carts at the yard and the woman of sixty was on the bench, and the bench has been the same bench for nine years and she has been on it every morning of them.

The woman of sixty said, “You went up.”

She said, “I went up four miles and I went up eight flights.”

The woman of sixty said, “And?”

She said, “There is a room at the top of the stair and there is a long table in it and there is not one thing on the table and there has not been one thing on it since I was ten years old.”

The woman of sixty said, “Was there a man in it.”

She said, “No. And there has not been one since I was ten years old either, and I have worked out that those are one thing and not two.”

The woman of sixty said, “That is the first thing you have come back down with in nine days and it is a nothing.”

She said, “It is.”

The woman of sixty said, “Then you have come back down with a nothing and you have got a face on you like a person who has come down a stair and found out there was nothing at the top of it.”

She said, “I have got a face on me like a person who has walked thirteen miles to find out a nothing and is going to walk four hundred yards to find out another one.”

The woman of sixty said, “You have not been on that board.”

She said, “No.”

The woman of sixty said, “Not once in nine years, and I have been on that bench every morning of them, and I have watched you stand in front of those posts twice and go away again both times, and both times it was because there was nothing on it.”

She said, “There is still nothing on it. It goes up on the first and it has gone up on the first with nothing on it since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,814.”

The woman of sixty said, “Yes. And it goes up on the first because a man comes out here on the twenty-fourth of the month and takes in what is on it, and then it goes up on the first with what he took in.”

She stood in the grit and did not say one word for a count of twenty, which is the longest she has gone without speaking to a person who is not on a mountain.

She said, “The twenty-fourth.”

The woman of sixty said, “I have said a number out loud on a bench and it is out loud.”

She said, “That is right and I am not going to ask you where you learned it, because you are going to tell me the same thing you have told me for nine years, which is that I am not the first person to need to know how a turn works.”

The woman of sixty said, “You are not.”

## She Goes Back On Purpose

She said, “I am going to stand at the two posts.”

The woman of sixty said, “They are four hundred yards east.”

She said, “I know how far they are. I have stood in front of them twice in nine days and I did not go near them either time because I had not got anything to find out.”

The woman of sixty said, “You have got nothing now.”

She said, “I have got less than I had. I have got a half answer and an empty room and a boy of sixteen and a locked drawer.”

The woman of sixty said, “Then what are you going to look for on a board.”

She stood in the grit at the foot of the bench and worked it out in the ordinary voice, out loud, at the ninth hour, on a store road, with nine men and two carts in a yard behind her.

She said, “I am going to look for the thing that everything I have found out in the last six days has been standing on.”

She said, “A thing goes to a place where there is somebody to receive it. For nine years everything I was announced as went into the ground because there was not one person in the place. And in this building, up four flights, there is a room that is built for a person to be in, and it is empty, and it is going to stay empty.”

She said, “So the way to be in a room is to be seen by whoever will put me in it. And a person cannot be seen at four miles. And a person cannot be seen on a road. And the only thing in this world that a stranger can see from four miles is a line in a hand on a board that goes up on the first of a month.”

She said, “And there has not been a name on the two posts four hundred yards east of this turn since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,814, and I have been reading boards since I was twelve, and that board has been the only thing in nine days of road that anybody has ever paid me for.”

She said, “I am going to stand at it in a day or two and I am going to work out what it is for, out loud, on my own, in daylight, with whoever watching, and I am telling you that now because I have said it out loud to a bench and a bench is a person and a person can be asked back about it later.”

The woman of sixty said, “You will be asked.”

She said, “I know I will. I have said it out loud and I have said it in front of nine men and two carts and I cannot take any of it back and that is on purpose, because a thing said in daylight to a person with a witness in it has happened twice, and I have learned that in a room with a lamp in it.”

She said, “I am going to the posts.”

She said, “Not tonight and not in the morning. I have just come down thirteen miles and I have got an empty room at the top of a stair in my head and I am not going to stand in front of a board at nine hours and say one word out loud that I cannot say again. I am going to sit on that bench for a day first.”

She said, “And then I am going to the posts. And I have said all of that out loud, which means that if I sit on that bench tomorrow morning instead, there is a woman of sixty who can ask me about it.”
