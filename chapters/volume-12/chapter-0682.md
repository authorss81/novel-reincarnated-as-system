# Furniture In A Room

She was in the building from the fourth hour of the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,084 to the ninth hour of the twentieth, which is six days, and in six days she did almost nothing, and that is the whole of the difficulty.

## Four Days With Nothing To Do In Either

There is one counter. There are twenty-two tables. There is a lamp on every table and about a hundred people at them reading things that have been carried up a stair somewhere.

There is no work in that room she is allowed to do.

She cannot be paid, because a page is money and her hand does not close on money for a reading, and it has not closed on one since a shed at a mill when she was fifteen.

She cannot be given a place, because a place in that building is a thing that goes in a book and there is a second line in that book with her name on it now, from the twelfth of the month beginning 2,084, written by a man of sixty-five who was doing his job.

She cannot be given a room, because the room is the third line and the room and the name are one arrangement.

She cannot be called, because to be called she has to be standing, and standing means recruited, and she said no to being recruited in daylight in this room on the twelfth with the book open.

So she stood at the end of the counter and she watched, and there is a great deal to watch.

She watched a shelf go out at the fourth hour and come back at the sixth. She watched a woman take sheets off tables and square them into piles. She watched the man with the brush, who has done the same nine strokes at the start of every page for nineteen years. She watched the floor get swept at the ninth hour by a woman with a broom who swept it the same way.

She watched it for six days and on the fourth day she worked out that she had stood still for nine hours a day and could not have told one hour from another, and that this is a thing she is extremely good at, because she carried stones in a ground for a year and a half and she could stand for a day and a half without moving.

## The Arm, And What Six Days In A Building Does

There is one other thing about the six days and it is not nothing, and she put it down standing at the end of the counter on the nineteenth of the month beginning 2,084 at the sixth hour, because she counts everything and this is the kind of thing she counts.

Her right arm was open on five days out of the six.

She has not put it on an arm in this building. There is no bar in this room and there has not been a bar in this room for nineteen years and there is not one going to be, and for six days she has carried nine feet nine of iron outside in a yard and stood in a room for nine hours with a hand she cannot shut hanging at her side, and on five of the six days it was open.

She said, out loud, “In this room I have one good arm for five hours out of every day and four fingers and a thumb, and everything I did in these six days I did with that.”

She said, “And in nine days of road there is not one person who has ever noticed, and that is correct of them and it is correct of me.”

## What The Room Gets For Free

Here is the sum of the six days, and she did it standing at the end of the counter on the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,084 at the seventh hour, out loud, in daylight, to about sixty people and no one in particular.

What she cost that room in six days: nothing.

Not a lamp. Not an inch of oil. Not a sheet of paper. Not a meal. Not a place at a table. Not a hand’s worth of work that would otherwise have been done by somebody else.

She was in that room for six days and it did not spend one copper on her and it is not one copper the poorer, and that is the only reason she was permitted to be in it, and she proved that on the first day and it took her nine seconds.

And here is the other half of the sum and it is the one that costs.

Nothing in that building has to account for her.

A reader is on a book. A reader has a rate. A reader has a room with a shelf in it and a lamp in it. A reader can be called twice in a day and not more, and the word *not* in that sentence is doing all of the work, because the whole of the protection in that trade is a limit.

She has no rate and no room and no shelf and no lamp and no limit, and the reason the building does not have to account for her is that there is nothing about her to account for. If she were not in the room the day would run the same.

That is what a person becomes when a trade says nothing about her, and she is not a person who is being kept. She is a thing in a room that has no rate against it.

## The Sheet She Did Not Ask For

On the nineteenth of the month beginning 2,084, at about the fifth hour, a woman at the fourth table put a sheet down in front of her.

The woman did not ask. She put it down the way you put a thing down on a table when you have twenty things to do and your hands are full, and then she went away, and the sheet was face up and the sun was on it and the hand on it was one Ning Yan cannot read.

She read it standing up in about nine seconds.

It was a strip about a length of ditch on the north road and a number of days against it and a date. It was a true sheet. Nothing in it was wrong. It was going up a stair at the fourth hour the next morning and if it had gone up unread the days in it would have been the days somebody said they were.

The woman came back at the sixth hour and took it away and said, “That is right,” in the ordinary voice, without stopping, the way a person says it when there are nineteen other tables.

She did not say anything back. She has not said anything back to a person in nine years and she is not going to start at sixty tables.

## Nine Feet Off The Made Road

She went out to the gate at the fourth hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 2,084 and stood in the yard with the bar on the ground at her feet and about nine feet off a man with a shut notebook under his arm, which is the way he has been for four years and will be until this is over.

He does not buy anything. He does not threaten anything. He has never asked her a question and he is not going to start.

She asked him one.

She said, “Is a person who is about to be counted by everybody supposed to be counted by somebody first.”

He stood nine feet off and thought about it for long enough that she got the whole of the answer before he began it.

“That is not my question to decide,” he said, “and I have been asked it twice before by somebody in your position and I have given the same answer both times.”

She said, “Give me the other thing.”

He said, “I have watched a person volunteer for a count.”

She said, “A count of what.”

He said, “Of a day. Of a man’s day, a woman’s day, everybody’s day, taken on a morning and done in an afternoon in a place where it is easy to see who is standing.” He turned the notebook over once and did not open it. “And the count was the only part of it anybody enjoyed. Not the work. The count. The work was dull and cold and they were tired by the second hour and every one of them would have walked out in the middle of it.”

She said, “And the count.”

He said, “The count was the part where they were the subject of something. Nine feet off, in the ordinary voice, and he did not sell a thing and did not buy a thing and did not raise his voice once, and I have never seen a person come off a count in eleven years the way you come off a job, which is better than before.”

## What It Costs To Be Furniture

And she paid it.

She paid it on the sixth day, standing at the end of the counter at the ninth hour of the twentieth of the month beginning 2,084, with a hundred people reading behind her and a lamp on every one of them that was worth nothing at that hour, and she said it out loud because she has said things out loud standing up in daylight since she was nine years old and she was not going to stop doing the only thing she is good at in order to be comfortable.

“I have been in this room six days and I have cost it nothing and it has cost me nothing and that is not two true sentences meeting. It is one true sentence wearing two coats.”

She said, “And here is what I have worked out and I am saying it here so that a woman of sixty-three can hear it from the end of the room and not have to come over.”

She said, “A person who is not on a roll and cannot be paid cannot stay. That is the rule and I have not broken it and I am not going to. There is no place for me in this building. There is no rate. There is no shelf and no lamp and no call and no limit on the call, because a limit is a thing you get for being on a book.”

She said, “And I am going to stay anyway.”

She said, “And I am going to be the one who reads the sheets not one person in this room can make out, and I am going to do it for nothing, and not one person in this building is going to be able to say afterwards that I was given a rate or a room or a place, and there is going to be no line anywhere with my name on it that says I was here.”

She said, “I want that said out loud, because in five years I bought the best thing I have ever had with my own usefulness, and I am about to give it back for no reason at all, and there is not one hand in this building that is going to be able to write down that I was ever here, including the one I asked to.”
