# Six Days Of One Sum

She gave it six days and the sixth of them was the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,144, which is the day she has to say it out loud, and the reason she needed six is that she cannot carry eighty-one days in her head with one good hand and an arm that is open four days in five.

Five of the six days were spent on the sum. The sixth was going to be the morning she finished and the morning she said it, and she knew that on the first of the five and did not like it.

The five days were the ninth, the tenth, the eleventh, the twelfth and the thirteenth of the month beginning 2,144.

## The Instrument

She wrote on the back of the strip.

Not in the eight leaves against her ribs. Those are hers and they are blank and they are going up a stair inside a coat in nine years and they are not for arithmetic, and she worked out that rule in nine seconds on the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114 at the north end of a ground of two hundred people and it has not moved since.

The strip was the right instrument for three reasons and she said all three out loud on the ninth, at the first hour, standing, in daylight, before she put the nib on it.

It is bigger than the palm of her hand, so a line has room in it. It takes ink, though not the way county paper takes ink, because it was made by hand out of rags and it drinks a stroke and then it holds it. And there is nothing written on the other side of it, and there is nothing written on this side, and if she writes days and dates down it and no name and no place on it anywhere, then it is not a shape and it cannot be found by anything, and nothing is coming off it.

That last one is the reason she chose it and she said so.

She said, “There is no name on this and there is not going to be, and a thing with no name on it is not a thing anybody is on.”

And then she said the harder half of it, standing, at the second hour, to a man of twenty-five who had come to the frame to look at the board and was still there:

“If there were a name on it and a date and a place and some true things said about the person the name belongs to, then that would be a shape, and a shape goes where a shape goes, and a shape goes where a thing that keeps records can find it. And that place is a board nailed up at the north end of a ground that four carts go past a day. It is not a strip in a bag on a person.”

The man of twenty-five said, “That is a great deal of care to take with a piece of paper.”

She said, “It is nine inches long and it is the only thing anybody has ever sent me and I am not going to spoil it by putting my name on it.”

## The Ink

The cake was opened on the seventh of the month beginning 1,874 and it has never been let dry since, and she has been carrying it across nine days of road in a bag with a dish and a lump of clay in it, and she has used about a nail’s worth of it in three hundred days.

The nib is a stub. There is not enough of the point to make a small hand, so the hand on the strip is a big one, and it is the hand of somebody with four fingers and a thumb on it and it is the hand of a girl of nine, because the size of it has not changed in ten years.

She got sixteen lines a day on four of the five days.

On the fifth day her arm was open and she got eight, and the difference is not a matter of will and she has never in her life been able to make an open arm faster by wanting it to.

Sixty-four lines in four days and eight on the fifth is seventy-two, and she went back to it at the fourth hour of that fifth day and put eight more on it, and that is eighty, and the eighty-first was going to be tomorrow.

The writing is the only thing she has ever done with that hand that no one has ever checked, and she found that out on the second of the month beginning 2,144 at about the thirtieth line, when she put the nib down and looked at what she had done and it was a hand of nineteen years doing the letters of a girl of nine, and every letter of it was right.

There was one blot on the fifth line of the second day and it is still there and she is not going to take it out, because a hand that cannot write a false line can still make a blot, and a blot is not a false line, and the sum does not depend on it.

## What She Wrote Down

One line for every day from the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054 to the thirteenth of the month beginning 2,144.

The date on the left in a big hand. And on the right, a running figure, which is the number of days from the first line to that line, counted with the first day counted as the first.

That was the only decision in eighty lines and she spent a whole day of the sixth on it before she made it.

It could be that the day a person gets on a slate is counted, or it could be that the count begins the next morning. There is no rule in this county about it, and there is no person in this county who has ever had a figure like this, and so there is not one person she could ask, and so she took the rule she has counted everything else in her life by.

She said, at the ninth hour on the eleventh, standing, in daylight, at the north end of the rows, with the bar on the ground and the strip in her left hand:

“I count a thing with the first day as the first. I counted the days I have been lighter that way and it has been right every day of it, and I counted the days between the ninth stage and the first stage past it that way, and it is a hundred and thirty-one and not seventy. So I am counting this way and I am saying so in daylight before I do, because if I am wrong the whole of it is wrong and there is no one else in this county who can tell me.”

## The Sum

Eighty days, counted with the first day as the first, and the fraction this county takes off a column before a board goes up is one in twenty.

Eighty divided by twenty is four.

She had that at the ninth hour on the thirteenth of the month beginning 2,144 and she stood at the board with it and felt nothing at all, which she had expected and had not expected.

Then she did the other half, which is the half that took her four minutes and cost her the rest of the day.

Eighty-one divided by twenty is not four. It is four and a twentieth of a day, and a twentieth of a day is an hour and twelve minutes, because a day is twenty-four hours and not one of those hours has ever been a different length from the others on this earth.

So the figure was four days and one hour and twelve minutes, and there was one line left to write, and the line was for today, and it did not exist yet.

And then, because she has never in her life carried a figure she has not checked three ways, she did it three times, sitting on the stone at the head of the first row with the strip across her knees, in the dark, at the tenth hour.

Once by going up from the bottom. Once by going down from the top. And once by the short way, which is to take the span apart into months and divide each piece.

Seven days of the month beginning 2,054, from the twenty-fourth to the thirtieth. Thirty days of the month beginning 2,084. Thirty days of the month beginning 2,114. And thirteen days of this month. Seven and thirty and thirty and thirteen is eighty, and that is the whole of it.

Thirty days is a day and a half, and two of them is three days exactly.

Seven days out of twenty is eight hours and twenty-four minutes. Thirteen out of twenty is fifteen hours and thirty-six minutes.

She said, out loud, to the stone and the dark and the rows:

“Eight hours and twenty-four minutes and fifteen hours and thirty-six minutes is twenty-four hours, and twenty-four hours is one day, and one day and three days is four days.”

She said, “And four days is eighty out of twenty, and eighty is the number of lines on this paper, and it is the same answer by three roads, and forty minutes of my life to get it, which is what it costs me now.”

And then she looked at the paper for a count of twenty and did the last of it, which is the part that will cost her more than the other two.

One more line is one more day. Eighty-one out of twenty is four and a twentieth, and a twentieth of a day is an hour and twelve minutes.

So the figure she will say out loud in nine hours is four days and one hour and twelve minutes, and the figure on the paper at this moment is four days, and one line is between them, and that line is the day she says it on.

## What It Is Not

And then she stood at the frame at the tenth hour of the thirteenth, in the dark, with eighty lines on a strip of paper in her hand, and worked out the other thing, and it took nine seconds.

It is not a secret.

There is no craft in it. There is no key and no hand that knows it and no book it is in and no man in nine days of road who could not do it. It is a date, a day, and a fraction, and the fraction is one in twenty, and the fraction is the same on every ground in this county and has been since before she was born.

She said, standing, in the dark, at the north end of the rows:

“I have been carrying a figure about myself since I was nine years old that I thought was mine.”

She said, “And there is no such thing as mine about it. It is a subtraction. Any person in this ground who can count to eighty can have it this morning, and there are forty people in this ground who can count to eighty, and not one of them has ever thought to do it, and the reason none of them has ever thought to do it is that not one of them has ever been told that there is a figure.”

She said, “And I have just told them there is one.”

She stood there in the dark and found that she had done the thing she had decided on the eighth of the month beginning 2,144 not to do, and that the eighty-first line was still not written, and that it was due in nine hours.

## Why She Kept It Three Days

There is one more thing about those five days and it is the part she cannot check against anything either.

She had made a decision on the eighth of the month beginning 2,144 not to say it until she could say it to the minute, and the reason she made it was that a figure about a person in this county is the first half of being found, and she does not know the answer to a question she asked standing up on a floor.

And she stood at a frame on the ninth, and on the tenth, and on the eleventh, and on the twelfth, and on the thirteenth, with about a hundred people lying down about her in the dark, and she understood that the question had nothing to do with it.

She had said no first and then changed it on the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,114 and she had given the reason before she changed it and the reason was true, and she is not going to be one of those people who counts the have and lets the have-not go.

Not one person has ever told her that being right about the world is a reason to be quiet in it.

So the five days were not the decision. The five days were the arithmetic, and the arithmetic took five days, and she is going to say it at the ninth hour tomorrow in daylight because that is the only way a thing gets to exist in a place where two hundred people can hear it, and she has not got a better reason than that and she is not going to look for one.
