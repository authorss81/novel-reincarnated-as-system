# Saying The Total

She went in at the fourth hour of the thirtieth of the month beginning 2,084 and there were about a hundred people at twenty-two tables with a lamp on every one of them, and the sun was on the boards through the shutters, and the sheet was on the wall at the end of the counter with a total on it and seven lines under the total, and she stood under it.

## The Fourth Hour

She had had it for nine days.

She had had it since the sixth hour of the twenty-first and she had carried it out into a yard and west to a board at the north end of a ground with two hundred people in it and up two miles of flat and through a gap in a wall and back, and she had said it out loud to a wall and to a board at the north end and to the boards of a room with the shutters down, and not one living person had heard it.

That was new and she understood why on the morning of the thirtieth, standing at the gate with the bar at her feet in a count of twenty-nine.

She knows what an announcement is. She asked a question about it standing up on a floor with a boy of sixteen in it and she got half of an answer and the half was that an announcement is made to arrive, and that if there is a person in the place it arrives, and if there is not one it goes into the ground with nothing to say whether it did not.

She was not going to say a number out loud to a wall again.

## What She Said

She said it in the ordinary voice, standing, in daylight, at the fifth hour of the thirtieth of the month beginning 2,084, to a hundred people in a room with the shutters up.

She said the total first, because it was the larger one.

“Ninety-eight thousand coppers. That is the figure at the top of the sheet on this wall for the twelve months ending on the twenty-seventh of this month, and it is a keeper’s sheet and it is nailed up at the height of a person’s eyes and anybody who comes down that lane can read it.”

Then she said the lines, because she is a person who says the whole of a thing.

“Paper, quills and ink, four thousand five hundred and sixteen. Lamp oil and wick, one thousand six hundred and eighty. Firewood, two thousand four hundred. Food, three thousand five hundred and sixty. The counter and the four doors, three hundred and sixty. The rate at two coppers the page, eighty-one thousand nine hundred and sixty. The rate at four coppers the page, six hundred and four. Three rooms, furnished, nothing at all.”

Then she said the two numbers and left about nine counts in the room before the second one.

“Those seven lines add up to ninety-five thousand and eighty.”

“Ninety-five thousand and eighty is not ninety-eight thousand.”

And then, because she does not stop a thing halfway and she has not stopped a thing halfway in nine years:

“The difference is two thousand nine hundred and twenty coppers and it is eight coppers a day for every day of a year and there is no line on that wall for it.”

## What The Room Did

Nothing happened for about nine seconds and that is the part she will remember.

About a hundred people, twenty-two tables, a lamp on every one of them, and the sound of paper and breath and nothing else, and after she finished talking, the sound of paper and breath and nothing else.

Then a man at the ninth table said, “Is that the top number.”

She said, “That is the top number.”

A woman at the second table from the door said, “Somebody has had it.”

She said, “Somebody has had eight coppers a day for a year and there is no line on that wall with their name against it and I have not been able to find out who.”

And then the room started, and she stopped talking over it, and what it did was not the thing she had expected, which was nothing.

It was about forty people saying numbers.

## The Numbers People Said

They said numbers for about ten minutes.

A man at the fourth table said, “Two thousand nine hundred and twenty. That is a page a day for two and a half years at the hard rate.”

A woman said, “That is a page a day for about four years at two coppers.”

A man at the eleventh table said, “That is a man’s wage in this county for about nine years, if a man is paid anything here.”

A reader of about twenty-five said, “That is two months of what the whole floor earns,” and about four people told her she had it wrong, and she worked it out again on the back of a sheet and got it right the second time, and she got it right the second time in four seconds flat, and she said so out loud to the table.

And Ning Yan stood at the end of the counter with her right arm hanging and her bag at her feet and listened to a room do arithmetic.

Four bodies had been doing addition under a lamp at four different tables every hour of every day for forty-five years.

It took about ten minutes and a figure that was on a wall.

## What Two Hundred People Did With A Number

They did it for about ten minutes and then about four of them went and got the sheet.

A man of about sixty went to the wall with a stool and took the sheet off two nails at the north end and carried it to the fourth table and put it flat on the boards and four people looked at it. A woman of about fifty did the sum a fifth way and got two thousand eight hundred and sixty, which is wrong, and said so out loud, and then did it a sixth way and got the figure that was on the wall.

A man at the eleventh table said, out loud, to nobody, that he had been in this room thirty-one years and had never once added that wall up, and that he was not going to start now.

A reader of about twenty-five asked her whether she would do it again from the top.

She said, “Yes.”

She did it again from the top, standing at the fourth table in daylight with four people either side of her and about forty watching from the tables, and it took her nine seconds.

And then a woman she has never spoken to and does not know said, in the ordinary voice, at about the fortieth minute:

“You have done that for eight years and I have watched you do it.”

And Ning Yan said, “Yes.”

And the woman said, “All right,” and went back to her page, and that is the entire conversation, and it is the first time in her life that a person in a room has watched her do arithmetic and said nothing about it but *all right*, and she had to go out into the yard at that point and stand behind a cart for about a count of sixty.

## What She Asked Instead

She had one question and she had had it since the seventh hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,084, and she asked it at the seventh hour of the thirtieth, standing, in daylight, to a woman of sixty-three with her hands behind her back.

“What is the money for.”

One question. She does not ask two at once and has not in her life.

Xie Changyin did not take long and she did not pretend to need time.

“The building,” she said. “The counter, the lamps, the paper, the food, the rooms, and the rate. That is what it is for and that is what it says it is for, and it is on the wall in a plain hand and I did not write it and neither did he.”

She said, “And I am going to say the second part once, in the ordinary voice, and then I am going to stop, and you will not get it out of me again in this building or on this ground.”

She said, “I am not permitted to tell you what the money is for above the building. Not by the building. By the thing above the building, and I do not know who holds it, and I have been in this room forty-four years and I have never once been permitted.”

## What The Man With The Book Said

And then a man of sixty-five with a book under his arm said it, and she had not asked him, and she has thought about that for a long time since and has not worked out whether she asked him.

He said, “I have kept the book in this room for forty-six years. I have the folders behind me and the dates on the shelves go back further than I have been here.”

He said, “I have never seen two thousand nine hundred and twenty on any sheet in this building and I have never seen it on any sheet that came into this county in eleven hundred years, and I can read every hand that has come down that lane since the second year I was at this counter.”

He said, “So it is not a hand you have not seen.”

She said, “So it is not a sheet.”

He said, “It is not a sheet that came into this room.”

And then he said the sentence that is the whole of what she got out of that building:

“There is no hand in this building that owns that line. I have looked. I looked this afternoon, before you said it, because I have been in this room long enough to know what a wall with a total on it is, and I did not know either.”

## Why She Said It

She said why she said it at the ninth hour of the thirtieth of the month beginning 2,084, standing at the end of a counter, out loud, because she says things out loud standing up in daylight since she was nine years old.

“I said that number in a room with a hundred people in it and I can never take it back, and I did it on purpose, and I want it understood that I did it in daylight and standing and I said the whole of it including the part that makes a person in this building look as though they have been sitting on something.”

She said, “Not one person in this building has lied to me. Every rate in this room has been paid on the seventh and the seventeenth and the twenty-seventh into a fold in the inside of a coat, and it has been paid that way for forty-five years, and I have proved that both rate lines come out in whole pages, and not one reader in this room has ever been paid short.”

She said, “And a thing that is true and paid on time for forty-five years is still not the whole of it, and the part of it that is not the whole of it is a line between two numbers on a wall, and there is no hand in this building that owns that line, and I am nineteen years old and I do not have anywhere to put a thing like that.”
