# The Bar Without A Strap

She was two miles short of the wall at the second hour of the eighth of the month beginning 2,084 and she had been on the flat since the fourth hour, and the flat is two miles long and there is nothing on it to lean on, nothing to put a bag down behind, and nothing to lie down on between the gate and the wall, which is a thing she had been told about by a county that had no reason to think about it.

## Six Days On A Made Road

She went off the turn at the first hour of the second of the month beginning 2,084 with the bar nine feet nine on a strap out of her own coat lining and she had said the night before, out loud, standing at the foot of two posts, that she was going east in the morning, and she went, and there is not one thing to report about the first two hours of the made road except that it goes east and that there is a bar on it.

The six days are two miles and a bit a day, and the two miles and a bit is the whole of what six days east of a turn did to her.

She can carry a bar nine feet nine on her left shoulder for about three hours. After that the shoulder goes and the strap cuts across the coat at the shoulder seam and the place where it cuts is the place that tells her when to stop, and she has been stopping at that place since she was sixteen years old. Two miles and a bit is three hours and about forty minutes of it on a good surface and it is four hours on a made road with a strap that is older than the road.

## What Six Days Took Off Her

The strap went at the second hour of the fifth of the month beginning 2,084, which is the fourth of the six days, six miles east of the turn.

It did not go all at once. It went the way a thing goes that has been holding for a long time, which is that it held for nine hours on the second day and fourteen on the third and twenty-two on the fourth, and then on the fourth, at the second hour, it parted at the stitch she had put in it herself four years back with a needle she does not have now, and a bar nine feet nine went down into the dust of a made road on her own side and lay there.

She stood on the made road and looked at it.

Then she got it up.

That is the part worth having. A bar nine feet nine weighs what it weighs and she has four fingers and a thumb on the right hand and the right arm is open four days in five, and there was a day in the six when the arm was open, and she got the bar up into her right hand and carried it two miles and a half that way with the fingers she has got closed round it and the arm she could not shut.

She has put down barrows with two hands for nine years. She has never once picked up nine feet nine off the ground with one hand and she did it twice that day and once on the morning of the fifth.

And then she could not put it back on her shoulder, because the strap was gone and the coat lining it came out of was torn in a strip four fingers wide, and the lining is the only thing on that bar that has ever held it against her body.

## What Two Days Of That Cost

Two days, and here is what came off her and what did not.

Her right arm was open on the sixth and on the seventh of the six days. It is open four days in five and it is open on the arm that was carrying. So on three days out of six she carried a bar nine feet nine in a hand with four fingers on it, on an arm that would not shut, for eleven miles.

What came off her was this: she cannot get the bar onto her shoulder any more. She can put it on. She cannot put it up, and she cannot take it off, and it takes her the length of twenty counts to get it from the ground to her shoulder now and about the same again the other way, and she does the twenty counts at the eighth hour and at the fourth hour and at whatever hour she stops.

On the morning of the seventh, at the gate, it took her a count of fifty-four.

She said that out loud, standing in the grit at a gate with a bar on the ground at her feet, at the eighth hour of the seventh of the month beginning 2,084, because she says figures out loud and has said them out loud since she was nine.

A man went west on the road above the gate with a load of something and did not turn his head.

## The Flat

The gate is two miles short of the wall and between them there is a made road that stops at the gate and then two miles of flat that was made by no one, and the flat is hard, and pale, and the colour of a floor, and there is not a stone on it the size of a fist.

She crossed it at the fourth hour and arrived at the wall at the second hour of the next day, having slept on it for one night, in the way she sleeps, which is not sleeping.

There is nothing to lie down on. She has said that as a fact before and this is where she found out what it costs: it costs a shoulder. A bar nine feet nine has to go down on the flat and a shoulder that has carried a bar since she was sixteen does not like being told about twice in two days.

## The Wall

It is standing open four feet and a bit.

She went through it at the second hour of the eighth of the month beginning 2,084 with the bar in her right hand, and she had been walking toward that four-foot gap for years, and behind it there is a flat.

There is a fence on the north side that goes on for a mile and then stops.

There is a row of stones on the south side, set out from the wall at intervals.

She did the intervals standing at the fourth stone, in daylight, with the bar on the ground, because she cannot help it and she has never in her life been able to walk past a row of anything without knowing the gap.

The gaps are not equal. The first three are the same and the fourth is short and the fifth is long and after that she stopped counting because she had nine years of counting in her and it was going to take the rest of the daylight and she had a bar to get onto a shoulder.

She has never worked out who set them out or for what, and she is not going to work it out on the sixth day of a walk with one hand on a bar.

What she wrote down in her head, standing there, was this: the fence stops and the stones go on.

## Into The Rows

The rows are past the wall. She did not count how far past the wall they are, and she noticed herself not counting, which is not a thing she does.

She went in at the fourth hour and she was in there for about two hours and she came out.

There are two hundred people in that ground and a stone at the head of each row and a board at the north end of it, and on the board there is a stroke and a number under the stroke, four of them, one for four men, and there has been one every day of it for nine years.

That is what a count is. She stood in front of it and understood it the way you understand a thing you have been looking at from the outside of a fence for two years.

A man does not come to the north end of a board in the evening. A man comes in the morning with a good arm and a bad arm and does his day’s work, and in the evening somebody who is not him writes a stroke and a number, and the number is days, and the stroke is a day, and it is the same figure every evening for nine years for four men, and the whole of the world the men in that ground live in is a wall with four numbers on it and a woman at a counter in a building where she is not counted at all.

## The Figure She Kept

She had a figure in her head. She has had it since the first of the month beginning 2,084 and it has been sitting in her since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 and there is not one piece of paper in the world with any part of it on.

Five hundred and thirty-one days. The figure at any day is the day less one thousand five hundred and sixty, and she has not had to work it out since she was twelve years old, and on the eighth of the month beginning 2,084 the figure is five hundred and thirty-one.

She stood at the north end of the rows at the sixth hour with two hundred people about ten feet apart doing what they do in a ground, and she worked the whole thing out loud in her own head in nine seconds, and she did not say it.

Here is why, and she put it to herself standing there, and it is the reason and not a reason she had ready:

She asked a question in daylight on a floor with a boy of sixteen in the room, on the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,034, standing up, one question, and the question was whether a person can be announced without being designated, and she has half an answer and cannot un-ask it.

A number said out loud in a ground with two hundred people in it is an announcement.

She has been announced nine times. Every one of the nine went into the ground with nothing on it, because there was nothing above the line to receive it, and she has not worked out what happens when there are two hundred people standing in a ground who can receive one.

She looked at the board at the north end with a stroke and a number under it for four men and she did not say a number out loud in that ground, and it was the first thing in six days that she did not do, and she has thought about it for two hours since.

The bar went onto her shoulder at the seventh hour in a count of twenty-eight, which is better than fifty-four, and she walked back out through the gap in the wall and down the two miles of flat and put the bar down on the flat at the ninth hour and could not pick it up for twenty counts, and slept on it the way she sleeps.

## The Thing She Has To Work Out

It is twenty-three days to the first of the month beginning 2,114. It is sixteen days to the twenty-fourth of this month, which is the day a man of forty-nine comes out to a frame at a turn twenty miles west of here with a stick of white chalk.

She is going to be twenty miles east of that turn on the twenty-fourth.

She has never missed a day of a column in her life and she has been on one for two months and she is about to be twenty miles east of a frame when a hand comes to it, and there is not one thing she can do about it.

And she has a figure in her head that not one person on this ground is going to be told, and she has not said it, and she has to go and work out what takes a fifth.
