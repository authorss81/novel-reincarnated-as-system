# The Reason For Two Posts

She stood in front of the two posts at the sixth hour of the twentieth of the month beginning 2,054, four hundred yards east of the turn, in daylight, on made grit, with the bar on the ground against her left boot, and she worked out what a board is for, out loud, on her own, in front of four people who came west in the morning and went east in the afternoon and who did not stop and did not leave.

She had told a woman of sixty on a bench that she was going to do it, and a bench is a person, and she had said it on purpose, and that was the price of saying it.

The frame is two posts of a good hard wood and a bar between them at the height of her chest, and a slate let into the lower half of the bar with a lip of wood round it to keep the rain off, and the slate has a groove round three of its sides where a thing is wiped off it, and it is clean, and it has been clean for nine years.

## What A Notice Is

She said, out loud, standing, in daylight:

“A board is a notice. It is not a wage and it is not a roof and it is not a hand on anybody’s back. It is a notice, and it is for everybody who comes along this road and not for the person it is about.”

She said, “And I have been standing in front of it for nine years believing that the only thing a board does is pay a person, and that is the one thing a board does not do.”

She said, “It pays a person out of what everybody else has been told. That is the whole of the payment. You are paid because four hundred people now know where you are.”

She said, “And that is the only reason anybody in this world has ever been paid for reading a board.”

She said, “Not because the board is good. Because the board is seen.”

## What The Fraction Comes Off

Then she was quiet for about a minute, because the second half of it is the half she has been carrying for four years and had never once put next to the first half.

She said, out loud, on a road, in daylight, with four people going past:

“The levy is one in twenty, counted in days, and it comes off a board before the board goes up.”

She said, “I have said that sentence out loud on a furnace ground in front of thirteen people and in a yard and in a room, and I have never once said it in the same breath as the sentence I have just said.”

She said, “So here it is, both of them, together, in one place, where they have never been together before.”

She said, “A board is the only thing that anybody in this world has ever paid me for.”

She said, “And a board is the only thing the fraction comes off.”

She said, “And they are the same object. Not two objects with one between them. One object. Four hundred yards east of this turn.”

## The Figure She Computed Herself

She did the arithmetic standing at the two posts in the grit, out loud, in daylight, the way she has done every figure in her life since she was nine years old.

She said, “One in twenty.”

She said, “I have been lighter five hundred and ten days this morning, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back.”

She said, “One in twenty of five hundred and ten is twenty-five and a half days. And at four hundred and twenty days, four years ago, I divided it by twenty in front of a gate and got twenty-one, and I sat in the grit with my mouth open because it came out exact.”

She said, “It comes out exact every time. That is what I am for. That is the whole of what a thing made to find people like me is for, and it is not a punishment and it is not a mistake, it is arithmetic, and the figure was chosen by somebody.”

She said, “And here is the part I have not put together in four years.”

She said, “The only reason the sum has not come off me is that I have never been on a board. Not once. Nine years of boards and one hundred and fifty miles in thirteen days and not one first of a month with my days in a column on a piece of wood.”

She said, “The thing that kept me alive is the thing I have been telling people was a humiliation.”

She stood at the foot of the two posts and did not say anything for about half a minute.

She said, “And I am going to get on this one.”

## What The Hand On The Slate Is Like

The groove round three of the sides of the slate is the width of a thumb, because a thumb is what a man puts round a slate to hold it while he wipes it, and the fourth side is not grooved because that is the side a hand rests on.

She put her own thumb in it. It fitted. She did that three times.

Then she took the stub of the nib out of the front of her bag and the cake of ink out beside it, and she set them on the lip of the frame with the slate under them, and she did not write.

She stood there with a nib and a bit of ink and a slate in a frame four hundred yards east of a turn for about two minutes, and the two minutes are the whole of what the day was for.

She said, out loud, to the frame, in daylight:

“I cannot write a false line. That is the rule on this hand and it has been on it since I was ten and I have not been able to get round it once.”

She said, “So the first thing that goes on this slate has to be true, and it is going to be true whatever it costs, and I am not going to find a way of making it come out true.”

She said, “And if I find out I cannot put my own name on it, then that is the answer too and I will have spent two days finding out that a thing I can do on a bar in a yard I cannot do on a piece of wood in a frame, and I will go and tell a woman of sixty on a bench that I tried.”

She put the nib back in the bag and the ink back beside it.

She said, “Not today. I have got two days and there is a man coming on the twenty-fourth and he is going to write with a hand I have never seen and he is going to ask me a question, and I am not going to know what I look like on a slate until I have watched one line of somebody else’s writing go into it.”

## The Reason She Is Not Afraid Of It

A cart came west at the fourth hour and a man with a dog went east at the fifth, and neither of them looked at the two posts.

She said, out loud, in the grit, at the sixth hour, to the slate:

“I have been afraid of one thing since I was ten years old and it is not a man and it is not a county and it is not a figure. It is being seen.”

She said, “And I have spent nine years arranging not to be seen, and it is the best arrangement I have ever made and it has cost me a name in two hands and a place to sleep and wages and a brother, and every one of those was a payment for the arrangement.”

She said, “And it worked. I am alive at nineteen. I have been lighter for five hundred and ten days and not one of them has gone back and not one of them has been taken off a board, because there has not been a board.”

She said, “And the arrangement is the reason I do not know whether my brother is alive. I could have found out in one day if I had gone and stood on a road where anybody could see me and asked a person to send word. And I could not be seen. And so I have not asked.”

She said, “That is what not being seen costs and I have been paying it every day since I was ten years old and I have called it being careful.”

## What She Said That Cannot Be Said Back

A carter came west at the seventh hour and went past. A woman came east with a bucket at the eighth. Two men came west with a handcart and one of them said “Morning” to her and she said “Morning” back, and neither of them looked at the two posts, because there is nothing on them.

She said, out loud, on the grit, at the ninth hour, with the bar back on her shoulder:

“I am going to say the second part of this out loud because I am not going to have it in my head on my own, and because a thing that is only in my head is the thing I have been living on for ten years.”

She said, “I am going to get on this board. And when I do, the only thing that changes about me is that a fraction of a number can now be taken off me by somebody who has never met me, off a piece of wood, before it goes up, in a column I cannot see and cannot stop.”

She said, “And the reason I am going to do it is that everything I have found out in the last six days says a person is announced where there is something to receive it, and there is a room four miles up a mountain with a long table in it and not one thing on the table, and the way to be in that room is to be found out by somebody, and the only way in this world to be found out is to be on a board.”

She said, “So the thing I have wanted for nine years and the thing that is going to take a piece of me are the same piece of wood, and I have known that since the seventeenth of a month four years ago and I have been carrying the two halves of it in different pockets.”

She said, “That is what a board is for. It is the thing that puts you where you want to be and the thing that lets the harvest find you, and it is one object, and there is not a second thing anywhere in this world that does both.”

She said, “And I am going to say it here, out loud, in daylight, at the foot of these two posts, because I said on a bench that I would and because a bench is a person and a person can be asked about it afterwards.”

Then she said, “And that is it. There is nothing else on this road. I have got three days and I am going to use two of them.”

She said, “I am going to stand here tomorrow and the day after and I am going to learn the hand that is on this frame before the twenty-fourth, because a man comes out here on the twenty-fourth and takes in what is on the slate, and I am not going to have him ask me what my name is when a hand I have never seen is holding a thing.”
