# What A Voice Cannot Be Checked Against

She spent three days at the north end of the rows in which nothing went wrong, and she has been more frightened of an arrangement in her life than she has been of a wall.

That is not a figure. It is the plainest thing in three days and she worked it out standing up on the second evening with a bar on the ground at her feet and a hundred people lying down forty feet off.

She had been asked for one thing in nine days of road and it was a question, and she had given it, and it had cost her nothing at all and she had been proud of that, and she had not once sat in a ground of two hundred people and let a day go past without checking it.

Three days is nothing. She has walked a hundred and fifty miles in thirteen days and slept on floors and eaten one loaf a day since the sixth of the month beginning 2,054, and three days is a morning and an afternoon and a night three times over.

And she cannot get through one of them without the ground being wrong somewhere in it.

## The Twenty-Eighth

The twenty-eighth of the month beginning 2,114 is day two thousand one hundred and forty-one, and she was at the board at the first hour, and he read back one day.

That is the shape of it now. It takes a count of forty and it happens at the first hour at the north end of the rows in daylight, and the four men come at the second hour with the barrows, and two hundred people do what two hundred people do.

What she could not get used to was how ordinary it had become.

On the twenty-first, when the permission was given, she had stood in the middle of that ground and heard the whole of her own life put back to her in a count of two hundred and had not been able to say one word at the end of it.

Now it was a count of forty and it was in the morning and there was a woman of forty at the seventh row who wanted to know where her own stone was and the man in the eleventh row asking the man in the tenth row something about a barrow.

A thing that big does not get to stay big. She knew that at nineteen and she had known it at twelve, in a shed at a mill, about a sheet of paper.

## The Twenty-Ninth, Before She Set Him

What she did on the twenty-ninth, before she said the false thing, was wait for it.

That is the part that took the whole morning. She stood at the frame from the first hour to the second and she waited for him to put one word down that was not true, on purpose, out loud, on a matter of no consequence whatever, so that she could catch it and be finished.

She gave him three. The weather at the ninth hour of the twenty-seighth. The number of barrows that go up and down that ground in a day. Whether the woman at the seventh row had a sister.

All three of them were small and none of them mattered and she gave them in the ordinary voice at the board and she listened to every word of the reading-back with her thumb on the frame.

Every one of them was true. He had been at the north end of that ground when the weather turned on the twenty-eighth. He had counted the barrows, which nobody in this ground counts, and had got nineteen. And the woman at the seventh row had a sister, and he knew that because she had said so in front of him on some day in the last month, and he said where, and she remembered her saying it.

She had three chances in a morning to catch him and she did not take one.

And she understood, standing at a frame at the second hour of the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 2,114 with her arm hanging and the light coming hard off the rise, that she had not taken one on purpose, and that wanting him to be wrong had moved out of her somewhere between the first one and the third one, and that she had not noticed it go.

## What She Set Him

She has spent nine years looking for a false line. It is the only hunting she has ever done and it is the reason there is eight coppers in a drawer four miles west of a square, and there has not been a false line in nine years that she has not caught, and she built an entire life on the catching.

So on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 2,114 she went looking for one in a place that had not been searched.

She said, standing, in daylight, at the north end of the rows, in the ordinary voice: “My arm is closed today.”

It was the second hour. Her right arm was open, the way it is open four days in five, and it had been open since the fifth hour of the morning, and the sleeve of her coat was hanging off it.

She said it and then she stopped and waited for what came back.

He said, “Say it again, and I will put it down as you said it.”

She said, “My arm is closed today.”

He said, “Do you want it in as what you said, or as what is the case.”

She said, “You do not decide anything.”

He said, “I said that back to you nine seconds ago. Say the answer and I will put it in.”

She stood there and felt how easy it would have been to say the other one. To say, put it down as what is the case.

That is one sentence and it would have been the end of the whole business, and it would have put a hand on the record, and she gave him six terms at the third hour of the twenty-first in front of two hundred people and the sixth of them is that he does not decide anything.

She said, “Put it down as what I said.”

## The Hole She Left In It

That is the whole of what happened on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 2,114 in a count of sixty, and she has been at it every day since.

The sixth term says it is a record and not a hand, that he does not decide anything, that she decides everything.

And a record that is only what she said cannot tell the difference between a true sentence and a false one, because she said both of them with the same mouth in the same voice, and there is no mark on either.

And a record that is only what is the case is a record with a hand on it, and a hand decides, and the sixth term says he does not decide anything.

So he put down what she said.

That is correct. That is the only thing he was permitted to do and he did it and it took eleven words, and they are in a voice in her head and not on anything.

## What It Costs To Be Believed

She stood there and looked at the ground for a while.

Five terms. One at a time. In daylight. Standing up. Said out loud before it is a line anywhere else. And a false line and she knows it inside a day and there is no road back for either of them.

The fifth term is the one she is going to have to live inside for the rest of this.

She said, standing, at the ninth hour on the twenty-ninth, to the two hundred rows and the board and the four men and the man inside her head:

“I told him a false thing at the second hour of this afternoon on purpose, and he has written it down true, because the only true thing he can write is what I said, and I said it.”

She said, “And he cannot check it against a page because there is no page, and I cannot check it either, because the page would have to be in a voice, and it is in a voice.”

She said, “So that term cannot be enforced. Not by him and not by me. I said it standing up in daylight in front of two hundred people and I could not carry it, and there is no seventh term, because a document agreed to in daylight is not a document that can be added to.”

## What She Wants

Then she stood at the frame and let the rest of it come up, and what came up was not the terms at all.

What she wanted, at the ninth hour on the twenty-ninth, with the light going off the slate and forty people lying down behind her, was for him to be wrong.

Not on anything. She does not care about the weather and she has not cared about the barrows since she was nine years old. She wanted one day in which the reading at the first hour would have nine words in it that were not so, so that she could put her hand on it and say, there, that is the instrument I have got, and get a night’s sleep out of it.

She has not slept eight hours in one place since she was nine years old and she sleeps four or five and she does not mind it and it has never once cost her a day.

And there is no instrument. That is the whole of it and it is the price of the six terms and she put the price on herself at the third hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,054, standing up, on purpose, with her eyes open.

## The Thirtieth

On the thirtieth of the month beginning 2,114 she opened her arm on purpose and said so, out loud, standing, in daylight, in front of two hundred people, and then she asked him to read the twenty-ninth back.

He read it back. She said at the second hour that her arm was closed.

She said, “That is what happened at the second hour. My arm was open and it had been open since the fifth hour of the morning and I told him the opposite on purpose to see what he would do with it.”

The ground was doing what the ground does. One of the four men looked up for a count of four and went back to a barrow.

She said, “Now it is true again, and it is true in three places instead of one, and it took me a whole day to put it right and it took him one sentence.”

Then she did the arithmetic, standing at the board with her thumb on the frame of it, because she cannot stop doing that, and the number has not changed since the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114 and it is not going to change on a first of a month any more than it has on the last four.

Five hundred and eighty-three days. Not one of them gone back. And every day of it hers, and not one day of it in a fold in a coat or on a slate or in a book, and she is nineteen years old and she has not been hungry for a long time and she is the lightest she has ever been.

## What She Keeps Doing

And on the evening of the thirtieth she did the thing she does, which is count, and there was nothing to stop her.

The four men write a stroke and a number under it in the evening, one each, four a night, and they have done it for nine years. Her line went in on the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,114 and there have been sixteen nights since that day, and sixteen times four is sixty-four.

She counted them at the ninth hour with the light going and got sixty-four, and there was one stroke too many at the top because the man who had the second hour had put his in early, and she left it.

Sixty-four lines on a board and not one of the four men can read the one that is hers.

The first of the month beginning 2,144 is at the first hour tomorrow and she is two days of road east of the frame where it goes up.
