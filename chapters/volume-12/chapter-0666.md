# Four Floors And No Answer

She spent the whole of the thirteenth of the month beginning 2,054 in the copying room and it produced nothing, and the nothing was the point of the day, and she had not known that on the twelfth when she went to sleep on the floor with the question still sitting in the room.

She was at the fourth hour on the floor. She was at the sixth hour at the bench with the book of eight leaves open to five blank ones. She was at the ninth hour at the window with her arms folded, counting what a boy of sixteen did in a day and finding out that she could not help with any of it.

## A Day That Produced Nothing

A page read alone at the fourth hour goes into the person who reads it, and she has known that since she was twelve and it is the reason for every rule she works by.

A page read out loud in daylight in front of one other person goes into both of them.

And on the thirteenth she had no page at all. The four sheets were in a drawer four feet away and she was not going to open the drawer in a day when she had asked a question, because a woman who opens a dead man’s handwriting in the same room as a question has stopped being a person who asked and become a person who wanted something.

So she sat in a lit room all day with nothing to do in it, and that is a thing she has not had to do since she was about nine.

## The Rack And What Is On It

The boy worked at the rack from the sixth hour, and she read over his shoulder because that is allowed and has always been allowed, and she has been reading his hand since she was fourteen.

Nine sheets a day. He did nine sheets on the thirteenth and she checked three of them against the pile and they were right, and the hand was better than hers and has been better than hers for four years.

And on four of the nine there was a date at the top, and the dates were all the first of a month.

She said, at the ninth hour, standing at the rack with her finger not touching the sheet, “Every one of these is for the first of a month.”

He said, “Yes.”

She said, “Nine sheets and four dates and the other five have no date.”

He said, “The other five are for the standing orders. Those go up the same day every quarter and they do not need a day written on them because everybody knows the day.”

She said, “And when the first comes, what happens to the five that have a date on them.”

He said, “They go up. On the first. At the first hour, in daylight, in a bundle, on an arm.”

She said, “And the nine days before the first, they are sitting on a rack in a tower.”

He said, “They are sitting on a rack in a tower for nine days and then they are said.”

## A Full Day’s Work Done By One Boy

She looked at the pile at the side of the room, and the pile was what he had done for the next nine days, and the pile was about a foot and a half high, and it had been a foot and a half high on the twelfth and it was not going to be any lower on the fourteenth.

She said, “That is nine days of work.”

He said, “It is nine days of work.”

She said, “And there is one of you.”

He said, “There was two of us until the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,024 and there has been one of me since.”

She said, “How long have you been at nine sheets a day.”

He said, “Since the twenty-fourth.”

She did the arithmetic standing at the rack, which is a thing she cannot help, and it came out where it always comes out: nineteen days of a boy of sixteen doing two days of work in one, on four counts of breath an hour less than the rest of the building, in a building four miles up a mountain where there is nothing to lie down on at the bottom and nothing to sit on at the top.

And she could not take a sheet off the pile.

That is the whole of the day and it is the thing that stood in it. She could not take a sheet off the pile and do it, because the moment her hand is on a county sheet her hand is in a county column, and a girl with a bar on her shoulder has no standing whatever in this building, and she said that out loud to a boy of sixteen at the ninth hour of a day that produced nothing.

She said, “I could do four of those in an hour.”

He said, “I know.”

She said, “And if I did it then there would be a hand in this county’s column that is not a keeper and not a boy and not a man, and it would be a hand with three fingers and a thumb on it and a missing index, and somebody would see it inside a year, and then there would be a question about me in this room and I am not going to be in this room when it comes.”

He said, “Then do not do it.”

She said, “No.”

## Three Of The Nine

She read all nine of them again on the second pass and she read them because she has read boards since she was twelve and she cannot stop herself, and because a day with nothing in it is the only kind of day she has ever been given twice.

The first was a road. Two hundred yards of the made road out of a town where the crown had gone, forty days of labour at the rate the rate, and every man who sent a man to it would have the days written against his name at the end of it.

The second was a gate. A gate on a boundary would stand shut from the fourth hour of the twenty-second until the first hour of the first of the next month, and the reason was frost, and there was nothing in it about anybody.

The third was six names.

Six names and, under each one, a number of days, and the numbers were four, nine, eleven, four, seven and nine, and the sum of them was forty-four, and she stood at a rack in a tower four miles up a mountain and added up a stranger’s days because that is what she is and she cannot stop it.

Under the six names there was a line that said the days would be paid at the office on the seventh and the seventeenth and the twenty-seventh, and a second line that said any man who wished to send a substitute should say so in writing before the first.

She said, out loud, at the rack, on the thirteenth of the month beginning 2,054, to the shelves:

“Six men. Forty-four days. Paid on the seventh, the seventeenth and the twenty-seventh. And a man may send another man in his place if he says so in writing and before the first.”

She said, “And nobody has told any of those six men what the sum is.”

She said, “And every one of the six can be found at the office on the seventh, the seventeenth and the twenty-seventh with his days, and he will be paid the rate, and he will go home.”

She stood there for a while.

She said, “That is what this building is for. Not for the sky and not for a road and not for a thing up a stair. It is for six men and forty-four days and a rate and a date, and it has been that for nineteen years, and it is very good at it.”

She said, “And not one of the six has ever had the sum read out to him.”

## The Price Of Not Asking Twice

She went down four flights at the ninth hour with the can of oil on her shoulder and the boy behind her, and she was at seven on the third landing for the length of it, and at the bottom she stood in the cold for a minute and a half.

Then she went back up.

She said, standing on the landing above the copying floor, with the lamp above her and the drawer four feet inside the room behind her:

“I have not asked him a second thing. That was yesterday. Today I have not asked him a second thing and there is a difference in it and it is not a small one, and I want it said out loud in this stair because it is the whole of what I have got.”

He said, “Say it.”

She said, “Yesterday I asked and I got half. Today I have had nothing and I am not going to go and take it out of him by asking again, and the reason I am not is that the moment I ask twice, a question stops being a thing I put into the world and becomes a thing I pull, and I do not pull.”

She said, “And the price of that is that I am standing on a stair in a building at the ninth hour with nothing to do for nine days, and he cannot come down here and get the second half for me, and he has told me he cannot, and I have believed him, and believing him is the thing I have to live inside for nine days.”

She said, “That is the price and I knew the price when I set the terms at the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,024 and I am not going to be a person who finds out what a term costs only when it is expensive.”

She went up the stair to the yard and out under the eaves, and the rain had stopped while she was inside, and the whole of the mountain and the four miles of made road and the mill with the wheel that had not turned in nine years were lying out under a sky that had not said one word to anybody in nineteen years.

She said, out loud, at the top of the track, at the fourth hour, standing, in the dark, to a boy of sixteen who had come out of a tower to put a cover over the lamp he had not been told to keep:

“Nine days. And then I go up those four flights whatever is at the top of them, and if there is nothing at the top of them then I have had nine days of nothing and I will have asked a question about the world in a room with a lamp in it, and both of those are mine and neither of them can be given back.”
