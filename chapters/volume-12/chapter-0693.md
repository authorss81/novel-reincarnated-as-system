# The Square In The Middle Of It

She gave the strip three days and on the third of them she knew what was on it and what was not, and neither of those was a thing she can use.

The three days were the second, the third and the fourth of the month beginning 2,144, day two thousand one hundred and forty-five to day two thousand one hundred and forty-seven.

## Paper

She has read paper since she was twelve years old. That is not a figure of speech. A county hand is a hand and paper is paper and a person who is paid to read a board for eight coppers a day learns in a year what a sheet is made of, because the getting of the reading is four-fifths of the job.

On the second day she held the strip up at arm’s length in the daylight at the north end of the rows and looked through it.

County paper comes off a press. It has chain lines in it about the width of a thumb apart and laid lines finer than she can count, and it has a deckle on two sides and a cut on the other two, and a man at the county seat reads four hundred sheets a year and can tell you which mill it came from by the chain lines alone.

This had none of it.

There were no chain lines. There were no laid lines. The fibre was long and it went every way at once and it was the fibre of a sheet somebody made out of rags in a vat with their hands, and the two long edges were cut with a knife and not torn, and the fourth edge had been torn for half an inch at one corner and stopped as though somebody had thought better of it.

She said, standing, at the third hour of the second, to a barrow going past:

“That is not county paper and there is not one sheet of county paper in this county with a torn corner.”

And then she did what she does, which is to go and get a second opinion out of the thing itself rather than out of a person, and it took the rest of the day and the whole of the next one.

## What A Sheet Is

She has read nine hundred sheets in her life. She was sixteen and she read a hand in four counties for eight coppers a page and a second rate for a hand that could not be made out by anybody else, and she has not been paid for a sheet since she was fifteen and cannot be, and there is not one instrument in this county that will pay her for one and choose to instead.

That is the trade she is in. Everything else she does — nine days of road, a year at a mill, a winter at a kiln, a tower with four hundred volumes in it — is something she did inside that trade.

A person who reads a hand for eight coppers a day learns the paper inside a year, because the paper is where the lies are. A hand can be copied. A sheet can be written in a hurry. Paper cannot be hurried.

County paper comes off a press nine miles down the road at the seat and it costs a ninth of what her labour costs and it is the same in every county because the same four people own the mill that makes it.

County paper has chain lines in it about the width of a thumb apart and laid lines finer than she can count, and it has a deckle on two sides and a knife edge on the other two, and it is wove on a frame and it is sized so that a nib will sit on it and not run, and it comes in reams of five hundred sheets tied with string.

This had none of it.

There were no chain lines. There were no laid lines. The fibre was long and it went every way at once and it was the fibre of a sheet somebody made out of rags in a vat with their hands, and the two long edges were cut with a knife and not torn, and the fourth edge had been torn for half an inch at one corner and stopped as though somebody had thought better of it.

A sheet made by hand is nine days of work and there are not nine people in this county who know how to make one.

## The Square

The square in the middle of it is the size of the flat of her hand.

That is four inches by four and it is darker than the rest of the paper and it is not wet and it has not dried. The fibres in it are pressed flat and the edges of it are sharp, four straight edges, and it is not a stain. A stain goes in and spreads. This was pressed.

And it is not ink and it is not grease. She put the corner of her thumb on it and her thumb came away dry.

And she did the other half of the test, which is that she wet a corner of it with the damp off the bottom of her own thumb after a night, because ink will lift off hand-made paper in a way it will not lift off sized paper, and the corner came up grey and stringy and it was not ink.

Then she held it under her nose at the seventh hour in the shade of the frame and she got the smell, and the smell was cold iron and old smoke and paper that has been shut up a long time in a room with no window open, and she has smelled that smell exactly twice in her life and both times were four flights above a copying floor.

## The Weight

Paper that size weighs nothing. She knows what paper weighs because she has carried boards and sheets for nine years and she can feel a pound of paper in a bag at nine feet.

This was heavier than it looked and she could feel it in the hand, and she worked out how she could prove it and there was no way to prove it, because the only thing she has to weigh it against is a lump of clay and a ring a finger’s width and two coppers she cannot count.

So she did the next best thing, which is that she counted the days it had been shut up by the state of it, and the answer is that she could not, and that the crease had been opened and folded again more than once by somebody who was in no hurry about it.

And then on the third of the month beginning 2,144 she found a way.

A bar nine feet nine with no strap on it will balance across a finger if you know where the weight is in it, and a ring a finger’s width will balance the other way, and a lump of clay will go down at the end of it like a plumb. She has been carrying those three things in a bag for nine years and she has never once used them for anything and there is no reason in this world why she should know how.

She put the strip on the bar where the balance is and the ring on it and moved the ring along until the bar came level, and then she put her thumb where the ring had been and did it again, and the two marks on the paper were four tenths of an inch apart.

Nine inches of hand-made paper, four tenths of a inch thick at one place and rather less at the rest.

That is heavier than county paper of that size by a third, and she said so out loud at the sixth hour on the third, standing, in daylight, at the north end of the rows, and she did not say it to anybody, and she has never said that figure to a person in her life.

## What It Is

She said it out loud on the fourth of the month beginning 2,144, at the eighth hour, standing, in daylight, at the north end of two hundred rows of stones, and she said it to the board because the board is the only thing in nine days of road that has ever taken a thing she said.

“A bundle is a square of oilcloth with a weight in the middle of it. That is what a boy of sixteen read off a carter of sixty-four at the bottom of a stair, and he has carried four of them up that stair in thirty-one years, and on each of the four a man came out of a low door and took his hat off.”

She said, “And this is nine inches of paper with a square of oilcloth pressed into the middle of it and nothing written on either side, and it came off the top of that stair, and not one thing that comes off the top of that stair comes down into this county.”

She said, “And the carter says the things that come down that hill are county paper and go to the county seat and are read by four clerks and put in a shelf.”

She said, “And this is nine inches of hand-made paper with a square of oilcloth pressed into the middle of it and nothing written on either side, and it is a third heavier than county paper of its size, and it came off the top of that stair, and not one thing that comes off the top of that stair comes down into this county.”

And then she sat down on the stone at the head of the first row, because there is a stone at the head of every row and she has never once sat on one in nine years of coming to this ground, and she went over it again the way she goes over a sum.

A bundle is a square of oilcloth with a weight in the middle of it. A man has carried four of them up a stair in thirty-one years and on each of the four a man came out of a low door and took his hat off and he did not see one inch past the man into the room.

Whatever a bundle is, it is not county paper. A bundle is what a thing is put in when it is going to be said.

And a thing that is going to be said does not come back down in a cart to a ground of two hundred people with nothing written on it.

## No Form For It

And then she did the arithmetic of it, and it took nine seconds, and she has been carrying it since.

The strip is the only thing she has ever had come to her from above the line, and she cannot be given a shape, and a shape is a date and a place and some true statements about a person in a hand that a thing which keeps records can find, and there is no form anywhere in this county that a nine-inch strip of blank paper can come on except the eight leaves against her ribs, and those are hers, and they are blank, and they go where she goes and nowhere else.

So she cannot be given it. There is nothing to be given. A thing with nothing on it is not a shape and a shape cannot be taken out of a shape.

She said, at the ninth hour, in the dark, to the board, with the strip in her hand:

“So somebody sent me a blank one and I do not know who, and I am not entitled to ask anybody, and there is not one person in nine days of road I could put that question to.”

She put it into the front of her bag on top of the key, and she stood at the frame a while longer in the dark, and the four men had gone and the ground was lying down and the slate at the turn twenty miles west was standing face-up on a frame four hundred yards east of a bench with nothing at all written on it.

And she thought about the eight leaves against her ribs, on which there is a date and a place on the first and four sentences on the second and four capital strokes on the third and five of her own that have been blank since the day she made them.

There is a key in the front of that same bag and it opens a drawer four miles west of a square where there is a fair hand and seven sentences and forty-one short strokes in a margin that she has promised herself twice now she is not going to look at.

Nine inches of blank paper against her ribs and four sheets of a dead man’s fair hand in a locked drawer and a lamp in a cage on the fourth landing of a tower, and every one of those is a thing somebody left where somebody could find it, and she has not touched any of them in nine days of road and she is not going to start now, because a blank thing that arrives on its own is not a gift and there is not one person in this county who gives anything to anybody without a rate at the end of it.

She lay down at the south end of the ground forty feet off a hundred other people, on the ground, with the bar along beside her and the bag under her head, and she did not sleep eight hours and she did not expect to.
