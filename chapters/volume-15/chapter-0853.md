# Above Nine

Eighty-four days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and fifteen days in that room. The cage out two hundred and eighty-two days. Days by which the right arm has been open at the elbow, five hundred and twenty-two, and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and fifty-seven days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in that room and no person in the nine landings above it and no person on any stair in that tower, and there was a lamp standing on the ninth landing of a building that had never had one, where it had been set down at about the sixth hour and twenty minutes of the ninth of that month, and a woman a long way above the ninth, at about the eighth hour of the thirteenth, with her back against a wall and nothing in either hand.

## Past The Ninth

She went up at about the seventh hour.

She said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the bottom of it, “Past nine,” and then she went, and she did not say anything else for about a count of forty.

The stair wound and there was no rail on it and the stone was cold under her left hand and the light went off the lamp four steps in front of her and stopped.

She said, a little way past the ninth, in the ordinary voice, “Ten.”

And she said, “And that is not a stage. There is nothing cut in my ribs about it and there is no figure at the head of any column and I am not saying a stage out loud on a stair.”

She said, “And it is a landing and I said I was counting landings.”

## The Cold

The cold came up out of the stone faster above the ninth than it had ever come up out of a floor in that building.

She said it out loud a long way up, standing, in the ordinary voice, “It is the cold,” and then she said the other part, which is her rule and has been her rule since the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen.

She said, “And it is not the cold of a person standing still. It is the cold of a person going up, and it got into my right arm about four landings back, and that arm has been open at the elbow five hundred and twenty-two of these days and it does not close and it is not going to close, and there is nothing to put over it.”

She said, “And I am going to say the next part out loud before I get frightened of it.”

She said, “It is not fear yet. It is going to be fear in about a count of a hundred and forty and I am naming it now so that I know which one it is when it arrives.”

## I Have Run Out Of Hands

She went up a landing and then another and then she stopped.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the seventh hour and forty minutes:

“Hold there.”

She stood in the middle of a landing with the lamp in her left hand and her right arm open in front of her and the light going about four steps in every direction and stopping.

She said, “There is a wall on the left.”

She said, “And there is not one on the right, and there was not one on the second of those landings either, and I had my hand out for it and there was nothing there and I have been doing that for three landings without noticing that I was doing it.”

She said, “And I have a lamp in this hand and a wall in that one and there is nothing to hold on the other side of me, and that is fine, because there has never been anything on that side of me. I have been walking along a wall for nineteen years and I have not once needed the other side.”

She said, “And I have run out of hands.”

She said it out loud and then stood still and said nothing for a long time.

She said, “That is the whole of what is in the way. It is not the cold and it is not the dark and it is not the building. It is that I have one hand on a lamp and one hand on a wall and a right arm that has not closed since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and there is a limit to how far a person can go up a stair with that.”

## The Landing With No End In It

She went on anyway for longer than that, and then she stopped for good.

She said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, at about the eighth hour:

“I cannot see the end of this landing.”

She said, “I can see four steps and about two hands of the floor and the wall on my left going up out of the light, and I cannot see whether this landing is a landing or a floor or a piece of the stair, and I have been putting one foot in front of the other on the assumption that it is a landing and I have had that assumption since I came round the last turning.”

She said, “And if it is not a landing then the thing I am standing on has no edges on the far side of the lamp and I could go on putting one foot in front of the other for a long time and not find out.”

She set the lamp down on the stone where she was standing.

The light went flat out from it and up the wall on her left and stopped.

She said, out loud, standing, “That is the edge of what I can do. That is not the top of anything. That is four feet of lamp in a building that is bigger than my lamp.”

## It Does Not Stop

And then the voice in her head said something, and she did not answer it, and she has not answered it since.

It said, “You keep going up because you think there is a number at the end.”

And she stood there for about a count of ninety with a lamp on the stone at her feet and did not say one word back.

And then she said the thing out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the eighth hour and twenty minutes of the thirteenth of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four, on a landing she could not see the end of, with her right arm open and her breath going seven counts on the rise.

She said, “It does not stop at nine.”

She said, “That is the whole of what I have found out in a month and I am going to say it as one sentence because there is not one word to put in front of it and there is not one word to put after it.”

She said, “I have been in this building five hundred and fifteen days and I have believed every morning of them that there is nothing above this line, and there is a stair going up out of the ninth landing and I have been on it for a long time and it has not ended and I cannot see where it ends.”

She said, “And every number I have ever had stops at nine. Every one. Mine, his, the stages, the rooms, the rows. Nine and then it stops. And I have been living inside that for nineteen years and I did not think about it once, because a thing that stops at nine is the shape of everything I have ever been told.”

She sat down on the stone.

She said, out loud, “And this building does not stop. That is the only thing I have got and I have not got what it means and I am not going to pretend to have.”

## And What She Did About It

She sat on that landing a long time with her back against the wall on her left and the lamp standing on the stone in front of her and the dark above and below her and nothing in it.

She said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour, “I am going to have to go back down.”

She said, “And I am not going to pretend that I am going down because I have finished. I am going down because I have got nothing left to go down with, and the nothing is two hands, and one of them is holding a lamp and one of them is holding a wall that is on the wrong side for the next four steps.”

She said, “And I did not take a count up there. That is the other thing I want said out loud and I have been holding it since about the seventh hour.”

She said, “I counted landings until the tenth and then I stopped counting, and I did not decide to stop. I found out that I had stopped when I got to a place where I could not see the end of the landing, and I went back four or five paces and stood still in the dark and worked out that I had not said a number since the tenth, and I did not go back and say it.”

She said, “A number is a thing you can hold. And up there I had one hand on a lamp and one hand on a wall and there was nothing at all for a number to go in. I have been holding numbers instead of stairs since I was nine years old, and up there I finally ran out of hands for them.”

She said, “And there is a stair in this building that goes up out of the ninth landing and it does not stop, and I cannot see how much of it there is, and I am going to have to come back up it in the dark to find out, and I know that about myself now and it did not surprise me.”

She said, “Five hundred and fifteen days is a long time to stand at the top of something.”

There was a lamp standing on a landing with no end in it, above the ninth, and a woman sitting down beside it with one hand on the stone behind her and one hand on the arm that would not close, and below her nine landings of building in the dark, and above her as much of it as four feet of lamp could not show.
