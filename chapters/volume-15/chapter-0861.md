# Not a Room Like the Others

One hundred and seven days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and thirty-eight days in that room. The cage out three hundred and five days. Right arm open five hundred and forty-five days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and eighty of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was a woman sitting beside it who had been above the ninth twice and come back down twice, and there was a question she had not asked the stair, and she was going to ask it.

## Whether Anything Is Above the Cells

She said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, at about the sixth hour of the sixth of the month, before she did anything else about it.

She said, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and seven days since the ninth. Five hundred and thirty-eight in that room. Three hundred and five since a lamp in a cage went out. Five hundred and forty-five with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and eighty of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. I have been in three rooms above mine and they were all the same room. A table twenty-two paces long. Forty boards face up under the middle. A bundle under the far end. A lamp out in a cage. A kept place of the length of a forearm that nobody has ever sat in. That is a cell, though I have never called it that out loud, and I am calling it that now.”

She said, “And for four hundred years no person has climbed past them, and I have climbed past nine landings twice, and both times I stopped where I could not see the end of a landing, and neither time did I find out whether there is anything above the cells.”

She said, “It is doubt, and I am naming it before I argue with it, because I do that.”

She said, “Not doubt of the stair. The stair is there and it goes up and my hand has been on it. Doubt of whether going up it a third time is work or habit. Twice I have gone up and twice I have come back down with four feet of nothing and a sentence about nine. A third time has to find out something or stop being work.”

She said, “And what I want is to know whether anything is above the cells. That is the want and there is nothing else under it.”

She left the lamp burning on the third landing and went up in the dark with nothing in either hand, past the fourth landing, past the fifth, with her left hand on the stone, and she counted the landings and said them where they were used.

## The Landing With No Doorway

She went past the sixth, past the seventh, past the eighth, and the cold came up out of the stone into her arm, and her breath went seven counts on the rise, and she stopped on each landing until it went four counts again, and then she went on.

She said, out loud, on the ninth landing, standing in the dark, “Ninth.”

She said, “And that is a landing and not a stage, and there is nothing cut in my ribs about it, and I have said it three times on this stair and every time it has been true.”

She went up past it, past nine, to the tenth, and she stood on the tenth landing in the dark with her left hand on the wall, and she felt along the wall the way she had felt along every landing wall for nineteen days, for the place where a doorway would be.

There was no doorway.

She went along the whole of that wall with the flat of her hand, slow, twice, and there was no break in it, and no frame, and no draught, and no change in the cold under her hand.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “There is no doorway here.”

She said, “And I want the whole of that said, because it is the first landing in this building that has not had one. One, two and three have rooms and I have been in all three. Six has a room and I have been in it. Four, five, seven and eight have doorways I have not gone through. Nine has the stair going up out of it. This one has nothing, and I have felt every hand of it twice.”

She sat down on the stone of that landing with her back against the wall, and she put her left hand flat on the floor, and the floor was warmer there than it had been on any landing below it, the way the stone of the ninth landing had been warmer on the side the stair went up eleven days before.

She said, “And it is warmer. I noticed it with the flat of my hand, and it is warmer, and the rest of this building has been the same cold for five hundred and thirty-eight days, and I am saying it out loud because the things I notice and do not say are the ones that cost.”

## Something, and Not a Room

She sat on the tenth landing in the dark for a long time, with her right arm open across her knees, and then she lay down flat on the stone and put her ear against it, the way she had put her ear against stone once before to hear forty boards, and she stayed there for about a count of a hundred and forty.

There was nothing to hear that she could name. No boots. No boards. No cloth. No hand along wood.

But the stone was not still the way stone is still. There was something in it, far above her, the way there is something in a wall when a person is working on the other side of it, too far to hear and too steady to be nothing.

She got up and stood on that landing in the dark, and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, as one sentence, because there was not one word to put in front of it and not one word to put after it.

She said, “Something is above the cells, and it is not a room like the others.”

She said, “And here is the whole of what I know about it, and it is short. No doorway on this landing. Warmer stone than below. Something in the stone above me that is steady and too far to hear. That is three things and every one of them is by hand or by ear, and there is not one word of guess in any of them.”

She said, “And it is hope. And I am naming it because it is the first hope in this building since I came up it, and it is small, and I am not going to make it bigger than it is. Something is up there and it is not another table with forty boards under it, and I have spent nineteen days climbing to find out exactly that.”

She went back down in the dark to the third landing, slow, with her left hand on the stone, and she sat beside the lamp, and the light went six paces out from where it stood and stopped, and she stayed inside the reach of it until her breath went four counts standing still.

## What She Does Not Bring Up

She sat on the tenth landing for a long time after that, with her back against the wall that had no doorway in it, and she thought about going back down for the lamp, and she said the thinking out loud, because thinking said afterwards is thinking made up.

She said, “Here is the thought. Go back down six landings in the dark, pick up the lamp off the third, bring it up here, put it on this stone, and look at this landing in light instead of by hand.”

She said, “And here is why I am not doing it. A lamp in a hand is light on about four feet of the world. I said that on the twenty-second and it was true and it is still true. Four feet of this landing in light is four feet I already know by hand, and everything past the four feet is dark whether I carry it or not, and the carrying leaves every landing under me unmarked.”

She said, “And there is more, said plain. If I bring it up and set it here, then this landing has a mark on it, and a mark is a promise to come back to, and I am not promising this landing anything. I found it today and I am leaving it the way I found it, with nothing on it, and that is not neglect, and I have worked out that it is not the same, and it is better, because neglect is forgetting and this is remembering on purpose.”

She put her left hand flat on the warmer stone and felt along it for about the length of two paces, the way she had felt along the ninth landing’s stair eleven days before, and the warmth was there, steady, a little more than the stone of the landing under it, and she said so out loud.

She said, “Warmer going up. Two paces of it by hand. And I do not know what it means and I am not going to find out tonight.”

She said, “And it is patience. And I am naming it because I have not had it once in twenty days of climbing, and it is small, and I am not going to make it bigger than it is. Something is above the cells and it is not a room like the others, and it will still be there tomorrow, and I am going back down to a lamp that is still burning, and both of those are true, and neither of them is going anywhere.”

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and a landing above the ninth with no doorway in it and warmer stone under her hand, and something in the stone above that landing that was steady and too far to hear.

And she had found out that there is something above the cells, and it is not a room like the others, and that was the day, and the stair went on up toward it whether she climbed it or not.
