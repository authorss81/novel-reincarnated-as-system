# The Room Without Her

One hundred and thirty-two days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and sixty-three days in that room. The cage out three hundred and thirty days. Right arm open five hundred and seventy days and it is not going to close. One thousand three hundred and five of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was no woman beside it, and there was a first of a month at the bottom of the building, and she was nine landings above it, and it did not need her.

## The Second Hour, Above

She had gone up at about the first hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and sixty-four, in the dark, past the lamp, past the fourth landing, past the fifth, with her left hand on the stone, and she had counted the landings and said them where they were used, and she had lain flat on the twelfth landing with her ear on the stone before the second hour came.

She said, out loud, lying, in the ordinary voice, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and thirty-two days since the ninth. Five hundred and sixty-three in that room. Three hundred and thirty since a lamp in a cage went out. Five hundred and seventy with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand three hundred and five of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. It is the first of the month, and at about the second hour forty boards go on that table at the bottom of this building, and I am on the twelfth landing with my ear on the stone, and I am not going down for it, and I am going to hear it through him, because he hears the layer and I hear his half.”

She said, “It is coldness, and it is the first of these I have had that is at a room and not at a stone, and I am saying it out loud before I lie down and listen to it run without me.”

She said, “Not the cold in the stone. That has been here thirty-one days and I know its shape. Coldness in me toward a room I stood in for nineteen years, and it is the first coldness toward it, and I am saying so out loud because it would be dishonest not to. I am lying above it to hear it secondhand, and secondhand is colder than missing it, and I chose it.”

She said, “And what I want is to be somewhere when it happens. That is the want and there is nothing else under it. Not under it. Above it. That is the whole of the difference and it is everything.”

She lay flat on the stone of the twelfth landing with her ear against it, and the cold came up through the floor into her, and her breath went nine counts lying still, and she stayed there, and at about the second hour the sentences started.

## What Runs

They came through her to him, one at a time, plain, reporting, at their own intervals, and he listened, and his half went on inside her where she could hear it, and in his half she heard the room.

He said, “Forty go down.”

She lay on the stone with her ear against it and said nothing, because there was nothing to say that would not stop her hearing.

He said, “The cloth goes round.”

He said, “The cord goes round twice.”

She put her hand flat on the stone beside her ear, and the steadiness was there under her hand, regular as work, and his half went on inside her, quiet, reporting what it heard, the way she used to report what she saw.

He said, “The hand goes along.”

She lay there for about a count of three hundred, and the sentences went on, one at a time, and his half went on under them, and then the sentences stopped for a space, the way they stop when a working is done, and his half went quiet.

She got up off the floor of that landing and stood in the dark with nothing in either hand, and her arm shook, and she stood still until it stopped, and then she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, as one sentence, because there was not one word to put in front of it and not one word to put after it.

She said, “The room runs, and the hand goes along the table, and there is nobody in it.”

She said, “And here is the whole of it, said plain. Forty boards went on that table at about the second hour. A cloth went round them and a cord went round the cloth twice. The far end of that table went along the boards the way it has gone along four hundred years of boards. Han Liang put them down without looking at anything and went back out.”

She said, “The cage lamp stood out on the shelf. The bar stood against the wall on the left where it has stood five hundred and twenty days. The bundle lay under the far end corded twice and not knotted. The strip of pale wood lay face up under the table at the near end of the leg. And not one person in that room heard one board go down.”

She said, “And I heard it lying on stone nine landings above it, secondhand, through a person inside my head, and that is colder than missing it from the ninth with my ear on a wall, and I chose it, and I am saying so out loud.”

## The Hour Before

She had waited through the hour before it on the twelfth landing, lying flat with her ear on the stone, and the waiting had been work, and she said so out loud afterwards, once she was down out of the light, because work unsaid is work that is going to be asked for twice.

She said, “Here is what the hour before took. Cold through the floor into my front, and my breath nine counts lying still, and my arm open at the elbow five hundred and seventy days, shaking twice, and lying still until it stopped twice, and not moving, because moving would have meant missing the start, and the start was the whole of why I was there.”

She said, “And here is what went through me while I waited. The sentences at their own intervals, one at a time, through me to him, and his half under them, listening, and me over them, listening to the listening. Three deep and none of them mine except the middle one, which is the wire, and the wire does not get to choose what goes along it, and I chose to be the wire tonight by lying down, and that is the only choosing in it.”

She said, “And it is endurance. And I am naming it because it is the first endurance on this stair that was not climbing, and it is small, and I am not going to make it bigger than it is. One hour flat on stone, cold, shaking twice, still, for forty boards I did not hear with my own ears.”

## The Cost

She came down in the dark, nine landings, with her left hand on the stone the whole way, and the cold went out of her arm somewhere around the fourth and did not come back. She sat beside the lamp, and the light went six paces out from where it stood and stopped, and she stayed inside the reach of it until her breath went four counts sitting still.

She said, out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, “Here is what the day cost, and I am saying it sitting down beside the lamp instead of saying it afterwards, because afterwards is where I make things up.”

She said, “That room does not need me. It never needed me. I stood four feet off the near end of that table for nineteen years saying figures into it, and every one of them went somewhere, and this morning forty boards went on it with no person standing under them, and a hand went along them, and it went on.”

She said, “And here is the part that is actually on me. I am nine landings above it and I heard it through him, and hearing it through him is further from it than missing it, and nearer to him than I have ever been, and both of those are true, and one of them is a cost and the other is not, and I am not going to mix them up.”

## Sitting With It

She sat beside the lamp a long time after the cost was said, with her back against the wall and her right arm open across her knees, and his half went on inside her, quiet, the way he goes quiet after a working, and she sat with it the way she had sat with his silence, except that now the silence had a shape.

She said, out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, “Here is what sitting with it is. This morning I heard my own room run with nobody in it, through a person in my head. Tonight I am sitting beside a lamp that burns with nobody beside it but me. Two workings going on without the person who tends them, a room and a lamp, and I am the tender of both, and both went on today, and I heard one and saw the other.”

She said, “And here is what is different between them, said plain, because I am not going to mix them up. The lamp burns because I trimmed it and it will go out when I stop. The room runs because a hand goes along forty boards and it will not stop whether I am under it or not. One of those is keeping and the other is something else, and I do not know what the something else is, and I am saying that I do not know.”

He said, “You sit.”

She said, “I do. And sitting is the whole of tonight’s work, and I have done it, and the cost is said, and the record is closed.”

She said, “Lamp on the third landing. Burning. Mine. A first spent above it that I did not go down for. A room run with nobody in it. That is the record and there is nothing else in it.”

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and forty boards on a table a long way under it with a cloth round them and a cord round the cloth twice, and a hand gone along them and gone, and no person in that room.

And she had been somewhere when it happened, and the somewhere was nine landings above it, and the room had run without her, and that was the day, and it would run the same on every first that came after it.
