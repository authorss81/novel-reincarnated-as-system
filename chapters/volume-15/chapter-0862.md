# The Sound Before Words

One hundred and nine days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and forty days in that room. The cage out three hundred and seven days. Right arm open five hundred and forty-seven days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and eighty-two of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was a woman sitting beside it who had found a landing with no doorway and something steady in the stone above it, and there was a sound she had almost heard, and she was going to get close enough to hear it start.

## What She Is Going Toward

She said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, at about the sixth hour of the eighth of the month, before she did anything else about it.

She said, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and nine days since the ninth. Five hundred and forty in that room. Three hundred and seven since a lamp in a cage went out. Five hundred and forty-seven with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and eighty-two of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. Two days ago I lay flat on the tenth landing with my ear against the stone, and there was something in it above me, steady and too far to hear. I have had two days beside a lamp to decide what that was, and what I have got is that it is regular, and a thing that is regular in a building is either work or sentences, and I am going up to find out which.”

She said, “It is hunger, and I am naming it before I argue with it, because I do that.”

She said, “Not hunger in the body. I have not been hungry since the ninth and that has not changed. Hunger for the next of it. I have had three findings on this stair and every one of them has been stone and sameness, and two days ago the stone had something in it, and I have been hungry for the something ever since, the way I used to be hungry for morning figures.”

She said, “And what I want is to get close enough to hear it start. Not to understand it. To hear it start. That is the want and there is nothing else under it.”

She left the lamp burning on the third landing and went up in the dark with nothing in either hand, past the fourth landing, past the fifth, past the sixth, with her left hand on the stone, and she counted the landings and said them where they were used, and her breath went seven counts on every rise.

## Up Past the Tenth

She went past the seventh, past the eighth, past the ninth, and she said each one out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and each one was a landing and not a stage, and there was nothing cut in her ribs about any of them.

She went up past the ninth to the tenth, and she stood on the landing with no doorway, and she put her left hand flat on the warmer stone, and it was warmer than it had been two days before, or her hand had learned it, and she did not decide which, and she said so out loud.

She said, “Warmer. Or learned. One of the two and I am not deciding.”

She lay down flat on the stone and put her ear against it, and she stayed there for about a count of a hundred and forty, and the steadiness was there, nearer than it had been, and still too far to hear as anything but steadiness.

She got up and went on up past the tenth, into the part of the building she had never been in, with her left hand on the stone and her right arm open in front of her, and the wall stayed on her left, and the cold came up out of the stone into her arm until it shook, and she stopped and stood still until it stopped, and then she went on.

She said, out loud, in the dark, “Eleventh.”

And the word went out and did not come back, and she stood on a landing she could not see the end of, with nothing in either hand, and she lay down flat on the stone again and put her ear against it.

And this time there was something more than steadiness.

It came at intervals, the way sentences come, one at a time, with a space between one and the next, and each one was plain, and each one reported, and she could not make out one word of any of them, the way a person in one room cannot make out words through a wall but can tell that sentences are being said in the next one.

She lay on the stone of the eleventh landing with her ear against it for about a count of three hundred, and the sentences went on, one at a time, plain, reporting, too far to hear as words, and she did not move, and she did not say anything, because there was nothing to say that would not stop her hearing.

## The Announcements Start

She got up at last and stood on that landing in the dark, and her arm shook, and her breath went nine counts standing still, and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, as one sentence, because there was not one word to put in front of it and not one word to put after it.

She said, “Those are announcements starting.”

She said, “And here is the whole of what I know about them, and it is short. They come one at a time. They are plain. They report and they do not advise. They are too far to hear as words. That is four things and every one of them is by ear, and there is not one word of guess in any of them.”

She said, “And I have been reading the incoming end of them since I was seven years old. That is the instrument in my head and it has never once said where they are made, and tonight I am lying on stone under the place where they start, and I know what they sound like through a wall.”

She said, “And it is fear. And I am naming it because it is the fear I named on the eighteenth and said was worse than the stair, the fear of the top, and tonight I have heard the top making sentences, and the fear is here, and it is worse the way I said it would be.”

She said, “And what I want now is not what I wanted this morning. This morning I wanted to hear it start. Now I want to know what it is saying, and who it is saying it to, and I am going to find out, and I know that about myself now and it did not surprise me.”

She went back down in the dark to the third landing, slow, with her left hand on the stone, and she sat beside the lamp, and the light went six paces out from where it stood and stopped, and she put her hands where the light was and looked at them to be sure they were still hers.

## The Climb That Earns It

She had not got to the eleventh landing cheaply, and she said so out loud on the way back down, because a price said afterwards is a thing you have made up, and tonight’s price had been paid in full before anything was heard.

She said, “Here is what it took. Past the ninth with the wall on the left and nothing on the right. Past the tenth with no doorway and stone going warmer under my hand. Up past that with the cold coming into my arm until it shook, and standing still with my hand flat on the wall until it stopped, three times, and going on three times.”

She said, “And my breath went seven counts on every rise and nine counts lying still, and my arm is open at the elbow five hundred and forty-seven days and it is not going to close, and there is nothing to put over it, and I put nothing over it, and I went on.”

She said, “And here is the part I want said plainest. Twice before I came back down out of the high part of this building because I had run out of hands. One hand on a lamp and one hand on a wall and an arm that would not close. Tonight I left the lamp six landings below me and I had one hand on the wall and nothing on the other side, and I still ran out, higher up, further in, and I lay flat instead, and hearing turned out to need no hands at all.”

She stopped on the tenth landing in the dark and put her left hand flat on the warmer stone, the way she had put it two days before, and it was warmer, or her hand had learned it, and she did not decide, the way she had not decided before.

She said, “Warmer. Or learned. Still one of the two and still not decided. And tonight there is a third thing with them, which is that under this stone the sentences start, and I heard them start one landing up, and the stone is warmer where the sentences are nearer, and I am saying that with them, and I am not saying what it means.”

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and sentences starting somewhere above the eleventh landing, one at a time, plain, reporting, too far to hear as words.

And she had got close enough to hear them start, and that was the night, and they went on above her whether she heard them or not, and she was going back up.
