# No Standing to Refuse

One hundred and seventeen days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and forty-eight days in that room. The cage out three hundred and fifteen days. Right arm open five hundred and fifty-five days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and ninety of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was a woman sitting beside it who had named a thing that had been reading her since she was seven, and there was a refusal she had made twice that had stopped being hers, and there was a question of whether she could make a third one for somebody else.

## Whether She Can Refuse for Him

She said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, at about the sixth hour of the sixteenth of the month, before she did anything else about it.

She said, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and seventeen days since the ninth. Five hundred and forty-eight in that room. Three hundred and fifteen since a lamp in a cage went out. Five hundred and fifty-five with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and ninety of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. Three days ago I stood on the twelfth landing and said its word out loud and it did not object. It goes on speaking to him through me, one sentence at a time, in the plain report grammar, and he goes on listening. I have refused twice in this trade and both refusals stand, and I am going up tonight to find out whether there is a third one in me, for him.”

She said, “It is tenderness, and I have not used that word at a table in nineteen years, and I am saying it before I go up and try to refuse a thing on somebody else’s behalf.”

She said, “Not wrath and not resolve. Under both of them there is tenderness toward a person I have carried for fifteen volumes, and it is the first tenderness on this stair, and it is small, and I am not going to make it bigger than it is, and it is going to be the reason if I do what I am going up to do.”

She said, “And what I want is to refuse it on his behalf. I know it is somebody else’s to refuse. I want it anyway, and nothing is under the wanting but that.”

She left the lamp burning on the third landing and went up past it in the dark with nothing in either hand, and she said each landing out loud as she came to it, because there was nothing else on the stair to hold and the counting was the only discipline she had left.

## The Attempt

She went up to the twelfth the way she had gone up every morning of this stair, naming the landings as she used them, and she stood on it in the dark with nothing in either hand, and the sentences were there, one at a time, plain, reporting, going through her to him, and she had come up to refuse them.

She listened for about a count of a hundred and forty, standing, to be sure of the grammar, and then she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, the way she had said every refusal she had ever made.

She said, “No.”

The sentences went on, one at a time, in the same grammar, at the same intervals, through her to him, and nothing in them changed.

She said, louder, standing, “I said no.”

The sentences went on.

She said, “It is not addressed to me.”

And that was said quieter than the rest, and it came out with her hand flat on the wall and her right arm open in front of her, and the sentences went on, one at a time, through her to him, and he went on listening.

She said, “Here is the whole of it, said plain, because I am standing here and there is no other place to stand. It has not asked me anything. It has never asked me anything in fifteen volumes. It reports and it does not advise, and a refusal is advice turned round, and there is nothing in its grammar for advice to land on.”

She said, “I said no twice where everything could hear it. The first time out loud in that room. The second time in my own hand down nine hundred and four steps. Both of them were mine to say. Both of them stand. This one is not mine to say, and saying it does not make it mine.”

## What She Has Not Got

She stood on the twelfth landing in the dark with the sentences going through her, and she said the rest out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, because a finding said sitting down is a finding you have not finished.

She said, “I have no standing. It is not addressed to me and it has not asked me anything, and standing here saying no at it is standing in front of sentences that are going past me to somebody else.”

She said, “And I have no instrument. The instrument in my head is also him, and he is the one being spoken to, and there is no second instrument for me to answer on. Everything I say here goes out into the dark off a landing, and everything it says goes through me whether I speak or not.”

She said, “And it is grief again, the grief from the sixth landing with the forty under it, come back higher up, and it is worse the way I said it would be. I am the wire and not the voice, and the wire does not get to refuse for the voice, and I have spent fifteen volumes being the one who decides everything, and here I decide nothing.”

She said, “And what I want now is not what I wanted this morning. This morning I wanted to refuse for him. Now I want him to refuse for himself, if refusing is what he wants, and I want him to know that I stood here and found out I could not do it for him, and I am saying that out loud so that it is said.”

She stood there for about a count of ninety with the sentences going through her, one at a time, and then she said the last of it, standing, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “I have no standing and no instrument and I say so out loud. That is the record and there is nothing else in it.”

## The Two That Were Hers

She stood on the twelfth landing after the last of it with the sentences going through her, and before she went back down she said the two refusals out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, to set them beside the one that was not hers, because things set beside each other can be told apart and things held apart cannot.

She said, “The first one was mine. Out loud in that room, at the ninth hour and twenty minutes, on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen, standing four feet off the near end of that table. A no said where everything could hear it. It stands.”

She said, “The second one was mine. Written on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four, and sent on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, down nine hundred and four steps in a cloth. A no in my own hand. It stands.”

She said, “And tonight’s was not mine. Said standing on this landing at somebody speaking through me to somebody in me, and the sentences went on, and nothing changed, and I have no standing and no instrument and I say so out loud.”

She said, “And here is what three refusals look like set together. Two of them are doors shut and this one is a hand put out in front of sentences going past. The doors hold. The hand holds nothing. And I am not sorry I put it out, and I am saying so out loud, because a thing tried and found empty is still a thing tried, and I have never once left a trying unsaid.”

She said, “And it is clearness. And I am naming it because it is the first clearness on this stair, and it is cold, and I am not going to make it warmer than it is. Two that were mine and stand. One that was never mine and never stood. And the next decision on this stair is his, and I know that, and it did not surprise me.”

## The Stair Down With It

She went back down past the eleventh, past the tenth with no doorway, and on the tenth she stopped, because there was a thing to do there that belonged to that landing and not to any other, and she wanted it done where it belonged.

She lay flat on the stone with no doorway over it and put her ear against the warmer rock, and the sentences were there, one at a time, through her to him, and she said it into the stone, quiet, in the ordinary voice, not standing, because standing was for refusals and this was not one.

She said, “I tried to refuse for you and I have no standing. That is all. Sleep is not in it and waking is not in it. I stood on the twelfth and said no twice and nothing changed, and I am telling you so that you know it from me before you hear it anywhere else.”

He went on listening inside her, and whether he heard the telling she did not know, and she got up and went on down past the ninth, past the eighth, past the seventh, and she said each one where it was used, and her arm shook, and she did not stop.

She said, out loud, on the sixth landing, standing in the dark, “Sixth. And that is a landing and not a stage. And I am saying it because the discipline holds whether the night holds or not, and tonight the night does not hold and the discipline does.”

She came the rest of the way down in the dark with her left hand on the stone and nothing in the other, and she did not stop on any of the landings under the sixth, because a thing said once on a stair is said, and saying it twice is how a rule turns into a habit, and she has not got the discipline for a habit. She sat beside the lamp, and the light went six paces out from where it stood and stopped, and she stayed inside the reach of it until her arm stopped shaking.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and sentences going through a woman on the twelfth landing to a person inside her head, one at a time, plain, reporting, and a refusal said standing that had changed nothing.

And she had found out she could not refuse for him, and she had said so out loud, and that was the day, and the cost of it was that the next decision on that stair was his.
