# Who Pays

“Two hundred and twenty-five days since the ninth stage. Six hundred and fifty-six days in that room. The cage out four hundred and twenty-three. This arm open six hundred and sixty-three days. One thousand three hundred and ninety-eight gone at this morning.”

She said the figures standing at the near end of the table in the making-place, on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four, with the lamp nearly out beside her and the register under her hands, five mercies read twice.

The bar stood six hundred and thirteen days against its wall, dated nowhere.

She stood with her hands on the table edge and breathed four counts standing still, the way she breathed for lines. Her right arm hung open at the elbow for six hundred and sixty-three days. The sentences went on above her one at a time, plain, reporting, through her to him.

## One Question in the Air

She had reached it. The bargain was on the table and every part of it was a mercy and it was made in good faith. One person was bound and the price was on the record. The ninth was shut.

“This is what the whole climb was built to put in the air,” she said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, with nothing in her hands. “I have carried it for six hundred and thirteen days of a bar and four hundred years of a keeping and I am asking it standing.”

She had carried it longer than that. Nine years of anger at a hole, aimed at the small taker because the small taker had a face she could say a name to. A month of arithmetic finding the great taker with no mouth at all. Six hundred and forty thousand said where it could be read. Five mercies with no price in any of them. All of it built to this one asking, and the asking was two words.

Her breath went four counts standing still. The sentences went on above her one at a time, plain, reporting, through her to him.

“Who pays.”

She said it standing, in the channel where the thing that keeps the ceiling reads, first in her own voice, the way she had said the count and the binding and the reading.

The two words went past into the traffic one at a time, plain, reporting, through her to him, and nothing answered at once, the way nothing answers figures at once, and she stood with her hands on the table edge and waited the way she had waited three hours on a cold floor for the ninth hour.

## Him, Carrying the Answer

“I want you to hear this to the end,” he said.

She stood at the near end of the table in the dark with her hands on its edge and heard him the way she heard everything now, undefended, and there was no room in her head that was his alone.

“I am hearing,” she said. “Asking is not taking and hearing is not answering. Say what is said to you.”

She stood at the table with nothing in her hands and held the hearing open on her side the way he held it on his, the wire held at both ends for the first time in nine years, her asking and his hearing and the keeping answering through both of them.

He was quiet for a count of forty, which was his way of holding the hearing open the way she held landings, and then he spoke, and what he spoke was not his own sentence, and she knew it the way she knew the traffic, which was by the plainness of it.

She stood with her hands on the table edge and heard the quiet change the way the traffic changes when something comes down it with weight, one sentence at a time, plain, reporting, in the voice that had answered figures for a month without explaining one of them.

“The ceiling pays,” he said. “The people under it pay. They are told nothing. It is the best arrangement in nine hundred years and for a thousand more.”

She stood at the table with nothing in her hands and let the four sentences go past her the way sentences go past, one at a time.

“Say it again,” she said.

“The ceiling pays,” he said. “The people under it pay. They are told nothing. It is the best arrangement in nine hundred years and for a thousand more.”

She stood at the table with nothing in her hands and held the four sentences the way she held figures, fully, without deciding in advance. Twice said, the way great sayings are said twice, and the same both times, plain, reporting, correct. The keeping did not vary its answer the way she did not vary her figures.

## The Argument, Had

“The Anchor is the gathered will of every soul dead believing ascent,” she said, standing. “Unbounded ascent drains it. The walls hold it. That is true and it is not the whole, and I named it on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and sixty-four.”

She put her hand flat on the register, finding the five mercies by the cuts.

“The keeping keeps whoever stands under it. The hearing answers. No sending. Kept where it stands. Open and unasked. That is true and every part of it is a mercy and not one of them is a lie.”

She lifted her hand.

“Six hundred and forty thousand dead believing. That is true and it is in the record and it cannot be taken out.”

She set the first true thing against the answer, standing, the way she set figures against each other.

“The Anchor is gathered will. The answer is told nothing. Gathered and untold sit next to each other and neither moves the other. The will gathers whether told or not. That is the first setting and it holds.”

She set the second true thing against it.

“The bargain is five mercies. The answer is the ceiling pays. Mercy and paying sit next to each other and neither moves the other. The keeping keeps whoever stands under it and the standing under it pays. That is the second setting and it holds.”

She set the third true thing against it.

“The count is six hundred and forty thousand. The answer is best in nine hundred years. Dead believing and best arrangement sit next to each other and neither moves the other. That is the third setting and it holds.”

She stood in the dark with the three true things next to each other the way she had stood with the anger in two places, and she looked at them the way she looked at every figure, which was for what was behind them.

Behind the Anchor was every soul dead believing, gathered, held, drained by no one climbing past. Behind the bargain was four hundred years of keeping that asked nothing. Behind the count was six hundred and forty thousand who were never told. Three trues and the answer set against all three, and the answer did not move any of them.

“The ceiling pays and the people under it pay and they are told nothing,” she said. “Told would gather. I said that about one and about a great many. Untold multiplies. That is true and it does not move the walls.”

## Right, and Worse

“It is right,” she said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, into the dark past the last landing. “That is the finding of the argument and it is worse than everything on this stair. Nine hundred years of the best arrangement and a thousand more, and the people under it paying, and none of them told, and every mercy on this table real.”

She stood with the rightness the way she had stood with the count, fully, without deciding in advance, and the rightness did not warm anything. Nine years of warm anger at a man and cold anger at a wall, and the wall was right, and the rightness sat behind her fourth rib, cold, unthrown.

The Scribe is correct and brief and courteous and has four hundred years of practice at not being interested, and it never lies and never explains and never asks, and the answer it gives through him is the same answer it gives to figures.

“That is what was said to you,” she said. “I heard it. It is correct. The ceiling pays. The many pay. Nothing told. Best in nine hundred years.”

She sat down at the near end of the table, because standing was for saying and the saying was done.

“I do not refute it,” she said, sitting, to the dark, to the table, to the man inside her head. “I do not accept it. Those are two different things and I am keeping them apart the way I keep landings apart from steps. The argument is had and it is right and I live inside it.”

She sat at the near end of the table with her hands flat on the stone and the four sentences in her the way figures sat in her, ceiling paying and many paying and nothing told and best in nine hundred years, and her breath went four counts sitting still, and the sentences went on above her one at a time, and the argument sat next to her the way the nothing had sat next to the fourteen makings, had, not won, right.

He was quiet for a long time, which was not gone quiet, and she could hear the difference the way she heard a room with one person in it.

“I carry it with you,” he said at last. “Right and worse both. That is the whole of what I have and I am not putting it down.”

“Had, not won,” she said. “Right and worse. I live inside it.”
