# What Speaks to Him

One hundred and eleven days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and forty-two days in that room. The cage out three hundred and nine days. Right arm open five hundred and forty-nine days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and eighty-four of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was a woman sitting beside it who had heard sentences starting above the eleventh landing, and there was a question she had carried for nine years without knowing it, and she was going up to put her ear against it.

## What That Is

She said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, at about the sixth hour of the tenth of the month, before she did anything else about it.

She said, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and eleven days since the ninth. Five hundred and forty-two in that room. Three hundred and nine since a lamp in a cage went out. Five hundred and forty-nine with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and eighty-four of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. Two days ago I lay on the eleventh landing and heard sentences starting, one at a time, plain, reporting, too far to hear as words. I have had two days beside a lamp to decide what to do about that, and what I have got is that there is one thing in my head that hears such sentences, and it is also a person, and I have not spoken to him since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four.”

She said, “It is dread. I have said a feeling out loud before I argue with it since I was nine years old, and dread is the name of this one, and I am saying it here, on the landing where I am going to find out.”

She said, “Not fear of the top. I have had that eighteen days and I know its shape. Dread of finding out that the instrument in my head is also somebody’s ear, and that somebody has been speaking into it all along, and that I have been the wall the sentences come through.”

She said, “And what I want is to find out what that is. It is the oldest want I have got, because I have been reading the incoming end of that layer since I was seven years old and I have never once asked where the sentences are made. That want has been in me since I was seven and it has never once come with anything attached to it.”

She left the lamp burning on the third landing where it had been standing since the twenty-third, and went up past it in the dark with nothing in either hand, counting as she went, fourth, fifth, sixth, and saying each one where it was used, because going up to find out where the sentences are made is not the kind of going up a person does without a count.

## Up to the Eleventh

She went on up, past the seventh, past the eighth, past the ninth, past the tenth with no doorway in it, saying each one where it was used, and none of them was a stage, and nothing cut in her ribs about any of them.

She came to the eleventh landing and lay flat on the stone and put her ear against it, and the sentences were there, nearer than they had been, one at a time, plain, reporting, and still too far to hear as words.

She got up and went on up past the eleventh, with her left hand on the stone and her right arm open in front of her, and the wall stayed on her left, and the cold came up out of the stone into her arm until it shook, and she stopped and stood still until it stopped, and then she went on.

She said, out loud, in the dark, “Twelfth.”

And the word went out and did not come back.

She lay down flat on the stone of the twelfth landing and put her ear against it, and the sentences were there, one at a time, and this time there were words in them.

They came the way announcements come. One sentence. A space. Another sentence. Plain. Reporting. Never advising. Never explaining. The grammar she had been hearing since she was seven years old without knowing it had a source.

And they were not for her.

## The Address

The first one came, and it went through her the way every announcement had ever gone through her, and it stopped where he was.

It said, “You are found where you stand.”

She lay on the stone with her ear against it and did not move.

The second one came, one sentence, plain, reporting.

It said, “What you carry is written.”

And with it she felt him turn toward it, inside her head, the way a person turns toward a voice speaking his name in the next room, and she knew, the way she had always known when he was listening, that he was listening.

The third one came.

It said, “To be answered, be heard.”

She got up off the floor of that landing and stood in the dark with nothing in either hand, and her arm shook, and her breath went nine counts standing still, and she did not fall, and she did not faint, and nobody had to tell her what it was.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “That is somebody speaking to him.”

The fourth one came, one sentence, plain, reporting, in her, through her, stopping where he was.

It said, “What is heard is kept.”

She stood on the twelfth landing in the dark with sentences going through her to a person inside her head, and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, before she argued with it, because that is what she does.

She said, “That is an address. It is addressed to him and it is coming through me, because I am the only instrument it has, and there has been exactly one of me at a time for fifteen years and it has been me.”

She said, “And it is polite. And it is correct. And it is offering. And it talks to him the way I talk to him, and I have never heard any voice do that, and that is the horror, and I am saying so out loud because it would be dishonest not to.”

She said, “And it is wrath. And I am naming it because it is the first wrath in this building since I came up it, and it is mine, and it is at a thing that is using my head as a stair to get to a person I have carried for nine years.”

## What She Does Standing There

She stood on the twelfth landing in the dark with her hands on nothing, and the sentences went on, one at a time, plain, reporting, and he went on listening, and she stood between them the way a wire stands between two hands, and she said the rest out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “I have been reading the wrong end of it. I said that on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four and it was true and it is still true. I have been reading the incoming end since I was seven, and the sentences are made above me, and they come down through me, and he is at the far end of me, and something has started speaking to him down the length of me.”

She said, “And I hear both sides. The speaking, because it comes through me. The listening, because he is in me. That is the whole of it and there is nothing else in it.”

## The Wire, Standing

She stood on the twelfth landing after the fourth sentence with her hands on nothing, and the sentences went on, one at a time, and he went on listening, and she felt the shape of her own place in it the way she had once felt the shape of a table leg under her hand, by going along it twice.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “Here is where I am in this. The sentences come down through me. He is at the far end of me. I am the length between them. That is the whole of my place and there is nothing else in it.”

She said, “And I have been that length for nine years without knowing it. Every announcement I ever read went through me to nobody I knew, and I called the far end nothing, and the far end was him, listening, the way he listened tonight, turned toward it like a person turns toward a voice speaking his name.”

She said, “And bitterness, and I will leave it the size it is. Fifteen years of sentences through me to a person in me, and I never once asked where they were made, and the reason I never asked is that the layer is not a thing anybody has a maker of, and I said that on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, and it was true, and it is still true, and it is also not the whole of it any more.”

She said, “A thing with no maker still has to be somewhere when it is made. I said that too, on the same day, and tonight I am lying on the stone under the somewhere, and the somewhere is above me, and the sentences start there, and they come down through me, and that is all I know, and it is enough for tonight.”

She went back down in the dark to the third landing, slow, with her left hand on the stone, past the eleventh, past the tenth with no doorway, past the ninth, past the eighth, and she said each one where it was used, and her arm shook the whole way, and she did not stop until she was sitting beside the lamp with the light six paces out from where it stood and stopping.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and sentences going through a woman on the twelfth landing to a person inside her head, one at a time, plain, reporting.

And something had spoken to him in the only channel there is, and she had heard it happen, and she had named it standing up, and that was the day, and nothing on that stair was ever going to be one-sided again.
