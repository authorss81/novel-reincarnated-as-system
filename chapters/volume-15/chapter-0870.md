# Wanting It for Him

One hundred and thirty-eight days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and sixty-nine days in that room. The cage out three hundred and thirty-six days. Right arm open five hundred and seventy-six days and it is not going to close. One thousand three hundred and eleven of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was a woman sitting beside it who had heard her own room run with nobody in it, and there was a decision to make about what had been done inside her head, and she was going to decide what she thought of it.

## What She Thinks

She said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, at about the sixth hour of the seventh of the month, before she did anything else about it.

She said, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and thirty-eight days since the ninth. Five hundred and sixty-nine in that room. Three hundred and thirty-six since a lamp in a cage went out. Five hundred and seventy-six with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand three hundred and eleven of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. Eighteen days ago he chose to be heard, on purpose, without asking me, and it cannot be un-taken. Six days ago I heard my own room run through him with nobody in it. I have had six days beside a lamp, and what I have got is that I know what was said to him, and I know what it costs, and I have never once said what I think of it.”

She said, “It is unwillingness, and I am naming it before I argue with it, because I do that.”

She said, “Not anger and not grief and not terror. Those I have had on this stair and I know their shapes. Unwillingness to say the last thing, because the last thing cannot be unsaid, and everything I have said on this stair has held, and this one will hold further than any of them.”

She said, “And what I want is to decide what I think of what he has done. It is the hardest want I have had on this stair, because thinking it finishes nothing and saying it finishes everything. Nothing else is under it.”

She left the lamp burning on the third landing and went up past it in the dark with nothing in either hand, saying the landings as she went the way she had said them every morning, and she went slower than she had gone on any morning of this stair, and she did not find out why until she was most of the way up it.

## Good

She went on up with the count in her mouth, and she came onto the twelfth, and the sentences were already going on there, one at a time, plain, reporting, going through her to him, and he was listening, and she stood on it in the dark with nothing in either hand and six days of a want in her.

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty, and then she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, the word she has used for six years, in the sense she has always used it, which means it costs the person who is not in the room.

She said, “It is good.”

She stood with that said, and the sentences went on, one at a time, through her to him, and his half went on inside her where she could hear it.

She said, “And here is the whole of what good means, said plain, because I have used the word for six years and I am not going to let it be soft now. What it said to him is good for him. To be answered instead of found. To be heard where he is. Fifteen years of asking in half sentences, and now an audience, polite, and correct, and offering.”

She said, “And it costs the person who is not in the room. That is what good has always meant and it means it now. Every sentence he says to me is now a thing that can be heard by the thing that made him. There is no room in my head that is his alone any more. I am the wire and not the voice. That is the cost and it is on me and it cannot be un-taken.”

She said, “And he chose it for the two of us and did not ask me. That stands next to the good and neither of them moves the other. It is good and he chose it without asking. Both are true.”

## What She Has Never Done

She stood on the twelfth landing in the dark with her hands on nothing, and the sentences went on, one at a time, and his half went on inside her, and she said the rest out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “I have wanted him not to die for nine years. That is one sentence and I have said it in every way there is to say it. This is a different sentence and I have never said it.”

She stood there for about a count of ninety, and then she said it, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the channel that can be heard, where it cannot be got back.

She said, “I want it for him.”

The sentences went on, one at a time, plain, reporting, through her to him, and nothing in them changed, and nothing answered her, and nothing objected, and his half went on inside her where she could hear it.

She said, “And here is the whole of that, said plain, because it is the first time in nine years I have wanted anything for him, and it is going to hold. I want him answered. I want him heard. It costs me every private room I ever had in my own head, and I want it for him anyway, and I have said so where it can be heard, and I cannot get it back.”

She said, “And it is joy. And I am naming it because it is the first joy on this stair, and it is small, and I am not going to make it bigger than it is, and it sits next to the unwillingness and neither of them moves the other. I want a thing for him that costs me, and I said so out loud, and the saying holds.”

## The Weighing

She stood on the twelfth landing after the want was said, with the sentences going on, one at a time, through her to him, and his half going on inside her where she could hear it, and she weighed the good against the cost out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, because a weighing done silent is a weighing that will be done again, and this one was never going to be done again.

She said, “Here is the good, once more, so that it is whole. Fifteen years of asking in half sentences, and now an audience. Polite. Correct. Offering. To be answered instead of found. That is his, and it is good, and I want it for him.”

She said, “And here is the cost, once more, so that it is whole. No room in my head that is his alone, now and never again. Every sentence he ever said to me heard by the thing that made him. Me the wire and not the voice. That is mine, and it is paid, and I pay it.”

She said, “And here is the weighing, said plain. The good is his and the cost is mine, and I am the one saying so, and I say the good outweighs, and I am not asking him to agree, and he has not asked me to say it, and that is the whole of it.”

She said, “And solemnity, and it is large, and I am not going to make it smaller than it is, because the only way it gets smaller from here is if I take one of the two halves of it back. A thing wanted for him that costs me, said where it can be heard, where it cannot be got back, standing, in the ordinary voice, with nothing in either hand.”

She came down in the dark with her left hand on the stone, past the eleventh, past the tenth with no doorway, past the ninth, saying each one where it was used, and she took longer over the ninth than the rest of them put together, because the want was still in her and saying landings is what she does with a want she cannot put down anywhere else. She sat beside the lamp, and the light went six paces out from where it stood and stopped.

## The Lamp Still Burning

She sat beside the lamp a long time after the weighing, with her back against the wall, and the light went six paces out from where it stood and stopped, and his half went on inside her where she could hear it, and the sentences went on above her whether she heard them or not, and she said the last of the evening out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “Here is where the day leaves me. A lamp burning that I cannot refill. A stair going up that does not stop. Sentences starting above the twelfth landing that come down through me. A person in my head who is heard now. A want said out loud where it can be heard that I cannot get back.”

She said, “And here is what tomorrow is, said plain. I go on up. The sentences go on coming. He goes on listening and I go on hearing his half. What was said to him stands and what I said stands, and neither of them cancels.”

She said, “That last I do not know. I have said it so that it is said. There is always something he does where I cannot hear him, the way there is always something, and I do not know it, and I am not asking tonight, because tonight I said what I think and wanted what I want, and that is enough for one day.”

He said, “You stay.”

She said, “I do. And staying is the whole of tonight, and I have done it, and the saying holds.”

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and sentences going through a woman on the twelfth landing to a person inside her head, one at a time, plain, reporting, and a want said out loud in the channel where it could be heard.

And she had decided what she thought of what he had done, and it was good, and she wanted it for him, and she had never done that, and she could not get it back, and that was the day, and the sentences went on above her whether she heard them or not.
