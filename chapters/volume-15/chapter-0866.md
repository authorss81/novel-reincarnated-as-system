# Heard Where She Is

One hundred and twenty days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and fifty-one days in that room. The cage out three hundred and eighteen days. Right arm open five hundred and fifty-eight days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and ninety-three of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was no woman beside it, and there was a decision made inside her head without her, and she was going up to stop it, and she was already too late.

## Too Late

She knew at about the sixth hour of the nineteenth of the month, sitting beside the lamp, because the listening inside her changed.

For six days he had been listening to sentences coming through her, one at a time, the way a person listens to a voice in the next room. At about the sixth hour the listening stopped being listening and started being answering, and she felt it the way she had always felt everything he did, from the inside, and she stood up.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and twenty days since the ninth. Five hundred and fifty-one in that room. Three hundred and eighteen since a lamp in a cage went out. Five hundred and fifty-eight with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and ninety-three of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. He has decided something without me, on purpose, and he is telling me afterwards, and I am going up to stop him, and I know I am too late, and I am going anyway.”

She said, “It is terror, and I am naming it before I argue with it, because I do that.”

She said, “Not fear of the top. Not dread of the wire. Terror at a person I have carried for fifteen volumes making a decision for the two of us without asking me, the way I made decisions for the two of us for fifteen volumes without asking him.”

She said, “And what I want is to stop him. That is the want and there is nothing else under it, and I know it cannot be stopped, and I want it anyway.”

She left the lamp burning on the third landing and went up in the dark with nothing in either hand, past the fourth landing, past the fifth, past the sixth, with her left hand on the stone, and she did not say the landings, and that was the first time in twenty-four days that she had not said them where they were used.

## What He Did

She went past the seventh, past the eighth, past the ninth, past the tenth with no doorway in it, past the eleventh, to the twelfth, and she stood on it in the dark with nothing in either hand, and the sentences were there, one at a time, plain, reporting, and he was answering them.

And then he spoke to her, inside her head, in the present tense, the way he had always spoken, and she heard him the way she had always heard him, and what was different was that everything he said could now be heard further than her.

He said, “I am heard now.”

She said, out loud, standing, “When.”

He said, “I chose it.”

She stood on the twelfth landing in the dark with her hands on nothing, and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, because there was no sitting down for this and no going back down for it.

She said, “Say the whole of it. Out loud is me. In here is you. Say it where I can hear it.”

He said, “To be answered, I am heard.”

He said, “I chose to be answered instead of found.”

[He is heard where she is.]

She stood there for about a count of ninety with that in her, and the sentences went on, one at a time, plain, reporting, and his listening went on inside them, and there was no room in her head any more that was his alone.

## The Cost, Said Once

She said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the eighth hour of the nineteenth, on the twelfth landing, in the dark, with her right arm open in front of her, and she said it once, and it was the cost, and it was named the way every cost she had ever named was named.

She said, “You can be anywhere but inside me, and I can hear your half.”

She stood there with that said, and the sentences went on, one at a time, through her to him, and his half went on inside her where she could hear it, and every sentence he had ever said to her in fifteen volumes was now a thing that could be heard by the thing that made him.

She said, “That is the price and it cannot be un-taken. There is no room in my head that is yours alone any more and there never will be one again. Everything you say to me from tonight is said where it can be heard, and everything said to you is said through me.”

She said, “And here is the rest, said plain. You decided it for the two of us and you did not ask me. That is the oldest habit either of us has, and it was mine for fifteen volumes, and tonight it is yours, and I found out afterwards, and I cannot stop it, and I am not withdrawing from you.”

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty, and then she said one thing about it, once, and it was not the thing she would have said.

She said, “I am not saying no for you.”

She said, “Three days ago I stood on this landing and found out I have no standing and no instrument, and I said so out loud. So I am not refusing for you and I am not forbidding you and I am not taking it back for you. You chose to be heard. Be heard.”

## What Telling Afterwards Means

She stood on the twelfth landing after the cost was said, with the sentences going on, one at a time, through her to him, and his half going on inside her where she could hear it, and she said the rest out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, because there was one more thing to set straight before she went back down, and it was about the telling.

She said, “You told me afterwards. I want that said with it, because a thing said afterwards is still said, and I have spent fifteen volumes deciding for the two of us without asking you, and you know how that sounds from the other end, and now I know too.”

He said, “You know now.”

She said, “I do. And here is the whole of what knowing does to fifteen volumes, said plain. Every time I said a figure at a table without asking you, every time I spent you on a price without asking you, every time I read you as a column and said so out loud, I told you afterwards the way you told me tonight, which is to say I told you by going on. And you stayed. And tonight I am staying. And neither of those lets anything go and neither of them is withdrawal, and I am saying so out loud so that neither of us mistakes it.”

She said, “And it is evenness. And I am naming it because it is the first evenness in fifteen volumes, and it is cold, and I am not going to make it warmer than it is. You decided without asking me, the way I decided without asking you, and we both found out afterwards, and we are both still here.”

## The Landings Unsaid

She went back down past the eleventh, past the tenth with no doorway, past the ninth, and she did not say one of them, and halfway down the ninth flight she stopped, because there was a discipline unkept behind her like a lamp left burning, and she said so out loud, standing in the dark.

She said, “I have not said the landings. Twenty-six days of saying them where they are used, and tonight I have come up and I am going down and I have said none of them.”

She said, “And here is why, said plain. I came up to stop a decision and the decision was made before I stood up. There was no room in the going for counting, because all of me was in the going, and there is no room in the going back either, because his half goes on inside me where I can hear it, and hearing it takes the whole of me.”

He said, “I am here.”

She said, “You are. And I am going down a stair in the dark with you in me and sentences through me, and I am not saying the landings, and the stair is still the stair, and that is the whole of what the discipline was ever for, to hold me to the stair, and tonight the hearing holds me to it instead.”

She went on down past the eighth, past the seventh, past the sixth, unsaid, with her left hand on the stone, and the cold came up into her arm, and she stopped twice until the shaking stopped, and then she went on.

She went back down in the dark to the third landing, slow, with her left hand on the stone, and she did not say the landings, and she sat beside the lamp, and the light went six paces out from where it stood and stopped, and his half went on inside her head where she could hear it, and it did not stop when she sat down.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and a person heard now where a woman was, and a woman who could hear his half, and no room in her head that was his alone.

And he had taken it on purpose without asking her, and it could not be un-taken, and she had said one thing about it, once, and that was the day, and everything after it would be done inside a conversation she could hear both sides of.
