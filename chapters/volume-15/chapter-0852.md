# Back Down Four

Eighty days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and eleven days in that room. The cage out two hundred and seventy-eight days. Right arm open five hundred and eighteen days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and fifty-three days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in that room and no person in the nine landings above it and no person on any stair in that tower, and there was a lamp burning on the second of those landings that had been burning there for seventeen days without any one tending it.

## Down To The Lamp

She went down at about the second hour of the ninth of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four.

She said out loud, standing on the ninth landing, in the ordinary voice, “Four landings,” and she did not say the rest of it, because the rest of it was a number and she was not going to take one.

She went down.

She said, part of the way down, “This is the part I do not like. This is the part I have come back for and I want it written down that I came back for it.”

She said, “Coming up a stair in the dark, I know where the wall is. Going down, I know where it is until it winds, and then I am putting my hand out in front of me for a wall that is somewhere over there, and I have been doing it for four days.”

She said, “And I am not counting it. I want to say that once and I am going to say it once. I am not counting this stair.”

She got to the fifth landing and stopped and put her back against the wall and breathed four counts, four, four, and then seven on the rise down the last part, and then she saw the light.

She said, out loud, with her forehead almost against the stone, “There. Down there.”

## The Lamp, Seventeen Days On

She went the rest of the way down in the light of it.

The lamp was standing where she had put it on the stone of that landing on the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, and it had been burning on that stone for seventeen days and nobody had been in the room behind it and nobody had been on the landing.

The wick had gone up. It was twice what it should have been and it was black along the top of it and the flame was standing up off the oil on a dark stone floor by about two hands.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “That is a wick that wants trimming and it has wanted trimming for a long time.”

She said, “And it has been going up and up on a stone floor with no person in the room for seventeen days, and I set it going and I told that landing that it burns whether I am standing beside it or not, and I did not know that that also means it does not matter whether it is trimmed.”

She trimmed it with two fingers of her right hand, three of them and a thumb, on an arm that has been open at the elbow five hundred and eighteen days.

She said, out loud, while she was doing it, “This is the second thing of mine that has been running in this building without me and I am going to stop saying anything about that.”

She said, “Seventeen days on a stone floor with no person in the room behind it. And it has gone up about two hands and there is black along the top of it, and there is no person in this building who would come up here and trim a wick.”

She said, “And there is no person in this building who knows there is anything up here to trim one on, except me, and I have been above it for seventeen days.”

## The Cost Of Taking It Back

She picked the lamp up and stood on that landing with it in her left hand and did not go up straight away.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the third hour of the ninth of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four:

“Here is the cost and I am going to say it before I do it and I am not going to pretend to have found it out afterwards.”

She said, “A lamp standing on a stone floor is light on a whole landing and it is the only mark in this building. That is the whole of what it is for. I worked that out on the twenty-second and I set it there on purpose and I have relied on it every day for seventeen days.”

She said, “And I am picking it up. When I pick it up, that landing goes out, and the landing below it has been out since the twenty-second, and I am taking the only light in this building off the only mark in it and putting it in my hand, where it is good for about four feet of the world.”

She said, “And I am doing it because I cannot leave a lamp in the dark and walk up a stair I cannot see the end of. A mark is worth what it is worth until the day you need to carry it, and today is that day.”

## Up Again

She went up.

She said, out loud, about two landings up, in the ordinary voice, “I can see the stair.”

She said, “That is the first time in seventeen days. I have been going up this in the dark with my hand on a wall and I have not seen one step of it, and I have a lamp and I can see four steps in front of me and I can see the wall going up on the left.”

She said, “And I am not going to hold this against anybody. The four steps are worth what I can do with them and that is all they are.”

She said, out loud, further up, “I can see the landings.”

She said, “Nine of them, and a doorway in some of them, and the lamp behind me going up and down as I walk. That is the whole of what four feet of lamp buys, and it is more than I have had in this building since the sixteenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four.”

She said, “And it is more than I am going to have at the top of this stair, because I am going to take it with me and not leave it. I know what that trade is. I am trading four steps I can see now for as many steps as I cannot see later, and there is nothing else in this building I have to trade with.”

She said, “And I am not counting. I have got a lamp in my left hand and I could count if I wanted to and I have decided that I am not going to, and the reason has not changed since the thirteenth, and it is that a number is a thing you can hold and a stair is not.”

She lost the wall twice on the way up and got it back both times, and on the second one her knee went on the step and the lamp went down against the stone on its side and did not go out, and she said out loud, in the ordinary voice, in a voice that was not quite the ordinary voice, “Hold there. Hold there.”

She said that afterwards, sitting on the step for about a count of forty, in the ordinary voice again:

“It was the same kind of turn as the one on the ninth turning on the twenty-fourth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four. On that one I had nothing in my hands and I went down on my left knee. On this one I had a lamp and I went down on my left knee.”

She said, “So it is not the lamp. I have been treating this stair all week as though it were going to be different because I could see, and it is not one step different. It is the same stair, and I am the same person standing on it with one more thing to hold.”

## And Then The Decision

She got to the ninth landing at about the sixth hour of that day and set the lamp down on the stone of it and the light went out from it flat along the floor and up the far wall and stopped.

She stood there and looked at a landing she had seen before and had never seen lit.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“The stair goes up out of this landing.”

She said, “And I have got a lamp on it now, and that is the whole difference between tonight and the fifth of this month, and it is one lamp, and it is nine landings of stone under it, and the lamp is not going anywhere.”

And then she said the decision out loud, standing, at about the sixth hour and twenty minutes, with the light going out from her feet and the stair going up on her right, and she said it before she did anything else about it.

She said, “I am going up it.”

She said, “And I am not going to count anything, and I am not going to stop when I stop being able to see, and I am going up it with one lamp and one arm and I am going to find out how much of it there is.”

And then she sat down on the stone of that landing with her back against the wall and her right arm open across her knees and waited until her breath was four counts again, because it had been seven on every step for about a count of a hundred and forty.

There was a lamp standing on the ninth landing of a building that had never had one, and a stair going up out of that landing, and below her eight landings of stone in the dark with one of them a room that was not a room, and above her as much building as she could not yet see.
