# What She Left

Ninety-three days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and twenty-four days in that room. The cage out two hundred and ninety-one days. Right arm open five hundred and thirty-one days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and sixty-six days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was a boy of sixteen asleep on the fourth landing of that tower with oil on his hands, and a lamp standing on the second landing above a room that was not a room, and a woman going down nine landings in the dark to a room she had lived in for five hundred and twenty-four days and had never once walked out of on purpose.

## Why She Goes Down

She said it out loud at about the sixth hour of the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four, standing on the stone of the second landing with the lamp beside her, in the ordinary voice, before she did anything else about it.

She said, “I am going down to that room and I am going into it and I am going to say what is in it out loud, and then I am coming back up, and that is the whole of what I am doing and it is the last of it.”

She said, “And I want the reason out loud before I go, because a reason said afterwards is a reason you have made up.”

She said, “Here it is. I have a lamp and a stair and no plan, and that is the twenty-second of this month, and the first of this month was twenty-one days ago and I was nine landings above it. There is a room at the bottom of this stair that I have stood four feet off the near end of every morning since I was nine years old, and on the morning of the first I missed it on purpose, and I have not gone down for anything since.”

She said, “And I am going down because I am not going to be able to go any further up this stair without having left one thing undone, and the thing I have left undone is that I have never once said out loud what is in that room. Not to a person and not to the stair and not to the room itself, which is the room I have said every figure out loud into for five hundred and twenty-four days.”

She said, “So I am going to say it, and I am not going to take anything, and I am not going to change one thing about it.”

She said, “And that is the last time I am going to walk down this one, and I am not going to make anything of that, because it is a stair and it has been here all along.”

## The Way Down

She went down at about the seventh hour of the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four and she did not count it.

She left the lamp standing on the second landing and went down the rest of that building in the dark on her feet with her left hand on the stone and her right arm open in front of her, and then she went into the tower stair at the near side of that room and went down eight hundred and eighty steps in two runs of four hundred and forty, and she did not count that either, and the four openings came in at the same places they have come in since she first came up that stair nineteen years ago.

She said, out loud, at about the fourth opening, standing on the step, in the ordinary voice, “There is the sun on that wall opposite the opening for about a count of forty every morning and I have seen it about nine times in nineteen years and I have not stood in it once, and that is the whole of what this stair has ever been for me.”

## What Is In That Room

She came into that room at about the ninth hour of the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four, in the dark, four feet off the near end of that table, and she said what was in it, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and she did not touch any of it.

She said, “Three pieces of wood face up about a foot in from this end of this table. One of them is mine and one of them has a man’s name cut into it in a third hand and came up this stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand six hundred and eighty-four and has never gone back down. And the third one sets a place out at the far side of this table.”

She said, “And a fourth piece of wood about a foot further along, which is the six terms, and which I put back face up on the first of this month at about the seventh hour.”

She said, “And a strip of pale wood face up on the floor under this table at the near end of the leg, which came up this stair in an armful on the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and which I put back under here on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, and which has a price at the head of it and four figures of mine in it.”

She said, “And a bar nine feet nine flat against that wall on the left, broken end towards the door, that has been there four hundred and eighty-one days and that I have not lifted.”

She said, “And a bundle under the far end of this table with two turns of cord in it and not a knot, that I have put a hand into five times and never pulled.”

She said, “And a lamp in a cage on the shelf at the far end of this table that has been out two hundred and ninety-one days and that no one has lit.”

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “And a place at the far side of this table, kept four hundred years, empty, and refused twice.”

She said, “And I have not opened the bundle and I have not lifted the bar and I have not sat in the place and I have not lit the lamp in the cage, and I am not going to do any of those four things today.”

## The Six, On A Stair

And then she went out of that room and across it to the near wall and through the gap in it and stood on the bottom step of that stair in the dark.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour and twenty minutes of the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four, “I am going to say them here.”

She said, “And I have said them every morning since I was nine years old and I have said them in that room every single time, and today is the first day in nineteen years that I am saying them anywhere else, and I am not saying them in that room first and then coming out here and saying them again. I am saying them once and it is going to be here.”

She said, “And I am not holding the second one for a hundred and forty counts and I want to say why before I say it. I held it for a hundred and forty counts on the seventh of this month in that room because I was waiting for something. I am not waiting for anything here. There is no table at the bottom of this stair and there is no hand at the far end of it and there is nobody to hear a sentence before it is a sentence, and a thing I have been doing for nineteen years does not have anything to wait for out here.”

And then she said the six out loud, one at a time, standing up on that step, in the ordinary voice, in the dark, the way she has said them in every room she has ever said them in since she was nine years old.

She said, “One thing at a time.”

She said, “Standing up.”

She said, “You say it out loud as you go. Every line. Before it is a line anywhere else.”

She said, “If there is one I have not heard first then it is not a record, it is a thing you did on your own, and there is no version of this where I find out about it later.”

She said, “And if you put down one word that is not true, I will know it inside a day, and there is no road back for either of us.”

She said, “And it is a record. It is not a hand. You do not decide anything. I decide everything, and the only thing you have is that it is written down.”

And then she said the second one, alone, standing, in the ordinary voice, on a step in the dark, with a lamp standing on the first landing over her head and four miles of mountain under her feet.

She said, “In daylight.”

And then she said it again, and she said why.

She said, “I said that term in daylight once, on that tower stair, on the third of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four, with the sun on the wall opposite the second opening, and I said at the time that there was not going to be a second time and I have not found out that there is going to be a second time.”

She said, “It has not been about the light for two months. It has been about the stair.”

And then she stood there and did not say anything else about them, because there is no seventh and there is not going to be one.

## And Then She Went Up

She went back into that room at about the ninth hour and forty minutes and carried the lamp back up to the second landing and brought it down the rest of the way and set it on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table, and the light went a hand’s width along it and stopped.

And she did not say a figure on that table.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour and fifty minutes, “There is no figure this morning.”

She said, “And I have said a figure at this table at the seventh hour every morning since I was nine years old and I said nothing this morning, on purpose, standing here, and that is the last thing about this room that I am going to be able to say.”

She picked the lamp up again and carried it to the gap in the wall and through it and put it on the stone of the first landing, and she did not leave it there.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the tenth hour, “I am taking a lamp and I am carrying nineteen years of arithmetic in my head and there is no more of it than that. The wood is under the table and the wood stays under the table.”

And then she went up.

And she was never going to stand four feet off the near end of that table again, and she knew that at the tenth hour of the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four, standing on a stone landing in the dark with a lamp in her left hand and her right arm open in front of her, and she did not say it out loud and she was never going to.

There were three pieces of wood face up about a foot in from the near end of a table twenty-two paces long, and a fourth about a foot further along, and a strip of pale wood face up under that table at the near end of the leg, and a bar against the wall on the left, and a bundle under the far end corded twice and not knotted, and a lamp in a cage on the shelf at the far end that was not going to be lit, and a place kept four hundred years and empty at the far side of it.

And there was no person in that room at the tenth hour of the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four, and there was not going to be one on the first of the next month either, and the room carried on.
