# Nothing In My Hands

Sixty-five days since the ninth stage. Four hundred and ninety-six days in that room. The cage out two hundred and sixty-three days. Right arm open five hundred and three days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and thirty-eight days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in that room and no person in the three rooms above it and no person on any stair in that tower, and there was a lamp burning on the stone of the second landing of a stair that had never been lit before the seventh hour of the sixteenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four.

## The Dark Above A Light

She said the fear out loud before she said one word else about it, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the fourth hour and thirty minutes of the twenty-fourth, on the stair, in the dark, with nothing in her hands.

She said, “It is fear.”

She said, “It came up out of my back about two landings ago and it is here and it has not gone and I am going to say it while it is here instead of after it stops, because that is the whole of what this morning has been for.”

She said, “And I want to be exact about what it is. It is not the stair. I have been on worse stairs than this one. It is that I am four hundred and ninety-six days into a building and I am going up it with nothing in my hands in the dark at the fourth hour of the day, and there is no person in this building, and no person in the next four miles of mountain either.”

She stood on the step for about a count of ninety.

She said, “And it is not loneliness and I know the difference between the two after nineteen years of having both of them named at me. It is that a person has one hand on a wall and an arm open in front of her in the dark and there is a lamp about two landings behind her that is getting further away every time she goes up a step.”

She said, “And there is a second thing under the fear and I am not going to leave it in there where I do not look at it.”

She said, “It is that no person would come. Not would not want to. There is nobody in this building but me and nobody in the four miles of mountain under it, and there is a boy four miles down that mountain who does not know that I am up here at all, because I have not told him and I am not going to, and he has never once asked me where I go in the day.”

She said, “So if I go up this stair and do not come down, then what happens is that there is no one to find out for a long time, and the reason it happens is a decision I made on the sixteenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four at about the fourth second of a conversation on the fourth landing.”

She said, “That is what I bought with the lamp. I have thought about that and I am going to keep thinking about it and I have not taken it back.”

## When Her Hand Went Off The Wall

She lost the wall on the ninth turning.

She said that out loud afterwards, standing, in the ordinary voice, on her right knee on a stone step with her left hand flat on the step in front of her face and her right arm open in the dark.

She said, “I lost it on the ninth turning. There is a place where the wall goes round a corner and my hand went off it and there was about a second and a half of nothing at all and then the step, with my knee on it, hard, the left one, which is the one I do not put my weight on.”

She sat there for about a count of ninety and did not get up straight away.

She said, “Nothing came off after me. That is not because the stair is good. That is because it is about the breadth of two feet at that point and I was against the inside of it.”

She said, “And I am not going to stand here and make that into a thing it is not. I am going to put it down as a hand that went off a wall on a ninth turning at about the fifth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, because that is the only thing it was.”

She got up. She put her left hand back on the wall and she did not take it off again for the rest of that going up.

## The Third Landing

The third landing was the same size as the other two and it was colder than either of them, and there was a doorway in the far wall of it, and she went through the doorway.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the fifth hour:

“There is a table in here and I can find it with my hands.”

She said, “There is a leg at every two paces. I have been counting paces in the dark in rooms I have never seen in the light for about nineteen years and I do it with the ball of my hand and it is the same thing as counting and it is not counting the stair.”

She walked it twice.

She said, “Twenty-two.”

She said, “And it took about a count of ninety, and I said nothing at all while I did it, and that is the first count of ninety I have let go by in a room for longer than I can remember.”

She said, “The flag under that table moves at about the nineteenth pace. I have known that about my own table since I was about eleven years old. I put a cup on it once and it went over, and I have never worked out whether it moves because the stone under it is uneven or because something under the stone is, and I have decided not to work it out.”

She said, “And tonight it went at the nineteenth pace under a table I have never seen with my eyes, and my hand felt it do it, and I am not writing that down.”

She said, “And under the middle of it there is a stack of boards face up, and under the far end of it there is a bundle with two turns of cord in it and not a knot, and on a shelf at the far end there is a lamp in a cage, and it is out.”

She stood at the far end of that table with both hands on the wood and did not say anything for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “And that is the same room again.”

She said, “And I said, in the second one of them yesterday, that there was nothing on the near end of it. I want to say that this one has nothing on the near end of it either, and that I have not got my hands on anything at all between here and the near end.”

## What She Could See From The Doorway

She went back out into the doorway of that third room and stood in it.

From there she could see the light from the second landing coming up out of the stair below her and round the wall of that landing, and it was a small piece of light about two landings down, and she could not see the room the light was in at all.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“Three rooms above my room. And I have seen three of them and I have not counted a step of any of it, and I cannot tell from here how much of it there is.”

She said, “I do not know how many doorways are in this stair above me. I have found three of them and I have not gone past the fourth landing since the twenty-second, and I am not going to stand here and let not knowing read as carefulness.”

She said, “It was not careful. I have found three doorways and I have been inside all three of them, and I have been past three more landings since the twenty-second and I have not put my hand into one doorway on any of them. A person who wanted to know what was in every room would have been in all three of those as well, and I was not.”

She said, “And I am not going to pretend that the third one is the top. I have been the top of things in this building for four hundred and ninety-six days and I have never once been right about it, and I am not going to stand in a doorway at about the sixth hour of the twenty-fourth of a month and be wrong about it twice in the same building.”

## The Thing She Was Going To Do

She sat down on the stone of that third landing with her back against the wall and her right arm open across her knees and said the rest of it out loud in the ordinary voice.

She said, “Here is what I am going to do and it is small and I am going to do it anyway.”

She said, “I am not going to look for anything up here with a lamp. A lamp reaches about eleven paces along a table and a table is twenty-two, and I have been trying to see things at the far ends of these tables with light and it is the wrong instrument and I worked that out on the thirteenth.”

She said, “I am going to walk the length of each of these tables with my hand on the leg of it, the way I have walked the length of mine two hundred days running, and I am going to do it slowly, and I am going to feel the whole of it.”

She said, “And I know what I am going to find. That is the part I have not said yet. I have walked that leg about two hundred days with my hand on it and I have never once been looking for anything, and if there was something on that table my hand would have had it two hundred times over.”

She said, “And I did not look for it, because not one person in the world had ever asked me to.”

She said, “So I am going to be up here a while.”

And she stayed on that third landing in the dark with a lamp about two landings behind her and a fear she had said out loud, and at about the fifth hour and forty minutes of the twenty-fourth of that month she went back into that third room and put her hand on the leg of that table at the near end of it.
