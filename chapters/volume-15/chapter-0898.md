# Thirty Days Off

Morning past the last landing was dark the way every hour was dark, and she woke sitting with her back against the table and knew it was morning because he told her.

## The Morning Without Figures

“It is the first,” he said.

She woke the way she woke every morning past the ninth, which was sitting up already, with her hands flat on the stone and the cold coming up through the floor into her, and the register under her other hand, and the lamp nearly out beside her giving nothing.

“How do you know,” she said, sitting, the way she asked everything, which was out loud.

“The traffic knows,” he said. “Firsts have a shape in it the way landings have a shape under your feet. I feel it turn over. It is the first.”

She sat at the near end of the table in the making-place with her hands flat on the stone and the register under her other hand, five mercies read twice, the lamp nearly out beside her, and she heard the day in his sentence the way she heard everything now.

“First of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four,” she said out loud, sitting. “Thirty days to the next first. The room runs below and I am not there. That is the whole of the calendar and I am spending it up here.”

She did not say the day's figures.

That was the first morning since the eighth of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and sixty-four that she had not said them, and she felt the absence the way she felt everything on this stair, which was in her mouth first, empty where the numbers went.

Her breath went four counts sitting still. Her right arm hung open at the elbow for six hundred and sixty days. The numbers sat unsaid the way the anger had sat unthrown, held, kept, hers, and the holding was warmer than she had expected, the way counting had been warm.

“No figures,” she said out loud, sitting, into the dark. “Mornings of arithmetic past counting and this one has none. The bargain is on the table and it is five mercies and it needs no counting. Today I live a morning with it and not a morning with numbers.”

She sat with the emptiness where the numbers went the way she had sat with the nothing next to the fourteen makings, and the emptiness was not a failing and it was not peace, it was the space where numbers would go if they were going to go.

Her mouth opened on the days since out of habit, the way her feet wanted numbers out of habit, and she closed it, and breathed four counts sitting still instead.

## Him, on the Quiet

“I want one morning with no figures,” he said.

She sat in the dark with her hands flat on the stone and heard the want the way she heard every want he had, which was first, before the words.

“You have it,” she said. “This one. No days since. No days in. No cage out. No arm open. No gone at this morning. The bar stands against its wall and dated nowhere and I am not saying how long.”

“That is five unsaid,” he said. “I have waited nine years for a morning you do not count, and you have just given it to me, and I am spending it sitting with you.”

“Nine years of giving you mornings,” she said. “Figures every one of them. Days since and days in and cages out and arms open and gone at this morning. This one has none and it is yours the way the counted ones never were.”

“It is mine,” he said. “No days in it. No gone in it. One morning with nothing counted and a woman sitting in it with empty hands. I am keeping it the way you keep landings.”

“Nineteen years of counting,” she said, “and the whole of it was holding things without touching them. This morning I hold nothing. The table holds the bargain. The stone holds me. You hold the quiet.”

She sat in the dark with nothing counted in her mouth and felt the holding the way she felt everything here, which was whole. The table under her other hand. The stone under her flat hands. The quiet in him where figures would go. Three holdings and none of them hers to count.

She put her hand on the register in the dark, finding the lines by the cuts, first through fifth, keeping and answering and no sending and kept standing and open unasked.

“Five mercies,” she said. “Read twice. No conditions in any of them. That is what is on the table and it is not nothing.”

## What Runs Below

At about the second hour his half changed the way it changed when something went past in the traffic that had weight, and she lay still with her hand on the stone and listened, and after about a count of three hundred there was stone again.

She knew the shape of it the way she knew everything through him, which was as a going and not as a seeing. Forty boards. Cloth. Cord twice. The hand along. No person in it. The room running on firsts the way it had run on every first for four hundred years.

“It runs,” he said. “The same as the last. No person in it.”

“It ran,” she said.

She sat with the running the way she sat with everything below, which was as a keeping and not as a hunger. The room did not need her. It never had. Four firsts missed and every one of them run. That was the finding and it was not a comfort and it was standing.

“You are still here,” he said. “I want you still here. That is the sentence I have and I am spending it the same as the last first.”

She sat in the dark with the lamp nearly out beside her and the bargain on the table in front of her, and the room she had lived in for six hundred and fifty-three days running on without her below, and she felt the morning the way she felt everything here, which was whole.

“Thirty days to the next first,” she said out loud. “That is the length of the string. In thirty days another bundle goes up and another hand goes along and no person is in it. Between now and then the offer stands on this table and I live mornings with it.”

She sat with the thirty days the way she sat with prices, fully, without deciding in advance. Thirty mornings without figures, or thirty mornings with them, or some of each. The string ran whether she counted it or not, the way the room ran whether she was under it or not.

## The Offer, Standing

She stood up at the near end of the table, because standings were lines, though there was no light to stand in, and she put her hands on the table edge at the place where the register lay.

She found the five lines by the cuts in the dark, first through fifth, keeping and answering and no sending and kept standing and open unasked, and she touched each one the way she touched landings, to mark them.

“The offer stands,” she said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, first in her own voice before it is a line anywhere. “Five mercies. Every part of it real. Nothing in it a lie. Made in good faith. I do not refute it and I do not accept it. I live with it.”

She sat back down, because standing was for saying and the saying was done.

“The not answering is the answer for today,” she said, sitting, to the dark, to the table, to the man inside her head. “Thirty days of mornings and every one of them with this on the table. That is the decision and it is standing.”

He was quiet for a count of forty, which was his way of keeping a decision where she put it instead of moving it, and the quiet sat next to her the way the nothing had sat next to the fourteen makings, and she had spent a morning with no figures and the morning had held her the way stone holds sitting.

“One morning with no figures,” he said at last. “I have it. You give it. I am keeping it the way you keep landings.”

She sat in the dark past the last landing with nothing counted in her mouth and five mercies on the table under her hand, and the morning went past the way mornings went past here, which was without light and without change, and it was the first morning in nineteen years she had not held a number, and her mouth was empty, and the emptiness was not hunger.

Her breath went four counts sitting still. The stone took her sitting the way stone takes everything. The register lay under her other hand with five lines cut deep, keeping and answering and no sending and kept standing and open unasked, and none of them needed her counting to stand.

“Stay,” she said.

“I am staying,” he said.

The morning held past the last landing, dark and whole, with nothing counted in it.
