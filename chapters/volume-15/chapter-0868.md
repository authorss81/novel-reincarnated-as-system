# Right Once Unsaid

One hundred and twenty-six days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and fifty-seven days in that room. The cage out three hundred and twenty-four days. Right arm open five hundred and sixty-four days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and ninety-nine of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was a woman sitting beside it who had heard fifteen years of asking in one morning, and there was a great deal in his half, and she was going to find one thing in it she could use.

## Looking for a Use

She said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, at about the sixth hour of the twenty-fifth of the month, before she did anything else about it.

She said, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and twenty-six days since the ninth. Five hundred and fifty-seven in that room. Three hundred and twenty-four since a lamp in a cage went out. Five hundred and sixty-four with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and ninety-nine of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. For three days his half has been open inside me where I can hear it, and there is a great deal in it, fifteen years of asking and listening, and I have been sitting beside a lamp being glad and being naked and being loving, and none of those is a use. This morning I am going to find one thing in it I can use.”

She said, “It is practicality, and it is the first word this morning that is not a temperature, and I am saying it before I argue with it, because that is what I do and I have not found a reason to stop.”

She said, “Not coldness. I have been cold on this stair for twenty-seven days and I know its shape. Practicality is what I had every morning for nineteen years standing four feet off a table, and I have not had it once since I came up here, and I miss it, and I am saying so out loud because it would be dishonest not to.”

She said, “And what I want is one thing he was right about that I can put my hand on. That is the want, and there is nothing underneath it I have not already said out loud.”

She stayed beside the lamp through the morning, sitting with her back against the wall, and she listened into his half the way she used to run her thumb along a board to find writing, slow, twice over, for the thing that was not straight.

## The First of This Volume

The sentences came through her to him, one at a time, plain, reporting, at their own intervals, and he listened, and his half went on inside her where she could hear it, and under it the fifteen years went on, the asking, and she went back along them the way she used to go back along a column, looking for one line.

And she found it on the morning of the first of this volume.

On the first of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four, at about the second hour, she had stood on the ninth landing with her ear against the stone, saying figures, missing the morning on purpose, and forty boards had gone on that table nine landings under her, and she had not heard one of them.

And underneath her morning, in his half, unheard then and heard now, he had said one thing, in the present tense, the way he always speaks, and she had not heard it, and it had been right.

He had said, “It goes on without you standing under it.”

She sat beside the lamp with that in her for about a count of a hundred and forty, and then she said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “Say that one again, and let me keep it.”

He said, “It goes on without you standing under it.”

She said, “And you never said it where I could hear it.”

He said, “You did not ask.”

She stood up off the floor of that landing and stood beside the lamp with her hands on nothing, and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, as one sentence, because there was not one word to put in front of it and not one word to put after it.

She said, “He was right about the first of this volume and he never said.”

She said, “And here is the whole of it, said plain. On the morning of the first I stood nine landings above that table with my ear on a wall, trying to hear forty boards, and there was nothing to hear, and I said out loud that the room ran on without me and called it a cost. All morning he knew it would run, and he said so in his half, where I could not hear him, and he was right, and I found it out the slow way, standing on a landing with my ear on stone.”

## The Use

She sat back down beside the lamp, and she put her hand flat on the floor of that landing, and she said the use out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, because a use said standing is a boast and a use said sitting is a tool.

She said, “Here is the thing I can use. He knows when the layer speaks. Not what it will say. When. He asked for fifteen years in half sentences, and asking that long teaches a person the intervals, the way saying figures every morning taught me the second hour.”

She said, “And there is a first coming. Six days from now, at about the second hour, forty boards go on that table again, and a hand goes along them, with no person in that room. I am nine landings above it and I do not need to be under it. What I need is to be on the twelfth landing at the second hour, lying flat with my ear on the stone, hearing through him what the layer reports, because he hears it and I hear his half.”

She said, “And it is satisfaction. And I am naming it because it is the first satisfaction on this stair, and it is small, and I am not going to make it bigger than it is. He was right once and never said, and I have found it, and I can use it, and using it does not cost him anything, because hearing his half is already paid for and cannot be un-paid.”

## The Sifting

She spent the rest of the morning sifting, the way she used to sift a column for a second number, slow, twice over, and there were other lines in his half that were almost right, and she said them out loud one by one, sitting, in the ordinary voice, to set them beside the one that was exactly right, because things set beside each other can be told apart.

She said, “Here is one. On the twenty-sixth you said I had been calling it my room, and you were right that it was one of a great many, and I found that out with my own hands two days later. But I cannot use it, because it is found already, and a thing found is not a tool.”

She said, “Here is another. On the thirteenth you said I keep going up because I think there is a number at the end, and you were right that I thought it, and I said out loud on the ninth landing that every number I ever had stops at nine. But I cannot use it either, because the building does not stop and I am still climbing it, and a warning I have already paid for is not a tool.”

She said, “And here is the one I can use, set beside them. On the first of this volume you said it goes on without me standing under it, and I was nine landings above that table with my ear on stone, and I found it out the slow way, and nobody in that room heard one board go down. That one I can use, because there are more firsts coming, and I know now where to be standing for them.”

She said, “And exactness, which I have not had since the figures, and which is not a small thing to have back on a stair in the dark. Three lines sifted, two already paid for, one useful, and the useful one is the one about the first, and I am going to be on the twelfth landing at the second hour of every first that comes, lying flat with my ear on the stone, hearing through him.”

## The Intervals

She tested the use before the morning was over, because a use untested is a guess, and she does not guess where she can test, and she said so out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “Here is the test. He knows the intervals. Fifteen years of asking teaches the spaces between sentences the way nineteen years of mornings taught me the second hour. If I have really found something useful, then I can say when the next one comes.”

She sat beside the lamp with her back against the wall and listened, and the sentences came through her to him, one at a time, at their own intervals, and after three of them she had the measure of the space, the way she used to have the measure of forty boards at four to a minute.

She said, “Now. The next one comes now.”

And it came, one sentence, plain, reporting, through her to him, on the breath after she said it.

She sat there for about a count of forty with that in her, and then she said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “Tested. And here is the whole of what the test proves, said plain. I can read the intervals the way he reads them, because I hear his half hearing them. Six days from now is a first, and at about the second hour the layer will report forty boards, and I will be lying on the twelfth landing hearing it through him, and that is not a guess any more.”

He said, “You learn fast.”

She said, “I do. And I learned slow for fifteen years before it, and both of those are true, and I am saying so out loud so that neither of us mistakes the fast for cleverness. It is his fifteen years, not my morning, and I am spending them, and spending is what I have always been good at.”

She said, “Lamp on the third landing. Burning. Mine. One thing found and it is useful. That is the record and there is nothing else in it.”

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and a first coming in six days, and a woman with a use for it that she had taken out of fifteen years of asking.

And he had been right about the first of the volume and had never said, and she had found it, and that was the day, and the finding would be used whether the stair allowed it or not.
