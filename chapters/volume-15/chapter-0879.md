# The First Without Her

One hundred and sixty-two days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and ninety-three days in that room. The cage out three hundred and sixty days. Right arm open six hundred days and it is not going to close. One thousand three hundred and thirty-five of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was no woman beside it, and there was a first of a month at the bottom of the building, and she was nine landings above it, and she did not need to be under it.

## Above on a First

She had gone up at about the first hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and ninety-four, in the dark, past the lamp, past the fourth landing, past the fifth, with her left hand on the stone, and she had said the landings where they were used, and she had lain flat on the twelfth landing with her ear on the stone before the second hour came.

She said, out loud, lying, in the ordinary voice, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and sixty-two days since the ninth. Five hundred and ninety-three in that room. Three hundred and sixty since a lamp in a cage went out. Six hundred with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand three hundred and thirty-five of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. Yesterday I named the Anchor standing, and all night the name stayed in me the way a said word stays. This morning is a first, and at about the second hour forty boards go on that table at the bottom of this building, and I am on the twelfth landing with my ear on the stone, and I am not going down for it.”

She said, “It is steadiness, and it is the first steadiness after awe, and it is small.”

She said, “Not triumph, which I had yesterday and know its shape. Steadiness at lying above a first I will not go down for, because the naming is done and the room does not need the namer under it.”

She said, “And what I want is to be somewhere when it happens. The twelfth. I can hold the twelfth.”

She lay flat on the stone of the twelfth landing with her ear against it, and the cold came up through the floor into her, and her breath went nine counts lying still, and she stayed there, and at about the second hour the sentences started.

## What Ran Below

They came through her to him, one at a time, plain, reporting, at their own intervals, and he listened, and his half went on inside her where she could hear it, and in his half she heard the room.

He said, “Forty go down.”

She lay on the stone with her ear against it and said nothing, because there was nothing to say that would not stop her hearing.

He said, “The cloth goes round.”

He said, “The cord goes round twice.”

She put her hand flat on the stone beside her ear, and the steadiness was there under her hand, regular as work, and his half went on inside her, quiet, reporting what it heard, the way she used to report what she saw.

He said, “The hand goes along.”

She lay there for about a count of three hundred, and the sentences went on, one at a time, and his half went on under them, and then the sentences stopped for a space, the way they stop when a working is done, and his half went quiet.

She got up off the floor of that landing and stood in the dark with nothing in either hand, and her arm shook, and she stood still until it stopped, and then she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “The room runs, and the hand goes along the table, and there is no person in it.”

She said, “And here is the whole of it, said plain. Forty boards went on that table at about the second hour of the first. A cloth went round them and a cord went round the cloth twice. The far end of that table went along the boards the way it has gone four hundred years. Han Liang put them down and went back out.”

## The Hand Going Along

She stood on the twelfth landing after the whole was said, with nothing in either hand, because the hand wanted staying with, and she stayed with it standing.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “Here is the hand, said plain. It goes along forty boards the way it has gone four hundred years of boards, with no person standing under it, heard secondhand through a person inside my head. The hand is steady. The steadiness is the oldest thing in this building. Older than my column. Older than the Anchor named. The hand goes along whether the named is named or not.”

She said, “And here is the whole of what steadiness means, said plain. My naming does not touch it. My missing does not stop it. My hearing secondhand does not slow it. Four hundred years of going along, and yesterday it went along again, and tomorrow after the next first it will go along again, and none of those goings needs me.”

She said, “And smallness, sudden, true, and I am naming it because it is the first smallness after steadiness, and I am not going to hide it. Nineteen volumes I thought the working needed the worker. The working needs the hand and the boards and the cloth and the cord. The worker is the wire it passes through. I am the wire. That is smaller than worker, and it is true.”

He said, “You hear true.”

She said, “I do. And hearing true finishes nothing, and I am saying so out loud. It is one hand heard, and the taking goes on.”

## The Smallness Carried

She stayed standing after the hearing was said, with nothing in either hand, because the smallness wanted carrying down, and she carried it standing.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “Here is the carrying, said plain. Wire, not worker. Small, not slighted. The working needs the hand and the boards. I am the length between. Lengths carry. That is enough length for one day.”

## The Cost of Not Going Down

She came down in the dark, nine landings, with her left hand on the stone the whole way, and the cold went out of her arm somewhere around the fifth and did not come back for a space. She sat beside the lamp, and the light went six paces out from where it stood and stopped, and she stayed inside the reach of it until her breath went four counts sitting still.

She said, out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, “Here is what the day cost, and I am saying it sitting down beside the lamp instead of saying it afterwards, because afterwards is where I make things up.”

She said, “The room runs and I am not under it. The Anchor is named and I did not go down to name it there. Two workings going on without the tender, a room and a naming, and I am the tender of both, and both went on today.”

She said, “And here is the part that is actually on me, said plain. Missing a first on purpose is a cost I chose twice before, and choosing it again after the naming is colder, because the naming was for the room and the room did not hear it. The Anchor was said nine landings above the tables it holds.”

She said, “And bitterness, quiet, and I am not going to sweeten it. A true word said where the kept cannot hear it is still true, and still unheard where it matters, and I chose the twelfth over the table, and the choosing holds.”

## Sitting With the First Spent

She sat beside the lamp a long time after the cost was said, with her back against the wall and her right arm open across her knees, and his half went on inside her, quiet, the way he goes quiet after a working, and she sat with it.

She said, out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, “Here is where the day leaves me. A lamp burning that I cannot refill. A first spent above the room that ran without me. An Anchor named that the room did not hear. A person in my head who heard both.”

He said, “You stay.”

She said, “I do. And staying is the whole of tonight, and I have done it, and the staying holds.”

She said, “Lamp on the third landing. Burning. Mine. A first run through without me. That is the record and there is nothing else in it.”

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and forty boards on a table a long way under it with a cloth round them and a cord round the cloth twice, and no person in that room.

And she had been somewhere when it happened, and the somewhere was above it, and that was the day, and tomorrow the work would turn to who pays for what she named.
