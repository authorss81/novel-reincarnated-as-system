# The First Without Her

She went up at about the first hour in the dark and left the lamp on the third landing where it had lain since the twenty-third of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four and went past it, past the fourth, past the fifth, saying each one where it was used, and she did not go out with a figure in her mouth because it was the first of a month and she had something else to do with the morning.

At about the second hour she lay down on the stone of the twelfth landing with her ear against the floor.

Two hundred and seventy days in that room, and she had been under every one of the firsts of a month that had fallen in it, and not one of them had come up through the stone to her from below. The bundle came up the second stair on the firsts whether or not she was there to hear it, and she had never once laid her ear on a floor on a first.

She was going to do it now.

“One hundred and sixty-two days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and ninety-three days in that room. The cage out three hundred and sixty days. This arm open six hundred days and it is not going to close. One thousand three hundred and thirty-five gone at this morning and not one coming back.”

Then she stopped, because she had said the figures on the first of a month going up a stair at about the first hour and that was nineteen days of habit in the wrong place, and there was a working about to start under her and she did not want to be a woman counting.

## The Second Hour, Nine Landings Down

The sentences started about a quarter past.

They came through the stone and through her, one at a time, plain, reporting, and went on to him, and his half went on inside her where she could hear it. She lay still with her ear flat and let them go.

Then, underneath them, something else started.

She had never heard it before because she had never lain on a landing on a first with her ear on the floor, and it was not a sentence and it was not a voice. It was the sound forty boards make when they go onto a table twenty-two paces long, from the bottom of a building that she had left two hundred and seventy days ago and had not gone back into.

He said, “Forty go down.”

She lay still and said nothing, because anything she said would have gone through her to him and she did not want to put a word in the middle of a working.

“The cloth goes round.”

“The cord goes round twice.”

She put her hand flat on the stone beside her ear. The sound came up through nine landings of building and arrived in her hand as a small steady pressure that went on and stopped and went on again, the way work goes on.

“The hand goes along.”

She lay there for about a count of three hundred while the sentences went on above her and the working went on below her and his half went on between the two of them, and then the working stopped the way a working stops when a working is done, and the sentences stopped for a space after it, and his half went quiet.

She got up off the floor of that landing and stood in the dark with nothing in either hand and her breath going nine counts standing still, and her arm shook, and she stood still until it stopped, and then she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice.

“The room runs.”

## What Ran Below

“Forty boards onto that table at about the second hour of the first. A cloth round them and a cord round the cloth twice. The far end of the table went along the boards the way it has gone along boards four hundred years. Han Liang put them down and went back out.”

She said it and then she said the thing that mattered about it.

“There was no person in that room.”

She had been under every first of a month in that room for two hundred and seventy days and she had watched the hand go along the table four hundred years' worth of mornings, and she had thought of it as a working that happened around her.

It had not been happening around her. There was not one person in it. There had never been one.

## Nobody Asked Him to Listen

“You were not sent for that.”

“No.”

“Nobody asked you to put your ear on it.”

“No,” he said. “I have been listening to that room for nine years and you have never once let me tell you what I hear in it, and today I put it in your ear myself because you were lying on a floor with your face against the stone and I could see the whole of it.”

She stood in the dark on the twelfth landing and let that go past her.

“I have been giving you figures every morning for nineteen days,” he said. “Every one of them was yours. That was the job. I have never once given you a thing I heard because I wanted you to have it, because you would have found a figure in it and taken it apart.”

“Was that the job.”

“That was the job.”

“And today.”

“Today I wanted you to have it,” he said. “It is not the same arrangement and you may as well know which of the two you are getting.”

She stood there and found that she did not have a figure for that and did not need one.

## Wire

She went over the whole of it once more standing, out loud, because a thing heard secondhand has to be said twice before the person who heard it will carry it.

“Wire, not worker. I have spent nine years thinking the working needed the worker. The working needs the hand and the boards and the cloth and the cord. The worker is the length the sentences travel along.”

She put her hand against the wall of the landing.

“A length carries. A length is not in the room. That is smaller than worker and it is true, and I have been calling myself the one who keeps that room going for two hundred and seventy days, and the room has been getting on perfectly well without me and went on doing the same thing this morning at about the second hour.”

She was quiet for a moment.

“I heard the hand go along from nine landings up through my own chest and the hand did not know I was there, and neither did the room, and neither did the bundle, and that is exactly what a wire is and exactly what I have been calling something else.”

## Going Back Up To Check

She went back up at about the fourth hour and lay down on the stone of the twelfth landing with her ear on the floor and gave it a count of three hundred.

The sentences went on. His half went on. Nothing came up from below.

She gave it another count of three hundred and then another, and on the fourth she took her ear off the stone because her face was going numb, and she lay on her back in the dark looking at a ceiling she had never seen, and said the finding out loud, lying, in the ordinary voice, because she had made this mistake once already this morning and was not going to make it twice.

“The wire carries the sentences. It does not carry the working. The working went through the floor for about a count of three hundred at about the second hour and after that it was gone, and the stone did not have it in it any more.”

She sat up.

“So this is an instrument with an hour on it. There is a working at about the second hour on the first of a month and it comes up through nine landings and after it there is stone. I can be here. I cannot be here all the time, and I am not going to sit on this floor waiting for a next.”

That was the other half of the cost, and it was a worse one than the not-going-down, because it meant the thing she had found this morning could be found again exactly thirty times in a life and not once of them would be on a day she chose.

## The Cost of Not Going Down

She came down in the dark, nine landings, with her left hand on the stone the whole way, and the cold went out of her arm somewhere around the fifth and did not come back for a space.

She sat down beside the lamp and said the cost sitting, in the ordinary voice, beside the light and not afterwards, because afterwards is where she made things up.

“The room ran and I was nine landings above it. I named the Anchor yesterday and the room did not hear it. Two workings went on today without the tender of either. I am the tender of both, and both went on.”

She put her hand on the place behind her fourth rib and took it off again without needing to.

“I have missed a first on purpose before. Twice. Both of those were cold and both of those were mine. This one is colder, because yesterday I said out loud where sentences go that the walls protect the gathered will of every soul that ever died believing it could ascend, and the tables under that ceiling are the whole of the trade, and I said it nine landings above them.”

She sat with it and let the stone go on being cold.

“A true word said where the kept cannot hear it is still true, and it is still unheard where it matters, and I chose the twelfth over the table.”

## Where the Day Leaves Her

She sat beside the lamp a long time after that with her back against the wall and her open arm across her knees and the light six paces out from where it stood and stopping.

“A lamp burning that I cannot refill. A first spent above the room that ran without me. An Anchor named yesterday that the room did not hear. A person in my head who heard both and who gave me one thing this morning that he was not asked for.”

She put her hand flat on the cold stone beside her and left it there.

The room had run.

It would run on the first after the next too, and on the first after that, and there was nothing she could do about any of it from where she was sitting, and she had chosen where she was sitting at about the second hour this morning with her whole hand and no reason.
