# Forty in the Dark

One hundred and four days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and thirty-five days in that room. The cage out three hundred and two days. Right arm open five hundred and forty-two days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and seventy-seven of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was no woman beside it, and there were forty boards going on a table a long way under her in the dark, and she was under the building the way a roof is under weather, and she heard none of it and knew all of it.

## The Morning Under Her

She had gone up at about the first hour of the third of the month, in the dark, past the lamp, past the fourth landing, past the fifth, with her left hand on the stone and her right arm open in front of her, and she had stopped on the sixth landing to get her breath, and that was where she was at about the second hour, standing in the dark with her hand flat on the wall.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and four days since the ninth. Five hundred and thirty-five in that room. Three hundred and two since a lamp in a cage went out. Five hundred and forty-two with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and seventy-seven of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. I am on the sixth landing in the dark at about the second hour, and under me at the bottom of this building forty boards are going on a table, and I am saying that out loud because I said yesterday that I would miss it on purpose, and missing a thing on purpose still means being somewhere when it happens.”

She stood on the sixth landing with her hand on the wall and stayed there for about a count of a hundred and forty, and there was nothing to hear, and nine landings of building stood between her ear and that table, and she could not have heard a board go down on it if one went down at that moment.

She said, “There is nothing to hear. I put my ear against stone for that once and I am not doing it again, because once was a finding and twice would be a habit.”

She said, “And here is the figure, and it is the only one this morning. Forty. That is what goes on that table while I am under this building, four to a minute, ten minutes, and then thirty counts for the cloth and the cord going round them twice.”

She said, “And it is behind me now. It went on without me and it did not stop, and I am saying that standing up in the dark, because yesterday I said it sitting down and today I am higher.”

She said, “It is grief, and it is the second time since the twenty-sixth, and I am saying so out loud because the second one is heavier than the first and I will not have the first one standing for both.”

She said, “Yesterday it was sorrow at sameness. Today it is grief at going on. A room I stood in for nineteen years went on this morning with no person in it, and a hand went along forty boards at the far end of it, and not one person heard one, and I chose to be six landings above it, and the choosing is the grief, and there is nothing under it but that.”

She said, “And what I want is to get above the ninth before this morning is over. Nothing else. That is the whole of it and I am not going to stand here and pretend it is a bigger want than it is.”

## Past the Ninth

She went on up in the dark, past the seventh landing, past the eighth, with her left hand on the stone and the cold coming up out of it into her arm, and her breath went seven counts on the rise, and she stopped twice and stood still until it went four counts again, and then she went on.

She said, out loud, on the eighth landing, “Eighth.”

She said, “And that is a landing and not a stage, and there is nothing cut in my ribs about it.”

She went on up to the ninth landing, and she stood on it in the dark with nothing in either hand, and the stair went up out of it, and she put her left hand flat on the stone going up at the same pitch as every other one of them.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the third hour, “Ninth.”

She said, “And that is the one I came out of eleven days ago with a lamp in my hand and six terms said out loud on a stair. The lamp is six landings below me tonight and I have nothing in my hands at all, and I am standing on the ninth for the first time without either of those.”

She went up past it, past nine, into the part of the building she had been in once before and come back down out of, and the wall stayed on her left for four steps and then there was nothing on the right, and she took her hand back and went on with one hand on the wall and nothing on the other side of her.

She said, “Tenth.”

And the word went out into the dark and did not come back.

She went on until she could not see the light of nothing, because there was no light, and until the cold got into her arm so far that it shook, and then she stopped and sat down with her back against the left-hand wall, and she stayed there.

## What Is Behind and What Is Ahead

She sat above the ninth landing in the dark with her right arm open across her knees, and she said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, because it had to be said and there was no standing up further to do it from.

She said, “Here is where I am. Above the ninth. The morning’s forty went on under me in the dark and I did not hear one of them and I knew every one of them. That is behind me and it is done and it cannot be undone.”

She said, “And here is what is ahead. The building goes on. I said twenty days ago that it does not stop at nine, and I have spent twenty days getting slower on the way to finding out what that means, and tonight I am past the ninth again with nothing in my hands, and it still does not stop.”

She said, “And it is resolve. And I am naming it because it is the first warm thing in eight days, and it is small, and I am not going to make it bigger than it is. I am above the ninth and the morning is behind me and the building goes on, and I am going on with it.”

She said, “And here is the last thing, and then I am going back down to that lamp. I have missed two firsts on purpose now, and both of them went on without me, and I am still here, and the stair is still here, and the lamp is still burning on the third landing, and that is more than I had eleven days ago, and I am saying so out loud because it would be dishonest not to.”

She came back down out of the high part of the building with her left hand on the stone, saying each landing as she came to it, and she went slower on the way down than on the way up, because going up she had something to get to. She sat beside the lamp, and the light went six paces out from where it stood and stopped, and she stayed inside the reach of it until her arm stopped shaking.

## What She Does Not Count

She sat beside the lamp for a long time before her arm stopped shaking, and while she sat she did the morning’s count over in her mouth, the way she had done every count since she was nine years old, to see if it would hold.

She said, out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, “Forty. That is the figure and it is the whole of it.”

She said, “And I am not counting the boards one by one, because I was six landings above them in the dark and no person counts what she cannot see. I am counting the number that goes on that table while I am under this building, and it is forty, and it is the same forty that has gone on it for four hundred years.”

She said, “And I am not counting the steps I climbed tonight. I counted landings and not steps the first night I went up past the light, and I have kept it every night since, and that is ten nights, and tonight I kept it past the ninth and past the tenth, and the count I have is landings and not one step more than that.”

She said, “And here is why, said plain, because tonight I found out. A step is a thing my feet do in the dark whether I count it or not. A landing is a thing I say out loud standing up, and saying it is the only light I have got up there. I am not spending the only light I have got on steps.”

She put her left hand flat on the stone of the third landing and felt the cold come up through it, steady, the same cold that had been in every floor of that building for five hundred and thirty-five days.

She said, “That is the discipline and it holds. Landings and not steps. Said where they are used. And tonight it held past nine, which is further than any number of mine has ever held.”

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and forty boards on a table a long way under her with a cloth round them and a cord round the cloth twice, and a hand gone along them and gone, and a woman six landings above where she had been that morning and higher than she had been in twenty days.

And the building went on above the ninth, and she was in it, and that was the morning, and there was more of it past where she had stopped, and she had not seen the end of it.
