# Not Waiting

Forty-nine days since the ninth stage. Four hundred and eighty days in that room. The cage out two hundred and forty-seven days. Right arm open four hundred and eighty-seven days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and twenty-two of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table with the light going a hand’s width along it and stopping, and there was no person in that room at the seventh hour, and there was a boy asleep on the fourth landing of that tower with oil on his hands.

## The Cold, And Then The Other Thing

She said the cold out loud first, standing, in the ordinary voice, four feet off the near end of that table, because that is the order she has used since the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen, and because it is the thing about her that she trusts.

She said, “It is the cold. It came up out of the stone out of my ankles on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen and it has been on and off since and it is not new.”

She said, “And it is not the whole of what I am feeling this morning and I am not going to let it stand as the whole, because the other thing is seven days old and it is about eleven times the size of the cold.”

She said, “A piece of paper went down nine hundred and four steps on the first of this month in a cloth with forty boards on the top of it, and nothing has come back up. I said out loud that I would not wait again and I have not waited and I am going to keep not waiting, and that is a decision and not a mood, and I made it at about the second hour and thirty minutes on the seventh and it has not moved since.”

## Not This Morning

And then she said the thing about the six out loud, before she did anything else, because she wanted the shape of the morning said first.

She said, “I am not going to say them this morning.”

She stood still for about a count of ninety.

She said, “I want that said where it happened. I said them in the dark on the seventh of this month at about the ninth hour and twenty minutes and I said the second one alone and held it a hundred and forty counts, and that place at the far side of that table has been kept four hundred years and I am the only person who has ever had to say that word about it, and it is done. It is not going to be done again in here.”

She said, “And that is not a rule. I have said that once this morning and I am not going to say it twice. It is that a person who has said a thing every morning since she was nine years old is allowed one morning where she stands up and says out loud that she is not doing it today, and nothing comes off anything, and it is the only difference I have got.”

She said, “And there is no seventh thing and I am not adding one.”

## Not Speaking To Him

She said, “And there is a voice in my head and it is a person and I am not speaking to him. I said that out loud in this room on the seventh of this month at about the ninth hour and ten minutes and I have not said one word to him since and I am not going to say it again after this one.”

She said, “That has been one day now and it has not been difficult, and I want that said out loud too, because the thing I expected to be difficult was not difficult.”

She put her hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “He is readable. That is paid for and it is not explained and I did not agree to it and I was not in the room. And I have said no twice, out loud, where the far end of this table could hear me. And those two things do not cancel. Both of them stand here this morning and I am not going to spend the seventh hour of the eighth of a month working out which of them counts.”

## What A Question Is For

She said, “And here is what I do, and it is the whole of what I have got, and it is not complicated.”

She said, “I ask where a thing comes from before I use it. That is the order and it has been the order since I was seven years old, and it is the only rule I have got that came before this room.”

She said, “I have asked it about water. I have asked it about oil. I have asked it about a bar of wood and where that came from and who cut it. I have asked it about a man’s hands. I have asked it about where a figure comes from before it gets into a column at the far end of a table, and that one took me eleven years and a person is in the answer to it.”

She said, “And every one of those answers has a person in it, or a place, or a hand that did the making. That is what the answer is. You ask a thing where it comes from and something comes back that made it.”

She said, “And there is one thing in this world I have read every day of my life since I was seven years old and I have never once asked that question about.”

She said, “It is the thing that puts the announcements down to me.”

## Where There Is No Maker

She said, “And I am going to say the reason for that out loud, because if I do not say it I will pretend to find out about it later and there is no version of this where I find out about it later.”

She said, “The layer is not a thing anybody has a maker of.”

She said, “That is not a mystery. I have worked that out a long time ago and it has never bothered me until this morning. A sentence arrives and it is whole and it is finished and there is no hand at the back of it and no place I could walk to and knock on. Every other thing I have ever used has had a somewhere behind it and this one does not, and so there was no one to ask, and a person does not ask a question that has nobody in the answer.”

She said, “That is why I never asked. It is arithmetic. It is not because I did not want to know.”

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “And I have been wrong for nineteen years and I worked it out about ninety seconds ago and I want to be exact about what I got wrong, because I have never in my life got anything wrong that I did not put the arithmetic on.”

She said, “I worked out that a question with no maker in it is still a question. A thing with no maker still has to be somewhere when it is made. Those are two different sentences and I have been standing on the first one for nineteen years and refusing to look at the second one.”

## The Question

She said it standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the seventh hour and thirty minutes on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, into twenty-two paces of dark, with the light going a hand’s width along the wood and stopping.

She said, “Where do the announcements come from.”

And then she stood there and waited, because there are things a person does after she asks a question, and one of them is being quiet to see whether anything answers.

She waited a count of a hundred and forty.

And there was a lamp going a hand’s width along the near end of that table and twenty-one paces of nothing behind it and a table twenty-two paces long with a hand at the far end of it that has not written a word in four hundred years, and there was a boy asleep four landings down with oil on his hands, and there was no stroke and there was no sound and there was nothing at all.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the seventh hour and thirty-five minutes:

“There is no answer.”

She said, “And I am going to say that in the order I say things in. The figures are that there is no stroke, no sound, and a lamp with a hand’s width of light in it. And the reason is that there is not one instrument in this building that would have heard the question, and I have known that since I was nine years old, and I asked it into twenty-two paces of dark in a room where the layer is not present at all.”

She said, “And I am not frightened by that. I want that said, because the cold can be mistaken for fright and it is the cold.”

## And Then She Decided

She said, “And here is the decision and I am saying it before I do anything else, which is the order.”

She said, “I am not going to wait to find out.”

She said, “I have waited once, on the first of this month, for about a hundred and forty counts and then forty more, and then I stopped. And I am not going to do it again, and the reason is arithmetic and it took me a night to get to.”

She said, “That stair does not bring anything back. Nine hundred and four steps going up with forty boards on a cloth and a cord going round it twice, and in nineteen years it has never once come up with anything that was not a board. It is a stair that carries other people’s arithmetic into this room. It is not a road and it does not go anywhere I can ask a question of, and a thing that cannot carry a question back is not a way of waiting.”

She said, “And I have been treating that stair as though it were an answer coming, for seven days, and it is not, and it never was.”

She said, “So I am going to ask it again somewhere it has not been asked.”

She said, “And I am not going to ask it in this room, because this room is not where the answer is. I can say that as a fact. I have been in it four hundred and eighty days and the layer is not in it and it has never been in it and I did not know until this morning that that was a thing a room could be.”

She said, “Everything I have ever traced went down and away. The rows are down. The turn is down. The levy is down. The bound is down and I am four miles up a mountain from all of it. In nineteen years I have traced every single thing I have ever had my hands on to somewhere below me and I have not once traced one above.”

She stood there for about a count of ninety and did not fill it.

She said, “I am going to look up.”

There were three pieces of wood lying face up about a foot in from the near end of that table and a fourth about a foot further along, and there was a bar against the wall on the left that had been there four hundred and thirty-seven days, and there was a bundle under the far end of that table with two turns of cord in it and not a knot, and there was a lamp out in a cage on the shelf at the far end, and there was a strip of pale wood face up on the floor under that table at the near end of the leg where no person but her has ever put a hand.

And a woman standing four feet off the near end of it who had asked one question out loud that morning and had got nothing at all, and who was not going to ask it twice in the same room.
