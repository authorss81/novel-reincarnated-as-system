# The Many Who Pay

One hundred and sixty-five days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and ninety-six days in that room. The cage out three hundred and sixty-three days. Right arm open six hundred and three days and it is not going to close. One thousand three hundred and thirty-eight of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was a woman sitting beside it who had named the Anchor and missed the first after it, and there was one question left under the naming she had assumed answered for six volumes, and she was going up to open it.

## The One She Assumed

She said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, at about the sixth hour of the fourth of the month, before she did anything else about it.

She said, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and sixty-five days since the ninth. Five hundred and ninety-six in that room. Three hundred and sixty-three since a lamp in a cage went out. Six hundred and three with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand three hundred and thirty-eight of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. Two days ago I named what the walls are for, and yesterday the room ran without me on a first, and all night one assumed thing sat beside the lamp with me. For six volumes I have assumed one person pays for the ceiling, and the one person is me, and this morning I am going to find out who pays.”

She said, “It is nakedness, and it is cold, and it is the first such nakedness since the hearing.”

She said, “Not bitterness, which I had yesterday and know its shape. Nakedness at having carried a grievance fifteen volumes against one taker, and finding the taking feeds a gathered will, and a gathered will is not fed by one.”

She said, “And what I want is the payers. All of them. I can hold all of them if they come one at a time.”

She left the lamp burning on the third landing and went up past it in the dark with nothing in either hand, with her left hand on the stone, saying the landings where they were used, fourth through eleventh, because the nakedness needed every one of them under it.

## Hundreds of Rooms

She stood on the twelfth landing in the dark with nothing in either hand, and the sentences went on, one at a time, plain, reporting, through her to him, and she said the building standing, because the building was the answer.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “Here is who pays, from my own arithmetic and not from any mouth. Hundreds of rooms, each with a table and forty boards and a bundle and a kept place with no person in it. My room is one of them. Every one of them has a tender who thins at the rate of stronger. Every one of them pays daily, dateless, without telling.”

She stood there for about a count of a hundred with that in her, and then she said the rest.

She said, “And here is the whole of it, said plain. One thousand three hundred and thirty-eight from me alone. The same order from every tender in every room, for four hundred years. A great many people, none of them told, every one of them thinned to hold a ceiling over a gathered will none of them was told about.”

She said, “Six volumes I assumed one person. The arithmetic says a great many. The grievance I carried as mine alone is carried in hundreds of rooms by people I will never meet, and none of us was told, and the not telling is part of the keeping.”

He said, “You see them.”

She said, “I do. And seeing them finishes nothing, and I am saying so out loud. It is a great many found, and the taking goes on.”

## The Weight of Many

She sat down on the stone of the twelfth landing with her back against the wall, because the many needed sitting, and she said the rest sitting, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “Here is the weight, said plain. One thinned person is a grievance. Hundreds thinned for four hundred years is a foundation the building stands on. My one thousand three hundred and thirty-eight is one stone in a wall of such stones, and the wall holds the Anchor, and the Anchor is real, and the wall is real, and both are built out of untold people.”

She said, “And horror, quiet, deep, and I am not going to look away from it. Not at the Anchor, which is gathered will and worthy. At the untold, which is hundreds of tenders thinning without being told, the way I thinned without being told, for something worthy they were never told about.”

She said, “And here is the part that stays, said plain. Worthy does not cover untold. The mercy is real and the untold is real, and one does not wash the other, and I am saying so out loud so that I do not let the Anchor excuse the keeping.”

She got up and stood with nothing in either hand, and she said it standing, because the finding was a line.

She said, “A great many pay, none told. That is found, and it holds.”

## How the Untold Is Kept

She stayed on the twelfth landing after the finding was kept, standing with nothing in either hand, because the untold wanted saying, and she said it standing.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “Here is how the untold is kept, said plain. No tender is told the taking feeds a gathered will. Each is left to call it his own hunger, his own fault, his own price. I called mine his for fifteen volumes. Han Liang called his the trade for three hundred years. Hundreds call theirs what the dark suggests. Untold multiplies. Told would gather.”

She said, “And here is the whole of what multiplying does, said plain. A hundred separate grievances never meet. A hundred separate thinnings never count themselves. One told gathering would count, and counting would ask, and asking would reach the lid. The lid is kept by keeping the counted apart.”

She said, “And fury, hot, clean, and I am naming it because it is the first fury after horror, and it is large, and I am not going to cool it. Four hundred years of kept apart, and I am one kept, and the keeping worked on me fifteen volumes, and seeing it does not break it, and that is the fury, that seeing holds no hammer.”

He said, “You see apart.”

She said, “I do. And seeing apart finishes nothing, and I am saying so out loud. It is one apart seen, and the taking goes on.”

## The Apart Counted

She stayed standing after the apart was seen, with nothing in either hand, because the apart wanted counting, and she counted it standing.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “Here is the count, said plain. Hundreds of rooms. Hundreds of tenders. Four hundred years of mornings. My one thousand three hundred and thirty-eight beside hundreds of the same order. No mouth has summed it. I will not sum it. Summing would be a figure with no derivation, and I say no number I cannot derive.”

She said, “And here is the whole of what unsummed means, said plain. The many stays many, uncounted, known. Tomorrow the many narrows to one, because among the payers is the one I called thief fifteen volumes, and his share wants measuring.”

## Down With the Many

She came down in the dark with her left hand on the stone, past the eleventh, past the tenth with no doorway, past the ninth, saying each one where it was used, and she took longer over the fifth than the rest, because the many were still in her and the fifth is narrow.

She sat beside the lamp, and the light went six paces out from where it stood and stopped, and she stayed inside the reach of it until her breath went four counts sitting still.

She said, out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, “Lamp on the third landing. Burning. Mine. A great many paying that I assumed was one. That is the record and there is nothing else in it.”

She said, “And here is what tomorrow is, said plain. The great many includes him, because he has been taking out of me fifteen volumes, and I have called the taking his, and tomorrow I work out how much of it was his.”

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and hundreds of rooms paying daily without telling, and a woman sitting beside the light with horror sitting beside her.

And she had found the payers, and they were many, and that was the day, and his share of it would be worked out in the morning whether she wanted it or not.
