# The Many Who Pay

She had assumed one person for six years and she had never once said the assumption out loud, and the reason she had never said it was that the assumption had a name in it and the name had been his, and saying the name would have meant admitting she had been keeping a fifth column.

She said the name on the sixth landing, in the dark, on her way up, and heard how it sounded, and kept going.

“One hundred and sixty-five days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and ninety-six days in that room. The cage out three hundred and sixty-three. This arm open six hundred and three days. One thousand three hundred and thirty-eight gone at this morning.”

She stopped on the ninth with her palm on the warm wall of the landing above it.

“For six years I have assumed that one person pays for the ceiling and that the one person is me paying you. I am going to find out tonight whether that is the shape of it or whether I have built a small grief on top of a large one.”

## What She Had Assumed For Six Years

She sat down on the ninth with her back against the warm wall and did the thing she had come up to do, which was to put the assumption down in front of itself and look at it.

“For six years the column has had two lines in it. One line: one thousand and more days gone. Second line: he. And every morning I have said both lines in the same breath in the same voice, which is what a person does when a figure and a person have got the same weight to them.”

She kept her palm against the stone.

“I never subtracted. That is what six years of looking at it without looking at it is. I never once took one thousand and more and asked how much of it was him and how much of it was the room and how much of it was me being nineteen.”

She said that last part quietly, because it was the one that had actually been in there.

“Three nights ago I put a figure in front of the thing that checks figures and it put eleven right. Yesterday I found out that I can check any figure I have ever written down. And this morning I have found out that the column I have been keeping for six years has a second line in it that was never a figure at all.”

She stopped on that and let it sit.

“No wonder the subtraction would not come out on the eighth. I have been subtracting a person. You cannot take a person off a count.”

## Hundreds of Rooms

She got to the twelfth and said it standing, because the building was the answer and the building had to be said standing.

“Hundreds of rooms. Each with a table and forty boards and a bundle and a kept place at the far side with no one in it. Mine is one of them.”

She set the side of her hand against the wall.

“Every one of them has a tender. Every one of them pays daily, dateless, without telling. One thousand three hundred and thirty-eight from me alone. The same order from every room, for four hundred years.”

She stood in the dark on that landing with it.

“So it is a great many people. And not one of them was told.”

## She Asks Him the Name

She sat down with her back against the wall, and she asked him something, and she asked it the way she had learned to ask, which was plainly and without setting it up first.

“What does it call them.”

He did not answer straight away, and she could tell the difference between his thinking and his not-answering now after a month of it.

“I report what the layer says to people. I do not have the layer’s vocabulary for its own staff.”

“That is not an answer.”

“It is the answer. There is no word. I have gone through nine years of sentences going into people and there is a word for the stage and a word for the ninth and a word for ascent and a word for settlement, and there is nothing in any of it for the person standing in the room who gets thinner so that the sentence can go out.”

She sat with it a while and let it get no smaller.

“So there is a word for everything that happens and there is no word for the one it happens to.”

“No.”

“Nine years,” she said, “and I have been reading the same layer you have and I have never noticed that either.”

“You would not have. You read the part of it that arrives. I have been listening for the part that leaves.”

She put that down somewhere and let it stay there.

## A Stone In A Wall

She said the weight of it sitting, in the ordinary voice, with her hand on the cold stone of the landing.

“One thinned person is a grievance. Hundreds thinned for four hundred years is a foundation. My one thousand three hundred and thirty-eight is one stone in a wall of such stones, and the wall is what holds the Anchor, and the Anchor is real, and the wall is real, and both of them are built out of people who were never told they were building anything.”

She looked at her own hand in the dark, three fingers and a thumb that had come apart on a chip of stair four days ago.

“For nine years I have been angry at one person and being angry at one person has been the warmest thing in my head every single day, and I would have gone on being warm at him until I died.”

She said that to the stair and not to him, and she noticed herself doing it, and she left it.

“The warmth was worth a great deal to me. It was also the reason the count never came to one.”

## How the Untold Is Kept

“Here is how it is kept,” she said, standing. “No tender is told that the taking feeds a gathered will. Each one is left to call it his own hunger, his own fault, his own price. I called mine his for nine years. Han Liang called his the trade and has been paid out of a forearm for three hundred years and never once asked what for. Hundreds call theirs whatever the dark suggests to them.”

She stood in the dark with her arms at her sides.

“Untold multiplies. Told would gather.”

She said it and heard it land and then had to go and sit down.

“One told grievance would count itself. Counting would ask. Asking would reach the lid. The lid is kept by keeping the counted apart, and it has been kept by that for four hundred years and it is kept today by me, on this stair, out of habit.”

## Fury

She got up again, which she had not meant to do.

“Fury,” she said. “Hot and clean. I am not going to cool it and I am not going to make it smaller.”

She put both hands against the wall of the landing, which was the first time since the eighth of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and sixty-four she had used both hands for anything.

“I have seen it. That is all. I have seen the shape of the whole working and it is right and it is kept and it is kept well, and seeing it holds no hammer. There is nothing at the end of this to hit.”

She stayed there with her hands on the wall for a count of forty.

“Nine years of being angry has just been made small by arithmetic and I have to go on carrying it at the size it actually is, which is not nine years and not one person but a great many of me and every one of them has been carrying their own and has never heard from another one.”

She took her hands off the wall and stood in the dark in front of it.

“And there is a second thing and I am going to say it before I lose my nerve.”

She got both hands to the wall.

“If the many is what I have just found, then the people in it are in it with me, and I am the only one who has found out. That is not a reason to keep it. That is a reason to say it where it goes, the same as yesterday.”

She felt the weight of that land.

“I am not going to use that as a reason today. Today is the day I narrow it to one, and a person who uses a hundred strangers as an argument for saying a thing about the man in her head has found a new way to aim at the wrong person.”

He said, “That is the first sentence in a month I do not have a shape for.”

“I have had a month of being wrong in public. You were not the only one paying attention.”

## The Sum She Will Not Do

She sat down and said the last of it sitting, quietly, without a voice at all for a moment and then with one.

“I will not sum it.”

That was not an evasion and she said so.

“Not because it is too large. Hundreds of rooms, hundreds of tenders, four hundred years of mornings. If I put those in a line I would have a figure by the afternoon and it would be the best figure in the world and there would be nothing behind it.”

She laid her hand out flat on the stone.

“Every number I have ever said out loud I could get at by going and doing the arithmetic twice. That is the whole of what a number is to me. I am nineteen years old and it is the last thing I have got that I found myself.”

She sat for a while with her hand where it was.

“The many stays many. Uncounted and known. Tomorrow it narrows to one, because he is in it. He has been taking out of me for nine years and I have been calling the taking his, and tomorrow I work out how much of it was.”

## Down With the Sum Unmade

She came down in the dark with her left hand on the stone, past the eleventh, past the tenth, past the ninth, saying each one where it was used, and she took longer over the fifth than over the rest because the fifth is narrow and the many were still in her.

The lamp was where she had left it. She sat down beside it with the light six paces out from where it stood and stopping and said the record, in the ordinary voice, sitting.

“Lamp on the third landing. Burning. A great many paying, none of them told, and one of them has a name.”

She sat with the lamp a long time after that.

She had not summed it. She was nineteen years old and there was a figure in her that would have taken four hundred rooms and nine years of mornings to make, and she had a lamp that could not be refilled, and she was not going to put the figure anywhere near it.
