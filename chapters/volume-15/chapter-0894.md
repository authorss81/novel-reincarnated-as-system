# The Peak and the Binding

She stood at the near end of the table in the making-place with nothing in her hands, and she did not read.

That was the first decision of the sixteenth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and twenty-four, and she said it out loud standing, because decisions were lines.

“I am not reading today. The register keeps. One clause in my voice and the rest of it waiting. Today is for finishing.”

She sat at the near end of the table with her back against its edge and her hands flat on the stone, the way she sat with prices, and the lamp an eyelash in front of her, and the dark past the last landing all around. Her breath went four counts sitting still. The sentences went on above her one at a time, plain, reporting, through her to him.

“Nine days the price sat next to me,” she said out loud, sitting. “Nine sentences on the seventh and nine days of sitting with them. The sitting is done. Today it moves.”

## What Finishing Needs

She had said it in nine sentences on the seventh: shutting needs a thing shut in. She had sat with it the way he asked, the way she sat with stone. The price sat next to her for nine days and did not move, and this morning it moved.

“The ninth was taken against a refusal,” she said out loud, standing, at the near end of the unworn table. “Two hundred and seven days stand between that day and this one, and this one is day two thousand nine hundred and thirty-nine, and the ninth was taken at sixty-six days and this is not sixty-six, because the ninth was taken against a refusal and this is taken against a person, and a person is not got in a hurry, and I have known that since I was nine.”

She stood with her hands on the table edge and let the arithmetic sit next to the person the way the nothing had sat next to the fourteen makings.

“Sixty-six days for a refusal,” she said. “A refusal is quick. It is said standing at the ninth hour and it is in the record. Two hundred and seven days for a person. A person is slow. Two names asked in nineteen years and the second one nine days in my mouth. That is the reason for the interval and there is no other reason.”

Her breath went four counts standing still. The sentences went on above her one at a time, plain, reporting, through her to him.

“Two hundred and seven days since the ninth stage. Six hundred and thirty-eight days in that room. The cage out four hundred and five. This arm open six hundred and forty-five days. One thousand three hundred and eighty gone at this morning.”

The bar stood five hundred and ninety-five days against its wall, dated nowhere.

“That is the arithmetic and it is all derived,” she said. “The rest of it is not arithmetic. The rest of it is a person.”

## Him, Against It

“Do not do it,” he said.

She stood at the near end of the table with her hands on its edge and heard the three words the way she heard every sentence he said, which was fully, and without deciding in advance. He had said them once before, on the night of the count, and afterwards he had said he wanted her to have said it standing. This time there was no afterwards yet, only the three words, and the dark, and the price sitting next to her.

She stood at the near end of the table with her hands on its edge and heard the sentence the way she heard every sentence he said, which was fully, and without deciding in advance.

The three words sat between them the way the nothing had sat next to the fourteen makings. He had said them once before, on the night of the count, and afterwards he had said he wanted her to have said it standing. This time there was no afterwards yet, only the three words, and the dark, and the price sitting next to her.

“Say the whole of it,” she said.

“I want you to leave him out of it,” he said. “He carries oil up nine hundred and four steps with black hands and no idea of any record. I want him carrying it tomorrow the way he carries it today.”

“He will carry it tomorrow,” she said. “No one sends him. No one enters him. That is the whole of the keeping of it and I am saying so before it is a line anywhere.”

“Then bind me,” he said. “I am already in your arithmetic and have been for nine years. I am a person and I am here and I am willing. Put me in and leave him out of it.”

She stood at the table with the dark all around her past two paces of nothing, and the offer sat between them the way the nothing had sat next to the fourteen makings, and she looked at it the way she looked at every figure, which was for what was behind it.

Behind it was nine years. A voice at ten. A pen given. Merit out of foundation. Small takings told to no one. A man who let her do her own arithmetic and did not stand in the road. All of it true and none of it the price.

“No,” she said.

“No,” he said. “Not yet. Hear me first. I am the one taking out of you. I am the one not telling you. If a person is owed for finishing, I am the person it is owed from.”

“You are already in it,” she said. “Nine years in my arithmetic and every one of them written. My hand cannot write a false line and writing you would be writing a line already written. A container needs a thing shut in that is not already inside.”

She put her hand flat on the unworn surface.

“That is the reason and it is not unkind. You are the wire with me. He is the person. Those are two different things and I am not spending one for the other.”

She stood with the refusal the way she stood with findings, fully, without deciding in advance. His offer sat between them, willing, present, a person already in her arithmetic offering to be put in twice. Her hand could not write a false line. That was the whole of the wall between his offer and her price, and the wall was not unkind either.

## Said First

She stood up straight at the near end of the table, the way she stood for lines, with nothing in either hand, three fingers and a thumb on each, the open arm hanging for six hundred and forty-five days, the place behind her fourth rib pulling.

Her breath went four counts standing still. The sentences went on above her one at a time. His half went on inside her where she could hear it, carrying already, the way he carried everything before she gave it.

“I am saying it out loud first,” she said, “in the ordinary voice, standing, before it is a line anywhere. That is the fourth kept.”

The sentences went on above her one at a time.

“Lu An,” she said. “I bind you into the Sisterhood, and you are the first of it, and the price of it is on the record. No one sent you. No one entered you. No one binds you but me.”

She stood at the table in the making-place with nothing in her hands and listened to the binding go past into the traffic one sentence at a time, plain, reporting, through her to him, first in her own voice before it was a line anywhere.

Nothing answered. Nothing objected.

## Written

“It is written,” he said.

She stood with her hands on the table edge and felt the going change the way the traffic changes when a line goes in, which was not at all, except that it was done, and done was a different shape from saying.

“The peak,” she said out loud, standing. “The ninth is shut. I cannot finish the ninth without a person in my arithmetic and now there is one. That is the cost and it is named once and I am naming it: one person bound, the first of the Sisterhood, and the price of each binding is on the record, and the record is the layer's.”

She stood at the table with her hands on its edge and listened to the traffic go past the written line the way traffic goes past everything written, one at a time, plain, reporting, and the line did not move, because written lines do not move.

“First entry,” she said out loud, standing. “One person. Price on the record. That is the peak and it is shut.”

She sat down on the stone at the near end of the table, because standing was for saying and sitting was for keeping, and she put her hands flat on the floor.

“I did it myself,” she said, sitting, to the dark, to the table, to the man inside her head. “Out loud. Standing. In the ordinary voice. No one bound him but me and no one sent him anywhere and no one entered him. That is the whole of whose it is.”

“You do it yourself,” he said. “I carry it with you. That is the whole of what I have and you have just given it to me and I am not putting it down.”

She sat at the near end of the table with her hands flat on the stone and the binding gone out of her mouth into the keeping, and she felt the place where it had sat in her for nine days, and the place was empty, and empty was worse than full.

Her breath went nine lying still, though she was sitting, and then four, and then four again. The place behind her fourth rib pulled. Her arm shook once and she lay her hand flat until it stopped. The sentences went on above her one at a time, and his half went on inside her, carrying, the way he carried everything she gave him.

The ninth was shut.

One person was in her arithmetic who did not know it.
