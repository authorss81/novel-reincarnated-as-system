# Nine Counted

Seventy-six days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and seven days in that room. The cage out two hundred and seventy-four days. Right arm open five hundred and fourteen days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and forty-nine days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in that room and no person in the nine landings above it and no person on any stair in that tower, and there was a lamp burning on the second of those landings about seven landings below where she was standing.

## Landings, And Not Steps

She said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the seventh hour of the fifth of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four, on the ninth landing:

“I am counting landings.”

She said, “And I am not counting steps, and that was the decision I made at the first hour of the thirteenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four at the bottom of that stair, standing, in the ordinary voice, and I have kept it for twenty-two of the days since.”

She said, “A landing is a thing I can be wrong about. A stair is not. And I am going to arrive at the top of this holding a number of rooms and not a number of steps, and if the number of rooms turns out to be wrong then I will be wrong about it in a way I can put my hand on.”

She said, “That is the whole discipline and I have not broken it once and I am not going to start tonight.”

## What Is On The Nine

She said the count out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, because that is the order.

She said, “One. Two. That is the one where there is a lamp burning on it now and has been since the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four.”

She said, “Three. That is the one where the kept place is, and where I said a figure out loud standing up in the dark on the twenty-sixth, and I am not going into that room again this month.”

She said, “Four. Five. Six. Seven. Eight. Nine.”

She said, “And that is the one I am standing on, and it is the ninth landing above the room at the bottom of that stair, and there are nine of them and I have walked every one.”

She said, “And there is a doorway in the far wall of five of them. There is a doorway in one, in two, in three, in six and in nine, and I have been inside three of them, and every one of those three rooms was the same room.”

She said, “And I went into the sixth on the twenty-seventh of this month at about the fourth hour because it was there, and it is the same room. Twenty-two paces, four feet off the floor, the flag at about the nineteenth, forty boards face up under the middle of it, a bundle under the far end corded twice, a lamp in a cage out on the shelf at the far end, and between the twentieth pace and the end a place of the length of a forearm and the breadth of a hand.”

She said, “And I came out of it again and I did not go into any of the others.”

## What Is Above The Ninth

And then she turned round on that landing and looked up.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the seventh hour and thirty minutes:

“There is a stair going up out of this landing.”

She said, “It goes up. It winds. There is no rail on it and there is no doorway in the wall of this landing on that side, and there is stone under my left hand going up at the same pitch as every other one of them.”

She put her left hand flat on that stone and felt up it for about the length of two paces.

She said, “And it goes up for longer than two paces.”

She said, “And the stone is warmer on that side than it is on this side of the landing, and I do not know what that means and I am not going to find out.”

She said, “I am going to say that I noticed it. I noticed it with the flat of my hand going up about two paces, and it was warmer, and the rest of this building has been exactly the same cold for five hundred and seven days.”

She said, “It is not much. It is one hand on a stone. And I am saying it out loud because I would have noticed it and not said it, and I have worked out in the last five days that the things I notice and do not say are the ones that are going to cost me the most.”

She said, “I have a bar nine feet nine against a wall that I have not lifted, and a bundle under a table that I have not pulled, and a gap in a table at the far side of three rooms that I found with my hand and have not told anybody about. That is what not saying things costs, and I know the price of it now.”

She said, “And I am not going up it tonight and I am going to say why, and the why is not that I am frightened of it.”

She sat down on the stone of that landing with her back against the wall.

She said, “The lamp is seven landings below me and it is the only light in this building above the fourth landing of that tower and it is on the second of nine. There is nothing on this landing and nothing on any landing above this one. I would be going up a winding stair in the dark with an arm open at the elbow and nothing in either hand, and I lost the wall once already, on the ninth turning, and I went down on my left knee.”

She said, “And I am not doing that again on a stair where I cannot see the next step.”

## The Thing That Is Actually Hard

She sat there for about a count of a hundred and forty and then she said the next part out loud, and it came out quieter than the rest.

She said, “And here is the thing that is actually hard and it is not the cold and it is not the stair.”

She said, “Nine.”

She said, “Every stage I have ever taken has had a number on it and the numbers stop at nine. One, two, three, four, five, six, seven, eight, nine, and there is nothing above nine anywhere in the world that I have ever heard of, and I have believed that for nineteen years and I was right to.”

She said, “And there are nine landings above my room and the stair does not stop at the ninth one.”

She put her hand flat on the floor of that landing.

She said, “That is not a figure I have got wrong. That is a building that has gone past nine and does not care what I have believed.”

She said, “And I want to be careful here, because I have got two things and I am not going to mix them up. I have believed for nineteen years that nothing in the world goes past nine. And I have been standing in this building for five hundred and seven days and there are nine landings above me and a tenth flight going up out of the one I am sitting on.”

She said, “Those are both true. And one of them has been true for nineteen years and the other one has been true since the thirteenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four and neither of them knows about the other.”

She said, “And I am not going to use the second one to get at the first. I have got a strip of pale wood about a foot under a table four flights and nine landings down that has a price at the head of it and four figures of mine in it, and every figure I have ever had has had something above it that stopped at nine.”

She said, “And I have never once asked what the nine was for. I have had nine pairs of cuts under that head since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and I have not put a figure in one of them, and there has not been a last one to put a figure in since the wood was cut.”

She said, “And I said out loud in that room that there is not going to be one, and I have not taken one, and I am not going to. I want that said from the ninth landing as much as from the room.”

She said, “But that is about me. And it is the only reason I have ever had for the world stopping at nine, and I have been sitting on the ninth landing of a building that does not stop.”

## What She Is Going To Do About It

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the eighth hour, with her right arm open in front of her and the dark going up out of that landing above her:

“I am going down to that lamp.”

She said, “And I am going to bring it up here and put it on this landing, and I am going to lose the light on the second and the first underneath it, and I know exactly what that costs and I have worked it out.”

She said, “A lamp standing on a stone floor is light on a whole landing and a lamp in a hand is light on about four feet of the world, and I have one of it, and I am going to put it where I need it and not where it is comfortable.”

She said, “And I am not doing it tonight. It is the eighth hour of the fifth of this month and there is one lamp in this building and it is seven landings down, and I am not going to walk that stair twice in a day to move a thing I could have moved once.”

She turned round and put her back against the wall of that landing, with the stair going up on her right and the dark going down on her left, and she stayed there.

There were nine landings above a room that was not a room, and a lamp burning on the second of them about seven landings below where she was sitting, and a stair going up out of the ninth one that neither of them stopped.

And she did not count it, and she was not going to, and the count she had at the end of that day was nine and not one step more than that.
