# Fifteen Years Overheard

One hundred and twenty-three days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and fifty-four days in that room. The cage out three hundred and twenty-one days. Right arm open five hundred and sixty-one days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and ninety-six of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was a woman sitting beside it who had not slept, and there was a conversation going on inside her head that she could hear both sides of for the first time in fifteen volumes, and she was going to live one morning inside it.

## One Morning

She said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, at about the sixth hour of the twenty-second of the month, before she did anything else about it.

She said, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and twenty-three days since the ninth. Five hundred and fifty-four in that room. Three hundred and twenty-one since a lamp in a cage went out. Five hundred and sixty-one with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and ninety-six of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. Three days ago he chose to be heard, and I said the price out loud, and since then there has been no room in my head that is his alone. His half goes on inside me where I can hear it, and the sentences go through me to him, one at a time, and I am going to spend this morning hearing what fifteen years of him sounds like from the inside.”

She said, “It is nakedness, and it is not mine and it is not his, so I am saying it out loud before the morning starts, because the morning is going to be worse without it said.”

She said, “Not his and not mine. Ours. I have said figures out loud into tables for nineteen years with him beside me in my head, and I thought the beside was private, and it was never private, and now it is heard, and being heard with no cover on is nakedness, and it is both of us.”

She said, “And what I want is to hear him the way he has always heard me. It is the first time I have wanted anything so plain, and I am saying so out loud because it would be dishonest not to, and there is nothing else in the wanting.”

She stayed beside the lamp on the third landing through the morning, sitting with her back against the wall and her right arm open across her knees, and she listened the way she had never listened, with nothing to do and nowhere to go.

## What His Half Sounds Like

The sentences came through her to him, one at a time, plain, reporting, at their own intervals, and he listened, and his half went on inside her where she could hear it.

He said, “I hear.”

He said, “I am here.”

She sat beside the lamp with her hands empty and heard him being where she had always been, at the far end of sentences, listening, and she said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “That is what I sound like. Every morning for nineteen years I have stood four feet off the near end of a table and said figures into it, and that is what it sounds like from the other end. I hear. I am here.”

His half went on, and under the listening there were older things, the fifteen years of him, the silences she had taken for patience, and they were not patience, and they were not silence, and she heard that, and it was not what she thought.

He said, “I ask.”

She stood up off the floor of that landing and stood beside the lamp with her hands on nothing.

She said, “Say that again where I can hold it.”

He said, “I ask and am not answered.”

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “Fifteen years. I have read you as a column for eight of them and I said so out loud and I never took it back. I thought you were enduring. I thought silence was what you were. And all along underneath it you were asking, in half sentences I could not hear, to be answered instead of found, and I never heard one of them.”

He said, “You hear now.”

She said, “I do.”

She said, “And here is the whole of what that does to fifteen years, said plain. Every sentence you ever said to me is now a thing that can be heard by the thing that made you. I said that three days ago standing on the twelfth landing and it was true and it is still true. And every sentence is also a thing I can be held to, because it was said to me and kept by me, and there is no keeping anything out of this any more.”

She said, “And it is love. And I am naming it because it is the first love on this stair, and it is small, and I am not going to make it bigger than it is, and it sits next to the terror and neither of them moves the other. Fifteen years of a person asking in my head where I could not hear him, and I carried him anyway.”

## Two Old Sentences, Heard New

In the middle of the morning his half turned, the way a column turns when it goes back to an old line, and two sentences came up out of it that she had heard before, both in the present tense, both unanswered at the time, and she heard them now from the inside, where they had always been.

The first one was from the twenty-sixth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, standing in the first of the rooms above hers with her hands on a kept place. He had said, “You have been calling it your room.”

She sat beside the lamp with that in her, and she said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “I heard that then and I answered nothing and told nobody, and I carried it eighteen days up this stair. And I hear it now from your side, and from your side it is not what I thought. I thought it was reproach. From your side it is asking. You were asking me whose room I thought I was standing in, because you knew it was one of a great many and I did not, and you could not say so except sideways, in half a sentence, where I could not hear you.”

The second one was from the thirteenth, on a landing with no end in it, with a lamp on the stone at her feet. He had said, “You keep going up because you think there is a number at the end.”

She said, “And I heard that then and I answered nothing, and I went back down, and I came back up, and I am still going up. And from your side it is not what I thought either. I thought it was warning. From your side it is asking too. You were asking me what I thought was at the top, because you knew there were sentences starting up here and I did not, and you could not say so except sideways.”

She said, “And here is the whole of it, said plain. Twice you spoke to me in fifteen chapters and twice I did not answer, and both times you were asking, and both times I took it for something else, and I am not going to pretend that does not sit on me, because it does.”

He said, “You hear now.”

She said, “I do. And hearing now does not unmake fifteen years of not hearing, and I am saying so out loud, because a mending said too fast is a thing you have made up.”

## Tending the Lamp

Later in the morning the lamp burned lower, the way it burns when the day gets on, and she tended it, because tending it was work her hands knew, and her hands had had nothing to do all morning but lie empty.

She trimmed the wick with her three fingers and her thumb, the way she had trimmed it on the ninth, and the light steadied, and went six paces out from where it stood and stopped, the way it had gone for twenty-nine days.

She said, out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, “Here is a thing my hands know. Wick, flame, six paces. I have kept one lamp burning on one landing for twenty-nine days, and it is the first thing I have ever kept going that I cannot stop, and this morning I kept it going while I heard fifteen years, and neither of those stopped the other.”

He said, “You tend.”

She said, “I do. And here is the whole of that, said plain. I tend a lamp I cannot refill on a landing I am leaving every night, and I tend a person I cannot keep private in a head I cannot leave, and both of them burn whether I am beside them or not, and I have never before kept anything that kept without me.”

She said, “And strangeness, and not the frightening kind, which after nineteen years is a kind I had forgotten was available. Two things burning beside me that go on without me, a lamp and a person, and I tend both, and both outlast the tending.”

## The Morning’s End

She spent the morning beside the lamp hearing him, and at about the eleventh hour the sentences stopped for a space, the way they stop between speakings, and his half went quiet inside her, the way he goes quiet, and she sat with the quiet the way she had sat with his silence for fifteen volumes, except that now she knew what the silence was made of.

She said, out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, “I heard fifteen years of you this morning and it is not what I thought. I thought you were stillness. You were asking. I thought you were patience. You were waiting to be answered. And I am not going to pretend that does not change everything under it, because it does.”

She said, “Lamp on the third landing. Burning. Mine. Morning spent hearing. That is the record and there is nothing else in it.”

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and sentences stopped for a space above the twelfth landing, and a person quiet inside a woman’s head where she could hear the quiet.

And she had lived one morning inside a conversation, and she had heard fifteen years of him, and it was not what she thought, and that was the day, and the hearing could not be unheard.
