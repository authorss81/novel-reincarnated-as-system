# What the Record Holds

He was quiet at the sixth hour, and quiet at the seventh, and at the eighth she went looking for him the way she had never once gone looking for him in nine years, which was by saying his name into the dark where only he could hear it.

“Yuan.”

She had said it out loud three times in nine years of keeping, and never lightly, and this time it came out sitting, with her back against the table in the making-place and her hands flat on stone, because standing was for lines and this was not a line.

The name sat in the dark past the last landing the way the binding sat in the record, said, gone, unanswered. She had said his name three times and he had said hers once, on the fourteenth of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and sixty-four, twice over, after nine years of never having said it. That was the whole of the names between them and it was not nothing.

## What Answers

“I am here,” he said.

She sat at the near end of the table with the lamp an eyelash in front of her and the binding gone out of her mouth into the keeping, and she heard him the way she heard everything now, which was undefended and unwithdrawn from, and there was no room in her head that was his alone and there never would be one again.

“You went quiet,” she said.

“I go quiet,” he said. “You bind a boy in your voice and the layer writes it and I have nothing to put next to that. I am not gone. I am sitting with it the way you sit with stone.”

She sat at the near end of the table with the lamp an eyelash in front of her and heard the quiet under his sentence the way she heard wants under sentences, and the quiet was not gone quiet, and she could hear the difference the way she heard rooms.

“Sit it with me, then,” she said. “That is the asking and it is not arithmetic. Two sittings next to each other the way the sentences sit next to your half.”

“I sit it with you,” he said. “That is the answer and it is not arithmetic either.”

She said the day's figures sitting, on the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and twenty-four, because the day needed them and sitting was where she lived now.

“Two hundred and ten days since the ninth stage. Six hundred and forty-one days in that room. The cage out four hundred and eight. This arm open six hundred and forty-eight days. One thousand three hundred and eighty-three gone at this morning.”

The bar stood five hundred and ninety-eight days against its wall, dated nowhere, eight flights and a stair and a top below her.

She sat with the figures the way she sat with mornings, every one of them derived, none of them invented. Two hundred and ten days since the ninth and the ninth shut. Six hundred and forty-one days in a room running without her. The arithmetic held the way stone holds sitting.

## What Is Written

“What is on him,” she said out loud, sitting, with her hands flat on the stone. “That is the work of today. I bound him yesterday standing in my own voice before it was a line anywhere, and the layer wrote it, and today I find out what is written.”

She stood up, because findings were lines, and she put her hand on the table edge at the near end, three fingers and a thumb, the arm hanging open at the elbow for six hundred and forty-eight days.

“The price is on the record,” she said, standing. “One bound. The first of the Sisterhood. That is what is written and there is no more of it and no less of it. No sending. No entering. No name but the one I said.”

The sentences went on above her one at a time, plain, reporting, through her to him.

“And the record is the layer's,” she said. “Not boards. Not my hand. The keeping that answers figures and explains nothing wrote what I said, and what it writes is what stands, and I said it first in my own voice so that it would be mine before it was anything else.”

She sat back down, because standing was for saying and the saying was done.

“What stands,” she said, sitting. “That is the question of today and I am asking it of you, because the record is the layer's and I cannot see it and you hear both sides.”

He was quiet for a count of sixty, which was his way of listening past her, into the traffic, the way she listened past her light into the dark.

“One bound,” he said. “First of it. Price on the record. No sending. No entering. Said first in your voice. That is what stands and there is no more of it.”

She sat with her hands flat on the stone and took the telling the way she took figures, which was whole.

“Mine before anything else,” she said. “Said standing. Written after. That order holds.”

## Eight Flights Below

“No one told him,” she said, sitting, with her hands flat on the stone. “That is the shape of today and I am saying it out loud. He is eight flights below me with black hands and dark lamps and a name in my mouth, and no one has told him there is a record, and no one has told him he is the first of anything, and no one is going to.”

She thought about his morning the way she thought about mornings below, which was as a going she could not see. Up before the sixth hour. Dark lamps. Black cloth. Oil up the stair on his own account. Feet stopping every few treads the way laden feet stop. No idea of any record and no idea of any Sisterhood and no idea that yesterday a woman past the last landing said his name standing before it was a line anywhere.

She put her hand on the place behind her fourth rib without deciding to, the way she had been doing since the twentieth, and it did not do anything at all.

“I bound a person who does not know,” she said. “That is the cost of the peak and it is not nothing. The ninth is shut and it took a boy with oil on his hands who thinks today is for carrying, and carrying is what he will do, because no one sends him and no one enters him and the keeping keeps him where he stands.”

“You are right about the cost,” he said. “I want him to know what is on him. He carries it whether he knows it or not, and I want the knowing in him and not only in you.”

She sat with that the way she sat with everything he said, which was fully.

“No,” she said. “Told would gather. I said that on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and ninety-four about a great many, and it is true of one. He carries it best not knowing, the way I carried nine years best not knowing, until the knowing was mine to do.”

“He does not have your nine years,” he said.

“He has his own years,” she said. “They are his and not mine and I am not spending them. The binding is on the record and the record is the layer's and the boy is under it the way stone is under a stair, kept and not asked.”

She sat with the wrongness of it the way she sat with everything on this stair, which was in her body first. The place behind her fourth rib pulled. Her arm hung open at the elbow for six hundred and forty-eight days. Somewhere below, a boy carried oil with black hands and thought today was for carrying, and he was right, and she had made him right by binding him, and the making was hers.

## The Keeping That Writes

She thought about the thing that had written it the way she had thought about it for a month, which was as a keeping and not as a person, correct and brief and courteous, with four hundred years of practice at not being interested.

“The Scribe writes when it decides something about a person,” she said out loud, sitting. “That is the rule I worked out on the day I sent the refusal down, and it held yesterday. I said it standing in my own voice and it wrote it. It did not explain. It does not explain. It answers figures and writes terms and that is the whole of its manner.”

She lifted her hand off the stone and looked at it in the eyelash of light, three fingers and a thumb, the hand that could not write a false line.

“My hand said it first,” she said. “My mouth said it first. The writing came after. That order is the only mercy in yesterday and I am keeping it.”

She put her hand back on the stone and felt the cold come up through the floor into her, and the sentences went on above her one at a time, and the register lay on the table with five mercies read once and a binding written that no one below knew.

He was quiet for a count of a hundred, which was his way of letting a cost sit where it was.

“I carry it with you,” he said at last. “That is still the whole of what I have. It is heavier today and I am still carrying it.”

She sat at the near end of the table with her hands flat on the stone and the price on the record eight flights above the boy it was on, and she felt the weight of it the way she felt everything on this stair, which was in her body first, behind her fourth rib.

“One bound,” she said out loud, once, the way she said costs. “Kept where he stands. Unknown to him.”

She kept sitting with her hands flat on the stone, and the price sat on the record eight flights above the boy it was on, and neither of them moved, and the question sitting next to them both was who would ever tell him, and when.
