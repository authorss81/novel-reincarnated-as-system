# One Of Hundreds

Sixty-seven days since the ninth stage. Four hundred and ninety-eight days in that room. The cage out two hundred and sixty-five days. Right arm open five hundred and five days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and forty days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in that room and no person in any of the three rooms above it and no person on any stair in that tower, and there was a lamp burning on the stone of the second landing about two landings down from where she was standing.

## The Length Of A Table By Hand

She went into that third room at about the fifth hour and forty minutes of the twenty-sixth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four and put her left hand on the leg of that table at the near end of it.

She said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “Figures first and the reason after. In the ordinary voice. Standing up.”

And then she said a figure.

She said, “Twenty-two paces. A leg at every two. The flag moves at about the nineteenth. Forty boards face up under the middle of it, and I can feel the second number on the top one and I am not going to say it.”

And she walked it at the speed of a person walking a table with a hand on it and not looking down.

And then she stopped.

## Between The Twentieth Pace And The End

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the sixth hour:

“There is a place.”

She said, “And I knew it before I had it, because I have walked past that place two hundred days without once slowing down.”

She said, “Twenty paces is the far end. That is where the cloth is under and where the far side of the table is, and I have walked the last two paces of that table with my hand on the leg of it in the dark on about two hundred mornings, and my hand has been on this place for the whole of every one of those.”

She said, “And it has never once stopped.”

She said, “Between the twentieth pace and the end of it.”

She put both hands flat on the wood and worked out the size of it by hand twice, going over it, and it took her longer than that.

She said, “It is of the length of a forearm and of the breadth of a hand.”

She said, “And it is kept.”

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “And it is not a seat. Nobody has sat in it.”

She said, “It is not wood. It is not the same as the rest of that table. It has been there long enough that it is not the same, and I cannot tell you how long, and I am not going to guess.”

She said, “And there is nothing on it. No wood turned over. No board. No cord. No cloth. Nothing put on it and nothing taken off it. It is a place on a table and no person has ever sat in it.”

She said, “And I know that it is not a seat better than anybody who has never been in a room, because a person who has not been in a room can get that wrong in about a second, and it is the only wrong available here.”

She said, “I have known it for twenty-five days. There is a board about a foot in from the near end of a table three landings down this building that sets a place out and says that no person has sat in it, and it says what keeping it is, and I read that board out loud on the first of this month at the fourth hour before I understood one word of what it was about.”

She said, “And I said nothing about it for twenty-five days. I went up a stair with nothing in my hands, and the first thing my hand stopped on, on the first day I used it, was that.”

She said, “And I did not find that out with the lamp, because I am not holding the lamp. I found it out with my hand, in the dark, walking that table the way I have walked my own two hundred days running.”

## What She Said Out Loud Standing There

And then she stopped, because she had heard herself.

She stood in the dark at the far side of a table with her hands flat on a place that is of the length of a forearm and the breadth of a hand and is kept, and there was no person in that room, and there had never been one, and she had said a figure out loud, standing up, in the ordinary voice, with her hands on it.

She said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it came out very quiet:

“That is what keeping it is.”

She stood there for about a count of ninety.

She said, “A board came up that stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four and it set out a place at the far side of a table, and it said what keeping it is, and I read it out loud in that room at the fourth hour before I understood any of it.”

She said, “It is kept by being there when the figures are said out loud.”

She took her hands off the wood.

She said, “And I have just done that. In the dark. On the twenty-sixth of this month, standing up, in the ordinary voice, with my hands on the place. And I did not put myself here and no one has asked me to and nothing in this building knows about it.”

She said, “And I do not know what it cost and I am not going to find out this month.”

## And Then She Went And Looked At The Other Two

She went down one landing and through the doorway of the second room and walked its table with her hand on the leg of it, at the same speed, in the dark, and she stopped between the twentieth pace and the end.

She said, out loud, “It is here.”

And she went down one more landing, past her own lamp, which she stepped over and did not pick up, and into the first room, and walked that table the same way.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “It is here as well.”

She put her hand flat on it for about a count of forty and then took it off and went out into the doorway of that first room and stood there.

She said, “Three landings down from this room is the room I have been in for four hundred and ninety-eight days, and there is a place at the far side of that table of the same length and the same breadth, and I have refused it twice, out loud, and on a leaf.”

She said, “And I refused it as though it were mine.”

She stood in that doorway and did not move for a long time.

She said, “That is the sentence. It took me eighteen days and a stair to get to and I am going to be standing in a dark room saying it to myself for some time now, and I am not going down there to say it out loud, and I do not know that I ever will.”

And then she stood in the middle of that room for about a count of a hundred and forty and said the thing out loud that had come up out of her while her hand was on it.

She said, “I have been in three rooms above mine and there is a kept place at the far side of every one of them, and there is not one person in any of them.”

She said, “And there is a kept place at the far side of mine, and it is the same length and the same breadth, and I have refused it twice.”

She said, “And I have been standing four feet off the near end of a table that is one of a great many, in a building that has a great many of them, and I have not known that until the twenty-fourth of this month.”

## The One Sentence

And then the voice in her head said something, and she did not answer it, and she has not answered it since.

It said, “You have been calling it your room.”

She stood there for about a count of ninety and did not say one word back, and she did not tell anybody that it had said it, and she is not going to say out loud in this room what it said.

## Said Standing, In The Ordinary Voice

She said the finding out loud at about the seventh hour of the twenty-sixth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, standing in the first of those rooms with her left hand on the leg of a table twenty-two paces long, in the dark, with one lamp burning on a landing above her and no person in the building.

She said it as one sentence, and she said it slowly, and she said it standing up in the ordinary voice, the way she has said every figure she has got since she was nine years old.

She said, “My room is not a room. It is one of a great many, and the only reason mine was different is that somebody was in it.”

And then she stood there with her hand on the leg of that table and said nothing at all for a long time, and she did not say anything else, because there was one more part of it and she was not going to say that part in the dark on a table that is not hers.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the seventh hour and forty minutes:

“And I am not going to be all right about it for some time, and I am not going to try.”

She said, “I have been all right about that table for four hundred and ninety-eight days, and I have been all right about it by not asking one question about where the light in it came from or who turned forty boards face up in a room I had never been in.”

She said, “And I asked one, on the eighth of this month, standing four feet off the near end of it. And here is the answer.”

There was a lamp burning on the landing above that room in a building that had never had a lamp in it, and three rooms above a table twenty-two paces long with a hand at the far end of it that has not written a word in four hundred years, and a bar against a wall on the left, and a bundle under the far end corded twice and not knotted, and a strip of pale wood face up under the table at the near end of the leg.

And in each of three rooms above that room, the same table, and a lamp in a cage out on a shelf, and forty boards face up under the middle, and a bundle under the far end, and between the twentieth pace and the end a place of the length of a forearm and the breadth of a hand that nobody had ever sat in.

And nobody in any of them.
