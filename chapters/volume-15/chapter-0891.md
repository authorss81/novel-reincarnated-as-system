# The Unfinished Ninth

The stone of the last landing was colder than any stone she had sat on in a month, and it told her so through the floor before she was awake enough to answer it.

She sat past the top of the stair with her back to the wall she had come up and her lamp an eyelash of flame in front of her, and she put her hand on the place behind her fourth rib without deciding to, and it did not do anything at all.

The cold came up through the floor into her the way it came every morning past the ninth, and she sat still until it stopped being news, the way she had sat still for two days on the twelfth with anger unthrown. Mornings past the last landing had no light in them. The lamp gave two paces where it stood and the dark took the rest.

## What Is Left to Do

She had climbed for a month. Fourteen landings counted and a fifteenth and a top past the last of them. A fall. A name asked. A count said where it could be read. Merit settled off the ground and gone.

“There is nothing on the page that says the vessel is finished,” she said out loud, sitting, into the dark past three paces of light. “Nine stages taken and the ninth taken on day two thousand seven hundred and thirty-two and nothing since says shut. I have been carrying an open thing for one hundred and ninety-eight days and calling it finished because there was nothing past nine to call it.”

Her breath went four counts sitting still. The sentences went on above her one at a time, plain, reporting, through her to him.

“Today I find out what is left to do to it, on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and twenty-four. That is the work and there is no other work on this stair.”

She sat past the top of the stair with her hands flat on the cold stone and her back to the wall she had come up, and the lamp an eyelash in front of her, and she did the sitting the way she had done it for two days on the twelfth, which was without working, letting the nothing sit next to the makings.

Her breath went nine lying still, the way it went lying still everywhere, and four sitting still. Her right arm hung open at the elbow for six hundred and thirty-six days and shook once, and she lay still until it stopped, once.

## Him, on Finishing

“I want the truth even when it costs you,” he said.

She sat on the cold stone past the last landing with her hands flat on the floor and heard the sentence the way she heard every sentence he said, which was fully, and without deciding in advance.

“That is a dear want,” she said. “Nine years you have wanted things for me and this is the first one that does not feed me.”

“I have fed you wants for nine years,” he said. “You are done with feeding. I want the truth out of you the way you want figures out of a column, and I want it even when it costs you.”

“Then listen,” she said. “It is arithmetic and it is nine sentences and I am saying it standing.”

She stood up off the stone, because findings were lines, with the lamp an eyelash at her feet and the dark past the last landing all around her and nothing in either hand.

“Stand with me,” she said. “That is not arithmetic. That is the asking and I am saying it out loud.”

“I am standing it with you,” he said. “Nine sentences. I have every one of them already and I am hearing them anyway.”

## Nine Sentences

“The ninth was taken on day two thousand seven hundred and thirty-two and this is day two thousand nine hundred and thirty and one hundred and ninety-eight days stand between them. A vessel built in nine stages holds what is put into it and nothing else. Nine years of my work went into it and four hundred years of keeping stood around it and neither of those is inside it.

“What is inside it is my own foundation and my own breath and nothing else. A container with nothing in it is not a container. Six hundred and twenty-nine days stand in that room below me and six hundred and thirty-six days stand open in this arm and neither of those closes anything.

“One thousand three hundred and seventy-one days are gone at this morning and not one of them comes back and not one of them is in it either. Shutting needs a thing shut in. The only person in this building who has ever been in the same room as me and not been sent anywhere is under me by eight flights and I have no name of his I can put anywhere.”

She stood past the last landing in the dark with nothing in her hands and listened to the nine sentences go past into the traffic one at a time, plain, reporting, through her to him.

Nothing answered. Nothing objected.

The traffic took them the way it took everything said standing, and his half went on inside her where she could hear it, and the nine sentences sat in the keeping the way figures sat, derived, said first in her own voice, hers before anything else. She stood with her hands empty at her sides and felt them go, and the going was lighter than the holding had been, though nothing on the stair had changed but her.

## What Shutting Costs

She sat back down, because standing was for saying and sitting was for keeping, and she put her hands flat on the cold stone.

The nine sentences sat in the traffic past her, said, gone, unanswered. Her breath went four counts sitting still. Her arm shook once and she lay still until it stopped, once, the way she had lain still for two days on the twelfth.

“Every stage I took was built out of my own foundation,” she said out loud, sitting. “That is the shape of it and I am saying so on cold stone. The taking thins the vessel to let ascent through and the vessel stands open and an open vessel is not finished. Finishing is shutting. Shutting costs a thing shut in.”

The cage stood three hundred and ninety-six days out on a shelf eight flights below her. The bar stood five hundred and eighty-six days against its wall, dated nowhere. None of those shut anything.

“A person,” she said. “That is the price and there is no other price on this stair. Not boards. Not days. Not breath. A person in my arithmetic, and the only person in this building who has ever stood in the same room as me and not been sent anywhere is eight flights down with black hands and a name in my mouth.”

She put her hands flat on the cold stone and felt the price sitting next to her the way the nothing had sat next to the fourteen makings, heavy, unthrown, hers.

“Nine stages built out of foundation,” she said. “Mine. Every one of them. The taking thins the wall to let ascent through and the thinned wall stands open and the open stands unfinished. I have been calling it finished because nine is where counting stops. Nine is where the building stops. This stair does not stop and neither does the vessel.”

She stopped, because the name was in her mouth and not on any board, and saying it here would put it somewhere she had not decided to put it.

“Not said,” she said. “Held. That is the arrangement until I say otherwise.”

## The Ninth, Not Finished

“You are quiet,” she said.

“I am here,” he said. “I have nothing to put against nine sentences and I am not going to invent any. You are right about the vessel and you are right about the price and I want you to sit with both before you spend either.”

“I am sitting,” she said.

“Sit longer,” he said. “I want you sitting with it the way you sit with stone. Two days. Do not spend a person the way you spend a morning.”

“Two days,” she said. “Sitting with a price the way I sat with anger. That sitting made nothing and the nothing was not a failing. I can sit with this the same way.”

“You sit two days and merit settles off the ground,” he said. “Sit two more and see what settles. I am sitting them with you.”

“You have never once asked me to wait,” she said.

“I am asking now,” he said. “Two days. That is the whole of the asking and it is not nothing.”

She sat on the cold stone past the last landing with the lamp an eyelash in front of her and the asking next to her the way the price sat next to her, two sittings side by side. Two days of sitting with anger had settled merit off the ground. Two days of sitting with a price might settle nothing, and the nothing would not be a failing, the way the last nothing had not been a failing.

She sat on the cold stone past the last landing with the lamp an eyelash in front of her and the nine sentences gone into the traffic and the price sitting next to her the way the nothing had sat next to the fourteen makings, and she did what he asked, because in nine years he had never once asked her to wait, and the asking was new enough to obey.

The place behind her fourth rib pulled.

“The ninth is not finished,” she said out loud, at about the ninth hour, sitting, with her hand on the stone. “That is the finding and it is nine sentences long and it lives here now past the last landing, and everything I do from here is finishing or it is nothing.”

She sat on the cold stone past the last landing with the lamp an eyelash in front of her and the nine sentences gone into the traffic and the price sitting next to her, and the sentences went on above her one at a time, and his half went on inside her, and the sitting held all of it without moving any of it, the way stone holds everything.
