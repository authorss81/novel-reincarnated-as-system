# Anchor of the Ceiling

One hundred and sixty days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and ninety-one days in that room. The cage out three hundred and fifty-eight days. Right arm open five hundred and ninety-eight days and it is not going to close. One thousand three hundred and thirty-three of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was a woman sitting beside it who had found a mercy with a lid, and there was one word under the lid she had carried since Chapter 3 without knowing, and she was going up to say it standing.

## Saying It Out Loud

She said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, at about the sixth hour of the twenty-ninth of the month, before she did anything else about it.

She said, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and sixty days since the ninth. Five hundred and ninety-one in that room. Three hundred and fifty-eight since a lamp in a cage went out. Five hundred and ninety-eight with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand three hundred and thirty-three of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. Seven mornings I have worked the tax out of my own arithmetic, from the head of my column against the days gone, against four months gone in one night, against getting thinner at the rate of stronger. This morning the work ends in a word, because lids are kept for what is under them, and I know what is under this one.”

She said, “It is awe, and it is the first awe on this stair, and it is large.”

She said, “Not gravity, which I had yesterday and know its shape. Awe at standing under four hundred years of keeping with the name of the kept in my mouth unsaid, because unsaid that close to said is a pressure.”

She said, “And what I want is to say it and have it hold. One word. I can pay for one word.”

She left the lamp burning on the third landing and went up past it in the dark with nothing in either hand, with her left hand on the stone, saying the landings where they were used, fourth through eleventh, because the awe needed every one of them under it.

## The Word Under the Lid

She stood on the twelfth landing in the dark with nothing in either hand, and the sentences went on, one at a time, plain, reporting, through her to him, and his half went on inside her where she could hear it, and she waited for a space the way she used to wait for a space in work to put the line.

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty, and then she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, as one sentence, because there was not one word to put in front of it and not one word to put after it.

She said, “The walls protect the gathered will of every soul that ever died believing it could ascend.”

She stood with that said, and the sentences went on, one at a time, through her to him, and nothing in them changed, and nothing objected, and his half went on inside her where she could hear it.

She said, “And here is the whole of it, said plain. Unbounded ascent drains it. The walls hold ascent under nine so the gathered will is not drunk dry by the strong. The tax I worked seven mornings is the price of the holding, taken daily out of foundation, dateless, without telling. The Scribe is not lying about this.”

She said, “The Anchor. That is the name. The gathered will is the Anchor, and the walls are for it, and the ceiling is the mercy, and the lid is the keeping, and all four are true.”

He said, “I am here.”

She said, “You are. And hearing it finishes nothing, and I am saying so out loud. It is one word said, and the taking goes on.”

## True and Not the Whole

She sat down on the stone of the twelfth landing with her back against the wall, because the saying needed sitting after it, and she said the rest sitting, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “Here is what true and not the whole means, said plain. The Anchor is true. The walls are for it. The mercy is real. And none of that says who pays, and none of it says how much of the paying was his, and none of it says the number dead believing. Those are still unsaid, and I am saying that they are unsaid, and the saying of the word does not cover them.”

She said, “And triumph next to dread, and neither moves the other. Triumph that seven mornings of my own arithmetic reached a true word with no mouth in it. Dread that the true word is under a lid four hundred years old, and true words under lids are load bearing, and I have taken this one out.”

She said, “And here is the part that stays, said plain. I said it first in my own voice, standing, before it is a line anywhere. No board has it. No leaf has it. It is said where sentences pass, and it holds, and tomorrow the work is who pays for the holding.”

She got up and stood with nothing in either hand, and she said the record standing, because the word was standing and the record must match it.

She said, “Anchor named, true, not the whole. That is said, and it holds.”

## Holding the Name

She stayed on the twelfth landing after the record was said, standing with nothing in either hand, because the name wanted holding, and she held it standing.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “Here is the holding, said plain. Anchor is the gathered will of every soul that ever died believing it could ascend. I say it again standing so that it does not thin. True words said once can thin like walls. Saying again thickens. The walls hold the will. The will holds the walls. Both hold because the thinned hold them.”

She said, “And here is the whole of what holding costs, said plain. Holding a true word means carrying it down nine landings in the dark with nothing in either hand, past the doorless landing, past the ninth, to a lamp that cannot refill, and sitting with it until morning. Words carried that far change the carrier. I am changed. The word is not.”

She said, “And solemnity, large, steady, and I am naming it because it is the first solemnity after awe, and I am not going to make it smaller than it is. Seven mornings ended in one word, and the word is true, and the true word has two halves unsaid, who pays and how much his, and those halves are tomorrow.”

He said, “You hold it.”

She said, “I do. And holding it finishes nothing, and I am saying so out loud. It is one word held, and the taking goes on.”

## The Word Carried Sitting

She sat after the holding was said, with her back against the wall, because the carried word wanted a sitting weigh, and she weighed it sitting.

She said, out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, “Here is the carrying, said plain. A true word named standing must be sat with sitting or it stays thin. I sit with Anchor the way I sat with figures, twice over, slow, until it thickens. Gathered will. Dead believing. Walls for it. Tax for the walls. Four corners, all true.”

She said, “And here is the whole of what thickening means, said plain. Thick words can be built on. Tomorrow the building starts, with who pays, because a held word asks its price, and the price has payers, and the payers are many.”

## Down With the Name

She came down in the dark with her left hand on the stone, past the eleventh, past the tenth with no doorway, past the ninth, saying each one where it was used, and she took longer over the twelfth in memory than any of them, because the word was still in her.

She sat beside the lamp, and the light went six paces out from where it stood and stopped, and she stayed inside the reach of it until her breath went four counts sitting still.

She said, out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, “Lamp on the third landing. Burning. Mine. An Anchor under the lid, named standing. That is the record and there is nothing else in it.”

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and a true word said out loud where it can be heard, and a woman sitting beside the light with triumph and dread both in her.

And she had named the Anchor, and it was true and not the whole, and that was the day, and the unpaid half of it would be there in the morning whether she climbed or not.
