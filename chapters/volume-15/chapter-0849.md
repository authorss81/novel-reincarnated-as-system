# Not Hers

Seventy days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and one days in that room. The cage out two hundred and sixty-eight days. Right arm open five hundred and eight days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and forty-three days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in that room and no person in the three rooms above it and no person on any stair in that tower, and there was a lamp burning on the stone of the second landing, and there had been one there every night since the twenty-second.

## Two Days Without Going Down

She had not been down.

She said that out loud at about the seventh hour of the twenty-ninth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, standing in the third of those rooms with her back against the far wall and her right arm open across her knees, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “Two days and two nights. I have not been inside the room at the bottom of that stair since the sixteenth of this month, and I have not been below the first landing since the twenty-fourth.”

She said, “I have eaten nothing because I am not hungry, and I have slept about a count of a hundred and forty at a time on that landing with my back against the wall, and I have not gone into any of those rooms at night, and I am not going to say why, and it is not a rule I have made.”

She said, “And I want it said out loud that I have been sitting in the dark on a cold stone floor for two days because of a sentence I found with my hand on the twenty-fourth of this month. Because that is a thing I have never done. In nineteen years I have not sat in a room and stopped.”

She said, “And I want to say what I did with the two days, because it was not nothing and I do not want it to read as though I had been resting.”

She said, “I walked all three of those tables twice. And I walked the boards under the middle of each of them with my thumb, the way I find writing, and there is a second number cut on the top board of every one of them, and it is the same second number in all three, and I am not saying it out loud and I am not writing it anywhere.”

She said, “And I went into the room on the sixth landing. That is the fourth room I have been inside, and there was nothing on the near end of that table either, and I stood at the near end of it for about a count of a hundred and forty and said four figures out loud, because it is what I do at the seventh hour and there was nothing else to do with my mouth.”

## What She Came Up Here With

She said, “And here is what I came up here with, and it is the reason I went up, and I want to say it plainly because everything that is wrong with the last two days is going to come out of it.”

She said, “I came up here with a refusal.”

She said, “I refused the place at the far side of that table. I said it out loud in that room at the ninth hour and twenty minutes on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen, standing up, in the ordinary voice, where the far end of the table could hear me, and I could not un-say it and I did not want to.”

She said, “And I wrote it on a leaf on the seventh of this month and put it in a cloth with forty boards on top of it and sent it down nine hundred and four steps, and it is the first thing I have ever sent anywhere, and it cannot be called back, and I would not call it back.”

She said, “And I had a picture of that in my head for nineteen years before I said it, and the picture was mine.”

## The Picture She Had

She said, “Here is the picture and it is the whole of what I am going to take apart.”

She said, “There was a room with a place in it. The place had been kept for four hundred years. A person kept it, and the person was going to be me, and it had been kept a long time and nobody had been in it and the keeping was waiting for me.”

She said, “And when I said no, I said no to a thing that had been kept for me. That was the shape of it. That was what it felt like at the ninth hour and twenty minutes on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and fourteen, and I have carried that shape for sixty-five days and I was not standing four feet off the near end of that table.”

She stood there for about a count of ninety.

She said, “I was standing in a room that is one of a great many, in a building that has a great many of them, and the place at the far side of it is not mine, and it never was, and it was not kept for me.”

## Where The No Came From

She said, “And I am going to say that out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, because it has been coming up out of me for two days and if I do not put it in the air it is going to come out in something worse.”

She said, “The refusal was not mine.”

She stood there and did not move while she said it.

She said, “Not the room. Not the keeping. Not the four hundred years. None of it. A person kept that place and that person is not me and is not anybody I have met and is not a boy of sixteen with oil on his hands, and I have known that since the seventh of this month and I did not let it be the whole of what I knew.”

She said, “And I refused it anyway, twice, out loud, and on a leaf, and I am not taking either of those back.”

She said, “And here is the part that is actually on me and not on the room.”

## What Is Actually On Her

She said, “For nineteen years I have stood four feet off the near end of that table in the dark and said figures out loud into it.”

She said, “And every one of those figures went into a table that was not mine. Every single one. Every morning since I was nine years old. I have said out loud in this building that a record does not need a light and does not need to be read, and I said that on the seventh of this month about a strip of pale wood, and I was right about the wood and I have been wrong about the table.”

She put her hand flat on the top of the table beside her.

She said, “I thought the room was mine because I was the only thing in it that had ever said anything out loud. And I am still the only thing in it. And that is not the same as the room being mine, and I have gone nineteen years on it and this is what nineteen years buys you.”

She said, “And the sentence from the twenty-sixth stands and it is not going to be softened this morning. The only reason that room was different is that somebody was in it, and the somebody was me, and the room did not do that. I did it. And when I go up that stair it is a room with a lamp in a cage on the shelf and forty boards under the middle of the table and nothing on the near end of it, the same as the three above it.”

She said, “And the part that is going to be in me a long time is this. In four hundred and ninety-eight days I never once looked at that table and worked out what it was a part of.”

She said, “I thought about the boy. I thought about the oil. I thought about my own arithmetic forty times a morning, and I thought about the hand at the far end of that table, and I thought about the second place, and I thought about what a price does not date.”

She said, “And I never once thought: this is one of a great many, and it is a building, and I am in a part of it.”

She said, “And the reason I never thought it is that there was no reason to think it. That is the whole of it. That is what four hundred and ninety-eight days of a person who is good at arithmetic does to her when every question she asks has an answer that is about herself.”

## And The Part She Is Not Going To Say

She said, “And I am not going to say the other part.”

She said, “There is one more thing in this and it is about what keeping is for, and I said out loud on the twenty-fourth of this month that I was not going to say it in the dark on a table that is not mine, and I am not going to say it here either.”

She said, “It is in the room at the bottom of that stair. It is four landings down and about two hundred days back and there is a board on that table about a foot in from the near end of it that sets a place out and says what keeping it is, and I read that board out loud on the first of this month.”

She said, “And I am not going down for it.”

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “There are two days to the first of this month. I am going to sit here on this stone floor and not go down, and that is not a punishment and it is not a decision either. It is that I do not want to go down and see it again from the near end.”

And then she said the last of that morning out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the eighth hour, with her right arm open in front of her and the dark going up in one direction and down in the other.

She said, “I have been the only person in a room for four hundred and ninety-eight days and I did not know that was a job.”

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the second landing with no one near it, and three rooms above a room with nobody in it, and a woman on a stone floor two landings below a ceiling she had not found, not going down, with two days in front of her and a first of a month at the end of them.
