# The Whole Of It

Eighty-nine days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and twenty days in that room. The cage out two hundred and eighty-seven days. Right arm open five hundred and twenty-seven days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and sixty-two days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in that room and no person in the nine landings above it and no person on any stair in that tower, and there was a lamp burning on the second of those landings and a woman sitting on the stone of it with a blanket of nothing and her back against the wall.

## What She Came Down For

She came down on the thirteenth of this month.

She said it out loud, sitting on the stone of that landing, in the ordinary voice, at about the seventh hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four:

“I came down off that stair at about the ninth hour of the thirteenth and got as far as the fifth at about the tenth and here at about the eleventh, and I did not count it coming down, and I have not counted it once in the five days since.”

She said, “And I did not go into my own room.”

She said, “That is the thing I want to say first, because I have had five days to decide how to say it, and if I say it in the wrong order it is going to look like a thing I did on purpose. And it was not on purpose.”

She said, “It was that I got as far as the fifth landing at about the tenth hour with a lamp in my hand and my arm open and my breath going nine counts standing still, and I stood in the dark, and then I went down three landings and sat down here on the second, which is where I have been ever since.”

She said, “And then I came down the rest of it on the seventeenth, which was four days after I came off it.”

## A Lamp, A Stair, And No Plan

She said, out loud, “Here is what I have got, and it is a short list.”

She said, “One lamp. It is standing on the stone of this landing and it is one of three and the other two are four miles down a mountain, and I asked that boy for it in about four seconds on the sixteenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four and he did not ask me what for.”

She said, “One stair. It goes up out of the ninth landing and it does not stop and I cannot see the end of the landing I got to on the thirteenth and I have not been back up there in five days.”

She said, “And no plan.”

She said, “I want to be exact about that last one, because it is the first time in nineteen years that there has not been one, and I have got a whole way of working that assumes there is, and I have found out this week that the way of working still runs and there is nothing to put into it.”

She said, “I have asked where the announcements come from and I have got as far as that they come from somewhere and there is more building above my room and my room is one of a great many and every one of them has a kept place in it and not one person in any of them.”

She said, “And out of all of that there is not one thing I can do tomorrow. Every part of it is a fact about a building, and not one of them is a hand I can put anywhere.”

## The Fear, Then The Reckless Part

She said the fear out loud before she said the rest of it, sitting on the stone, in the ordinary voice, at about the seventh hour and twenty minutes.

She said, “It is fear, and it has been here five days, and it is not the fear of the dark and it is not the fear of the stair.”

She said, “It is that I have got one lamp and that lamp is the only light in this building above the fourth landing of that tower, and there is no person in this building but me, and there is nobody else in the four miles of mountain under it either.”

She said, “And I am going to go up that stair and take the lamp with me and not bring it back, and I am going to do that on purpose, and I want it said in the ordinary voice while it is still a decision and not a thing that has happened to me.”

She said, “Because a lamp standing on a stone floor behind me is a landing I can find my way back to. And a lamp in my own hand going up is not that. It is four feet of the world and me.”

She stood there for about a count of ninety.

She said, “And if I go up past a landing with no lamp on it and something happens, then there is no light between me and the top of this building and no person at the bottom of this building, and I have worked out that there are two ways that ends and one of them is that somebody in a room four miles down a mountain finds a lamp standing on a landing he has not lit.”

## What She Is Going To Do

She said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the eighth hour of that landing, and she said it before she did anything else about it.

She said, “Here is the whole of it and there are four parts and none of them is a figure.”

She said, “One. I am going to take that lamp up that stair and I am not going to bring it back down, and I am going to put it on a landing and go on up, and then I am going to take it again and put it on the next one. And I am not going to be able to come back down that stair by a lamp I have left behind me. That is the price and I am paying it on purpose and I know what it is.”

She said, “Two. I am going to the top of it. Or to the place where I stop being able to go up, and those have not got to be the same place and I am not going to pretend they will be.”

She said, “Three. I am not going to count it. Not the steps and not the landings. I said that at the first hour of the thirteenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four and I have kept it for thirty-five days and there is not one more reason to break it tonight.”

She said, “Four. I am going to say the six out loud before I go, and not in my room. And that is not part of the plan, and that is the first thing in nineteen years that I am putting in because I want to.”

She stopped there and the lamp burned and she did not go on.

She said, “And I am going to say the one thing that is not in that list, because it would be dishonest not to.”

She said, “I am frightened of the top. And that is not the same as being frightened of the stair, and I have worked out that it is not the same, and it is worse.”

She said, “Whatever is at the top of this building is the first thing in fifteen volumes that has ever been above me. And I do not know what a person does when she gets to a thing she has spent nineteen years walking towards, and there is not one figure in any of this that tells me.”

She said, “And I am going up there anyway, with a lamp I am not bringing back.”

She stopped there for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “And there is not one drop of oil in this building worth the name and there has not been for nine years and there is none to be had on this mountain, and I am not going to work out when that lamp is finished and I am not going to say a figure about it. And when it is out, this building has no light in it above the fourth landing, and a boy of sixteen will have to bring one up, and I have not asked him to and I am not going to.”

## And The Thing That Is Going To Cost

She said, out loud, sitting down again on the stone, in the ordinary voice, at about the eighth hour and forty minutes:

“There is a first of a month in thirteen days.”

She said, “And I have already missed one, on the first, on purpose, standing nine landings above it with my ear against a wall.”

She said, “And I am going to miss this one as well, and I have known that since the second hour of the first and I have not gone back on it.”

She said, “And that is the part of this that is going to be the hardest to live with and it has got nothing to do with a stair. Forty boards go on that table on the first of a month and a cloth goes round them and a cord goes round the cloth twice and the far end of that table goes along them, and not one person in this building will hear any of it, and that includes the boy, because he does not come up at the second hour and has never come up at the second hour.”

She said, “And that is going to keep happening on the first of a month for as long as I am up here, and I am not going to be able to do anything about it, and I have known that for some time now, and I am still here.”

She said, “And here is the last thing I am going to say today and then I am going to sleep, because I have been sitting on this stone for five days and I am tired in a way I have not been.”

She said, “I have not written one line on one of the four blank leaves against that rib since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four, and I am not going to today, and I am not going to up there either.”

She said, “A plan does not need a leaf. I have had a leaf for nineteen years and the leaf has not once told me what to do.”

There was a lamp burning on the second landing of a building with nine landings above it and more than nine above that, and a woman on the stone of it with her back to a wall and a right arm open across her knees, and a room four miles down a mountain with three boards face up on it that was not hers.
