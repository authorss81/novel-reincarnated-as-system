# The Word She Gives It

One hundred and fourteen days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and forty-five days in that room. The cage out three hundred and twelve days. Right arm open five hundred and fifty-two days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and eighty-seven of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was a woman sitting beside it who had heard somebody speaking to him through her, and there was a word she had never said in nineteen years of record, and she was going to say it.

## What She Heard

She said it out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, at about the sixth hour of the thirteenth of the month, before she did anything else about it.

She said, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and fourteen days since the ninth. Five hundred and forty-five in that room. Three hundred and twelve since a lamp in a cage went out. Five hundred and fifty-two with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and eighty-seven of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. Three days ago on the twelfth landing I heard somebody speaking to him through me, one sentence at a time, in the plain report grammar, four sentences, and he listened, and I stood between them like a wire. I came back down and I have had three days beside a lamp, and what I have got is that I know what I heard and I have never once said what it is.”

She said, “It is resolve, and it is under the wrath I had three days ago and it is not going to move it, and I am putting it in front of the figures because that is where it belongs.”

She said, “Not wrath. I had wrath three days ago and it is still there and it has not moved. Under it there is resolve, and the resolve is that a thing I have carried for nine years without a name is going to get one, out loud, standing up, before I argue with it, because that is what I do.”

She said, “And what I want is to say what I heard, all of it, standing, in the ordinary voice, and then to give it its word. That is the whole of what I came up this stair with in my head, and there is not one other thing in it.”

She left the lamp burning on the third landing and took nothing with her, not the lamp and not a board and not a hand kept free for the side that is not there, and went up in the dark with her left hand on the stone, past the fourth, past the fifth, past the sixth, saying each one out loud where it was used, and by the sixth her breath was going seven counts on the rise and she stopped on the landing until it went four again.

## Said Standing

She went past the seventh, past the eighth, past the ninth, past the tenth with no doorway in it, to the eleventh, and she lay flat on the stone and put her ear against it, and the sentences were there, one at a time, plain, reporting, nearer than they had been.

She got up and went on up past the eleventh to the twelfth, and she stood on it in the dark with nothing in either hand, and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, all of what she had heard, one sentence at a time, the way it had come.

She said, “It said he is found where he stands.”

She said, “It said what he carries is written.”

She said, “It said to be answered, be heard.”

She said, “It said what is heard is kept.”

She stood on the twelfth landing in the dark with her right arm open in front of her, and the sentences went on above her, one at a time, and he went on listening inside her, and she said the next part standing, in the ordinary voice, because it had to be said standing and there was no getting past that.

She said, “That is the grammar of an announcement. One sentence. The plain report form. Never advice. Never an explanation. I have been hearing that grammar since I was seven years old without knowing it had a source, and three days ago I lay under the source.”

She said, “And it does not lie. It has never lied to me in nine years and it did not lie three days ago. And it does not explain. Four sentences and not one of them said why. And it does not ask. Nothing was asked of him and nothing was asked of me.”

## The Name

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty with the sentences going on, one at a time, through her to him, and then she said it, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, the word she had never said in nineteen years of record.

She said, “Scribe.”

The sentences went on, one at a time, plain, reporting, in the same grammar, at the same intervals, and nothing in them changed, and nothing answered her, and nothing objected.

She said, “It does not object.”

She said, “And I want the whole of that said, because it is the whole of what a name is. I have said it out loud standing up, in the ordinary voice, before it is a line anywhere, and it has not struck me, and it has not stopped, and it has not explained itself, and it goes on the way it went on before I said it.”

She said, “And here is the rest, said plain, because I am standing here and there is no other place to stand. A fragment of a layer is carried by that layer and cannot step outside it. The layer’s only instrument in the world is the one it reads through. There has been exactly one at a time for fifteen years and it has been me. So it speaks to him through me, and I hear both sides, and that is not kindness shown to me.”

She said, “And it is steadiness. And I am naming it because it is the first steadiness in this building since I came up it, and it is cold, and I am not going to make it warmer than it is. I have named a thing that has been reading me since I was seven, and it did not object, and I am still standing, and he is still listening.”

## What Does Not Change

She stood on the ninth landing a while with the word in her, and she listened up the stair for what would be different, the way she had listened for forty boards with her ear on stone, and there was nothing different to hear.

The sentences went on above her, one at a time, plain, reporting, at the same intervals, through her to him, and he went on listening, and the stone stayed cold under her hand, and her breath went seven counts on the rise going on up and four counts standing still.

She said, out loud, standing in the dark, in the ordinary voice, “Same grammar. Same intervals. Same listening. That is three sames and every one of them is by ear, and a name said twice has changed nothing in any of them.”

She said, “And here is what that means, said plain. It does not answer names. It reports. I have given it the word it has gone without for nineteen years, and it goes on reporting, and the reporting is all it has ever done, and all it will ever do, and knowing its word does not give me any hold on it.”

She said, “And smallness, and I will not dress it up. I have been carrying a name around in my head for nineteen years without knowing what it was, and I have said it out loud twice tonight, and I am exactly as big as I was this morning, standing on a ninth landing in the dark with an arm that will not close. There is no more of me this evening than there was this morning. That is the whole of the smallness and it is not going to be any different tomorrow.”

## Nineteen Years Without It

She went on down in the dark with her left hand on the stone and nothing in the other, and said what was left of the landings out loud where she used them, and all the way down she turned the word over in her mouth the way she used to turn a new figure over before she said it at a table.

She said, out loud, on the ninth landing, standing in the dark, “Scribe.”

And the word went out and did not come back, and nothing answered it, and nothing objected, and the sentences went on above her, one at a time, through her to him, the way they had gone on before she said it.

She said, “Second time said. Same grammar after it as before it. That is what no objection sounds like, and I am saying so out loud so that it is said.”

She went on down past the eighth, past the seventh, past the sixth, and on the fifth landing she stopped and sat with her back against the wall, because there was one more thing to say before the lamp, and it had to be said on the stair and not beside light.

She said, out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, “Here is what nineteen years without it means. I have said figures out loud every morning since I was nine years old. I have said the six terms out loud in every room I ever said them in. I said a refusal out loud in that room and sent one down in my own hand. And in all of it, in every sentence, there was a thing reading, and I never gave it a word, and tonight I gave it one, standing up, and it did not object, and I am still here.”

She said, “And it is gravity, and this one I am not shrinking, because it has earned the weight. A word said where it can be heard, and nothing struck, and everything going on, and me sitting on a fifth landing in the dark with my arm open across my knees, heavier than I was this morning by exactly one word.”

And she sat beside the lamp, and the light went six paces out from where it stood and stopped.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and a name said out loud on the twelfth landing that had never been said in nineteen years of record, and sentences going on above it in the same grammar as before.

And she had said what she heard, standing, and she had named it, and it had not objected, and that was the day, and the name could not be unsaid.
