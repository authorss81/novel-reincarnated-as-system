# Four Seconds

Fifty-seven days since the ninth stage. Four hundred and eighty-eight days in that room. The cage out two hundred and fifty-five days. Right arm open four hundred and ninety-five days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and thirty days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was a boy of sixteen asleep on the fourth landing of that tower at the first hour with oil on his hands, and he had three lamps and two of them were with him on that landing and one of them was standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of a table twenty-two paces long with the light going a hand’s width along it and stopping.

## Down To The Fourth Landing

She went down at about the seventh hour, without saying anything out loud on the way, which was the first time in nineteen years she had gone down that stair without saying something on it.

She got to the fourth landing and he was awake by then and sitting on the step with his back against the wall and his hands in his lap, and there was oil on the backs of both hands and there was oil on the inside of one wrist.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“I am going to ask you for something.”

He said, “All right.”

She said, “And I am going to ask you for it before I explain it, because I have thought about the order of those two things for a day and a night and this is the better order and I do not know why.”

She said, “I want a lamp.”

And then she could not get the next thing out for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said that afterwards, standing, in the ordinary voice, and she said it without dressing it up, because the whole of that landing is the only place in nineteen years where she has been stuck.

She said, “I have said figures out loud in that room every morning since I was nine years old and I have never once had to get past the first one.”

She said, “And it is not that I did not have the words. I had them at the seventh hour. It is that you are a person, and every figure I have ever said out loud went into a room with nobody in it, and there is not one of them in my mouth that was ever said to anybody, and I have not found out until this morning that those are two different things.”

She said, “So that is what it is like. I have wanted to do it and I have not done it, and the reason is a thing about this stair and not about you, and I would rather you had that from me in that order.”

He said, “All right.”

## The Whole Of The Ask

He said, “Which.”

She said, “Any of them.”

And then he did not do that.

She said that out loud afterwards, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the foot of that stair, because she wants the whole of it out loud and not the neat version:

“He said *which* and I said *any of them* and I meant it, and he looked at me for about a second, and then he looked down the stair, and then he picked up the lamp on the step beside his knee and put it on the step in front of me and went back to putting his hands together, and that was four seconds.”

She said, “I have counted them. It is not a figure about him, it is a figure about me, and it is the only one of the two.”

She said, “And he did not ask me what for.”

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the seventh hour and ten minutes on that landing:

“He has a cut on the inside of his right thumb, about the length of a grain of rice. It has been bleeding and stopped and bleeding again and he is holding that hand the way a person holds a hand that they have decided not to look at.”

She said, “And I am not going to ask him about it. He has never once asked me a thing about myself and there is not one line anywhere in this building that says where he goes in the evening when something has happened to him, and if I ask him about his thumb tonight then that is the thing I asked him for the first time.”

She said, “And he is a person. I am not going to make him into a thing in my record. Those are two different sentences and I have had two hundred days to get them the right way round.”

She said, “He wanted to hand me something. That is all that happened this morning and I am going to be able to work at it for the rest of the day and I am not going to make it into more than that.”

## What Four Seconds Is

She stood on that landing for about a count of a hundred and forty with the lamp on the step in front of her and did not pick it up straight away.

She said, “No one has asked that boy for anything in nineteen years.”

She said, “That is not a figure of mine. It is a figure of this building and I have got it from standing in a room at the seventh hour for two hundred days with a person in the doorway and it is the only count in the record that I did not make myself.”

She said, “And a person who is not used to being asked for a thing does not have the question ready. That is all four seconds is. He had to go and find out whether I meant one or the other, and I gave him an answer that was too big for the question, and he had to work it out himself, and he worked it out in about a second.”

She said, “And then he had it in his hand to give me, and there were three seconds before that, and the three seconds were him getting to a place where he was allowed to hand a thing over.”

She said, “I have never been in a position to hand a thing over and I did not know that it is a position. I have been the person at the end of everything in this building for nineteen years and I did not have one side of that until about four seconds ago on the fourth landing.”

She said, “He is good at oil and he is not stupid and he has never once been asked, and the four seconds are the cost of that and not the fault of it.”

## The Lamp On The Table

She picked the lamp up in her left hand and carried it up the second run on her feet and she did not count it, and she put it down on the wood of that table beside the other one.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“There are three lamps in this building. One of them is the one I have just been given and it is in my hand. One of them is on this table and it is the reason this room has had a person in it on and off for two hundred days. And one of them is at the bottom of that stair with him.”

She said, “And I am not taking the one off this table.”

She put her hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “I want that said before anything else. I have been in this room four hundred and eighty-eight days and the lamp on this table is the only thing in it that has ever come from a person, and I am not the person who takes it, and there is not one drop of argument in that.”

She said, “And I have taken his oil once, on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and ninety-four, and I said it out loud in front of him that morning and he emptied a lamp into mine and went down eight hundred and eighty steps with an empty one in his hand.”

She said, “And that was the first. This is the second thing I have ever taken off a boy who is not bound and not entered and not sent, and it took about four seconds, and I am going up a stair with it.”

## And What One Lamp Is On That Stair

She lit it off the lamp on that table and it took about a count of nine and the wick wanted trimming and she trimmed it with two fingers of her right hand, three of them and a thumb, on an arm that has been open at the elbow four hundred and ninety-five days.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the seventh hour and thirty minutes:

“Every lamp I light on that stair is a lamp he does not carry up eight hundred and eighty steps.”

She said, “That is the whole of the cost and I have got it out in one sentence and it took me a day to find out that it was one sentence and not twenty.”

She said, “There is no store on this mountain. I said that out loud once, standing at the bottom of that stair, and I am not going to say it a second time in the same voice, and there are three lamps and that is all the arithmetic there is.”

She said, “And I am not going to turn his count into mine. His count is how many lamps he carries between here and there. Mine is how many days I have stood at that table. Neither of those is an oil figure and I am not going to build one out of the two of them tonight.”

## And One Thing She Did Not Say

She stood at the near end of that table for a moment with the lamp lit in her left hand.

She said, “I have had that boy in a room for two hundred days and I asked him for a lamp this morning and got one in four seconds, and I am not going to ask him his name, and I have said that out loud more than once and the reason has not changed and I am not going to give it again.”

She said, “There are fifteen days to the first of this month.”

She said, “And I am going up that stair this morning with one lamp and one arm and there is not a rail on it past the fourth flight of anything I have ever walked, and I am going to find out tonight what a person does when she cannot hold the wall and the light at the same time.”

She took the lamp and went across that room to the near wall and put her left hand flat on the cold stone at the second opening, and the light went in and out of the gap and showed her about four feet of stone going up.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “Hold there.”

There was a bar against the wall on the left that had been there four hundred and forty-five days and a bundle under the far end of that table with two turns of cord in it and not a knot and a lamp out in a cage on the shelf that was not going to be lit.

And a boy on the fourth landing with oil on his hands and one lamp, and a woman in a gap in a wall with a lamp and an arm open at the elbow, and neither of them had asked the other for anything else.
