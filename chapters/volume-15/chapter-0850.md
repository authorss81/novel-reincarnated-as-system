# The Room Ran On

Seventy-two days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and three days in that room. The cage out two hundred and seventy days. Right arm open five hundred and ten days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and forty-five days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was no person in that room at the second hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four, and there was no person in it at the seventh hour either, and there was a lamp burning on the second landing of that stair and a woman standing on the ninth landing with her ear against the wall.

## The Second Hour

She said the figures out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, on the ninth landing, at about the second hour, because she says them at the second hour on the first of a month whether or not there is a person within nine landings of her to hear them.

She said, “Forty boards.”

She said, “Nine hundred and four steps going up, and the step at the turn does not change. Four to a minute, ten minutes, and then thirty counts for the cloth and the cord going round them twice.”

She said, “And then the far end of that table.”

And then she stopped, because she had said enough of it and she wanted to hear it.

## She Put Her Ear Against The Wall

She put the flat of her hand on the stone and she got her ear down to it and she stayed there for a count of a hundred and forty.

And there was nothing.

She said, out loud, on her knees on the stone, in the ordinary voice, at about the second hour and ten minutes:

“There is no stair to hear. There is nine landings of building between that table and my ear and I could not hear a board go down on it if one went down on it at this moment.”

She said, “And that is the first time in nineteen years I have put any part of myself against this building to try to hear something in it, and I did it because I wanted to hear forty boards go down on a table.”

She said, “And I am not going down.”

[Forty boards are on the table at the top of the second stair.]

She stood there on that landing for about a count of forty and did not say anything at all.

Then she said, out loud, at about the second hour and twenty minutes, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“That is another bundle that has come up that stair since I came into this room, and it is the first one I have been told about while it was happening.”

She said, “And it was one sentence and I am not going to ask what is in it, because I have known since I was seven years old that they are not for asking, and I have asked one question out loud in the last month and it has been enough.”

She said, “And I am not going to pretend that I did not feel it go through. It is the only instrument I have got and it has never once said anything to me about a thing I was doing, and this morning it said something about a table nine landings under me and I was not at it.”

Then she said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“That is all right. That is what it is for. It was never going to be for me.”

## What Goes On In A Room With Nobody In It

She said, “And here is what happens down there this morning and I am going to say all of it out loud, standing, at the ninth landing, in the ordinary voice, because I have said it out loud at the near end of that table on the first of a month for nineteen years and I am not going to stop saying it now that I am not under it.”

She said, “Forty boards go on that table. A cloth goes round them and a cord goes round the cloth twice. And then the far end of that table goes along the boards, the way it has gone along four hundred years of boards, and it copies what goes across it.”

She said, “And that is the whole of it. There is no part of that morning that has ever needed me.”

She said, “And I want to be exact about what that does to nineteen years of my mornings, because it is a big thing and I am not going to have it going past me on a landing in the dark.”

She put her back against the stone and slid down it a little way and sat there with her right arm open across her knees.

She said, “I thought I was the reason that room did anything. I have said figures out loud into that table every morning since I was nine years old and every one of them went somewhere, and I have built nineteen years of a method on the fact that where I say it matters.”

She said, “It goes somewhere because it goes on a table. And the table was there before I came and the table will be there after I am not.”

She said, “And I am not going to say that makes the nineteen years worth nothing, because that would be the cold talking and it is not that. I am going to say what is true and it is smaller than what I thought.”

## What It Is

She said, “Nobody in that room heard forty boards go on that table this morning.”

She sat there for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “That is the first morning in nineteen years that nobody in that room heard one.”

She said, “And there is a hand at the far end of that table that has not said a word in four hundred years and it went along those boards this morning at about the second hour and ten minutes with me nine landings up a stair with my ear on a wall.”

She said, “And it did not stop because I was not in it. And it did not stop because I was in it either. That is what I have known since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four and I have said it out loud in that room and I want it said on a landing nine landings above it where it cannot reach me.”

## And She Did Not Go Down

She got up off the floor of that landing at about the third hour.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“I am not going down for it.”

She said, “And I am not going down because I would get there in about a count of a hundred and forty and stand four feet off the near end of that table about half an hour after it had all happened, and I would have missed it, and I would have known that I had missed it, and that is two worse than not having been there.”

She said, “And that is the first time in nineteen years I have chosen to miss the first of a month on purpose.”

She said, “And the part that is actually on me is this. It went on. That room went on this morning with a dead lamp in a cage on a shelf at the far end of it and three boards face up about a foot in from the near end and a fourth about a foot further along, and not one person in it, and it went on.”

She said, “That is a cost. I am not going to call it anything else this morning. A thing I have stood under for nineteen years went on without me and it did not stop, and I am not going to sit up here and be glad about that.”

There was a lamp burning on the second landing above a table twenty-two paces long, and forty boards face up about a foot in from the near end of it with nothing on them, and a hand gone along the far end of them and gone, and a bundle under the far end corded twice and not knotted, and a bar against the wall on the left that had been there four hundred and sixty days, and a strip of pale wood face up under the table at the near end of the leg.

And the room ran on, and nobody heard it, and she was nine landings above it with her back against a wall.
