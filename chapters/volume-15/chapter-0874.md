# The Shape of Taking

She found a chip of the stair on the sixth landing, about the size of a thumb and flat enough to get a hand behind, and she put it down on the step and picked it up again to see whether it was loose.

It broke.

It broke in her fingers with a short dry sound, into two pieces and then into four, and the four pieces went down the step past her foot and out of the light.

She stood there holding the grit of it in her open right hand and looked at her hand, three fingers and a thumb, which had lifted a bar nine feet nine and carried it ten years and had just come apart on a chip of a stair.

So that is what it is.

Then she did what she would have done to a figure she did not trust, which was to get a second reading.

She went down two steps and found another chip, bigger, and put it behind the same fingers and lifted. It broke. She tried the edge of the stair itself with the heel of her hand and got a scrape and no stone. She put her thumb under the grit on her palm and pushed it against the tread and it went to powder in the pressure, and there was no way on earth to tell whether the tread had gone or her hand had.

She stopped after that, because three readings of one thing is enough to carry a morning.

She said the day's figures out loud on the spot, standing, in the ordinary voice, with the grit still in her hand, because she was not going to leave a thing like that unsaid for an hour.

“One hundred and forty-eight days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and seventy-nine days in that room. The cage out three hundred and forty-six. This arm open five hundred and eighty-six days. One thousand three hundred and twenty-one gone at this morning.”

Then she went up and she did not stop on the ninth.

## Not Fear

She said the rest of it on the tenth landing, which had no doorway in it and had been warmer than the ninth for as long as she had been climbing.

“Not fear, which I had yesterday and know its shape. Hunger. And not in the belly. Hunger to know what a taking is for.”

She put her open hand out in front of her and looked at what was left in the creases of it.

“A taking with no purpose is theft. A taking with a purpose is a keeping. I have called it theft for nine years and I have never once asked which of the two it is, and I have found out this week that I could have asked at any hour of any day since I was nine.”

She carried on up past it.

“I want the shape of it. Not the whole. The shape. I can hold a shape.”

## Stronger and Thinner

She said the body's part standing on the eleventh, because the body had something to say before the arithmetic did and she had learned, in the nine days since the eighth of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and sixty-four, what it was like to leave a thing to the second half of a morning.

“Here is what my body knows. I have been getting stronger every span since the ninth. I have been getting less solid at the same rate. Both true. I have said neither out loud until this morning and I have been calling the first the stage and the second the stair and neither name was honest.”

The cold came up into her arm and shook it and she stood still until it stopped.

“Stronger means the ninth holds more. Less solid means there is less of me to hold it. A lamp burning brighter on less oil. I have felt both for one hundred and forty-eight days.”

She turned her hand over.

“A chip of stone came apart in this hand this morning because I have been getting brighter on less oil for one hundred and forty-eight days and the wall of the vessel is going thin to carry the ninth.”

## The Arithmetic Standing

She stood on the twelfth landing in the dark and the sentences went on above her, one at a time, plain, reporting, through her to him, and she put the whole of it in one go, standing, because it had been in her in pieces for three days and it was ready.

“Four months went in one night to start it. Ten days and twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes stand at the head of every span. One thousand three hundred and twenty-one are gone now. And I am less solid at the rate I am stronger.”

She stood with that for about a count of a hundred.

“A taking that leaves a person stronger and thinner is not eating the strength. It is eating the solidity and leaving the strength. The strength is the vessel filling. The thinness is the vessel wall going thin to hold it.”

Then she said it, because it had been sitting behind all of that since the eighth landing.

“Something takes foundation out of me daily, dateless, and puts capacity in its place. The capacity is real. I can feel it do work. The foundation is gone and I can feel that too.”

## Asking Him

She sat down on the stone with her back against the wall and asked him straight out, in the ordinary voice, sitting, which she had not done in nine years and which she had made herself do twice in three days.

“What does it feed.”

He was quiet for a bit.

“I have felt it go,” he said. “Every span, for nine years, the same way you feel a thing leave a room you are standing in. I have never once seen where it goes. I have looked. There is a direction and there is no end in the direction.”

“Then you have looked for nine years.”

“Yes.”

“For what.”

“For a thing I could name. I cannot name it. If I name it wrongly we build on it.”

She sat with that. He had spent nine years failing at something and had never once told her, and she understood why at once and did not like understanding it at once.

“You did not tell me because you did not want me to have a wrong name.”

“Yes.”

“That is the first useful silence you have ever kept.”

“It is not silence. It is a failure with a reason attached. Do not make anything of it.”

She made nothing of it. She sat on the cold stone and thought about a man who had been failing in the same direction for nine years without telling the only person in the world who could have helped him do it, and who had told her on the fourteenth instead, and who was right to.

“I am going to ask you again,” she said. “Not tomorrow. When I have something to bring.”

“When you have something to bring,” he said. “Yes.”

She sat there a while longer on the warm stone with her arm across her knees.

It had been four days since she had started this and three of them had been him telling her the shape of his own ignorance, which was not a thing she had expected from him and was considerably harder to work with than comfort would have been.

## Mercy or Theft

She got up and said the shape standing, and she said it well, because she had been carrying it since the eighth landing and the stair was cold and she was done with the cold.

“A wall thinned lets more through. That is what thin is for. So the ninth is a vessel, and a vessel thinned at the top lets something up out of it, and what goes up out of a thinned ninth is ascent.”

She put her hand on the wall of that landing and pushed, not hard, just enough to feel the stone answer.

“My foundation is being thinned to make a larger hole. My ascent goes through it easier than it would otherwise. I am not paying for capacity. I am paying for passage.”

And then the anger came, and it came the way it used to come, and she let it.

“For nine years I thought the taking fed him. The arithmetic says the taking feeds a passage. A hole made larger, and ascent going through a larger hole, and not one person told me, and he did not tell me, and he could not name it.”

She stopped on the warm stone and shut her eyes for a count of forty.

“I have been angry at a person for nine years and the arithmetic says I have been angry at a hole.”

And then, because she was a person who finished her own sentences even when finishing them cost her:

“A hole made larger is not a person. I do not get to be angry at a hole. So I have to ask the next question, which is what a hole made larger is for, and if the answer is that it is for somebody then somebody is back in it, and if the answer is that it is for everyone then there is no one to be angry with.”

She put her hand flat on the wall of that landing.

“I have nine years of anger with nowhere to set it down and two possible places to set it down and I have not looked at either of them. That is tomorrow. Or it is the day after. It is not going to be this morning, this morning has a hole in it.”

## Down With Four Pieces of Chip

She came down in the dark with her left hand on the stone, past the eleventh, past the tenth, past the ninth, saying each one where it was used, and she took longer over the sixth because that was where the stair had come apart in her hand.

The lamp was where she had left it and the light went six paces out from where it stood and stopped. She sat down beside it and put the four pieces of the chip in a line on the stone in front of her, in order, the way she put figures.

She put the day into words, sitting, in the ordinary voice.

“Lamp on the third landing. Burning. A taking that thins the wall so the ascent comes through easier. Found out of my own arithmetic, with one chip of stair as evidence and no mouth in the world telling me anything.”

She sat and looked at the four pieces.

“I am not calling it mercy,” she said. “I have not earned that word and I have not tested it. I am calling it a passage and I am going to go up and find out what is on the other side of it, because that is the only question left that is worth a cold stair.”

He said, “I will be here.”

“You will be here whether or not I say it.”

“Yes,” he said. “That is not the same thing.”

She put the four pieces of the chip into a line on the stone in order, the way she put figures, and left them there while she slept.
