# The Other End

Fifty-one days since the ninth stage. Four hundred and eighty-two days in that room. The cage out two hundred and forty-nine days. Right arm open four hundred and eighty-nine days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and twenty-four days lighter at this morning and not one going back.

There was a lamp standing on the wood a foot in from the near end of that table with the light going a hand’s width along it and stopping, and there was no person in that room at the seventh hour.

## She Asked It Again And Again

She asked it again at the seventh hour on the tenth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four, standing four feet off the near end of that table, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “Where do the announcements come from.”

And nothing came back, and she waited a count of a hundred and forty, and nothing came back.

And then she asked it a third time at about the seventh hour and forty minutes, in the ordinary voice, standing still, with the light going a hand’s width along the wood and stopping, and nothing came back that time either.

And then she said out loud, standing, at about the eighth hour:

“Nobody is going to answer it. I want that said in the ordinary voice and I want it said now and not at the ninth hour when I am tired.”

She said, “And I am not disappointed. There is no person in this building who knows and no instrument in this building that would hear it and there is a hand at the far end of this table that has not said a word in four hundred years. I asked into twenty-two paces of dark three times and I got three answers and the answers are all the same one.”

She said, “So it is mine now.”

## Anger, Named First

She said the next part out loud before she argued with it, because that is what she does.

She said, “It is anger and it is at me and it is not cold.”

She said, “I have been angry in this room before. I have got a use for it and I am not going to dress it up as anything else.”

She said, “Here is what I am angry about. I did not ask a question for nineteen years that it took me ninety seconds this morning to ask. I have asked where a thing comes from every day of my life since I was seven years old. I have asked it about water and about oil and about a man’s hands. And there is one instrument in my head and I read it every single day and I have never once asked where the sentences in it are made.”

She said, “And I could have walked out of this room in about a count of a hundred and forty. That is how long it takes to get from this end of this table to the doorway the stair comes in at. There has been a stair four feet behind me for four hundred and eighty-two days and it goes down and it goes up and I have not gone up it once in nineteen years.”

She stood there for about a count of ninety.

She said, “And the reason I did not go up it is not that I could not. It is that I never had a reason to, and that is the whole of it, and it is the worst kind of reason there is.”

## What The Layer Is

She said, “And so I am going to do what I do with a figure, and take it apart, and see what is on the other side of it.”

She said, “Here is everything I have got about the thing that puts the announcements down to me, and it is nineteen years long and it is short.”

She said, “One. It arrives whole. It does not build in front of me. There is no half of a sentence and no warning and nothing that arrives and then finishes. It is finished when it gets here and that is the only property of it I have ever been able to stand on.”

She said, “Two. It is not in this room. I am standing four feet off the near end of a table in a tower four miles up a mountain and I can read it as clearly standing up as I can with my eyes shut. It has not been in this room once in four hundred and eighty-two days. That is not my discovery from this morning. I have known that since I was nine.”

She said, “Three. There is no maker of it. That is nineteen years of looking and there is no hand at the back and no place and no person, and I have said that sentence out loud more than once in this room and I meant every word of it.”

She said, “Four. And an announcement does something. That is the part I have never once looked at.”

## The Two Ends

She said, “And here is what I have got for that, and it took me about a count of a hundred and forty and it is the whole of this morning.”

She said, “An announcement does something to the world. It turns up on a turn and people are on it by the next morning. It puts a levy in a column and a levy gets taken. It puts a first of a month in and forty boards go up a stair. I have spent fifteen years watching the far end of those sentences land on people and I have never once, not once, gone backwards from a thing that happened to the sentence that did it.”

She said, “I have been reading one end of it.”

She said, “I have been reading the end where the sentence has already arrived and already landed and already happened, and I have been doing it since I was seven years old, and it has never once occurred to me that there is an end where it has not landed yet and somebody has to be holding it.”

She put her hand flat on the top of that table.

She said, “I have been reading the wrong end of it.”

She said, “And I am going to say what that costs to be able to say, because it is not free and I do not want it in this room for nothing. Nineteen years. Nineteen years of reading the part that had already happened, and calling it the whole of the instrument.”

## The Answer She Gave Herself

And then she answered her own question out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at about the eighth hour and thirty minutes, and she answered it the way she answers everything, which is by saying what she knows first and then saying what she does not know.

She said, “Where do the announcements come from.”

She said, “They come from somewhere.”

She stood there for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “I have had that sentence ready since I was seven years old and I have never once said it out loud, and it took me a day to find out I had it.”

She said, “No maker. That stands and it is not going anywhere. I have got it in my mouth again and it is the sentence I have been standing on, and it is the one I have got a use for.”

She said, “A sentence cannot be put together in the place it arrives in. It would not be an announcement, it would be a thing that happens where you already are. It has to be made in one place and carried to another, because that is the only way a sentence can arrive somewhere you are not.”

She said, “So there is a place. That is not a mystery and I am not going to treat it as one. It is a place, and it is made of stone or it is made of something, and it is not in this room and it is not four miles down a mountain, and I have never once been in it.”

## And Then What She Is Going To Do

She said, “And here is what I am going to do about it and I am going to say it out loud before I have worked out whether it is any good.”

She said, “I am not going to ask a question in a room where the thing does not come. I have done that three times this morning and I am not doing it a fourth time. If it has to be asked, it has to be asked somewhere it can arrive.”

She said, “And I cannot get there by waiting, because there is nothing coming, and I cannot get there by writing, because I have four blank leaves against that rib and I have said out loud that I am not going to use them, and a refusal that went down that stair a week ago has not been answered and I am not going to send a second one to be told no twice.”

She said, “So it is a place, and I have to go and stand in it, and the only way I have ever got anywhere in nineteen years is on foot.”

She said, “And there are twenty-one days to the first of this month. I am saying that out loud because that is the morning this room does the one thing it does, forty boards and a hand, and I have been standing under it for nineteen years and I am not going to pretend that it does not matter that I am not.”

## And One Thing She Nearly Did

And then she stopped, and looked at the doorway at the near side of that room, and said one more thing out loud that she had not meant to say.

She said, “I was going to ask him.”

She said, “He came up with that lamp at the seventh hour and I have been in this room two hundred days with a boy of sixteen and I nearly asked him where sentences come from, and I caught myself doing it about four seconds before the words arrived, standing here, and I want it said that I caught it.”

She said, “And I am not going to ask him. He is sixteen and he carries oil and there is not one thing he has ever been in a position to know about, and I would have been asking him because there was nobody else in the room and not because I thought he had an answer.”

She left the lamp where it was. The light went a hand’s width along the near end of that table and stopped.

There were three pieces of wood lying face up about a foot in from the near end of it and a fourth about a foot further along, and a bar against the wall on the left that had been there four hundred and thirty-nine days, and a bundle under the far end with two turns of cord in it and not a knot.

And a woman standing four feet off the near end of it who had been reading the wrong end of a sentence for nineteen years, and who was going to stand in front of that wall on the thirteenth of this month, if she was still in that room on the thirteenth, and look at every part of it she has not looked at.
