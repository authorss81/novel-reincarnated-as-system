# Tomorrow Comes Anyway

One hundred and one days since the ninth stage. Five hundred and thirty-two days in that room. The cage out two hundred and ninety-nine days. Right arm open five hundred and thirty-nine days and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and seventy-four of the days gone at this morning and not one coming back.

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing above a room that was not a room, and there was a woman sitting beside it who was getting slower, and there was a stair going up out of the ninth landing that was the same all the way, and that was worse than different.

## The Same All the Way

She went up at about the seventh hour of the thirtieth of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and four, and she left the lamp burning on the third landing, and she went in the dark with her left hand on the stone, and it took her longer than it had taken her three days before, and she said so out loud, because a thing not said is a thing that is going to cost.

She said, “Figures first and the reason after. One hundred and one days since the ninth. Five hundred and thirty-two in that room. Two hundred and ninety-nine since a lamp in a cage went out. Five hundred and thirty-nine with this arm open and it is not going to close. One thousand two hundred and seventy-four of the days gone and not one coming back.”

She said, “And here is the reason. I am going on up, and I am slower than I was, and the landings are the same, and that is the whole of what is against me today.”

She went up two landings in the dark, and the cold came up out of the stone into her arm, and her breath went seven counts on the rise, and she stopped on the fifth landing with her hand flat on the wall and waited until her breath went four counts again.

She said, out loud, standing in the dark, in the ordinary voice, “Fifth.”

She said, “And that is a landing and not a stage, and there is nothing cut in my ribs about it, and I am saying it because a rule used has to be said where it is used.”

She went on up, and the sixth was the same as the fifth, and the seventh was the same as the sixth, and on the seventh she sat down with her back against the wall and stayed there for about a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “It is sorrow, and I am not going to argue with it, because I know what arguing with it costs and it costs an hour and a lamp.”

She said, “It is not the cold and it is not the slowness. It is that every landing is the same landing, and I have been climbing sameness for eight days, and sameness is worse than height, because height ends and sameness does not.”

She said, “And what I want is one landing that is not like the last one. I know I am not going to get it. I want it anyway, and there is nothing else under the wanting.”

## What Tomorrow Is

She sat on the seventh landing in the dark for a long time, with her right arm open across her knees, and then she said the next part out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, because it had to be said standing and there was no getting past that.

She said, “Tomorrow is the first of the month.”

She stood there in the dark with that said, and the stair went up above her and the stair went down below her, and neither of them had changed because she had said it.

She said, “And I want the whole of it said, because a thing said halfway is a thing you have made up. Tomorrow at about the second hour forty boards go on that table at the bottom of this stair. A cloth goes round them and a cord goes round the cloth twice. And then the far end of that table goes along the boards, the way it has gone along four hundred years of boards.”

She said, “And Han Liang carries them up nine hundred and four steps with his own hands and puts them down without looking at anything, and he has done it four hundred years, and he was paid out of a forearm, and he said no to it once, and nobody heard it.”

She said, “And I am not going down for it.”

She stood on the seventh landing in the dark with her hand flat on the wall, and she said it again, because once was a fact and twice was a decision, and she wanted the decision on the page of the stair and not only in her head.

She said, “I am nine landings above that table, counting the ones I have climbed and the ones I have not, and forty boards are going on it tomorrow morning, and I am not going to be standing under them, and I have known that since the second hour of the first, and I have not gone back on it.”

She said, “And that is the second first I have missed on purpose. The first one I missed from the ninth landing with my ear against a wall. This one I am missing from higher up, going higher, and I am not putting my ear against anything, because there is nothing to hear from up here and I know it.”

## The Price of Not Going

She went down in the dark with her left hand on the stone, and it took her longer than the way up had, and she sat beside the lamp, and the light went six paces out from where it stood and stopped, and she put her hands where the light was and looked at them.

She said, out loud, sitting, in the ordinary voice, “Here is the price, and I am saying it before it is paid, because a price said afterwards is a thing you have made up.”

She said, “Forty boards go on that table tomorrow and a hand goes along them and I will not hear any of it. Not the boards and not the cloth and not the hand. That room will run without me for the second month together, and nobody in it will hear one board go down, and that includes Han Liang, because he puts them down without looking and goes back out.”

She said, “And here is the part that is actually on me. I chose the stair over the table. I chose it on the thirteenth and I chose it on the twenty-second and I am choosing it again tonight, and every choosing is colder than the last one, because the table goes on whether I choose it or not, and the stair only goes on while I am climbing it.”

She said, “And I am not sorry. And that is not hardness, and I have worked out that it is not the same, and it is worse, because hardness would mean I did not feel it, and I feel all of it, and I am staying up here anyway.”

She said, “Lamp on the third landing. Burning. Mine. Left on purpose. A first coming in the morning that I am not going down for. That is the record and there is nothing else in it.”

There was a lamp burning on the stone of the third landing, and a woman sitting beside it with her back against a wall and her right arm open across her knees, and a stair going up out of the ninth landing that was the same all the way, and a morning coming at the bottom of it that she had named and was not going to meet.

## The Hand Out for a Wall

She went up one more landing in the dark after that, to be sure the decision would hold where the stair was steepest, and on the turning she put her hand out for a wall on the right without deciding anything, and there was nothing there, and she took her hand back.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, “There it is again.”

She said, “I have done that on every turning since the first night I went up past the light. My hand goes out for a wall on the right and there is nothing there, and I take it back, and I go on. Seven days of it and I have said it twice.”

She said, “And here is what it is, said plain. I have walked along a wall for nineteen years with my left hand on it and never needed the other side, and now I am on a stair with one side missing, and my hand keeps checking the missing side, as though a wall I never had could have gone somewhere.”

She stood on the eighth landing in the dark with nothing in either hand, and she put her right arm open in front of her the way she had carried it for five hundred and thirty-nine days, and she laughed once, short, in the ordinary voice, because there was no person to hear it and soften it.

She said, “That is the whole of what is in the way and it has always been the whole of it. One hand on a lamp I have left behind me. One hand on a wall that is on the wrong side for the next four steps. An arm that has not closed since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four. And a hand that keeps checking for something that was never there.”

She said, “And I am going on with all of it. Not because it is enough. Because it is what I have, and what I have is what goes up.”

And she had said it out loud, standing, and the saying could not be unsaid, and that was the day, and the building went on above her whether she climbed it or not.
