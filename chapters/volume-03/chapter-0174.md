# One, Two, Three

The second hour on the seventeenth of the month had a queue in it that went out into the square, which it did not usually have at the second hour, which was the first thing she understood on the step.

The county was reading the winter return, and the winter return was read in public, and anybody might stand in the room.

She came in at about the second hour with her filing copy under her right arm and her left hand inside her coat and she was the fourteenth person in the queue, and the queue went eleven people back to the door and then six more outside it, and every one of them was there because there was a queue.

## The Room

It is a low room with a counter down one side and a table at the back and the district's books in a press under the counter, and eleven windows in it that let in about as much light as eleven windows let in in the second month of the cold.

Sun He was at the table with the return open in front of him and the clerk beside him, and the return was a long sheet folded in three, and the crease of it was soft, and it had been folded and unfolded twice a year for nineteen years.

“Member Ning Yan,” said Sun He. “Stand where you can be heard and where you can see the sheet.”

She took the second place at the side of the table, where a witness stands, and a man from the queue was put in the first, and there was no ceremony in it at all. It was an ordinary arrangement.

He turned the sheet round.

## One, Two, Three

“One,” said Sun He.

He read the way he always read, at the speed of a man who has read the same column eleven times, out loud, in a room, because a sheet is copied at the quarter and at any application and a sheet that has been read is a sheet that has been read.

“One. Two. Three.”

He went over two ruled lines.

It took about a second and a half and it was the loudest thing that happened in that room that morning, and it was not a sound. It was a man not saying two words and the not-saying of them being in the middle of a column with a queue behind it.

“Five. Six. Seven.”

He went over two more ruled lines. The same width. He did not hurry them and he did not slow them and he did not look up.

“Nine.”

The nine was the announcement. It was on the sheet because a county has to enter a stage that was announced, and he had entered it on the fourteenth with the date and the place and the fact that the member had made it, and there it was in the ordinary hand in the last line of the column, at the top of a column that had three holes in it.

## The Rulings

He wrote in the margin of each of the three gaps, in the ordinary hand, the county's formula, which is short and has been on every form in that office since before he came to it.

*No stage entered for this county at this date.*

Three times, down the margin, beside three gaps, in the same four words.

Then, at the foot of the column, where a registrar puts the thing that governs the column, he wrote: *Member present.*

And then he said it.

“The county’s ruling is that an absence is not a breach.”

That was the whole of it. He had said it three times in nineteen months — twice with a man in this room who had said the opposite thing at a public counter in the summer — and both sentences were in this county's book and no clerk could write down both of them being right.

He said it, and he stopped, and he did not look at the back of the room, and he did not add anything to it.

## The Hand

“Then the member signs the column,” said Sun He, “and the clerk will give her the hour.”

She had done this twice a week since the middle of the summer. Twice a week for five months, and the figure took eleven minutes where it used to take four, and she wrote it with the flat of the second finger, every figure finished by a drag out to the right, no wrist turn anywhere in it, and it was legible and it was hers and it was slower than anything else in the building.

There were twenty people in the room and eleven of them were in the queue behind her and the queue did not shuffle.

She put her left hand flat on the table, and the heel of it was not there, and every person in that room could see the shape of what was not there from where they stood, and one person in particular could.

The first figure took about fifty seconds. She had never timed one and she timed it then.

The second took less. The fourth took less than the first by a little. By the seventh her hand had found the drag and stopped needing the decision about it, and the ninth went onto the line in about twenty seconds, and the whole of it came to just under eleven minutes, and the room did not say one word during any part of it, and the clerk did not look at his watch once, and Sun He read something else.

At the end she put the pen down and pushed the sheet two inches toward him, which she could not have done at the speed she had done the signing and did anyway.

## What Was In The Room

“You will now stand there and be a subject,” said the man at the back.

He had come to the table when the signing was done and he had not interrupted any of it, and he had a sheet of his own out and folded, and he had been standing at a distance the entire time with his hands loose in front of him.

He put the sheet on the table and turned it round.

It was one page. It was headed by an office that this county does not name in any book the county keeps, and it was signed by that office and not by a person, and it carried no agent's name on it, and she had seen paper exactly like this before, in a room like this one, in the middle of the winter.

Bai Zhu read it out. He read it the way Sun He read a column — at the speed of a man reading a document to the room it will be entered in — and he did not editorialize one word of it.

What he certified, in a Warden's hand, in front of a registrar and a clerk and two members of the public standing in a queue:

That the thing in her head was real.

That it was unauthorised.

That it was carried by a licensee in good standing, whose licence, ceiling, conditions and defect stood, and whose seventh condition travelled with her and was not answered by this instrument.

Three sentences. He read them twice, because a certification is read twice, and then he put the pen down and looked at Sun He and said the part that was not on the sheet.

“I am referring the dismantling upward,” he said. “To the Warden. To this office.”

Sun He did not move.

“I do not hold the instrument that would let me do it here,” said Bai Zhu, “and I am not going to do it inside this bound without it. A field man of a Warden's office cannot unmake a System inside the bound without a Wall writ, and there is no such writ in this county, and I have had one certified copy and a receipt off this counter in eleven weeks and bought nothing else.”

Nobody said anything.

“So I am writing a line,” he said, “and the line goes up, and it will be answered or it will not be answered, and either way it will be answered above this county and not in it. That is the whole of what I am able to do in this room and I have done it.”

## The Referral

He wrote it at the table, in the room, with the queue behind it, and it took him about four minutes.

It was a sheet and a number and a date and the word *refer*, and it said that a System was carried in this county by a named licensee under a licence countersigned in this county, that it was unauthorised, that the field agent of the district could not execute a dismantling without an instrument not held in this county, and that the matter was therefore referred upward to the office of the Warden for such instrument as that office might issue.

It said her name. It said her stages, in order, which were the ones in the column he had just read in a low room to twenty people: one, two, three, five, six, seven, nine.

It said her hours.

It carried no agent's name. That was in the sheet and it was in the book and she had learned nine months earlier, in a worse hour than this one, that a document which carries no name is the shape that everything dangerous takes, because a name can be refused and an office cannot.

He signed the covering line and the signature was the office's and not his.

## The Request On The Shelf

“There is one more thing,” said Bai Zhu, “and it is on that shelf by the door, and you have read it.”

She did not say anything.

“It was written eleven weeks ago with a number and a date, and it asked for an instrument not held in this county, for use in this county, after the reading of the winter return.” He put his hand flat on the table. “The reading has been done. It goes up on the next crate, and it goes up with this, and I have told you that it goes and not that it does.”

He picked up the sheet and folded it once and gave it to the clerk.

## The Subject

The clerk entered it in a press and the press shut, and there was a sound in the room that a clerk's press makes and not one other sound in it.

Sun He put his hand flat on the returned sheet for a moment and did not say anything at all.

A woman was standing in the second place at the side of his table with her left hand flat on the wood and a column signed on it in eleven minutes, and the thing that had just been referred upward out of the county had a subject, and the subject was in the room.

It was in the room because of a piece of paper she had opened in a county office in the middle of the winter, in front of two witnesses, with the true terms read out to her, and had refused aloud, and had signed in three lines with the flat of a finger.

Every term on the offer had been real. It would have worked. It would have paid a creditor she had never met in full on a named day, and left her licence and her stages standing, and taken the thing out of her head on a date inside the winter, and it would have been over.

She had said no in a room with two witnesses and had put her name on the paper, and here was the result of that, going up a stair at the second hour of a morning in a county that does not name the office it goes to.

She did not wish she had said yes. She checked, standing there, in about the time it takes to draw a breath, and there was nothing in her about it, and she had been careful about that for three months and she was careful about it now.

## The Step of The County Office

She went out into the cold with the filing copy in her right hand and the ninth in her column and the referral in a press under a counter, and there were eleven people in the queue behind her who had heard all of it.

The shelf by the door had ten things on it.

Sun He had not looked at her once in four minutes of it and had stamped the attendance and had ruled the gaps and had written four words at the foot of a column, and he had not said anything else, because he had said it three times in nineteen months and there was no fourth sentence in this county that would be better than the first three.

She went down the step, and it was the seventeenth of the month, and there was one day left in which a piece of paper could still be filed in this county that would go past everything she had just finished.
