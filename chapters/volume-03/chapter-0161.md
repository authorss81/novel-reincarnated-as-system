# Where He Is Right

It was a colder morning than the first one.

That was the whole of the difference and it was enough. There was ice in the queue on the step, about fourteen people inside waiting, and the fire in the corner had gone out at about the seventh hour and nobody rebuilt it.

She came in from the middle of the cold at the third hour, with her left hand under the lining and four loads a day in her for the first time in a month.

Second chair. Same side. He was in the same place at the same distance, which meant he had come in at the second hour and stood and waited, which was not a thing anybody has to do.

Sun He brought the member's sheet across and turned it round.

## In Order

He read it out at the same ordinary volume he had read it at a month ago, to a queue that wanted to be served.

“One. Two. Three.”

Then the width of two ruled lines.

“Five. Six. Seven.”

Then the width of two ruled lines again.

It was the same width. She had known it would be and she had still found it hard to stand there and be read.

## The Ruling, a Second Time

“The registrar's ruling of this year is unchanged,” said Sun He. “An absence is not a breach. An empty line in a stage column is an absence, and an absence is not a broken term, and no inference is entered against this member.”

He put the sheet down and squared it.

“That is the ruling. It is not made today and it is not made for the first time.”

He had said it in the summer in front of eleven people. He had said it in the autumn at a counter in front of a queue and a clerk. He had said it a month ago in the same room and the same voice.

And he had said it now, for the third time, with a man at the back of the room who had been present for two of the three.

She understood what he was doing about four seconds before she understood why he was doing it.

He was not defending her. That had stopped being what it was about a long time ago.

He was entering the ruling.

A county writes down what is said in it. That had been the sentence she had used at the counter in the summer when she had chosen this room for a refusal, and she had used it as a weapon and it had been a weapon, and a weapon does not stop being a weapon when it is put back in the sheath and set down where other people can see it.

Sun He had been reading an absence out loud, in front of a man, three times, in a room with a clerk in it, on the record.

And in the summer, at a counter, in daylight, that same man had said out loud that a paper written about stages and not carrying the stage is a paper that has failed one of its own terms.

Both of those things were in this county's book.

Neither of them had been there together before this morning.

## What He Wrote

She signed at the third hour and nine minutes. The clerk ruled the line and turned the sheet so the queue could see it was signed, and she filed her copy at the counter, and the man was at the back of the room with his book open on his thumb.

He did not write while the column was being read. That was new, and she noticed it, and it turned out to matter.

He wrote after. He wrote for about a minute and a half after Sun He had finished and the room had gone back to being a room with a queue in it, and he wrote the same two things twice, in two places, and she could see the shape of his hand moving and could not see a word of it from where she was at the counter with her copy in her right hand.

She stood at the counter for about eleven seconds longer than she needed to, watching a man write.

He had taken about ninety seconds at the counter in June. She had watched him do it then too.

## The Step

She did not go past him. She went out of the door.

He was on the step, in the same place, with the book shut and a glove on and no glove on, and he looked at her for about two seconds and then he said:

“I am not going to ask you about the second one.”

“No,” said Ning Yan.

“I am going to say the true thing about it once and then I am not going to say it again, and I would rather you had it now than work it out at the third hour of a morning in six weeks.”

He turned the book over.

“I said in June that the next stage has to go into that column. I have been in this room twice since. Both times the column has been read and both times there has been a space at the end of it.”

He shut the book.

“A registrar in this county has ruled three times that a space is not a breach. And a space is what I said it was. And the registrar and I are both right, in the same room, on the same morning.”

He put the book under his arm.

“That is all. I am not asking. I am not going to ask. If you ever decide to say it to me, I will write it down and you will be the only person in nine counties who has ever said it to me, and I will not have asked you for it.”

“Why are you telling me now.”

“Because it is going to come up at a reading,” he said, “and a reading clerk reads a column at the second hour in front of a room and does not know what is in the spaces, and a registrar rules the spaces, and I will be in that room, and if you have not thought about it before that day then you will be thinking about it at the third hour of a morning.”

She stood on a step in the ice with a copy of her own sheet in her hand.

“No,” said Ning Yan.

“I know,” he said. “You have been not saying no to me at a counter in this room since the thaw without ever once saying it, and I have not asked you for anything, and that is not going to change, and I want you to hear the whole of that in one breath so that you do not have to spend a morning on it.”

## The Cold, and the Arithmetic of It

She went out to the quarry and put in eight loads and lost the rest of the day, and that was the second morning in two months, and about four hundred coppers since the refusal, and none of it was the point.

The point was standing on a step in the ice and being told, courteously, by a man who had bought one thing off a county and nothing since, that he had made his case seven months ago at a public counter and that the case had just been entered by a registrar who did not know there was a case.

That was all he had done. He had not moved a piece. He had not asked one question of one person in seven months. He had not threatened, had not lied, had not touched her, had not entered her rooms, had not paid a man, had not put a word in a keeper's ear, and had not once done anything that a person could call wrong.

He had stood at a counter in daylight and said two true things to a room.

And a room writes down what it says.

## What Is in the Two Books

She worked it out that evening on the mat in the loft, which was a thing she had started doing and had not decided whether she was going to keep doing.

In the county's book there was a column with seven entries and two spaces, and three rulings that an absence is not a breach, all in Sun He's hand, all in front of a clerk, all dated.

In a book on a shelf in an office above this county there was a certified copy of a schedule with the same column on it, and a receipt for one coin, and a lawful answer with five sheets in it, and a return that a keeper in a tower had copied in a fair hand on a quarter's bundle with a fresh line at the top of it.

Both books said the same thing. Both books said: the column stops at seven.

One of them said *she did not do it* and one of them said *there is nothing there to do*, and both of those were true, and about four hundred people in nine counties who had heard her body get to six and get to seven had never heard either sentence.

She had lived for a year inside the gap between those two sentences and had never had to.

She lay in the cold and looked at the roof and understood that from that hour she would be in the gap once a month, at the third hour, in a chair, in a room, while a registrar said the first sentence out loud and a man at the back of the room wrote down the second one.

And she understood the thing about it that she had not understood at the counter in the summer, which was that she had been furious with Sun He for being right and had never once stopped to consider what being right had been for.

It had been worth a coin. That was all it had ever cost him.

## The Second Thing

“You are not going to tell me it was your fault,” said Yuan.

“No.”

“You have been holding it since the step.”

“Yes.”

“It is the fourth time this year that a thing has happened in this county because a piece of paper said what it said,” said Yuan. “The relicensing. The audit. The refusal. And now this. And every single one of them was you, and every single one of them was you doing the correct thing, and I am not going to be the voice that says it back to you, and you have not asked me and I am not going to.”

She lay on the mat.

“I know,” said Ning Yan. “I have been waiting about four hours for you to say it and you have not and I am going to sleep now.”

“Sleep,” said Yuan. “There is a reading in about three weeks and it is at the second hour and it is going to be read in order.”
