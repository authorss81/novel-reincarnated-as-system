# Nine

The ninth hour on the Qinghe floor is the middle of a shift and the only hour in the day when the whole of a crew is on the floor at once.

That had been decided by nobody. It had been decided by the furnace and the working of it, and it was the hour when the setting came out of the mark and the grate was loaded and the carry went, and nine men in a crew stood in the whole of the light at the near end of the floor and none of them had anywhere to be for about eleven minutes.

She used it for that. She had known she would use it for that since the fourth week of the cold end.

## The Morning

Eight men at the near end and herself at the mark, forty feet off, with the ninth standing up in her like a thing in a boot and the cold coming in off the open side of the floor.

Qin was at the grate. Gao was at the grate. She did not look at either of them for the first hour and a half and she worked the whole of it at the mark with her hand on the cold stone, and the thing inside her went from loud to enormous between the second hour and the eighth.

“It is going to be the loudest thing that has ever happened in that building,” said Yuan.

“I know.”

“You have three minutes of it left and then the setting comes out and there will be nine men on the floor for about eleven minutes.”

“Yes.”

“You could hold it four more days and let the column take a fourth hole.”

“No.”

“No,” he said. “You could not. And I am not going to say it a second time, because you said the same thing to yourself four days ago and you were right then as well.”

She put both hands flat on the stone at the mark.

“Say it when I say it and not before.”

“All right.”

## The Ninth Hour

The setting came out of the mark and the grate was loaded and the carry went, and nine men stood in the whole of the light at the near end of a furnace floor forty feet from her.

“Let it go,” said Ning Yan.

[Assembly Hall of Qinghe. Ning Yan of the Qinghe furnace crew, servant-born, under a ceiling written in her own hand. What is carried past the seventh is let go. Condensing, stage nine, at Lowwater is held.]

It went out of the bound in every voice in nine counties at the same moment.

There was a lamp burning in a tower four miles down the hill with four hundred and eleven volumes in it and a keeper at a bench under it with a pen. There was a woman in a wash house with a leg set crooked and a cloth of four coppers against her chest.

There was a hall with no roof and a board on the wall of it and nine men who had read a word on the front of their own schedule that said *irregular*. There was a county office on a square with a registrar in it who does not hurry, and a shelf by the door with eleven things pinned to it.

There was a man at a board behind a well with eight frames on the wall and four loads a week coming down a hill he did not know where from. There was a man in a room over a tanner’s door who was not from the Bureau, writing in a small book, at the ninth hour, with a receipt.

Nine counties.

Four seconds.

She felt it the way she had felt the sixth and the seventh and the fourth, and the difference from all three of them was that there was nothing to hold afterwards.

That was the whole of it. Every stage she had ever held had gone out of her and left a space that her life had to live in. This one went out and took the space with it, and she was standing at a mark at the cold end of a furnace floor with her hands flat on the cold stone and she was full, top to the soles of her feet, with no gap anywhere in her anywhere, and she had never in her life been that.

It was over and then it was ordinary again, and the ordinary was at the top of the second realm.

## And Then The Shift

She got through the rest of it the way a person gets through a thing that is not finished.

The setting came out of the mark and she walked it out. A woman at a board behind a well stopped dead in the middle of a sentence to a carrier and then finished it. A girl at a trough in a wash house put a cloth down. Nine men in a market square four miles off looked at nothing in particular, and one of them put his hand on the wall he was standing next to.

That is what an announcement is inside a bound. It is not a sound. It is a thing that arrives in everybody at once, in the voice they already have, and for about four seconds every person inside it is standing exactly where they were.

Then the shift went on, everywhere, all at once, the way a shift goes on.

## The Room

Nine men on a furnace floor hear an announcement the way everybody inside the bound hears one.

It comes out of the air in their own voices, in a room they are standing in, with nobody in the room making it. She had forgotten that. She had spent five weeks at a mark forty feet from nine men working out what it would sound like from inside a room, and the answer was that it would sound like the room.

Nobody moved for about a second and a half.

Then Gao took his bar off the grate and put it down and stood with his hands away from it.

Then Qin said, “Right,” and went and got his coat.

That was the whole of it. That was the entire response of the crew of the Qinghe furnace to the licensed one going to the top of the second realm in nine counties at the ninth hour, and she stood at the mark with her hands on the stone and understood that the room she had been defending for four weeks had just filled up with people and would never be hers again.

“Gao,” she said.

He turned round.

“Go back to work.”

“I am going to,” said Gao. “I stood there because a thing came out of the air in my own voice and I have never had that happen before, and I am sixty-one years old and I have been in this building nineteen winters and that is the first one.”

“All right.”

“You said the thing about the nineteenth,” said Gao. “I am not asking about it now.”

“No.”

“I am going to be asking about it in about a week,” he said, “and you are going to answer me then and not now, and that is the arrangement and I have had a whole year of watching you arrange things so I have not got the order of it wrong.”

He went back to the grate.

“Good,” said Yuan.

“No,” said Ning Yan. “It is not good. It is done. Those are not the same and you have known the difference for longer than I have.”

## What It Cost

She worked it out at the mark with the setting going on behind her and the crew not coming near the far end of the floor for the rest of the shift, which was new, and which was the thing.

The thing was not the nine counties. Nine counties was over before the setting came back out of the mark.

The thing was that she was now, on paper, in the ordinary way, at the top of the second realm, and the column on her licence would read one, two, three, five, six, seven, **nine**, and under those nine lines there would be three holes instead of two, and every office above this county that ever read that column would read a woman who has been standing at the top of it for four months and has twice put a stage out of the paper on purpose.

And in fourteen days a paper was going to go up a stair out of this county with her name and her hours in order on it, and it was not going to be a paper she could hold.

She said it out loud. She had not planned to say it out loud and she said it at the mark, at about the ninth hour, to nine men within forty feet, because the alternative was that nine men worked out what she was standing in the middle of on their own over the next fortnight and came to it wrong.

“There is a thing coming on the nineteenth,” she said.

The floor went quiet in the way a floor goes quiet.

“Not the reading. A thing after the reading. A man will send a paper up past this county about what is in my head, and it will go out of Lowwater, and he has not got the right to stop me before it goes.”

“Gao,” said Qin, from the grate, without turning round.

“He is not lying and he has never lied to me,” said Ning Yan. “I am telling you now instead of letting you find it off a board in a month, because that is what happened with the last one and I said I would not do it twice.”

“How long,” said Gao, “is between now and it going.”

“Fourteen days.”

“Fourteen days,” said Gao, “and you are going to be doing the fifth week of a cold end in them.”

“I am.”

“All right,” said Gao, and picked his bar up off the grate and went back to it.

## The End of the Shift

The shift ended and nine men went off the floor and one of them, at the end, came up the forty feet to the cold end and stood at the edge of it, where a man can stand and look at a floor and not be standing on a floor.

Qin.

He did not say anything about the nine. He stood there for about a second and a half and then he said, “It was you,” which was not a question, and which was not about the ninth hour.

“No,” said Ning Yan. “I did not make it.”

“It came out of the room.”

“It came out of me and the room was where I was standing.”

He nodded once and went off the floor.

She stood at the cold end of an empty furnace floor in the second month of the cold with the mark cold under her hand and eleven minutes of ordinary work left in the day, and nine counties full of a fact about her that could not be got back.

She had put a stage down twice in her life on purpose and had paid for it twice. The first had cost four months of paper and the only document she had ever had. The second had cost the width of a hole in a column that an office above this county had read correctly.

This morning had cost fourteen days and a room, and the fourteen days were going to be spent on the nineteenth whether she liked it or not.

She would do it again in the morning.
