# Walking In

The season's road had been shut for five weeks and the trains were not running and a carrier's yard in Lowwater in the first month of the cold is a place where about forty men are looking for two days' work that is not there.

She went on the sixth day, at about the second hour, and she went in the way she went into everything, which was to the fourth table and not the first, and she asked the man who was standing at it whether a boy of fourteen had come in off the down train in the autumn with a lead line and no escort.

He looked at her for a second and a half before he answered.

“You are the licensed one.”

“Yes.”

“Off the fourth bridge,” said the man. “He came in with a hundred and forty other people over about nine days and I remember him because he came the whole way with a line and not an escort, and I told him at the gate that nobody walks a lead in the cold without saying so at the gate, and he said a man had taken his ear at the side of the road and told him to walk.”

## Four Days a Week

He was not hurt.

That was the first thing and it went through her hard enough that she had to put her hand on the fourth table.

He was in a yard on the far side of Lowwater, four days out of five, and washed cloth on the fifth because cloth got washed whatever the weather was, and he was owed four coppers a day and had been paid three on two of the weeks and three on one of the others, and the yard's book said he was a boy and a boy gets a penny less.

He was fifteen. He had walked about six miles off a down train in the autumn in the cold with a lead line and no escort, and he had slept one night in a barn at the sixth mile and had got in.

“You want to see him,” said the man at the fourth table.

“I want to know that he got in.”

“He got in,” said the man. “Why do you want to know?”

Because a man in good boots had read a description of her aloud on a country road six miles from a market and had put his hand on a boy's ear to do it, and that was in the summer, and the boy had been fourteen then and was fifteen now, and she had sent him up the grade.

That was not a thing she could say to a man at a fourth table.

“Because he is owed a penny a day,” said Ning Yan.

The man looked at her for another second and a half and then said, “Aye,” in the voice of a man who had decided that this was the reason she had given and was going to let her have it, and pointed across the yard at a boy hauling a harness out of a stall with one arm and watching them both.

## What He Gave

He came over and he knew her before she had said anything, and she understood why. She had sent him up a grade on a dry road in the summer with about ten minutes in him and a road four miles long, and she had not looked back, because looking back was the other thing.

“The licensed one,” he said.

“Yes.”

“I thought you would be a bigger one,” said Ruan. “You read small.”

He had not been asked to say anything. He was fifteen and he had a harness under one arm and he had said a thing about a description being read aloud on a road, and there were eleven men within about thirty feet of them and a yard clerk on a box at the gate.

“Then you remember it,” said Ning Yan. “You remember him reading it.”

“I remember all of it,” said Ruan. “He read it off the paper like a list. I did not know what it was for. I thought it was a thing about a bounty and I thought he wanted to know where you slept and I told him the road and the county because he asked me nicely and he had hold of my ear.”

He shifted the harness.

“He was going to come back,” said Ruan. “He said he had eight left. I have been in this yard since the autumn and he has not been back and there is a dead man on the Watch's book at the bottom of the bridge and the Watch came and asked us nine of us whether any of us had seen a man in boots.”

## The Line in the Watch's Book

He said it without any weight on it at all, the way a boy says a thing that has been written down and has nothing to do with him.

Nine witnesses to an entered act, in one line, in a county's record, with a date and a road and a ditch and a boy of fourteen's lead line found on the body.

And the line that said the boy had read a description off a paper on a road was not in there, because there was no line in nine counties that said a boy had read anything, and what was on the Watch's book was that nine men had seen something happen, and the thing they had seen happen was a body.

She had put him on a road and put a description of herself in his ear, and what came out the other side of six miles of cold was a line in a record with his name against it.

“You are in the book,” said Ning Yan.

“I am in the book,” said Ruan.

## What She Did

She did not give him money. She had thought about it on the road for about a mile and she had worked out that money handed to a carrier's boy on a carrier's yard in Lowwater in the first month of the cold is a thing that gets written on the back of something within about a week, and she could not afford a thing that gets written on the back of something, and neither could he.

What she did instead took eleven seconds and she did it because it was the only thing available that cost nothing and put a fact in a room instead of taking one out of it.

She turned round and looked at the clerk on the box at the gate, who was a woman of about thirty with a very good hand and a book open on her knees, and she raised her voice by about as much as a person raises their voice in a yard without shouting.

“Clerk. I am the licensed one at the stone end in Lowwater. Servant-born, off the Qinghe furnace crew, and the description you have is mine.”

Eleven men stopped what they were doing, and the yard went quiet in the way a yard goes quiet.

“A man read a description of me out loud on the road below the fourth bridge in the autumn and took a boy's ear while he did it,” said Ning Yan.

Eleven men had not moved.

“If that description is read again anywhere, in any yard, by any man, that description is about me and not about him. He is a witness and not a subject. The two are written down differently and I want that said out loud where you can all hear it, and I want you to write it in your book.”

She was finished in about nine seconds.

The clerk wrote for about twenty, and did not ask her name, and did not ask for her licence, and wrote it in the yard book with the hour on it, and that was the whole of the price.

## The Price

It took her about four minutes to understand what she had just done, standing at the fourth table with a harness under one arm three feet away.

She had said her crew, her county and the road, out loud, in a yard with eleven men in it, to a clerk with a very good hand.

In the wash house she had said one true sentence about herself and three washerwomen had heard it and it had been in a market inside a week. That had been at the third hour of a morning and it had cost her a county hour.

This had been at the second hour of an afternoon in a carrier's yard and there were forty men in a yard in Lowwater in the first month of the cold looking for work, and a clerk with a good hand had written all of it down, and it was not going in a county book.

It was going in a yard book, which is a smaller thing, and a yard book is copied at every gate in the county by every man who cannot read.

## The Yard Afterwards

She did not go back.

That was decided in about four seconds at the gate, and it was the hardest of the three things she did that day. It was not hard because it was dangerous.

She had got what she came for and more. And a woman who goes back to a carrier's yard twice in one winter is a woman with something to do with that yard, and a yard that knows a thing about her is a yard a man over a tanner's door can walk into on any afternoon of the week.

Ruan was pulling a harness into a stall.

“Ruan.”

He turned round with it.

“You said he asked nicely,” said Ning Yan. “That was the right thing to do and I want you to keep doing it and I am not going to come back here, and that is so that if anybody comes and asks you anything about me you can say a man asked you nicely and you told him a road and a county, and that is all of it and it is the true version.”

“All right,” said Ruan.

He was fifteen. He went back to the harness and did not ask her why, and she went up the lane with her left hand under a strip of lining and her right hand open because it was open, and the two of them had cost her about a hundred and forty coppers and a morning and the ability to ever look a carrier in the face again for nothing.

And at the top of the lane, in the cold, she worked out that she had done it anyway, and that she would do it again, and that the reason she would do it again was not the boy.

It was that a woman who could stand in a yard and say a true thing about herself out loud was the same woman who had said *no* in a county office twenty days earlier in front of two witnesses, and the two of those had cost her about the same, and she could only afford one kind of them, and she had picked.
