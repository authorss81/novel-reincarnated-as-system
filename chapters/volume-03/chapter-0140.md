# Paid in Sequence

The boy was fourteen and he was a carrier's boy on the down train, which meant he had been put on a train at Lowwater with a lead line and told to walk, and he had walked about six miles.

She found out the distance later and it did not help.

## The Road Below the Bridge

It was the ninety-third day and the road was dry for once, the first dry day in eleven, and the light off the cut came down onto the surface and made it look like the floor of a hall.

She was on the off side of the near horse with the quarterstaff in her right hand and eight carts behind her and the bridge a quarter of a mile up the grade. The contractor's big man was a hundred yards ahead with the leader, because he always was, because he said a guard who can see the whole train cannot guard it.

The boy was ahead of her, not on her side, which was wrong, and she noticed it the way she had noticed a load roll for six weeks, before anything had happened, in the half-second before.

He was standing with his back to her and a man was standing in front of him with a sheet of paper in one hand and the boy's ear in the other.

The man was not a carrier. He was a man of about forty in a coat that had cost more than a load of dressed stone, and he had boots on a road where nobody wore boots, and he was reading from the paper.

He read it out.

That was the thing she could not get past afterward, and she went over it for weeks. He was not threatening the boy. He was not shouting. He stood on a country road six miles from a market and held a boy of fourteen by the ear and **read him his own description**, out loud, in the flat voice of a man doing his work, the way a file is read to a man in the water and not to him later.

*A woman of about eighteen, servant-born, a furnace crew of Qinghe, right arm open about one time in three, licensed by the county of Lowwater to practise past registration under Clause Four, ceiling at the ninth stage of Condensing, hours on a Qinghe schedule annexed from a hiring sheet and irregular.*

*Stands at a water channel behind a well at Lowwater at the second hour.*

*Say where she sleeps.*

The boy said nothing. Boys of fourteen say nothing on that road because they have been on it before.

She put the staff down.

She did not remember doing that. The staff was in the mud behind her left boot and her hand was not on it any more and she had about eleven minutes of road in front of her with a hired guard a hundred yards up the grade with his back turned.

## What He Said

He saw her before she had covered ten paces. He did not run and he did not shout and he let go of the boy's ear, and she understood that he had been standing on that road for some time waiting for the licensed one to come down it, and that he had a description of her that was eight weeks old.

“You are on a road,” he said. “You are under a hire and your hire is written down and I can read it to the man at the weigh-station in about a quarter of an hour.”

“Yes,” said Ning Yan.

“You are not going to pretend you cannot count to nine.”

“No.”

“Then you will want to know what I am before you do the other thing,” said the man, “because I would rather not have to write down what your face does.”

He put the paper in his coat.

“I am working an entered act,” he said. “That is the whole of it and it is a description and not a threat. I have been working it since the autumn and I have not touched anybody and I do not intend to touch you.”

He shifted his weight and looked at the mud where the boy had been standing.

“I need a place, a county and a name. I have eight proofs left and they are all further away than this one, and a man who takes the place off a boy on a road gets it in a day.”

The boy had not moved. He had a lead line in his hand and he was looking at the stones.

“Go on past him,” said Ning Yan.

The boy looked up.

“Go on past him and up the grade and do not stop and do not look back and if he comes after you run for the bridge.”

“You are the guard.”

“I am the guard. Go.”

The boy went, on legs, up the grade, fast, and did not stop and did not look back, and she heard him going for about four hundred yards and then not.

## Telling Him First

“You are going to kill him,” said Yuan.

“Yes.”

“I have to tell you what it costs before I spend it, and I have to tell you now, because the channel is at about three weeks and I am about to do something I have done twice before and I am not going to do it a third time in your head without asking.”

She stood in the middle of a country road in the dry light with a man in good boots eleven feet off.

“Say it.”

“It comes out of your foundation and it does not return,” said Yuan. “That is not new and I have told you every time and it will still be true.”

“Go on.”

“The tax takes sequence, not substance. It takes the order and leaves the strength. What it would take from you this time is the pace.”

She felt for it, the way you feel for a thing in a pocket you have not opened in a month.

It was there. Five weeks of a road, with a train on it, and a boy on the ground, and a wheel that needed watching a quarter of a mile before it got there. It was not a technique and it was not written down anywhere and it was not in the Nine-Step; it was a thing she had built out of eight hours a day and a dry quarterstaff and a grey horse that had decided the whole of it was beneath it.

It was the best thing in her body.

“It cannot be relearned,” said Yuan. “I took the left-hand order out of you in the thaw and it was gone whole. This is the same kind of thing. It will go out of you and practice will not bring it back, and it will not bring back the part where you know where a wheel is going before it arrives.”

“How long does it buy you.”

“About ten weeks.”

“Out of three.”

“Out of about three.”

The man in good boots took his hand out of his coat.

She looked at the dry road and the boy's lead line in the mud where he had dropped it and the bridge a quarter of a mile up the grade, and she did the whole sum in about a count and a half and she did it in her head and she did not need anything for it.

Three weeks of a voice in her head and ten weeks of one, and the ten weeks bought a boy of fourteen who would still be walking this road in the spring.

“Say yes or say no,” said Yuan. “I am asking.”

“Yes,” said Ning Yan. “Spend it. I say yes.”

## The Killing

He had a length of chain on his belt and he took it out.

She had a quarterstaff and one arm and no order.

What she had was shoulders.

That was the thing about it, and it was the thing she could not explain afterward to a man with clean hands or to a clerk in a county room.

It was also the only thing that had ever worked for her. Before a thing happens she knows where a person's weight is going to be, because she has spent fourteen months reading nine men at a furnace mouth and forty men at a board in a lane. She could not write the rule down. She had never once been able to write the rule down.

His weight went left. He had been left-handed with the chain since she came round the cart and his left shoulder came down first because a man who has spent his life being paid to talk to people does not fight like a soldier.

She went under the chain.

That was all it took. She went under it with her left forearm up and her head down and she came up inside the arc of the swing, and she put the staff into the place where a man's ribs are when he is standing up and has just made himself wider than he is.

It did not work the way it works in the legs.

She had no order at all. She had known there was going to be no order — she had spent it three seconds before, standing in the road, saying yes — and what she had left was the memory of where the order had been, in her legs, in a way that her hands could not reach.

The staff went in. He folded.

And then he was on her, because a man of forty in good boots who folds does not go down; he goes down on top of the thing he hit, and forty stones of a coat that cost more than a load of dressed stone came down on top of a woman with one arm and a quarterstaff and nothing in the order of her.

They went over together on the dry road and the staff went out from under them both.

She got one hand on his collar.

The cold opened.

It came up the left ribs from the crack and it went down, the way it went on the thaw at the foot of a tower when a channel tore, and it was not a decision and it was not a thing she could use and it went through her body while the man on top of her was getting his arm up.

The channel went over and she held it. That was the only thing she had. She held it shut with the flat of her left forearm across his throat and she held it shut with about four pounds of weight behind it and she was aware, for one whole breath, of every person on the road from the bridge to the weigh-station.

The chain came down on the back of her hand.

The hand opened and the channel came over and she lost about two seconds, which she did not get back, and she came out of them on her back on a dry road with a boy six hundred yards up the grade and a train behind her and a man of forty getting up.

She got up first.

There was no order. There was a staff under her left hand and a man in front of her with his weight going left because it had gone left twice, and she did not know what the first move was and she did not need to, because she knew where he was going and a quarter of a mile of a road is a long time and she had watched a grey horse decide that a whole cart train was beneath it for six weeks.

She hit him on the way past.

The bridge was a quarter of a mile up the grade and she did the whole of it on her knees in the dust and got there before he was down, and she stood on the bridge with a quarterstaff in her left hand and her right arm hanging and watched a man in good boots come up a country road on one knee.

He got to the third cart.

[Assembly Hall of Qinghe. The escort road below the fourth bridge. One of the entered is ended by her hand while she stands in her own hire, and the order of the road is taken out of her in the taking.]

She heard it happen. She was standing on a bridge at the ninth hour with eight carts behind her and the sound of one man falling on a country road about four hundred yards down, and nothing came out of the air, and nothing came into her, and there was no heat and there was no settling and there was no sentence anywhere in nine counties.

She stood on the bridge for about forty seconds waiting for it.

## What It Was

“That is not the same.”

“No,” said Yuan. “That one was settled the moment it was in the act. This one was not entered. That man was working an entered act and he was not himself on one.”

“Then what is it.”

“It is a body,” said Yuan. “A man working an entered act, ended by her hand, on a road the layer watches. It settles. But it settles small, because there is no name against him and no act against him, and the layer settles against the working, not against the man.”

“How many weeks.”

“About ten.”

She stood on a bridge and did the arithmetic and it came out the same both times and she had known it would.

Ten weeks. And the pace gone out of her. And a boy of fourteen somewhere up the grade with a lead line in his hand.

The quarter of a mile back down took her most of a count and a half and she went down it on the left side because the right side was not there.

## Afterwards

The big hired guard found her at the bridge with the staff and the eight carts and a boy who came back down the grade crying in the way a boy of fourteen cries when he has been allowed to stop.

The Watch came before dark. She told it once, in her own words, in the ordinary voice, standing at the bridge with a Watch of nine men, a hired guard and a boy's lead line in her hand, and she did not soften any of it and she did not put a cause in it that was not hers.

She had walked down that road in the thaw with a bundle in a dead man's coat.

The four sheets behind a fifth item had come from her. The county had found them because a boy of seventeen had a finger. A keeper of sixty was off the stair for a quarter because of a column she was written into.

And a man in good boots had put his hand on a boy on this road and read him a description of her out loud. The whole of the arrangement that put him here was a claim of eight hundred jin entered in four counties and travelling on the licence in her coat in oilcloth against her ribs.

“I did not ask him anything,” said Ning Yan. “I was the guard. He was working the act. He put a hand on a boy on a road I am paid to walk. That is all of it and all of it is true.”

“You could have asked him.”

“I said one true thing to a man at a desk in a market at the start of the season and he has used every part of it since,” said Ning Yan. “I am not asking a man anything again.”

She walked the boy down to Lowwater and gave him back to the carriers' shed and told him, in about eleven words, that he should have run at the first gate and not the fourth, and he said he would, and she believed him because boys of fourteen say it and then do it.

Then she went four miles up a mountain with a quarterstaff and came up four flights of stairs on a bad arm, and Feng was in the copying room with a clerk from the county office sitting at his bench with a satchel on his knees.

The clerk stood up when she came in and looked at her arm and then at her face, and then went back to the bench without saying anything, because clerks have been in rooms for nineteen years and they know the two things that are not going to be asked about.

She went up to the loft and lay on the mat with her right arm out at the side and her left hand wrapped round a quarterstaff she did not have the strength to put down.

“They do not know how it works,” said Ning Yan.

“No.”

“They get the act and the death and a woman with one arm, and they write it down because that is what a road is.”

“Yes.”

“And there is nothing in nine counties they can write that says what he was.”

“No,” said Yuan. “There is nothing they can write, and that has not changed since the thaw, and it is not going to change, and I have told you what it is worth and I have told you before I spent it, and you said yes, and you are not going to be able to un-say it.”

She lay on the mat in the dark.

Two bodies.

The first one had been in the ground thirteen weeks at the foot of a tower, and the county had written a body, a road and a ditch, and had not been able to write one line about what the man had been working.

This one was going to be in the ground under a bridge on the Lowwater road with a boy's lead line entered as a thing that was found on the body. The county would write that, and the eight hundred jin would go on standing exactly where it had stood since the autumn.

“Ten weeks,” said Ning Yan.

“About ten weeks.”

“And the pace is gone.”

“Yes.”

“How long does it take to notice that it is gone.”

“You will notice it on the next train,” said Yuan, “when the wheel goes and you do not know about it before it arrives.”

She put the staff down on the boards of the loft and let it lie there.

“Good,” said Ning Yan.
