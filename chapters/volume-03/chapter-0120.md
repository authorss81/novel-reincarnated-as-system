# Where the Work Is

The description came on the fourth day and it came from the oil end, and it came out of a foreman's mouth at the third hour in the voice of a man doing his job.

“The licensed one. Take her to the water, the licensed one sets it down like it's got a family.”

Eleven people heard it. She was one of them.

It was not a name. That was the thing she stood in the lane and worked out for about four minutes, with the mat on her shoulder and the grit in her teeth, while the lane went on around her the way a lane does.

A name costs a man something. You have to have been given it, and you have to keep it, and if you use it wrongly somebody can take it back off you in a room. A description costs nothing. You make one up on the spot out of the thing in front of you and it is true and it is nobody's fault, and it travels, because a description is a thing a person can pass on without deciding to pass anything on at all.

*the licensed one.*

She had been announced to nine counties in the middle of the afternoon in the spring and it had told all of them she was at Qinghe. She had been on a county sheet of her advancement with her name in the wrong order. She had a stage column that read one, two, three, five and everybody in nine counties had heard the last of it out loud in a voice that is not a voice.

And this was better than all of it. This was a lane, and a foreman, and five words that any one of forty people could have said, and by the second hour of the day on the fourth of the first week of summer there was no office in nine counties that could have struck it.

“It is a description,” said Yuan, “and it is going to be in that lane for a year.”

“It is a description.”

“You cannot get it back. There is no office in nine counties that keeps a column for what a foreman called a woman at a water channel, and if there were, I could not read it, and you know both of those things.”

“I know them,” said Ning Yan. “I worked out four days ago that a lane at the third hour is exactly where the standing travels, and I came down the mountain anyway, and I am not going to stand here at a water channel being clever about it after the fact.”

## The Third Hour

The load came down wrong at the third hour on the fifth day.

There is no such thing as a load coming down wrong. A mat of broken stone does not fall, because a mat of broken stone is four corners and a rope and a woman under one shoulder, and if the rope goes it goes because the rope was old, and it was, and there are eleven men at the stone end who will tell you that a rope is old every day of the year.

But the mat came off her shoulder.

It did not slide. It went forward and down, the way a thing goes when the corner of it that was holding finds that the arm under it has decided, at the third hour on the fifth day of the first week of summer, that it is not holding anything at all.

It went forward into the water channel and it broke on the stones at the edge and eleven loads of broken stone came apart in a foot of water in front of forty people and the noise of it was the noise of a hundred small hard things finding a floor at once.

She stood in it with her right arm out to the side and her left hand down in the water to the wrist.

The rope was on the far bank.

Eleven men and one foreman and nine carriers and the man at the water and the man at the table stood in a lane forty paces long and looked at a woman who had just put a whole load into a drain, and about forty of them were waiting, in the ordinary way a crowd waits, to find out what a person does next.

She had about two seconds in which whatever she did would be the thing everybody remembered.

“You have got an arm,” said the man at the water, helpfully, in the tone of a man who is stating a fact to fill a silence.

“I have got one,” said Ning Yan.

She got the rope.

It took her four minutes and it took her both arms twice and once she had to put her knee in the water to get a knot to come loose with a hand that could not feel the knot, and when she had it she pulled the mat out of the ditch and put the stones back on it in the wrong order because there was no other order available.

She got the whole of it to the far bank, and she stood there in a wet channel with eleven people watching.

“Deduct it,” she said to the man at the table.

He looked at her.

“Deduct what.”

“Two coppers for the rope and whatever you want for the time I am standing here not carrying, and put it against my name on the board and do not put it in a column called anything.”

“Deducting it from you is the same as deducting it from the lane,” said the man, “because the lane is paid by loads and there is no loads.”

“Then take it out of my six tomorrow.”

“I will take it out of your six tomorrow,” said the man at the table, “and I will say out loud at the table that I have taken it out of your six tomorrow, so that everybody in this lane hears that I have taken it out of the six of the licensed one and did not take it out of anybody else.”

He said it. He said it at the table in the ordinary voice of a man keeping a board.

Nine hundred names in eleven years, and about forty second-times, and he had just made a hundred and forty-first.

## Eleven Strangers

They worked with her every day and they had not asked her one question.

That was the strange part, and it was the part she turned over on the fifth night on the loft mat with the coat for a blanket, and it was the part she had not been ready for because she had come down the mountain braced for the other thing.

She had braced for a foreman who wanted to know what a licence was. She had braced for a carrier who wanted to know what an eight hundred jin claim was. She had braced for a man in a good coat, or for Hou Sanliang's man come back from the south, or for a broker's counting lane to open up in a street forty paces long.

And what she had got was eleven people who worked stone and oil and water and paper in a town where her name was in a county book four times, and none of them knew it, and not one of them asked.

The foreman at the stone end asked her if her arm was always like that and she said it was not and he said *right* and went back to his board.

Two of the carriers had tried to give her the lighter mat at the second hour and had stopped when she took the heavy one and had not mentioned it again.

The man at the water had asked her where she was from and she had said a place eleven days down the road and he had said he had a cousin there, and that had been the end of it.

It was not kindness. It was that a lane is a lane and a person in it is a person in it, and the single most expensive thing that could ever have happened to her in a working life was to be interesting, and she had spent a year being the most interesting person in nine counties and she had never once been on the receiving end of the cure.

She lay on the mat and put her arm out to the side and said it out loud into the room, because she said things out loud into rooms.

“I have gone five days in a lane and nobody has asked me a single question and I have found that I do not know what to do with it.”

“You have found that it is restful.”

“I have found that it is restful and I do not trust it,” said Ning Yan, “because a lane is the only place in this bound where nobody has ever wanted anything off me, and that is exactly what a place looks like when it has not found out what I am yet.”

## The Carrier

The carrier who brought the word was nineteen and could not read and copied sheets by counting them, which made him extremely good at the parts of a sheet that are numbers.

He came in at the ninth hour on the fifth day, drinking, and put it on the table where she was paying for water she did not need to buy, because it took four minutes to say anything at all in this lane after the fourth hour.

“The man at the first desk,” he said. “The one who buys nothing.”

“He is still buying nothing.”

“He has stopped asking what a schedule is,” said the carrier.

Ning Yan put the coin down on the wet stone.

The lane went on. Somewhere behind her a mat came off a shoulder and went down a bank, which is an ordinary sound that happens eleven times a day in this lane, and she heard it and did not turn round.

“Say the rest of it,” she said.

“He is asking what hours a licensed woman works,” said the carrier. “He has been at the first desk twice a week since the thaw and he has asked four carriers that and he has asked it like it is a thing about a job and not a thing about a person, and Pei Lu said licensed woman and the man wrote it on the back of his hand and said *that is the word, thank you*, and then he asked her what hours.”

Pei Lu. Not any of the names she had.

“He has never said what he is,” said Ning Yan.

“He says he is not from the Bureau.”

“He is not from the Bureau.”

The carrier looked at her. That was the first thing he had done in five days that was not about water or sheets or a cousin eleven days down the road.

“Why are you looking at me like that,” said Ning Yan, “when you have brought me a thing about a man who buys nothing and does not give his name and is standing at a desk in a market twice a week asking what a licensed woman works.”

“Because you are the licensed one,” said the carrier, “and he wrote the word down.”

## What She Did With It

She went up the tower stair at the ninth hour with twelve coppers and a wet right sleeve and grit in the seam of the coat, and she stopped on the fourth stair, where a cough that had been coming up from the tower door and stopping to look at something had stopped four times since the middle of the spring, and she stood there for the length of three counts and then went on up.

At the top she got the docket out and wrote it in the wash code with the lamp turned away.

*Man at the first desk. Asks carriers what hours a licensed woman works. Wrote the word licensed woman on the back of his hand and said that is the word. Four carriers in six weeks. He still buys nothing and he still gives his name to nobody and he is still not from the Bureau, which is true.*

*He was asking what a schedule was and now he is asking what a person does with a day. He has stopped reading the paperwork and started reading the lanes, and he has got a word off a carrier's mouth in nine counties in six weeks, and I gave him that word on the fourth day by standing in a channel at the third hour where forty people could hear what I am.*

She read it and did not soften it.

*It was worth the six loads. I want it written down that I know what it cost and that I would do the fourth day again.*

The second line was the true one and she looked at it for a while and then left it.

“It is worse than a man reading paper,” said Yuan.

“It is worse than a man reading paper.”

“He was not going to find the lanes. The lanes are four paces wide and there are nine of them and nobody writes a county column for where a person stands at the third hour, and he found them out of a carrier's mouth in six weeks while buying nothing at all.”

“I know.”

“You put it in a log,” said Yuan.

“I put it in a log,” said Ning Yan, “because I am not going to be a woman who is six months into a lane and one morning decides she never saw the thing that was walking up and down it. If I write it down then it is a thing I knew, and if it ever comes to the day when somebody says I did not see it coming, I will have the page.”

She got the second licence out of the lining and squared it on the mat and looked at the scope line in the county's hand, the five words in the middle of it that had taken four months and a dead man's name and a defect in her own hand to buy.

*Servant-born. Granted to draw sect qi, and to take paid work outside the crew, and to be taught past her stage, under countersignature at this county.*

“There,” she said. “That is the whole of it. That is the line a man at a desk in a market has just heard out loud from a carrier, and there is no version of this in which I am legal and invisible at the same time, and I did not know that when I signed it and I would have signed it anyway.”

“You are not going to stop going to the lane.”

“No,” said Ning Yan. “I am not going to stop going to the lane. And I am not going to stand about in it either. I am going to work the hours where the work is, at the hour the work is, and be the licensed one in front of every person in this town who wants to be, because that is what I bought in the spring and I am going to spend it where it can be seen.”

Down the four miles of dark timber, the tower lamp was burning in its cage, and at the bottom of it a lane forty paces long had a board on it with chalk on it, and a woman who worked there had not been in that lane for nine hours, and a carrier's word was already in a market four miles away in the back of a hand that had not been washed.

The hunt had been on the schedule for six weeks.

It was in the lane now, and it had got there for nothing, out of a mouth that had not been asked anything by anybody.
