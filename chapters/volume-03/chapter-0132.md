# Relicensed

The return was on Sun He's table.

She saw it when she came into the room, because a quarter's crate from the Lantern Register at Lowwater goes onto the county's own table at the fourth floor and it stays there until the registrar has it, and day thirty-nine was in the fifth week and the fifth week was when Sun He read a return and did not hurry.

She had carried a bundle of paper down four miles on the thirty-fifth day and she had spent the four days since in a lane behind a well with the crate's contents already in a county building, and she had not known that until she was in the door and looking up at the stair.

The queue ran out of the door and along the step. She was ninth in it with the oilcloth bundle under her arm, and behind her, eight feet back, at the counter on the other side of the room, a man with clean hands was reading a public notice.

He had bought nothing since the twenty-third day of the first week of summer. He had a certified copy of her furnace schedule in a folder somewhere with a receipt folded square against a counter edge, and he had a room over the tanner's under a name with a doubled letter in it, and he had said two true things about her terms in this room to a clerk of four years and had gone home.

She looked at him for about a count and a half.

He did not look at her. He was reading the notice on the wall about the water rate, and he had a sheet of something in his hand that was not the notice, and he read that too, in the ordinary way of a man filling in a form.

She did not go out of the room. She went one place further back in the queue, behind a woman with a basket, and waited.

## In Order

Sun He had the quarter's return open at the table with the furnace schedule squared on top of it and her four lines in his own hand on a docket beside it, and he had read all of it, and he had a clerk at his elbow.

“Number nine,” said Sun He.

She came up and put the oilcloth on the table and unwrapped it flat, and the six of the county's own clerks in that room went on doing what they did, and the queue went on being a queue, and a man at the counter went on with the form he was filling in.

“You are relicensing,” said Sun He.

“I am relicensing.”

“The county reads an application in order and out loud,” said Sun He, “and it reads it to the room, because an application read silently in a corner of this office is an application somebody can come back and say was misread.”

He squared the sheet.

“Then I am going to stand up.”

He did stand up, which she had not expected, and put his thumb on the top of it, and read it.

“A relicensing made under Clause Four of the Servant Advancement Settlement by a servant-born of Qinghe holding a licence to practise past registration, countersigned at this office at the third hour of a day in the middle of the spring.”

“Three legs,” said the clerk at his elbow. “Seal, clearance, countersignature.”

“Sponsors' seal of the maintaining members of the Hall of Qinghe, all three, on one sheet, in the corridor at the west end, on the thirty-fifth day of the quarter.” He moved his thumb down.
“Quota clearance from the office of the furnace building of Qinghe, granted at the fourth hour of the thirty-first, signed in the quota book and not on this sheet, and the applicant does not enter into that office and was not in a room with the man who keeps it until the day of the signing.”

“That is the leg,” said the clerk.

“Ceiling declared in the applicant's own hand.” Sun He read the two of her lines and did not do anything with his face at all, in a room with a queue behind her. “Condensing, stage nine. And a defect declared in the applicant's own hand, and an aperture cracked through, stabilised at the crack, permanent, and a line under it saying that the defect restores nothing, which is new on this sheet and which I am entering as her line and not as mine.”

He turned the page.

“No petition for the Foundation without relicensing. The second relicensing requires a schedule this office does not keep past the boundary.”

“That is on the form.”

“It is on the form and I have read it to her twice at the side counter in the spring,” said Sun He, “and the second one has never existed and is not going to exist while this office does not keep it, and I am entering it because she has put it in her own hand and it is the only sentence on this page that a registrar cannot act on and a member can.”

## The Seven

He read them to the room and the room listened the way it listened to everything, which is to say completely, without appearing to.

“First. At every county hour of this filing, in person, and in her own hand. No surety may stand for the applicant again without relicensing.”

He looked up.

“I will say that one out loud twice, because it was written in the spring against a licence that has now been relicensed, and a line in a book that has been overtaken is read the way a registrar reads it and not the way a woman reads it. It stands. It means the next one. There is no version of this office in which a dead man becomes a living one because a piece of paper changed.”

Nobody in the queue moved.

“Second. The hours are those of the furnace schedule of Qinghe, certified by the Hall's clerk and returned to this office at the quarter. No other schedule counts.” He read the next sentence of it. “The certified annex to that schedule is furnace hours by certification and not by place, and the annex is part of that schedule and is on the same sheet with the same Hall’s mark, and there is no other schedule in this licence.”

That one landed in the room differently. She had not been ready for it and she knew it from the way the clerk at his elbow wrote a word in the margin.

“Third. Practice at or below Condensing nine. Nothing above it is licensed.”

Nine. He said it once and it went out into the queue and along the step and out of the door into the square, and it was the first time in four months that the county had read her number out loud in a public room, and she had put it there herself in her own hand on a table in a corridor.

“Fourth. No petition for the Foundation without relicensing, and the second relicensing requires a schedule this office does not keep past the boundary.”

“Fifth. The defect is entered and is permanent. This licence restores nothing to the aperture.”

“Sixth. The certified annex hours return with the quarter, on the same crate and in the same order as the schedule they are annexed to, and an annex that is not returned is an annex that is not on the schedule.”

He stopped there and squared the paper.

“Seventh. There is entered against this filing the standing claim of eight hundred jin, entered in four counties against the Chosen One, standing and unanswered, which travels with this licence and is not affected by it.”

The room was very quiet.

“You have read that one out in a queue before,” said Ning Yan.

“I read it out in a queue in the spring and it is the sixth condition and the last thing on that paper, and today it is the seventh and the last thing on this one,” said Sun He. “It is in this office's book and a claim in a book cannot be unlisted by a man stamping a different document. I am required to enter it. You may read it.”

She read it. It was the ordinary shape. Eight hundred jin, entered in four counties, proof by body, place and name, standing and unanswered, and the date it was first entered in the autumn.

## What It Gave and What It Cost

He took the stamp and set it down and did not press it.

“Before I do this,” said Sun He, “you will hear me say the thing about the paper, and then I will do it.”

“This is the second relicensing of a licence issued at this office in nineteen years. There has been one before yours and I was not the registrar for it. What that one was is not in this book and is not a question I will answer for a queue.”

The queue waited.

“What has changed is that the labour is on the sheet and it is now on the sheet twice. Nine hundred and forty hours of a tithe and three hundred and eleven of a season and four weeks of an annex in a certified column of its own, continuous from the first entry of the book, and I have had a boy of seventeen run a finger down the whole length of your line and not find anything, because there is nothing in it to find.”
He set the stamp square with the edge of the table. “That is what this licence is worth. It is worth a stamp. It is still not worth a thing you have to be careful about on a road, and the seventh condition is the reason that is true and not a lie.”

He pressed it and lifted it.

*Licence to Practise Past Registration. Clause Four. Relicensed. The Bureau of Records, county office at Lowwater.*

She watched the ink come up off the paper. It was the same bad county ink and the table made the same sound.

“Condensing, stage nine,” she said.

“Condensing, stage nine, in your hand, above mine,” said Sun He. “And the Foundation barred again, and the claim entered again, and eight hundred jin that I did not choose to enter and am required to.”

“Can it be answered.”

“It can be answered by a body, a place and a name, and there are eight of those left and every one of them is further away and dearer than the last, and there has been no change in that since the winter.” He read the seventh condition back once more to the room because that is what conditions on a licence in this office are for. “There is entered against this filing the standing claim of eight hundred jin.”

She signed it in her left hand and the clerk turned the sheet so the queue could see that it was signed, and Sun He wrote the hour in the county book in the ordinary hand of a man doing his duty.

And then, without looking up:

“You may pick up your licence.”

She picked up her licence.

## The Queue

He had said it nineteen years ago to a woman in the rain and he had said it again to the same woman eleven weeks ago and he had not said it this time.

She had noticed. She had been in this room eleven times in a year and she had noticed it at about the fourth minute of a stamp, and there was a queue at the counter and a clerk copying and a man at the counter with a form, and none of it was for her.

She did not go and thank the man at the counter for anything, because he had not done anything, and she did not look at him on the way out, because looking at him would have been the first thing either of them had done in this room that was not entirely ordinary.

She went out into the square in the middle of the afternoon with four feet of oilcloth-backed county paper in her hand with seven conditions on the back of it and a ceiling at nine on the front of it, and the crowd went round her the way it went round the well, and the man at the counter filled in his form.

That was the whole of the first hour of it.

## The First Hour After

She went up the tower stair at the ninth hour and Feng was on the copying floor and she put the licence on his bench with her left hand and he looked at the front of it and then turned it over and counted the conditions with a brush handle and did not say anything.

“Seven,” said Feng.

“Seven.”

“The claim on the back is the seven.”

“The claim on the back is the seven and it is in his hand and he is required to enter it.”

He put it down square with the edge of the bench, which is what he does with everything.

“It is going up the stair again,” he said.

“It is going up the stair again and it is going down the road in the next bundle with the hours on it, and there is an office on the other side of a thing I cannot see that is going to get all of it in order.”

“Everything gets up the stair,” said Feng. “That is what a stair is.”

She went up to the loft and lay on the mat with her arm out to the side and the licence on the boards beside her, and said it out loud into the room, because she said things out loud into rooms, and there was nobody in it.

“I have got what I wanted.”

“You have got it,” said Yuan.

“I have got a ceiling at nine in my own hand on a document that a registrar read out loud in a room with nine people in the queue, and I have got seven conditions on the back of it, and the seventh one is the claim, and there is no version of that document in which the claim is not on it.”

“No.”

“So I have got what I wanted and the thing that wanted me is on the same piece of paper as the getting of it, in a county hand, in order, twice.”

She lay still for a moment.

“There is no triumph in that,” she said. “I have been walking four months toward a number and the number is in my hand and I have not once in the last four minutes wanted to shout. What I have got is a bigger box and the same eight hundred jin, and a bigger box is not a better one, it is just one that holds more of me.”

## The Count

“How much have you got left.”

She said it into the room at the ninth hour, lying on the mat, to nobody, in the ordinary voice.

“About three weeks,” said Yuan.

She lay with it.

“Three weeks.”

“Three weeks at the rate you are using me, and you are using me by your own list, twice a day, and never idled, and that is the only reason it is three weeks and not one and a half. Six weeks at the second bunk in the spring, and you have had it down to three in five and a half weeks of days.”

That was the number. That was the only number in a year that had ever moved the way arithmetic moves.

She did the sum against the calendar on the wall, with the wood of the loft behind her head, and it came out the way it always came out when she did it honestly.

Three weeks of him. And a ceiling at nine that she had not reached the first step of. And six weeks of double schedule standing between one and two, which was the same six weeks, and it did not go past three.

“How much of it is the stone lane.”

“Most of it,” said Yuan. “You talk to me out loud on a stone lane eleven times a day and you did not used to, and the lane is where you found out that your arm goes before you know it, and there is no version of you working a lane that is not a version of you working me.”

“I have never thanked you for that.”

“No,” said Yuan.

“I am not going to,” said Ning Yan. “I told you in the spring that I was not going to ask you to stop and I meant it and I have not asked you, and it stands, and you are going to be dry inside a month whatever either of us does about it.”

She lay on the mat with her arm out to the side and the licence on the boards and the lamp going on the landing because it was always going.

She did not tell him to stop.

And down in the square, four miles from the ladder she had just come off, a man with clean hands filled in the last line of a form at a public counter and gave it back to a clerk, and the clerk put it with the quarter's bundle on the fourth floor of the county office.

It had her name on it, and it had the address of an annex, and it had a ceiling of nine in it, and it had eight hundred jin standing against a Chosen One who was licensed, capped no longer at five, examined inside a schedule a man in Hanhui had certified, and readable in order by anybody in nine counties with a stamp and a reason.

It had cost him one coin and one sheet. She would not find that out until much later, and by then she would not be able to do anything about it, which is the shape of the whole of this.
