# The Cold End

The cold end of the Qinghe floor was at the far end, forty feet past the grate, past the setting-bench, past the place where the crew stood, and it was the coldest working place in the building and the only one in it that a man could be on his own at for a whole shift without it being an arrangement somebody had made.

She was not posted there.

That was the thing she had been carrying about for four weeks without turning it over, and it came out on the fourth week like a stone coming off a bench. She was not the crew's. She was a member of the crew and a licensed practitioner and the holder of a licence with a ceiling in her own hand, and there was no one in that building with the standing to post a woman to the cold end for a season, and nobody had posted her.

She signed for her own shift every morning with the flat of a second finger, and then she walked forty feet past nine men to the cold end and worked there alone.

## Ten Days

It had been ten days since a man of the crew had come to the cold end for any reason at all.

Not ten shifts. Ten days, in which she had worked nineteen shifts at that end with nobody behind her past the setting-bench, and the crew had covered it — the way they covered a thing they did not discuss — by working the near end harder and finishing earlier and getting off the floor before she did.

She had noticed it in the third week and told herself it was the arithmetic of the quarter. Nine men on the near end, one on the cold, the floor was shorter by a man, and a crew that is one man down does the ordinary thing and gets off early.

In the fourth week she stopped telling herself that.

She had spent the autumn and the whole of the winter believing the crew's silence was something they had done for her, and it was, and it had cost them nothing that she could see, and she had built the whole of the last four weeks on the version where the empty end of the floor was hers.

It was not hers. It was empty. Those are different and she had been living in the gap between them since the day the winter schedule went on the board.

## The Fifth Week of Five

The ninth was coming and it was not close.

The eighth had been the strangest thing that had ever happened inside her and it had felt like an address — a specific place with something in it that stayed. This was the opposite of that. This was loud, and it was everywhere, and it was the whole of her from the hair down, and there was no finding it without looking for it, and it had started in the second week of the cold end and had got louder every day since.

She had held it five times since the fourth stage. Three of the five had gone out of her into nine counties at once and two had not, and the two that had not had left a hole in a column the same width as any other hole, and her life since had been the space where each of them used to be.

The sixth was going to be different and she had known that since the winter schedule went on the board, and the reason was not the stage.

It was the floor.

There was nowhere in that building for a thing that size to happen quietly, and she had been spending four weeks finding that out on her own, and the finding was that she could do it at the cold end and nowhere else, because forty feet of empty floor was the whole of what she had, and the crew had taken the other half of the room by never standing in it.

“I can do it here,” she said, “and I cannot do it anywhere else, and the reason I can do it here is that nine men have been kind to me for four weeks.”

“The nine men are not being kind to you.”

“I know,” said Ning Yan.

“I do not know what they are doing.”

“No,” she said. “Neither do I. That is what I have been getting wrong for four weeks. They have not been doing anything. A thing that is not done is not a thing that was done to me.”

## Two Hours at the Grate

Gao came to the cold end twice in the shift on the fourth day of the fourth week, both times to fetch something that could have been fetched at the near end and was fetched at the near end.

Both times he stood for about four seconds first, at the edge of the light, where a man can stand and look at a floor and not be standing on a floor.

She had wanted, both times, to say a name. Not his. Any of them.

The roster at the near end was that any man of the crew could sign for a shift and the floor was checked against a mark, and if she had said one man’s name out loud in front of the others — *put him on the cold end for the last hour, I have the last hour alone again* — then that man would have come, and the other eight would have known she had asked.

And the asking would have been the end of the quiet and the beginning of a thing that one man on a cold end in the second month of the cold could not be at that end alone twice a week anyway.

So she said nothing, and the two hours at the grate were the two hours at the grate.

## What She Was Actually Doing

She worked.

The cold end at the second month of the cold is the end where the air comes in off the mountain road off the open side of the floor and goes straight out again through the open side, and the cold end is cold because it is the end where the work is done once and then has to be done again, and the whole of a shift is standing in moving air while a thing you cannot leave goes through a stage it will do again in nine minutes.

That is what she had been doing for four weeks, every day, alone, and it is the shape the fifth week is made of.

Six weeks of double schedules in the summer had made her sixth. Five weeks of an escort road had made her seventh, and two weeks of fever with it. Five weeks of a quarry sledge had made her eighth, and she had held that one down to a stove in a hut with a bench and a slate on the wall.

The ninth was going to be made of standing still.

## The Thing About Standing Still

Every stage she had ever held had been made by work, and every one of them had been made by a thing she could count.

Six weeks of double schedules. Five weeks of a road. Five weeks of a sledge and a rope.

The ninth was going to be made by four weeks of standing at a mark in moving air, and there was nothing in it to count, and that was the difficulty and it had taken her the whole of the fourth week to understand it.

A body that is being built wants to be able to tell what is going into it. Stone off a face can be counted by the rope and a load off a sledge can be counted by the man on the rope, and a week's work can be written on a card with an hour in at the left and a load in at the right. She had built four stages in her life on work she could add up.

This was not work. She had been standing in the cold at the far end of a floor for nineteen shifts, in the same place, at the same mark, doing the same nine minutes over again and over, and there was no card for it and there would not be one, because a card is a thing that certifies an hour and this was not an hour at all. It was a fifth week of nothing, in the cold, at the end of a building, alone.

That was what the eighth had been too, and she had put that one in a hut on purpose because a hut has a stove and a bench and a door, and this one had no door in it at all and was forty feet from nine men who would hear every sound she made and would not be able to help with any of them.

She stood at the mark at about the fourth hour on the fourth day and let the thing run through her without steering it, which was the only technique she had for it and which she had worked out on the second week and had not told anybody about.

It worked. It worked because there was nowhere in her for it to go but through, and it came up out of her feet and went off the top of her head about nine seconds later, and the ninth hour came, and the shift went, and the fifth week had four days left on it.

## What She Said About It

On the fourth night she said it out loud on the mat in the second bunk, in a room with nine men breathing in it, at the volume a person uses when they are talking to themselves and are not being careful about it.

“I have been keeping a room.”

Yuan did not answer for about four seconds.

“Yes,” he said.

“I have been keeping forty feet of an empty floor for four weeks and I have been defending it and it is not mine and it is not even empty by accident. Eight men walked away from it in the middle of the winter and did it politely and did not discuss it, and I have been standing in the middle of it for four weeks calling that a position.”

“It is a position.”

“It is a distance,” said Ning Yan. “And I have been standing in it.”

“You are at the cold end in about three days.”

“I am.”

“And the floor is forty feet long and there is a ninth in it.”

“There is a ninth in it,” she said, “and it is the loudest thing that has ever been in that building, and it is going to come up in me in the fifth week whether there is anybody in the room or not, and the whole of the difference between the two is that if I do it at the cold end with nine men in the building, then nine men are in the building.”

“That is right,” said Yuan.

“No,” said Ning Yan. “That is the reason I am going to. That is not the same as it being right.”

“Those are different,” he said, “and you have known the difference between them longer than I have.”

She lay in the cold and listened to nine men breathe.

## The Fourth Morning

On the fourth morning she went down the four miles with the hand under the lining and got her hours into the county at the counter, where the clerk pressed the binder without a question, and then she went round to the shelf by the door because it is the only free thing in the building and she had eleven minutes.

There was a new thing on it.

A single sheet, third from the top, with a date on it and a line under it, and the line said that a member was required to be present at the reading of the winter return on a day she knew, at an hour she knew, and that a member whose hours were read was to stand in the room while they were read.

It was dated six days earlier. It had a number. It had not been sent anywhere at all.

She read it twice and then she stood in the county office for about nine minutes looking at a piece of paper that told her she had to go into the one room in this county that she had told a man in the spring she would never stand in.
