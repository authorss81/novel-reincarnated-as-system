# Barred

She took the second licence out of the oilcloth at about the sixth hour of the morning and put it on the bench under the lamp in the wash house, which was the only flat surface in four miles that belonged to her, and she read the back of it for the first time since the summer.

Seven conditions. She did not read them out and she did not read them again. She had read them once and they were the same seven things and they were the reason the document existed.

The claim was the seventh and it was the last thing on the paper.

She put it back in the oilcloth and put the oilcloth against her ribs above the Clay Plate, which was where it had been since the fourth day of the summer, and went down the four miles with it and walked up the four miles to the county office on the square.

She had about a day and a half of light left and she was going to file it today, because the thing she had learned in nineteen months of paper was that a document does not do anything until it is on a counter, and everything on the counter goes up on the crate.

## The Sheet

She wrote it herself, on a sheet of the Hall's paper, at a bench in the Hall with nine men at the near end of the floor behind her.

It was four lines.

*To the Registrar of this county. Application by a member in good standing for a relicensing, with the ceiling at Foundation, the first stage, in the applicant's own hand.*

*Applicant: Ning Yan of the Qinghe furnace crew, servant-born. Current licence countersigned in this county with the ceiling at Condensing nine, relicensing complete, conditions seven.*

*Ground: the applicant's hours record is continuous from the origin date and is held in this county in full.*

*Asked: that the three legs be completed and that the countersignature be given.*

That was all of it. Nine weeks earlier she had written an application for a relicensing with the ceiling at Condensing nine on the same bench with a pen that had been ground down to a stub, and it had taken four months of paper and three men and a registrar to get that one through.

This one took about a quarter of an hour.

She read it twice, and she did not add anything to it that she had not got, and she did not put a single sentence in it about the eight hundred jin or about the seventh condition or about a man's room over a tanner's door.

## The Counter

The young clerk at the counter took it and read it and then read the first line of it again.

“Foundation,” he said.

“Foundation, the first stage.”

“That is above your ceiling.”

“Yes.”

“That is very much above your ceiling,” said the clerk, in the voice of a young man being careful.

“It is one stage above my ceiling,” said Ning Yan. “A ceiling of nine and a ceiling of Foundation one are the same kind of thing. I am asking this office to put a number on the top of a piece of paper that I will then reach.”

The clerk looked at her for a second and then went and got Sun He.

## Sun He

He read it standing up, which he did not usually do, and he read it twice, and the second time he read it with his thumb on the third line.

“Your hours record is continuous from the origin date.”

“It is. It has been since the tithe book opened and you hold it in four volumes.”

“You hold it,” said Sun He. “I copy it. It does not matter. Yes.”

“Then the first leg is a clearance from the quota office.”

“It is,” said Sun He. “And I will write the request for it and send it up, and the quota office will ask me for a schedule, and I will not have one to give them.”

## What She Filed It With

Behind the four lines she folded in the filing copy of her own licence.

That was not a thing she had to do. Nothing in this county requires an applicant to bring the document that grounds the application, and a clerk at a counter would have taken four lines on a sheet of Hall paper without looking at anything else, and a clerk at a counter would have been within her rights.

She had done it on purpose, and she had thought about it for most of the walk up the four miles, and the reason was the seventh condition.

The claim was the seventh and the last thing on the paper. Any office above this county that read her petition would have to find out what she was and what she stood under and what travelled with her, and the answer to all three was on one sheet of paper, and if she filed without it then the only version of her that went up that stair was four lines in her own hand.

She folded it in behind, with the front outward, so that a clerk could see the front of the licence without turning anything over.

That put the claim in an office's hand for the first time in the whole of that claim's life.

She had thought about that too, on the hill, for about a mile and a half, and she had gone on doing it.

Because there were two possible answers to an application like this one. The first was that the office read it, and did nothing, and filed it. The second was that the office read it, and read the thing behind it, and had to answer the claim before it could answer the petition.

She was not owed that and she was not owed anything, and an eight hundred jin standing claim against a person asking for paper is not a lever and she knew that perfectly well.

But she had spent nineteen months learning that a thing nobody has looked at cannot be refused, and a thing that has been looked at can only be refused, and she had no intention of putting a document into an office that could be answered with a stroke.

The folding was the whole of it. It cost one sheet of paper and forty seconds at a bench.

## The Bar

He took a pen and then he put it down and took up the clerk's, which was the difference between a thing entered and a thing ruled, and she had watched him do it twice in a year.

“An application for a relicensing is entered,” he said, writing. “And it is routed. And where it cannot be completed in this county, the reason is entered against it in the ordinary hand, and the application is not returned.”

“Entered,” said Ning Yan. “Not refused.”

“Entered. Routed. Barred in this county.”

He wrote for about a minute and a half, in the ordinary hand, at the speed he wrote everything, and then he turned the sheet round and put it on the counter where she could read it.

It said that the application was received and numbered on that date by the Registrar of this county; that the first leg, a clearance from the quota office, required a schedule of hours worked at the ceiling asked for; that this county holds no schedule of work done past its boundary and is not able to certify one; and that the ground of the bar was the absence of a schedule and not the standing of the applicant, her licence, or her hours.

And then a last line, which was not a formula and which he had written slower.

*The answer to this application is not this office's to write.*

He signed it in the ordinary hand and dated it and stamped it with the filing stamp and then he did something she had not seen him do in nineteen months of watching him, which was to look at a document before he put it in the press.

## It Cannot Be Taken Back

“Once it is entered it cannot be withdrawn by you,” he said.

“I know.”

“If you want it out of the book you would have to ask this office to strike it, and this office does not strike applications, and if I did it I would have to enter that I had done it and why, and you would have two documents in this county instead of one.”

“I am not going to ask you to strike it.”

“No,” said Sun He. “I did not think you were.”

He put the sheet into the press with two other things and the press shut, and there was a sound a clerk's press makes.

“It goes up on the crate,” he said. “It goes up to the district, and the district will read it, and the district will send it on, and every office above this county that reads it will read the date on it.”

“Yes.”

“It is dated the day after a referral went up out of this county,” said Sun He. “Both papers will be in the same crate and they will be read in the same room in the same hour, and anybody who reads one of them will read the other.”

“Yes,” said Ning Yan. “That is the point of it.”

## What It Buys

It buys nothing today. She worked that out at the counter in about nine seconds because there was no other thing to work out.

It does not add a stage. It does not restore the aperture and it does not remove the defect line and it does not shorten the road and it does not put a surety back.

It does not answer the claim on the back of the licence, because a claim is a debt and a relicensing is not a payment, and it does not make the thing in her head lawful, because lawful is the whole of what is being argued about and she is asking for paper, not a verdict.

It buys a date and a number and a bar in the ordinary hand, in a county that has a book.

That is all. A document with a number on it, in the only direction there is, with an office above this county written on it and not described, saying that a woman at the top of the second realm asked this county for the paper that would carry her into the next one, and this county wrote down that it could not do it.

Nine months ago the thing in her head had been the reason she was hunted.

She had spent every month since making it into paper, on the theory that paper is the only thing in this world that survives being wrong about. The licence. The claim. The refusal. The stage column with its honest holes in it.

This was the last application she would be able to make out of this county, and she made it knowing the answer, on the day after a paper went up past every office she had ever stood in front of, and she did not have to be asked twice and she did not hesitate and there was nothing in her about it at all.

## The Counter

The clerk gave her a slip with the number and the date on it, which was the copy, and she put it in her coat.

“Ma’am,” said the clerk, “for what it is worth, I have been at this desk since the thaw and you are the first person who has ever filed anything here that I could not finish.”

“I know,” said Ning Yan. “You could not finish it because it is not yours to finish.”

“No, ma’am,” said the clerk. “But most people who come in here do not know that, and they take it a great deal better from me than they are going to take it from whoever answers it up there.”

She went out into the cold with a slip in her coat and a licence against her ribs and forty-eight hours of nothing in her calendar, and two pieces of paper going out of this county in the same crate in the same hour, in opposite directions, both of them true.
