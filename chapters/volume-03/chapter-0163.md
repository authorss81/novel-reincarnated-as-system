# Not Held in This County

She walked the week's annexed hours down the four miles herself on the hundred and eighty-fourth day, because a member's hours go down in person and it is four miles and there is nothing else to do with them.

She got it on the counter at about the fifth hour and the clerk took the binder and put it in a press with two other things without asking her a single question about it, and she stood in the office with about eleven minutes before the queue moved, and she looked at the shelf.

## The Shelf by the Door

Every county office keeps one.

It is a shelf, and everything pinned to it has gone up out of this county and is not coming back down, and anybody may read it, and it is the only free thing in the building.

Returns going up. Bundles going up. A request for a certified copy that went up in the spring. A correction of an address that went up in the autumn. Two sheets with a district mark on them about a quarter's oil. A carrier's licence application. A slip about a boundary post.

Eleven things on a shelf the width of a door.

And one of them, about a third of the way down, was a request.

It was a single sheet with a number at the top and a date and a line under it, and the line said that an instrument not held in this county was requested from the office above it, for use in this county, after the reading of the winter return.

Nothing else on the sheet.

No name. No complaint. Nothing about a furnace crew, or a stone end, or a voice, or a column with two gaps in it. A number, a date, a line, and a space for a clerk to put an answer in, and the space was empty.

## What a Request Is

She read it four times and she understood all of it and none of it and she stood at the shelf for about nine minutes.

It had not been sent.

That was the first thing and it was the thing she had been afraid of for about a week, and it was not sent. It was written and it was in a press and it was logged and the number had been given to it by a clerk who had not been asked what it was for, and it was sitting on a shelf in a county office in the cold with a date on it and nothing behind it.

It was a shape.

And she understood, standing there, what she was looking at, and it was this: a man in a good coat in a county is not the end of anything. A field man with a room over a tanner's door, a certified copy and a receipt, a lawful answer with five sheets in it, and a Listener in a tower who does not need to be asked — none of that is a thing that can be used.

A thing that can be used is not in this county.

It is not in any county. It is behind an instrument whose name a clerk in this building does not put on a shelf.

A field man cannot sign for that, or borrow it, or do it inside a bound with a writ he does not hold. The only thing he can do about it, lawfully, in eleven weeks, with everything else correct, is write a line on a sheet and let a clerk give it a number.

That was the whole of the eleven weeks.

He had not been hunting her. Hunting is what people do when they are waiting. He had been building a file that a room could use when the room was found, and the room was not found, and the room is not going to be found before the winter reading is done.

He had attended two readings, and bought one thing off a county, and asked a man at a board what a furnace schedule was, and read five sheets in a copying room, and never once asked her one question.

She had spent eleven weeks learning to be afraid of a man who was doing administration.

## What She Did About It

Nothing.

She stood at the shelf for nine minutes in a county office with eleven people in it and did not copy the number and did not ask the clerk about it and did not go and stand eight feet back and ask the man about it.

There were four things she could have done and she had done arithmetic on all four in about nine seconds, standing at a shelf.

She could have asked. That would have been a member objecting to a document in a room with a clerk in it, and it would have been written down twice.

She could have waited for him to come in and stood where she wanted him to see her reading it. That would have been the third thing of that kind she had ever done and it would have told a man with a book that she understood what was on a shelf, which is the one thing she had never once given anybody in eleven weeks.

She could have gone to Sun He. Sun He stamped and copied everything upward to something it never named, and had done for as long as she had been signing anything at all, and she had never once in her life been able to find out what that something was, and asking him would have been asking him to look upward on purpose, in writing.

And she could have gone and looked for the thing herself, which is what she had been doing since she was fourteen months old, and there is no way to look for it, because it is not a document and it is not a place. It is an instrument, and instruments are not kept in a county.

So she looked at the number for eleven more seconds and then she put it back and took her own copy and went out into the cold.

## The Whole of What Is Left

She stood on the step of the county office on the one hundred and eighty-fourth day of the second month of the cold with her own filing copy in her right hand and did the arithmetic, and it came out where it always came out.

About three weeks of what was in her head. Eleven weeks of winter left in it.

A licence in oilcloth against her ribs above a clay plate, with seven conditions and a ceiling of nine in her own hand and an eight hundred jin claim standing on the back of it that had a date against it in a counting room she had never been in.

A hole in a stage column in four counties that read one, two, three, five, six, seven, and then a space.

A reading in about three weeks, at the second hour, in front of a room, with a registrar in it and a man at the back of it and a clerk who turns the sheet round so that the queue can see that it is signed.

A line on a shelf in a room that said an instrument not held in this county was requested from the office above it, after that reading.

And a winter with no paid work in it behind her, and a quarry three miles east that pays twelve coppers a load because of a hand, and about two hundred and thirty coppers in a tin in a wash house, and four miles of hill and a flight of stair that costs three counts.

Eleven weeks against three.

She had been counting that kind of arithmetic since the first week of the year and it had never once come out.

## The Cold, and the Answer to the First Question

“You cannot read it,” said Yuan.

“No.”

“You cannot read the sheet and you cannot read what is behind the sheet and you cannot read the number on the top of it, and you have been standing on a step for four minutes hoping I am going to tell you that there is something about it I can see from here.”

“No,” said Ning Yan. “I was hoping.”

“I cannot read past your skin,” he said. “I have not been able to since the day before the word in a cold room with a folded paper on the mat, and I am not going to be able to until you say the word again, and the word costs four and a half weeks and you have about three, and you will not say it, and I am not going to ask you to.”

“I am not going to say it.”

“No,” said Yuan. “And I am not going to be the reason you cannot. You closed that door yourself in front of two witnesses and it is the only thing either of us has done this year that anybody can point at and be sure about.”

She stood on the step in the cold.

“Do you think he will send it,” said Ning Yan.

“I do not know,” he said, “and I am not going to tell you what I would like.”

She waited.

“What I would like is for a line to sit on a shelf for two more years while you get to nine and come out the far side of it with a licence and a crew and a plate. I have wanted that every day of this winter. It is not going to happen, and the reason it is not going to happen is me, and I would rather you had that on a step in the cold than worked out in the spring.”

She did not say anything for about nine seconds.

“All right,” said Ning Yan.

“All right,” said Yuan.

## The Stone

She went out three miles east and put in six loads before the light came off the cut, at twelve coppers a load, with the left hand under a strip of lining and the right hand open about a third of the time, and Kan wrote the hour and the number on a card in the same line and did not ask about the hand and was never going to.

She had a crew of nine and a hall with no roof and a board with a word on the front of it that she had put there herself, and a keeper on a fourth stair who was warm for the first time in twenty-two years and would report her upward truly for the rest of his life, and a girl in a wash house with a leg set crooked who had told three washerwomen in advance so that she could not be thanked.

She had the first licence crooked over the foot of her own bunk four miles away, going soft at the crease of a defect line she had written herself with four months of paper to think about it, and the second one in oilcloth against her ribs, and she was not going to nail it up, and she had not been going to nail it up for four months.

She did not forgive him.

She had said on a mat in a loft that she would not ask him to stop, once, in the spring, and had not asked, and was not going to, and that was a year old now and it was the same arrangement it had been that day except that he was not going to be able to stop either, because there were three weeks of him left and she had said the word once in a year and was not going to say it again.

She put six loads on the stone and went home up a hill of a thousand feet and stood at the top of the third flight in the dark in a room with ten sleepers, and the count was three, and she let it be three.

Four miles down the same hill, on a shelf by the door of a county office, a sheet with a number at the top of it and a date and a line that said an instrument not held in this county was requested from the office above it, for use in this county, after a reading.

Nothing had been sent. And a woman with about three weeks of a voice left in her, standing at the top of a stair in the second month of the cold, understood for the first time that the paper which could finish her was not in this county, and was not going to be brought to it, and that the only thing between her and it was a line on a shelf in a room where anybody may read.
