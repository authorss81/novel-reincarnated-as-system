# The Man Who Stayed

Qin was twenty-two and had been on the crew two winters and had never once had a line in anything she had ever read about herself, and he came to the cold end on the last morning of the fifth week and stood in it and did not work.

She noticed him at about the second hour, in the light off the setting-bench, at the edge of the forty feet, and she did not stop what she was doing, because the whole of the fifth week was going into what she was doing and she had about a day left of it.

He stood for about nine minutes.

## What Standing There Is

There is no work at the cold end that a man can do without being told what to do. That is not a rule anybody made. It is what a setting-out end is: a licensed person stands at a mark and the mark decides what happens next, and a second pair of hands at a mark is a second pair of hands in the way, and nine men in a crew have learned in nine months not to go to a mark unasked.

He was not there to work. Everybody in that building would have known it from the first ten seconds, because he had his arms down and he was not looking at the mark.

He was looking at her.

Not at her face. At her hands, and at her back, and at the place behind her sternum, which is a thing a man looks at when he is trying to work out whether something is wrong with another person and does not have the words for it and cannot ask.

He stood there for nine minutes and she worked, and after a while she sat down on the setting-bench at the cold end with the hot side of her back to the stone, and he came four steps closer and stopped, and neither of them said anything for about half a minute.

## What She Did Not Ask

There were four things she could have asked him and she worked out all of them in the half-minute.

*Why are you standing there.*

*Go and get Gao.*

*What do you want.*

*Nothing. Go back.*

And under all four of them was the fifth, which was the one that mattered and which she did not ask, because asking it would have been asking a man of the crew to explain himself on a furnace floor with eight others within earshot, and there is no version of a crew in which a man gets to explain himself in front of eight others and keep the shape of his life afterwards.

She had learned that in a hall with no roof.

So she said the only thing that was any use.

“You can stand there,” she said. “You cannot help and you have to keep out of the mark, and if your right shoulder comes past that stone I will put it back.”

“All right,” said Qin.

And he stood there for the rest of the shift.

## The Reason He Would Not Give

He had come to ask her something. She was certain of it and he was not going to say it, and she worked out the shape of it in about the second hour.

She had held four stages in her life and let three of them go and put the fourth down on a stove on purpose, alone, on purpose, with a stove and a bench and a door.

She had never once had a witness to any of it.

It was this: for ten days the crew had kept away from the cold end, and for four weeks before that they had kept away from the reading, and for a month before that they had kept away from the word on the front of their own schedule, and every single time it had been the crew deciding, as a crew, that they were not going to be in the room for whatever came next.

And then a woman had told nine men at the ninth hour on a furnace floor that a piece of paper had said something, and had not asked any of them for anything, and had gone and stood forty feet away alone for a week.

Qin was twenty-two. He had been on the crew two winters. He had watched eight men get good at leaving a room, and he had worked out that the leaving had a shape, and he had come up the forty feet to find out whether the shape had a name before he was the only one of nine left in it.

He was not going to ask her that in front of eight men.

She was not going to give it to him.

So he stood there for a shift, and at the end of the shift he came four steps closer and said, “The fifth week,” and she said, “The fifth week,” and he went back to the near end and put his shoulder under a bar and did not look at her again until the light went.

That was the whole conversation. It was the only conversation she had had with a man of the crew in two months that was not about work.

## What Came Up

It came up at about the fourth hour of that same afternoon and it came up like water coming up under a door.

The eighth had been quiet in her for four months. It had been an address — a place with something in it — and she had carried it the way she had carried everything since the hut at the quarry: as a thing she was holding on purpose, in a room by herself, at the cost of about eleven seconds each time and the whole of the space where a stage used to be.

There was no room in that for what came up on the last day of the fifth week.

It was not an address. It was a **loudness**, and it was everywhere at once, from the top of her head to the soles of her feet, and it was not in her chest and it was not in her head and it was not anywhere she could put her hand on, and she was standing at a mark on a cold furnace floor with eight men within forty feet and no door at all between her and any of them.

She got through the rest of the shift on her feet.

She did the last hour of the setting-out at the mark with her hand on the cold stone and her right arm open the whole of it, and she wrote nothing down, and she did not go and get Gao, and when Qin came past the edge of the light at the end she watched him go to the near end and put his shoulder under a bar and she did not send him away, because there was nothing to send him away from.

## Holding It

She had held it four times in her life.

The first four had been let go, out loud, into nine counties at once, and she had known on the morning after each of them that she was going to do it again, and had worked out about how long, and had worked.

The fifth one sat in her like a stone in a boot.

She took it up the four miles and the third flight and into a room with nine men breathing in it, and she lay in the second bunk with the heel of her left hand under the lining and the right arm open, and it was there all night.

She did not sleep and she did not decide anything until about the sixth hour, when she lay in the dark and worked out the whole of it in the order a person works out a thing they are frightened of.

If she said it, nine counties would know exactly where she stood. The county would have to enter it. The column would get a nine at the top of it and there would be three holes under it, and every office above this county that read a column would read a woman who has been at the top of the second realm for four months and has put two of the stages out of the paper on purpose.

If she did not say it, the column would get a fourth hole, and a registrar would rule an absence an absence, and an office that keeps a list would read an absence as concealment, and it would be right about that, and she would be doing the thing she did in order to be suspected of doing it.

She had been holding that stage down since the middle of the summer. There was nothing left to hold.

## The Thing She Was Actually Choosing

“It is not about the paper,” said Yuan.

“No.”

“It is about the crew.”

“No,” said Ning Yan. “The crew will read it off a board in a fortnight whatever I do, and nine men can count, and they have been able to count for four weeks.”

“Then it is about you.”

“It is about the last door,” she said.

She lay in the dark and said it again, because it was the true version and it was going to be hard to be asked about it later.

“I have held four stages and let three of them go and I put the fourth down on a stove in a hut on purpose. That has been the whole shape of my life since the spring: I find the room, I shut the door, and I hold it. And there is no door in this.”

“There is no door in this,” he said.

“No,” said Ning Yan. “And I am not going to go out to a bank four miles up a mountain and shut one, and I am not going to hold this in a hut with a bench in it, and I am not going to let four days go by and call it a decision.”

## Tomorrow

The ninth hour of the following morning was the ninth hour of a working shift on a furnace floor, and the cold end was forty feet from eight men, and she was going to stand at the mark and say it in the ordinary voice with her hand on the cold stone.

There was one thing to do first, and she wanted it entered properly, and there was exactly one person in this county who entered things properly and reported them upward whether or not they were entered.

Four miles down. The tower. The copying floor. A fair line in fair hand, entered as a thing that happened in the building.

She got up at about the fourth hour and went down the hill in the dark.
