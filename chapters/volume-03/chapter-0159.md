# Before It Comes

She told them on the fourth evening, at the ninth hour, on the floor, a little under three weeks before a clerk's hand was going to put a sheet on a board in front of them.

She had thought about how for nine days and had not got anywhere with it, and the reason she had not got anywhere was that there are about eleven ways to say a thing like that and every one of them is a different woman saying it.

She put down the bar and stood up straight, which cost her something, because the floor at the ninth hour is the one place in nine counties where she had ever been listened to by eleven people at once.

“Twenty-eight days ago I refused a piece of paper,” said Ning Yan.

Nine men stopped, in the way a furnace floor stops, which is not all at once.

“A man from an office above this county laid it in front of me in the county office, in front of two witnesses. It offered to certify a thing away that is in my head, over one winter, painless, and to leave this licence standing exactly as it is, and to pay the eight hundred jin out of a purse that is not this county's.”

Gao said, “Why.”

“Because it is true,” said Ning Yan, “and because it does not touch the four announcements.”

Nobody said anything for about nine seconds, which is about nine years.

“That is the whole of it,” she said. “It was true, all of it, and it does not touch the four announcements. It is in this house in under three weeks, on the winter schedule, under our hours. You are all going to read it. I would rather you heard it from me at the ninth hour than off a board.”

She picked the bar back up.

“That is the notice,” said Ning Yan. “There is no second part and there is nothing anybody has to do about it.”

## The Grate

She worked the rest of the ninth hour and then the two hours past it.

At the second hour past the ninth, Gao came and stood on her other side at the grate, which he had not done in about a year because there are four men on a grate and nine on a crew and the arithmetic of a grate does not have room for a fifth and she had been taking the place of a man who was in the shed with a bad back.

They did not say anything about it for four hours. He loaded and she loaded, and neither of them mentioned that he had come over, and when the shift came off he went to the shed and she went down to the crew quarters and the whole of it was that.

And that was the entire response of nine men to the news that their licensed one had been offered something by an office above the county and had turned it down, and she lay in the second bunk that night and understood for the first time in her life that there are men who will not ask, and that it is not cowardice, and that it is the most expensive thing anybody has ever given her.

## The Mat

It came on the same night, at about the third hour, on a mat in a cold loft in Lowwater, six hours after she had put a bar down on a furnace floor and told nine men a thing. It came the way it had come the last time — without noise, in the middle of a body that was already occupied.

She lay there and let it go all the way down, and it went down and it did not come out.

That was the difference between this one and the one in the summer at the end of a season. That one had gone out of her into nine counties in about nine seconds and there had been nine counties in it and a lamp burning on the fourth landing and a keeper with four hundred and eleven volumes to copy an announcement into by the second hour.

This one she took hold of.

It went down about nine hundred feet and it stopped, and it sat in her, and she could feel exactly how big it was and exactly where it was and exactly what would happen if she let it out.

“Do not say anything,” said Ning Yan.

“I am not going to say anything,” said Yuan.

“You have been not-saying it for about six weeks and I want it recorded that I know you have been counting.”

“I have been counting,” he said. “I have not been counting out loud and I am not going to now.”

## The Arithmetic of It

It took her until the light came off the cut, which was the wrong way round, because she had planned to do it before.

If it went out, it went out at the fourth hour of a morning to every person inside the bound at once, and the whole of nine counties would have it before breakfast, and it would be on the stage column of a licence that is hung on a furnace schedule in four counties by the time the reading came, and it would be true and it could not be taken back and it could not be corrected.

And there was no way to set a date on that.

The last two had gone at the fourth hour of a morning. Both of them. Both times chosen by her, and both times not chosen by anything except a body with nothing to put in it.

She had been at seven for weeks. If this went out now it went out at the third hour of a night, in the second month of the cold, with no notice and no preparation. A furnace crew four miles up a mountain, a girl in a wash house, and a keeper on a fourth stair would all hear it before they had finished being frightened about it.

And it would be nine.

Nine is the top of the second realm, and the number on her licence in her own hand, and the last thing this relicensing will ever carry her to. Above it there is nothing without another relicensing, and another relicensing needs a schedule the county does not keep past the boundary.

She had been carrying that arithmetic since the day it was written. She had never once wanted to arrive at the end of it in the dark at the third hour of a morning.

If it did not go out, then the county would read nothing having happened.

That was the whole of it and it was the whole of it because Sun He had read a column out loud in front of nine people a month ago and then said out loud that an absence is not a breach, and had put the pen down, and had been entirely right.

And in the summer, at a public counter, in daylight, a man had stood eight feet from a queue and said two true sentences about that column, the second of which was that a paper written about stages and not carrying a stage is a paper that has failed one of its own terms.

Both of those things were true.

Both of those things were still true, and they had been true at the same time for seven months, and the county had ruled one of them in front of a room and the other one was standing at the back of that room with a book.

She had never had to live in the gap between those two, and from the next time she sat in that chair she was going to have to live in it for a month at a time.

## What the Sheet Will Look Like

She worked the sheet out on the floor of the loft with a pencil stub against a scrap of the crate paper, because a woman who is about to hand a registrar a piece of paper ought to know what is on it.

Six entries down the right-hand side. One, two, three, then the gap after the three, which had been there since the spring and which a registrar had ruled an absence in front of a queue.

Five, six, seven, and then the gap after the seven, which would be new, and which would be exactly the same width, and which would be there because she was going to be sitting on a mat a thousand feet up inside her own chest holding it there.

Two gaps on one line. One fourteen months old and one about to be three weeks old, and nothing else in that sheet all the way down.

A reading clerk reads down a column. He does not know what the gaps are for. He is not asked. There is a precedent in this county and a book in this county with a rule in it and the rule is that an empty line is an absence and an absence is not a breach, and a reading clerk has four hundred other sheets and a queue outside and no instruction whatsoever to care about the width of a gap.

And a man in a good coat who has read one column in his life, out loud, at a counter, in daylight, to a room.

She put the pencil stub down.

She would be handing Sun He a sheet with two gaps in it. That was the decision. Not a secret and not a lie — a sheet, with two gaps, handed over by her, in person, at the reading, with her name at the top of it in her own hand and her own hour under it.

And in about four weeks he would read it down in front of a room, and he would say the ruling, because he always says the ruling, and he would say it the way he had said it to her in the summer — to the room, and not to her — and the man at the back of the room would write it down and then write for a minute after the room had finished, and then go out into the cold and wait for a month.

## Saying It

“Do not argue with me,” said Ning Yan.

“I am not going to argue with you.”

“I am telling you what I have decided and I am telling you before the day, not after, and that is the whole of what I want from you in this.”

“You are telling me what you have decided and you are telling me before the day,” said Yuan. “That is all I have ever asked for and you have given it to me twice in a year and both times you were right and both times I have had nothing to say afterwards, and this is going to be the same, and you know it is going to be the same, and that is not a reason to stop.”

She lay on the mat.

“Then say the one thing you have been holding since the sixth day,” said Ning Yan, “and then say nothing else for ever about this, because if you say it now I will hear it for the rest of the winter.”

He was quiet for a long time. Not the length of a long time. The length of about six counts.

“You will be eight,” he said, “and there will be no paper in nine counties that says so, and you will be eight inside your own chest where you can feel it, and it will be the second time, and you know exactly what the second one was.”

“Yes,” said Ning Yan.

“That is all.”

“That is all,” she said.

She lay in the cold on a mat above a copying room with nine hundred feet of something in her chest and days of it behind her, and she did not let it out and she did not cry and she did not say anything else.

About an hour later she went down four flights and out three miles east and put in ten loads before the light came off the cut, with all of it still in her, and the block ran until the cut went black.

“That is the last of the night,” said Yuan.

“I know what is in it,” said Ning Yan. “Do not tell me what is in it.”
