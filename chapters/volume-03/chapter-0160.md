# The Second Hole

Five weeks is not a long time. She had done six weeks of double schedules in the summer between the relicensing and the sixth stage and had counted every day of it, and she had done five weeks of an escort road before the seventh stage and had counted that too, and both of those had been work with a thing at the end of it.

This was five weeks of stone with the thing inside her the whole way.

It was not an ache and it was not a weight. That is what nobody could have told her and what she had not been able to work out until the fourth week, sitting on a sledge at the lip at about the second hour with a bar across her knees.

It was an address.

Every stage she had ever held had gone out of her into nine counties in about nine seconds, and her life since the first one had been the space where it used to be.

Now there was something in that space and it was staying. It was there on the six days a week when the hoist ran and it was there on the days it did not. It was there on a mat in the loft, on the third flight of a crew quarters, and in a county office at the third hour with two witnesses and a man at the back of the room.

It did not get quieter. It got more specific. By the third week she could find the edge of it without looking for it.

## The Grief She Worked

She worked it in loads, and she worked the same grief with which she had worked Du Hong, and she had known from the moment she sat down on a sledge in the fourth week of it what it was, and she had gone on putting it on the rope anyway.

It was not about the road.

It was about a boy of fifteen in a yard with a harness under one arm, who had said *I thought you would be a bigger one. You read small*, and who had given a stranger a road and a county because the stranger had hold of his ear and had asked nicely.

He had walked six miles off a down train in the cold in the autumn, on a lead line, with no escort, because a woman he had met for nine minutes on a dry road had put her staff down and told him to go up the grade.

He was owed four coppers a day. He was paid three.

And she had seen him once, for about nine minutes, in a yard with eleven men in it, and had told a clerk with a very good hand in front of all of them that the description was about her and not about him, and had then written the whole of that down and handed it to forty men who cannot read, and had gone.

She could not go back. Not because it was dangerous — because a second visit is a thing with a shape, and a woman who visits a carrier's yard twice in one winter has a business there, and a man over a tanner's door walks into a yard every afternoon of the week and has the whole of the rest of his life to be in it.

So she put it in loads.

Five on an ordinary one, six on a day when the block was running and the ice was off the grade, and that was the whole of it. Twelve coppers a load, six days of that in a week, and five weeks of it from the first morning she said yes at the lip of that cut.

That is what she worked it with, and it worked, in the way that working it does.

It did not make it better. It made it into a thing that had been carried, which is different and cheaper.

## The Hut

She went into the hut on the thirty-third day of the quarry, at about the third hour of the afternoon, and shut the door, and it was a hut with a stove and a bench and a slate on the wall with a week's numbers on it, and it was empty because it was empty at the third hour of the afternoon.

She had thought about where. She had thought about it for four days and she had worked out that it could not be the loft, because the loft was over a copying room and a keeper was in it, and whatever she did in that loft was a thing that happened in that tower. It could not be the quarters, because nine men slept in that room.

The hut was thirty paces from the hoist and it had a door and a stove in it and nobody in it, and it was the only place she had found in nine counties where a thing she did would not be seen by anybody who would have to make a decision about it afterwards.

She shut the door.

“No,” said Ning Yan.

It was the ordinary voice. She was not going to say it to a dark room with a lamp in it; she had done that in the spring in a room over a copying floor and it had gone out of her into nine counties.

She said it to a stove, at the third hour of an afternoon, in the ordinary voice, out loud, with her hand on the bench.

“No.”

And it went down.

She felt it go. Nine hundred feet of it, down through the crack that had been let open in the spring on a road below the fourth bridge and had not closed since, and down past the place where the breath went and into somewhere she could not get at, and she sat on a bench in a hut at a quarry with her hand flat on the wood and her right hand open on her knee and it took about eleven seconds.

Then she sat there for about a quarter of an hour.

## What Went Out

Nothing.

There was no sentence in nine counties. There was no voice at the fourth hour of a morning, and no lamp in a cage, and no keeper with four hundred and eleven volumes to copy an announcement into by the second hour, and no girl in a wash house, and no queue, and no queue's clerk with a good hand writing a date.

Nothing went anywhere at all.

There was a woman on a bench in a hut at a quarry outside Lowwater with a stove going and a week's numbers on a slate on the wall, and she was at the eighth stage of Condensing, and there was not one piece of paper in nine counties that said so.

## What the Filing Recorded

An hour worked at a stone end on the thirty-third day of the quarry, certified by the Hall's copyist off forty-one cards in a wooden frame, stamped irregular, carried four miles up a mountain to a board in a Hall with no roof.

That was all. The filing recorded an hour, because an hour is what it records, and it records the same whether a woman held something down in a hut or carried it up a hill.

The stage column on her licence read one, two, three, and the width of two ruled lines, five, six, seven, and the width of two ruled lines.

It was the same width.

That was the thing she understood on the bench, and it was the whole of what the winter had bought her: a suppressed stage does not leave a bigger absence. It leaves exactly the same absence. Nine is not a hole of a different shape from four. A hole is a hole.

A reading clerk reads down a column. He does not know. A registrar rules an absence is not a breach, because a registrar has a book with a rule in it and a queue outside and nineteen years of doing it that way.

And an office above this county, which keeps a list, and which has in it one column read in public in daylight by a man with a certified copy and a receipt, reads an absence differently.

He had said so in June, in front of a room, in words.

He had been right.

## What She Gained

She felt it, on the bench, about four seconds after it went down, and there was nothing to be frightened of in that four seconds, and she let herself have it.

It was like putting a heavy stone down.

There had been a weight in her chest since a night eight days ago in the second month of the cold that she had not known was a weight until it stopped, and it was the weight of carrying a thing that had to be decided about every single day, and it had been deciding about it every day since, and now it was not going to be decided about any more.

It was gone and it was eight and it was hers, and there was no notice period on it and no hour set by an office above the county and no sound in nine counties.

And there was a hole in a column nine counties wide.

## What She Said About It

“You have not asked,” said Yuan, on the road that evening, three miles west and then four up the mountain.

“No.”

“You have never not asked.”

“You had a week to say one word,” said Ning Yan, “and you said one word, and it was the right one, and I have been working on the same thing since a night eight days ago and you were not in the room.”

“I was in the room.”

“You were in the room the whole time and you have not had one opinion about it,” she said, “and I want that recorded, and I want you to know that I have noticed, and I am not going to thank you for it and I am not going to say anything else about it.”

He did not.

They went up four miles and the third flight cost three counts going up and eight coming down, and there were nine men breathing in a room that had held twelve three winters ago.

## What Was Left of the Channel

“How much of it is there,” she said, on the mat, at about the second hour, on the fourth night after it went down.

“About three weeks,” said Yuan.

“Out of what.”

“Out of the ten it was on the morning you said the word in a cold room with a paper folded twice on the mat,” he said. “Four and a half of them went on that. There were five and a half on the afternoon you walked out of the county office, and thirty-six days of twice a day have come off them since.”

“And that is what your own spring conditions are worth, and it is the only thing standing between three weeks and none at all. And you have told me twice this week that you are not going to ask me for another reading, and I am not going to offer you one.”

“I am not going to ask for one.”

“No,” he said. “And that is what I said on the afternoon I put it down.”

He was quiet for a count.

“The standing is yours now and not mine. It was always yours. I was holding it because you told me not to, then you told me to, and then you told me not to again, and this time it is not me deciding that I will not. It is me not being allowed to.”

She lay in the cold.

“That is different,” said Ning Yan.

“Yes,” said Yuan. “It is the first thing in this year that is different, and you did it on purpose, and it is the only good thing that has come out of this run of weeks and I am not going to pretend otherwise.”
