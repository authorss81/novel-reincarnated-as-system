# The Dead Writ

The rain came through the Assembly Hall of Qinghe in four places and had been coming through it in four places since the autumn, and there was a table at the west end under a dry awning that one of the three maintaining members had put there himself four years ago, and the rain went around it.

She came up on a cart day with a docket in her coat and got wet on the four-mile road in the ordinary way and came into the Hall with water running off the hem onto a floor that had four pools in it and had had them since the thaw.

The Hall clerk was at the copy bench with his back to her and a sheet in front of him, and she did not go over, and he did not turn round, and that was the arrangement that had been in place between the two of them since the winter and neither of them had ever put it into words.

## The West End

Lu Kang was under the awning with his hands behind his back. Deng Chao was at the end of the west table with a cup he was not drinking from. Pei An was standing at the rail looking out at the rain coming through the place where a roof used to be, which is what the three of them did when there was nothing in a room they wanted to be in the middle of.

She put the filing copy on the table under the awning and squared it with her left hand and did not put anything else on it, because everything else she wanted was not paper she could carry.

“I am asking for the seal,” said Ning Yan.

Nobody said anything.

“I am asking for the seal on a relicensing. A second application, three legs, and the first of them is your three marks on one sheet, and I have not got the sheet yet, and I am asking now because I am four miles down a road to find out whether I am going to spend four weeks being refused.”

Lu Kang took his hands from behind his back.

“Say the number again,” he said.

“Condensing nine.”

“Condensing nine,” said Lu Kang. “Not Foundation.”

“Not Foundation,” said Ning Yan. “Foundation is barred without a second relicensing and the second relicensing needs a schedule the county does not keep past the boundary, and I have read that on my own licence twice a day for two months. I am not asking you for Foundation. I have not asked you for Foundation since the winter. I am asking you for the top of the second realm.”

“You have written it in your own hand,” said Lu Kang, “and not in mine.”

“That is in the filing and it is not going to change.”

“I know it is not going to change,” said Lu Kang. “I wrote the rule and it says the applicant's own hand, and I have watched you obey a rule that has cost you four months and two friends and a man's life, and I am not going to ask you to break it for the sake of a conversation on a wet afternoon.”

## The Door That Is Not Locked

That was all he said, and then he stopped, and the rain came through the fourth place and went across the floor to the low corner where the water had joined itself into one body about a week ago.

She had prepared three things to say and she had not said any of them.

She had prepared *I know what is in your second book.* She had prepared the fourth line of a debt paper, read upside down over a clerk's tray in a county office in the winter, with its eleven-month-and-eleven-day spacing and its citation of the ledger of the maintaining members of Qinghe by name.
She had had it ready since the seventeenth day, written out in the wash code on a docket in a coat lining, and she had not taken it out and she was not going to take it out, and the reason she was not going to take it out was not mercy and was not fear.

It was that she did not have anywhere to put it.

Not there. There were four people in that room and one of them was a clerk with his back turned and one of them was a table with rain on it, and the second book was worth nothing to a registrar and everything to a broker, and the only office she could have put it in that would have acted on it was four miles down a road, and that office's rule was that a claim in a book cannot be unlisted, which meant a claim put there would live.

And underneath all of that, and she was honest enough with herself at twenty years of practice to look straight at it, she did not want to spend it. Nine households struck in one stroke over eleven years, and a name in a county book with a broker's paper on it, and a thing she could put in front of three men that would change what they did in the next ten minutes. That was a weapon and she had never held one in her life.

So she stood in the rain-light under a dry awning with three men who were not answering her and she felt the thing she had braced for and it was not there.

They had not refused.

That was the whole of the difference and it was the worst of it, because a refusal is an answer. A refusal can be argued with, worked around, appealed to a higher office, waited on, or bought.
Three men saying nothing is a door that is not shut, standing open in a wall, with nobody behind it, for as long as the three of them can hold their faces like that, and they can hold it for a very long time, because they have had practice since the sixth day of the winter.

“Lu Kang,” she said. “I am going to ask you a second question and I am going to ask it once. If you do not want it, say so and I will go.”

He looked at her.

“Ask it.”

“Why,” said Ning Yan. “Not why not. Why you have not said no. You stamped my schedule in the spring and you did not say a word and you let me say out loud, in front of your clerk, that the writ died on a county sheet the hour I signed the ceiling, and then you went out into the rain. You have had eleven weeks. You have had a whole road.”

## What He Said

“It has not been revoked,” said Lu Kang.

“I know it has not been revoked.”

“It has no road left to anywhere,” said Lu Kang, “which is a different sentence and you have never once put the two together, and you should. There is a difference between a paper that has been withdrawn and a paper that has been overtaken. A paper that has been withdrawn has to be destroyed. A paper that has been overtaken sits where it is and everybody goes round it.”

“So it is still there.”

“It is still there and it is still signed by three of us and it cannot be used for anything at all, and if you went to the Foundation tomorrow you would be doing unregistered practice under a schedule you do not appear on, and it would breach the sponsorship that issued it, and the breach would be on a county sheet in nine counties in about four days.”
He said it without any weight in it at all. “That is not me refusing you. That is a document being dead.”

Then he said the other thing, and he said it to the rain coming through the fourth place rather than to her, and it took her eleven weeks and the rest of that afternoon and most of the following night to understand that he had told her the reason and had not known he was doing it.

“I will tell you what a member of this crew at nine is,” said Lu Kang. “It is a member inside our own schedule. She is on a sheet this Hall keeps, with her hours in a column, certified by our clerk and stamped by me, and returned to the county at the quarter.”

“She is examinable in that schedule by any office that comes with a stamp and a reason, and I have known that since the market day you went down the road with Du Hong's papers, and Deng Chao has not slept well about it since, and neither has Pei An, and neither of them will say so.”

“You are telling me the seal is dangerous.”

“I am telling you the seal is *known*,” said Lu Kang. “There is one thing that has ever advanced on the furnace schedule of Qinghe in eleven years and it is standing under this awning with one arm, and it advanced on it because it was on it, and every office that has ever come near that sheet has therefore known about it since the day it happened.”

He took his hands back behind his back.

“A member of this crew at Foundation is on a writ we cannot use, and a member of this crew at five is a woman who can be examined exactly as far as five and no further, and neither of those is worth three seals to me tonight.” He looked at her. “Nine is worth three seals. Do not ask me why tonight. Ask me in four weeks when you have the other two legs and bring me a sheet with something on it.”

She did not say thank him.

She stood under a dry awning in a building with no roof, in the rain-light, and understood that she had been answered and that the answer was conditional and that the condition was a piece of paper she did not have.

## What She Did Not Say

She put the filing copy back in the coat.

“I am not going to say it,” she said.

“Say what.”

“The thing I have been standing here holding.”

Lu Kang looked at her for a moment longer than he had looked at her in any conversation she had had with him in eleven months.

“No,” he said. “You are not. And that is the only reason any of the three of us is still standing on this side of this table.”

Then Deng Chao put his cup down on the board, which he had not done all afternoon, and said, in a voice she had heard twice before in her life and both times about numbers:

“Your line was nine hundred and forty asked and nine hundred and forty worked at the end of the spring and it has been level since the day the certificate was signed. If you put a hire into that book it stops being level and you cannot make it level again by wanting to, and the man who keeps that book will not move a figure for one arm.”

“I know,” said Ning Yan.

“You will be annexing.”

“I will be annexing.”

“Then go and annex it before you come back up here,” said Deng Chao, “because I am not putting my mark on a sheet that says nine while your hours are in two places at once, and neither is he, and neither is he, and you may tell them I said so and they will say the same.”

She went out into the rain.

The floor had four pools in it and the low corner had one body of water in it that had been four bodies in the thaw, and the rain came down in four places on a building where a furnace schedule had been certified in the spring by a man who had read all of it and looked at none of the households, and she went across it and out and did not look back at the awning.

## What She Carried Down the Road

The whole of four miles, in the rain, on a cart day, with a docket in a coat that she did not take out.

She was not refused and she was not answered either, and she had walked four miles up a mountain to be given a sentence that amounted to *bring me a sheet with something on it*, and there was no way in nine counties to get a sheet with something on it without first doing two other things to two different offices, and one of those two offices had already told her the price of it out loud and had not enjoyed it.

Nine was worth three seals.

Nine was worth three seals because a woman at nine is examinable inside their own schedule, and three men who had spent two volumes refusing to have a member they could not account for had finally worked out that the one member they could account for was the safest thing they had ever put their names to.

They were not doing it for her. They were doing it because of the writ.

She got that far on the second mile and then she had to stop in the lee of a cut wall for a while because of the short breath, and she stood in the rain on the Lowwater road with four miles still to go and the pass behind her and the season's cart days counted, and she worked out the order.

Clearance first. Not the seal first.

She had asked for the seal and the first thing she had been told was *go and annex it before you come back up here*, and the second thing had been a man who could not put his mark on a sheet that said nine while her hours were in two places at once.

Both of those were the same sentence, and it meant that three men were going to hold three marks off a sheet until a man with a quota book in a room with one window had put her hours somewhere a county could see.

In the spring the seal had come first and she had gone to three men before she had gone to a clerk.

This time three men were telling her to go and be refused by a clerk first, and they were telling her to do it themselves, and neither of them had said the word *because* once.
