# A Name Nobody Keeps

The county found the stroke on the ninety-first morning at the third hour, on the fourth item of the first sheet, in a fair hand, in a room with six clerks in it and a queue out of the door.

It had been there since the thaw. It was a single line drawn through a column of figures with a dry brush and it went from the top of the column to the bottom of it and it did not stop at the figures.

“Ninety-one,” said a clerk. “Copy three. Struck.”

Sun He came and looked at it and did not touch it.

“Whose hand,” said Sun He.

“Unknown. It is in the tower's fair hand or a copy of it. It could be either.”

“When.”

“There is no date on it. It could be the day it was delivered or it could be any day in the two months after.”

Sun He read the item again.

“Item four is a list of four certified county copies delivered to the Lantern Register at Lowwater and entered upward for none of the quarter in which they were delivered or any quarter since,” he said. “And one of the four has been struck.”

“One of the four has been struck,” said the clerk, “and the list does not say which, and I have matched them by the county mark and it is the third of the four.”

Sun He wrote something on a slip and gave it to a young man and the young man went out of the room.

## The Search

It took until the second night hour and it was done by three clerks and a ledger from the high shelf, and they were thorough, and they were doing their job.

They went through the county indexes for eleven years. They went through the incoming bundle register for the same period. They went through the requests register, which is the book where a thing that arrives from outside gets written down with the office it came from and the day it arrived, and they found four hundred and sixty entries, and one hundred and nine of them were from offices outside the bound.

Every one of those hundred and nine had an office in it that a county could put on a line.

“Nothing,” said the clerk of four years, at the second night hour, to six people and one registrar. “Nothing in eleven years from an office this county does not have a line for. Not one.”

“Run it wider.”

“It does not run wider. This is the register.”

“Then it came in outside the register.”

“Then it came in outside the register,” said the clerk, “and there is no version of this office in which that is a surprise, because the register is the register and a thing that does not come into it does not come into it.”

Sun He stood at the table for a while.

“Enter it as it is,” he said. “A striking on copy three of four, undated, unattributed, on a request from an office not entered in this county's request register in eleven years. Enter the office as it stands on the sheet, and if it is not legible enter that it is not legible, and do not enter an inference.”

“Is there an inference.”

“There is a great deal of inference,” said Sun He, “and every office that has ever had one of these has had about nine, and eight of them were wrong, and the wrong one is what gets written down and read later by a man who was not here.”

He wrote the line himself, which he did not do.

## The Tower

They sent two clerks up the four flights at the fourth hour of the ninetieth and she was on the stair with a slate frame under her arm.

They did not go into the copying room first. They stood on the fourth stair and asked Feng Lushi, in the doorway, with the lamp going behind him, whether he would come down to the county office.

“Will I be needed up here.”

“No,” said the clerk. “We would like four sheets.”

“Which four.”

“The four county copies of a Qinghe furnace schedule that went into the copying room in the thaw,” said the clerk. “Behind the fifth item of the third volume off the warm shelf. We would like them read at our table and we would like them read now, and after that you are welcome to come down in whatever capacity your office thinks proper.”

Feng got his coat.

He did not ask who had asked. He did not ask for a paper. He went into the copying room, took four sheets out from behind the fifth item of a volume, put them inside his coat against his chest with the struck one uppermost, and came out and went down four flights.

## What He Was Asked

She went down with them because she was in the stairwell and there was nowhere else to be, and she stood at the end of the table for the whole of it.

They did not ask him to recognise a hand.

She had braced for it in the lane on the way down, on four miles of thinking, and she had braced for a clerk putting two sheets side by side on a county table, and it did not happen, and when she worked out why it did not happen she felt something in her chest go cold and stay cold.

They asked him about the dates.

“When were the copies delivered.”

“In the second week of the thaw,” said Feng. “By a man off a cart, who read them in my presence and I read them in his.”

“When did you put them behind the fifth item.”

“The same afternoon.”

“When did you first observe the striking.”

Feng stopped.

That was the only time he stopped, and it was for about a count and a half, and then he answered it in the ordinary voice of a man who has been in a room like this for twenty-two years.

“I observed it in the second week of the thaw,” said Feng, “on the day after they were delivered.”

“And you reported it.”

“No,” said Feng.

The clerk's brush stopped.

“Why not.”

“Because the striking was on a county copy,” said Feng, “and a county copy is not mine. It came from the county and it was struck by an office, and that office is not in this county's register.”

He put two fingers on the table, not touching anything.

“If I had reported a striking on a county copy, then the first thing this county would have done is look at whether the county's own copy had been altered. It had not been altered. It is this copy in this tower that was struck, and the county's copy in the county's room is not struck, and I went and looked at it in the thaw to be sure.”

“You went and looked.”

“I went and looked and it was not struck,” said Feng, “and then I did nothing, because there was nothing in this room that had happened.”

The clerk wrote it down and read it back and Feng said it was true.

Then they asked him the last thing.

“Who asked for it to be struck.”

“I was given a request,” said Feng. “It came up in a quarter's crate. It is ordinary county stationery and it is sealed and it carries the office on it, and I am required by it to copy the shape of what it names and not to read it, and I did as it asked and I did not look at the office's name, and I am not going to look at it now.”

“That is a position you took deliberately.”

“It is the only position there is for a keeper,” said Feng, “and I have held it for twenty-two years, and it is not because I do not trust the office.”

## One Sentence

She heard the word *office* go up and down off the county table four times in about an hour, and she stood at the end of it with her hands at her sides and did not say one word in that room.

She said one at the wash house, at the ninth night hour, to nobody, with the water running.

“That office is not in any of my books either,” said Yuan.

She had not been expecting it and it landed harder than the stroke.

“Do not do that.”

“It is not a discovery. I have never been able to read it and I said so four times,” said Yuan. “I am telling you that a county register with eleven years in it came back with one line about an office it could not put on a line.”

“That is not surprising you.”

“No,” he said. “That is the part I cannot stand. I do not know what it means and I am not going to build a room out of it tonight.”

“It was struck before me.”

“It was struck before you and it was struck in a tower and by an office, and it was struck off a county copy, and I have never once been able to read the name of the office attached to the request I can remember, and those are two facts about the same kind of thing and I am not going to build a room out of them tonight.”

“All right,” said Ning Yan.

“You can say what it is.”

“I can say what it is,” said Ning Yan. “In the spring a sealed slip came up in a crate in this tower and a man of sixty copied the shape of a word he could not read and struck it out of four copies because an office asked him to.”

She put her thumb on the edge of the trough.

“He did not recognise the hand and he did not read the name. Nothing in this county has asked him about it since. And now a different office has struck a county copy in the same tower, and the county cannot find either of them.”

She stood in the lane and let the water run.

“Two requests in a year,” she said, “both from outside, both to the same tower, both naming an office the county does not keep, and I have not spent a single thing of mine on either one, and the county has just written down that it cannot find them.”

“No,” said Yuan. “You have not.”

## The Suspension

It came on the ninety-second morning and it was one line and it was not a punishment and it was not a dismissal, and the man who read it out said so four times because clerks do.

Registrar, county office at Lowwater. The keeper of the Lantern Register at Lowwater is suspended from the compilation and entry of the quarterly upward bundle for one quarter from the date of this line, on the ground of a discrepancy between the return of one quarter and the bundle returned with it.

The upward bundle for the quarter in question is carried by a clerk of this office.

The volumes and the copying floor of the Lantern Register remain in the keeper's hands. The column remains in the keeper's hands and is not altered by this line. Nothing in this line removes the keeper from his office, his stipend, his volumes, or his house.

The district does not terminate an examination.

“Read the first sentence of the last line again,” said Ning Yan.

He read it again. An examination ends when the office that opened it closes it, and this office does not close examinations, and it has not closed one in nineteen years.

She put her hand on the counter and left it there for about a count and a half.

That was what she had been afraid of since the eighth hour on the stair and it was not what came.

Nothing came. Nobody came. Feng Lushi stood in a copying room four miles down a road with four hundred and eleven volumes and a column that stood at five and a lamp burning in a cage on the fourth landing, and a county clerk was going to walk up those stairs once a month with a satchel and take the bundle out of the room and carry it down and up to the county.

And the four county copies of a Qinghe furnace schedule went into a satchel on the ninety-first afternoon and were read at the county's own table, in the county's own light, by people who had never met the man who carried them down a road in the thaw.

## What It Had Cost Him

She worked out the whole of it in about nine minutes standing in a lane at Lowwater, and she worked it out twice, because she did not trust it the first time.

He loses a quarter.

A quarter of copying is a quarter of everything. Every announcement of every stage in nine counties, for a quarter, is carried up a stair by a clerk who is not him, in a hand that is not his, and it goes into the volumes in a hand that is not his, and the record of the bound for three months is a record that nine counties can read and that the man who has kept it for twenty-two years did not make.

And his line at the back of the fair volume is now on the upward bundle, in order, with the working lines and the column and the volume descriptions, and it is being carried up by a clerk.

“Every hour I copy for you goes upward truly,” said Yuan.

“No,” said Ning Yan. “Not that yet. It goes upward once, at the quarter, and it goes up because it was asked for, and a man over the tanner's did not have to do anything about it.”

“It goes upward once at the quarter now, and after the quarter there is a suspension, and after the suspension there is a keeper with nothing to do with his stair and a bundle that will not go up, and in about four months a man with clean hands is going to have a very simple question for him.”

“I know,” said Ning Yan.

“He does not need to ask you anything. He has never needed to ask you anything. He asked a tower.”

She stood in the lane and watched a man at a board chalk her on for the ninth load, and she put her hand on the mat and went out to the water channel, and the fee for the morning was gone and the fee for the afternoon was about to be gone, and there was a quarter coming in which she could not afford to lose either of them.

“Do not be pleased,” she said. “I know what you are about to be pleased about.”

“I was not going to say anything.”

“You were going to say that he is off the stair because of a bundle with my hours in it.”

“I was going to say that the word *observer* is not the word,” said Yuan, “and that I have been calling it one since I started, and that the man in the county office has just written down a word for it that is better than mine, and I do not like the feeling of having been right about a shape and wrong about what it is for.”

The mat went into the water at the second hour and she worked the rest of the day in the ordinary way.

At about the fourth hour a clerk of the county office came up four flights of the tower stair with a satchel, for the first time in twenty-two years, and did not knock, because he was a clerk in a building and not a man who knew anybody in it.

She went up after him with the last of the light and did not knock either.
