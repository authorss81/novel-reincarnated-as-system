# What Stops Talking

The paper had been on the mat for five days and it took her about ninety seconds to open it, because a thing you have decided not to do for five days turns out to be very fast once you start.

She read it standing up in a cold room above a copying room at Lowwater on the morning of the one hundred and thirty-sixth day, with Feng Lushi's lamp going in its cage on the fourth landing below her, and she read it twice, and she did not sit down.

It was four sheets. It was headed by the Warden's office. It was signed by the Warden's office, and it carried a number in the corner and nothing else that named a person of any kind.

She had known every term on it since the afternoon before, because a man had read them through her eyes at a price she had named out loud in front of a keeper, and every one of them was true.

Nothing about it had changed in five days.

## The Room He Chose

He was in the room over the tanner's at the second hour with the door open and the strip of sacking over the frame, and he had a table cleared and two chairs at it, and the paper was on the table in a square, and there was a lamp on it and no other light.

“I have the witnesses coming,” he said.

“Where.”

“Here,” he said. “It is my paper and it is my room and a thing done in a room like this is clean.”

“No,” said Ning Yan.

He did not ask why. He stood with one hand on the back of the nearer chair for about two counts.

“Where, then.”

“The county office on the square.”

He looked at her.

“I am not going to be difficult about it,” he said. “I would have said yes before you finished the sentence. I want you to understand that I would rather you had it in a room with no clerk in it, because everything that is said in that building is written down twice and one of the copies is not mine.”

“I know that,” said Ning Yan. “That is why I want it there.”

“All right,” he said.

## The Room She Chose

The county office on the square at the third hour had nine people in it and a queue down the step and Sun He at his table with nineteen years behind him and a pen that made a sound like somebody clearing their throat.

The Hunter came in with the four sheets under his arm and gave his name to nobody, and stood about eight feet back from the counter, and waited while she walked to the table and put her own filing copy on the wood.

She asked for the room.

Sun He looked at the queue and then at the sheets under the man's arm and then at her, and did four things in the order he did them, which is a thing she has watched him do since the spring and has never once seen him do them out of order.

He told the queue to wait.

He told the clerk at the near counter to bring a second chair.

He took a sheet and wrote at the top of it the hour and the room and the fact that a member had asked that a matter be heard in the office and had named the reason, which was that anything said in the office is written down.

And then he said, to the man, in the ordinary voice of a man doing his duty:

“State the paper.”

## The Terms

It was read out in the office by Sun He, who was not asked to and who did it because a clerk of the county reads what is laid in front of him, and his clerk wrote it down at the same time in a hand that was not his.

The Warden's office would enter a certification, on a named day before the end of the winter, that the voice heard at Lowwater by a member of the Qinghe furnace crew, servant-born, is a misread natural echo.

The designation standing over that member would be drawn down over one winter, without injury and without a stage being taken from her.

The licence would stand. Its terms, its ceiling, its conditions, its defect and its seventh condition would all stand, and it would be carried in the county's register exactly as it stands this morning.

And the standing claim of eight hundred jin, entered on the back of that licence, would be answered out of the Warden's own purse.

It took about four minutes and it did not stop and it did not soften and there was not one sentence of it that a man could later call a lie.

Sun He read the last line and put the sheets square on the table and said the hour out loud for the record.

## Who Pays

“Who pays it,” said Ning Yan.

“Ask it properly,” said Sun He, not looking up, “and I will answer it properly, because I have a book here that says nothing about it and I would like there to be one.”

She asked it properly.

“The Warden's office,” said the man. “Its own purse. Not this county's — the county pays nothing. Not the three men whose seal is on your licence — they pay nothing, they are not mentioned anywhere on those sheets.”

“Entered how, in this county.”

“As a standing claim satisfied by a third party, on a named day,” he said. “Which is a thing counties write two or three times a decade and which costs this office nothing and which I have to enter whichever way it goes, because I enter what is put in front of me.”

“And the money goes to the creditor.”

“The money goes to the creditor,” he said. “Hou Sanliang of Lowwater, in full, on the day. He will be written down as paid and he will stop being a creditor and the claim will stop standing and it will stop travelling on your licence, because a claim that is answered is not a claim that can be enforced and cannot be re-entered by anybody for anybody.”

She stood at the table in a county office for about four seconds.

A year. She had carried eight hundred jin on the back of a licence for a year, in oilcloth, against her ribs, above a clay plate, through a fever and a road and a body on a ditch bank.

The only reason it was a weight at all was that one man in Lowwater had not been paid. She had never been in a room with him, and two separate offices had told her she could not pay him, and she had asked nobody for a way to.

And here was a purse with the amount in it, and one sentence of paperwork, and a date.

“Then it is a real offer,” she said.

“It is a real offer,” he said. “I have never given you one that was not.”

## What Happens to the Thing in Her Head

She looked at the second sheet for a while.

“What happens to it,” said Ning Yan.

He did not answer for about as long as it took him to decide that he was going to answer properly.

“It goes out,” he said. “On a date inside the winter that I am not going to guess at. From that day there is nothing in your head that answers, and you will not be able to hear one if there is one, and I am not going to tell you the day until you are told the day.”

“What happens to it.”

“That is the second half of the question and it is a fair one.” He put his hands flat on the table. “I cannot answer it. That is not evasion and I am not going to improve on it by being careful. I do not know what happens to it, and neither does anyone in this room, and the sheet says nothing about it because the sheet does not know either.”

“Does it hurt.”

“No,” he said. “That is in it. The designation is drawn down without injury and without a stage being taken, and that is not a comfort I am adding; it is a term, and it is the only term on that paper about your body at all.”

## The Clause in the Middle

She read the whole of it again, slowly, because she had read it twice in five days and both times she had been reading for the line about the thing in her head.

It was the fourth line of the second sheet, in the ordinary place, in the ordinary words, the way a thing is written when the person who wrote it did not think there was anything to hide in it.

*Announcements already carried to the bound are not comprised in this instrument and are not affected by it.*

She read it four times.

“Four of mine have gone out,” she said.

“Four of yours have gone out,” he said. “At the fourth hour of a morning, to everybody inside the bound at once, and they cannot be recalled, and there is nothing in these sheets that reaches them, and I did not put that line in and I would not have taken it out if I could have.”

“You knew it was there.”

“I read the sheet you read,” he said. “I did not know it would matter to you until you read it out loud in this room.”

She put her hand flat on the wood.

Nine counties. The third hour of a morning in the spring, and the fourth hour of a morning at the end of a season, and about four hundred people in four counties who had heard a woman's body get to six and get to seven.

There was a girl in a wash house who had heard it and told three washerwomen in advance. There was a keeper on a fourth stair who had written one of them down in a fair hand at the back of a fair volume. There was a man over a tanner's door who had stood at a public counter and said out loud that the next stage had to go into that column.

And on the day this paper named, in that same bound, there would be nothing in her head to explain a single one of them.

Not one sentence of it was a lie.

That was the difficulty. It had been the difficulty since before the fever and it was the difficulty now with two witnesses and a clerk's pen in the room.

## The Refusal

“No,” said Ning Yan.

The room went on. It is a county office and it does not stop, and the queue went on down the step, and the pen went on.

She turned round to the man who had not lied to her for eleven weeks.

“No,” she said again, because the office had to have it twice and because she wanted it in her own voice at the ordinary volume, in front of two people who would have it written down. “I will not hand it over, and I am saying it out loud, and it is the third of the month and I am saying it in a county office because a county writes down what is said in it and I would like this in a county's hand.”

“All right,” he said.

“Write down that I was offered it and that I refused it and that I was told the terms were as read and that I verified them yesterday by my own means and that I am not repeating how.”

“I am not writing down how,” said Sun He, without looking up. “I am writing down what was offered, what was said, and the hour.”

## The Signature

The refusal was three lines and it was on the fourth sheet, under the terms, where the paper had left a space for it, because a paper that offers something and expects a signature leaves a place to put no.

It said that the member named at the head declines the instrument; that she has read the four sheets; that she refuses them.

She wrote it in her own left hand, which is the hand she writes everything in, and it took about eleven seconds, and the arm on the wrong side of it did not open, and there was nobody in that room who needed to know that.

Then the date, in her own hand, and then her name, in her own hand, in the order it goes.

The Hunter read it and did not touch it.

## The Name

The clerk had the Warden's book open before he had the refusal wet.

It came out of a press under the counter and it was not this county's book, and it was kept in this county.

Sun He said, when she looked at it, that it had been on that shelf for longer than the man standing at the table had been alive, and that it was one of two, and the other was up the road at the cross, and that both of them were looked at about twice a year by a man from the district who never wrote in either.

The clerk wrote the date, and the office, and the instrument, and the fact of the refusal, and then he got to the line that wants the agent and stopped with the pen on the sheet.

“Sir,” he said. “Name, for the lay of the paper.”

The paper itself carried none. Four sheets headed by an office and signed by an office with a number in the corner, and a man had carried them to a county and laid them on a table and had not put his name on one of them, in eleven weeks, to anybody, anywhere.

The clerk waited with the pen up.

“Bai Zhu,” he said.

He said it the way a man says a word he has been given for a form. He did not look at her and he did not look at Sun He and he gave it once and it was over.

The clerk wrote it.

And Sun He wrote it too, in the county book, in the column where names of offices and officers go, in the ordinary hand of a man entering a thing he has been given, because a county writes down what is said in it and he had been at that table for nineteen years and he was not going to start making an exception on the one morning it mattered.

She watched two men write the same two syllables into two different books.

Three years of a stranger at a first desk, buying nothing, giving his name to nobody, and he had given it to a form.

## What She Did Not Say

Outside, on the step, in the cold, with the queue going round her, she stood still until her breath came back and then she walked to the end of the lane and stood against a wall where the wind had gone round the corner.

She had four and a half weeks less than she had had on the morning before the fever and about three weeks of him left and a claim standing on the back of her licence with her name on it and a creditor in Lowwater who would wait another year because she had said no in a county office.

She did not thank him for the reading.

He had put the terms into her head a day and a half before she needed them and it had cost him nothing to do and it had cost her four and a half weeks that do not come back out, and she had said the word *read him* out loud on a keeper's floor in front of a man who reports in full whether or not it is entered.

She did not forgive him for it.

“Do not,” she said, to nobody, at a wall in a lane, in the ordinary voice. “I know what you are going to say and you are not going to say it.”

“I am going to say that the offer was real,” said Yuan.

“I know it was real. I read it.”

“I am going to say that four announcements are going to go out of you in this county in the next two months whatever is on that piece of paper,” he said, “and that the paper does not reach them and neither does anything else, and that there is no version of the winter where somebody in nine counties is going to be told an explanation.”

He was quiet for a count.

“That is all of it,” he said. “You have a licence in oilcloth and a claim on the back of it and a man with your name in two books in this county, and none of those three things was true on the day before the fever.”

She stood at the wall.

“No,” said Ning Yan. “It was not.”
