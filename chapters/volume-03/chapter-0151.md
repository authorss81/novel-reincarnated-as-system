# The Attendance

The thing she had not expected was that it would cost her a morning.

She had gone into the county office on the square expecting to be a person in a book for a while, and she came out of it at the fourth hour with a day in the quarter written on her in a hand that was not hers.

The morning.

That was the whole of what they had taken, and they had taken it without a word of malice and without any appearance of doing it on purpose: a fixed day, every four weeks, at the third hour, at the county office, in person, signed.

## The Line in the Book

She went back the next day to ask about it and she asked it badly and Sun He corrected her, which is a thing he is good at.

“Who put it there,” she said.

“It is in the standing order,” said Sun He, “and I entered it at the fourth hour of the day before yesterday, and I entered it in the ordinary way, and I would like to get on with the queue.”

“Whose standing order.”

“The district's, under a refusal entered in a Warden's book.” He wrote a number without looking up. “A member who has declined an instrument laid for her by an officer of that office attends that office once a month by the standing order, and the attendance is entered against her member's sheet, and it is not optional and it is not a request and there is no version of it where a man in a coat asks you to be polite about it.”

He read the number back and then said, with the pen where it was:

“You are the first one this office has had.”

## The Copy

Two things, and she asked about both of them at the counter on the way out, and the clerk at the near counter answered the second one because it was his trade and it was not a matter of the book.

The first was whether it came off the licence.

“It does not come off the licence,” said Sun He, from the table, not stopping. “The licence is a licence. It has a ceiling and seven conditions and a claim on the back of it. A refusal is not a condition and it is not a breach and nothing on that paper has been altered, and you could put your hand on any county's copy of it this morning and every word of it would be what it was on the thaw.”

“So it is additional.”

“It is on your member's sheet,” said Sun He, “which is a different paper from your licence, and a member's sheet is copied at the quarter and at any application and once in a while for no reason at all, and it goes up with whatever else goes up, and that is the whole of what your refusal does to a piece of paper.”

The clerk answered the second one without being asked, because she had put the question on the counter in the shape of a question and clerks like a question on a counter.

“Copies go with the schedule,” he said. “The schedule goes to the Hall at the quarter with the annex on it. Whatever is on your member's sheet comes with it.”

## The Arithmetic of a Winter

She did it on the road between the square and the lane, four times, in four days, and then she did it properly on the third flight of the crew quarters at Qinghe at about the ninth hour with nine men asleep in twelve bunks.

Furnace floor, four and a half hours a day, and it did not stop in the cold because a furnace is the one thing in nine counties that gets more work when everything else stops.

Lane behind a well, six days when the carts come, five when they do not, by the load.

A morning every four weeks at the third hour, in Lowwater, in person, in her own hand, on a fixed day.

Four miles of road between the middle of the one and the other, and the third flight between the door and the bed, and the third flight now cost three counts instead of two and had done since the fever.

She did not do the arithmetic for the money. She had been doing the money for eleven months and the money was a fixed price on a slate and the price did not care what she thought.

She did it because a month is four weeks and a winter is eleven of them, and a quarter is only three. A quarterly return had cost her one morning in ten weeks. A monthly attendance would cost her one morning in four.

That was the whole of what a refusal cost. Not a hunt. A diary.

## What a Missed One Is

She asked that one too, on the fourth day, because she is a person who asks while there is still time to be told the answer.

“A missed attendance is entered separately from your hours,” said Sun He. “That is the reason I am telling you now and not in the winter. Your hours go in one column and they are made of work and they can be made up. An attendance is in a different column and it is made of one thing and there is no way of making it up except by attending.”

“So it does not break the filing.”

“It does not touch the filing,” said Sun He, “and I want to be exact. You are going to hear a man in a good coat explain the difference to somebody one day and I would rather you had my words than his.”

He put the pen down for a moment.

“An attendance is not your hours. Nothing about a missed attendance can be paid for in hours, and nothing about it is a breach of anything on your licence. But if you miss one it is written down in a column that a man in this building reads every morning of his life.”

He put the pen down and squared the sheet.

“That is the whole of what I have got for you,” he said. “I am not going to say I am sorry, because it is not my order and it is not my book and I do not think you want me to be sorry in front of a queue.”

## The Crew Read Their Own Schedule

She worked out the rest of it that night in the loft with the lamp going on the fourth landing below her, and the rest of it was worse than the morning.

Her member's sheet was copied at the quarter.

The quarter's bundle went to the Hall at Qinghe with the annex on it.

And at Qinghe there were nine men who had read the word *irregular* on the front of their own furnace schedule in the middle of a season and had asked one question about it, which she had answered in one sentence and gone back to the cold end for.

Nine men who read their own schedule. That was the phrase Feng Lushi had used in the spring about the crew and she had not liked it then either.

So somewhere in the next three weeks a sheet would come down a road to a Hall with a clerk's hand on it, and it would carry her hours and the stage column and the irregular stamp twice, and it would carry, in the ordinary place, that a member of that crew had been offered something by an office above the county and had said no in a county room in front of two people.

And they would not have to ask her.

They would read it, the way they had read the other thing, and the whole of the furnace floor would know by the second hour of a shift.

## The Morning After

On the morning after, Da Shan chalked her on and she put nine loads on the water channel, and the carts came at the ninth hour and the light went off the cut at about the third hour of the afternoon, and she walked back up four miles to the crew quarters with the short breath coming at the willows on the same rise as always and stood there about four seconds and went on.

“You have done your arithmetic,” said Yuan.

“I have done my arithmetic.”

“And it has come out worse than you expected and better than you are afraid of,” he said, “and you are going to keep doing it every four weeks for eleven weeks, and on the eleventh one of those mornings you are going to be somewhere four miles from a stone end at the third hour in the cold, and there is not one thing in the next three months that is going to change that number.”

“No,” said Ning Yan. “But there is a man in that room every month now who used to come once, and I would like to know what he has written down in the two months he has not been.”

“He has written down what he came to write down.”

“Then he will have written down the attendance,” she said, “and the date, and that I came.”

“He will have written down the attendance,” said Yuan, “and the date, and that you came, and he will have written down that you refused a thing in writing in a county room with two witnesses, and that is going to be the most useful line in his book, and it is going to be useful to him because of what you did and not because of what he did.”

She stopped on the road at the turn at the willows.

“Say the rest of it.”

“There is no rest of it,” said Yuan. “I have been telling you for four days that the rest of it is arithmetic.”
