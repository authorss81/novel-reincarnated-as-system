# Three Counts

The stage came on the fourth night of the fever, and she had been in the loft for four days by then, and she had about a count and a half of anything at all.

## The Four Days

A physician's hour on a county sheet. A frame carried four miles up a mountain by a man at the Hall's own door, which she had asked for and had been given in the ordinary way, and which cost her a stamp and about two hours of a thing she did not have.

Nine men at a furnace floor, four and a half hours a day, and the short breath on the second flight.

The fever had gone to the cold line and stayed there. It was not the channel tearing — she knew what that was and it was not that — it was the crack running cold because nothing was going through it, and the body deciding about itself while there was nothing to put in it, and the answer it had arrived at was no.

She was at the fourth stair on the fourth day when it happened, on her way up from the Hall's own door, and she had to sit down on the step.

## The Fourth Night

It came up out of her in the dark at about the second hour of the night and it came the way everything came now, without noise, in the middle of a body that was already occupied.

She lay on the mat and let it go all the way down and it took about nine seconds and then it was in her back as well, and then it was in the crack, and then it was nowhere in particular and she was seven and a half stages of Condensing and lying on a mat in a loft above a copying room with the lamp going in its cage on the fourth landing.

It was the fourth night of a fever. She was not going to be able to hold a conversation for about six hours. She had roughly that long to decide one thing.

She said it out loud into a dark room, which she did, in the ordinary voice, to nobody, with about eleven words in her.

“Third hour in the morning. And say it now.”

He had it. He had had it ready since the road, since the day before the stage that came before it, because he could see it coming in her and he had not said one word about it for five weeks.

“Say it.”

[Assembly Hall of Qinghe. Ning Yan of the Qinghe furnace crew, servant-born, entered upward through the Lantern Register of this county. What is carried past the sixth is let go. Condensing, stage seven, at Lowwater is held.]

It went out of the bound in every voice in nine counties at once.

There was a lamp burning on the fourth landing below her, and a keeper on the bench under it with four hundred and eleven volumes to copy an announcement into by the second hour. There was a girl of twenty-four in a wash house four miles down a road, and three washerwomen, and a man at a first desk in a square, and a county office on that square with a registrar in it who does not hurry.

Nine counties.

She had wanted four stages and she had four, and every one of them had cost somebody something, and this one had cost her two weeks of lying on her back.

“Did you have to,” she said.

“No,” said Yuan. “You could have held it and it would have gone nowhere and the county would have ruled the hole an absence and it would have been true and it would have cost you nothing and you would have been at six at the end of the season instead of seven.”

“I know that,” said Ning Yan. “I am not asking whether I had to. I am asking whether you are going to tell me in about a month that it was the wrong thing.”

“I am going to tell you in about a month what it cost,” said Yuan. “And it is going to cost two weeks and a stair, and that is what it is going to cost, and I have told you before the fever started and not once since, because you asked me once and once is the number.”

She lay on the mat and listened to it go round nine counties.

It was out. There was no version of any of this in which nine counties did not know at about the third hour of a morning in the last fortnight of a season that the licensed one at the water channel behind a well in Lowwater was at the seventh stage of Condensing, with a ceiling of nine in her own hand, and an annex on a furnace schedule in four counties, and a claim standing against her on the back of the same sheet.

“It is cold in here,” she said, at about the fourth hour.

“It is,” said Yuan.

“Go quiet.”

He went quiet.

## Two Weeks

They were two weeks and she was in the bed for eleven days of them.

The fever went up and came down and went up, and the crack ran cold the whole time, and there was a night in the middle of it — she was not sure which — when she sat up on the mat at about the third hour and could not remember whether the road had shut.

He told her. That is what he is for when she cannot ask anyone, and he did it in about eleven words and she said *all right* and lay down again.

Feng came up with a physician's instruction at the second hour on the sixth day and left it on the bench in the loft and went down without coming in, and it said that the member is to be carried and not to walk, and that the district will pay for a carrier, and that the member may decline the carrier.

She read it four times and then she sent it down to him at the third hour on the seventh day with a clerk's slate and her own left hand on the back of it, and what she had written on the back was the three words the forms allow for in that place.

She was carried down four flights by a man from the Hall's own door, which is not a county carrier and is not on the county's sheet, and which cost nothing because he owed a furnace crew a lamp in the thaw and she never mentioned it and neither did he.

## The Copies

He began copying her up at the second hour on the ninth day.

She was in the loft and she could hear it, which she had never been able to do in fourteen months of going up and down that tower, and it was a brush on a hard surface and it was very regular.

Two things about it.

The first was the sound of a fair hand working faster than she had ever heard him work, on a column that stood at five and had stood at five for twenty-two years and which now had a figure entered against it at the bottom in a hand that was hers, copied out in a hand that was his.

The second was that she could tell from the stair how many times he started a sheet.

He wrote about her for four days. Not about the announcement — the announcement is the layer's, and it goes everywhere, and a keeper does not copy it. About the furnace schedule, the irregular annex, the fifty-eight asked and the fifty-eight worked, the irregular stamp, the two marks on the ceiling at nine, the seven conditions.

Four days of it, in a fair hand, going up.

She lay in the loft and listened to it and did not go down.

“Do not go down,” said Yuan.

“You are not going to stop telling me what not to do.”

“I am going to stop in about four days,” said Yuan. “And you are going to want to go down about four times a day for the next two weeks, and you are not going to, because he has a fair hand and a fire and a hundredweight of coal and what he does not have is a single person in this county who is not asking him how he is.”

She lay and listened to it.

## The Stair

On the eleventh day the fever went off her.

It went in about an hour, the way water goes off a stone, and she was colder afterward than she had been during it, and she sat up on the edge of the mat at the second hour of an afternoon with the lamp on the fourth landing going and the whole of the tower underneath her.

She went down.

Four flights. She had gone up four flights every day for fourteen months, twice a day, with a crate in the thaw and a bundle in the summer, and she had gone up them at the ninth hour and come down them at the second and never once had anything to say about them.

She got to the second landing and she had to stop.

Not much. About four seconds. The kind of thing you would not mention to anybody.

She went to the copying floor and Feng was on the bench and he looked up and he did not ask her a single question, because he had worked that out on his own in September, and she sat down on the stool and put her hands in her lap.

He went on copying for about nine minutes.

Then she got up and went up four flights, and she stopped on the second landing for about four seconds, and she got to the top.

## Three Counts

She found out what it was about nine days later, at the furnace floor at Qinghe, on the third flight of the crew quarters, with four and a half hours of a settling shift done and nine men in a room with twelve bunks and her in it.

She went up the third flight and she had to stop at the top of it, and the stop was not four seconds.

It was three counts.

She stood in the dark in a room with ten sleepers and counted them, because she had been counting her own breath for two years and she could not stop doing it now that it had changed. One. Two. Three. Then it came.

Three, on a flight, at the top of it, eleven weeks after a fever.

It was eleven weeks since a rise she had gone up forty times. She had done two counts on that rise for fourteen months and had told nobody about it, and she had built two stages in the same fourteen months.

She stood at the top of the third flight in the dark and let it be the true number and then she went down to the bunk and lay in it.

That was the price. That was all of it, that was the whole of what the seventh stage cost in a body, and she had not been told about it and had not been asked and had said yes in a dark room on a mat at the second hour of a morning in the last fortnight of a season.

## What Was Left

The season's road was shut.

The lane behind a well had four weeks of it left in it and then it was a board with nobody at it and a frame with nothing chalked on it, and that was eleven days of a winter she had to get through before the thaw opened a cart day.

Her licence was in her coat against her ribs, in oilcloth, above the plate, and it was not nailed up, and the first one was still crooked over the foot of her own bunk four miles away going soft at the crease of a defect line she had written herself.

Nine men and her in twelve bunks. Ten sleepers.

A keeper four miles down the road with a hundredweight of coal in a tower, a chair at a county's second hour, and four days of a fair hand copying her upward for an office above this county.

A girl of twenty-four in a wash house with a leg set crooked and a quarter of a slab.

A man over a tanner's who had bought two sheets of paper in a year and had not bought anything today, and who had not needed to, because there was a fair hand in a tower four miles down a road that did the whole of it for him and would keep doing it for as long as that man was alive.

An eight hundred jin claim entered in four counties, travelling on the seventh condition of a relicensed licence, standing and unanswered.

And about three months of a voice in her head, of which she had said once, in the spring, that she would not ask him to stop, and had not asked, and was not going to.

She lay in the second bunk in a coal-smelling room with her arm out at the side and nine men asleep and a lamp at the foot of the stair, and the short breath went to three on the stairs and it did not come back to two, and she let that be a thing she had paid for and not a thing that had happened to her.
