# The Bill For One Question

She counted what the asking was going to cost on the twenty-second and twenty-third of the month beginning 1,604, item by item, in her head, in the ordinary voice, and the total came to more than she had and it did not stop her.

## How She Did It

She does it the way she has done it since she was eleven years old. In order, fast, inside her head, and then she says it out loud to the trough and adds up what she said.

That is the only instrument she has. She cannot go and ask anybody what a question costs and she is not going to try, and there is no one inside of twenty-two miles who would tell her the truth about it anyway.

So she did it on the rail at the post with the bar across her knees, at the sixth hour of the twenty-second, and she got through the whole of it in forty minutes, and then she did it again at the ninth hour of the twenty-third and got the same figures and one more.

## The Road

Eleven miles of strip road and eleven of made road, there and back, and a wait somewhere in the middle of it because the light goes at the eighth hour in that month and there is nothing between the gate and the wall.

Three days. She worked that out on the first pass and did not want it.

She can carry a bar nine feet nine for eleven miles on made ground at a mile an hour, which is what she did on the thirteenth of a month and which took her from the fourth hour to the seventh and left her an hour of light and not much more.

It is three days of not being on a road gang, and the road gang is two loaves a day at the rate a woman behind a counter says out loud, and that is six loaves she does not eat and does not earn.

And her arm is open four days in five and it does not ask her first, and she has got no idea at all how she is going to be at the end of the third day and she is not going to find out by planning it.

That is five of the bill and she counted it in under a minute.

## The Witnesses

This is the line she had never counted before and it took the other thirty-five minutes.

She cannot keep a place by staying in it and she never could, and there is not one person inside of twenty-two miles who is standing and waiting to hear what she says to a man who keeps a book nine hundred years deep. She has worked that out by the fifth time and it has not got easier and it is not going to.

So she went through the people she knows.

The keeper has just told her in the plainest words available that he does not do favours at a post, and he is right, and she is not going to put it to him again.

The boy of eleven is eleven years old and has been looked at by her twice in two years and has never asked her one question. He cannot be sent twenty-two miles and she would not do it and he would not go.

The carter of thirty-five has a load and a horse and a trough to get to, and he said seven sentences to her about a stone once and she is not going to be the person who asks a man with a load to walk to a wall on flat ground in the middle of the afternoon.

She got that far and stopped, and then she went back over it, because she has never in eight years been in a room where somebody else decided.

The only person left on this ground is a boy of fifteen on a chain at a gate, two miles short of the wall, and he is the last human being on the road.

And he is a keeper. He keeps a gate and a chain and a cup on a wire and he has never been asked for anything by anybody and he cannot walk to a wall in the middle of the day because somebody has to be at that chain when the next cart comes.

She wrote all that out in her head at the seventh hour of the twenty-second and then she sat on the rail and looked at it.

To have a person standing there she has to ask a person to stand there.

## The Line With No Mark

And there is one more, and it is the largest one, and she did not want to write it down on the twenty-second and she wrote it down on the twenty-third anyway because she is a person who finishes.

There is a name in this that is not hers.

She has carried a question about a brother for eight years and it is the only thing she has ever wanted out of any room that any man has ever kept her in.

There is no arrangement on this ground that gets her both. If she asks him what else is on it, then there will be a day when a name has to be said out loud in front of another person at a wall, because that is what a name on a list is and she is not going to find out otherwise.

She has decided she will not say it at the first asking.

Not at the second one either, if it can be helped, and she is going to have to go back twenty-two miles to find out whether it can.

That is a piece of the question she is choosing to leave shut, on purpose, and she has never in nineteen years chosen to leave anything shut on purpose. Everything she has not asked has been shut by somebody else.

## Twenty-Two Days

There is a line in the bill she cannot fill in and she put it in as a line.

On the twenty-second of a month that has gone he said twenty-two days to her at a jamb in daylight and then went eleven feet away and did not follow her into a room, and she has never once earned it, and she cannot make it a distance and she cannot make it an interval and she cannot make it anything at all.

So it goes on the bill as twenty-two days, unspent, of what she does not know, and it is the only entry on the whole of it that she cannot price.

That is what she did with it, and it is not nothing, because a blank you have written your name against is a different thing from a blank somebody else has left.

## The Line She Cannot Price

There is one more and she could not put a figure against it and she put it on the list anyway.

She has never asked a keeper for anything in eight years. Every room she has walked into in that time has had a thing in it with her name on it and a way of saying no, and she has said no in daylight in front of people, and being the person who says no in the room has been the whole of what she is.

If she says this out loud at a wall then she is a person who has asked, and there is no going back to being one that has not, and every room she stands in for the rest of her life will be a room she has already spoken in once.

She will still have the no. She is not giving the no up and she is not trading it for anything, and she wants that put down because the sentence she has to say out loud next to the asking is the one that makes her sound like it.

She counted that at nothing, because she does not know what it is worth, and then she wrote underneath it that it is not nothing.

## What The Count Came To

Here is the whole of the bill, item by item, as she said it out loud to the trough at the ninth hour of the twenty-third of the month beginning 1,604.

Three days of road and six loaves she does not earn. One arm that opens four days in five and a body that is lighter than it was on the eighth of a month and three stages past the end of what this ground holds.

One man with a paste pot who will not come and is right. One boy of eleven who cannot come. One carter with a load. One boy of fifteen on a chain, who can come two miles and not one mile more.

One name she is not going to say.

And one thing called twenty-two days that she has been carrying for a month and cannot use.

That is the bill and it is a large one and she has got nothing against it at all, and she sat on the rail and let that be true for about four minutes.

## The Fourth Hour Of The Twenty-Third

And it did not stop her, and she wants that written down with everything else, because she expected it to and it did not.

Here is what she worked out at the fourth hour of the twenty-third with the bar on her shoulder and one loaf and a half in the top of a bag.

A count is not a veto. She has been treating every count she has ever done as though the total had to come out under a figure or the thing would not happen, and she got the nine days right and the eleven sacks right and the four sentences right, and not one of those totals ever told her whether to do a thing.

The count tells her what it costs. It has never once in nineteen years told her whether it is worth it, and she has been carrying the wrong instrument for the whole of her life, and the reason is that nobody has ever let her use the other one.

She wanted it very much. That is what the count found. It did not find a reason.

She got up off the rail at the fourth hour of the twenty-third of the month beginning 1,604 and put the bar on her shoulder and went east on a strip road with one loaf and a half in a bag, and the nine miles to the turn was there at the eighth hour and she sat down on the bench and looked east and got up again, and she did not go and look up the road behind her one single time.
