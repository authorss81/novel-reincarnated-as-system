# Under The Sill

She walked four days east and came onto the flat at the dusk of the eighth of the month beginning 1,784, and a boy of fifteen was sitting at the gate with his back against the post and his legs out across the road, and he did not ask her one question about it, which is what that boy has done for five months and has never once done otherwise.

## Four Feet And Then Some

She has stood four feet into that opening twenty-two times in daylight on her own side of it with a bar on her shoulder, and she has never been past a man of nine hundred years.

The gate is two miles west of the wall and there is nothing between the gate and the wall to lie down on, and she crossed that in daylight at the second hour of the ninth with a carter four hundred yards out on the strip road who did not get down.

She stood at the west jamb at the fourth foot and she counted, because she counts, and the fourth foot is where she has stopped every time since the month beginning 1,604.

Then she went past it.

There was no bar, no gate and no chain, and the far side was empty. The plank table stands in the opening with nothing on it and there was a man of nine hundred years behind it on the far side, and he did not look up, and she went by on the east side of the table with her shoulder to a hand’s width of the wood.

She got ten feet and then ground under her feet, and she stood on it for a while with her heart going, because she has been on this side of a wall exactly once in her life and she was seven years old and she went through it and came back without being asked by anybody.

## A Hundred Yards Off

The window is a hundred yards off the east face and she could see it from the opening before she went through it, which means somebody standing in the opening could see it too, and there is a man who stands in that opening in daylight.

It has one pane in it.

That is all there is of the window and it is not a cottage window and it is not a shop window. It is a pane set in a frame twice the width of her head, and a sill under it washed so many times that the stone has gone back a finger’s width in the middle, and a roof over it that has not come down.

There is no light in it at the second hour of the ninth of the month beginning 1,784 and there was no light in it on the fourth of the month beginning 1,694 and there was none in it on any day she has been on this ground before, and she has now stood under it three times.

## Two Hours Under A Sill

She stood under it from the second hour to the fourth and she did not go in.

There is a door under the sill and the door is not shut, and she put her hand flat on it at the third hour and she did not open it, and she is not going to be the person who opens a door that nobody has asked her to open.

So she looked.

There is a room in there. There is a floor and there are two walls and a third wall at the back with something low against it, and there is a sill across the front of it and a length of sill on the left-hand wall, and there is a handle turned down on the inside of the door.

That is what she saw, and it is not very much, and she looked at it for two hours because she had nine days of road in her legs and nothing else to do with her eyes.

Then she stepped back to go and find the cut in the made road and she looked back at the window to make sure of where she was standing, and the room was still there, and the walls were still there, and she could not tell you in that moment whether the low thing at the back was a bench or a bed.

She stood in the grass and worked at it for nine minutes.

A bench. It was a bench.

She went back in her own head to the second hour and looked at the low thing again, and it could have been either, and there is no way to settle it, because the only record of what was in that room at the second hour of the ninth of the month beginning 1,784 is a girl standing under a sill, and a girl cannot keep what she finds by holding it.

That is the sixth stage. It has not stopped working since the second hour of the nineteenth and she had started to hope it was only about a rut in wet ground.

## The Boy Of Fifteen And The Gate

There is a boy at that gate from the fourth hour to the ninth and he has been there since the month beginning 1,604 and he has never once asked her for anything, and she worked out in the first ten seconds of the fourteenth what that is, because she is nineteen and it is the one thing on this ground she has not had to pay for.

He is there because somebody sends him and he does what he is sent to do and he is not paid for this part and he would not know what to do with a person who thanked him.

She said good to him and he said good back and that was all of it, and she has said good to that boy perhaps forty times in five months and it has never once cost either of them anything.

And then, because she had the length of the made road in front of her and a cut somewhere in it that she had never seen:

“How far is it to the crossing.”

He thought about it properly, which she had not expected.

“Two miles past where the road dips. There is a cut across it and a chain at the near end of the cut,” he said. “And he is there. He is there most days.”

“Who.”

“He keeps it.” The boy of fifteen said that the way he says everything, which is once and without decoration. “The one who keeps it was not there before. He came and he does it.”

She stood in the road with that and understood that a crossing on a made road has a keeper, and that a keeper is not an office and does not sit at a counter, and that whatever is done at a crossing is done by a man sitting in the open with nothing to write on.

She has asked four questions at a wall and got three terms and a day.

She did not ask the boy of fifteen a fifth thing on that road. She said good to him again and went east.

## The Four She Did Not Spend

She stood under the sill at the fourth hour with ten days in front of her and four questions standing in a book nine hundred years deep and not one of them answered.

She has never spent one of them anywhere except that opening.

That is the arrangement and she has kept it. One at a time. At the wall. In daylight. Forty-four miles and two days a question. He gave her those three terms at the seventh hour of a month beginning 1,604 and she has not broken one of them since the day she was given them.

And the truth underneath the terms, which she worked out on the second of a month beginning 1,694 and has not moved since, is that a question is not a thing you spend on a thing. It is a thing you spend on a book, and every one of the four has been standing in that book since the month beginning 1,604 and it is going to go into that book when it is asked and nowhere else.

So she could have asked one here. Under a sill, on the east side of the wall, in daylight, out loud. It would have gone into the same book.

She did not.

Here is the arithmetic, and it took her four minutes on the grass, and she got it right.

She has four questions and ten days and the wall is a quarter of a mile behind her and the list shuts on the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784, and after that there is no book anywhere in the world with a question in it that she is permitted to put into.

If she asks one here, it is one fewer at the wall.

That is all. There is no version where asking it here is cleverer. It is the same question in the same book and it costs the same and it comes out on the same day.

## What The Not-Spending Costs

And here is what it costs her, and she did not see it until she was standing at the window.

She cannot go to that opening with a question, because she has decided she will not take a fifth, and the four she has go into a book, and by the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784 that book is shut.

So what she takes to the wall is four questions, and he has told her four terms for questions, and there is nothing else in her hands.

She has a bar nine feet nine. She has a skin and a bag with a dish and a lump of clay and a ring a finger’s width and a stub of a nib and a cake of ink she has not used in two months and nine coppers she cannot count.

And there is a book of eight leaves made out of a blanket against her ribs, with a date and a place on the first leaf, four sentences on the second, and six leaves that are hers and that she has decided twice already not to write in.

She looked at that window for nine minutes, because it is the only thing on the far side of that wall that is hers to look at, and at the end of the nine minutes she got out a thing she did not expect.

She wanted to ask him about it.

Not one of the four. She wanted to walk four feet into that opening on the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784 and say two sentences about a room with a bench in it, out loud, in daylight, with a boy of fifteen behind her, and find out what a bench is for.

And she could not.

That is not one of his terms. That is hers. She made it on the second of the month beginning 1,694 at forty feet of lane with a loaf in her hand and she has kept it for ninety-six days, and she kept it because a woman of fifty handed her a place and not a question, and a place is the only thing anybody has ever been able to give her that she can still use.

She has nothing else.

She is nineteen, ten days from a wall shutting, and she is standing under a sill on the east side of it with nothing in her hands that is not a question, and she built that, and it was the right thing to do on the day she built it.

## Where She Went Instead

She left the window at the fifth hour and walked back to the opening and stood in it for a quarter of an hour, four feet in, on her own side, with the plank table in front of her and nothing on it.

Then she went back out and did not go west.

She went west, along the made road, past the gate and the boy of fifteen and nine miles of it, looking for the cut.
