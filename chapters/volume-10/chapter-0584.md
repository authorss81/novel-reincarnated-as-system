# A Name Held Past Its Day

It is not a field and it is not a room with a floor and a table in it.

It is a space with nothing in it that he can stand on and a thing in it that he can look at, and both of those are the first things he checks, because he has been inside the layer three times in his life and he starts every time by working out what kind of place he is in before he lets anything arrive.

There is no light in it that comes from anywhere and there is no shadow. That is not new.

What is in it this time is a closing.

## The Closing

It does not have a line on it. That is the first thing he looks for and it is not there, and the absence of it is the finding, because everything else on this ground that ends has a line in it.

There is a day. The nineteenth of the month beginning 1,784.

He put that day into the air himself, in daylight, in the open, with a boy of fifteen four feet off, because a girl had been standing in an opening for thirty-eight days saying nothing and he had run out of reasons to wait.

And it is not a line. It is a day that the whole practice agrees on, and nothing he can see agrees on anything else, and that is what nine hundred years of a man keeping one book correctly looks like from inside.

## The Question He Goes Looking For

He has one question and he has had it since the twenty-seventh of a month beginning 1,694, when a girl stood four feet into an opening and gave him four sentences about a house with a standing stone in the yard and he took them and did not put them down.

He comes here to answer it on the ninth of the month beginning 1,724 and he does not want to answer it on the ninth.

“Whether a name can be held open past the closing without leaving somebody on the wrong side of it.”

He looks for the answer the way he looked for the morning and did not find it.

## The First Night

He goes for the rule first, because there has to be one, and there is not.

There is no rule anywhere in this space that says what happens to a name after the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784. There has never been one, because a thing that has been kept for nine hundred years without anybody asking about it does not accumulate instructions.

What there is instead is practice, and practice is nine hundred years long, and it has one shape.

A name goes in. A name stays. And when the practice stops, the names go the way kept things go, which is to say accurately, and to a place that does not come with a name on it and does not come back.

He has never seen a name taken out. He has not seen one cross over. He has never seen a hand reach into nine hundred years and bring one back to the top, and he has had four hundred years in which to see it and he has not.

## The Second Night

So he goes for the exception.

He looks for a second door. He looks for it the way he looked for a second hand in this space at the fourth of a month beginning 1,634, and there is not one, and the reason there is not one is the simplest thing in the world.

A name is held by being written. To hold a name open past the closing, somebody has to keep writing it, because writing is the holding, and the writing is what stops.

He looks for another instrument on this ground that writes the way his book writes. He looks for four hours.

There is a board at a counter behind a mill. A carter with a board at a turn, four times in nine years, four names. A chalk on a string at a face, which writes weights.

None of them is it. None of them is anywhere near it. A board on a cart does not keep a person findable for a day and a half; it is not an instrument for this and it never was, and the girl would have been able to tell the difference from a girl of nineteen who reads boards.

## The Answer

It cannot.

He finds that out on the third night and it takes him four hours to stop looking for the other answer, because there is always another answer, and there is not one here.

A name cannot be held open past the closing by him. The closing is the end of the instrument. When the book shuts, whatever was held by it goes where kept things go, and that is accurate, and accuracy is everything a keeper has.

And the thing he is actually asking about is not his book, and he knows that, and he has known it since the twenty-seventh of a month beginning 1,694.

He has been the wrong side of a wall since the eighth of a month beginning 1,514, because a girl told him to open it and he opened it, and the opening is still open and he cannot come back across it.

He knows exactly what the wrong side is. It is not a punishment. It is a man standing in the place where a thing is visible and not in the place where it is done, and he has been there for two hundred and nineteen days, and he has had more to look at from there than he has had from the other side in his life.

## The Other Answer, Which Is Worse

So he goes looking for what else could hold it, because there is one thing on this ground that has held a name open for a girl for eight years and it is not a book.

It is her.

Not her memory. She has not said the name out loud where a person could hear it in eight years, and he knows that, and she has told him the shape of it by not telling him.

It is her walking. Twenty-two miles each way, twice a month, on a bar, with four fingers and a thumb and an arm open four days in five, on a body that has been lighter every single day since the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,544, to stand in daylight at a wall on her own side and not say anything.

That is what has been holding it open. There is no instrument. There is no clerk and no book and no board on a cart at a turn.

There is a nineteen-year-old with a bar, and the count of her walking is the only listing in the world for that name.

## What It Comes To

He sits down in a place with no floor, which he cannot do and does anyway, and he gets it in a few seconds and then he cannot put it down for the rest of the night.

A name can be held open past the closing.

It is held open by her, and only by her, and it costs her every mile of it.

And when the book shuts on the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784, the holding does not stop. It stops being a listing and it becomes a woman.

There is no second keeper for that trade. There is nobody else who will stand in daylight at a wall with a bar on a shoulder and a name shut behind her teeth.

He has looked. He goes and looks for four hours, because he knows what a second one would look like, and it would have to be somebody who cannot be paid and cannot be kept and cannot go home, and there is exactly one of those on nine days of road, and she is nineteen, and she is standing on the wrong side of her own entry as *lost*.

So the answer to what he came here to ask is no, in the only sense that matters.

A name cannot be held open past the closing without leaving somebody on the wrong side of it. It cannot be held open at all except by somebody who is left on the wrong side of it, permanently, by the holding.

## The Part He Is Not Going To Tell Her

He looks at the last of it for the rest of the night and there is a piece he is not going to put in front of her, and he is not leaving it out of kindness.

She has had a name shut behind her teeth for eight years, and she has been proud of it, and he knows she has been proud of it because people are proud of the things they have chosen and she has chosen that one since she was eleven.

He is not going to tell her that it never did anything.

A name she has not said is not kept anywhere. She worked that out herself on the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,634, sitting at the end of a bar in the flat with the light going off a face, and she has had three months with it since and she has not once said it out loud to herself.

He is not going to be the one who says it, because she already has it, and because a thing told to a person by a man she cannot pay and cannot keep and who cannot get across a wall is not a thing she can put anywhere.

What he is going to do instead is the one thing he has that she will take, because she cannot pay for it and cannot refuse it and cannot get it back once it is said.

He is going to tell her what it will cost, in daylight, where she can hear it, before she does the thing.

## What He Is Going To Do About It

He comes out at the seventeenth of the month beginning 1,724 with the answer and it is the plainest thing he has ever had and it is one sentence long.

He is going to put it in front of her.

Not in the space. In daylight, at the wall, on her own side, with a person hearing her say it, because he has learned everything he has for this from the way she stands and the only currency she has ever accepted from him is a thing said where it cannot be taken back.

He is going to tell her that if she says a name at that opening on the twenty-fifth of a month beginning 1,724, the name does not go anywhere. It does not go into nine hundred years. It goes into her, and it stays there, and on the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784 she will be the only record of it in the world and she will not be able to put it down.

And then he is going to stand on his own side of a plank table and let her do it anyway, which is the one thing he has not done in nine hundred years, and it is the only reason he comes out of this space at all.
