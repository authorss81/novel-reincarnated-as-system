# Eleven Feet Of Boards

She stood at the wall for the seventh and eighth hours of the thirteenth of the month beginning 1,604, on her own side of it, in daylight, with nobody there, and she did not ask.

## Eleven Miles From The Turn

She was off the bench at the fourth hour with the bar on her shoulder and the loaf in the bag and a skin that was down to a quarter, and she made eleven miles of made road by the seventh hour.

She is faster on that road than she is on the strip road and she has never had a reason to find out until now. A made road takes a load on the light end and the bar does not jump, and the arm opened at the eighth mile and shut again at the ninth, and she came over the flat two miles out with the wall standing up ahead of her in the middle of ground with nothing on it at all.

The wall is higher than she had it. It is higher than a person and it goes off east and west past the edges of what she could see, and there is one place in it where it is not.

## The Gate

The boy of fifteen was on the chain at the seventh hour and he put it down, the way he has done it nine times in two months, and asked the question.

She had answered it once, and the answer was two letters and she had got through it in about nine seconds.

This time she said what it meant.

“I am not going through,” Ning Yan said. “I am going to the wall and I am standing on this side of it for an hour.”

“All right,” the boy said, and put the chain up for a cart behind her, and then looked at her for a second longer than he usually does.

“Will you want the chain up after,” he said.

“No,” she said. “I will be walking back at the dark.”

That is the most the boy of fifteen has ever said to her in nine times at a gate and it took eleven seconds.

## The Wall In Daylight

The opening is four feet wide and it has been four feet wide for as long as he has been on this ground, and the jambs on both sides are dressed stone and they are not nine hundred years old.

The table stands in it on two trestles. It is a plank table five feet by two and there is nothing on it, and there has been nothing on it since the twenty-third of a month that has gone.

He was on his own side of it with his hands flat on the wood, the way he had put them for fifteen days when she was making him answer questions, and he has been putting them like that every hour since without being asked.

She has not been inside a wall for two months and she is not going to be inside one today, and she put her feet on the dressed stone at the west jamb and stopped there with her shoulder against nothing, because there was nothing to put it against.

She stood about four feet into the opening with the metalled road at her back and four feet of ground on the other side of him, and she did not go any further than that.

## She Does Not Say His Name

She was going to.

She had it in her mouth at the sixth hour of this walk and she had carried it for a mile and a half, and she did not say it, and the reason is a thing she worked out on the made road and had been holding all morning.

She said his name once, on the twenty-fifth of a month that has gone, at the ninth hour, in front of three people, and he answered it. One time is all a name is worth, and she had told herself that standing four feet off a ladder with nobody in her hearing, and there was no better reason on this ground to go back and take it back.

So she said a different thing.

“Is there water out here,” Ning Yan said.

He did not look up off the table.

“There is a cup on a wire at the gate,” he said.

Nine words. He has said nine words to her in a month and thirty in the three weeks before that, and she asked him a question about water on her second day of trying to ask and he told her where the water is.

She drank out of her own skin anyway because she had carried it nine miles and she was not going to leave a mouthful standing at a trough somebody else fills.

## Where Asking Is Done

Then she worked the rest of it out, standing on a dressed stone at the west jamb with the light going off the top of the wall.

Here is what asking is done at, and she had never been told and she got all of it in eleven minutes on the thirteenth of a month.

It is done at the wall, and not anywhere else. It is done on her side of the wall, and not inside it. It is done in daylight, and not at night, and not into the ground, and not along a made road at the eleventh mile to a man who cannot hear her.

And it is done in the open, in front of people, the way a man with a rag puts a name down in a flat book with three names in it that anybody coming up the road can look at while he is doing it.

That last one she had worked out at the ninth hour of the ninth of a month, sitting on a rail, and she had not known then that it was a rule and only that it was what she wanted.

The difficulty was standing there in daylight seven feet from a man with his hands flat on a table.

There was nobody.

Four hundred yards east there was a bar on two posts with nothing on it. Eleven miles west there was a gate with a boy of fifteen on a chain. The rest of it was flat ground in every direction and a wall, and the light going, and one person on this ground inside of twenty-two miles who was not standing where she was standing.

She had spent eight years making sure there was nobody in a room when she said no.

It turned out that was the whole of the difference between the two things.

## She Has To Go And Get A Person

She was angry about that for a quarter of an hour and it was the first clean thing she had felt in four days.

She had come out here on the four words and she had got everything else right — the daylight, the wall, her own side of it, a man to say it to — and the one thing that was missing was a second person.

There was no arrangement on this ground for producing one. She could not bring the keeper. She could not bring the boy of eleven, who is nine miles off and eleven years old and has never once asked her anything. She could not bring a carter who has a load and a horse and a trough to get to at a wall in the middle of flat ground at the seventh hour.

She stood there and worked out that the last thing she needed was the first thing that had to happen, and that it was twenty-two miles each way.

And then she did the thing she has been doing for eight years, which is to stand in the road and be the one who has gone without, and she was still standing there when she noticed she was doing it, and she stopped.

At the gate the boy had asked her whether she would want the chain up after. That was a question he asked a stranger in passing because there are not many things to say to a stranger at a gate.

She did not use it. She has been at a gate nine times and she has never once used anything anybody said to her there.

## Four Hundred Yards East

She looked east because it was the direction the light was not.

Four hundred yards out there is a bar on two posts with nothing on it, and she has gone past it eleven times in two months without once wanting to go through it, and the reason she has never wanted to is not in her anywhere and she has stopped trying to find it.

Past that, about a hundred yards off the wall and about half a mile east of the bar, there is a window.

She saw it in daylight at the seventh hour of the thirteenth of a month for the first time in her life, standing where asking is done, nine feet from a table with nothing on it.

It is the size of a hand. There is one pane in it and a frame around it and a sill, and there is a building round it with a roof that has not come down, and there is no light in it.

On the twelfth of a month that has gone she was eleven miles west of this and she did not know this building was here and has not asked and was not going to.

It did not change while she looked at it. That is not a small thing. She stood on a dressed stone at the west jamb of a wall with a man on the other side of a table and looked at it for about four minutes and it did not change.

## What She Is Standing There For

Here is what she had and what she did with it.

She had eleven miles of road behind her and a gate at the end of it with a boy on a chain who had offered to put the chain up after, and a wall, and daylight, and a man, and the four words.

She had been nine hundred yards and a wall from the finding and she had it in her ears twice, and she had said his name once and been answered, and she had read four sentences back out of a book of her own at an opening in a wall to a man who shut the book on it.

And at the seventh hour of the thirteenth of a month she stood four feet into an opening in a wall on her own side and had the four words ready and could not put them into the air, because there was no one on this ground inside of twenty-two miles who would have heard her say them.

She said them once anyway, to herself, at the eighth hour, in daylight, with her mouth barely open, and they did not go four hundred yards.

She did not say them twice.
