# Nothing To Lie Down On

She left the post at the second hour of the sixth of the month beginning 1,724 with a bar on her shoulder and nothing in the bag, and there is no bread anywhere within nine days of road, and she went twenty-two miles east for two hours of standing.

## Nine Miles Of Strip Road

She made the strip road between the second hour and the eighth and her right arm went open on the fourth mile and stayed open, and she put the bar over her left shoulder and walked nine miles of it one-handed, which is a thing she has done four times.

At the ninth hour she was two miles short of the gate with the made road in front of her and the bar on her left shoulder and her right arm hanging, and there is nothing between the gate and the wall to lie down on.

She lay down in the broken ground north of the made road for two hours and did not sleep, and counted, and counted again.

## The Made Road In The Night

Seven counts on made road at the third hour.

Not six. Seven, and it came back in five, and she wrote that in the dirt with a finger and looked at it and put her hand flat over it, because a number written in the dirt at the third hour is a number she can argue with and she did not want to argue with it at the third hour.

She came onto the flat at the seventh hour of the seventh of the month beginning 1,724 and there was a boy of fifteen on the chain at the gate, and she had worked out at the ninth mile that she was not going to ask him for anything this time.

## What He Said About It

“You have not been in four days,” the boy said.

“No.”

“You did not ask me this time,” the boy said. “You asked me last time and I came.”

“No,” she said, and then again, when he looked up the made road at her: “No.”

“All right,” the boy said, and hung the chain on the post and stood there.

That is all of it, and he did not ask her a question about the four days either, and she stood four feet off a boy of fifteen at a gate and worked out, in about nine seconds, that she has four days of not asking him and four days of him noticing, and that he has been keeping the count as well as she has, and has never once mentioned it.

## Two Miles And A Wall

She came onto the dressed stone at the seventh hour of the seventh of the month beginning 1,724 and she stopped four feet into the opening at the west jamb, the way she has stopped fifteen times.

The plank table was in the opening with nothing on it.

He was on his own side of it.

She stood there for two hours. She said nothing at either hour. He said nothing at either hour, and at the fortieth minute he took his hands off the plank and stood up straight, which took a while, and then he sat back down.

That is all of the seventh.

And she was aware, the entire time, that she had said a thing to him on the twenty-seventh of a month beginning 1,694 that she cannot take back, and that his hands had stayed on the plank, and that she had come back twice to find out whether the hands would move.

They did not move.

And on the way out she had worked out what she was frightened of, and it was not his hands. It was that she had come twice to find out whether the hands of a keeper would move, and she had gone forty-four miles each way to look at them, and she had spent a fortnight of stone on it, and there had been nothing on the board at the store when she got in.

## What The Last Four Standings Have Been

Eleven stands in thirty-eight days and four since the third of the month beginning 1,694.

Twelve is not a number. She wrote it down on the strip road at the fourth mile at the fourth hour of the eighth of the month beginning 1,724 and looked at it and wrote it down again under it, because a woman of sixty at a bench had told her that a keeping shuts on a day and does not care who is standing in front of it, and she wanted to see whether twelve was a day.

It is not a day. Nothing is a day. It is a girl with an arm open four days in five walking twenty-two miles in two nights to stand in a hole in a wall and not say anything, and the only thing it has produced since the third of a month beginning 1,694 is a pair of hands that will not move.

She has been doing it because she decided on the twenty-fifth of a month beginning 1,634 that it was the only method that had ever worked on a wall, and it is a method, and it worked on the twenty-ninth of a month beginning 1,664 and it worked on the third of this month.

It has worked twice in thirty-eight days and it costs forty-four miles a time.

## The Terms

She thought about them on the made road at the ninth hour of the seventh with most of it in front of her and she has thought about them every day since the twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,604 and they have not moved and are not going to.

One at a time. At the wall. In daylight.

There are four questions standing. She is not allowed a fifth. The first one has been standing for a hundred and three days and it is holding the other three.

And she had worked out at the gate at the seventh hour, standing four feet off a boy of fifteen, that there is no part of those terms that says how long she may stand, or how many days she may stand in a row, or what happens to a girl who stands in an opening for seven days on a body that has been lighter every day since the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,544.

The terms are about what she may ask.

They say nothing about what it takes out of her to be in the place where she may ask it.

## What She Was Afraid Of On The Eighth

She stood at the wall at the seventh hour of the eighth of the month beginning 1,724 and did not ask anything and did not say anything, and at the ninth hour she made herself do the sum, standing on dressed stone in daylight, and it took four minutes and it is the first time in a month she has been frightened of a number she made herself.

She has seventy-one days.

She has four questions and one a month at the best of it and every question costs forty-four miles and two days.

And she has worked out, sitting on a stone at the head of a lane on the twenty-ninth of a month beginning 1,694 and again here, that on the twenty-fifth of a month beginning 1,724 she is going to stand four feet into that opening and say a name out loud where a man of nine hundred years can hear her.

And to say it in daylight, and he is not going to write it, and it cannot be un-said.

And then she has to be able to get up and walk twenty-two miles west afterwards.

Here is the thing that made her hands go cold on dressed stone at the ninth hour of the eighth of the month beginning 1,724.

She does not know that her body will give her seven days.

She has never asked for seven days. She has never needed seven. She has walked forty-four miles in two nights on a third of a loaf and got in with nothing and got up, and she is nineteen and she has never once been unable to get up.

But standing is not walking. Walking has a place to stop. Standing in daylight is four feet into an opening with a person on the other side of a plank table and nothing whatever to do with her hands.

She looked at her own right hand with four fingers and a thumb on it and then at her left arm, which was open and was not going to shut because she asked it to.

## The Two Ways It Could Go

She made it a third of a column on the dirt of the broken ground at the third hour of the eighth of the month beginning 1,724, and it is the only forward arithmetic she has done in a month.

A name said at a wall goes into a book nine hundred years deep and a man who cannot be stopped writing her will write it, and she will be able to ask after it, and it will be true on the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784 whether or not it is true tomorrow.

Or a name said at a wall goes nowhere. It is two words in the air in daylight in front of people, and it cannot be un-said, and there is no record of it anywhere at all, and it buys her nothing.

She has had a hundred and four days to work out which of those two she is going to go and do, and what she worked out in the dirt at the third hour of the eighth of the month beginning 1,724 is that she does not know, and that not knowing is the first thing in a month she has been unable to put in a column.

Both of them cost her the standing.

That is the part that is not about the list at all and there is no part of it she can get round.

Seven days on dressed stone in daylight with four fingers and a thumb and an arm that is open four days in five, on a body that has been lighter every single day since the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,544.

She has been counting days for eight years and the days had always been a number of days.

She had seventy-one of them and she did not know that she had seventy-one days of standing, and those are not the same thing, and she had never once in her life confused them because she had never needed more than two at a time.
