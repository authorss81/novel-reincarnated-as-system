# The Bag Goes East Without Her

He stood up off the flat ground beside the post at the fifth hour of the twenty-eighth of a month beginning 1,574 and put the bag on his shoulder and went east, and she stood four feet off the ladder and let him go.

## The Twenty-Eighth

She had wanted it since the ninth hour of the day before. She had sat on the rail at the fourth hour of this morning with a whole day of not saying anything in her and she was still wanting it.

She got it.

The light was low and behind him when he went, so she saw the shape of the bag against the strip road before she saw the rest of him, and the shape of it under the cloth was nine inches wide and flat and it was the only object on nine days of road that is nine hundred years old.

He was gone past the trough before the keeper came out of his door.

## Getting Up Off The Flat

Getting up is a thing. He was on the ground on the low side of the post for a night and a day and he got his weight under him with one hand on the wood, and then he stood there four seconds to let a leg come.

He does not have a limp the way a man with a bad hip has a limp. His weight is on one leg and it is not going to be otherwise and he has had nine hundred years to get used to it.

The carter of thirty-five watched him do it and did not offer a hand and Ning Yan understood why. You do not offer a hand to a man of that age who has not asked for one, on a strip road, at the fifth hour, in front of people.

She wanted to offer one so badly her left arm came up about four inches out of her side.

## Nine Miles She Did Not Walk

She worked it out at the sixth hour, standing in the dust, and it is the only arithmetic she did in three days.

He came west out of that ground at the eleventh hour of a month and stood at a post at the ninth hour of the twenty-fifth and he told her his name was Shuyin, and she said his name out loud in front of three people, and he answered. That is three days and it is twenty-two miles and he is going back at the fifth hour of the twenty-eighth, which gives him the daylight and puts him there at the third or the fourth hour of the thirtieth.

There is nine miles of strip road between this post and a turn, and eleven miles of made road between that turn and the wall, and a gate on the made road with a boy of fifteen on a chain, and then the flat ground and the opening in the wall.

She could catch him in two hours. She is not as slow as him on open ground and she has nine hours of daylight in front of her.

She stood at the trough and she watched the back of a bag going east and she did not go.

## Why She Did Not Go

Three reasons and she had two of them at the sixth hour and the third at the ninth.

She has not asked him for anything in eight years and she was not going to walk nine miles of strip road beside a man of nine hundred years in order to start now at the fifth hour of the twenty-eighth of a month.

He would have to talk to her. She can see what happens to a person who is standing at a jamb with his hands on the edge of a plank, and she put him there for fifteen days, and she has no intention of doing it again on a road with carters on it.

There is nothing to carry east. His bag is his and it weighs what it weighs and she cannot take a thing into her own hands anyway and she cannot put one into anybody else’s, and she has known both of those things since she was fourteen.

And the third one she got at the ninth hour sitting on the rail with her hands hanging.

If she goes east now she does not come back to this side of the turn. And the whole of what she has is a bar and a skin and a bag and a stone with eight leaves on it nine miles behind her, and she has not eaten since the sixteenth and there is no bread on the made road east at all.

## The Wall Stays Open

There is a wall in the ground twenty-two miles east with a hole in it four feet wide and it is open, and it has been open since he opened it from the inside at her word on a day in the month beginning 1,564, and nothing in this world shuts a wall back up once it has been opened from the inside.

He is on the wrong side of it for good, and nothing in this world shuts a wall back up once it has been opened from the inside. He opened it going away from her and it did not go back, and the room is gone as a place he works.

He is going to walk into that ground at the third hour of the thirtieth and stand in an opening with a table in it and a door shut on eleven feet of boards on the other side of the jamb.

There is nobody in that room to let him in.

That is what she has done and it was not a mistake then and it is not one now, and she sat with it at the ninth hour for the length of a cup of water.

## The Post Is Hers To Stand At

And here is what changed, and she did not expect it, and it came at the tenth hour with the sun off the rail.

Nothing came east on the strip road all day.

No cart, no rider, no man with a notebook, no second carter with lime, no boy with a board. Eleven hours of flat ground going east to a bend a mile off and nothing on it came past that post except air.

By the second hour of the afternoon the only people on nine days of road were the keeper, the boy, and the carter when his load came round, and one woman on a bar.

There is not one person west of here who has come for her and there has not been one since the month beginning 1,544.

The post was a place a man had kept for nineteen years for three names, and a place where a man with a paste pot filled a board off a cart every nine or ten days, and a trough and a rail and a ladder and a flat stone with her own eight leaves on it.

It is not hers. She has no wages on it and she has no name on it and if she goes west to the turn tomorrow she goes to a turn with a bar on her shoulder and she does not come back to it.

But she can stand at it.

That is the whole of what she has and it is not nothing. She cannot keep a place by staying in it and she never could, and there are four hundred people on this ground who will put her somewhere for the night if she asks and not one of them would have asked her.

A place that nobody is keeping for her is the only kind she has ever been able to stand at, and for four days there have been five of them in it and now there are three.

She stood in it until the dark and she did not go and look up the strip road one single time, and that is the first thing she has been able to do with a want in four days.

## Three Names And Eight Leaves

She did it at the eleventh hour with the light off the board and the post door open and a man asleep behind it.

The keeper’s book is flat and the size of two hands and it has a string round it, and three names are in it in one hand going down the years, and it has been under the bottom of that ladder for nineteen years and the ladder goes over it forty times a day.

She untied the string.

The first name is a woman of the county and there is a day and a year against it. The second is a boy and it is written smaller than the first. The third is a man who was old when it went in and she does not know what old is on a page.

There are no words. There is no reason and no place and nothing said about any of the three except that a person came along a strip road and a man wrote them down on the day they passed, and that was the whole of the business and it was enough for nineteen years.

Her own book was lying an inch away from it with a date and a place on the first leaf and four sentences on the second.

She sat on the flat stone in the dark with both of them in her hands, the keeper’s in her left and hers in her right, and she put them down again the way she had found them, with the inch of stone between them.

There are three kinds of keeping on this ground and she has now been in the middle of all three.

A man with a rag keeps three names and never asks. A man of nine hundred years keeps every one and asks nothing and answers once. And she has a book of her own with four sentences on a leaf that says why anything is kept at all, and she made it out of a blanket out of her own coat lining and it is on a stone because she could not think of anywhere else to put it.

The first one does not know what is in hers. The second one knows what is in his. And she has been left holding the only one of the three that says what it is for.

## What Is East Of Here Now

At the second hour she sat down on the rail on the low side with her back to the post and she got the count out again and it came to the same thing it has come to for four months, and this time she was looking at it with something in her that was not there on the morning before.

Nothing is coming to her and everything she has is carried.

And there is one direction in the world with anything in it. A bag went east out of this post at the fifth hour of the twenty-eighth of a month with a nine-inch book in it, and it is twenty-two miles off by now and closing, and the only person on this ground who has ever written her name down in anything is walking toward it.

She does not know what she will say to him when she gets there.

She knows the road, because she has walked every mile of it, and she knows the strip road goes out of that ground and not back into it, and she is going to have to do it on foot because there is nobody out there who will carry a girl with a bar for nothing and nobody who will carry her for a rate she can pay.

She looked east at the dark end of it for a quarter of an hour and she went to sleep on the ground at the foot of the post with the bar under her head, which is the second night she has spent on a flat stone in nine months, and there is nowhere else.
