# What A Given Place Costs

She came onto the strip road at the fourth hour of the twenty-third of the month beginning 1,694 and got to the post at the ninth hour, and she sat down on the flat stone at the foot of the ladder at the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth and did not get up for two days.

## What She Is Carrying East And West

The bar is nine feet nine. The skin is a skin. The bag has a dish and a lump of clay in it and a ring a finger wide and a stub of a nib and a cake of ink that she has not used in two months.

Against her ribs, on the side where the paper is, there is a book of eight leaves made out of a blanket, and there are two things against her ribs now and she has not called either of them anything.

Nine days on a face and nothing on a board. Four miles of road walked to a shut door and then walked past.

And eighty-five days.

## The Column

She made it at the seventh hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 1,694, in the dust in front of the flat stone, with a finger, the way she has made every column since she was eleven years old.

Four lines. One for each of the four questions standing in a book nine hundred years deep.

What else is on it. Asked on the twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,604. Not answered.

What it is for. Asked on the thirtieth of the same month, alone, with nobody in the hearing. Not answered.

What the word does. Four letters at the end of her own entry. Asked on the fifteenth of a month beginning 1,634. Not answered.

Will you tell me when. Asked on the twenty-first of a month beginning 1,634. Answered *there is a day*.

And then, because she has eighty-five days and nothing to do with them, a fifth line, which she wrote in different dust.

The pane of glass a hundred yards off the wall.

## What The Column Told Her

It told her in about nine minutes what she had been refusing to arrange for sixty-nine days.

She has not been allowed a fifth, and she knows why, and the why has not changed since the twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,604, and she is not going to set it out again in the dust to feel it.

She has been reading the column as a queue. It is not a queue.

If she had the choosing of it she would spend the first line next, and then the second, and the second is the one that says what all of it is for, and a person who has been written as lost on a thing she has never been told the purpose of is a person who is owed the purpose before anything else.

She cannot have the choosing of it. One at a time, at the wall, in daylight, and the one that moves is the first one, and the first one has been standing for eighty-nine days.

And there is a fifth thing the column told her that she had not put to it.

There are four lines and eighty-five days and one at a time. That is three questions at the very best, with a month between each, and there is no arrangement by which the fourth one gets asked at all before the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784, and she had never done that sum.

She had been carrying four questions as though four questions were four things she owned.

## The Fifth Line

And here is what she spent two days on, and it is the only thing in a month that she did not know on the twenty-second.

She had believed for seven weeks that the four questions about the pane of glass were outside all of this.

They are not outside it. That is what the cottage taught her and it took her two days and most of a night to get it out properly.

A pane of glass, a sill, a roof that has not come down, a hundred yards off a wall on the east side. It is not at the wall. It is not in daylight of a question. Nobody in the world is going to answer her about it.

And it is a hundred yards off a road.

The road is nine miles of strip, then the made road, then two past a gate. Forty-four miles and two days to stand at the end of it, and then a hundred yards, and then whatever is behind the glass.

She cannot ask it. She may not ask it. She may not go and look at it in any month she has.

And she had been carrying it since the fifteenth of a month beginning 1,604 as four questions that cost nothing but a walk.

## Why The Cottage Proved It

She went through the two days twice and she can put all of it in four sentences.

The cottage is a place where a person was. The glass is a place where a light was.

A thing somebody tells you about is a thing that happened. Everything anybody has ever told her about her brother is a thing that happened, and every one of those things is in the past, and the present tense of it is two words a man of nine hundred years said on the twentieth of the month beginning 1,604, which is there is a day, and that was about the closing and was not about a boy.

There is not one thing in nine hundred years, in a mill, a cottage, a lane mouth, or on a made road in a month, that is in the present tense about him.

She had been treating them all as directions. They are all records.

## What Being Given A Place Did To It

Here is the sentence and she got it lying on her side on the stones at the foot of the ladder at the second hour of the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,694, in the dark, with her arm open.

A woman of fifty at the end of a lane handed her a house with a shut door, and it cost nothing, and it was the only direction she had in eight years.

And it showed her the shape of the other thing.

A thing somebody tells her about is a place where the thing was. The cottage is where a boy was. The glass is where a light was.

And the moment she knows that shape, every one of the four questions she has been saving for the window becomes a question she has to spend a trip on, and there are eighty-five days, and the trips are two days each and they go east while the only direction anybody has ever given her goes west.

She has not lost the four questions. She still has them. They are hers and she has not spent one.

She has lost the days they would have cost, and she did not know she was spending them until a woman gave her somewhere cheaper to stand.

## The Two Days On The Stone

She is not going to describe the second of those two days, because nothing happened in it, and that is the finding and not a gap.

She sat on the flat stone at the foot of the ladder from the seventh hour of the twenty-fourth to the seventh hour of the twenty-fifth with her bag on her knees and the bar on the ground, and she did the column, and she went over the four questions, and she did the arithmetic on the distance to the turn and the distance to the gate, and she slept for three hours in the middle of it.

And at some point in the middle of the twenty-fifth she noticed that she had done the column three times and could not remember doing any of them.

That is the part she wants in. She did not write down anything she had not already written. She did not get anywhere in two days. She has not done that since she was a child and she has never once let herself do it, because a person who lets a day go empty is a person who has to admit that the days are not all the same size.

She made them the same size. She did the column three times and slept three hours and ate a quarter of a loaf and it is a day, and it is like every other day, and it was not one.

## What Is Left

She got up at the seventh hour of the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 1,694 and put her hand flat on the flat stone, and it was bare, and it has been bare since she took her own book off it with her own two hands.

And then she did the thing she has done every day since the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,634, which is to check that the lever is still where she left it.

Asking twice. One asking, put in twice, so that both go in the book and neither gets answered, and he gets to the first one sooner or he never gets to it at all.

It is the only thing in the world that would make him come to the first question faster. It is the only thing in the world that would make the first question never answered.

She is carrying it on purpose and she has been carrying it on purpose for a month, and it is everything she has that is not a question, and she looked east down nine miles of strip road in the daylight and did not pull it.
