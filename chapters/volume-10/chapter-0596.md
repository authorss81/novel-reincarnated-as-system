# The Shut Book

She was standing on the flat outside the jamb on her own side when he did it, in daylight, at the ninth hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,784, and she was not asked, and nobody on nine days of road knew it was going to happen at that hour.

## What Came Out Of The Bag

There is a plank table standing in the opening with nothing on it, and at the ninth hour a man of nine hundred years went behind it and came back with a bag.

She has stood four feet into that opening twenty-three times and the bag has been behind him every one of them, eleven feet of boards shut on a door behind that, and she had never once asked what was in it.

It was a book.

That is all of the description and she will not improve on it. It is nine hundred years deep and it is bound in something that is not cloth and not leather and it is four inches thick and it is bigger than a closed hand and smaller than both of her forearms laid together.

It has been carried in a bag. The corners of it are soft.

## How He Put It Down

He put it in the middle of the plank table and he put it down flat, and he took his hands off it.

He did not open it. She was watching his hands from four feet away and he did not open it, and there was no page and there was no ink and there was no brush, and there was nothing on the table on either side of it.

She has stood there for a hundred and seventy-six days waiting for a page to be turned and she has never once got one, and on the day the book shut she was twenty feet from it and she did not get one either.

He put his left hand flat on the cover.

Then he shut it.

## What Shutting Was

It took him four seconds and she counted them.

He did not press down. He did not drive a strap over it. He did not say anything while he did it and she was close enough to hear him breathing and the breathing did not change.

It is a book and he shut it, and that is all of it, and nine hundred years of a practice comes down to a man putting his hand on a cover and taking it off.

She watched his hands stay on the cover for a count of thirty.

And then he took them off and put them down at his sides, and he looked past her at the opening and out at the made road, and that was the end of it and nothing on that ground changed while he was doing it.

## What Was Not Said Out Loud

He said one more thing, and it was not out loud, and she heard it the way she hears everything he says.

“He is not going to stop it and I did not ask him to.”

That is all he has ever given her about a thing he could have had an opinion on, and she did not answer it, and she would not have been able to say afterward whether it was a thing he had said that morning or a thing she had been carrying for six months.

She has never once put a question to him that was not about a book or a road or a day, and there is a question she has had since the month beginning 1,574 and has never put, and she did not put it today with a shut book standing six feet off her shoulder.

That is all of him. Nine years. He counts with her, he has read out every figure she has ever been given, he has got a number wrong once and corrected it in her head before she had finished counting, and she has never asked him where he learned to count.

## The Boy Of Fifteen

The boy of fifteen stood at the west jamb through all of it with his eyes on his boots and he did not look at the book once.

She has taken him to that opening four times now and she asked him each time, and she asked him this time at the gate on the eighteenth without saying why, and he came without asking one question about it.

When the book was shut he said, “Is it over.”

“Yes.”

He nodded and said nothing else for another hour, and that is the only conversation that boy and she have ever had about a thing finishing, and she is going to be able to remember it for the rest of her life.

## The Numbers She Wrote That Day

She wrote three numbers in the dust on the flat at the fourth foot of the opening and she did not rub any of them out, which has not happened before.

Six. One at a time. At the wall. In daylight.

And underneath, smaller, in the same hand: four, at the chain, on the twelfth of the month beginning 1,784.

She looked at them for a while. There is no column anywhere in the world those four numbers belong in, and she has been putting numbers in columns since she was eight years old. This is the first time in eleven years that she has written something down knowing that no one will ever add anything to it.

She left them on the flat and they were still there on the twentieth of the month beginning 1,784 when she came back, because nothing sweeps a flat at the west jamb of an opening in a wall, and there is a wind off the made road at some hours of the day that takes dust and some that does not.

## What Did Not Happen

Here is what she watched for, and she watched for it the whole time, and she is putting down that she watched.

No sound on the other side of the wall. There is nothing to lie down on between the gate and the wall. She has been lying in broken ground north of the made road for nine months and she knows exactly what this ground does when a thing finishes.

Nothing came. No man, no cart, no shouting, no light in the window a hundred yards east, no change in the air. The carter two miles west of the gate went on with his day.

And the book sat on a plank table in daylight with a girl four feet off it and a boy of fifteen standing at the west jamb looking at his boots.

That is what closing a list that has been open for nine hundred years looks like from four feet away.

She got nothing for it. She had gone out on the second of a month beginning 1,754 and worked a face for nothing and got nothing and put a nib to a blank leaf and put it away, and she had said a name into an empty yard on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,724 and got nothing, and this is the biggest thing that has ever happened to her on this ground and it came out as a man shutting a book.

## That He Did Not Ask Her

That is the part she has not put down.

One at a time. At the wall. In daylight. Forty-four miles and two days a question.

Those were his terms and she has kept all three of them for one hundred and seventy-six days and she has never once broken one and nobody on nine days of road could have made her break one.

And he did not ask her whether she wanted to watch. He did not ask her whether she had anything else. He did not ask her whether she was finished, and he did not say *there is a day* again, and he did not look at her once between the bag and the cover.

He has kept a rule for nine hundred years. Nobody has ever stood at that opening and asked him a question about what he keeps, and a girl asked one at the seventh hour this morning and he gave her the smallest thing on it and he did not ask her one single thing back.

He shut it because he shuts it, and he shut it a day early in the light because he is a man who does a thing when it is time to do it.

She had gone out and said there is no list anywhere in the world that has his name in it where anybody can ask after him, and she had gone out and said *she is going to find him herself*, and he had heard both of those.

## What He Said After

He said one sentence, to himself as much as to her, in the ordinary voice, and then he picked the bag up off the floor and put the book into it.

“That is shut.”

That is all he said. He did not say where it went. He did not say what *lost* does. He did not say what the word at the end of her own line is for, and she did not ask, and there is no book left in the world she is permitted to put that question into.

She stood there for a quarter of an hour.

## What She Did Not Do

She did not ask him to leave it open.

That is the thing she will have to be able to say for the rest of her life and she wants it written in the order it happened in.

He did not ask her. She did not ask him. There was no asking at any point in the ninth hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,784 and both of them let that be so, and a shut book does not open again because somebody stood next to it and wanted it to.

She did not say thank you to anyone. She had said it once in this year, to this man, in the month beginning 1,694, for four sentences about a house, and it bought nothing and she did not expect it to buy anything, and she was not going to say it to him for shutting a book.

She went and stood on the flat for another hour and twenty minutes after he took the bag back behind the door on those boards, and the opening held a plank table and the light coming in over the eastern jamb.

It cannot be un-shut. That is all of it, and there is nobody to be sorry for it, because nobody did anything wrong.

## The Twentieth Of The Month Beginning 1,784

She came back at the seventh hour on the twentieth because there was nowhere else on this ground to go at seven in the morning.

The opening was open and there was a man of nine hundred years behind the plank table doing what he has done since the month beginning 1,574, which is a thing with his hands that she has never been close enough to see, and there was nothing on the table.

She stood four feet in for two hours and he did not say one word and she did not say one word.

And then she said the only thing there was left to say on that ground, out loud, in daylight, on her own side, to a man who cannot be thanked.

“There is a window a hundred yards east of your wall,” she said. “There is one pane in it and there is a bench in the room behind it and I have not been in. It came from me at the fifth hour of the seventeenth and it is not in a book, so it is only in two places now, and one of them is me.”

He said: “I know the one you mean.”

That is the second time he has said it and he has not asked her a single question about it and he will not, and the two words are the entire of what a nine-hundred-year-old man ever gave back to a girl who gave him something of her own.

Then she went west, because a man of nine hundred years was going to close that door and walk to the turn with her, and she was not going to let him do that alone on a road that has nobody on it.
