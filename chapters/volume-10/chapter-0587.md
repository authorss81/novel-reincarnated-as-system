# Counting From The Wrong End

She was nine miles from the post at the seventh hour of the eighteenth of a month and she had nine feet nine on her shoulder and no reason at all to be walking west, and she went west.

## Nine Miles With A Bar

Nine miles of strip road with a bar is a day’s walk and most of another one, and she did it in a day because there was nothing behind her that would have made a second day necessary.

Her right arm was open. She had written a two on the flat stone that morning and the two was correct and she had carried nine miles of it.

Four counts of breath, and seven at the third hour on the rise, and four again on the flat, and she wrote the seven in the dust at the side of the road with a finger and did not rub it out because nobody would come along that road and look at it.

She was lighter at the ninth hour than she had been at the seventh. She has been lighter every single day since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 without one day of it going back, and there is nothing she can do about it and she stopped being able to do anything about it somewhere around the fourth month.

## The Count Stops Adding Up

Here is the thing that went wrong on that road, and she found it out at the second hour without meaning to.

She counts. She has counted since she was eight years old and there has not been a day in ten years that she did not count something in, and the count is the only instrument she owns that nobody can take off her.

Every number she has written since the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,724 has been counted from that day.

Fifty-four on the twenty-fifth, at the wall, with the name in the air. Fifty-one on the twenty-eight, at the foot of a ladder. Forty-seven on the second of a month. Forty-one on the eighth, on the flat stone. Thirty-one on the eighteenth, with the bar on her shoulder.

That is five numbers, and they run backwards, and every one of them was a count of the same thing: days left between this morning and the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784.

She wrote the next one at the second hour of the eighteenth, in the dust at the side of the road, and it came out as thirty, and she wrote it and looked at it.

Then she stood in the road with a bar on her shoulder and worked out what was wrong with it, and it took her the length of a hundred paces to get there.

The number was right. She was thirty-one days from the closing and she was walking west and the closing is east.

The number is the same either way. That is what a count is. A count does not know which way you are going and neither does anything else it has ever counted.

She had been running a subtraction in one direction for a month on a body that was walking in the other, and the reason she had not seen it was that she had not been going anywhere.

## The Four Things She Did Not Do

There is a road east from that stretch, back to the post, and she was two miles onto it before she noticed that she had turned off it on purpose.

She stood in the strip of ground between the two and thought about the four things she had not done on that road, and she has been thinking about them since.

She did not go back and stand on the flat stone at the foot of the ladder. There is nothing under that stone but a groove worn by a keeper’s boot going up and down for nineteen years, and she put her own book on it once and took it off it with her own two hands, and nothing goes back on it.

She did not go out to the face and put her bar up on the stone and ask the man with the chalk for the rate, because the board goes down on the second of a month and the second of a month beginning 1,784 is the twenty-fourth of this one, and she cannot be at a face four miles west of a turn and at a wall thirteen miles east of it on the same morning, and she worked that out thirty-three days ago and it has not improved.

She did not take the book of eight leaves out of her coat. There is a date and a place on the first leaf and four sentences on the second and six leaves that are hers and that she has decided not to write in, and she has decided twice now and she is not going to decide a third time on a road.

And she did not say the name out loud again.

That last one is not a thing she had to stop herself from. It costs nothing. She has said it nine times in her own head since the twenty-fifth and it is the only sentence in her life she can put down and pick up as often as she likes, and she has not said it aloud since the yard, and she noticed she was on the edge of it twice in four miles, and she did not.

## What That Is Worth

She rubbed the thirty-one out of the dust with her heel, because there was no point in a number lying in the road four miles from a post where a boy at that post would come along it in nine days and put his foot in it.

Then she stood there and got the second half of it, and this one took longer, and it is the thing she actually took west with her.

For a month the count was the only thing in her life with a direction in it.

She could not eat. She could not be paid. She could not be given a place by anybody and she had said so out loud to a man of nine hundred years and been right. She had no wages, no shed, no road, and one bar and a bag with a dish in it and a lump of clay and a ring a finger’s width.

And she had thirty-one days, and the days were the only clock she had, and the clock had been pointing east in a hand she could read without looking at it.

Take the clock away and there is a girl of nineteen on a road with a bar, and nothing in her that points anywhere.

That is what the counting was standing on top of. It was never a plan. It was a direction with numbers in it.

## The Turn At Dusk

She came onto the turn at the ninth hour with her arm hanging and her boots gone and the light going off the stones.

The bench is on the store road at the turn and the woman of sixty was on it, in the same place she has been on for three months, with a needle and a length of thread and a string of beads she had not finished.

She looked up. She said nothing.

That is what the woman of sixty does. She has four sentences in three months and she has used all of them on other days, and there is nothing left in her for a girl with a bar who has walked nine miles to stand in front of her.

Ning Yan did not ask her anything. There was a bench and a woman and a stretch of road and thirty-one days, and every direction ever handed to her pointed west.

Four hundred yards east of the turn there was a bar on two posts with a board nailed to it, and the board was bare, and there was a trough and a rail and nothing else on that side of the road that she had ever been told about.

She stood on the turn for nine minutes. Then she went and put her bag down under the bench and lay along the bench with her boots off and her bar across her and slept for two hours and forty minutes, which is what she has been getting on open ground since the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,724.

Nine years of people come to this turn and stop at it. That is what the turn is for and it is not a gate and nobody has ever posted anything at it, and a woman of sixty has sat on a bench at the side of it long enough to have seen most of them do it.

She has never once asked herself why. She got it out of a woman at the bench in the third month of this year and it has sat at the back of her head since, and the thing it means is that a road which takes people somewhere and a road which brings them back are not the same road, and the difference is about two hundred yards of ground on the east side of a bend in this one.

She came here because it was nine miles away and because she had never once in eight years had a reason to stand on it.

## What She Decided About Morning

She came out of it at the second hour of the nineteenth with the cold coming up out of the ground and her arm worse than it had been and thirty days in front of her.

Here is the decision, and it took her no time at all, and she has thought about it every day since and it has not got worse.

She is not going to count from the twenty-fifth any more.

Not because the count was wrong. The count was never wrong. A number that says thirty-one days to the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784 is true whether she is walking toward it or away from it or sitting still.

She is stopping counting from it because a number that comes out of a day is a way of looking backwards, and she has been looking backwards for thirty days on a body that has not stopped moving.

From the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,754 she counts from the nineteenth.

One. Today. And then whatever tomorrow turns out to be, and she will not know what it is until she is standing in it.

## What Was In Front Of Her

She got up and picked up the bar and went four hundred yards east of the turn to where the bar on two posts stands over the strip road, because it was the only thing on that ground she had never looked at, and she had been walking past it for thirty days.

The light was coming up behind her. There was dew on the ground in the low line where the road dips, and the low line ran east off the strip road and out into ground that has never had a wheel on it.

And in the low line, where the dew was lying, there was the shape of something.

She stood at the bar on two posts at the second hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,754 with nine feet nine on her shoulder and looked at it for a long time without moving, and she worked out in nine seconds that it was wheel-marks, and they were healed over almost flat, and they were not nine days old or nine months old.

They were older than the lime at the cottage.

They went east.
