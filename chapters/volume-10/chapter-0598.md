# Past The Opening

She left the turn at the second hour of the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,784 with a bar on her shoulder and a direction and a length and nothing else.

## East Without A Rut

Eleven miles across open country. A quarter of a mile past the opening.

That is all she had, and she had been carrying it for eight days and it had gone out of her the way everything goes out of her, and on the morning of the twenty-fifth she stood on the stones at the turn and got all of it out of her head and wrote it on her own wrist with a finger and dust and said it out loud once.

Eleven miles. A quarter of a mile past.

Then she went east.

She walked two days to get to the gate and she went at forty degrees off the strip road and she was wrong by a mile and a half at the first day and she corrected on the second by the sun and a ridge, because a girl who has walked nine miles of strip road for a year can walk a bearing.

She did not find the line. It had gone. It had gone the way a rut in wet ground goes and she had six days of evidence of that going out of her own mind and she was not going to look for it.

She found the gate road at the seventh hour of the twenty-sixth because she had told herself a distance and a quarter and the ground came up flat in front of her where it should not have, and there was a boy of fifteen sitting at it with his legs out across the road.

He looked up when she came off the ground at an angle.

He said: “You are not coming off that road.”

“No.”

“You have been on it before,” he said. “I have never seen anybody come off it.”

That is the whole conversation she had with the boy of fifteen on the gate on the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 1,784, and she has been back over that sentence more times than anything else that boy has ever said to her.

## Past The Gate

Two miles with nothing to lie down on and she did it at the second hour of the twenty-seventh and got to the flat at the fifth hour.

The opening was open. There was a plank table in it with nothing on it and there was no man of nine hundred years behind it and she understood where he was and did not go and find out.

She stood four feet in for about a minute and then went out the far side of the jamb.

Nobody stopped her. There was nothing on that ground to stop her with.

## The Ground Beyond It

It is a long way from the flat to where the line came out and she did it in three days, and there is nothing beyond that flat on this ground that anybody has ever described, because nobody has ever walked it.

There is a road. That is the first thing, and it is not the road she knows.

It is narrower and it is well made and it goes on past the place where she would have stopped looking.

Along it there are barrows.

Not a cart-load. Rows of them, cut into the ground and filled and built up, in long lines, with a stone at the head of each, and the lines run east for as far as she could see on the third day.

There were people at the barrows. She counted nineteen on the second afternoon from the top of the high ground and she stopped counting and started watching, and she watched for two hours.

They were working. There is no other word for it and it is not a punishment and there is no man standing over any of them, and the work is done at a pace people choose and it is slow and they stop sometimes and sit down.

And that is all there is to it, and she had crossed a long way to see it, and it looks like a field.

## Him

She saw him on the fourth day at the ninth hour, a couple of miles out, at a barrow on the first of a month, and she knew him before he turned round because of the way he stood, which is the way her own father stood, and she has not said that word to herself since she was eleven years old.

He is nineteen years old and he has been in that ground nine years and he has a coat that has been mended at the elbow, and he is ninth barrow down.

He turned round.

She stood thirty feet off with a bar on her shoulder and she did not move for a long time, and she has counted that too, and it was a hundred and forty seconds.

Then she said it out loud, on open ground, in daylight, a long way east of a turn with nobody else within four hundred yards.

“Ning Wu.”

He did not come across the ground.

## What He Said

He said: “You are shorter.”

That is the first thing he said to her in nine years and it is true and she has been five inches taller than she was at eleven and she did not have an answer ready and she did not need one.

She said: “Yes.”

And then nothing for a while. A man came past two barrows off and looked at the two of them and went on, which is the first thing about this ground she understood on the fourth day: not one person here comes and looks at anything, because there is no one here to look.

He was not surprised.

That is the part she was not ready for and she had four days of it after. He was not surprised and he was not glad and he was not afraid. He asked her one question.

“How did you get through.”

“West. On foot. There is a gate and there is a bar across the door and a man of nine hundred years behind the table and he shut the book on the nineteenth and I walked out.”

He looked at that for a second and then he said: “Did he ask you anything.”

“One thing. I asked him one thing.”

“What did you ask.”

“I asked him what else was on it,” she said. “And he gave me a number, and it was six, and it was not your name.”

## What She Found

Here is what she found, and she is going to put it down plainly because she is a person who counts and there is nothing else available.

She found her brother alive, in person, in her hearing, on the first of a month. Once. She is not going to get a second one.

She found him at nineteen and she found him working barrows at a pace he chooses, and she found that he has been fed for nine years and that he has hands like her father had and that he does not look like a person who has been starved and does not look like a person who has been kept in a room.

She found that he does not know what a book is.

He did not know what a list was. He did not know what *lost* means. He had a number of six said to him on open ground by his sister and it went into his head the way a number goes into anybody’s head, and he has never in his life asked where numbers come from and he is not going to start.

And she found that he has not said one word about the nine years. Not one. He has not asked her a single question about where she was or what she did or who wrote what, and she has had four days of standing on that ground with him and waiting for it and it has not come.

He did not say her name either. He has not called her anything since *you are shorter*, and she said her own name into an empty yard a month ago and it cost nothing, and it cost nothing to say it here as well.

## The Four Days And What She Did Not Say In Them

She stayed four days and a night.

She had no wages and no one to work for and a brother in a barrow ground, and she slept on a bank four feet from him for three nights and she did not once ask him for anything, and he did not once ask her for anything, and there was not one arrangement made between them in four days.

Here is the list of what she did not say and she has gone over it since and she is putting all of it down.

She did not say sorry. She has had a word for it since she was old enough to have one and she has not used it on anybody in nine years and she was not going to start with a boy working a barrow.

She did not say that she had spent four days of walking and a number of questions on finding him.

She did not say thank you, and she does not thank people on this ground, and the one time she has said it in nine years it was to a man of nine hundred years for four sentences and it bought nothing.

She did not say that she would get him out, because she cannot get anybody out of anything, and she said the truth about that to a woman of fifty at a fire at the end of a lane on the fourth of the month beginning 1,694 and the woman put a loaf in her hand and did not say it back.

And she did not say her mother’s name, which is in her mouth every day of her life and which she has not said out loud since she was eleven years old, and she had it ready on the second night sitting on a bank four feet from a brother in the ground.

## The Bill

Here it is and she worked it out on the fourth day sitting on a bank at the fifth hour with a bar across her knees.

She is nineteen years old and she has no wages and no place and no road and no list, and her own name is in a shut book nine hundred years deep with one word at the end of it that she still cannot use, and she has found the thing she walked for.

And it does not settle anything.

Not her entry. Not the six. Not the four who went over a chain on the twelfth of the month beginning 1,784 and were not her brother and were never going to be. Not the stopped wheel. Not the lime. Not a boy of ten counting loads out loud so the men could hear him do it.

Nothing is settled and nothing was settled by finding him and she is going to be carrying all of it for the rest of her life, and the only thing that has changed is that she does not have to wonder any more.

She had four days and a night on that ground with a brother in it and in all of it she did not get one thing she could put anywhere.
