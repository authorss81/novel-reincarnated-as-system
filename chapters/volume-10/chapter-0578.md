# The Door She Did Not Open

She worked the face on the nineteenth, the twentieth and the twenty-first of the month beginning 1,694 and on the twenty-second she went past the house with the shut door and did not open it and did not stop for long, and that is the whole of what the twenty-second is.

## Nine Days On A Face

She filled nine barrows on each of the three days, which is a full nine, and the man with the chalk wrote nine each time and said the number out loud, which is what he does.

The arm was open for the last five hours of the nineteenth and for all but two of the twentieth and it shut on the twenty-first at the fourth hour and stayed shut, which is faster than it usually is and she did not trust it and did not use it.

She slept on the stones at the turn on the nineteenth and on the floor of the lime cottage on the twentieth, in the same spot with her back against the bar, and on the twenty-first she slept at the bench, which is a bench with a post and a rail and is not a bed and has never been anything but a place to sit down.

Nothing came out of her on any of the nine barrows.

She has been lighter every day since the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,544 and she was lighter on the twenty-first than she had been on the nineteenth, and that is the finding from the twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,634 sitting in the dirt with her hand on her own ribs, and it has held for two months.

## The Woman On The Bench

On the nineteenth she sat down on the bench at the turn for the light going, the way she has sat four hundred times, and the woman of sixty was already on it with the sacking over her knees and a length of string and a needle in it.

The woman did two inches of the string and said nothing.

Ning Yan had four things ready and she said none of them, and she has been not saying thank you to that woman for a long time now, and she would have said something that day if anything had been there to say, and nothing was.

She got up at the ninth hour and went west four miles and slept on the floor of a shut house with her back against a bar across the door.

And that is everything the twenty-second began with, and it went in, and she wants it written down because there is a version of the last three weeks where a woman of sixty hands a girl a direction and it does not happen, and the reason it does not happen is that a woman of sixty with a needle in a string at the eighth hour is a person who has said four sentences in three months and is not going to say a fifth.

## The Store Road

She went west on the twenty-second at the second hour with a bar on her shoulder, because the store is a mile beyond the cottage and the store is the only thing on this ground that has ever paid her.

The store road is made, with a kerb, and there is nothing on it for four miles.

She came onto the cottage at the third hour of the twenty-second of the month beginning 1,694, and the bar was across the inside of the door and the yard was exactly as it had been on the fourth and the fifth.

The standing stone was in the ground in front of it, waist high on a woman, leaning two fingers out of true, and she put her hand on the top of it and it was cold and it was only a stone.

## The Door

She stood at it from the third hour to the fourth, with her hand on the bar across the outside of the door.

Inside there is a room with a stone floor and a hearth and a trough and a nail in the frame at the height of a hand and a clean place on a sill.

She knew all of that and she had known it for eighteen days.

There was nothing behind the door that she had not already stood in front of, and a door that is shut is only a door, and she had spent eighteen days treating four marks in an empty room as an address.

She did not open it.

She wants that written down because it is everything she did and did not do on the twenty-second of the month beginning 1,694 and she is not going to have a better version of it later.

## What She Had Come There To Do

She can be honest about the rest of it, and this is the part she would have left out of a column if she had been writing one.

She came four miles west at the second hour on the twenty-second of a month beginning 1,694 with a bar on her shoulder to stand in a yard where a boy counted loads out loud ten years back, and she stood in it for an hour, and the hour produced four marks and a barrow-way and no person at all.

She had been hoping for one of three things and she will say what they were because she has said them out loud in her own head on two made roads.

She was hoping for a smell. That is the one she is least proud of and it is still the one that comes first when she lets herself have it.

She was hoping for a handprint or a height-mark or a thing cut on a door frame with a figure in it, because a figure would be proof and she has never had proof of anything in eight years.

And she was hoping, underneath both of those, for a person to be there. Not a house. A person who would look at her and know her.

None of the three came, and she had known by the ninth hour of the fifth of the month that none of the three was coming, and she came four miles west anyway on the twenty-second, and that is not optimism. That is a girl who has somewhere to be.

## The Part That Changed

Here it is, and she got it standing on a made road four miles west of a turn at the fourth hour of the twenty-second of the month beginning 1,694, looking east at most of the road she could walk in a day and a half.

A woman at the end of a lane gave her a place. It cost nothing. It was the first direction she had in eight years.

And the direction points west.

The wall is nine miles east of the turn and twenty-two east of the post, and it is the only place in the world where a question can be asked, and there is a date on it now, the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,784, and it is ninety days away.

The cottage is four miles west of the turn.

Every single day she spends on that place is a day she is not on that road. A girl who has four miles west to walk is a girl who is not walking east. And she worked this out on the twenty-second of a month beginning 1,694 in four seconds standing in a yard, and it is the answer to a thing she has been carrying since the seventh hour of the fourth.

The gift and the loss are not two things.

It is one thing. Somebody gave her somewhere to be, and somewhere to be is the only thing on this ground that is cheaper than walking east, and she took it in four seconds at a fire on the fourth of a month with a warm loaf in a cloth in her right hand, and she has been walking west ever since.

## What She Did Next

She kept walking.

That is the sentence and she has made it the plainest one she can, because all of the last four minutes was a girl standing in a yard deciding whether to stand in it.

She kept walking west past the cottage and past the dead mill and into a store at the fifth hour on the strength of nothing, and she stood at a counter in front of a woman of fifty who said the rate out loud and there was nothing on the board and she said so.

And then she stood at that counter for four minutes and worked out that she had no bread and no wages and no wages coming until the second of a month beginning 1,724 and that she had ninety days to it.

Then she went out and turned round and picked up her bar and walked east, and she was at the bench at the turn at the ninth hour of the twenty-second of the month beginning 1,694, with ninety days in front of her and a shut door four miles behind her, and she had decided on the whole of it in a yard.

## What She Did Not Tell Herself On The Way

Here is the last of it, and she got it at the ninth hour of the twenty-second of a month beginning 1,694 sitting on the bench at the turn with her bar across her knees, and it is the thing she is least proud of and the only thing in these three days she would write down twice.

The two best days she has had since she asked a question out loud were the fourth and the fifth of this month, and they were in a shut house with a bar across the door that she did not open.

And she knows now, four miles east of it, that she was not resting. She was standing still on the ground a woman pointed at, and a girl who stands still is not a girl who is walking east, and every hour of it was an hour of the ninety.

She has lost nine days of the ninety in nineteen days and she has got four marks in an empty room and a loaf a woman put over a sill, and she would not take the nineteen days back if she was offered them, and she is not going to say that out loud to anybody, and she said it here.
