# Four Letters She Cannot Use

She went at four letters for three nights on the sixteenth, seventeenth and eighteenth of the month beginning 1,604, and she did not sleep in any of them, and at the end of the third night she knew one thing the word is not, and it is the only thing she got.

## The Sixteen Hours On The Sixteenth

The gang was on the drain again on the sixteenth and the foreman measured it at dusk the same as ever and she packed forty yards and metalled a hundred and did both without the fourth count going once, which is the best day she has had on a road since the month beginning 1,544.

She did not feel any of it after the seventh hour.

She lay on the flat stone at the foot of the post with the bar under her head and the fourth sentence of the book lying open in her head where it has lain since the sixteenth of a month that has gone.

*Lost.*

Four letters. A man of nine hundred years wrote them in his own hand under a date and shut the book on them, and there is nobody in this world who has said what it does, and that includes the man who wrote it.

She has never asked him. That is the part she has to keep in front of her all night, because it is the part that would make it easier if she had.

## Every Thing She Has

She is a person who counts things. That is not a figure of speech about her, it is the way her head works and it has worked that way since she was eleven years old, and she put the word on everything she had got, one at a time, for five hours, in the dark, on a stone.

She put it on the ground. Not that, whatever else turns out to be true.

She put it on the three names in the flat book. No, and she was in the dark of that room for four seconds and she can still see the second name written smaller than the first.

She put it on the eight leaves and the four sentences on the second of them. No. Those are hers and they are on a stone nine miles west and a man of nine hundred years heard them read out loud and shut a book on them and did not touch that one.

She put it on the trough, the bench, the bar on two posts, the ladder, the fourth rung that is loose only when it is wet, the shovel, the paste pot, the cord and the peg.

She put it on the window a hundred yards off a wall, and she had to stop on that one, and that is where the sixteen hours went.

## What It Is Not

She put it on her brother at the third hour of the seventeenth and she was still awake at the eighth hour of the eighteenth, and here is the whole of it in four lines.

Ning Wu is a line with no mark.

That is the thing. That is what he has been since she was eleven years old and it is the only description of him she has ever been able to use, and it was true on the sixteenth of the month beginning 1,574 and it is true on the eighteenth of this one.

He has never been on a page. He is not in a list and he is not in a book and he has never once been written down by a person who writes things down, and the word she is working out at three in the morning is a word that can only be written about an entry.

So it is not about him. It never was.

She has known that for eight years without having to be told and she had been carrying a quarter of a hope about the four letters, and it was not a quarter of a hope about being found or being looked for.

It was about him.

She put it down at the eighth hour of the eighteenth of a month, sitting up on a flat stone with her arms round her knees, and she has not got it back and she was not going to get it back.

## What That Cost Her

That is the whole of the price and she is going to write it down properly because she has spent three nights on it and nobody is going to tell her it was cheap.

It cost her three nights of sleep and she is lighter than she was on the eighth of a month and she has three stages past the end of any realm a county holds and her arm is open four days in five and she has to be on a road at the first hour in the morning.

It cost her the only thing she has had for eight years that was not about what has been done to her.

Every other thing in this has been a person doing something to her: the entering, the keeping, the rating, the reading back, the four sentences on a page, the word at the bottom of it. All of it is a room she did not choose to be in.

That was the other one. That was a word she could have read as a thing about somebody else, and if it had been, then for four seconds in the third hour of the seventeenth of a month she would have been a person with good news about her brother.

She was not. She is a person with a word on her own line, and the word is four letters long, and there is no good news anywhere in it.

## The Anger She Is Not Allowed To Keep

She got angry at the fifth hour of the sixteenth of a month and she had it for most of a night and she had to put it down in the morning, and this is the part she would not want written down if she could choose.

She was not angry that she is on a line.

She was angry at four letters. A man wrote four letters and a date and shut the book, and she has spent three nights on nine days of road trying to make a mouth out of them, and he is nine hundred years old and has kept the whole of this ground and has never once been asked to explain himself and is not going to be.

She had about eleven minutes of that at the fifth hour and it came out of her the way the other thing comes out of her, and she was glad of it for two seconds and then she was not.

Because she went and looked at it honestly, which she has learned to do since she was eleven, and he was not cruel about it. She went through all four letters and there is nothing cruel in any of them.

A man of nine hundred years wrote down that a person got past the top of what this ground has and that the thing at the top of it took her, and he wrote it in his own hand under a date and shut the book. He did not enjoy it and he did not enjoy refusing to say what it means, and he was not refusing.

He was writing down a fact.

That is the whole of the anger and it took two seconds to look at and she put it down on the flat stone and left it there, because the anger she is allowed to keep is the anger at a book and not the anger at the man, and she is not going to have the second one.

And she worked out, sitting on the stone with the bar across her knees, that this is the other thing that has been sitting on her for a week.

She is going to ask a man what else he keeps, and she has just spent three nights finding out that the first thing he kept of her was four letters that she cannot use, and there is no arrangement on this ground where asking him about a window is easier than asking him about that.

It is going to cost. She has known that since the eighth of a month and she has been counting the days since and this is the first number that has come up.

## Twenty-Two Days

There is one more thing she put the word on and it does not work either and she is writing that down because it has been standing in her for a month and she cannot leave it where it is.

At a jamb, on the twenty-second of a month that has gone, in daylight, with a book shut on a table, a man said twenty-two days to her and then went eleven feet away and did not follow her into a room.

She has been over that in twenty-two different ways since. She has made it a distance and made it an interval and made it a number of things he had left to do, and it is not any of those, and the only fact she has about it is that she has never once earned it.

She put *lost* on it at the fifth hour of the eighteenth and it came apart at once.

Twenty-two days is not a word that is written on anything. It is not on her line and it is not on a line with no mark, and she has been carrying it since the twenty-second because a man said it to her and he has never been wrong in her hearing, and she cannot put it in the same drawer as a word he wrote down and that is the whole of what she knows about it.

## What She Got

She got one thing and she got it by taking something away, which is a way of finding out that does not tell you what is there.

Here is what she knows about the word at the ninth hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,604, having gone at it for three nights.

It is four letters. It is in a hand that has not written anything else on that page. It is under a date. It was written by a man who was not being cruel to her and who was not being kind to her either, and who was writing down a fact about a line, and she has had two nights to find a reason to say he was doing it to her and she has not got one and she is not going to keep looking for one.

And she knows what it is not about.

That is three days and three nights and she gained one subtraction and no answer at all, and the sun came off the rail at the seventh hour and she was on the flat stone at the foot of a post with her bag against her back and a bar nine feet nine under her arm.

She said *lost* out loud once, in the ordinary voice, at the ninth hour, to the trough.

It did not do anything.
