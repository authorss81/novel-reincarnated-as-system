# Twenty-Two Miles Without An Answer

She was two miles short of the gate on the night of the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,604, lying in low broken ground with the bar under her head and a loaf and a bit in the bag, and she got up at the second hour and went west because there was nothing on this side of her to walk toward except a post.

## What She Is Carrying

She said it out loud once, to herself, in the dark, sitting up in the stone with the loaf in her hand.

Twenty-two miles and an inch of stone between it and the flat stone under a ladder. Nine of strip road, eleven of made road, two of the last of the made road past the gate. She had done it in three days going east with a boy of fifteen four feet off her and she was doing it in one going west with nothing.

And a question in a book with a date on it that was now four days old, and three terms that had not moved in the four days, and a rule that said she did not get another one until the first was answered.

She put the loaf back in the bag and stood up, and standing up took her two goes, which it did not take her two months ago.

## Two Miles Of Made Road

The ground she had got out of the road into was grey and broken with stones standing out of it four inches apart, and she went along it to the metalling and then west on the metalling with the bar on her shoulder.

There was no light anywhere on it and no cart on it and no person on it, and she walked it in the time it takes to say four things slowly and she thought about none of them.

What she thought about instead, for both of those miles, was the fourth count of her breath.

It has been four everywhere since she was eighteen years old and it goes for four on flat made ground with a load in good air, and at the mile it went for five and stayed there for a count and a half and came back.

She noticed it, and she kept walking, and that is the whole of what happened on those two miles.

## The Boy At The Chain

The boy of fifteen was on the chain at the fourth hour with a lamp hooked over the post, and he had been asleep sitting up with his back against the gate post, and he came up off it fast enough that the chain came off the hook.

“You are going back,” he said.

“I am going back,” Ning Yan said.

He looked at the bar on her shoulder and at the sky, which was the ordinary way of working out what time it was.

“You have walked it in the dark.”

“I have walked the first two of it in the dark.” She put her hand out and stopped the chain swinging. “How long does the chain want to be up for you.”

“There is one cart most hours,” the boy said. “It has not been.”

“All right,” Ning Yan said. “Put it up for it.”

She went on west and the boy stood at the gate with the lamp in his hand and did not call after her, and she was two hundred yards off before she understood that he had not asked her what she had asked at the wall two days ago.

He had asked her nothing since and he was not going to, and she has been at gates nine times and she has never once had anybody not ask her anything before, and it took her four hundred yards to work out that it was the first ordinary thing that had happened to her in two days.

## Eleven Miles Of Made Road

She made the eleven miles between the gate and the turn between the fourth hour and the eleventh.

A made road takes a load on the light end and the bar does not jump, and she is faster on that road than on the strip road and has been since the first time, and eleven miles in seven hours is the best she has done on it and she did not once stop.

She stopped twice. Both times in the ditch on the south side, both times for four minutes, and on the second of them she sat down and could not get up the first time she tried.

That is new. She went through the fourth count nine times between the fourth hour and the eleventh and it came back in four every time except twice, and on the second of the two it took seven, and she sat in a ditch six miles from the gate with a bar across her knees and worked out that a body that is lighter than it was on the eighth of a month does not have anything spare left in it for the road.

## What Came Out On The Eleventh Mile

The eleventh mile from the post is two miles along the made road, and the arm went open at a quarter past the seventh hour and she did not shut it and did not try.

That is the arrangement and it has never been anything else. Four days in five, and she has never once been able to make it shut on demand, and a bar on one shoulder with the other arm hanging is a bar on one shoulder that is nine feet nine with three feet of it doing nothing.

She went the last four miles that way. It is slower and it is not dangerous on a made road with a kerb on both sides, and she had four counts for every one of them, and she came into the turn at the eighth hour with the arm open and the light going off the top of the wall behind her to the east.

## The Bench At The Turn

She did not stop at the bench.

She stood at the end of the strip road for a minute with the bar off her shoulder and her arm hanging and looked east down eleven miles of made road she could not see the end of, and then she went west.

That is the whole of what she did at the turn and it is the first time in two months she has been at that bench and not sat on it, and she has thought about why since.

There was nothing at the turn to sit down about.

## Nine Miles Of Strip Road In The Dark

The strip road runs nine miles west and there is nothing on it but a bend a mile out and low rough grass and a cart track that leaves and comes back twice.

She did it between the eighth hour and the second hour. A mile an hour and something in the dark on rough ground with a load and no shoulders, which is half what she does in the daylight, and she went through the fourth count for four at the fourth mile and it came back in four and she went on.

At the seventh mile she stopped for nine minutes in the low grass and drank a quarter of a cup out of the skin and did not eat, because she had decided at the gate that the loaf was for the morning and it was going to be for the morning.

## Four Days Is Not A Number Yet

She worked that out on the made road at the seventh mile, between the gate and the turn, in the dark, with the bar on her shoulder.

Four days is not a number.

That is the thing she had been carrying west and it is a very stupid thing to carry and she could not put it down. A man of nine hundred years took a question four days ago and he will take it for as long as it takes him, and there is no way on nine days of road of finding out what as long as it takes him is, and four is not a number, and four is not nothing either.

She has been asked nothing in eight years that she could not answer with one word, and this is the first question anybody has ever put to her where the answer is a length of time and she is not allowed to ask what length.

She got to the turn at the eighth hour with the arm open and she did not stop at the bench and she did not drink out of the skin, and she walked nine miles of strip road in the dark on that.

## The Stone Under The Ladder

She came onto the flat at the second hour of the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 1,604 and the post was a mile ahead of her in the last of the light, and she went to it.

The trough had water in it. The rail was where the rail is. The flat stone under the bottom of the ladder had the book on it.

It had been there thirty-one days. She knew that without doing it, because she had done it at the twenty-eighth of the month before she left and the number had not changed since and she had walked twenty-two miles in between.

She stood at the foot of the ladder for four minutes with the bar across her knees and did not touch anything, and there was nothing on nine days of road to touch.

Then she got her coat open and got the loaf out of the bag and ate it sitting on the flat stone with the book nine inches off her left knee, and it was a brown loaf with more bran in it than a person wants, and she ate all of it.

## What Is Left Of The Walk

Here is the bill, and she wrote it out on her own knee with a finger in the dust because that is what she does.

Nine miles of strip road. Eleven of made road. Two of the last two. Twenty-two, in three days, and she has done it in one going west, which she would not have believed at the wall.

One loaf and a bit, eaten. An arm that was open for the last four miles and is shut now and will open again. A body that sat down in a ditch six miles out and could not get up the first time.

And a question in a book with a date on it that is four days old and has not been answered.

That is everything the walk cost her and she has been two months learning to count things properly, and the thing that is going to sit on her is not one of them.

She asked for what else is on it, out loud, in daylight, with a boy four feet off, and she got three sentences and a rule in exchange, and she went home on her own two legs.

The expensive part was not the twenty-two miles.
