# He Is Still Talking

She was at the turn for six days, from the twelfth of the month beginning 1,814 to the dusk of the seventeenth, because there is nowhere on nine days of road she was sent and the turn is the only place on it she can stand and not be going anywhere.

## Nothing On The Board

There is a bar on two posts four hundred yards east of the turn with a board nailed to it, and loads get chalked on that board when they go out, and there has not been one load on it since the second of a month beginning 1,784.

She went and looked at it on the second day and it was bare and the chalk was still on the rail beside it from the last time, and the rail had rain on it.

Nine years of people turn at this turn and do not turn back. That is all a turn is. It is not a gate and nothing was ever posted at it, and a woman of sixty sat on a bench at the side of it long enough to have watched most of them do it.

And there is nothing on the board now, and there is going to be nothing on it for as long as she stands and looks at it, because a board takes what is going somewhere and nothing on nine days of road is going anywhere.

That is the first thing the closing did that she can see from the outside. It did not change a single thing on this ground, except that no one is being written down in words that two people can hear.

## What She Does About Six Days

She counted.

Six days and a board with nothing on it and a bench and a woman of sixty and a trough and a rail and a bar on two posts.

She counted the days because she counts. She counted the dust off the rail and the water in the trough and the length of her own boot and the number of stones in the third row of the wall by the trough, which is nineteen, and which is the same nineteen on the sixth day as on the first.

She has never been able to make a count stop counting. That is the thing that has happened since the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,754 and it has not let up and she has not been able to make it let up, and she has spent six days standing at a turn doing it anyway.

On the fourth day she sat down on the bench beside the woman of sixty and stayed there for two hours and neither of them said anything.

On the fifth day a carter came through with an empty cart at the fourth hour and stopped the horse at the trough the way carters do, and she stood four feet off him while the horse drank and he looked at her and she looked at him and neither of them said a word about anything, and he went on east.

There is a person on nine days of road who does that and there are nine more of him and she cannot tell them apart and she is not going to.

## A Brother Going West

He came past the turn at the dusk of the seventeenth of the month beginning 1,814, on foot, from the east, with nothing on him.

She saw him at four hundred yards and she knew him at four hundred yards, because of the way he walks, which is the way she walks, which came from a man whose name neither of them has said out loud in nine years.

She did not go into the road.

He came up to the turn and he saw her and he stopped, the way two people stop at about six feet.

She said: “You are going west.”

“I am.”

“There is nothing west of here.”

“There is a mill with a wheel that has not turned for nine years and there are people in it,” he said, “and I have not been in one of those either.”

That is all he said. He did not say that he was glad to see her and he did not say that he was not. He stood six feet off a girl of nineteen on a turn in the dusk and she did not ask him one question, and there were nine or ten questions available to her and she has spent four of them standing there and she has not asked the fifth.

He went on west at about the eighth hour. She stood on the stones and watched him go up the four miles to the cottage at the stopped lime bed and past it, and she did not call after him and she did not follow him and she has not said thank you to her brother in her life and she was not going to start.

## The Book In Her Coat

Against her ribs, on the side where the paper is, there is a book of eight leaves made out of a blanket.

The first leaf has a date and a place on it and nothing else. The second has four sentences on it that she has read out loud once and will not read again.

The other six are hers.

She has decided twice not to write in them. She put a nib to the first of them at the fourth hour of the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,724 and she put it away, and she has had room in it ever since, and she has not used a day of it.

She took it out at the ninth hour of the fourteenth of the month beginning 1,814 and she held it for about an hour in the dark with the stub of a nib and a cake of ink she has not used in two months, and then she put it back against the paper.

There is room in it. That is the last fact and it is the only one of the seven things she owns that is not a debt.

## What Is East Of Her

There is no list east of her any more.

She has no book to put a question into and she has no second question and she is not allowed a fifth and the lever is rubbed out in the dust on the flat outside a wall on the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,784 and has not been written since.

The six are gone where kept things go and the man who said so said it was exact.

And there is not one page anywhere east of her on this ground that has either of their names in it where anybody could go and ask after them. Not hers and not her brother’s, and she has looked, and there is nothing to look in.

Six stages past the ceiling of a realm a servant-born girl has never had, and no wages coming, no shed, no lane, no store that is hers, and a brother walking west of her who will not walk with her.

## Two People She Has Not Thanked

A man of nine hundred years shut a book in daylight on the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,784, with nothing on a plank table and no one asked him to, and then he walked thirteen miles west to a turn with a girl and went back east twenty-two miles to a door on eleven feet of boards, and he did it because she was walking west.

She did not say thank you to him and she is not going to.

She has said it once in nine years, in the month beginning 1,694, for four sentences about a house, and it bought nothing and she did not expect it to buy anything, and she was a girl standing in an opening who had never said it out loud in her life.

She is not going to say it to a man of nine hundred years for shutting a book. His book is shut and his door is shut and he is alive and old and not thanked, and there is no arrangement on this ground that would let her say it and mean it as anything other than a girl being afraid of what she owes.

And there is a brother.

She did not say thank you to her brother either, on the dusk of the seventeenth of a month beginning 1,814, at about six feet, on a turn, and she has been carrying that for nine years and she will carry it for nine more.

## What The Six Days Were For

She stood at the turn and she got something out of the six days and it is not much and she wanted to have it before the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,814, because on the eighteenth she is going to have to decide what she does with herself and there is no list to tell her.

What she got is this.

For eight years every road anybody gave her pointed at a place somebody had been. A cottage four miles west. A mill five miles west. A lane four miles west with a woman at forty feet of it. A face four miles west. A store five miles west behind a wheel that stopped nine years ago.

And the only thing in the world that was pointed at was a wall twenty-two miles east with an opening in it, and she has been to it twenty-three times and she has got six out of it and a shut book and a brother.

Every direction she has ever been given was west and every single thing she has ever found was east, and she worked that out on a turn at dusk on the seventeenth of a month beginning 1,814 without having to write it down, because it is not a number.

## The Thing She Has Not Asked

Here it is, and she is putting it down on the seventeenth of a month beginning 1,814 at the dusk, out loud, on a turn, to nobody, because she has been carrying it since the month beginning 1,574 and she has never once put it anywhere.

There is something in her head that counts.

It has counted with her for eight years. It reads out every figure anybody hands her before she has finished taking it in. It got a number wrong once, in the month beginning 1,634, and it corrected itself in her head before she had finished being wrong.

It has never once said what it was before it started counting.

Not in the month beginning 1,604, when she asked it three questions and got no answer and worked out that it was answering a question and only a question. Not on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,724, when she said a name out loud in an opening in a wall and it cost nothing. Not on the first of a month beginning 1,814, when she stood thirty feet off a brother in a barrow field and did not get one word of gladness out of him.

Ten volumes of roads and four walls and a furnace that will not light itself and a hand that cannot write a false line, and she has never asked it what it was.

She has never asked him.

She does not ask him yet.

He is still talking.
