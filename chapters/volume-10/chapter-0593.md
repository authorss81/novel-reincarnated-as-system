# Where Kept Things Go

They came at the fourth hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 1,784, the way the man at the chain said they would, and she was standing on the far bank with the bar on her back and she had asked for nothing.

## The Fourth Hour

Four of them and two with them.

They came down the made road from the west on foot and not in a cart, and the two with them were not on that road for anything else, and there was not a sound, which is the part she was not ready for.

No noise.

She had been standing on a far bank since the second hour waiting for a sound.

They came up to the near end of the chain and stopped, and the two men did not say one word to the man at the chain, and the man at the chain did not say one word to them. He picked the chain up off the post with his left hand and took the slack out of it and stood to one side.

That is all there is to it. There is a chain and a hand and a step forward and then the other side.

## What They Look Like

They were not in chains and there was nothing on them.

That is what she has not been able to get past since the fourth hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 1,784, and she looked at all four of them and she could not find one thing on any of them that said where they had come from.

The first was thirty or so and had a coat that had been mended at the elbow. The second was a young woman of twenty-five with a shawl over her head and her hands empty. The third was old, sixty or so, and slow, and the two men waited for her at the far edge of the cut without hurrying her. The fourth was a boy. He was fifteen or sixteen and he went across on his own without looking down and he went the fastest of the four.

None of them said anything. None of them asked anything. The man at the chain did not ask them a single question and she stood eight feet away with an open mouth and understood in four seconds that they had all been asked.

## What The Man At The Chain Said

He said it after they were on the other side, to her, because she was standing there and because she had not moved, and he said it once.

“There is no place to join. I will not tell you that it is a punishment, because it is not one and I have watched people say that it is and I do not say it.”

“A name that is kept goes where kept things go. That is what keeping is.”

“It goes where kept things go and it does not come back and it is not anywhere you can walk to.”

“The going is the same for all of them and it is exact. A thing that is taken and a thing that goes are the same thing and that is everything I know how to do.”

“That is four sentences and I have not said them since a man with a bar asked me once.”

## Why He Said It To Her

She asked him why at the fifth hour and he said it in the ordinary voice, and this is the part that undid her for two days.

“You are the only person who has ever stood on that bank and watched one go over and then asked me what it was,” he said. “The others want to know if they are on it. You wanted to know where it goes.”

He turned the kettle and got up off the stone.

“That is not a question anybody asks me. That is a question about a road, and I have been at this crossing three years and I have never been asked a question about a road.”

## What She Counted

Four.

She counted them going across at the fourth hour and she counted them again at the fifth hour when they were small on the made road, and it was four both times, and there is a carter’s board with four names on it nine years back that she has only ever heard described by a man of seventy who has not got three of the four.

She sat down on the bank with the bar across her knees and wrote it in the dust.

Four.

And then she sat there and worked out what she had, and it came out in a minute and a half, and it is the thing she carried east for the rest of that month.

Nine years back there were four on a board and the four went east.

This morning there were four and they went east.

She cannot tell you that it is the same four. She has no instrument that would tell her that and there is none on nine days of road.

But a thing that goes east off that road comes off it at a crossing, and a crossing has a man at it, and the man has said where it goes, and he says the going is exact.

That is all there is to a road like that. It is not a punishment and it is not a trade and it is not a wage-book. It takes people off a road and it does it accurately and the accuracy is the entire of it.

## Whether It Could Be Her

She sat on that bank through the sixth and seventh hours and worked that out as well, because it is the thing she would have thought about first at fifteen and at nineteen she has to think about it anyway.

It cannot be her.

Not because she is six stages past a ceiling and not because she has been written down as lost in a hand nine hundred years old, though both of those are true and she thinks about them.

It cannot be her because she cannot be kept. That is the third stage, it has never gone back, and nothing since has undone it. Nobody on nine days of road has ever put her anywhere and asked her to stay, and she did not manage that by being clever. She managed it by being a person that a keeping does not hold.

And she looked at that bank and understood the second half of it, which took her longer.

A road that keeps people does not decide who is worth keeping. It takes whoever comes. The woman of twenty-five with a shawl did not volunteer. The old one did not volunteer. The boy of fifteen did not volunteer and he went across on his own without looking down, and he went the fastest of the four, and there is nothing in that which says anything about him.

If she had been a person that a keeping could hold, she would not be sitting on a bank counting. She would be in one of those four positions on the made road at the fourth hour with a man with a board, and she would have been since she was eleven years old, and there would have been no four days of being able to walk and count and say no.

She got the whole thing in four minutes and then she had to sit on the bank for an hour because of it, and there was nobody on that ground she could have said any of it to.

## The Two Days After

She stayed on that bank for two more days and there was nothing to do on it and she did it anyway, because she had eight days and a wall two miles past a gate and no reason on earth to be on a far bank in grass.

She counted the two men coming and going. She counted the times he opened the chain in two days: nine, and eight of them were men going east with a barrow or a sack and one was a woman going east with a basket.

She tried to work out the ratio of what goes east over that chain to what goes east along that road, and she got nowhere in two days, because a girl who cannot count nine coppers in her own hand is not going to get a figure out of two days of a bank.

And she lay in the grass at night and did not sleep more than two hours and forty minutes and she had not had bread since a woman at forty feet of lane put a quarter of one into her hand on the fourth of a month beginning 1,694.

## The Boy Who Went Over Fastest

She came back to the fourth of the four for three days after and she could not put it down.

The boy went across on his own and he did not look down and he went the fastest of the four, and she was fifteen once, and she has been on a made road at night with nothing between nine days of country, and she knows what it looks like when a young person has decided that the fastest thing is the way out of standing still.

He was not frightened. That is the part she keeps coming back to and cannot get past.

The old one was frightened and the two men waited for her at the edge of the cut and did not hurry her. The young woman was not frightened and she was not calm either; she was doing the thing a person does when they have been doing a thing for a long time, which is going through it, and she did not look at the water either.

The boy was not frightened and was not doing it out of long practice, and he went over in three strides.

She sat on a far bank with a bar on her knees and worked out, in the end, that there is no room on this ground for a boy of fifteen to be frightened at a cut in a road, because there is nowhere on nine miles of road for him to be frightened to, and a boy who has learned that at fifteen goes over a chain in three strides because that is the fastest thing there is.

She turned the two of them over, who says good and says nothing else and has done for five months without being asked one question. And the boy at the chain, who said nothing and went fast. The same age. Nine miles apart on one road, with the same two boys on it.

She is nineteen. One bar. She cannot be kept. In four hours she is going to walk into an opening and ask a question.

## Where She Went On The Third Morning

At the sixth hour of the fourteenth of the month beginning 1,784 she got up off the far bank and put the bar on her left shoulder and walked east.

The made road runs from the post eleven miles and then there is a gate and two miles of nothing to lie down on after that, and she had the gate in the ninth hour and the flat at the flat in the seventh hour of the fifteenth, and a boy of fifteen at the gate who said good and nothing else.

She stood four feet into the opening at the west jamb on the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,784 at the ninth hour, with the plank table in front of her and nothing on it, and a man of nine hundred years on the other side of it.

Seven days.

She had nothing to hand him that was not a question.
