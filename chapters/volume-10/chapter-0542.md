# The Night With No Place In It

She slept on the flat ground at the foot of the post on the thirtieth of the month beginning 1,574 with her bar under her head and her bag against her back, and the post she is standing at a foot and a half from has a bench inside it and a door that was not latched, and nothing came of either.

## The Thirtieth

The light goes at the eighth hour in that month and it is colder at the end of a month than it was in the middle of it, and there is nothing on nine days of road between the post and the bend a mile east.

She had eaten nothing since the sixteenth of this month and she had not been hungry since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544, and those two facts sit next to each other and neither of them is going to move.

Her right arm went open at the ninth hour and stayed open. It is four days in five and this is one of the four. It shut at the second hour of the night and opened again before she had it under the bar.

## The Door Standing Open

She got up off the stone at the second hour because the cold came into her right side where the arm was open and stayed there.

The keeper’s door was open four feet away and there was a bench round three sides of the inside of that post and a man asleep on it with his boots off, and the lamp in there was out, and if she had walked four feet and turned round the side of the post she would have been in a warm room with a person in it who keeps three names under a ladder.

He would have had to wake up. He would have had to put his boots on or not put his boots on and make a decision about a girl of nineteen with a bar on the shoulder, and then it would have been a thing that had happened, and it would have been a thing somebody had let happen to him.

There is nobody on nine days of road who would have asked her for anything she can give and there is nobody on nine days of road she is going to put in the position of one who gives.

She has not been given anything since the month beginning 1,544 and she is not going to be given a night’s sleep out of a post on the thirtieth of a month, and if the cold took her in the next hour she would rather it did that than have it be a thing a man with a rag had done.

So she stood in the dust in the middle of the night at the foot of her own ladder and she got her coat open and closed it again and she went and lay back down on the flat stone with her shoulder on it, because the stone under a ladder is dry and the ground around a post is not.

## The Reach At The Ninth Hour Of The Night

That is the part that costs the most and it costs nothing to anybody but her.

She woke at the fourth hour of the morning with the bar lying across her chest and one end of it in the air past her knees, and she tried to lift it.

Four fingers and a thumb on the right hand and the arm open four days in five and the whole of nine feet nine of it wanting to come up off the stone at once.

It went for four counts.

Not four counts and back in four the way it does on flat ground at the sixth hour. Four counts and nothing behind them, and the ground going round, and the dark going from one shape to another shape and back.

She lay there with a bar across her chest in the dark and she waited for it the way you wait for a dog that has gone out of the room.

It came back in eleven.

She counted it. Eleven is not four and it is the first time in nine months it has been more than four on flat ground with a load on her, and she lay there afterwards on a flat stone under a ladder at a post and worked out that what changed at the fourth hour of the morning is not the ground, because the ground is the same ground she was on at the ninth hour.

It is that she was asleep, and she could not call a halt to it, and the thing that came out of her at the fourth hour of the morning did not wait for her to be ready.

## Fourteen, Not Twelve

Here is what she got right on the flat stone at the fifth hour, and it took her four minutes.

The sixteenth of this month was a day she can name. The thirtieth is a day she can name. Between them there are fourteen and she has been carrying twelve for four days because a count stops at the part of it she does not want to stand at.

Fourteen days without anything to eat.

She is three stages past the end of a realm and there is a coin-sized place open low behind her fourth rib that has been open since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,274, and fourteen days has not come out of her as anything she could put a name on. Her body stopped asking for it four months ago and she worked out at the time that it was finished.

It has not been hunger. She does not want to say that it is not hunger, because it is not, and because a body at the top of a realm goes along without bread the way water goes along without a channel.

What it is, is a thing that stops being able to do a thing. Fourteen days and she cannot lift a bar off her own chest in four seconds and she could on the fifteenth.

## What Fourteen Days Took

She did the inventory on her back on the stone at the sixth hour, because it is the only thing she can do that costs nothing and she wanted the number before she walked.

What she has lost since the sixteenth is four things and three of them she will get back inside a week.

The lift of a bar off a flat stone, which was eight counts on the ground on flat at the sixth hour when she could do anything at all, and is eleven in the dark, and she does not know what it is on the stone at the sixth hour this morning and she is not going to find out by lifting it.

The sleep. Two nights in a row on the ground with a bar under her head and she came up out of both of them at the fourth hour with her mouth open and her heart going, and a body that has not had anything to eat for fourteen days does not stay down past four hours and she has not slept a whole night since the eighth of a month that has gone.

The hand. The palm of the left hand has gone soft along the line where the bar sits and it is not a cut and it is not a blister and it does not hurt, it is only there, and she has had that for three days.

And the fourth thing is not a thing that can be counted and she wrote it down in her head anyway: she cannot be met where she is expected. There was a cart going to come west on this road in the afternoon for four days running and go on past her, and it is going to do that again tomorrow, and she worked out at the sixth hour that she is not counting any of it.

That is not the same as the count she has been keeping. That one does not have a number in it and she cannot do anything about it at all.

## What The Ground Kept

She lay on the flat stone and thought about the ground, because that is what she does at five in the morning and she has done it since she was eleven.

The ground under a ladder is dry and it is flat and it is four feet from a door that was not latched and it has kept her for two nights, and that is the whole of what it has done.

It did not ask her for anything. It did not hold her because it is a post. It did not keep her because anybody decided she was to be kept.

She asked it for nothing.

That is the sentence she got on her back on the stone at the fifth hour with her arm open again and her chest hurting where the bar had been lying, and she has turned it over four days and it is the truest thing she has got.

She is at the foot of a ladder with three names under it and a book of her own beside them and she is not being kept by any of it. She has nowhere and she asked nothing for it, and the reason she has nowhere is that she has not asked, and she is nineteen and that is a thing she did on purpose and cannot undo and is going to have to go and change.

## The First Of A Month

The light came at the sixth hour and she knew from the angle of it on the west side of the post that it was the first of a month, and she worked out which one and then worked out the day, and it was the first of the month beginning 1,604.

She sat up off the stone and it took her two goes, and her right arm was shut and went open again in the time it took.

There was a cart on the strip road at the seventh hour going west with boards on it, and a carter on it who said good to her the way he has said good to her four hundred times, and the trough was dry at the rail because the horse had had the last of it at dusk.

Nine miles west is a turn, and a quarter of a mile off the turn is a bench on a store road with a store behind it, and five miles west of a mill whose wheel has not turned since before she came there is a woman behind a counter who has sold her bread for two years at two coppers a loaf and has never once asked her a question about anything.

She has no coppers. She has fourteen days of nothing behind her and a bar she cannot lift in four seconds.

She looked west down the strip road for a quarter of an hour and then she went and filled the skin at the trough, which took eleven minutes because the dipper is on a chain and the chain is short, and she is going to have to start a nine-mile walk in the next hour or she is going to be standing at this post on the second of a month with nothing behind her at all.
