# Nothing Coming And Everything Carried

She counted what she had on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,574, in the ordinary voice, sitting on the rail at the post with a bar across her knees, and the count came to the same thing it has come to for four months.

## The Morning After A Whole Day

There were five of them there at the fourth hour and six at the seventh, when a second carter came through with a load of lime and stopped at the trough because the horse would not go on without it.

The man with the bag was sitting on the flat ground on the low side of the post with his back against the wood and his legs out in front of him the way a man sits when he has walked three days and stopped.

He had not said anything since the eleventh hour yesterday.

She had not either.

There is nothing on nine days of road that a man and a woman can say to each other for two days, and she has had eight years of finding out what to say in a room, and yesterday she used none of it.

## What She Is Carried With

A bar nine feet nine, held at the light end, the strap bound out of her own coat lining and about two inches of lining left in it.

A skin, three-quarters full, because she filled it at the trough yesterday in four minutes with nothing else to do.

A bag with a dish in it and a lump of clay in it and a ring a finger’s width, which is the ring she has been carrying since the month beginning 1,314 and has never once put on and has never once thrown away.

A sheet of paper against her ribs, folded flat on the left side of the open place low behind her fourth rib, opened with her own two hands in the fourth month of a year she could name and never once called anything.

And a book. Eight leaves the size of a closed hand, folded in half three times, a date and a place on the first leaf, four sentences on the second, six empty ones that are hers.

## The Book Is Not In The Bag

It is on the flat stone under the bottom of the ladder, an inch of stone between it and a flat book with three names in it that has been under there nineteen years.

It has been there one day.

That is the whole of what she has put down in nine days of road in the last month and it is the only one of the four things above that she cannot put her hand on.

She thought about that for a while on the rail, with her thumb going up and down on the bar where the wood is smooth.

## What Is Coming

Nothing.

That is the count and it is the only part of it that took any working out, and she has known it since the eighteenth of a month and has not said it out loud to anybody in nine days.

No coppers in hand. Three went to bread in the fourth month of a year she could name and none has come since and none is coming, and there is no person on this road who has owed her anything since the two hundred times a woman behind a counter said good to her and took two coppers a loaf the whole time.

No wages. She has not been on a board for pay since the month beginning 1,514.

No offer standing anywhere. The one on the step of the shed at the end of the lane has stood since the second month of a year she could name and does not need an answer and will not get one.

No shed, no room, no bench of her own, no man who has said come in.

That is the whole of what is coming to her in her life at this hour on the twenty-seventh of a month, and she counted it out loud in the ordinary voice to a strip road, once, and stopped.

## The Count That Is Only Hers

She did the rest of it the way she has done it since the eighth of a month nine volumes back, which is inside her head, in order, fast.

The bar for carrying and not for opening. The skin for water. The dish and the clay and the ring for nothing anybody has ever needed. The paper for the record and the book for the four sentences.

The arm open four days in five and the four counts of breath everywhere including inside a loaded reach, and the eight of eleven holds, and the missing finger, and the coin-sized place low behind her fourth rib that came open on the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,274 and is not going to close.

She can take the weight of a sack of lime with her left hand and about a fifth of it with her right on a good day, and she cannot do the other four fifths of anything, and that has not changed since the sixteenth of the month beginning 1,544.

And she is three stages past the end of a realm and none of it has ever come in as money.

## Nine Or Ten

Here is the thing she got wrong and she found it at the seventh hour, sitting on the rail.

She has been telling the count as nine for a day.

She last had anything to eat on the sixteenth of this month. That was ten days ago, because the sixteenth is not the day before yesterday and it has not been for some time.

Nine is what it was on the twenty-fifth, and she has carried the nine forward the way a person carries a number that is finished, and on the twenty-sixth and now on the twenty-seventh it should be ten and then eleven.

She has been counting a count that stopped.

That is the whole of it. It is not a mistake in the arithmetic. It is that she reached the end of a number she could stand at the end of and left it there.

## The Second Half Of The Question

The food is not the question, or it is not only the question.

She worked that out on the rail in about four minutes and she has thought about it every day since the eighteenth of a month and got the same four minutes every time.

The question is what she is going to do about the eleventh day.

She is not going to sit at a post and wait to be found, because she has been found, and the finding wrote a word on a page and shut the book, and nothing has come of it but a line and forty seconds.

She cannot be given anything and she cannot be paid, so the whole of the world is out of the question and has been for four months.

There are four hundred people on this ground and every one of them trades, and a bar nine feet nine is what a person trades with, and she has not offered it to one of them since the month beginning 1,544.

So there is one road and it is nine miles west and she has not started down it.

## A Plank And One Good Arm

The carter of thirty-five came back at the eighth hour with a bar of his own and a length of board across the soft ground by the trough, and he put the bar under one end and looked at her.

“Hold that,” he said.

She did not say anything. She got her left hand under it and her right under the other end, four fingers and a thumb, and she took the weight up off his bar before he levered with it.

It is four feet of board and a carter is putting three hundredweight of his own shoulders through the far end of it, and what came across into her hands was about a sixth of the weight and it stayed a sixth.

Her breath went to four counts inside the hold at the fourth second and she stood in it at five and it came back at seven, and the carter levered the stone round in the dirt and did not count and did not notice and did not say thank you, because it was nine in the morning and he had asked.

That is the first thing anybody has asked her to do in nine days, and it took a quarter of an hour, and she stood in a loaded hold with four counts of breath in it and was exactly as good at it as the left half of any other person on this road.

She has a bar nine feet nine and a good arm and a body that is past the end of what any county holds, and the last time she offered either of them to a trade was the month beginning 1,544, and she has worked out why she stopped, and the reason is that nobody asked her first.

## What She Wants To Know

At the ninth hour she got to the end of it and she wanted one number and there was no way of getting it.

She wanted to know how many days she has.

Not what her body can carry and not what the fourth count will do on the fourth mile. The other one. The number of days between where she is and the place where a body at ten days without bread stops being a person who is going to do something on the twenty-second day.

There is nobody on nine days of road who would know it and would tell her, and the one man sitting against the post with his legs out in front of him has been keeping nine hundred years of other people’s numbers and she has not asked him for one of his own.

She said it out loud at the ninth hour on the twenty-seventh of a month, in the ordinary voice, to the trough and the post and the flat ground, with six people on it and not one of them hers.

How many days is it.

Nothing came back. The trough did not have that in it and the ground under her feet is nine hundred years old and has never once answered a question of hers that was not about a weight or a length.

The man against the post had his eyes open and he was looking up the strip road and she would not put it to him and he did not offer.

She sat on the rail with her hands hanging between her knees and the bar across them and the answer not coming, and she went on not saying anything to the man four feet off, and the day went on past her the way it does.

The eleventh day is tomorrow.
