# A Smaller Answer Than A Yes

She went to a wall on the twenty-first of the month beginning 1,634 and asked her fourth question and got back something smaller than the first answer she ever got, and she spent the rest of the day and most of the night understanding that she would rather have had the first one.

## What She Decided On The Nineteenth

She decided it at the ninth hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,634 at a flat stone at the foot of a ladder, and she told nobody and she did not write it down.

Three days, twenty-two miles each way, and forty-four of them. She had been making it forty-six for a month by counting the two miles past the gate twice, and she stopped doing that at the ninth mile on the made road because it did not make her any less tired.

She was going to do it in one push.

Out at the second hour of the twentieth with a loaf and a half and a skin, walk through the night, stand at the wall from the seventh hour of the twenty-first to the eighth, lie up a mile short of it, come back at the seventh hour of the twenty-second, stand an hour, and walk home and be at the post at the second hour of the twenty-third.

One loaf and a half for three days. No sleep between the two stands. And the reason she decided it, which is the reason she is ashamed of, is that she was working out what she could afford.

She is lighter than she was on the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 1,604. She went through the fourth count on the made road on the fifteenth for seven and she has not had a day under four since. There is not a whole lot left in her to spend on not walking, and a day of not walking is a day of not having asked.

## The Man With The Notebook

He was on the made road at the fourth hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 1,634, four miles east of the gate, walking west with a road under him and a horse and a load, and there was another man walking beside him at nine feet off with a notebook shut under his arm.

She had not seen him in four months and she had not once gone looking.

He did not stop. He came level, and he did not come inside nine feet, and he said good to her the way a man says good to somebody on a road.

She stopped.

He stopped, because she stopped, and he waited for her to say what she wanted, and she wanted to say it and she had it ready in about a second, which is faster than it used to be.

## Whether A Person Who Asks Is Safer

“Is a person who asks about a list safer than a person who does not know it is there.”

She said it in the ordinary voice on a made road at the fourth hour of the twenty-first of a month beginning 1,634 with a bar on her shoulder and a man nine feet off.

He did not answer straight away. He put his hand flat on the notebook under his arm the way a man does to a thing he has not opened in a while.

“I do not know,” he said.

“All right,” Ning Yan said.

“No,” he said. “That is the answer and then there is the other thing, and I am going to say it because you stood on a road and stopped for it and not because I have been waiting to say it.”

He looked at the road and not at her.

“You asked me,” the man said. “That is the third question anybody has ever asked me that I would rather not answer.”

That is all of it. He said nine words of ordinary sentence and then he put his weight forward and went four feet off again and she stood in the road with a bar on her shoulder until he was forty yards off.

He did not ask her one question. He has never asked her one question and he did not ask her what she was doing four miles from a gate at the fourth hour of the morning.

## The Wall On The Twenty-First

She stood four feet into the opening at the west jamb at the seventh hour of the twenty-first of a month beginning 1,634 with the sun on the wall above her and her mouth dry again.

She had been walking for twenty-nine hours and she had eaten a third of a loaf.

He was on his own side of the plank table with his hands flat on the wood, and he had not lifted either of them off it when she came onto the stone.

She waited a count of four and four times that, and then she looked past him once at the door shut on eleven feet of boards and did not look at it twice.

## Will You Tell Me When

“Will you tell me when.”

Six words. She had had it ready since the second hour on the made road and she said it in the ordinary voice, standing up, in daylight, on her own side.

He looked at her for four seconds.

“There is a day,” he said.

## What She Was Given

Here is what happened to the rest of that hour, and she has gone over it eleven times and it comes out the same way every time.

He said four words.

The first answer she ever got from him was at the seventh hour of the twenty-fifth of a month beginning 1,604, when she asked him whether her question had gone in and he said *Yes.*

One word. A whole word. A thing with both ends on it.

A day is not a whole word. A day is a shape with a number somewhere inside it and he has put the shape on the table between them and he has not put the number in her hand, and he is not going to, and she cannot ask for it because she has four questions in the book already and the first one is holding the other three.

She asked him a yes or no question and he gave her a shape, and a shape cannot be worked with.

## A Smaller Answer Than A Yes

She worked out what she had actually been given while lying in the low broken ground a mile short of the wall at the ninth hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 1,634, with a third of a loaf left in the bag and no light anywhere on that ground.

On the twenty-fifth of a month beginning 1,604 she got a whole word and she had gone home and been happy about it for a day.

Today she got less than a whole word and she cannot put it down.

Because a shape can be counted. There are a hundred and something ways a day can be a day and a number inside one of them is a number, and she has been counting things since she was eleven years old and she is going to be at that wall every day between now and the end of her life trying to get the figure out of a shape that he has already decided to give her.

She had nothing this morning. Now she has a day with nothing in it.

That is worse, and she is the only person in the world who can say so, and she lay on the ground in the dark for a while being angry about it in a way she had not been angry about anything for four months.

## The Morning Of The Twenty-Second

She came back at the seventh hour of the twenty-second of the month beginning 1,634 and stood in the opening for an hour and he did not say one word and she did not say one word, and she had forty-four miles of road in front of her and a third of a loaf.

That hour is the one she would go back to. She stood four feet into an opening at the west jamb with the sun coming over the top of it and her right arm open and hanging, and a man nine feet off on his own side of a plank table with his hands flat on the wood, and neither of them said anything, for a full hour, in daylight.

She has stood in an opening four feet off a man nine hundred years old for an hour and it is the longest that has ever happened and nothing at all came of it.

She had walked twenty-nine hours and eaten a third of a loaf when she stood in it and that is all of it, and she stood there for an hour anyway, and afterwards she could not have told anybody why.

Two men went west on the made road inside that hour and neither of them came inside twenty yards, and a cart went out at about the fortieth minute and the driver did not look, and the whole of the hour was ordinary, and ordinary is the thing she cannot put down.

## The Road West After

She left the opening at the eighth hour of the twenty-second of the month beginning 1,634 with the sun still on the top of the wall behind her.

There is a day. That is what she was carrying, and it is a shape with a number somewhere inside it that he has decided she does not get.

She got as far as the bar on two posts and sat down on the ground at the foot of it, and she worked out there, with her arm open and the light going, that she had come out here for a yes and had gone home with a shape, and that she would rather have had the yes.
