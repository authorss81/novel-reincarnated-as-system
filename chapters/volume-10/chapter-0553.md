# What Else Is On It

She said four words out loud at a wall on the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 1,604, at the seventh hour, in daylight, with a boy of fifteen four feet off, and it cannot be taken back.

## The Eleventh Hour Of The Twenty-Third

She got up off the bench at the eleventh hour of the twenty-third with the bar on her shoulder and a loaf and a half in the top of the bag, and she went off the turn into the dark in the direction she had turned round in two days before, and she made six miles of made road by the fourth hour of the morning.

There was nothing on it. Not a cart, not a rider, not a man with a notebook, and she went the whole way without seeing one light, and that is the first time in two months she has walked in the dark that far and not once come round on somebody.

She sat down once, at the third mile, in the low broken ground off the north side, for nine minutes, which is the second time in three days that she has sat down on that ground.

## The Gate On The Twenty-Fourth

The boy of fifteen was on the chain at the sixth hour and he put it down, the way he has done it ten times in two months now, and asked the question.

“I am not going through,” Ning Yan said. “I am going to the wall.”

“All right,” the boy said, and picked the chain up, and then looked at her standing there with a bar on her shoulder and did not put it down again.

She had it in her mouth for two minutes and she was not going to get it out faster than that.

She has asked a boy of eleven whether a rung was loose and a foreman whether she could have the drain. Neither of them was a keeper.

## The First Time She Has Asked Anybody For Anything

“Will you walk the last two miles with me,” Ning Yan said.

The boy of fifteen did not answer straight away. He looked at the chain in his hands and then up the made road east, where it goes two miles to a wall with an opening in it, and then back at her.

“The chain has to be up for the cart,” he said.

“And when does the cart come.”

“There is one most hours.”

“All right,” Ning Yan said. “I will be two miles off your chain. If a cart comes before I am back, put it up and let it through and do not come after me.”

He thought about it for four seconds.

“It is four miles there and back,” he said. “I have never been past the wall.”

“I know,” Ning Yan said. “That is why I am asking you and not anybody else.”

He put the chain down and hung it on the post and stood there.

That is the whole of the first asking. A boy of fifteen at a gate agreed to walk two miles and stand four feet off a wall, and the reason he did it was not kindness and she did not say one word about kindness and he did not ask her to.

## Two Miles On Made Ground

He was faster than she expected and he talked for nine minutes and then stopped, which is the ordinary order of it.

He asked her what she was going to say. She told him, because she worked out in a mile that if she was going to have him standing there she was not going to have him standing there not knowing.

“You are going to ask him what else he keeps,” the boy said.

“Yes.”

“All of it at once?”

“No,” Ning Yan said. “I have got four words. What else is on it.”

He looked at that for a while and then he said, and it is the only thing he said about it:

“My father put a name down at a post once.”

She did not ask him about his father. She walked beside a boy of fifteen for another mile with a bar on her shoulder and she did not ask him one question about it, because he had not asked to be asked and she has been on the other side of that for eight years and she meant to stay there.

## The Wall

They came onto the flat at the seventh hour with the light behind them.

The wall is higher than a person and the opening in it is four feet wide and there is a plank table standing in it on two trestles with nothing on it, and a man on his own side of it with his hands flat on the wood.

She had seen it four days ago on her own and had got nothing out of that but a place.

She did not say his name. She had told herself at the wall on the thirteenth of a month that one time was all a name is worth and she was not going to go back and take it back now that there was somebody in the hearing.

She stopped four feet into the opening with the metalled road at her back.

The boy of fifteen stopped behind and to the left, four feet off, and stood there and said nothing at all.

## The Quarter Of An Hour Before She Said It

She had the four words ready and she did not say them for about nine minutes.

She had been ready since the fourth mile of the strip road and she went over them the whole way like a woman going over a key, and standing on a dressed stone at the west jamb of a wall in daylight with a boy four feet off behind her she found out what she actually had, which was that she was not refusing.

She was about to be refused.

That is the whole of it and it took her nine minutes to get it down to that shape. She has been the person who says no in the room for eight years and she has never once been the person standing on the other side of it waiting to hear it, and there is no practice for it anywhere in nineteen years of her life.

Her mouth went dry at about the fifth minute. That is not a figure of speech either. She has been hungry for twenty days and she has been lighter than she has ever been since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 and her mouth went dry at the fifth minute and she had to swallow twice to say anything at all.

And she wanted it. That is what was in her and she would not have believed it at the ninth of a month. She wanted a man with his hands flat on a plank to look up off it and answer her, and there was no arrangement of her whole life that had prepared her for wanting something this badly from somebody who has never done her a kindness.

She looked past him once. There is a door in there with eleven feet of boards in it and it has been shut since before she came to this ground and she is not going to look at it again.

## What Else Is On It

She waited for a count of four, which is a habit and not a decision, and then she said it.

“What else is on it,” Ning Yan said.

In the ordinary voice. Standing up. In daylight, at the seventh hour of the twenty-fourth of a month, with a man nine feet off on his own side of a table and a boy of fifteen four feet behind her in the hearing.

It took two seconds and it is four words long and she has said it nine times in her life and eight of them were to a trough or to herself in the dark.

## Nothing Came

Nothing came.

She waited for the length of time she had decided to wait, which was a full count, and then she waited four times that, standing on dressed stone with the sun on the wall above her and her hands down at her sides.

He did not look up off the table. That is the first thing and it is worth setting down, because she had built the last four days of her life on a man putting his hands flat on that plank for fifteen days in a row and answering six questions, and she had gone out here with the belief that he would look up.

He did not look up off the table, and he did not answer, and nothing came out of the air either.

She waited for the thing that has counted with her since she was eleven to say something about any of it, the way it has said something or nothing about every single thing she has ever done, and it did not say anything, and it has not said anything for a long time.

Behind her the boy of fifteen shifted his weight from one foot to the other and said nothing.

## What It Looks Like From Four Feet Off

That is what she found out at the wall, and she had not thought of it in three days of counting, and a boy of fifteen found it out for her in a minute because he had nothing else to do with his eyes.

He looked at the table, and at the opening, and at the back of her, and then he said it to her in a low voice, not to the man, in the way a boy says something at a gate.

“He does not look at you,” the boy said.

“No,” Ning Yan said.

She stood there with that and she did not have anything to put against it, and it is true, and it went through her at about the fourth minute.

She had asked a man what else he keeps, in the open, in daylight, with a person hearing it, and the wall did not open and the table did not get anything on it and the door shut on eleven feet of boards did not come open, and the person she put her question in front of had watched a man not look up while he did it.

She had thought for three days that asking out loud was the thing that would cost her.

It cost her that, and it took four seconds, and it was not the one she had budgeted.

## He Took His Hands Off The Table

Then, at about the eighth minute, he took his hands off the plank.

He has not done that once in ninety days. She has seen him do it four times in ninety days and three of them were because he was about to leave, and she has known about his hands for thirty-seven days and he has never once taken one of them off that table while she was standing at the front of it.

He put them down at his sides and stood up straight, which took a while, and he looked at her for the first time since she had been at the wall.

He did not look at the boy.
