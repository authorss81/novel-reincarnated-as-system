# The One Thing It Bought

She said a name at a wall on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,724 and it bought her exactly one thing, and she worked out which one in the four days it took her to walk home, and she has had twenty-three days of carrying it and thirty-one days left to live in it.

## Two Days West

She left the flat at the ninth hour of the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,724 and made the gate by the third hour of the twenty-sixth and the strip road by the ninth.

The boy of fifteen walked the first two miles with her and then stopped at the chain and said good to her and did not ask her one question about it, which is what that boy has done for four months and has never once done otherwise.

She lay down in the broken ground north of the made road and slept for two hours and forty minutes with her bag under her head, nine miles from anything, and she did not dream and she did not wake up once.

On the twenty-seventh at the ninth hour she came onto the made road at the turn with her bar over her left shoulder and her right arm hanging and she was a day and a half lighter than when she went east, and she has been lighter every single day since the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,544 without one day of it going back.

She got to the post at the second hour of the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,724.

## The First Thing She Did

She took the book of eight leaves out of her coat at the fourth hour of the twenty-eighth, on the flat stone at the foot of the ladder, in daylight.

The first leaf has a date and a place on it and nothing else. The second has four sentences on it. The other six are hers.

She sat there with a stub of a nib and a cake of ink she has not used in two months and she put the nib to the first of the six and she held it there for a long time.

Then she put it away.

Not because she has decided not to write in them. She decided that on the tenth of a month beginning 1,634 and it has not moved and she is not going to move it on the twenty-eighth of this one because she has said a name out loud.

Six blank leaves she has chosen not to write in are a sentence she is writing to somebody every day, and she does not know who, and she has not known for two months, and she is not going to find out by using them.

## Seventeen Days

She stayed at the post, and seventeen days is what it took, and she did not know that on the first of them.

The thirtieth of the month beginning 1,724 she spent on the flat stone with her back against the ladder. The first of the month beginning 1,754 she spent the same way.

On the second she walked east to the store, fourteen miles, and stood at a counter and asked the woman of fifty one question, which is the first question she has put to anybody in thirty-three days, and it was when the next board goes down.

The woman said the second, in the ordinary voice.

That was the last time she would be paid anything for stone, and she worked the face on the fourth and the fifth anyway and came back with nothing, and she had known she would come back with nothing at the fourth hour of the fourth, and she went out on the fourth, because the alternative was sitting on a flat stone under a ladder.

The arm was open on six days of those seventeen. On the ninth she could not get it above her shoulder and she did eight of the nine barrows with it hanging and her back and her knees, and the man with the chalk said every figure out loud as he wrote it and did not once look at her arm.

On the eleventh a board came down the strip road and the boy of eleven carried the near end of it and put it against the rail and got his breath back, which took him longer than it should have.

She was standing on the flat stone when it came.

She had four things ready and she asked none of them, and the reason she gave herself afterward, sitting on the stone with her bag on her knees, was that a boy of eleven had already told her once that a thing said out loud where two people are is out, and she was not going to hand the same two words to a second person on nine days of road in the same month.

He said nothing either. He has said nothing to her since the seventeenth except what a boy says to a rail.

And she stood there and understood something she had not understood on the twenty-eighth, which is that saying a name once in a yard eight years ago bought her nothing, and saying it twice in front of two different people would have bought her less than nothing, and the whole of the difference between a girl who has said a thing and a girl who has said a thing twice is that the second one has made it ordinary.

She has one mouth and it is nineteen years old and she is going to spend it carefully, and that is a thing she decided on the eleventh of a month beginning 1,754, standing on a flat stone, and it is the only decision those seventeen days produced.

## The Shut Door On The Fourteenth

She walked east out of the post on the fourteenth of the month beginning 1,754 at the second hour, on no reason at all, and she did not go to the mill and she did not go to the face.

She made the turn at the seventh and stood on it for eleven minutes and then went forty feet down the lane to a shut door.

She had told herself on the road out there that she was going to the mill. She was not going to the mill.

She stood in the yard for two hours and twenty minutes and the standing stone was where it was and leaning two fingers out of true and she put her hand on the top of it and it was cold, and the bar was across the outside of the door, and she did not open it, and she would not, and she has said so four times now.

And there was lime under the door that had not been there on the fourth of the month beginning 1,694.

There was a scatter of lime under the door on the fourth, and it had gone hard, and she had walked in over it and out over it without noticing a thing. On the fourteenth of the month beginning 1,754 there was fresh lime over the old, pale and loose and not set, in a band a foot wide at the hinge end, and the grit of it was ground into the barrow-way where somebody had swept it out and then stepped back in.

She stood in it for twenty minutes.

Somebody had been out of that house in the last four days and had put a thing down and had come back in, and nobody on nine days of road knew it but her, and she did not know what the thing was, and she has never in her life known what it was.

## The One Thing

Here it is, and she had twenty-three days of it to work out, and she got it on the seventeenth on the stones at the turn in nine minutes, and it is shorter than she thought it would be.

He did not write it.

Two words went into the air at the seventh hour of the twenty-fifth of a month beginning 1,724 in daylight, and a man of nine hundred years had a brush in a bag eleven feet behind him and did not turn round and get it.

So the name is not in the book.

It is not in nine hundred years. It will not be true on the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784. It will not be in a thing anybody can ask after, and there is no line on it, and no keeper holds it, and nobody on nine days of road knows a thing about it except a boy of fifteen at a gate who did not hear two words and a carter four hundred yards east who saw a girl say them and did not come closer.

So she can say it again. Any day. In a yard, in the dark, to herself, in the open, for nothing, and it will cost her exactly what it cost her on the twenty-fifth, which was nothing.

Eight years of keeping one thing shut on purpose, and all it bought is that she can say it.

## What She Does Not Have To Live With

And this is the part she was not ready for and she got it lying on the flat stone at the eighth hour of the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,724.

She does not have to carry it.

He took the four sentences and he has not let them go stale and he is not going to. She gave him a house with a standing stone in the yard on the twenty-seventh of a month beginning 1,694 and his hands stayed on the plank, and that is in there now and it will be true on the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784 whether or not it is true on any day in between.

The name of her brother is not in there. It is in her mouth, where she can let it out and put it away as many times as she has days.

So there is one thing she has said in her life that she can put down.

And she has never once wanted to put it down less than she wanted to put the other four things down, and she is nineteen, and she understood a minute after she said it that she is not going to be able to put this one down either, and that it does not matter, because it was never going to be in a book anyway.

## The Five Numbers She Wrote

She counted them because she counts, and the count came out the way it came out on the fourth of the month beginning 1,694 and she let it.

She wrote five of them and not thirty-one of them, and she wrote five on purpose.

Fifty-four, on the twenty-fifth, at the wall, with the name in the air and nothing bought.

Fifty-one, on the twenty-eighth, at the foot of a ladder, with a nib in her hand and a blank leaf in front of her.

Forty-seven, on the second of the month beginning 1,754, on the stones at the turn.

Forty-one, on the eighth, on the flat stone, with her finger in the dust.

And thirty-one on the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,754, and it is the last number she has and the first one she has ever had that she cannot get more of by walking faster.

She did not write the forty-six. She has never once left a day out of a column in her life and she left twenty-six of them out on purpose and did not tell anybody, not even the boy of eleven, who is the only person on this ground she has ever told a lie to by leaving a number out.

The lever is still there. Asking twice. One asking put into the air twice so that both of them go in and neither gets answered, and it would come to the first one sooner or it would never come to it at all.

She has carried it on purpose for a hundred and nineteen days and she is carrying it now and she did not pull it on the twenty-fifth of a month beginning 1,724 in the best hour of her life, when she had a name in the air and a man nine hundred years in front of her and no question standing between the two of them.

She checked it at the flat stone at the fourth hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,754 and she rubbed it out of the dust with her hand and wrote it again, which is the thing she has done with it every day, and then she sat there with a bar over her shoulder and an arm that was open on two days in five and a body she could not weigh anywhere and thirty-one days.

## What Is In Front Of Her

Everything anybody has told her about where her brother is points to a place he was.

A house four miles west of a turn with a standing stone in the yard and a bar across the door, and she walked four miles and found four marks in an empty room and no person.

A mill one mile beyond it where a man of seventy knows more about that cottage than anybody alive and less than it was.

A road east where a carter put four on a board and took them away and did not come back for a long time.

A book nine hundred years deep with a day on it and a name in it and nothing anybody will read out loud.

And the sixth thing, which she has never approached and is not going to approach in thirty-one days, and which is a hundred yards off the east side of the same wall she has stood at twenty-two times, and is a window with one pane in it and a sill and a roof that has not come down.

Four questions about that window and she has not spent one of them.

She has never had a road. She has had four days of stone, a bar, a book with six blank leaves in it, one lever she is carrying on purpose, a loaf a woman put over a sill because she chose to, and thirty-one days to be in the world.

## The Two Things That Are Now The Same Thing

Here is where she got to on the seventeenth of the month beginning 1,754, lying on the stones at the turn with her bag under her head and nothing in it, and it took her most of the night and it is the last piece of arithmetic she has got.

Every place anybody has sent her is west of a turn.

A cottage four miles west. A mill five miles west. A lane four miles west with a woman at forty feet of it who puts bread over a sill. A bench at the turn with a woman of sixty who has not said a fifth sentence in three months. A stone face four miles west. A store five miles west behind a wheel that stopped nine years ago.

And the one thing that is east is a wall twenty-two miles off with an opening in it, and she has been to it twenty-two times, and on the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,724 a boy of fifteen walked the last two miles for her because she asked him and did not ask him one question about it.

She worked it out at the fourth hour and it came out in four seconds, and then she lay there for a long time with it.

Everything she has been given points to where he was, and the only thing in the world that is pointed at is the place where she says his name out loud and cannot take it back.

She has been following a name for seventy-four days.

There is no road to it.

There is a road to a wall.

## The Part She Is Not Going To Say To Him

There is a thing she has not asked in two months and she is going to have to ask it or not ask it in thirty-one.

Not a question at a wall. She is not allowed a fifth and the first one is standing and she has not been allowed a fifth since the twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,604 and she has not broken it and she is not going to break it now.

But he is a man she can put a thing in front of, and she has put one in front of him already, and he received it and did not put it down.

So there is a thing she could hand him on a wall in daylight, at forty-four miles and two days a time, and it is a road that was never told to her.

She has not got one.

And she is not going to go and get one out of a book nine hundred years deep by asking, because a question is the only instrument she has and every one of them takes something out of the rest, and she has four and she needs the first one, and she would rather walk four hundred miles in the wrong direction than spend one of them on a man to ask whether a brother who is alive is alive.

There is a way of standing still that costs a person the count.

That is all of it and she is not going to solve it on a flat stone.

She got up at the seventh hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,754 and picked up nine feet nine of it and went west to a place she had never been to and had no reason to go to. The last thing she did before the light came was say the name of her brother out loud into an empty yard where two people could hear it if either of them turned round.

It cost nothing.
