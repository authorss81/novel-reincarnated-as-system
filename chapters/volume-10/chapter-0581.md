# Bread Given Without Asking

She asked the woman of fifty one question on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,694 and was given a loaf and not one word about a boy, and she has been turning that over for four days and it is the whole of what she learned on the road west.

## Forty Feet In Again

She came off the strip road at the second hour of the twenty-ninth with her arm still open and walked the last four miles with it hanging, and she went down the lane past the face that is four miles long and turned in at the forty feet, because there was smoke coming out of a pot on three stones in front of a shut door.

The woman was on the stool. She looked up and she did not get up and she did not look as though she had not expected anybody.

“You are back,” the woman said.

There was nothing on the sill that day and nothing in the yard but the barrow with the wheel off it and the rope and the bucket, and the woman had a cloth on her knees and a loaf under the cloth.

“You went east,” the woman said. And then she went back to what she was doing, which was nothing she could be identified at.

That is three sentences of noticing and not one of them was a question, and Ning Yan stood in the yard and understood that the woman of fifty had watched her go east on the twenty-sixth and come back on the twenty-ninth and had put the two together and had not said so.

## The Question

She had it ready. She had had it ready for two days, on nine miles of strip road and four of made, and she had walked it past four times without stopping, and she has never in her life put a question she could not answer into a person.

“Ten years back,” Ning Yan said. “The boy who counted the loads. What became of him.”

And she said it in the ordinary voice, at a woman, in daylight, standing four feet off, which is the shape every asking on this ground has, and she had gone back over it eleven times and it is the only thing she has ever wanted off anybody that was not in a book nine hundred years deep.

## What The Woman Said

She did not stop what she was doing and she gave eight sentences and she gave them as she had them.

“You have not asked me one thing since the first time you came past this door and now you have asked one.”

“I will give you what I have and not what you asked for.”

“I do not remember his face. I remember what he did with bread.”

“He was hungry in the second winter and I gave him a loaf and he did not say thank you and I did not think less of him for it.”

“That is everything I know about him, and I have never once said how I knew it.”

“Bread goes in the body. Go on, you are grey.”

That is eight sentences and then she put the loaf in a cloth and held it out, and Ning Yan took it, and the bar across the inside of the door did not move.

## What She Did Not Get

She did not get a direction. She did not get a year. She did not get four at the end of anything, or a street, or a trade, or a thing he was carrying.

She got eight sentences about bread from a woman who has remembered a boy’s manners for ten years and has never once told anybody what her face looked like.

And she had asked for one thing. She came four miles off a face to ask one thing and she got a loaf and a woman telling her that she had not asked her a single thing since the first time she came past, which is true, and which is also a thing a woman says when she wants a girl to know that the asking counted.

## The Part That Is Going To Last

Here is the thing she has not been able to put down since, and she is putting it down at the fifth hour of the thirtieth of a month beginning 1,694 on a stone at a turn.

The woman said you have not asked her one thing since the first time she came past the door.

She said it as a fact and not as a reproach and she was correct.

A month is most of a month and most of a month is walking. In the time since she first came past that door a girl has walked three hundred and sixty miles, taken nine barrows a day off a face, been given two loaves she did not ask for, stood in an opening in a wall twice, and given four sentences to a man nine hundred years old about a house with a shut door.

And a woman of fifty at the end of a lane has counted every one of them, from a stool, through a shut door, and has said nothing about any of it, and has put bread out twice.

Ning Yan has never been looked after by a person in her life. She has been fed by people who were owed and worked for by people who were paid and refused by people who were correct.

This is a different thing and she does not have the name for it and she is not going to go and find one at four in the morning, and she is going to go and find the boy instead.

## What She Was Given

A loaf.

That is all of it, and she has been holding it in both hands at a lane mouth on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,694 while a woman of fifty wipes her hands on her sleeves, and it is the first thing anybody has given her in ninety-five days that did not come with a condition on it.

She has no wages. She has no place and no road and no shed and a board at a store with nothing on it. She has four questions standing in a book nine hundred years deep and none of them answered.

And she asked for one thing and got bread.

## What She Did Not Do

She did not ask a second question, and that was not restraint, and she wants it said plainly because everything else in this is plain.

It was that she had nothing left to ask with.

She is not allowed a fifth at the wall and she knows what happens if she puts one in anyway. She has known since the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,634 that the only lever in the world is asking twice, and asking twice is the one thing that would make the first question never get answered, and she has been carrying it on purpose for seventy days.

And standing in a lane mouth with a loaf in her hands she understood that the rules are not only at the wall. There is one keeping and one taking and a book nine hundred years deep, and a girl of nineteen cannot spend either of them.

She had spent everything she had for asking on a bar of shut door wood on the twenty-ninth of a month beginning 1,694.

## The Difference, Which Is The Whole Of It

She worked it out on the stones at the turn on the thirtieth at the fifth hour, sitting with the loaf on her knee and the light coming up behind the ground east of it, and it took her nine minutes and it is the only thing in ninety-six days she has been able to use.

A loaf is given.

A place is kept.

The woman of fifty handed her bread for no reason at all and she will go on handing it to whoever comes past, and in a month it will be inside her and there will be no record anywhere that it happened and nobody has to answer for it and nobody is worse off.

A man of nine hundred years took four sentences off her on the twenty-seventh of this month and there is no bread in that and no way to thank him for it, and it is still there.

He did not write it and he did not refuse it, and his hands stayed on the plank, and there is a version of her standing at that opening where it is true on the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784.

That is the difference. One of them gives you a thing and it is gone. The other one gives you a thing and it is nine hundred years deep and it does not have a date on it and it is not going to let go.

## What She Felt About It

She was not angry at the woman of fifty for eight sentences about bread and a door that did not open.

She was angry at herself, and she has been angry at herself for eight years and has got very little out of it, and on the thirtieth of a month beginning 1,694 she got one thing out of it and she wrote it in the dust at the turn with a finger.

She would have liked to be refused.

That is all of it and she is not going to dress it up. A person who is refused can walk away and know what they are.

A person who is handed bread at a lane mouth at the second hour of the twenty-ninth of a month beginning 1,694 has been given an account that does not close. She went east twenty-two miles three days ago and gave four sentences about a boy to a man who will not put them down. Now she has a loaf and an open arm and a lane behind her that is four miles long with a face at the end of it.

She got to the post at the ninth hour of the first of the month beginning 1,724 and she was a day and a half lighter than when she left it, and there were seventy-eight days.
