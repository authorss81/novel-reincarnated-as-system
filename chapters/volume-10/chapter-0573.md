# The Place She Was Given

She was at the house with the shut door at the ninth hour of the fourth of the month beginning 1,694 and she was still at it at the second hour of the sixth, and in between she did not go anywhere.

## The Lime Cottage

It is one room with a stone floor and a bar across the inside of the door and a hearth at the west end that has not been lit in ten years and a trough under a spout that runs when it rains.

The lime bed is behind it, out in the open, and it is a low flat place in the ground with a hard white crust on it and stone cut out of the side of it in steps that go down and then stop because the stone below them is not good.

There is a barrow-way worn into the ground from the bed to the yard, about a foot wide and about two inches deep, and it has been worn by one barrow going one way and back four hundred thousand times, and it is the only thing in that yard that is a mark a person made.

Ning Yan stood on the way at the ninth hour with a bar on her shoulder and looked down it and it did not take her anywhere.

## What She Looked For

She looked for three days of worth of the habits of a person and she found four things, and she is going to put all four down because there were only four.

A barrow-way, worn, one width.

A hearth with the middle of the fire never laid on one side.

A nail in the frame of the door at the height of a hand, with a mark around it where something hung that was not a tool.

And a place on the sill of the one window where a thing had been set down often enough to take the grey off.

That is everything ten years leaves in a house, and she stood in it and counted it the way she counts everything, and the count was four and none of the four was a person.

## The Night She Went To The Door

She got up off the stone floor at the second hour of the fifth of the month beginning 1,694 and went to the inside of the door and put her left hand on the bar across it.

She stood there for a long time with her hand on it and she is not going to describe what she thought about, because what she thought about was whether there was anything in a shut house worth having, and she worked that out in about nine seconds and the answer was no, and she put the bar back down on its brackets.

It was not a temptation. She wants that said because it is the truth and it is the duller thing.

It was a check. She had been given a door and she wanted to know what was on the other side of it the way she wants to know everything, once, with her own hands, so that whatever anybody tells her afterwards she can weigh against what she saw.

And she could not weigh it, because the bar was on the inside and nobody had said she might not open it and nobody was going to stop her.

She stood there with her hand on a bar of wood and worked out that a girl who opens a door that is shut on her is doing the one thing she has spent eight years not doing, and that she is doing it at two in the morning for no reason at all except that she cannot stand a shut thing she has not been inside of.

She put it back down and went and lay on the floor with her back against it and did the count.

And she is not proud of that either, and she is not going to pretend to anybody that she was, because she is a person who opens things.

## The Fifth And The Sixth

She was not hungry. She ate a quarter of the loaf on the fifth and a quarter on the sixth and put the rest in the bag and it did not get any better or worse in two days, because that is what a loaf does in two days in a dry room.

Her arm was open on the fifth and shut on the sixth at the third hour, and it shut of its own accord in fourteen, which is the ordinary span, and she did not hurry it.

She slept on the stone floor with her back against the bar and her bag under her head and she was awake most of the night, and what she did in the night was the count, and the count went a hundred and five, a hundred and four, a hundred and three, and on the second morning she got as far as a hundred and one and then stopped doing it, because it is the same number wearing different clothes.

## The Thing She Was Actually Doing There

And here is the part she has not told anybody, including herself, until the second hour of the sixth of the month beginning 1,694, lying on a stone floor with her hands behind her head.

She was not looking for him.

She had been telling herself since the ninth hour of the fourth that she had come four miles to find out about a boy, and that was true for about a mile and a half, and then it stopped being the reason.

What she was actually doing was sitting still.

That is all. Two days in a shut house on a stone floor with a bar across the inside of the door, doing the count and sleeping in pieces and eating a quarter of a loaf twice a day, and not going anywhere.

She has not sat still for two days in nine months. She has worked nine miles between the second hour and the seventh. She has walked four hundred and eighty-four miles in thirty-eight days. She has not had two days in a row in three years, and the reason she has not is that a person who sits still has to be somewhere she is not leaving.

And she was, and it was the best thing that had happened to her since she asked a question out loud, and she had gone four miles west to get it.

## The Thing She Wanted And Got

She wanted to want to be there.

That is the honest shape of it, and she worked it out on the sixth at the sixth hour sitting with her back against the bar in the doorframe with her knees up, and it took her a long time and she got it in the end.

She had walked nine miles of strip road and the made road and two miles past a gate in thirty-eight days and stood in an opening in a wall eleven times and said nothing at any of them, and she came four miles west to a shut door and got the first two days in a month she has not spent on her feet.

That is what a place to be is. It is not a roof. It is a piece of ground where a person is not doing the next thing.

She had it for two days and she was nineteen and it was the best two days she has had since the twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,604, and she is not going to be ungrateful about that, and she is also not going to lie to herself about what it cost, because she has been counting since she was eleven.

## What It Cost

Here is the bill, and she did it at the sixth hour of the sixth of a month beginning 1,694, on a stone floor, in daylight, with the door open a hand in width.

She was at a wall eleven times in thirty-eight days. She was at one of them on the third of this month.

She has been four miles off that wall since the seventh hour of the fourth and she has gone east once in two days and that once was a woman on a bench and a lane.

She was not able to ask anything on the fourth. She was not able to ask anything on the fifth. She is not able to ask anything today.

She did not decide not to. There was no moment where she weighed it and chose the shut door. She came west at the ninth hour on the strength of a thing a woman said at the end of a lane, and she walked four miles, and now she is at a shut door, and there is no road between here and a wall that is twenty-two miles long in one direction and nine in the other.

A girl who has a place to be is not a girl who asks.

That is all it cost, and it was not the door. The door cost nothing. The door is a bar of wood across the opening and a nail in the frame at the height of a hand.

## What She Is Going To Do About It

She did not leave on the sixth.

She said out loud to an empty yard that she was going to leave at the seventh hour and go east and that the four miles back was four miles and that there was a face at the end of the thirteen and that the board goes down on the second of a month and she had better be at the face when it does.

And then she lay on the stone floor with her hands behind her head and did not move for another hour and forty minutes, and the reason she gave herself, and she wrote it down in the dust with a finger so that it would be there in the morning, was this.

The place is where he was.

He was ten years back and he is not there now, and she has walked four miles and stood in a yard and found a barrow-way and a hearth and a nail and a clean place on a sill, and none of the four is him and none of the four is anywhere he is.

And she has two days of it, and it was the best two days in a month, and she cannot tell yet whether those two things are the same thing or two things that happened to her on the same piece of ground.
