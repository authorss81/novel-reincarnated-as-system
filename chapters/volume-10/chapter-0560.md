# Work That Does Not Want A Name

She found it on the third of the month beginning 1,634 at the head of a lane four miles west of the turn, and it was everything she had gone there to look for, and by the ninth hour of the fourth she had worked out what it was going to cost her.

## Nine Miles East And Four West

Nine miles of strip road east to the turn and then four miles west on the store road, which is a made road with a kerb on it and a bench at the turn and nothing else on it for the whole of those four.

Thirteen miles. She was at the head of the lane at the seventh hour of the third with the bar on her shoulder and her arm open on the ninth mile, and she had been walking since the second hour and she had had nothing to eat since the second hour of the twenty-sixth of the month before.

That is seven days without a loaf and she is not hungry and that has stopped being the interesting thing about her body somewhere around the sixth day.

## The Head Of The Lane

The lane is a cart track between two banks and it ends in a flat with a shed on the north side of it and a working face in the ground behind the shed, and there is stone out of the face that has been worked all summer.

There were six men on it. A breaker sitting in a shed of boards with a steel wedge and a hammer, six barrows, and a man of forty with a slip of board and a stick of chalk on a string.

Two hundred yards off there was a heap of dressed stone and a heap of the same stone that was not dressed, and the difference between them was the trade itself, and there was a bar between the two heaps.

She stood at the end of the lane and watched four barrows go out and one come back, and worked out in nine minutes that this was a thing measured by weight.

## How It Is Measured

She asked the man with the board about it at the eighth hour and he said it out loud, which is what a rate is.

“Out of the face, by the barrow,” he said. “The bar goes to the heap of dressed and the heap of dressed goes down the lane on a cart to the mill road. And the mill’s wheel has not turned since before either of us and that is not my business.”

“And the barrow.”

“The barrow is chalked on the board when it is full and the board goes down to the store on the second day and the store takes the bread off it.” He turned the board round. “A full barrow is so much bread at the rate a woman behind a counter says out loud, and she says it out loud every time, and it has been two coppers a loaf for as long as I have worked this face.”

## The Question She Had Come To Ask

She asked it at the eighth hour of the third of a month, standing in the flat with six men on it and a breaker going inside the shed.

“Do you count hands.”

He looked at her and then he looked at the board.

“No,” he said.

“That is all I wanted.”

“That is all there is,” the man said. “Nobody here is on a number. A barrow full is a barrow full and a barrow short is a barrow short and I write what is on the barrow and the store gives out what the board says and neither of those is a man.”

He said that the way the foreman said his figure, out loud, in the ordinary voice, because it is what you say when somebody has asked you what a thing is and you have answered it before.

## The Man On The Face And What He Said Twice

She asked him at the eighth hour of the third of a month and got all of it in a minute, and then she asked him two more questions at the eighth hour of the fourth, standing in the flat with the light going off the face, and both of them were about numbers and both of them were free.

“Does it matter who is on it.”

“No,” the man said. “It matters how much is in the barrow.”

“And if a barrow comes up short.”

“Then the barrow is short,” the man said, “and I put a figure against the barrow and not against the man, and the store gives out what the board says, and on the second day it goes down.”

He said that the way he says everything, which is once and out loud and without any room in it for a second meaning.

She had gone eleven miles to find a trade that does not want a name and she had found one inside a minute, and she stood in the flat and worked out that there was exactly one thing wrong with it and she had known that at the fifth hour of the first day and had talked herself past it twice.

## What She Was Afraid Of

She wanted it so badly that she stood in the flat and was frightened of it for four minutes, and she has not been frightened of anything at a post or a lane in nine months, and she worked out afterwards that it was because of the fourth of a month at the end of this one.

There is no ration drawn on hands in this trade.

Which means there is nothing that would be missed.

She has been standing at a gang for four days where nine men could not lift one of nine stones on the heavy end and a foreman with a finger could not say she was there, and she had worked out that the being-not-there was an outrage done to her by a man who was doing his job.

Here there is no outrage. Here there is not even a foreman with a finger. Here a girl with a bar can break stone from the second hour to the seventh, get into a cart short, and be short on a board, and six men and a breaker and a man with a chalk will not know that she was ever on the face at all.

That is what she went eleven miles to find and she had it standing in front of her inside two hours.

## The Fourth Of A Month

She worked it. That is the ordinary part.

A barrow is a load on two wheels and it takes eleven trips to fill one with dressed stone, and she filled four of them between the second hour and the seventh hour on the fourth of a month, and the arm was open for the best part of the second day and she did not shut it and did not try.

Her fourth count went for four inside a loaded reach nine times and came back in four six of them.

The man with the board chalked four barrows full and one barrow short, and he said the number out loud when he wrote it, which is what he does with numbers.

## What It Actually Costs

She did the sum at the seventh hour on the fourth, sitting on the bar at the end of the lane with the light going off the face, and it came out wrong for what she had planned.

The lane is four miles west of the turn. The turn is nine miles east of the post. The lane is thirteen miles from the post.

The bread is not at the lane. The bread is at the store, one mile further on, because the board goes down to the store and the store gives out what the board says.

So it is fourteen miles out and fourteen miles back for one day of stone, and she has to be at the store when it opens, and the store is behind a mill with a wheel that has not turned since before she came to this ground.

Twenty-eight miles for the days she breaks stone.

And the terms are one at a time, at the wall, in daylight, which is twenty-two miles each way and two days.

## The Question She Took Home

Here is what it comes to and she is going to have to answer it somewhere between here and the wall and she did not have it at the seventh hour of the fourth of a month.

Forty-four miles to ask. Twenty-eight miles to eat.

She has one body. She has an arm that is open four days in five and a count of four that goes for five inside a loaded reach and a count of breath that took seven once in the last four days, and she is lighter than she was on the eighth of a month beginning 1,604, and she is lighter than she was on the twenty-sixth of it.

And she cannot ask at a trough, and she cannot eat at a trough, and the two of them are twenty-two miles apart in opposite directions.

She got up off the bar and picked up her own and started walking east with nine feet nine of it on her shoulder and the board going down to the store in the morning without her on it.

Somebody is going to weigh that board on the second day and put a number against four barrows and a short one, and nobody is going to know that she was on the face.
