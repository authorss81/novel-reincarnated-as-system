# The Smallest Thing On The Page

She asked it at the seventh hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,784, four feet into an opening in a wall, on her own side, in daylight, with a boy of fifteen four feet off behind her and a carter stopped on the made road two miles west of the gate who did not get down.

That is the setting, and every part of it is one of his three terms.

One at a time. At the wall. In daylight.

Forty-four miles and two days a question, and she had made the eleven miles and the two miles and the day, and she had not broken one of them in a hundred and seventy-six days, and there was no room in her for anything else.

## What She Asked

Here is the question, and she has never written it down in any form and she is not going to now.

What else is on it.

Six words in the ordinary voice. She said it the way she says things to the man with the chalk, on a face, at the ninth hour, because a man with a chalk says figures and not speeches and it is the only voice she has that has never once cost her anything.

The plank table had nothing on it.

He had been on his own side for one hundred and seventy-six days, and he had not been asked anything in ninety days.

The second of the month beginning 1,754 was the last day on which stone of hers was ever paid for, and she said so out loud at a shut door, and he wrote the date down and did not write her name.

He is a man who keeps a place and not a person, and she has never once been able to get anything else out of him by standing near him.

He did not answer for a long time.

## The Wait

She counted it, because she counts, and she got to a hundred and eleven and then she stopped counting because a number she is watching go up is not a number she wants.

The light did not move. The opening is eight feet high and the light comes in over the eastern jamb at the seventh hour and puts a bar of it on the plank table from the west end to about the middle, and she watched it reach the middle.

The boy of fifteen was four feet off behind her and he shifted his weight from one foot to the other, once, and did not say anything.

The carter two miles west of the gate did not get down.

And the thing she had, standing there, was the six words in the air with nothing coming back under them, and it is the only sentence she has ever put into the world that nothing has ever come back under, and the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,724 was the first one of those and it cost nothing, and this one cost a hundred and seventy-six days of walking.

## Two Days To Get There

She was at the chain from the twelfth to the fourteenth of the month beginning 1,784 and she came off the far bank at the sixth hour of the fifteenth and walked eleven miles of made road with the gate at the ninth hour.

On the eighteenth she stood four feet into the opening at the seventh hour and at the ninth hour and at the second hour of the afternoon and she did not ask anything, and he did not open the book, and she went and slept in the broken ground north of the made road for two hours and forty minutes with her bag under her head and she did not dream and she did not wake up once.

On the nineteenth she came back at the fourth hour.

That is what one at a time at the wall in daylight costs in feet, and she has paid it nineteen times this year and this was the last time she is ever going to pay it.

## The Three She Had Ready

She had three other things written on the flat of her own head and she had them in order, and she did not say any of them, and she wants it on the page that she did not say them because she chose not to and not because she forgot one.

*What is it for.* That one is a question about a purpose and she does not want a purpose. She has wanted a fact since the month beginning 1,604 and she has been offered purposes her whole life and every one of them has had a person at the end of it who wanted something.

*What does the word do.* That one is about herself. She is written down as lost and has been since the month beginning 1,724 and there is a man on the other side of that table who put it there and has never said what it does, and she could have had it today at the seventh hour in daylight with a witness.

And she did not ask it, and she will not tell anybody that she did not ask it, because she was not going to spend the last asking of a list nine hundred years deep on the one word that is about her.

A number. She did not have that in her at all. She wrote a number in the dust this morning and rubbed it out and she is not going to say that either.

One at a time, and she has one left in her and there is one day left on the book, and she is going to spend it on the question she asked first.

## What He Answered

He said: “The smallest thing on it is a number.”

And then he said the number.

“Six.”

## What She Did With It

She did not move for a while.

Six.

There is a book nine hundred years deep and she has spent one hundred and seventy-six days and a hundred and forty miles walking for that word, and a man of nine hundred years gave her a number with two digits in it in the ordinary voice on the morning the book shuts.

She counted it once, out loud, without deciding to.

Six.

And then, because she is nineteen and has been counting since she was eight years old and because a number is the only thing in the world she can hold:

There were four on the chain.

The fourth hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 1,784, a man with a kettle and a dog and a chain at the near end of a cut in a made road, four of them and two with them, and she counted them going across and counted them again at the fifth hour when they were small on the made road, and it was four both times.

She had thought the number was four.

There are six on the page.

That is all of what she had and it came out of her standing four feet into an opening with a bar on her shoulder.

## What It Is Not

It is not a name and he did not give her one and she had told him on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,724 that his book is not going to have one from her.

Two names are missing from a page of six and there are four people gone east off a road and she cannot tell her that it is the same four, because she was not asked a question and she did not get an answer, and a man who keeps a chain does not keep a list either.

It is not her. Her own name went into that book at the month beginning 1,724 as lost and it is not coming out of it today and no shut book can take it out, and that is one of the six or it is not one of the six and she did not ask, because she has four questions and she has had one today.

It is not her brother. It has never been in nine hundred years. She said it out loud in an opening on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,724 and a man with a brush in a bag behind him did not turn round and get it, and that is the one thing about that day that came out right and she has had five weeks to want it to be a lie.

So the six are six people she has never met, going somewhere she cannot walk to, on a day she will not be told.

## What He Said After

He said one more thing and it was not in answer to anything, and she is putting it down because it is the only sentence he has given her in eight months that was not a term or a date.

“The number is not a favour,” he said. “You asked at the wall and the number is what is on it. It would have been six whether you came or not.”

He was right about that and she could not bear it for about an hour afterward.

She said: “I know.”

And she did not say anything else, and she did not say thank you, because a man of nine hundred years does not get thanked by a girl who asked him a question in daylight, and she has not said it once in nine years and she was not going to start.

## What Is Left Standing

Three questions.

What it is for, asked on the thirtieth of the month beginning 1,604. What does the word do, asked on the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,634. And the one with an answer already in it, *will you tell me when*, answered *there is a day*, which is the only answer she ever got out of him that did not cost her something.

They go into a book that shuts at the end of this day, and they stand there, and there is no fifth and there will never be one, and after today there is no book in the world she is permitted to put them into.

She stood in the opening until the eighth hour. He did not say anything else and she did not ask him anything else, because one at a time is one of the three terms and she had just used hers.

At the eighth hour he put his hands flat on the plank table and left them there, and she understood that he had done the thing he has done at the end of every one of the hundred and seventy-six days she has stood there, which is that he stops talking first.

The light had come off the plank by then. The opening is eight feet high and the light comes in over the eastern jamb at the seventh hour and puts a bar of it on the wood from the west end to about the middle, and by the eighth hour it was gone off the table and onto her boots.

The boy of fifteen said good to her at the eighth hour and went off up the made road toward the gate and did not say one word about anything, which is what that boy has done for five months and has never once done otherwise.

The carter two miles west of the gate was not there any more.

She said the number out loud once more, standing four feet into an empty opening.

“Six.”

And then she went and stood on the flat outside the jamb on her own side and waited, because there was one hour left of the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,784 and she wanted to be standing in daylight when it shut.
