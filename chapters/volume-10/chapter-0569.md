# A Day Is Not A Thing She Can Hurry

She went thirty-eight days without putting a question into the air and she stood in an opening in a wall eleven times, and on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,664 a man of nine hundred years told her that a day is not a thing she can hurry, and gave her one.

## Thirty-Eight Days

Here is the whole of it and she wrote it out at the head of a lane at the eighth hour of the second of the month beginning 1,694, on a board of flat stone, with the chisel marks of other people in it.

Thirty-eight days, from the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,634 to the second of a month beginning 1,694.

She made eleven stands in an opening in a wall, on her own side, in daylight, at the seventh hour, and said nothing at any of them.

Four hundred and eighty-four miles of road, of which a hundred and ninety-eight was strip road on a body that went lighter again somewhere in the middle of it and that she has not been able to put back since.

Four questions in a book nine hundred years deep, unasked on, and not one of them answered.

That is the entire account and it is everything those thirty-eight days bought.

## What She Changed On The Twenty-Fifth

She changed it on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,634 at the sixth hour, sitting at the foot of a ladder with nothing in her bag, and she did it in four minutes and it is the only method that has ever worked on a wall.

She stopped asking.

She had worked out at the ninth hour of the twenty-third that she could not ask for a date without putting a fifth question into that book, and that the first question was holding the rest of it, and that every question she added made it worse.

So she stopped asking, and she went and stood there, and she said nothing, and she went home, and she did it again.

The first time she did it she felt like a fool by the fourth mile west. The second time she felt like a fool by the second hour at the wall. The third time she stopped feeling like anything at all about it and that was worse, because it meant the standing had stopped being a thing she was doing to a man and had started being a thing she was doing to herself.

## The First Nine Stands

They were all the same and she has not got nine separate days out of them and she is not going to invent any.

He was on his own side of the plank table with his hands flat on the wood.

She stopped four feet into the opening at the west jamb.

She said nothing for the length of an hour and then she went west.

On the fourth stand her arm was open for all of it and she stood an hour with it hanging and it did not shut and it does not shut on demand.

On the sixth stand there was a cart on the made road that stopped four hundred yards off and a man got off it and looked at the wall and got back on and went on, and he did not come closer, and she stood there and let a stranger see her standing there doing nothing.

On the ninth stand she had stopped counting his hands and she counted her own breath instead, and it was four on the seventh hour of a month and four on the eighth hour of a month, and it came back in four every time.

## What Changed In Her Body

She is nineteen years old. She is lighter than she was on the eighth of the month beginning 1,604 and lighter than she was on the twenty-sixth of it and lighter than she was on the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544, and she has been lighter every single day since that eighteenth without a single day of it going back.

Her fourth count went for five inside a loaded reach nine times a day on the face at the head of a lane and came back in four seven of them.

Her right arm is open four days in five. It was open for both stands on the last of those days and it was open for all of one night on the made road at the third hour, and it came back in fourteen.

She is not hungry. She has not been hungry since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 and she has worked out that that is finished and it has stayed finished through thirty-eight days of it.

And she has never once been so tired in her life and she has never once been unable to get up.

## The Bench On The Eighth Of A Month

She went past the turn at the eighth hour of the eighth of the month beginning 1,664, on her way east with the bar on her shoulder, and the woman of sixty was on the bench with a piece of string and a needle in it.

She stopped.

The woman looked up and she said good to her, and she said nothing else, and she went back to two inches of string.

That is the whole of it and it took forty seconds.

She had sat next to that woman for a day and a night at the beginning of the month beginning 1,634 and had heard ten sentences about a mill and a lime bed and a house.

She had thought on the way west that she would come back and say thank you for them, and she has not come back in thirty-two days, and there is no arrangement anywhere on this ground by which a girl with a bar on her shoulder interrupts a woman of sixty doing string to say a thing she said once.

She stood there for another minute and then she went on east, and she got to the bar on two posts four hundred yards out at the third hour of the ninth, went past it, and got to the wall at the seventh hour.

She stood in the opening for an hour and said nothing and he said nothing and she went west.

## What He Said On The Twenty-Ninth Of A Month

She stood in the opening at the seventh hour of the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,664, on her own side, in daylight, on her own two feet with nothing said in front of her, and at the fortieth minute he took his hands off the plank.

He put them down at his sides and stood up straight, which took a while, the way it does.

“A day is not a thing you can hurry,” he said.

She did not move and she did not say anything, because she had not come for a sentence and a sentence was not what she had come for, and she had told herself on the made road at the ninth mile that she would take the first thing he gave her and not ask what it was.

## The Nineteenth

He looked at her for four seconds and then he said:

“The nineteenth.”

Two words. She had asked him a yes or no question on the twenty-first of the month beginning 1,634 and he had given her a shape, and now he has given her one number out of it, and it is not the number she wanted, which is a date, and it is not a yes.

The nineteenth of a month.

That is what she carried twenty-two miles west on the twenty-ninth of a month beginning 1,664, and she could not put it anywhere, because she does not know which month, and a day without a month is not a day.

She did the whole of it twice at the flat stone at the foot of a ladder, on the thirtieth, with a finger in the dust.

The nineteenth of any month is the same nineteen times over. The nineteenth of the month that is going. The nineteenth of the next one. The nineteenth of a month nine years off. There is no way to tell one of those from another without the month, and the month is the thing she does not have.

## What She Decided In The Dark After

She decided it on the first of the month beginning 1,694, at the fifth hour, lying a mile short of a wall with a bar under her head and the twenty-third of the month beginning 1,634’s arithmetic going round and round in her head.

She is not going to ask him for the month.

She is not going to put a fifth question into that book and she is not going to say a name at that wall and she is not going to say anything else at all, ever, in that opening, on her own side, in daylight.

She is going to go back and stand there, and she is going to stand there until he finishes a sentence he has already started twice.

That is everything she has left and it is the worst of it, because it requires her to go on doing the one thing that has been closing a list for thirty-eight days, on the chance that a man of nine hundred years will get to the end of his own sentence in front of a person who is not going anywhere.

She lay in the dark with the number on her.

She has a day of a month and no month, and she cannot ask, and she is not going to stop going.
