# Six Empty Leaves That Are Hers

She carried a book of eight leaves against her ribs for two days and did not open it, and when she did open it she found that the thing it had been made for was over and the thing it was for had not started.

## The Twelfth Of A Month

She got up at the second hour of the twelfth and went thirteen miles west with the bar and worked the face until the seventh hour and then walked back and did nine miles of strip road east in the dark.

She had it in her coat the whole way. That is the first thing to put down and it is not a small thing.

From the seventh hour of the eleventh of the month beginning 1,634 to the third hour of the thirteenth, a book of eight leaves made out of a blanket went against her left side under a bar, and she had a hand on it four hundred times, which is the amount of times a person touches a thing they cannot see and are not sure is still there.

She did not open it in daylight on the first day. She told herself it was because it was raining at the lane and it was not raining at the lane.

## Eight Leaves Made Out Of A Blanket

She opened it at the seventh hour of the twelfth, on the flat at the foot of the ladder, in daylight, sitting on the flat stone with her back against the ladder upright.

A sheet of blanket folded in half three times is eight leaves and it is the size of a closed hand and it has a corner gone out of it, which came off in a room with boards for a floor on the far ground a long time ago and which she never repaired.

The first leaf has a date on it and a place under the date and nothing else.

The second leaf has four sentences on it in a hand half an inch tall.

There are six leaves with nothing on them at all.

She has read all of it once, in a room, a long time ago. She did not read it again on the twelfth and she wants that written down because it took her four minutes at a flat stone not to.

## What Is On The Second Leaf

Four lines and four things that are true.

The reason they are true is not that she worked them out twice. It is that the hand cannot go anywhere else.

That is the whole arrangement and it has been the arrangement since she was seventeen years old, and a person with two hands and a whole right arm could have written four sentences that were nearly what she thinks, and these are exactly what she thinks.

She looked at the four lines for nine minutes on the flat at the foot of a ladder and she did not say them out loud, because she has said them out loud once, at an opening in a wall, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, to a man, and he did not write anything and he did not agree and she went home.

That is everything the book was made for. It was made to be said once, out loud, to one person, and to be right, and that happened and it is finished.

## The Six Empty Ones

She said at the ninth hour of a month a long time ago, standing at a table, that she was going to leave them, and that the empties were hers and that no one else was going to fill them.

That was before a wall on the twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,604, and before three terms, and before a word with four letters at the end of her own entry in a book nine hundred years deep.

Six blank leaves.

Her name is on a list in a hand she has never seen, with one word after it that she cannot use, and she is going to be counted somewhere she cannot be found, and the only piece of paper in the world that has nothing on it and belongs to her is the one she has been carrying about nine days off a stone.

She has been carrying it for two days and she has not opened it and she worked out on the made road at the ninth mile on the twelfth why, and it came down to three things and all three of them are true.

## The Boy Of Eleven And The Book In Her Coat

The boy of eleven looked at her coat four hundred times on the twelfth of the month beginning 1,634 and he did not ask, because he asked her one question on the tenth and got an answer and has not asked her a second one in two days.

A book of eight leaves made out of a blanket inside a girl’s coat under a bar is not a shape anybody at a post has any reason to have an opinion about, and she is the only person on nine days of road who knows it is there.

She noticed at the eighth hour of the thirteenth that she had put it in the inner pocket against the side and not in the bag, and that she had done that on the eleventh of a month at the seventh hour without deciding to.

## The Three Reasons

She cannot write in it because there is nothing in it that she has not already said out loud, and one thing that she has not said out loud, and she is not going to say it in ink to a person who will never read it.

She will not fill it because the four lines on the second leaf were written by a hand that cannot write a false line, and she has spent eight years refusing documents that did not say what they were for, and a line in a book is a line in a book.

And here is the one that is the real one and she had it at the fourth minute on the made road.

She is nineteen years old and she is standing in front of a thing with a day on it, and she asked for the day, and the asking is what brought it.

Anything she writes in those eight leaves now is a record written by a woman who has a number of days. She has been somebody’s record for eight years. She has never once been the one holding the pen.

She is not going to start a book of her own by being afraid.

## What She Had In The Bag

There is a stub of a nib wrapped in a corner of cloth in the top of the bag and a cake of ink the size of a farthing, and she has been carrying both of them since she made the book out of a blanket in nine days, and she has not thrown them out in two months, and she noticed that on the thirteenth and did not know what to do about it.

That is the part she could not argue with.

Everything else she said to herself on the made road had a reason attached to it. That one did not. She has been carrying a nib and a cake of ink for two months across nine days of road in a bag with a dish and a lump of clay and a ring a finger’s width in it, and she never once used them for anything.

## What The Book Is For Now

Here is what it is for and it took her the whole of the thirteenth of the month beginning 1,634, and she is nineteen and she has been keeping things for eight months without knowing it and she has never kept one thing in her life.

It was made to be said once.

It is now the only paper on nine days of road that no person has asked about. Not the keeper’s flat book, which is asked about every nine or ten days. Not his book, which is asked about by everybody including her. Not a sheet of paper against her ribs that she has carried for four years and has never once called anything.

This one is hers. She made it out of a thing she was wrapped in. Nobody knows it exists except a boy of eleven who asked one question about it four days ago and did not ask a second.

And she is carrying it against her left side while she walks to a wall and stands on dressed stone in daylight waiting to be told a day.

That is a keeping. She has been kept for eight years by people with paste pots and columns and books nine hundred years deep, and this is the first thing she has ever had to hold, and it weighs what a closed hand weighs.

## Holding It At Night

She did not sleep with it in her coat.

She lay down at the foot of the ladder at the ninth hour of the thirteenth with the bar under her and her right arm open four days in five, and it was against her ribs where the paper is, and she could not get a count of four.

So she took it out and put it in her left hand and lay there on her back with a book of eight leaves open in her hand, six of them empty, and she got her breath back and she did not put it away again.

That is everything that happened on the night of the thirteenth of the month beginning 1,634, and she was not frightened by it and she was not proud of it either.

She spent twenty minutes getting used to the idea that there was a thing in the world she was keeping, and that not one person had asked her to.
