# The Keeper Does Not Write Her Down

She stood at the post on the nineteenth, twentieth and twenty-first of the month beginning 1,604 waiting to be written down, and a man with a paste pot looked straight at her for eleven seconds and did not do it.

## What She Was Waiting For

It had been on her for four days and she had not told anybody, because there was nobody on nine days of road to tell.

A book of eight leaves had been on a flat stone under a ladder at the ninth hour of the twenty-fifth of a month that has gone, in daylight, in front of three people. That had been a thing put down where it would be found.

She wanted to be found.

She had not said that out loud until the eleventh hour of the eighteenth and when she said it she did not like the shape of it. A girl of nineteen with a bar on her shoulder had been standing at a post for eleven days wanting a man with a rag to write her name into a flat book with three names in it.

That is not a want she would have admitted to the post in the ninth of a month.

## The Board On The Nineteenth

The cart with the paste comes when it comes, and it came on the nineteenth at the seventh hour with boards on it, and the man got down off the tail with the rag in his hand and began at the top of the board.

She stood four feet off the ladder with her left leg out in front of her and the sun on it, which is where she stands.

She stood there for the whole of the seventh hour and the eighth hour waiting for him to come down the ladder and untie a string.

## Eleven Seconds

He did it at the ninth hour with the pot under his arm.

He came down the ladder backwards, three rungs, and washed the rag in the trough and wrung it out over the dust and hung it on the rail, and then he turned round and looked at her and he looked at her properly, which is not a thing he has done in two years.

He looked at her face. Then he looked at the bar on her shoulder. Then he looked at her boots.

That took about eleven seconds and she stood in all of it and did not move and did not put her hand out.

Then he went into the post and shut the door behind him and did not latch it, which is what a man does in his own building.

## She Wanted It Badly Enough To Be Angry

She was angry for a minute afterwards and then she had to stand there and be honest about why, which took her four minutes standing in the dust by the rail.

She was not angry that he had not written her down.

She was angry that she had wanted it, and how much she had wanted it, and that she had gone eleven days and twenty-two miles and asked a boy of fifteen at a gate a question she had never asked anybody, and had been working towards a man with a paste pot putting her in a book.

That was not the asking she had gone to the wall for.

She had been trying to get somebody to record her. That is a different thing entirely and it is the oldest thing she does and she has been doing it since she was eleven years old.

## What A Post Is

She got him back out at the second hour of the twentieth with a question, and she had it ready and she did not have to make any of it up.

“What do you write down,” Ning Yan said.

“Who passes,” the keeper said.

“That is all?”

“That is all of it. A name, and the day, and the year.”

She had known that. She had read the flat book in the dark at the eleventh hour of the twenty-eighth of a month with both books in her hands. She wanted it in his mouth anyway, because she wanted to go on to the next thing.

“What would make me pass.”

The keeper took the pot out from under his arm and set it down on the rail.

“Going by,” he said. “Four or five times a month somebody comes along this road who is not going anywhere and they are on their way through in a morning and I write them down and that is the last of it.”

“And if I stay.”

“Then you are not passing,” the keeper said, “and I have not had anybody stay at this post in nine years and I am not going to start writing them down for staying.”

## The Next Question

“What if I asked you to write me down.”

He looked at her for a bit.

“That would be me doing you a favour,” he said, “and I do not do favours at a post.”

She had expected something else. She had gone at that question with a bar on her shoulder at the second hour of a month and she had been ready for a yes and she was ready for a no and she was not ready for that.

“All right,” Ning Yan said, and that was the end of it.

She went and did the gang at the fourth hour and came back at the dark and she had eleven hours on the road to think about it in and she used about nine of them.

## What He Does With A Name

She asked him the last question at the seventh hour of the twenty-first, with the board dry and the trough full and the boy of eleven twenty feet off with a board he was not carrying anywhere.

She asked it out loud, in daylight, at a post, with a boy in the hearing, and it is the second question she has ever put to a keeper.

“What do you do with a name you have written.”

He did not answer straight away. He went and untied the string on the flat book under the bottom of the ladder and she thought, for four seconds, that she had gone too far, and then he took it out and showed her the page, which she has already read in the dark with both books in her hands, and he put it back and tied the string.

“Nothing at all,” the keeper said. “It goes down and I put the string round it and the next one goes under it. I have never in nineteen years wanted to know where any of them went and I am not going to start.”

Seven sentences, and it is the whole of what a post does, and he put the pot back under his arm and went in and shut the door.

He did not answer straight away. He went and untied the string on the flat book under the bottom of the ladder and she thought, for four seconds, that she had gone too far.

Then he took it out and showed her the page, which she has already read in the dark with both books in her hands, and he put it back and tied the string.

## The Third Question She Did Not Ask

There was one more and she had it ready at the seventh hour and she did not ask it, and she wants that written down because the whole three days came out of not asking it.

What would make you look at a stone.

She had it half out of her mouth when he came out with the pot and she took the whole of it back in and swallowed it, and the reason is that it would have been a request, and a request is a favour, and he has just told her in the plainest words available that he does not do favours at a post.

She could have asked him to look. She could have said, there is a book on the flat stone under your ladder that has been there eleven days and you have walked over it forty times a day and you have not seen it.

He would have looked. That is the worst of it. He would have looked at it and said nothing and known what was on it and gone on carrying the pot, and there would be a thing on that ladder that he knew of and had been told of it by the person who put it there, and she would have made a man with a rag part of her own keeping by asking him to see her.

She wanted the opposite of that so badly on the twenty-first of a month that her hands were cold on the rail.

## Who Is Going To Be Standing There

Here is what she got out of three days at a post and she got it at the ninth hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 1,604, sitting on the rail with the bar across her knees.

She would not have wanted it.

That is the finding and she did not expect it and it took her eleven hours on a road gang to get to. A name in that flat book is a day and a year and nothing else. It does not say where a person went. It does not say whether they came back. It does not say a single thing about a person except that they came along a strip road once and a man had a pot in his hand.

She wanted being found. What is on that stone is not being found.

And the other half of it is this.

She has been seen for eleven days. A carter thought about her book for nine of them and came and said seven sentences about it at a trough. A boy of eleven has not been able to stop looking at a stone. A keeper walked over that stone going up and coming down forty times a day and did not see it, because a man does not keep off a thing he does not know about.

Being seen is not being written.

Nobody on this ground ever wrote her down and she has been standing in the middle of eleven days of it with a bar on her shoulder, and she did not know until the ninth hour of the twenty-first that she had been standing in the middle of something, and she has still got it, and she is not going to be able to give it away to anybody who asks her what she is.

She did not go and look at the stone. She has not looked at it in two days.

She looked east down the strip road at the eleventh hour with her arm shut and the trough behind her, and there was nothing on it, and she was thinking about a person walking two miles with a bar on his shoulder so that two people can hear four words.
