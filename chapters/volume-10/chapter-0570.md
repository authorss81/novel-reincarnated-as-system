# A Hundred And Six Days

She stood in an opening in a wall on the third of the month beginning 1,694 at the seventh hour in daylight with a boy of fifteen four feet off her, and a man on his own side of a plank table finished a sentence he had started twice, and the day the list shuts is the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,784.

## The Third Of The Month Beginning 1,694

She was off the bench at the turn at the fourth hour of the second of the month beginning 1,694 with a loaf and a bit in the bag and a bar on her shoulder, and she had been out at that gate eleven times in thirty-eight days and she had not asked him for anything since the twenty-second of a month beginning 1,604.

She made the nine miles of strip road between the fourth hour and the eighth and lay down about a mile short of the gate, and she lay there two hours of it and did not sleep, and she came onto the two miles of made road before the sky had finished and was at the gate with the chain across it at the sixth hour of the third.

She had worked out why at the ninth hour of the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,664 and it had taken her four minutes.

She did not need a person hearing her. He never said that it was a term. She said on the thirteenth of a month beginning 1,604, standing four feet into an opening at a west jamb with a plank table in front of her and nothing on it, that asking is done in the open in front of people, and that was her finding and not his, and she had built thirty-eight days of walking on it.

She was going to un-build it for one morning, and it was the first thing she had ever changed her mind about in public.

## The Boy At The Gate

The boy of fifteen was on the chain at the seventh hour and he put it down and asked the question, and she said the answer she says, and then she stood there.

“Will you walk the last two miles with me,” Ning Yan said.

He did not answer straight away. He looked at the chain in his hands and then up the made road east, where it goes two miles to a wall with an opening in it, and then back at her.

“You have not asked me for a month,” the boy said.

“No.”

“All right,” the boy said, and hung the chain on the post and stood there.

## What She Said About It

She told him on the two miles, because she had worked out in half a mile that if she was going to have a boy of fifteen standing four feet off her at that opening she was not going to have him standing there not knowing what for.

“I am going to stand there and I am not going to ask anything,” she said. “He is going to say a thing and I want a person in the hearing who is not him.”

“That is all right,” the boy said.

They came onto the flat at the seventh hour with the light behind them, the way they had come onto it a month before with four words in her mouth and nothing at all in hers this time, and he stopped behind and to the left, four feet off, and stood there.

She had walked twenty-four hours and had eaten a third of a loaf out of what she carried, and the rest of it was still in the bag.

## The Opening At The Seventh Hour

She stopped four feet into the opening at the west jamb with the metalled road at her back.

He was on his own side of the plank table. She said nothing. She waited a count of four and four times that, and then she waited the length of an hour, and at the fortieth minute he took his hands off the plank and stood up straight, which took a while.

“The nineteenth of the month beginning 1,784,” he said.

That is all of it. Seven words and a figure, in daylight, with a boy of fifteen in the hearing, and he did not say one word about why, and she did not ask him for one word about why.

## The Nineteenth Of A Month Beginning 1,784

She did the sum standing on dressed stone with her hands down at her sides.

A month is thirty days and a month begins every thirty days, and the first day of a month was day one hundred and thirty-four, which is the only thing anybody ever told her about any of it and which she worked out herself when she was eleven years old.

The first of the month beginning 1,784 is day 1,784. The nineteenth is eighteen days on from the first.

Day 1,802.

She had it in eleven seconds and she had it right, and she stood there and let it stand.

## A Hundred And Six Days

Day 1,802 and she is standing on day 1,696.

A hundred and six days from where she is standing.

She did it twice, out loud, with the boy of fifteen four feet off her looking at the back of her head and not asking.

That is what she came out here for and she has been out here eleven times in thirty-eight days and four hundred and eighty-four miles of road and she did not ask for it and it came anyway, and it came to a girl of nineteen with no wages, no place and no road, standing in daylight on her own side of a wall, with a hundred and six days in front of her and no bread and an arm that is open four days in five.

## What She Set Going

And here is the part, and she got it standing at the west jamb at the seventh hour with the sun coming off the top of the wall, and it is not in anybody’s mouth and she is not going to put it in anybody’s mouth.

He did not refuse her. He has never once refused her and she has had four questions off that wall and three terms back and one number out of a month.

He answered. And she asked.

The list was there before her. It has been there for nine hundred years and a man has been keeping it at that wall since before her grandmother was born, and there are names on it that go back further than anybody can count.

And there was no day on it.

She has understood that in four seconds standing on dressed stone. A thing that nobody looks at does not have a day. A thing that nobody asks about does not have an end, because there is no reason in the world for it to have one.

She went to a wall on the twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,604 and she asked a man what else is on it, in daylight, in the open, with a boy of fifteen four feet off, and it went into a book nine hundred years deep and it cannot be un-asked.

And she asked.

## What She Said To The Boy On The Way Home

She asked him on the two miles west and he answered it in the gate manner, which is the way she has come to expect a boy of fifteen to answer anything.

“What did he say.”

“He said the nineteenth of a month that begins in a year,” the boy said. “And I do not know what that is and I am not going to ask him.”

“You could have asked him.”

“No,” the boy said.

That is the whole of it. A boy of fifteen at a gate in daylight on a made road was told something he has no way of placing and he did not ask a question about it, and he had been out of bed at the seventh hour on a working day to stand four feet off a wall for a girl who asked him to.

She walked nine miles of strip road with that and it took her about the first two.

## The Bill

Here it is in the plainest words she has got, and she is nineteen and she has been counting since she was eleven years old and she has never once been able to make a count come out the way she wanted it to.

She asked a question about a list.

The asking is what has given the list a day. The only thing that had been keeping it open for nine hundred years was nobody looking at it, and she looked at it, and the thing that was open is now shut on the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,784.

A hundred and seventy-five days from the seventh hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 1,604 to the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,784.

She has been part of that count the whole time. She went out to a wall eleven times in thirty-eight days and stood in it saying nothing, and she thought she was the one keeping the count.

She was one of the days of it. She is one of the days of it. She is standing here inside it.

## What Is Left Of The Road

A girl of nineteen was going to spend a hundred and six days looking for her brother, and the hundred and six days were there because she had opened her mouth.

She has a bar nine feet nine and a skin and a bag and four fingers and a thumb and a book of eight leaves with six blank ones against her ribs, and an arm that is open four days in five, and no wages, no place, no shed, no lane of her own, no road that goes anywhere she has been, and no coppers coming.

And four questions in a book nine hundred years deep, and not one of them answered, and one of them holding the other three shut, and she cannot tell which one.

And a hundred and six days.

Here is what she worked out standing on dressed stone at the eighth hour, with the boy of fifteen waiting and not going anywhere, and it is the only arithmetic left to her and it took nine minutes.

Every question she asks from today narrows what can still be found. She knows that now and she worked it out herself and nobody told her. A list that is being asked about is a list being finished, and it finishes on the hundred and sixth day whatever she does with the hundred and fifth.

And there are four questions in it and she is not allowed a fifth, and the one that is holding has not been answered in sixty-nine days, and she has a hundred and six.

She has a hundred and six days and one question a month at a wall at best, if he ever gets to it, and every one of them takes something out of the rest.

She looked east down eleven miles of made road with the light going.

Then she looked at the boy of fifteen standing four feet off her in the opening with his hands at his sides, waiting for her, because she had asked him to and he had come.
