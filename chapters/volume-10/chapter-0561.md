# Nine Years Of Going East

She sat on a bench at a turn for a day and a night on the fifth and sixth of the month beginning 1,634 and watched nine years of people go east, and the woman who was already sitting there told her in ten short sentences that a thing shutting is not done to anybody.

## The Fifth Of A Month

Thirteen miles to the lane at the second hour and the board went down to the store at the seventh, and she took the bar and went east and got to the turn at the ninth hour of the fifth because she had wanted the light going.

There was a woman already on the bench.

That is how it began. She had been at that bench four hundred times in two months and it had been a bench with a post and a trough and a bar on two posts with nothing on it, and on the fifth of a month beginning 1,634 there was a woman of sixty sitting on it in the last of the light with a piece of sacking over her knees.

She did not get up. She moved her feet along the bench four inches.

## What She Watched

Here is the count, and she made it the way she makes all of them, sitting still with her hands in her lap.

Between the fifth hour of the fifth and the eighth hour of the sixth, on a bench at a turn, on a made road that goes east eleven miles to a gate and then two miles to a wall:

Two carts east at the seventh hour of the fifth, both loaded, both empty coming back at the second hour of the sixth and the fifth hour of the sixth.

A man on foot at the fourth hour of the sixth, walking east with a bundle and a bar, who got to the bar on two posts at the seventh hour and went past it and was not on that road again in the time she was sitting there.

A woman and two children at the second hour of the sixth going east on foot, and they went past the bar on two posts and were not on that road again in the time she was sitting there.

And a carter she knows, who came west at the ninth hour of the sixth, and who stopped at the trough and put his horse in and did not go on east at all.

That is six passages in a day and a night and she wrote them out on the dust on the rail with a finger, and then she rubbed them out, because she understood inside four minutes that she was doing the thing she has always done and that it was going to be worth nothing at all.

## What She Learned About Doing Nothing

She had counted in a book she made out of a blanket and in a book nine hundred years deep and in the dust on a rail, and she had been doing it since she was eleven years old, and it was the only instrument she had ever had.

And she sat on a bench at a turn for a day and a night and the sum came to six passages and none of them had a name on them and none of them had anything to do with her, and she rubbed it out.

That is the largest thing she learned at that bench and it took her most of the second of the sixth to see it, and she wrote nothing down about it and has not written anything down about it since.

## The Woman Who Was Already There

The woman was sixty. She had a shawl and sacking over her knees and a piece of string and a needle and she did two inches of the string while Ning Yan sat down and she did not stop when she was asked anything.

Her name was not given and she did not give it and she was not asked twice.

She had been sitting on that bench nine years. She said so at the second hour of the sixth, in the middle of something else, in the ordinary voice, and she said it the way a woman says how long she has had a chair.

She had come down the store road from the mill with what she had and stopped at the turn, and there had been a man on the bar on two posts then and nothing on it then either, and she had been on that bench every day since.

## Nine Years Of Them

She said the thing at the fourth hour of the sixth of a month beginning 1,634, with her needle in the string, and it was not a speech and it was ten sentences and she did not stop to say them.

“I have sat here nine years and I have not seen one person go east and come back.”

She went two inches on the string.

“That is not a thing I think about. It is a thing I have watched.”

“Nobody past that bar keeps anybody forever. The mill kept men and the mill shut. The lime stopped and the house beyond the bar keeps nobody at all now and it kept four once.”

“Everything on that ground that keeps a person shuts on a day. It shuts because it shuts.”

“Nobody shuts it on anybody. And they do not come back, and in nine years I have never once thought that was done to them.”

That is the whole of what she said and then she went back to the string.

## What She Wanted To Say To Her

Ning Yan had four things ready and she did not say one of them.

She wanted to ask what the mill kept and she wanted to ask about the house beyond the bar and she wanted to ask why they do not come back, and she wanted to ask the one she actually wanted, which was what happens to a person when the thing they are kept in shuts.

She has been the person who says no in a room for eight years and she has never once in her life sat next to somebody for a day and a night and said nothing, and it cost her four separate times in two hours and she let it cost her.

What she got instead was ten sentences from a woman of sixty about a mill and a lime bed and a house, and none of them had anything in them that was meant for her.

## What A Closing Is

She got it on the made road at the second hour of the seventh of the month, nine miles west of the turn, with the bar on her shoulder and her arm shut.

A keeping shuts.

That is all that the woman said and it is more than anybody has said to her in nine months, and it took her a mile of getting it into a shape she could hold.

A thing that keeps people is built and it runs and one day it stops running, and the people on it go somewhere else, and no one has to decide anything for that to happen and nobody has to be punished by it.

Her own keeping is a list nine hundred years deep with her name in it and one word at the end of it, and it has a day it shuts, and she found out at the ninth hour of the sixth of a month at a bench that this is not a punishment.

Nobody shut it on her.

Nobody shut anything on her. A man of nine hundred years has been writing down names at a wall since before her grandmother was born and she is one of the names and it is not a punishment, and a woman of sixty on a bench with a needle in a string is the reason she knows that.

## The Part That Is About Her

And here is what she would not let herself off for, sitting on the flat at the foot of a ladder at the second hour of the seventh with the book nine inches off her left knee.

She is the only person on nine days of road whose keeping has a day.

The woman on the bench has been sitting there nine years because a mill shut, and it is behind her and it is over and she is not going to be told a date.

She asked a man at a wall on the twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,604 what else is on it, and the answer to that, whenever it comes, will be a number, and the number will be a day somebody chose on a bench with a needle in a string, and it will be the only date on nine days of road.

She looked at the stone for a while.

She has been counting a list shut for six days and she had it in her head the other way round. She had it as a thing that was closing on her. It is not closing on her. It is closing, the way a mill closes, and she is the only one on this ground who has been told there is a day to it, and that is not the same as being the only one it is happening to.

She was still sitting there at the third hour with the light going when she worked out what that actually meant and why it was worse.

If a closing is not a punishment, then it does not care who is standing in front of it.

And she is standing in front of one.
