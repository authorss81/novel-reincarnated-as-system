# Everything Behind The First Question

She counted what was left to find on the seventeenth, eighteenth and nineteenth of the month beginning 1,634, sitting on a bar at the head of a lane, and when she had finished the column she found that it pointed at one thing and she was the only thing standing in the way of it.

## The Seventeenth Of A Month And An Inventory

She worked thirteen miles of face between the second hour and the seventh on the seventeenth, the eighteenth and the nineteenth, and she did the counting at the seventh hour with the light going and the board going down to the store in the morning.

Here is everything she has got in the world, in the order she put it down, and she wrote it in the dust on the bar with a finger and then did it again from memory and got the same six items.

There is a window a hundred yards off a wall twenty-two miles east, with one pane in it and a sill and a roof that has not come down, and there is no light in it, and she has four questions about it and has not asked one.

There is a count of twenty-two days that a man said at a jamb and she has never been able to place it against anything.

There are four letters at the end of her own entry in a book nine hundred years deep that nobody in the world will say out loud, including the man who wrote them.

There is a door shut on eleven feet of boards on the other side of a jamb that she looked at once and is not going to look at again.

There is a bar on two posts four hundred yards off that has had nothing on it since before she came to this ground.

And there is a brother.

## The Six Weeks She Had Spent

She put the column in front of her and looked at it for nine minutes and then she worked out the thing about it that she had not been seeing.

Five of those six are behind the first question.

Not because they are too small. Because of the sentence a man said at a wall on the twenty-fifth of a month beginning 1,604, in his own mouth, while she was standing on dressed stone with her mouth still dry from the day before: he does not have the rest of it while there is one standing open.

Every one of those five needs him. There is no other door to any of them on nine days of road and there is not another person in this world who knows whether a hand’s width of glass has anything in a building behind it.

So they are all on the far side of a question she asked in twenty days ago, and they will stay there until that one is shut, and she cannot shut it and she cannot hurry it and she cannot go round it.

## Four Questions About A Pane Of Glass

She had not been within a hundred yards of the window since the fifteenth of a month beginning 1,604 and she was not going to get within a hundred yards of it before the first question was answered, and she worked that out on the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,634 and put it in the column as one line rather than four.

Whether there is a room in it. Whether there was a light in it and what put the light out. Whether anybody is in it. Whether it is on what he keeps.

Four questions and she has not spent one of them and she has not been near the building.

All of it cost her four days of nothing at a stone at the foot of a ladder on the eleventh of the month beginning 1,634, and the four days were not a fair price and she knows it, and there is no arrangement on this ground by which a person gets a closer look at anything by waiting longer.

## The One She Will Not Say

She left it off the column for two days and wrote it on the third.

That is a thing she decided on the twenty-second of a month beginning 1,604, at the flat stone at the foot of a ladder, when she counted a bill in full and one of the lines of it was a name she was not going to say.

Ning Wu.

She has said it out loud in her own head every day for eight years and she has said it to a trough and to herself in the dark and to not one other person since she was eleven years old, and on the twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,604 she walked two miles with a boy of fifteen four feet off her and asked four words that were not his name, and she has carried that decision into every day since.

Not at the first asking. Not at the second if it can be helped.

It is the only thing she has kept shut on purpose in nineteen years, and she kept it shut out loud, and it is hers, and she has been proud of it.

## The Direction The Narrowness Points

Here is the direction and she worked it out on the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,634 at the seventh hour sitting on the end of a bar with a loaf she has not cut open in her bag.

She has been spending everything else.

She spent the window by deciding not to spend it. She spent the road by walking it. She spent the four letters by putting a question about them into a book on the fifteenth of a month beginning 1,634, and she spent three nights of sleep lost on them two months back and got one subtraction for it.

Everything she has touched in eight years of this she has touched with a bar or with her feet or by standing still.

The one thing she has not touched is a name, and the reason she has not touched it is the only reason she has.

She did not keep it shut to save it. She kept it shut because saying a name out loud in front of another person at a wall is a thing she had never done, and she did not want to be the person who did it.

She worked that out in four minutes and it was the worst four minutes of the three days, and the reason it was the worst is that the decision she was proudest of in eight years turns out not to have been about him at all.

## Not Asking Is Also A Way Of Losing It

She sat on the end of a bar in the flat with the light going off the face and did the part she did not want to do.

There is no arrangement anywhere on this ground that says a question she has not asked is held.

The terms do not say it. He did not say it. He said one at a time, at the wall, in daylight, and a question she asks goes in the book the same as a name, and a name she does not ask about is not a thing he is keeping for her, it is a thing that has not happened.

She has been treating the not-saying as a holding. It is not a holding. It is an absence, and she cannot tell the difference between an absence and a thing that has been put somewhere safe, because she has never had the second to compare it with.

Which means the eight years she spent deciding not to say his name bought her nothing at a wall.

It bought her herself, which is not nothing, and she is not going to be ungrateful about it in the dark. But it did not put him anywhere. It did not put him anywhere it can stay.

## The Only Lever She Has

And here is the last thing, and she got it at the ninth hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,634 and then she picked up her bar and walked east nine miles in the dark without lighting anything.

He told her on the twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,604 that she does not get a second until the first is answered.

He told her on the same morning that if she does not wait, it goes in twice, and neither of them gets answered.

Those are the two halves of the same rule and she had them in a drawer for twenty-six days and she opened the drawer at the ninth hour of the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,634 on a strip road with nothing on it in any direction.

There is exactly one thing in the world that would make him get to the first question sooner.

It is the one thing that would make it never get answered at all.

She walked the whole nine miles thinking about it and she did not put it down and she did not put it away, and when she got to the flat at the foot of a ladder she took the loaf out of the bag and cut it with four fingers and a thumb and sat down on the flat stone with the bar across her knees.

She is nineteen years old and there is one lever and it is in her own hand and she is not going to pull it, and not pulling it has just become a decision she is carrying on purpose instead of a thing she never thought about.
