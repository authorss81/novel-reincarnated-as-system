# Nothing On The Board

She worked thirteen miles of face on the thirteenth, fourteenth and fifteenth of the month beginning 1,694, and on the seventh hour of the fifteenth she walked a mile further on to the store and there was nothing on the board for her.

## Three Days

Here is the trade and she can say all of it because she stood in the flat at the eighth hour of the third of a month beginning 1,634 and asked a man with a piece of board and a stick of chalk on a string what a barrow was worth.

Out of the face, by the barrow. A full barrow is a number of loaves at a rate a woman behind a counter says out loud, and it has been two coppers a loaf for as long as the man with the chalk has worked that face.

The barrow is chalked on the board when it is full. The board goes down to the store on the second day. The store takes the bread off it.

A barrow takes eleven trips to fill, and that is the figure on the face. She filled a full one a day on the thirteenth, the fourteenth and the fifteenth, and the arm was open for the last four hours of the fourteenth and for all of the second half of the fifteenth, and she did six of the six on the fifteenth with the arm hanging and her back and her knees.

Thirty-three barrows, full, chalked by a man who said the figure out loud every time he wrote it, which is what he does with numbers.

## The Store

The store is behind the mill, and the wheel of the mill has not turned for nine years, and the store is a counter and a shutter and a woman of fifty who has sold her bread for two years.

There were four men in it and a boy carrying sacks and a dog under the counter, and the woman behind it had a cloth round a loaf and a figure in her head the way a woman who says a rate out loud has a figure in her head.

She got there at the seventh hour on the fifteenth with her right arm hanging and the bar on her shoulder and three days of stone in her legs, and she said good evening, and the woman said the rate out loud, to her and to a man at the end of the counter and to nobody in particular, which is what a rate is.

“Then there is nothing for you,” the woman said. “The board came down on the second.”

She said it the same way she says the rate, which is to say she said it out loud and did not soften it and did not apologise for it, and then she went back to her loaf.

## What Ning Yan Did At That Counter

She did not argue, and she wants that written down before anything else, because everything she felt at the ninth hour of the fifteenth of a month beginning 1,694 is that she did not argue and cannot account for it.

She stood at the counter for a minute and a half with the arm hanging and looked at the board, which is a board on the end wall with chalked columns on it and the top column rubbed out every time a new one comes down, and there were four marks on it and none of them were hers.

She could have asked the woman when the next one goes down. That is a free question. It is not a question about anybody and it costs nothing.

She did not ask it.

She worked out why on the strip road at the ninth hour, and it is that asking a woman of fifty when the next board goes down is asking her to remember her, and a person who is remembered has to be on something, and she is not going to be on a board on this ground, and she knows that now at a barrow handle the way she knows four fingers and a thumb.

So she did not ask, and she did not argue, and she paid two coppers for a very small quantity of nothing out of a bag with no coppers in it, which did not happen because she did not offer, and she left the counter at the seventh hour with nothing in her hands and her arm still open.

## The Second Of The Month

That is the whole of it, and she stood at that counter for a minute and a half before she got it out.

The second of this month is the day before the third, and the third is the day a man on his own side of a plank table gave a girl a date, and she was at that wall from the seventh hour of the second to the eighth hour of the third, twenty-two miles east, on her own two feet.

The board went down at the seventh hour on the second, which is exactly when she was standing four feet into an opening in a wall saying nothing.

Nobody at a face knows where anybody is. The board does not care about walls. The board went down on the second with nothing on it for her because on the first and the second of that month she was not on the face, and there is no hand on this ground that writes down anybody who is elsewhere.

Thirty-three barrows. Three days of a body that has been lighter every single day since the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,544.

Two coppers a loaf. Six loaves she did not get.

## What She Was Angry About

She was not angry about the six loaves and she wants that in the first line because she has checked it twice.

She was angry for about nine minutes on the strip road east of the store, at the ninth hour of the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,694, and it was not at the woman behind the counter, who said a rate out loud and has said it out loud for two years and owes her exactly nothing.

She was angry at the second of the month, which is a day, and at the seventh hour of it, which is an hour, and at the fact that she was standing in daylight in an opening in a wall on the one day in a month when the only work she has left gets written down.

She has stood in that opening eleven times in thirty-eight days and said nothing at any of them. And the first of those eleven cost her six loaves, and she chose it, and she would choose it again, and that is the part that made her put both hands on the bar and lean on it on a strip road in the dark.

There is no arrangement on this ground by which a person is paid for standing still and also paid for being somewhere, and she had never needed one until the twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,604, and she found out on the second of this month in the worst way available.

## The Part Of The Anger That Was Fear

And underneath the nine minutes there was a thing she did not look at for two days and then looked at on the seventeenth at the flat stone, and it is this.

A list that gets asked about closes.

She worked that out on the third of the month beginning 1,694 in an hour of daylight with a boy of fifteen four feet off her, and she has been carrying it since.

And here is what nobody told her and what she has had four days to sit with: a board on a counter is the same shape. It does not know she is at a wall. It does not know there is a wall. It goes down on the second and the column is rubbed out and there is no space anywhere in it for a girl who was twenty-two miles east saying nothing to a man.

The two things she has been told are closing are both closing on her on the same day of a month, and she worked that out sitting on a stone with her bag on her knees, and she could not do one single thing about either of them.

## What She Took Anyway

She walked thirteen miles east and she got to the post at the third hour of the sixteenth and she did not sleep, and she sat down on the flat stone at the foot of the ladder with the book of eight leaves against her knees and she did not open it, because there are six empty leaves in it that are hers and she has decided not to write in them and she has not changed her mind about that.

She worked out the arithmetic at the flat stone in about nine minutes and here it is whole.

The store gives out what the board says. The board goes down on the second. Work done after the second goes on the next board. Work done on the first and the second of a month goes on the board that goes down on the second.

So she has to be at the face on the first of a month to be at the store on the second of it.

And she cannot be at the face on the first of a month and at a wall two miles past a gate on the first of a month, because those are four miles apart and she has never once in her life been in two places at the same time and has no arrangement on this ground that would let her start.

## The Thing She Took Anyway

She took the face on the nineteenth, the twentieth and the twenty-first of the month beginning 1,694, which means she will be at the store on the second of the month beginning 1,724, and she will be paid for nine days of stone.

And she did not take it because she thought it was the better trade. She took it because she has no wages, no place and no road, and the alternative to work that pays is a body with nothing in it and ninety days of walking left in it.

She put her hand flat on the flat stone under the bottom of the ladder at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of the month beginning 1,694, and it was bare, and she had put that there with her own two hands a month ago, and it stays bare.
