# What A Count Does To Bread

The ration on a road gang is drawn against the number of hands, and the number of hands is taken at the muster by a man with a finger, and on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,604 the finger went along the line and there were nine on it, and she had been standing at the end of that line in daylight for the whole day.

## The Twenty-Seventh

She worked the full day. Eight hundred feet of bed packed and metalled and the first of the forty feet of kerb started at the second hour and going on behind it.

Her arm opened at the fourth mile and shut at the seventh. Her fourth count went for five inside a loaded reach nine times and came back in four every time except once, at the tenth hour, when it took seven and she stood in the ditch beside the road with a bar on her shoulder until it came back.

She ate nothing at midday because there was nothing at midday.

## Nine Rations

The ration is drawn at the gate at the post by the keeper’s boy off a cart every second day, and the gang’s is a loaf a day and a half for each hand, at the rate a woman behind a counter says out loud, out of what the gang earns and not out of anybody’s pocket.

That is nine coppers a head a day and a half and there are nine men.

The boy of eleven put nine loaves in the barrow and pushed it out along the mile and gave them out one at a time as he came to each man, and he did it the way he does everything, which is without stopping and without looking at anybody’s face.

He got to the end of the line where she was standing and he stopped.

That is all he did. He stopped with the last loaf in his hand.

## What The Foreman Said

The foreman was forty feet off and it took him nine seconds to come over and it took him a while to get there, and by then the boy of eleven had put the loaf back in the barrow and was pushing it back up the mile.

“You have not drawn,” the foreman said.

“I have not been drawn,” Ning Yan said.

He stood there. He had a cord round his waist and a peg in his hand and he is a careful man who has measured a length of road at dusk every day for eleven years.

“I counted nine at the muster,” he said.

“I know you did.”

“I counted nine at the muster and I counted nine again at dusk yesterday and I got nine both times.” He turned the peg over in his hand. “If you were on the number I would draw you one. That is the whole of it. I do not draw bread for a person I cannot put in the number.”

“All right,” Ning Yan said.

She wanted to say four things and she did not say one of them, and she has been the person who says no in a room for eight years and she stood there on a made road in daylight and let a man with a peg be exactly right.

## The Thing She Was Not Going To Say

Here is what she was not going to say and why she was not going to say it.

She was not going to say count me again standing there, because she had been counted twice and had come out ten both times and moving six feet had not done anything except cost her six feet.

She was not going to say you saw me all day, because being seen is not being written, and she worked that out at a post over three days in the month before and she is not going to un-work it because it is inconvenient.

And she was not going to say ask me at the gate instead, because the gate has a boy of fifteen on a chain on it and a chain is not a number either.

What she said was all right, and then she picked the bar up and went back to the forty feet of kerb and worked the rest of the day.

## The Boy Of Eleven At The End Of The Line

He is eleven years old and he has been on that post since he was nine and he pushes a barrow out along a mile of strip road twice a day and comes back with it empty, and he does not run.

He stopped with the last loaf in his hand and he stood there, and nine men went on eating, and eleven minutes later he put it back in the barrow and pushed it up the mile.

That is the whole of what happened and she has been turning it over since.

He is not a keeper and he does not do favours and he was not asked anything by anybody. He had nine loaves and nine men and a girl at the end of the line who had not been drawn, and he could have given her that loaf and nobody would ever have known, and he did not do it.

She is not going to say that she is glad he did not. What she is going to say is that a boy of eleven in the dust made a decision in four seconds about a thing he had never been asked to think about, and that he made the same decision the foreman made, and that there is something about this road she has not understood yet.

## The Men

Nine men on a gang that is fed by a number, and not one of the nine said anything to her about it, and not one of them put his loaf out on a stone for her when he found out.

Two of them looked over. One of them asked her, at the second hour of the twenty-eighth, whether she had got a place to sleep, and she said the flat at the foot of a post, and he said that will be wet, and went back to his kerb.

That is the whole of it. She has worked on nine days of road with nine men for four days and she knows what they weigh and she knows which of them can lift and which of them cannot, and none of them has ever asked her one question about anything that is not a kerb.

She is not going to be angry about that either. It is what she would have wanted.

## The Part That Was Not Fair On Her

She got that at the eighth hour standing in the ditch with her arm hanging and she wants it written down because she would rather have the unfair part than the tidy part.

Nobody at that post did anything to her.

She has been on this road nine days of it and she has eaten every day since the fifteenth of a month beginning 1,574 right up to the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 1,604, and she is not hungry, and she has not been hungry since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 and she worked out two months ago that that was finished and it has stayed finished.

That is what makes it cheap. Nobody at that store is going to notice a girl who is not hungry.

And that is the first time in nine months that being past the ceiling of a realm has cost her a thing she could put on a hand and count, and it is a loaf, and the loaf is the shape of it.

## The Evening Of The Twenty-Eighth

She did not eat on the twenty-seventh and she did not eat on the twenty-eighth, and she went to the gang both days because the work is the work and the foreman is not unkind and there is a gang of nine men who have not been told a thing.

On the second evening she did a sum sitting on the flat stone at the foot of the ladder with the book nine inches off her left knee, which is where she does all of her sums now, and the sum is this.

She can be seen. She cannot be counted. A gang is fed by a number of hands and the number of hands is a count.

Therefore a gang is not going to feed her, and she did the whole of that in four minutes and it took her the rest of the day to accept that she would rather have it worked out in four minutes than not worked out at all.

## What She Is Going To Do About It

Here is the decision and she made it at the ninth hour of the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,604 with her arm shut and the bread in her not there.

She is not going to stand still again on the flat in front of a foreman with a finger.

And she is not going to chase the foreman, and she is not going to ask the keeper to write her down, and she is not going to ask the boy of fifteen to put a name on a chain, because all three of those are asking a person for a thing to do with her and she has done enough of that in eight years to know what it costs.

So it has to be work.

Not work that counts hands. Not work that keeps a column of men on a board with a name against it. Work measured by the weight that comes off something and the length that is standing at the end of the day, because that is the only kind she has ever been paid in, and a bar takes both of those and there is nobody on nine days of road who has to put a number on her to do it.

There is a lane off the store road four miles west of the turn, and there has been stone coming out of a ground at the head of it all summer, and she has gone up that lane twice in two months and got nothing off it because she had a gang at a rate.

She has got nothing now.
