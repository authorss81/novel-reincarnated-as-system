# The Arithmetic Of Endurance

She got to the store at the seventh hour of the second of the month beginning 1,724 and there were three things on the board with her face against them and a woman of fifty behind a counter saying the rate out loud, and she walked nine miles back with nine loaves and she did not feel any of it.

## Seventy-Seven

She did the count at the flat stone at the foot of a ladder on the second of the month beginning 1,724, at the ninth hour, in daylight, with the bag on her knees, and it took her eleven minutes and she did it seven times over two days and it came out the same seven times.

Here is everything she has, in the sequence she put it down.

Seventy-seven days. A month is thirty days. Seventy-seven is two months and seventeen days.

Four questions. One at a time, at the wall, in daylight, and the first one is standing, and the first one has been standing for ninety-eight days and it is holding the other three.

Fourteen miles to a face. Twenty-eight for a day of stone. Forty-four for a question, and two days on the road, and nothing between the gate and the wall to lie down on.

Nine loaves, which is nine days, and she is not hungry, and a loaf is a day she does not have to think about.

Four fingers and a thumb. The right arm open four days in five and it does not shut on demand. Eight of eleven holds. Four counts of breath, and six on made road at the third hour and seven once in the last month.

A small open place low behind the fourth rib, the size of a coin, open since the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,544, and nothing has come out of it since the twenty-sixth of a month beginning 1,604.

And one lever.

## The Lever

She wrote it at the bottom of the column in the dust and she looked at it for a while and did not go near it, and here it is whole, because a column that leaves it out is not a column.

Asking twice.

Put one asking into the air twice and both of them go in the book and neither of them gets answered, and he comes to the first one sooner, or he never comes to it at all.

It is the only thing in the world that would move the first question. She has carried it on purpose for seventy-six days and she is carrying it now.

She is not going to pull it.

She wrote that under it in the dust too, and then she rubbed the column out with the side of her hand, which she has never done before, and she has been making columns since she was eleven years old.

## The Store Run

She left the post at the first hour of the second of the month beginning 1,724 and got to the counter at the seventh hour, and she got it exactly right for the first time in a month.

Three things on the board with her face against them. Nine loaves. A cloth round the loaf and a figure said out loud to her and to a man at the end of the counter and to nobody in particular.

She put them in the bag and she did not say thank you, because a woman who says a rate out loud is a person she has been paid by and she has worked out since the eighth of a month beginning 1,604 that she is not going to stand in a road and tell two people who were owed exactly what they were owed that they were kind about it.

And she walked fourteen miles back with nine loaves in a bag and a bar on her shoulder and got to the post at the ninth hour, and she lay down on the flat stone and slept three hours and did not wake up once.

That is the only time in three months that anything has gone right on the first attempt, and it was a day of standing at a counter.

## What The Count Is For Now

Here is the change and it happened on the fourth of the month beginning 1,724, at the post, in daylight, with nothing going on.

For eight years the count was a plan.

She had been counting because a count could be wrong and a wrong count could be corrected and a corrected count was the only kind of future anybody had ever let her have. You counted the days and the days behaved. You counted the bread and the bread came. You counted the hand and the hand stayed at four fingers and a thumb.

And on the fourth of a month beginning 1,724 she did the column seven times and the seventh was identical to the first, and she sat there afterwards and understood that she was not finding anything out.

She was not measuring anything. She was passing the time between one number and the next one.

That is all it had become, and it became it somewhere between the second of the month beginning 1,634 and the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,694, and she did not notice it happening because a thing that is working on you the whole time never announces itself.

## The Six Empty Leaves

She got the book of eight leaves out of her coat at the fourth hour of the fifth of the month beginning 1,724 and put it on her knee on the flat stone.

The first leaf has a date and a place on it. The second has four sentences on it that she read out loud once, to herself, in the dark, and has not read since and will not read again. The other six are blank.

They are hers. They are the only thing anybody in this world has ever handed her that she was allowed to write on, and she has had them since the tenth of a month beginning 1,634, and she has had a stub of a nib and a cake of ink in the same bag the whole time, and she has not used the ink since the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,604.

She put her hand on them and she thought about writing the column in, seven times a day, in a book that goes in a coat against her ribs.

She did not do it, and the reason is the one she gave at the head of a lane on the twelfth of a month beginning 1,694, which is that six blank leaves she has chosen not to write in are a thing she chose, and a thing she has chosen once a day for a month is a sentence she is writing to somebody.

She put it back in her coat against the side where the paper is.

There are two things against her ribs and she has not called either of them anything.

## What The Seven Times Was

She did it seven times over two days and she can put the seven in sequence, because she numbered them, because that is what she does.

The first was at the ninth hour of the second, straight after a store run, and it took a quarter of an hour and it was useful.

The second was at the ninth hour of the second again, an hour later, and it took seven minutes because the arithmetic was warm, and it gave the same answer.

The third was at the second hour of the third, in the dark, before she had got up off the stone, and it gave the same answer in nine minutes.

The fourth was at the seventh hour of the third, at the head of the lane, before she picked up a barrow, and it gave the same answer.

The fifth was at the second hour of the fourth on a dressed stone face with the light going off it, and she wrote it out and it gave the same answer.

The sixth was at the ninth hour of the fourth at the foot of a ladder and it took four minutes.

The seventh was at the second hour of the fifth and it took her a minute and a half, because she could do it in her head, and that is the one she is going to remember for the rest of her life.

Seven counts. The first one cost a quarter of an hour and the last one cost a minute and a half and the answer did not move once.

## What She Found Out About Herself

And here is the thing, and she did not expect it and it is the only finding she has had in a month.

She did not stop because the count stopped working. She stopped because the count had stopped being the reason.

A count is a way of finding out whether a thing can still be changed. She has used one every day since she was a child, in dust and in margins and on the back of a hand, and every single count she ever made was a count of something that might come out a different way today than it did yesterday.

And on the fifth of the month beginning 1,724 it came out the same way seven times, and it cannot come out a different way, because the number of days between now and the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784 does not depend on anything she does or thinks or is.

She had been running the most careful instrument she owns on the one question it cannot answer, for eight years, and it has taken her until seventy-seven days from a day to find that out.

## The Third Day And The Fourth

She worked the face on the third, the fourth and the fifth of the month beginning 1,724, nine barrows a day, because the second was a store day and a face day does not fit on the same legs.

The man with the chalk said the figures out loud. She filled nine a day and did the last three of the fifth with the arm hanging.

Nothing came out of her on any of them. It has not come out of her since the twenty-sixth of a month beginning 1,604 on work that a store pays a figure for, and she has been turning that over for two months and it has not got any further along than that.

At the seventh hour of the fifth she got her bar and stood at the head of the lane and looked east, nine of strip and eleven of made and two past a gate.

She was leaving in the morning and there would be no bread at the other end of it and she would be at the wall inside two days.

She was doing all of it because it was the fourth day out of seventy-four and there was nothing else in the fourth day, and that was all of the difference between a count and a plan, and she stood at the head of a lane at the seventh hour of the fifth of the month beginning 1,724 and found that she could not remember what it had felt like to make one of these numbers go somewhere.
