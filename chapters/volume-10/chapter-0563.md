# The Book Off The Flat Stone

She took her own book off the flat stone under the ladder at a post on the eleventh of the month beginning 1,634, in daylight, in front of a man with a paste pot, and she has not been able to put it back the way it was since, and that is the change and it does not go round.

## The Tenth Of A Month At The Foot Of The Ladder

The gang was finished and she was out of bread and she had nothing to do at the post between the second hour and the eighth, which had not happened to her one time in two months.

So she sat at the foot of the ladder with the bar across her knees, which is where she does everything now that she thinks about, and she looked at the stone.

It is a flat stone two feet by a foot, off to the left of the bottom rung, and it has been there since before the keeper came and it is not on the flat book or off it. There is a string round the flat book under the bottom rung with a knot in it, and there is an inch of bare stone between that book and hers.

The boy of eleven was holding the near end of a board and not carrying it anywhere, the way he has stood four hundred times in two months.

## What The Boy Of Eleven Asked

He asked her at the fourth hour of the tenth of the month beginning 1,634, and it is the first question he has ever asked her that was not about a rung or a barrow or the water.

He has been not looking at it since the twenty-fifth of a month that has gone. She has watched him not looking at it every day for forty-five days and she has never once given him a reason to say anything about it.

“Is that yours,” the boy said.

Two feet off, in daylight, with a board in his hands and not one person within nine feet of either of them.

“Yes,” Ning Yan said.

He waited. That is the whole of the boy of eleven and it took eleven seconds.

“All right,” the boy said, and went back to holding the near end of a board he was not carrying anywhere.

That is all of it. He did not ask what was in it and he did not ask where it came from and he did not touch it, and he has not mentioned it since, and it is the largest single thing that has ever happened to her in front of another person on nine days of road.

## Forty-Five Days And An Inch Of Stone

She sat there with it until the light went and she got the whole of it out, and here it is at the seventh hour of the tenth of the month beginning 1,634, on a rail at a post.

She put it down at the ninth hour of the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,574, in daylight, in front of three people, with her hand in it and the first leaf written on. Four fingers and a thumb and a hand that cannot write a false line, and it went down on a flat stone under a ladder at a post, and she did not ask anybody to hold it.

Not the keeper. Not Shuyin, who had walked west on foot and gone back east on foot with a bag and a brush in it. Not one person on nine days of road.

That was the shape of it and she had gone eleven days and twenty-two miles and asked a boy of fifteen a question she had never asked anybody in order to get a man to put her name somewhere.

And then she had asked a man at a wall what else is on it.

## What The Carter Said At A Trough

There is a carter of thirty-five who comes through that post with a horse and a load and who said seven sentences at that trough on the fourth of the month beginning 1,604, nine days after it went down, and she has had them for forty-five days.

He said that a thing put down in the open in front of people is put down where it will be found.

He said that finding is not keeping.

He said that nobody at a post thinks that except the people who put things down.

He said four more and she has not been able to use any of the four, and she has had that sentence turning over for a month and a half, and this is what she got out of it at the seventh hour of the tenth while sitting on a rail.

Finding is not keeping. That is the one she wants to use and it does not work any more, because she has been asking about keeping for six days.

She has asked a man at a wall, in daylight, in the open, with a person hearing it, what else is on the thing he keeps.

## What Changed At Six Days Old

Here is the arithmetic and it took her eleven minutes on the rail and it is the reason she did not sleep that night.

A thing that is put down and not asked about is kept one way.

That is what the stone was. It was a thing put down in the open where it would be found, and it was not asked about by anybody, and it lay on a flat stone under a ladder beside a flat book with three names in it and an inch of stone between them, and a man walked over it forty times a day.

A thing that has been asked about is not kept the same way twice.

She asked. On the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 1,604, at the seventh hour, at a wall, on her own side, with a boy of fifteen four feet off, and it went in a book nine hundred years deep, and it cannot be un-asked, and he has told her so himself.

And the thing she asked about is the same kind of thing as the thing on the stone.

She went back over it about nine times and it came out the same every time and she could not get a different answer out of it by wanting a different one.

So the flat stone is not a flat stone with her book on it any more. It is a stone with a book on it that a person has asked about, and it is going to be that from now on, and there is no version of it where a man with a rag walks over it forty times a day and does not know what it is.

## The Eleventh Of A Month

She did it at the seventh hour of the eleventh, in daylight, with not one person on the mile.

She put her left hand flat on the stone first, which she did not plan, and then she took the book up off it with four fingers and a thumb and her left hand under the corner.

It weighs what a closed hand weighs. That is the whole of what it weighs and it weighed that at the ninth hour of the twenty-fifth of a month beginning 1,574 and it weighs it now.

She put it inside her coat.

## The Keeper Came Out

He came out of the post at the seventh hour with the pot under his arm and the string round the flat book in his other hand, going down the ladder backwards three rungs, the way he does it.

And there was a girl standing at the foot of his ladder with the peg in her hand and the flat stone bare.

He stopped on the second rung.

He looked at the stone for a long time. Then he looked at her.

He is a man of fifty-five or near it with three names under a ladder for nineteen years and no office behind any of them, and he has never once done her a favour, and he looked at that stone for eleven seconds and she stood in all of it.

Then he went into the post and shut the door behind him and did not latch it.

He did not ask her where it had been. He did not ask her how long. He did not say one word about the fact that he had walked over a book for forty-five days going up and coming down, and she wanted him to say it and she wanted it so badly her hands were cold on the peg.

## The Stone Is A Stone

Here is what she cannot put back and she wants it written down at the ninth hour of the eleventh of the month beginning 1,634, standing at the foot of a ladder with an empty stone in front of her.

She can put the book back. The stone is two feet by a foot and it has been there since before he came and there is nothing wrong with it.

And if she puts it back there tonight it will be a book on a stone that a man walked over for forty-five days and did not see, and that is not what it was.

That has gone. She asked, and asking does not come off a thing, and the six days between the twenty-fourth and the thirtieth of the month beginning 1,604 are not going to be un-asked by a girl putting a book down on a stone in the dark.

She stood there for a while with the peg in her hand and then she put the peg down on the flat stone where the book had been, which is not a thing either of them asked for and which she did anyway.

The stone is a stone.

She is the only person on nine days of road who has ever taken anything off it and there is no arrangement on this ground for putting a thing back the way it was, and the only person in the world who could tell her whether a keeping she has taken back is still a keeping is twenty-two miles east on his own side of a plank table with his hands flat on the wood, and she is not going to spend an asking on it.

She went up and got her bag and did not go anywhere with it, and she put the book inside her coat against the place where the paper is.

There are two things against her ribs now and she has never once called either of them anything.
