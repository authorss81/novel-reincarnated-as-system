# Eleven Miles To The Turn

She walked eleven miles east on the eleventh of the month beginning 1,604, turned round, and came back, and the reason she turned is that there was nothing to ask a man at eleven miles.

## The First Hour On The Tenth

She went at the first hour with the bar on her shoulder, the skin three-quarters full, and two loaves in a cloth in the top of the bag, which she had earned on a road gang at the rate a woman behind a counter says out loud.

Not one person on nine days of road asked her where she was going.

That is the whole of it and it took four seconds. The keeper was inside with his pot and the door was shut. The boy of eleven was sitting on the rail eating something out of a bowl and he moved his feet out of the way and she went past. The carter came through at the sixth hour going west and said good to her the way he has said good to her four hundred times, and she said it back, and he did not look up.

There is not one person on nine days of road who would have asked her, and she had known that at the seventh hour of the ninth and she went anyway.

## Nine Miles Of Strip Road

The strip road runs east from the post for nine miles and there is nothing on it but a bend a mile out and low rough grass on both sides and a cart track that leaves it and comes back to it twice.

She went the way she goes. A mile an hour and something on flat made ground with a load, and it took the whole of the daylight and she sat down twice, both times on the cart track and both times for four minutes.

She is lighter than she was on the eighth of a month and she has been lighter every day since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544, and a body that has been lighter for a month walks quicker than one that has not, and she did nine miles between the first hour and the eighth hour and she was at the turn with light left.

The arm went open at the fourth mile and shut at the seventh, which is four days in five and this is one of the four, and it did not open again before the turn.

## The Turn

There is a bench at the turn and a trough and a bar on two posts with nothing on it, and the bench is of the same wood as the bench outside a store five miles west of here and it has been in the sun since before she came to this ground.

She put the bar down and sat on the bench with her back against the post and drank a quarter of a cup out of the skin and looked east.

The strip road stops at her feet. There is no mistaking it. The metalling starts nine feet away with a kerb on each side, and it goes on east, dead straight, for as far as she can see in good light, and there is nothing on it at all.

She had never looked at that road before the ninth of a month. She had gone through the turn nine times in two months and every time she had been going somewhere else.

## Two Miles On Made Ground

She slept about six hours on the bench with the bar under her back and she was up at the second hour of the eleventh and made two miles on the metalled road in an hour and a bit.

The difference came at once and she had forgotten it, because two years of her life went on a strip road and then a store road and then a lane.

It is quiet. Not silent. A made road carries sound a long way and she could hear a horse working at a bit four hundred yards off for about ten minutes before she saw anything of it.

The ground on both sides changed at about the one-mile mark. It stopped being grass and became something low and grey and broken, with stone standing out of it here and there and no fence anywhere along four hundred yards of it.

Nothing was growing in it.

She had come out of a month of kerb and drain and a road gang and she stood on a metalled road looking at a mile of ground that had been left, and she worked out in a minute that it had been used for something and stopped being used.

## The Eleventh Mile

She stopped at the eleventh mile from the post, which is two miles along the made road, at the fourth hour of the eleventh, and she stood there for the better part of an hour.

There was a gate ahead of her, far enough off that she could not see the chain on it. Past the gate the made road goes on and the ground on both sides of it is flat, and she knows it is flat because she has been on it and there is nothing between one part of it and another.

That was not what stopped her.

What stopped her was that she could have stood there at the eleventh mile and said four words out loud to a man who was eleven miles further on, and they would not have arrived, and she would not have found out that they had not arrived, and in a month she would have been able to tell herself she had asked.

She has asked a keeper for nothing in eight years. She is not going to build a version of the first one that can be carried out in the dark by a person who cannot hear it.

That is what the eleventh mile was for, and she turned round on it.

## Out Of The Road In The Dark

The light goes at the eighth hour in that month. She came back to the turn at the seventh hour of the eleventh and there were four carts on the made road going east in that hour, which is more than she has seen on it in two months.

She did not stop at the bench. She walked on west in the dark with the bar on her shoulder, because she wanted to be at the post and she did not particularly want to be at the post, and she got a mile and a half past it.

The second cart came round behind her without a light of any kind on it, and a cart at a mile an hour in the dark does not make a sound until it is on top of you, and she went off the metalled road sideways into the broken ground on the north side with a bar on her shoulder and about six inches of loose stone under her boot.

It went all the way in to about the knee.

The second count went for four and then it did not come back and she was down on her left hip on loose stone with nine feet nine of bar across her and a cart going past at nine yards with a man on the tail who did not look at her because he was not looking at anything.

It came back in twelve.

She lay on the low grey ground with her face in the stone dust and counted it out loud, twice, because the first count came back at nine and she wanted to see it again.

Then she got the bar in front of her and put her weight on it and came up on her feet in three goes, and stood there with the cart gone and the road empty and her right arm open and no light anywhere on it.

## Walking Back In The Dark

She walked the rest of the way at a mile an hour, which is a quarter of what she had been doing.

There is a thing about an arm that is open four days in five. On flat ground in daylight it is a weight she can carry. At night, on ground she has never walked, past the point where the cart came, it is a thing that makes its own arrangements and she does not get a say in them, and she understood that at the third hour of the twelfth with two miles left.

There was no place to lie down in. That was the whole of the night.

She went on west off the metalled road at a mile an hour, which is a quarter of what she had been doing, and the metalling gave out a mile from the turn and then the strip road began and ran nine miles.

There is a thing about an arm that is open four days in five. On flat ground in daylight it is a weight she can carry. At night, on ground she has never walked, it is a thing that makes its own arrangements and she does not get a say in them, and she understood that somewhere about the third mile with the breath still not right.

She sat down once, in the low grass a mile and a half off the metalled road, at the eleventh hour, for nine minutes. She did not lie down and she did not sleep, and there was nothing within nine hundred yards of her that she could have lain down on.

She came onto the strip road at about the second hour of the twelfth and had a mile and a half left, and she did the last of it.

## The Day She Did Not Go Back

She came onto the flat at the third hour of the twelfth, and the post was a mile ahead of her, and she went to it.

She sat on the rail at the foot of the ladder with the bar across her knees and she did not leave that rail for the rest of that day.

She had come nine miles out, made two miles on the made road, turned round at eleven, and walked nine miles back in the dark. There was no arrangement of the four months before this one that would have had her sitting on that rail at the seventh hour of the twelfth instead of standing at a post on a strip road.

That is what she wanted. She wanted to be the kind of person who sits at a turn for a day.

She had two loaves and she ate one of them and put the other one back in the bag, and she filled the skin at the trough because the trough had water in it and she has never once in two months walked past a trough that had water in it.

## What Was East Of The Post

Here is the thing she had to sit with all day and here it is.

There is nobody on nine days of road behind her and not one of them has come for her. There is nobody on eleven miles of made road between the turn and a gate, and nobody between the gate and a wall.

There is nobody between that wall and the ground beyond it, which is the flattest ground she has ever stood on and the emptiest. There is a man nine hundred years old at the end of it with the only room on this ground that anything has ever been written down in.

And she was going to sleep nine miles short of all of it, on a flat stone at the foot of her own ladder, with an arm that opens four days in five. If she was not up in four hours in the morning then there was nobody on this ground who would know where she was.

She lay on the flat stone with the bar across her and her right arm open and the loaf in the bag under her head.

She slept five hours and she had not expected more than one of them, and she was up at the fourth hour of the thirteenth.
