# The Same Three Terms

She asked a second question at a wall on the thirtieth of the month beginning 1,604, at the seventh hour, in daylight, on her own side, with nobody at all in the hearing, and she got the three terms back word for word.

## The Fourth Hour Of The Twenty-Ninth

She had nothing to eat and she went anyway, which she wrote down against herself afterwards as the first thing in a month she had done without working out why first.

The bar went on her shoulder at the fourth hour and the bag had the skin in it and the dish and the lump of clay and the ring a finger’s width, and there was no loaf in it and there had not been one since the second hour of the twenty-sixth.

That is everything she had. Nine miles of strip road to the turn, eleven of made road to the gate, two more to the wall.

She is not hungry. She has not been hungry since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 and she worked that out two months ago and it has stayed worked out.

Not hungry is not the same as fed. She got nine miles done by the eighth hour on no bread since the twenty-sixth and she was slower than the eighth of a month and she knew it at the seventh mile and did not do anything about it, because there was nothing to do.

## The Gate On The Twenty-Ninth

The boy of fifteen was on the chain and he put it down and asked the question, and she said the answer she says, and then she stood there with the bar on her shoulder for longer than she needed to.

She had it ready. She had had it ready since the second hour of the twenty-seventh at a flat stone at the foot of a ladder and she had it ready and she did not say it, and the reason she did not say it took her a full minute at a gate in the dark.

The reason is that a boy of fifteen put a chain down once for her and walked two miles into flat ground and stood four feet off while a man of nine hundred years did not look up, and he has a cart to let through most hours and he was owed nothing and he has never once asked her for it.

She is not going to ask him twice.

She told him she was going to the wall and he put the chain up for a cart behind her and looked at the bar.

“You have nothing on the bar,” the boy said.

“No,” Ning Yan said.

He thought about that and then he picked up the chain and hung it on the post and did not say anything else, and she went on east, and that is a whole gate.

## Eleven Miles Of Made Road And Two More

She made the eleven miles between the fourth hour and the eleventh and then went on past the gate in the dark and lay down a mile short of the wall in the low broken ground off the north side of the made road, which is where she has done it three times now.

She did not sleep much and she was up at the third hour and she did the last two miles in daylight and the light came up on her a mile out, on made ground, with a bar on her shoulder, alone, and there was nothing anywhere in the world for her to be doing.

## The Wall

He was on his own side of the plank table with his hands flat on the wood, the way he has been every hour since the fourteenth of a month beginning 1,574.

She stopped four feet into the opening at the west jamb with the metalled road at her back. There was nobody behind her at all. There had been a boy of fifteen there two days ago and this time there was flat ground and the bar on two posts a mile off and a trough.

She had thought about that for two days and she had decided it was hers to give up and not his, because she had worked out at the ninth hour of the thirteenth of a month that asking is done in the open in front of people, and that was her finding and not a term he had given her, and she was the one who had decided it mattered.

## What Is It For

She had it ready and she said it once, in the ordinary voice, standing up, in daylight, at the seventh hour of the thirtieth of a month, with nobody in the hearing.

“What is it for.”

Four words. The same number of words as the first one, which was not a decision and which she noticed eleven seconds after she had said it.

Nothing came. She waited a count and four times that count and he did not look up off the table, and then he did.

## The Same Three Terms

“One at a time,” he said. “At the wall. In daylight.”

That is all of it. He did not add a word and he did not look at her while he said it and he put his hands back on the plank and stood there.

She waited out another count in case there was more, and there was not.

“You said you would answer the first one.”

“I said nothing about when.”

“And this one is a question too.”

“It is a question,” he said.

“And it goes in the book the same.”

“It goes in the book the same.”

## What That Cost Her In Miles

Here is the sum and she did it standing on dressed stone at the west jamb of a wall in daylight with her arm shut.

One at a time, at the wall, in daylight.

At the wall is nine miles of strip road, eleven of made road and two of the last of it, and it is twenty-two miles each way and she has now done it four times in ten days.

She can do the twenty-two in a day. She cannot do the forty-four in a day, and she cannot do the forty-four in a day and a half either, because there is nothing between the gate and the wall to lie down on except ground with stones standing out of it four inches apart.

So it is two days and forty-four miles for every asking.

She has been eating at a loaf a day and a half and she has had none of that since the twenty-sixth, and the terms are not going to carry a loaf.

## What It Is In Months

The part that came after the miles was the part she had not had before, and she stood there and let it arrive without doing anything about it.

One at a time means the second one is behind the first one. The third one is behind the second one. There is a queue and she is at the front of it and she has no idea how long it is.

She has no idea how many questions there are.

She knows what she wants to ask. She has had a list of it since the thirteenth of a month, when she stood at this wall with a bar on her shoulder and worked out four questions about a window a hundred yards off and one about a count of twenty-two days that she has not been able to place, and a brother’s name that she decided she would not say in front of a person at a wall.

That is her list. It is six things long and she has spent one of them and she has asked one thing that is not on it.

And the queue moves at the speed of a man who is nine hundred years old and who has not said that he is going to hurry, and she cannot make it move faster by standing here, and she cannot make it shorter by asking, and she cannot make it shorter by not asking, because it is not hers and it was never hers.

## What She Decides

She decided it on the made road at the ninth mile with the light going.

She is going to go on coming.

That is the whole decision and she made it out loud, on a made road, to nobody, at the ninth hour of the thirtieth of the month beginning 1,604, and she has never in her life said a thing like that out loud on a road on purpose.

And the second half of it came at the eleventh mile with the bar jumping on the stone and the arm open on the last of it.

She is going to come fed.

She has nothing now. She has not had a loaf since the twenty-sixth and she is going to the wall on an empty bag, and in four days she will do it again, and in eight, and she worked out standing in the dark two miles short of the wall that a person who walks forty-four miles a question cannot also walk eight miles a day for bread, and that the two of those are going to be the whole of her life from here.

So she is going to find work that is measured by the weight and the length, four miles west of this post at the head of a lane, and she is going to ask a man there for it as soon as there is a kerb left on the mile she is standing in.

She did not eat on the twenty-ninth or on the thirtieth. She got to the post at the second hour of the first of a month and she was lighter than she had been at the sixth hour of the twenty-sixth, and there was forty feet of kerb left on that mile, and she went to it.
