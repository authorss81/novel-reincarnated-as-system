# The Window A Hundred Yards Off

She carried a question about a window a hundred yards off a wall twenty-two miles west, and she did not ask it, and she did not put it down either, and by the fifth hour of the fifteenth of a month it was the heaviest thing she had.

## Back On The Made Road

She left the dressed stone at the eighth hour and she had the gate by the ninth, and the boy of fifteen put the chain down and asked the question and she said the one word that was not a word.

“I am going back,” she said.

That is the whole of that gate. He put it up for a cart behind her and did not say anything else, and there was a moon up that did the road in front of her.

She went eleven miles of made road between the eighth hour and the third hour of the fourteenth with the bar on her shoulder, and she was slower than she had ever been over it, and it had nothing to do with her legs.

She was slow because she was carrying the thing and she could not put it down anywhere on that road.

## What She Actually Saw

She has been thinking about it since the eighth hour of the thirteenth of a month and she has got exactly this much and not one inch further.

It is a low building. It is a hand’s width of glass in a frame with a sill under it, and there is a roof on it that has not come down in a hundred years of weather, and the wall it is set into runs off east about nine feet and stops.

There is a door on the south side of it and the door is shut and there is no light anywhere in the building.

That is the whole of what a girl with a bar on her shoulder can see about a building in daylight at four hundred yards, and she stood on a dressed stone for four minutes looking at it and that is four minutes more attention than anybody has ever paid it that she knows about.

She did not go to it. She went eleven feet, the length of the opening, and she was not going to go four hundred yards across that ground at the eighth hour with one arm and no light and nothing out there at all.

## What She Is Not Going To Ask About It

Here are four things she could stand at a wall and ask him, in daylight, with a person hearing it.

Whether there is a room in it.

Whether there was a light in it and what put the light out.

Whether anybody is in it.

Whether it is on what he keeps.

That is the whole of her list. She worked it out at the third hour of the fourteenth on a made road in the dark and she had it by the fourth and she carried it for eleven miles after that.

She is not going to spend her asking on any of the four.

That is not courage and she did not arrive at it by being brave about it. It is arithmetic. She has worked out how to ask a question that costs nothing to hold, because asking one at a time means there will be another one after this, and if the first one she ever puts to him is about a light in a window she will not have a question left for the thing she went out there for.

A window a hundred yards off a wall is not a small thing.

It is also the only thing she has found on this ground that nobody told her about, and she has been out here four months and everything else has come to her from a person with a reason to bring it.

## Four Hundred Yards Out

She went past the bar on two posts at the fourth hour and she stopped on this side of it, which is what she has always done.

It is two uprights and a cross piece and it has had nothing on it since before she came to this ground and she has gone past it eleven times in two months in one direction or the other and she has never wanted to go through it.

She looked at it for about a minute at the fourth hour and worked out the one thing she had not worked out before.

Whatever is on the far side of that bar, he could have walked past it any hour of any day for nine hundred years and put something on it, and he never has, and there is nobody on this ground who would notice if he had.

There are four hundred people west of here who fill a board every nine or ten days with a paste pot and write three names into a flat book under a ladder, and they would not notice a bar on two posts going over to the east of them either.

That is not a finding about him. She wanted it to be and it did not turn out to be. She has found out that he does not use anything to keep anything, and that is a smaller thing than the four sentences on the second leaf of her own book, and she has put it in the place where she keeps things she cannot use yet.

## The Made Road In The Dark

There was no room and no shed between that bar and the gate on the fourteenth of a month, and she had eleven miles of made road to do in the dark and she did it.

Her fourth count went for four at the seventh mile and came back in four and she walked on. She sat down once, at the sixth mile, in a ditch, for nine minutes, which is the longest she has sat down anywhere since the month that has gone.

She did not sleep at the gate. There is nothing there to sleep against except a gate post and a cup on a wire, and she had done that on a bench two nights before and she was not going to do it twice.

She came onto the strip road at the turn at the fourth hour of the fourteenth and made the nine miles by the second hour of the fifteenth, stopping once at the trough to fill the skin, and she did not eat the loaf until the eleventh hour of the fourteenth because she wanted to have it.

## Carrying It

She has been at the post four days now and there are three people on it and none of them know what she is.

The keeper fills a board off a cart every nine or ten days. The boy holds the near end of it. The carter comes through with a horse and a load. None of them has asked her one question in four days about anything that is not bread or a board, and she has not asked them one question either, and she is not going to, and that is settled.

What she is doing is carrying a window.

Here is the thing that is actually heavy about it and she did not expect this to be the heavy part.

It is not the answer. She has gone at it for four days and the answer is not what she is carrying.

It is that somebody had a light in a window and does not have a light in a window now, and she does not know one thing about why, and she has three ordinary explanations and she has turned all three over until they are worn.

The lamp went out. The person who kept it is asleep or is away or is ill. Somebody put it out on purpose and meant it to be out.

All three of those are ordinary. All three of them are things that happen to buildings on flat ground at the end of nine hundred years of nobody looking at them.

None of them is a reason.

She is not going to build a fourth one at a trough to make it easier to carry, and she is not going to ask, and she has not asked, and she is going to have to walk past it in daylight again.

## What It Is Going To Do To Her

And this is the part worth putting down at the fifth hour of the fifteenth of a month beginning 1,604, on a rail at a post, in daylight.

It is going to sit on her.

She has carried things before. She carried a bar across a strip road for two years and a skin and a dish and a lump of clay and a ring a finger’s width, and every one of that came out of her pocket when she put it down, and she has never in her life carried a thing that she could not put down.

A window a hundred yards off a wall is not a thing she has. It is a thing she does not know, and it is going to be on her every hour from now until somebody tells her, and the only person alive who could tell her is eleven miles east and a wall off, and she has decided she is not going to spend the first asking on it.

So she has decided to carry it. That is all she has done. It is not a plan and it is not a step and it is not the beginning of anything.

It is a question in her own head that she has agreed not to put into the air, and she is going to walk nine miles of strip road east with it on the next day she goes, and then eleven miles of made road, and it is going to be standing on the dressed stone beside her when she opens her mouth at that wall.

She filled the skin and she looked east down the strip road, and there was nothing on it, and that was the first morning in eleven days that looking east had not given her anything to do about.
