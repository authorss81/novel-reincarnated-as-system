# What Nine Years Did Not Take

She told him all of it on the second morning, sitting on a bank four feet off his barrow with the work going on around them, and she told it in short sentences because he is nineteen and has been in a field for nine years and does not have the words for any of it.

## What She Said To Him

A man of nine hundred years keeps a book. It is nine hundred years deep. It is behind a wall twenty miles east and there is a girl fifteen at a gate who says good and a boy of eleven at a post who carries boards and a man of fifty-five at a cut in a made road who opens a chain in daylight for anybody who asks.

There are four on a page. There were four on it. Six now.

On the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784 he shut it in daylight with nothing on the table and no one asked him to.

And her own name went into it at the month beginning 1,724 as lost, and there is a word at the end of her line that she still cannot use, and it was in there before she ever heard of any of this and it is in there now, and it is not coming out.

She said all of that in four minutes and he stopped working somewhere in the middle of it and stood with his barrow handle in his hands.

## What He Asked

He asked one question and it was not the one she had ready for.

“What word.”

“Lost,” she said.

He stood there for a while with it.

Then he said: “I have been in this ground nine years and there are two hundred-odd people in it and not one of them is written anywhere, and half of them have been here longer than me and were here before me.”

He put the barrow handle down.

“So you are further gone than I am,” he said. “I am only not written. You are written and it says that.”

## What She Said Back

She said: “I am going to take it out.”

And she said it the way she says things, which is as a fact about what she is going to do and not as a hope, and he looked at her for a second and then said the thing she has not put down since.

“With what.”

She did not have an answer for that and she has not had one since and she went over it on the long walk west four hundred times.

A book nine hundred years deep shut on a plank table in daylight by a man who is alive and old and has gone east and will be doing the same thing again on whatever day he gets to. One at a time. At the wall. In daylight. And there is no book left in the world she is permitted to put a question into, and he knows all of that, because she told him all of it in four minutes on a bank.

## What He Did Not Do

He did not forgive her and he has not and she has had five days of it and it is going to be the rest of her life.

Here is how she knows, and it is not a speech and it is not an accusation and it came out of him the way figures come out of the man with a chalk.

On the second morning she said, “I am going to come back,” because she was going east and there was nothing else in the plan of it and she said it in the ordinary voice.

He said: “You can come. It is not the same as being let.”

And then he picked his barrow handle up and went back to work and that was the end of it.

That is all she got. Nine years, one face, four days and a night, and a brother of nineteen working a barrow at a pace he chooses, and one sentence with a door in it.

She has not forgiven him for anything either, and that is a separate thing and has not got confused with this, and she is putting it down so that nobody can tell her later that she confused it.

## What Her Entry Is

She asked him on the third day whether anybody in that ground had ever come back out of it.

He said: “Not one in nine years that I have been here.”

“Then it is not a place you can be kept,” she said.

“It is not a place you can be taken out of,” he said. “You came in on your own legs. That is different and it is worse, because you can go out on your own legs and you can walk in again, and nobody here is going to stop you either way and nobody here is going to ask you why.”

## The Five Days And Then She Went

She stayed five more days and she worked two of them.

She worked a barrow. She is six stages past a ceiling with four fingers and a thumb on one hand and an arm open four days in five and she put up about a third of what a man of nineteen puts up, and nobody said a word to her about it and nobody said a word to her about anything.

No one is paid here. There is no board and no chalk and no store with a counter and no rate said out loud, and there has not been for nine years, and she found that out by asking a man of fifty at a barrow on the fourth day and by being told, in the ordinary voice, that there is no such thing.

She got nothing for four days of it. She has got nine coppers she cannot count and no wages since the second of a month beginning 1,754 and she has not had bread since the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,724.

She left at the sixth hour of the eighth of the month beginning 1,814 with a bar on her left shoulder and her right arm hanging.

He walked her a mile and then he stopped and turned back and said: “Go on.”

She went on.

## What She Was Wrong About

She has gone back over this a hundred times and here is the only part of it she cannot get past.

She walked four hundred miles on a bar with no bread, expecting a face.

She did not expect to be forgiven and she has never once pretended to anybody, least of all to herself, that she was. She has had that worked out since she was eleven years old and a man in her head had said the one sentence that put her name in a book.

What she expected, and she did not know she expected it until the first of a month, was that he would be glad.

That is all of it. A brother nine years in a barrow field on the other side of a wall, and a girl with a bar standing thirty feet off him, and in her own chest before she got there was a thing that was not a plan and was not a want and was not one of her four questions.

It was about her.

She has spent eight years refusing documents and refusing keepings and refusing to be given a place by anybody, and the one thing she wanted out of eleven miles of open country was that a person would be glad to see her.

He was not glad. He was not sorry. He asked what word.

And when she said she would come back he said she could come and it is not the same as being let, and he was right about that the way the man at the chain was right about the chain, and she got the same sentence from two men on this ground in eight months and neither of them was being unkind.

There is not one person left in her life who is going to be glad to see her. That is not a punishment and it is not a loss anybody has done to her on purpose. It is what nine years does to a person who was ten and counted loads out loud so the men could hear him do it, and she has put it down and she is not going to make it mean anything else.

## The Bench On The Tenth

She sat on the bench at the turn on the tenth of a month for two hours and the woman of sixty was on it and did not look up.

The woman of sixty has not said a fifth sentence in four months. She said four of them in the month beginning 1,754 and she has been on that bench every day since and has not said anything to anybody, and Ning Yan stood four feet off her for two hours and did not ask her a thing.

And she understood that she did not want to ask her a thing, and that wanting to is a different thing from needing to, and that the only thing anybody on nine days of road has ever put over a sill for her without being asked was a loaf on the fourth of the month beginning 1,694.

There is a bar on two posts four hundred yards east of the turn with a board nailed to it and nothing chalked on it.

She has seen a board written on twice in two years and both times it was figures off a face she had worked.

## What She Carried West

She did thirteen miles in two days and she made the turn at the seventh hour of the tenth of a month and the woman of sixty was on the bench and did not look up.

Here is what she walked for, in the order it happened and not in the order she wanted it in.

She found him.

She cannot stop finding him. He is not something she is holding and there is no book anywhere that has his name in it and he is not going to be taken off a road by anybody, because there is no board and no carter and no chalk.

He has said the thing he has to say to her and he said it once, and if she stands in front of that man at that barrow a second time and asks him whether she is let in, she will be asking a question she already knows the answer to, and she has four questions standing in a shut book and she is not going to spend one of them on a brother who is alive.

She cannot keep him. That is the sixth stage and it is the shortest sentence she has ever said about anything: she found him and she cannot keep what she finds by holding it, and the reason is not a stage and is not a wall, it is that he has his own hands and his own barrow and his own nine years and he does not need a girl of nineteen to stand four feet off him at all hours.

And he does not forgive her, and it settles nothing, and there are six people on a shut page and one of the six could be anybody and she will never know which.

She is nineteen years old. She has no wages, no place, no shed, no list and no road, and she has a brother alive who will not walk with her, and a shut book nine hundred years deep with her own name in it as lost.

She is at a turn at dusk and there is nothing on the board.
