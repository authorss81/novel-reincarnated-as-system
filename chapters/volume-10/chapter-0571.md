# The Count She Is Doing To Herself

She came off the strip road onto the made one at the second hour of the fourth of the month beginning 1,694 with a bar on her shoulder and a hundred and five days in front of her, and she had walked eighteen hours since a man on his own side of a plank table put a date into the air.

## The Bar On Two Posts

The bar on two posts stands four hundred yards east of the turn and it has had nothing on it since before she came to this ground, and she went past it at a walk because there is no reason to stop at a bar with nothing on it, and that has been true of every one of the four hundred times she has been past it.

Behind her, two miles east, past a gate with a chain on it, there was a man standing at a plank table with his hands at his sides.

She did not look back at that and she had made herself a rule about it on the made road at the ninth mile and she kept to it.

## Eighteen Hours

Eighteen hours off a body that has been lighter every single day since the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,544, and four counts of breath at the second hour on made road that went to six for the last three of the strip, and the right arm open for the last nine of it and still open now.

She had eaten a third of a loaf in the dark at the fourth hour on the broken ground north of the road, sitting on her own bag with the bar across her knees, and she was not hungry and had not been since that eighteenth, and she had worked out six weeks ago that that is the reason a starving walk is cheap on her and expensive on everybody else.

It is cheap because there is nothing to hurry for.

## What She Had To Eat

A loaf and a bit.

The loaf is the whole of it and she has had it since the second of the month beginning 1,694 when a woman of sixty did not sell it and a girl of nineteen did not pay for it, and she has been eating off it in pieces for two days, a quarter at a time, in the dark, standing up.

The bread that pays for itself is fourteen miles away at a counter behind a mill with a wheel that has not turned for nine years, and it is drawn against a board that goes down on the second day of a month, and the second day of this month was the day before the day a man gave her a date.

She did the sum about that on the sixth of a month beginning 1,694 and it came out the same every time she did it, which is the only kind of sum that never gets better.

## The Bench At The Turn

She got to the bench at the turn at the fourth hour with the light still coming up behind the ground east of it.

There was a woman already on it, sixty or near it, with a piece of sacking over her knees and a length of string and a needle in it, and she did two inches of the string and she did not get up and she did not look at anybody.

Nine years she had been sitting on that bench. Ning Yan had been past it four hundred times and had stopped on it twice, both times by accident, and both times had got four sentences out of her about a mill and a lime bed and a house beyond a bar on two posts that keeps nobody now and kept four once.

She stood there for two minutes with the bar on her shoulder.

She had come west with one thing in her head and it was not a question, because a question costs forty-four miles and two days and she had asked four of them in seventy days and had not been given an answer to one of them.

She wanted to be somewhere on the fourth of a month where there was somebody else in it.

That is all of it, and it took her a minute and a half to admit.

She did not sit down. She walked on west.

## The Hundred And Five

She did the count in the dust of the store road a quarter of a mile on, sitting on the kerb with the bar across her knees, and it took her nine minutes and she did it twice.

Day one thousand eight hundred and two is the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,784. She is standing on day one thousand six hundred and ninety-seven.

One hundred and five.

Then she counted down, which she has never done before, because a count up is a thing that has happened to her and a count down is a thing she is doing.

One hundred and five. One hundred and four. One hundred and three.

She got to ninety-eight and stopped, because she worked out at ninety-eight that the count had not changed anything at all and that she was doing it because the fourth of a month was four hours old and she had nothing to do with it.

Then she did it again from the top to see whether it came out different the second time.

It came out the same. It has come out the same every time since the third of the month beginning 1,694 and it is going to come out the same on every day between now and the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784, and there is not one version of it that is better news than the others, and that is not a thing anybody has ever had to say out loud about a date before.

## The Road She Had Walked To Get Here

She sat on that kerb for two hours and did not walk a mile in them, which she has not done since she was a child, and what she did with the two hours was go over the thirty-eight days.

Eleven stands in an opening in a wall, and she said nothing at ten of them and a date at the eleventh, and four hundred and eighty-four miles of road, of which a hundred and ninety-eight was strip road on a body that went lighter in the middle of it and has not put that back.

And what those thirty-eight days bought was one number and one set of three terms and a sentence a man said on the twenty-ninth of a month beginning 1,664, which was *a day is not a thing you can hurry*.

That is all of it.

She has been counting since she was a child and every count she has ever made was a count of the way back to somebody, and this is the first one she has made of the way forward, and it has come out into a hundred and five that do not have a single thing in them except the number.

## What A Count Is For Now

Here it is, and she did it sitting on a kerb with her finger in the dust, in daylight, on the fourth of a month beginning 1,694, with nobody on the road inside four hundred yards.

A month is thirty days and a month begins every thirty days.

One at a time. At the wall. In daylight. Forty-four miles for one and two days of walking to get it and to get away from it, on an arm open four days in five, and there is nothing between the gate and the wall to lie down on.

There are four questions in a book nine hundred years deep and not one of them has been answered and she is not allowed a fifth, and the first one is holding the other three shut.

At the very best, one of them in a month, and there are a hundred and five days, and a hundred and five is three months and a bit.

So there are three more times she can stand in that opening and say one thing.

Three.

And a hundred and five days of everything else, and the everything else is thirteen miles of walking and a stone face and a barrow and a store that gives out what a board says.

She put her hand flat on the dust.

The days are the only thing she has that is not a question. She cannot make them go faster and she cannot buy another one with anything she has, and they are the only part of this that is not narrowing.

## What She Has Not Said At The Turn

She did not say thank you to a woman of sixty for four sentences about a mill and a house, and she has been not saying it for most of a month now and she has thought about it on four separate made roads since, and she worked out the reason on the seventh and it was the plain one.

She has not been paid by her. Nothing on this ground lets a girl with a bar on her shoulder interrupt a woman doing string to say that what she said once was worth something.

She would have been saying thank you for being owed to her, and a woman of sixty with a needle in her hands at the fourth hour is not owed anything by a girl of nineteen who cannot count where she stands.

## The Decision

She got up off the kerb at the sixth hour of the fourth of the month beginning 1,694 and she picked up the bar and turned her back on the wall twenty-two miles east, which she has done eleven times in thirty-eight days and has never once done with a plan.

And this time she had one.

She was going west, thirteen miles to a face, and she was going to spend the hundred and five days on the ground and not at that opening, and she was going to keep four questions standing and not put a fifth in, and she was going to find whatever could be found by a bar and a pair of feet and nothing else.

The days are hers. The questions are not.

She thought about that for a minute and a half and it is the only sentence she has ever put together that she is sure of.

A question costs forty-four miles and two days and it narrows what is left, and she has four of them and one a month at the best of it, and the four of them are the only currency there is, and she is not going to spend a coin of it on a day that has a bar in it.

The days are hers. A hundred and five of them. She has never had a hundred and five days that were not somebody else’s day first.

## Getting To The Face

She got to the head of the lane at the ninth hour with the arm shut and walked four miles of cart track between two banks to a flat with a shed on the north side of it, and six men on a face behind it.

There was a man of forty with a slip of board and a stick of chalk on a string, and six barrows, and a breaker in a shed of boards going on a steel wedge.

She did not ask him anything. She had worked out on the fourth of a month beginning 1,634, standing in that same flat, that the trade does not want a name and that the only thing she had wanted out of it was that.

She picked up a barrow handle with four fingers and a thumb and worked from the second hour of the ninth past the ninth to the seventh hour of the fourth of the month beginning 1,694.

It was eleven trips a barrow, and she did four barrows, and the arm went open inside the fifth and stayed open, and she did the sixth and the seventh and the eighth with it hanging and her back and her knees.

The man with the chalk said the figure out loud every time he wrote it, which is what he does with numbers, and nobody on that face knew her name at the end of it and nobody had asked for it at any point in the five hours.
