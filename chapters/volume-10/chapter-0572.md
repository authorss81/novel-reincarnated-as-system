# A House With A Shut Door

She came off the face at the seventh hour of the fourth of the month beginning 1,694 and went four miles east up a cart track between two banks, and forty feet from the store road there was a low house with a shut door, and a woman of fifty sitting outside it, and the woman had been sitting there.

## Forty Feet In

Ning Yan had walked past that forty feet six times in ten days and had not once looked at it, because it is a house with a shut door at the end of a lane off a road that goes nine miles east to a wall, and there is a great deal on this ground that is not her business.

There was a standing stone set in the ground in the yard in front of the door, waist high on a woman, and the door had a bar across it and no lock, and a stool outside with a pot on three stones and a fire under the pot that was not big enough to cook anything.

The yard behind the house was ten feet by twenty and there were four things in it: a barrow with one wheel off it lying on its side, a length of rope, a bucket, and a scatter of lime under the door where somebody had put it there a long time ago and it had gone hard.

The lane itself is a cart track between two banks and it runs four miles west from the store road to a face. Everything at that house is turned towards the lane, which is what a house does when it belongs to a trade and not to a person.

The woman of fifty looked up. She had flour on the front of her sleeves and she had been looking down the lane for some time.

“You are the one with the bar,” the woman said.

## What The Woman Said

She said it the way a rate is said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and then she went on putting a flat loaf on a stone at the edge of the fire without waiting to be asked anything.

“There is a house four miles along this road where the lime used to be worked, and there is a stone in front of it, and the door has been shut since I was carrying water.”

“He was there. A boy, near enough ten years back, and he counted the loads out loud so the men could hear him do it, and he was not one of ours and nobody told him anything and he did not go east.”

That is all she said. Then she turned the other loaf over and wiped her hands down the front of her sleeves and looked at the lane again, and she did not say one word about how she knew any of it.

Ning Yan stood in the dust forty feet from a store road with a bar on her shoulder and did not say one word.

She wanted to say four things. She wanted to ask the name of the house, she wanted to ask the year, she wanted to ask what the boy did to the loads that needed counting out loud, and she wanted to ask the last one, which is what became of him.

She asked none of them, because she has known since she was a very young person that the first question you put to a stranger decides what you get for the rest of your life, and she had four questions standing in a book nine hundred years deep and she was not going to spend one of them at the end of a lane.

## Ten Minutes At A Shut Door

She stood there for ten minutes after the woman turned the loaf over, and she has put down that she counted them, because she counts.

What she did in the ten minutes was try it.

She took the four sentences and turned them over the way she turns a barrow over when it comes up short, looking for the thing in them that will not carry. There was no year in them except near enough ten years back. There was no name. There was no house named and no man named and no road named except that it was four miles along this one, and there was not one word in the whole of it that could be wrong in a way she could check.

It was the first uncheckable thing anybody has handed her in eight years.

She had spent all of those eight years refusing documents. A document has a hand on it and a date and a place and a person who can be asked whether it is true, and every one of those four things is a door out of it, and she has gone out of all of them eight years running.

A thing a woman of fifty says out loud at a fire at the end of a lane has none of the four. It has no hand on it, no date but ten years back, no place but four miles along this road, and there is nobody to ask, because the woman is going back to her bread.

That is why it went into her so fast. It cannot be un-asked, and she has been trying to get out of documents since she was a child.

## The Fifth Stage

The little open place low behind her fourth rib, the size of a coin, had been quiet for a month, and it was not quiet now.

She felt it go the way it goes — no sound in it, because there is nobody inside to be told, and the sound goes into the ground.

She stood there through it with her hand flat on her own side and the woman at the fire not looking up, and it took nine seconds, and after it she was exactly as tired as she had been before it and exactly as hungry as she had been before it, which she was not at all.

## The Loaf

The woman put a loaf in a cloth and held it out without a word and without getting off the stool.

Ning Yan took it and there was a loaf in her hand at the seventh hour of the fourth of the month beginning 1,694 that she had not earned and had not asked for and could not pay for, and it was still warm.

She did not say thank you, and she has not said thank you to a person on this ground in eight years and she has worked out exactly why, and the reason has nothing to do with the woman of fifty.

She said: “How much is it.”

“Nothing,” the woman said. “Go on.”

## What She Was Holding

Here is the thing and it is everything the fourth of a month beginning 1,694 was, and she did not have the words for it until the ninth hour on the four miles east.

She had been given a place.

Not a date and not a number and not an answer. A place, with a stone in front of it and a door shut on boards, four miles along a road she has walked six times.

In sixty-nine days she had asked four questions at a wall twenty-two miles east and had been given three terms and a day, and every single one of those had narrowed what she could still ask.

And a woman at the end of a lane had handed her a direction for nothing, at the seventh hour, out loud, with a shut door six feet behind her and a standing stone in the yard.

She had a place to be tomorrow.

She stood on the kerb at the store road with a warm loaf in a cloth in her right hand and she looked west, and her arm was open and she had not noticed it open.

Then she said out loud, on a kerb, to nobody, the thing that has been the thing for eight years, and it came out of her the way a word comes out when a person has not said it in daylight in eight years.

Ning Wu, she said.

And then she got on with the four miles, because a girl who says a name to a kerb at the fourth hour of a month has not said it to anybody, and she knows that better than anybody alive.

## The One Thing She Did Not Do

She did not ask the woman of fifty a single question, and she did not go into the house, and she did not say his name.

Here is why, and she worked it out standing on the kerb with the light going, in a few seconds, and she had it for the rest of her life.

A woman who tells you where something is has not told you where it is now. She has told you where it was, on a day she remembers, from a place she was standing on.

But it is a direction.

It is the first direction she has had in eight years that did not come out of her own head at four in the morning, and she took it, and she walked west four miles at the ninth hour of the fourth of the month beginning 1,694 with a bar on her shoulder and a loaf she had not paid for, to a house with a shut door and a stone in the yard, and the standing stone was only a stone.

## What She Did With The Rest Of The Afternoon

She got to the cottage at the ninth hour and she stood in the yard in the last of the light and she did not go inside, because the door is shut and there is nobody in it to let her in, and she is not going to be the person who opens a door that somebody else bars.

Then she sat down on the step with the bar across her knees and she ate a quarter of the loaf and it was warm in the middle and cold at the crust, and she put the rest in the bag, and she lay on the ground with her back against the door and her hands behind her head and she looked at a sky with no light in it over a yard that had nothing in it.

She had been given a place to be and she was in it on the first evening and it was the first time in eight years that she had gone to sleep anywhere she had chosen on purpose.

And she was nineteen, and there was no bread on the ground for nine miles in any direction except a quarter of a loaf a woman had put in her hand, and she was lighter than she had been at the sixth hour, and she slept seven hours, which is the longest she has slept since the twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,604.
