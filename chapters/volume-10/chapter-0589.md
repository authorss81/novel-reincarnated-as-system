# What The Board Pays For

She turned round on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,754 at the sixth hour with a bar on her shoulder and thirty-three miles of road in front of her and no bread, and she went west, because a board goes down to the store on the second of a month and the second of this one is in five days.

## The Twenty-Eighth

Here is what she had on the twenty-eighth, and she wrote it out on the kerb in the dust with a finger because she counts and because it is the way she keeps the inside of her head in order.

No wages since the second of a month beginning 1,754, which is the twenty-ninth day of nothing.

No bread at all since the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,724, which is a month, and which is the last day a woman of fifty at forty feet off a lane put a loaf into her hand without being asked and without her paying for it.

Four fingers and a thumb. An arm open four days in five. Eight of eleven holds. A bar nine feet nine.

And twenty-one days.

She is not hungry. She has not been hungry since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 and she worked that out months ago and it is finished, and a body that is lighter than it has ever been is a body that does not want anything put in it.

But a bar is nine feet nine and nine miles of strip road is nine miles, and she has done that on water and on nothing for eight days at the end of the month beginning 1,724, and she came back.

## Thirty-Three Miles

She did it in three days, which is more than she thought she could do, and she wrote three numbers in the dust at the end of each one and rubbed them out the next morning.

The first day she made the post at the seventh hour and stopped there for an hour and a half because her right arm had gone at the fourth hour and she could not get it above her shoulder again after that.

At the post she stood in the yard for twenty minutes and did not go up to the ladder.

The flat stone at the foot of the ladder is bare and it is going to stay bare. There is a groove worn in it by a keeper’s boot going up and down for nineteen years and three names have been under it in nineteen years and there is nothing on it now and nothing is going back on it, not by her and not by anybody else, and she stood in the yard and looked at it and did not go up.

The boy of eleven was at the rail with a board against it. He did not say one word. He has said nothing to her since the seventeenth of a month beginning 1,694 and he was not going to start at the twenty-eighth of this one.

The second day she made the turn at the sixth hour. The woman of sixty was on the bench, the turn was empty, and the board on the two posts was bare again.

The third day she walked the four miles west to the face and she got there at the second hour of the thirtieth of the month beginning 1,754, with her bar on her left shoulder and nine feet nine of stone under her arm.

## The Face

There is a face four miles west of the turn and it is measured by weight and not by hands.

A man with a chalk on a string chalks a board. Every barrow that goes on comes up the face and goes into the low and the figure is said out loud as he writes it, so that the men at the low can hear it, and the board goes down to the store on the second of a month and the store gives out what the board says.

She has worked that face on six sets of days in this year. She has never been paid a great deal and she has never once been paid late, because a day not worked is a day not paid and no day can be moved in either direction by anybody.

The first of the month beginning 1,784 was a first.

She worked from the sixth hour to the ninth and she put up nine barrows and the arm was open on six of the nine and on the other three of the nine she did it with her back and her knees.

The man with the chalk said every figure out loud as he wrote it and did not once look at her arm, and she has worked out over six sets of days that he is not being kind, he is being exact, and a chalk and a string do not care what a woman is doing with her shoulder.

At the ninth hour she said: “How much.”

He said the number out loud.

## The Rate Said Out Loud

Wei Ran’s team came along the four miles at the ninth hour of the first of the month beginning 1,784 and stopped where there was room to stop, and Wei Ran was on the box and she said the rate before Ning Yan had got the bag off her shoulder.

“Six coppers the four miles and you have the bar.”

“Six.”

“Six, and the bar goes in the back and not on the seat, because the seat is for sitting in and the bar is not for sitting in, and if somebody comes looking for a bar in my cart at a gate I am going to be annoyed about it.”

That is a trade and there is nothing in it that is not a trade. She has carried her twice for the rate by the end of the thirty-first of the month beginning 1,784, and she said the number out loud both times, and she has never once said a word that was not about the two of them and a distance.

On the way out she said one other thing, which was an opinion, and she said it looking at the road.

“You are going to find out inside an hour that you cannot count them,” she said. “I have watched a person do that. It is not the counting. It is the hand.”

“Do not tell me what is coming.”

“It is not coming. It is already here,” Wei Ran said. “It came in with the third stage.”

And then she said the rate again at the face, for the road back, which she was obliged to do, and that was the second time.

## The Store On The Second

The store is behind the mill at five miles west of the turn and the wheel outside it has not turned since before she came to this ground.

The board came down at the second hour of the second of the month beginning 1,784 with her nine barrows on it in a column of figures and nothing else, because that is what the board is for, and the woman behind the counter read the column and did not read it to herself twice and put down nine coppers and a loaf.

Nine coppers.

She had six from Wei Ran and she had had nine coppers in hand for four minutes.

She put them on the counter and counted them.

Nine.

She put them in the front of the bag and counted them again without taking her hand out of her head, which is the way she has counted since she was eight years old.

Ten.

She took them out and laid them on the counter again in a line and counted the line.

Thirteen.

She did it a fourth time and got nine. She did it a fifth time and got ten again, and on the sixth she put her hand flat on the counter and left it there and did not do it again.

The woman behind the counter watched all of it. She is a woman behind a counter and she gave out what the board said and she did not say one word about it and she is not going to, and she was not being careful. She was giving out bread and coppers at the rate the board said and a person at her counter who cannot count them is not a thing she has any business with.

## What She Worked Out On The Step

She sat on the step of the store with the loaf in a cloth in her right hand and she got it out in two minutes, and it is the same finding and she had known it was coming on the twenty-seventh and she had told the only person on nine days of road that she had no wages to carry.

Everything she found stopped being findable in her about two hours after she found it.

The line in the dew. That had been told to her twice and she had worked it out and believed it.

And now nine coppers on a counter, counted four times, in front of a woman who is not wrong about anything.

She put her hand in the bag and took out four of the nine and put them on her knee, one at a time, and named each one as she put it down, and she had nine at the end of it and she did it again with the other five and she had nine at the end of that too.

Two ways, and both of them right, and she has counted accurately every day since she was eight years old and she has never once had to do it twice to get the same answer.

She is nineteen, on a step, with a loaf and nine coppers she cannot count, and she is not going to be able to count them again by the end of the month.

Holding a thing is somebody else’s work, and there is not one person on nine days of road whose work it is.

She sat on the step until the light went and she did not eat the loaf, and then she ate a quarter of it, and the rest went in the bag next to a ring a finger’s width and a dish and a lump of clay and a stub of a nib and a cake of ink she has not used in two months.

## The Arithmetic Of The Morning

She got up at the sixth hour of the third of the month beginning 1,784 and did the one count that mattered, out loud, in the ordinary voice.

She is going east.

Not to the post. Not to the face. Not to a wall she has stood in twenty-two times.

To the gate, two miles from where she stopped on the twenty-seventh, and past the gate and the wall and the opening, to the place a rut in wet ground came out on the far side, a quarter of a mile beyond a place she has never been.

She is going there because the marks have been gone six days and there is nothing else in her head out of that country at all, and because she has sixteen days, and because if she does not go now she will stand at a counter somewhere for the rest of her life handing a number of nine coppers to a woman who is owed exactly that and no more.

Nine coppers is the first thing she has earned since the second of a month beginning 1,754.

She cannot count them.

She is going east anyway.
