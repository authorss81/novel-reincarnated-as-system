# What Comes Out Of The Ground

She carried stone for three days on the strip road west of the post for the rate the store woman says out loud, and on the eighth of the month beginning 1,604, at the ninth hour, the ground took something out of her for it, and it told her what it was going to take before it took it.

## The Sixth Of A Month

There are nine men on a road gang and a keeper with a paste pot who does not have anything to do with them, and the gang is on the strip road at the mile west of the post because forty yards of it went out under a cart in the thaw.

The rate is a loaf at two coppers and it is the store woman's rate and the keeper said so out loud on the first morning in front of all nine of them, and there is a line of it on no paper anywhere.

That is nine coppers a head a day and a half and it comes out of what the gang earns and not out of anybody's pocket, and she was the tenth pair of hands and she was paid the same as the nine and nobody said one word about her having come on with nothing.

A bar is not a tool on a road gang. She used it anyway for the short end of a kerb and a lever for a stone, and after the first morning the men stopped watching her do it.

## What A Day Is Measured By

The gang is measured two ways and the foreman is careful about both of them.

By weight: the stone comes off a cart at the second hour and there are nine loads a day and they are the same nine loads every day, and the loads are counted off the cart and not off the men.

By length: what the gang is judged on at dusk is the length of road standing whole at the end of the day. Eighteen feet of kerb. Forty feet of drain. A hundred yards of bed packed and metalled before the light goes.

Nobody is measured. That is the whole arrangement and it is an old one. A man can be slow and still be fed, and a man can be quick and it does not put a farthing on him, and there is no board at that post with a column of men on it and there never has been.

She worked it out on the first morning and she worked it out wrong for about two hours.

## The Second And The Third Day

Stone is not sacks of lime. A dressed kerb is two hundredweight and you cannot put your arms round it, so it goes on a bar with four people, or on a bar with two people and one roller, or with one person on the light end and a man taking the weight with his hip.

She took the light end of everything for three days and she did the shovel work and the packing and forty yards of a drain by herself because forty yards of drain is a job you do on your knees.

Here is what three days came to, and she counted it because she counts everything.

Her arm was open for the best part of two of the three days and it shut on the third. She went through the fourth count inside a loaded reach nine times on the first day and four times on the second and once on the third, and each time it came back in four and she went on.

She ate two loaves a day. She slept on the flat ground at the foot of the post with the bar under her head. She put her left palm on a piece of glass on the second morning at the quarry face and it did not bleed enough to be worth stopping for.

And on the evening of the second day she was lighter than she had been in the morning, and she put her hand in her coat to check, and she did it twice.

## Told Before Spent

Here is the part she had never had before and it came at the second hour of the third day while she was packing the bed behind the last of the kerb.

The small open place low behind her fourth rib, the size of a coin, that came open on the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,274 and is not going to close, got warm.

That is the only word for it and it is the wrong word and she has been at four different ways of saying it since and none of them is better.

It is not a pain. It is the feeling of a floor with a hole in it where something is standing under the boards, and she put her hand flat on it through her coat and she understood, in the ordinary way you understand that a man behind you is about to speak, that something was going to come out of it, and that it was going to come out of her, and that it was not going to be a thing she could put her hand over.

She told one person and it was the boy of eleven at the ninth hour of the second day and what she told him was that she was not to be disturbed for a while.

She did not tell him why and he did not ask and he sat on the rail at ten feet off and did not come near her for two days, which is the single most useful thing anybody has done for her in a month.

## The Ninth Hour Of The Eighth

She was packing the last of the bed at the ninth hour on the eighth of the month beginning 1,604 when it came.

It did not hurt and it was not slow and she was not able to stop it, and the whole of it took about the time it takes to put a hand flat on a table.

Something went out of the place low behind her fourth rib, down through her, into the ground under the road she had just packed.

The men were forty feet off. The foreman was on the cart. There was nobody within nine feet of her and nobody on nine days of road saw a thing, and there was no sound, and there was no light.

And then it was over, and she was on her knees in the stone dust with her left hand flat on the ground.

## What It Came Out Of

She did not move for about two minutes.

It had not taken anything she could put a name on. She checked her hands, her arm, her back, her breath, and she did the count she has done since the eighth of a month nine volumes back, and everything she had on the strip road that morning she had at the end of it, including the four fingers and a thumb and the missing finger and the arm that has been shut since the seventh hour.

What it had taken was out of her foundation.

That is the only sentence she has for it and she knows it is out of somebody else's mouth and not hers, and it is the right sentence, and she sat in the dust and repeated it four times and it did not stop being true.

She is lighter than she was at the seventh hour. She has been lighter every day since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 and this is the first time she has been able to say how much in a single morning.

## The Work Does Not Measure Her

Here is the finding and she got it on the flat at the post that night with the bar across her knees.

Three days.

Eleven sacks onto a sill at a store, and three days of stone, and nine days of a post and a trough, and twenty-two miles on a bar in the month beginning 1,574. Forty yards of drain and eighteen feet of kerb and a hundred yards of bed, all of it done by a body that is three stages past the end of any realm a county holds.

And what came out of the ground at the ninth hour of the eighth of a month was not in proportion to any of it.

It was not in proportion to the weight she carried, because the weight on the first day was a fifth of the weight on the third and what came out on the third was not five times what came out on the first, and there was no first, because nothing had come out of her on the first or the second in three days of stone.

It was not in proportion to the length either. She packed forty yards of drain on the third and three hundred yards of metalling on the first two and the road gang's length is judged at dusk by the foreman with a cord and a peg.

She has worked on nine days of road since the eighth of a month nine volumes back and she has never once been on a board that measures her. The work is measured by the weight and the length and she can take both of those with her left hand and a bar, and the ground took a piece of her for it anyway, and there is no column on any board between here and the turn with her name against a figure.

## What It Is For

She did not sleep that night and she was not hungry and she was not tired in the way a person is tired.

Nobody saw it. The foreman marked off forty yards at dusk and the keeper did not see her go down on her knees in the dust and nine men walked past a girl packing a road.

And she has not told one person on nine days of road, and there is one person on this ground who would know what came out of her and what it bought, and he is twenty-two miles east and he came west on foot and went back east on foot and she has not asked him for one thing in eight years.

She got to the end of the day and she got to the end of what she has got and here is what is left of it.

Something came out of her and went into the road she had packed and she has no way of knowing what it was for and she is not going to be told by a man who keeps a book of everybody's numbers.

The bar was on the flat stone at the foot of the post when she got back to it and she had to take the weight of it in her left hand to lift one end of it, and she is lighter than the bar is, and she was not that on the second of a month.

She got the end of it up at the second try and she carried it down to the trough and she filled the skin, and in the morning she would go out at the first hour and do it again, because the foreman measures the length at dusk and there is forty feet of kerb left, and she was not going to find out what any of it was for by stopping.
