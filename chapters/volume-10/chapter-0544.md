# The Carter At The Trough

The board at the post was filled at the seventh hour of the fourth of the month beginning 1,604, nine days after she put a book of eight leaves on the flat stone under the ladder, and the boy of eleven was holding the near end of it while it went up.

## The Fourth Of A Month

She stood at the post from the fourth hour because there is nowhere else to stand and she has had that settled for four months, and the day before she had eaten for the first time in fifteen and the day before that she had stood in the dust until the dark with a man nine feet off her and not said a word.

A post is a post. The rail is two feet off the ground and the trough is at the foot of the wood and the ladder goes up to the board and not past it, and there is one cup on a wire on the east side for anybody who wants to drink.

She sat on the rail from the fifth hour with her left leg stretched out in front of her and let the sun come on it.

## The Rag And The Pot

The cart that brings the paste comes through when it comes.

It came at the seventh hour with boards on it and the man with the paste pot got down off the tail with the rag in his hand, and he did not look at Ning Yan and he did not look at the stone, and he began at the top of the board the way he has begun every nine or ten days for nineteen years.

She watched him from four feet off.

There is a thing she has never once been able to decide concerning that man and it is this: he has walked over the flat stone at the corner of it, going up and coming down, for two days, and it has a book on it, and there are three names in the flat book beside it, and in two days he has not once put his hand out to any of it.

That is not carelessness. A man who does not know what is on a stone does not keep off it.

He is a man who has been keeping three names under that ladder for nineteen years and he has never asked what anybody puts down beside them and he never will, and she put hers there because that is the only kind of keeping there is out here and she was right about that, and she is going to be wrong about a good deal else in the same decision.

## Nine Days On A Stone

Nine days.

She has not picked it up. It is on the flat stone with an inch of stone between it and the flat book, folded in half three times, eight leaves the size of a closed hand, a date and a place on the first one and four sentences on the second and six empty ones that are hers.

Nine days in the open at the foot of a ladder at a post where a cart comes every nine or ten days with a paste pot in it.

She put it down in daylight in front of three people because she had worked out in thirty-seven days and twenty-two miles that a thing put down in front of people is not put down where nobody will find it.

She has been right about that twice in two days and she is sitting on a rail at the seventh hour working out what else it carries.

## What He Says At The Trough

The carter of thirty-five came over to the trough with his horse at the eighth hour and it was the same carter who had put her bar up onto a cart on the store road a week ago.

He filled the dipper and gave it to the horse and then stood there with his forearm on the rail.

“That book you left under the ladder,” he said. “I have been thinking about it.”

He let that stand for a bit, the way a man does when he is saying a thing he has had in his mouth for nine days.

“You put it down where all three of us could see it do it,” he said. “That is not asking him to mind it. If you had asked him he would have told you he had no room for it and he would have been telling you the truth.”

He filled the dipper again and the horse drank it.

“Anything you leave out in the open, somebody walks past,” he said. “Nobody has to decide to.”

Seven sentences and that is all of it, and he did not say one word of what was in the book or who it was for, and he went back to his boards.

## The Boy Of Eleven

The boy was holding the near end of the board again at the ninth hour because the keeper made him hold it while the paste went on, and he looked at the stone when he thought she was looking at the rail.

She has looked at that boy twice in two years and he has never asked her one question.

She looked at him and he looked at the stone and she said nothing, and he said nothing, and the two of them stood on a strip road in daylight looking at a flat stone with a book on it.

He is eleven years old and he has had nine days of that book in front of him at the foot of a ladder, and he cannot read, and she does not know what he thinks it is, and she is not going to ask him because she does not want it in his mouth.

He is the only person at that post who has never once looked at it as though it were nothing in particular. The keeper does not see it. The carter has decided. The boy is still working on it.

## The Trough And A Word About Work

She cleared the trough at the tenth hour because the dipper had a skin on it and a horse had drunk the last of the water twice.

It is four feet of stone hollowed out with a slab across the low end and twenty pounds of silt in the bottom of it, and she got it out with her hands and the heel of her left hand in a quarter of an hour, and her right arm was open by the end of it and she washed her own hands in what was left.

The keeper came out with the pot under his arm and stood at the corner of the post and watched the whole of it.

“We are short on the road,” he said.

“All right,” Ning Yan said.

“I will be here at the first hour and the gang comes on the second.”

That is all that was said and he went back in and shut his door, which he has not shut on her before in two years, and he did not lock it.

She stood at the trough with wet hands in the dark and understood that she had just been hired for something by a man with a paste pot who has never asked her one question that was not of bread or a board, and that he had not said the rate out loud because he does not know what the rate is and neither does she.

## Found

Here is what she got at the ninth hour of the fourth of a month with the paste drying grey on a board and a horse drinking at a trough.

She has been treating that stone as a safe place.

It is not a safe place. It is a place a cart comes past, and a trough everybody in the last nine days has stood at, and a ladder a man goes up and down forty times a day, and a spot where a boy of eleven has stood every day this week with nothing else to look at.

Four sentences written in her own hand in soot off a chimney breast on paper she made out of a blanket in nine days, on the second leaf of a book of eight, saying why anything is kept at all, on a flat stone under a ladder on a strip road, nine miles west of a turn.

That was hers and not in his book and not in anybody’s office and no reader on nine days of road could be sent for.

And a carter has thought about it for nine days and a boy has not been able to stop looking at it.

She sat on the rail and let it be true for a minute.

She did not put it there to be safe. She put it there because keeping is what that man does and there was nowhere else on nine days of road to put a thing down.

She thought that putting it down in the open in front of three people meant it would stay hers.

It is going to be found. That is what she built. She built it exactly as she meant to and it has taken nine days to come back and tell her what she made.

## What She Is Going To Do About It

She went and stood four feet off the ladder and looked at the two books on the stone, the flat one with three names in it that has been there nineteen years, and hers, which is nine days old and has a reader in it now that she did not put there.

She did not pick it up. She is not going to pick it up. Picking it up is putting it back inside a coat and there is a man of nine hundred years twenty-two miles east who will find out what she has done the day after she does it, and she has not asked him anything yet and she is not going to start by telling him where her book is.

She wants it on a stone in the open until she has asked. After that it will not matter what is done with it.

And it has been nine days, and there are three people on this road who have had nine days with it, and one of them has had it in his mouth for nine days and one of them has had it in his eyes since the second day.

She stood at the ladder and the sun was off the board and the trough had a skin on it, and the thing she had put down in the open on the twenty-fifth of a month was found, and she had built it that way, and there was no way on this ground to unbuild it except by going twenty-two miles east and asking a man what else he keeps.
