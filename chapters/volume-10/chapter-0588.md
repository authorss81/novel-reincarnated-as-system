# A Finding That Would Not Stay

The marks in the low line east of the bar on two posts were older than the lime at the cottage, and she stood at the second hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,754 and made herself prove it before she let herself have it.

## What She Did To Make Herself Believe It

She went back to the bench and got her bag and came out and put her heel in the mark, so that she would know which one it was, and then she went thirty yards east along the low line and turned round and looked back at it from the other side.

Then she walked back to it.

It was there. Her heelprint was in it and her heelprint was four inches long and she had put it there ninety seconds before. The ground round about it was marked with the shapes of two wheels a cart’s width apart, healed almost flat, with the dew lying in them and holding them.

The lime at the cottage went hard nine years back. The miller said the lime stopped nine years back and said it the way a man says a thing that happened to a wheel he was standing next to.

A cart that goes over ground that holds water leaves a mark that is still a mark when the ground dries, because the ground gives and the rut is a low place, and nine years of rain does not raise a low place.

She did all of it twice more, out and back, and got the same answer, and she got the time of day wrong twice working it out, because a healed rut reads differently with the sun behind it than with the sun on it, and she had to turn her back on the light both times to see it.

Then she sat down on the rail of the trough with her bar across her knees and said his name out loud on a road four hundred yards east of a turn where a cart had stopped and a carter of thirty-five had got down off the box and was looking at her.

## The Carter

He had come up from the east and the horse was steaming and the cart was empty and there was nothing on it.

He looked at her for a while and then at the ground she was sitting on and then at the ground behind her.

“That line runs a long way,” he said. “It comes out on the gate road.”

“You have been over it.”

“Twice. Nine years ago and once since, and I did not know that was what I was on either time.”

He said it in the ordinary voice, and he was not being kind and he was not being unkind, and he got back up on the box and sat with the reins in his hands.

Then he stopped, and he did not turn round.

“If you want it, get it while it is wet,” he said. “It goes by the ninth hour.”

## The Sixth Stage

The little open place low behind her fourth rib, the size of a coin, had been quiet for a long time, and it was not quiet now.

She felt it go the way it goes. No sound in it, because there is nobody inside to be told, and the sound goes into the ground.

She sat on the rail of the trough through it with a bar across her knees and a carter thirty feet off on a box, and it took nine seconds, and after it she was exactly as tired as she had been before it and exactly as hungry as she had been before it, which she was not at all.

She was lighter at the second hour of the nineteenth than she had been at the seventh hour of the eighteenth. That is the two hundred and twelfth day of it and there has not been one day in a row since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 that she has weighed what she weighed the day before.

## What The Sixth Stage Took

She found it out in the low line, on her knees, with dew coming through the knee of her coat, and it took her a few seconds and she has not been the same since.

Before this, a thing she found was a thing she had.

Not in the hand. In the sense that mattered, which is the sense she has been living in since she was nine years old and kept a name behind her teeth for eight: once she had seen where a thing was, she had it, and it stayed had, and nobody in the world could take it out of her, because it was in her and not in a book.

That is over.

Finding stops being keeping.

She got it the way she got the last two, which is that she did not have to be told anything. She got up out of the low line and walked back to the place where she had left her heelprint so that she would know where she had started from, and the heelprint was there, and she stood on it, and then she looked up for the mark that would have told her she was in the right place.

She could not find it.

The heelprint was hers and it was four inches long and it was in a low line with dew in it, and the two cart-marks beside it were there if she knelt and looked at them the way she had looked at them ninety seconds before, and there were four other people who could have told her where she was, and she was standing on the exact spot and the finding would not come up out of her.

It came up when she knelt. It came up in two seconds once she had her hand on the mark.

And she understood what had happened on the way back to the bar on two posts, walking, and she was not frightened of it and she was not going to be able to put it down.

She can find anything.

And she cannot keep what she finds by holding it.

Holding it is somebody else’s work.

## The Working Of It, The Same Day

She gave it the rest of the morning because she wanted it proved wrong.

She walked the line east for two hours. She counted the paces, because she counts, and she counted them twice and got the same number both times, which put a mark on the ground at the third hour of the nineteenth that a carter with a board would have been glad of.

Then she turned round and walked back west along the line to see whether she could find the bar on two posts by the marks alone, without the bend in the road and without the trough.

She could not find it.

She was in the right place. She knew it was the right place because she put her own hand on the same stone at the fourth hour and it was warm and shaped the way it was shaped.

She stood on ground she had walked at the third hour with a bar on her shoulder, and she could not have drawn you a line from there to the marks. She stood there for nine minutes getting more and more angry about it, and then she stopped being angry and started thinking, which is the thing she does instead.

If she could not find it, she could not go back to it.

Which meant she had to go forward. And which meant that a thing found on this ground stays found for two hours and then it is gone out of her, and the only way to have it at all is to keep walking on it while she has it.

She thought about the last hour of it, when the line was that long across country with nothing on it and the dew gone and her four counts of breath. She thought about it and she could not get past the beginning of it, because it comes down to one thing, which is whether she can walk that line in two hours on a body that is lighter than it has ever been, and the only way to find that out is to walk it and find out.

That is not a rule anybody taught her. It is not in a book and it is not a stage and it does not announce itself.

It is a thing that is going to take her brother out of her hands if she stands still for two hours.

## What The Line Is

She walked it east that afternoon until the dew went, and the carter had been right about the ninth hour, because at the eighth hour she had to stop and put her two fingers in the ground to find the near wheel, and by the ninth she was following it the way a person follows a rail, with her boot on the soft part and not looking.

It runs out across open ground that has never had a cart on it, and it does not go to the store road and it does not go to the lane to the face.

It goes east-northeast across open country that has never had a wheel on it, and it comes out on the gate road, on the far side of the wall, a quarter of a mile beyond the opening.

She stood at the ninth hour of the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,754 with her boot on a rut that has been healing for nine years and worked out all of what it is in a minute and a half, standing up, in the open.

A carter came down that road with a board nine years back, put four names on it and took them east.

They went this way.

That is what she got, standing in a field, on the second day of a count she started that morning at the turn with a bar on her shoulder.

She has thirty days and the marks will not be there in two hours.

She has to walk them now.

## Six More Days Of It

She walked them for six more days and she will say it plainly, because it is the whole thing the sixth stage is, and nothing since that morning has explained it any better.

On the twentieth of the month beginning 1,754 the dew was off the ground by the eighth hour and she went on over the line with her two fingers in the earth every hundred paces, and by the ninth hour she was finding a rut and then losing it and then finding it again, and by the second hour of the afternoon she was walking in a straight line across country with nothing in front of her, trusting a thing she could not remember.

On the twenty-first she got it back twice. She stopped at the third hour, put her bag on the ground, and walked the last mile of it in a circle, and found it, and then lost it on the turn round.

On the twenty-second she stopped trying to hold the whole length of it and started holding the next two hundred yards, and that is the only thing that worked and it is not a thing anybody could teach a girl.

There is no bread on all that open country and there has not been for nine years. She had a quarter of a loaf in her bag from the woman at the end of the lane that she had been carrying since the fourth of the month beginning 1,694 and she ate it on the twenty-third and there was nothing after it.

On the twenty-fourth her right arm went and stayed gone. She carried the bar on her left shoulder and in her right hand and she did eleven miles of that.

On the twenty-fifth the line came out onto the made road four hundred yards short of where the cart said it came out, and she stood on a made road with a bar on her shoulder, east of the post, on the twenty-fifth, which is the day she said a name into an empty yard a month earlier and it cost nothing.

## Where She Stopped

She stopped on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,754, at the ninth hour, two miles short of the gate, because the ground in front of her had nothing left in it to find.

That is the sentence. She has had eight years of being able to hold one thing shut behind her teeth and she had five days of a rut in wet ground, and the second of those held longer than the first.

There are three days left in the month beginning 1,754 and eighteen in the month after it and then the nineteenth, and she is on a made road two miles short of a gate, and there is nothing east of her that she can hold.

She sat down on the kerb with her bag between her boots and worked out what she has, and it took her about a minute.

She has a road she can name, a gate two miles off, a bar, and a body that will do two hours of it and not six.

And she has eight days of walking behind her that she cannot get back to and would not want to.

She did not go through the gate that day. She lay in the broken ground on the west side of the made road and slept for two hours and forty minutes with her bag under her head, and in the morning she turned round and went west, because a board goes down to the store on the second of a month and she had not earned a thing since the second of a month beginning 1,754.

She has no wages and she has not had bread in a month.

She walked back for it.
