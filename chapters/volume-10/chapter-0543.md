# Two Coppers Against Forty Sacks

She walked nine miles west on the second of the month beginning 1,604 with a bar on her shoulder and fourteen days behind her, and she caught a cart on the store road at the eighth hour, and she paid for the ride with her own two hands in front of nine people.

## Nine Miles West

It took her the whole day and she went the rate she goes, which is a mile an hour and something on flat made ground with a load, and the arm went open at the fourth mile and shut at the seventh and she did not sit down at the turn because sitting down at the turn is a thing she has done four hundred times and today she wanted the miles.

At the ninth hour of the day before, she had sat on a rail at a post and asked out loud how many days she had, and the trough and the post and the flat ground had not answered.

So she got up and she went and found out. That is not the same as asking anybody anything.

There was nothing on the strip road going west at all until the eighth hour at the turn, and at the turn there is a bench and a trough and a bar on two posts with nothing on it, and a cart came round the corner from the south with sacks on it.

## Nine Coppers A Head

The driver had his feet up on the rail. The woman on the front bench was twenty-two and she had a team of four and her name is Wei Ran and she has said good to a store woman four hundred times and nothing else to her ever.

“Where,” Wei Ran said.

“West,” Ning Yan said. “As far as the store.”

“Nine coppers a head,” Wei Ran said. “And the bar goes in the cart and it is not in the rate.”

That is what a rate is. She said the same figure to the driver and to the two men on the second bench in the next four minutes, and if she had said a different number to a girl with a bar than to a man going to the mill, it would not have been a rate and everybody on that cart would have known it inside a day.

“Two of us,” Wei Ran said, “and one of you. Nine coppers a head is what I say.”

“I have none,” Ning Yan said.

She said it in the ordinary voice, standing at the side of a cart on a made road at the eighth hour with four people on it, because there is no arrangement on this ground in which you get on a cart without saying what you have, and because she had said it out loud four times in two years and had never once got a different answer.

## The Number Said Again

“The same,” Wei Ran said.

And then she said it again, out loud, to the whole bench, which she does every time.

“Nine coppers a head,” Wei Ran said. “You are on the cart for nothing and I am not going to chase you for it, and I would have said the number just the same if you had money in your hand.”

“And if you ever have money in your hand I am going to take it,” Wei Ran said, “and I would rather you knew that now than worked out that you were the one person I never asked anything of.”

“I am not being kind to you,” Wei Ran said. “I am being the same to you. It is the only arrangement I have ever found that has never once tried to keep anybody.”

That is all of it. There is nothing in it that is not a trade and there is nothing in it at the end of the day that she can point at and call a gift, and she got the bar up onto the boards with four fingers and a thumb and her left hand under the middle of it.

## Forty Sacks And Two Lengths

The load is forty sacks and two lengths of timber.

The timber is for the mill's door frame and the mill's wheel has not turned since before she came, and the frame has been off its pins since the winter before that. The sacks are the store's own and they go back east on the same cart, and the only thing in the world they cannot do by themselves is get off a cart and onto a floor, and there are two men on that cart who have come five miles to do it and are going to do it in an hour.

“You will take them in,” Wei Ran said.

“Forty,” Ning Yan said.

“Forty. And the two lengths up onto the sill, because that is length and I do not have a man who can hold a length.”

She said the words weight and length out loud on a made road at the eighth hour to a woman of twenty-two with a team of four, and she did not know why she said them until afterwards, and afterwards it was the whole of the day.

## At The Store

It is five miles past the mill and then some, and the wheel was where it has been for the whole of the time she has been on this ground, and the store is two rooms and a shuttered window and a bench outside it two feet off the made road.

The two men got the forty sacks off in fifty minutes and she took eleven of them off and up onto the sill.

That is a hundredweight and a bit on a good arm and a sixth of one on the other, and the right one went open at the ninth one and stayed open for the last two, and she did the eleventh one with the arm hanging and her back and her knees.

Her fourth count went inside the loaded reach on the ninth sack and took four counts to come back, and she stood on the store floor with a sack under her right arm that she could not put down for about nine seconds because putting it down was going to take both hands.

Then she put it down.

The store woman came round from behind the shelves with her slow way of moving and she did not say one word about the sack and she did not ask her where she had been or what she had been doing for fourteen days.

## Two Coppers A Loaf

“It is two coppers a loaf,” the woman said. “Same as it has been two years.”

“I have none,” Ning Yan said.

“Then that is the two coppers you have not got,” the woman said, “and it is the same to you as it is to everybody who has come in here with none since I opened this door.”

She said the rate out loud, to a girl and to a woman of fifty who was at the end of the counter and to nobody in particular, which is what a rate is.

“I will take work against it,” Ning Yan said.

“I will take work against it,” the woman said. “And I will say what it is so there is no mistake about it later. Eleven sacks is not a loaf. Eleven sacks is the work of eleven sacks and I am not going to tell anybody else that is a loaf.”

That is the whole of what the woman at the store has been good to her for two years and she said all of it in about half a minute without looking up.

## What She Ate

She ate it at the bench outside the store with her back to the shuttered window, and it was two coppers a loaf and it was the fourteenth day that came to an end.

It was not good. She has eaten four hundred loaves from this counter and it is a brown loaf with more bran in it than a person wants at the fifteenth day.

She ate all of it and she drank from her own skin afterwards and she sat on the bench for a quarter of an hour with her hands in her lap and did not stand up when she had finished, which is not like her.

The count she has been telling stopped on the fifteenth of a month beginning 1,574 and it stopped because she did something about it, and nobody kept her for it. A woman behind a counter said a number out loud. A woman with a team said another one out loud twice. A girl with a bar and four fingers and a thumb took eleven sacks off a cart and put forty up onto a sill and did not ask anybody for one thing.

## The Way Back

Wei Ran put the bar back on the boards at the seventh hour and said the number once more on the way out, to the two men and to a man going to the mill, because she says it every time.

“Nine coppers a head,” Wei Ran said.

Nine coppers a head and a hundred yards of made road and then the whole store road east with the bar lying along the length of the cart where it has lain for eight miles, and she got back to the turn at the second hour and then nine miles of strip road west on her own with nothing to carry.

She was at the post at the eleventh hour of the third of a month with her arm open and her legs gone and a loaf in her that she bought with her hands.

She had not been given anything in fifteen days and she was not going to be given anything in the next fifteen, and she had eaten, and the reason is that she worked for it out loud in front of strangers.

She wanted to say thank you to a woman of fifty and to a woman of twenty-two and she did not say it to either of them, and the not saying had nothing to do with the year and everything to do with the fact that she had paid them and she was not going to stand in a road and tell two people who had been paid exactly what they were owed that they had been kind about it.
