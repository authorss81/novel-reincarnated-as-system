# Asking Is The Narrowing

She asked a third question at a wall on the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,634 and got the three terms back for the third time, and then she stood on dressed stone for an hour and worked out that asking is the thing that closes, and it cannot be opened again.

## The Fourteenth Of A Month

She went out at the second hour of the fourteenth with a loaf and a half and a book of eight leaves against her ribs, and she made the twenty-two miles between the second hour and the ninth, and she was on her feet in the low broken ground a mile short of the wall by the ninth hour and she slept four hours.

She was up at the third hour and did the last two in the dark, because the light does not come up on that flat until the sixth hour in that month, and she was at the bar on two posts before the sky had finished.

That is five walks she has made to that wall in sixteen days and the road has stopped being a thing she thinks about.

## What Does The Word Do

She had it ready before she left the post.

There are four letters at the end of her own entry in a book nine hundred years deep and she cannot use them. They are *lost*. She has had them since the twenty-second of the month beginning 1,574 and she has had three nights of sleep lost and one subtraction gained against them and no answer.

She said it once, in the ordinary voice, standing up, in daylight, at the seventh hour of the fifteenth of a month, four feet into the opening at the west jamb, on her own side, with nobody in the hearing.

“What does the word do.”

Five words. Four letters. She looked past him once at the door shut on eleven feet of boards and she did not look at it twice.

## The Same Three Terms Again

“One at a time,” he said. “At the wall. In daylight.”

She waited out a count and four times that and he did not say anything else, and then she waited another count because she had got used to it.

“No,” she said.

“No.”

“You said you would answer the first one.”

“I said nothing about when.”

“And that goes in the book the same.”

“It goes in the book the same.”

He said that one the way he says everything, which is once, and out loud, and without any room in it for a second meaning.

## The Tenth Minute

Here is the thing she has not been able to put down since and it is not a finding and it is not a feeling and it is not in anybody’s mouth.

At the tenth minute of standing in that opening she came within four words of asking him whether it was going to be a month.

Not asking. Coming within four words of it, with her mouth already shaped around the first one, and the rest of it standing ready behind it, four words long, the same number of words as every question she has put at that wall.

She did not ask it and she cannot tell you why in a way that would satisfy anybody.

The reason she gave herself on the made road at the eleventh mile was that asking it puts a second one in the book before the first is shut, and the rule for that is four words and it is the only rule about this that she has actually been told twice.

And underneath that, nine seconds lower down, where she was not looking straight at it, was the other thing.

She did not want to hear him say that it was going to be a month.

She wanted to stand on that dressed stone at the seventh hour of a month on her own side in daylight, at that opening, with that man nine feet off on his own side of a plank table, and have it go on being a day and a night and a loaf of bread and nothing else, for as long as she was alive.

She went back to the bar on two posts an hour later and lay down in the low broken ground and thought about that for a long time.

## The Sentence He Gave Her On The Twenty-Fifth

Here is what she had been carrying since the seventh hour of the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,604, at a wall, with no one there but the two of them, and she had not looked at it properly in twenty days.

She had taken it for a queue.

One question at a time. She gets the second when the first is shut. She has six things she wants and she has asked none of them and she has to go in order. That is a queue and she can work with a queue and she has been counting since she was eleven years old.

It is not a queue.

On the twenty-fifth of a month beginning 1,604, at the seventh hour, at that same wall, he told her something she did not price because she had asked him why those three terms were the terms, and he answered her, and she did not know then whether asking why was free.

Here is the whole of it, in the order she has had it in her head since that morning.

*“A question you ask me goes in the book. The same as a name. It is a thing that has happened and it is on the list of things that have happened and I do not have the rest of it while there is one standing open.”*

## What That Sentence Says When You Sit Down With It

She sat down on the flat at the eighth hour of the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,634, with her back against the bar on two posts four hundred yards short of the wall, and she read it over about nine times.

He does not have the rest of it while there is one standing open.

Not while she asks. Not until he gets to it in his own time.

While one stands open.

Her own question is the thing that is holding it shut.

That is all of it and it took her four minutes and then she sat on the ground for an hour.

She went out to that wall on the twenty-fourth of a month and she put four words into the air in daylight, and they went into a book nine hundred years deep, and they are on the list of things that have happened, and he cannot get at the rest of anything while that one is standing open in there.

And she is the reason it is standing open.

## Asking Is The Narrowing

Here is what she had believed and what is true, and she is going to write both down because the first one is the shape she has been carrying for twenty days and she wants it off her.

What she had believed: that asking was taking her place in a line, and that everything on that wall was still there, and that it was waiting for her turn.

What is true: she asked, and the asking went in, and the going in is what shut the rest of it, and it is shut by a rule Shuyin has kept the same way for nine hundred years and has never once had to break.

Every question she asks makes it worse and none of them makes it better, and she cannot stop, because the only way any of it comes back to her is that she asked.

There is no version of this in which standing at that wall in daylight is free.

## Three Questions In A Book

Here is the count, and she did it on the ground at a bar on two posts at the ninth hour of the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,634, and she had to sit down twice doing it.

What else is on it. Asked on the twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,604, at the seventh hour, in daylight, with a boy of fifteen four feet off.

What is it for. Asked on the thirtieth of the same month, at the seventh hour, in daylight, with nobody in the hearing.

What does the word do. Asked on the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,634, at the seventh hour, in daylight, with nobody in the hearing.

Three questions in a book nine hundred years deep.

He told her on the twenty-fourth that she does not get a second until the first is answered, and he told her on the same day that if she asks twice it goes in twice and neither of them gets answered, and she took him at his word both times and she kept the rule.

She has kept the rule perfectly.

One of those three is holding the rest of it. She cannot tell which one. She cannot tell because he has never once said which one, and she is not going to ask, and she knows exactly what asking would do to the answer to the other two.

## What She Is Carrying Back West

Forty-four miles in three days, a loaf and a half, and one understanding.

She got up off the ground at the bar on two posts and made the two miles and stood in the opening for a quarter of an hour after he had told her nothing for the third time in twenty days, and he did not look up off the plank at any point in it, and she did not say one word.

Then she went west, and on the made road at the fifth mile she worked out everything she is going to be able to do about it.

She is going to have to stand in that opening and wait, and the waiting is what closes the rest, and the closing is what makes the waiting necessary, and there is no standing still in it and no way of hurrying it and no second question.

She is nineteen years old and she has three questions in a book and one of them is the only door she has, and she put it there herself, and it cannot be un-asked.
