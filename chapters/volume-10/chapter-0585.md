# Eight Years Of Keeping Shut

She said the name of her brother out loud at the seventh hour of the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,724, in daylight, four feet into an opening in a wall, on her own side, and a man of nine hundred years did not write it down.

## The Seven Days

She left the post at the second hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,724 and made the wall by the seventh hour of the nineteenth, and then she stayed until the seventh hour of the twenty-fifth.

That is everything she did, and it is the only thing in a hundred and twenty-one days that had never once occurred to her to do.

There were seven days of it. There is nothing between the gate and the wall to lie down on, so she went back down the eleven miles of made road and lay in the broken ground north of it and came up again, and she did that six nights out of the seven, and on the fourth night she did not go back down at all.

She had no bread. She had nine days of walking and nothing else.

Her right arm was open on four of the seven. On the fifth she could not get it above her shoulder and she put her hand on the dressed stone and pushed and it came up about a foot and went down.

Her fourth count went to seven at the third hour of the third night and came back in five, and it went to seven twice more before the seventh hour of the twenty-fifth, and she wrote all three in the dirt with a finger.

And she was lighter on the twenty-fifth than she had been on the nineteenth, the way she has been lighter every single day since the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,544, without one day of it going back.

She was not hungry. She has not been hungry since that eighteenth and she has worked out six weeks ago that that is finished.

## What Each Of The Seven Days Was

She wrote them out on the fifth of the month beginning 1,724, at the foot of the ladder, on the flat stone, with a finger, and they are everything seven days holds and there is not one thing in them she would have put in a column in a better month.

The nineteenth. Two hours, standing. A boy of fifteen behind. He said the sentence and then the hour went by in silence and she did not leave at the end of it, which was the first thing she did on that day that she had not planned.

The twentieth. Two hours. She could not get her arm up past her shoulder. She stood with her left hand on her right wrist and it stayed.

The twenty-first. Two hours. She said nothing and he said nothing and she went west and lay in the broken ground and did not sleep and came back up.

The twenty-second. Two hours and forty minutes. That is the longest she has stood in one place. She did the count for all of it and it went round and came round and she could not hold it in her head past two numbers.

The twenty-third. One hour and she had to sit down. There is a kerb inside the opening on the near side and she sat on it four feet from the plank table for an hour with a man of nine hundred years on the other side of it.

The twenty-fourth. Two hours standing and she did not walk back down the road at the end of it, which she had told herself she would, and she lay in the eleven miles of made road with a bar under her head.

The twenty-fifth. Two hours, and then two words.

That is seven days.

She stood in that opening fifteen times in thirty-eight days and four more times in the month after that, and before this week the whole of it came to about thirty hours. This week she did fourteen. Every one of them was in daylight, which is one of the three terms, and she has not broken one of the three in a hundred and twenty days.

## What He Said On The Nineteenth

She stood four feet in and he was on his own side of the plank table and there was a boy of fifteen four feet off her behind, because she had asked him at the gate at the sixth hour and he had come without asking one question about it, which is what that boy does.

She had not asked for anything. She had not come for an answer. She came for seven days, and on the first of them a man of nine hundred years spoke before she did, in daylight, on his own side of a plank table.

“Say it here and it goes into you and stays.”

That is all he said. Then he took his hands off the plank and put them flat on the wood and did not say anything else for the rest of that hour.

## What She Did With It

She took that sentence and turned it over for four days standing in an opening on a body that was going lighter the whole time, and here is what she got out of it, and she got it on the twenty-third of the month beginning 1,724 at the eighth hour.

It is the answer to the thing she has been carrying since the twenty-seventh of a month beginning 1,694, when she gave him four sentences about a house and he took them and did not put them down.

A thing he keeps does not go out of date. That is what nine hundred years of one man being careful adds up to, and it is not a virtue, it is just what the instrument is.

So if she says the name, it will not go into nine hundred years. It will not be true on the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784. It will not be a thing anybody can ask after. It will be a thing that a girl said once in daylight in front of a boy of fifteen and a man of nine hundred years, and after that it will be only hers.

He told her that before she asked him for anything.

## The Twenty-Fifth

She came onto the flat at the seventh hour of the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,724 with the light behind her, the way she has come onto it four times in seventy-five days, and she stopped four feet into the opening at the west jamb.

The boy of fifteen stood behind and to the left, four feet off.

There was a carter on the made road four hundred yards east who stopped his cart to look at the wall and did not get down and did not come closer, and she was aware of him the way she is aware of a carter at four hundred yards, which is as a thing that is there.

The plank table had nothing on it.

He was on his own side.

## What She Said

She said it out loud.

“Ning Wu.”

Two words, in daylight, in the open, four feet into an opening in a wall on her own side, with a boy of fifteen four feet off her behind and a carter four hundred yards east and a man of nine hundred years on the other side of a plank table.

She said it in the ordinary voice. She had it ready and she had had it ready since the fourth of the month beginning 1,694 and it did not come out in any shape she had not practised, which she is not proud of and is not going to pretend to be.

## What He Did

He did not write.

She watched his hands and his hands were on the plank at the level of the wood and they stayed at the level of the wood, and there is a brush in a bag on his side of the opening and he did not turn round and get it.

He did not say it was true. He did not say it was not. He did not say anything at all for four minutes, which she counted, and then he took his hands off the plank and stood up straight, which took a while, and sat back down.

That is everything that happened.

Eight years of one thing kept shut on purpose ended at the seventh hour of the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,724 with nothing bought.

## The Part That Cannot Be Un-Said

A boy of eleven at a post told her in eight sentences on the seventeenth of a month beginning 1,694 that a thing said out loud where two people are is out, and you cannot go back and say it quieter, and you cannot un-say it to him.

He was right and he has never once heard her say it.

And a carter four hundred yards east of a wall saw a girl of nineteen stand in an opening in daylight and say two words to a man behind a table, and he did not hear what they were, and he did not come closer, and he will be on that road again in nine days and will not know.

It is out. That is the finding and it is on the page in daylight with three people inside it.

She cannot unsay it. She cannot get the twenty-fifth of a month beginning 1,724 back and stand in it and say nothing. That is done and it was hers and nobody made her do it.

And here is the second half, and she got it sitting on a kerb four feet from a plank table at the ninth hour with a boy of fifteen four feet off her, and it is the part she is going to have for the rest of her life.

It cost her nothing.

Two words, a walk of forty-four miles, seven days of standing on dressed stone with no bread, four fingers and a thumb and an arm open on four days out of seven and a body that had gone lighter every day of it.

And the name is in the air, and the yard has it, and a boy at a gate has not heard it and cannot unhear that he saw it, and a carter four hundred yards off has got nothing at all, and a man of nine hundred years has got a place and a date and a rule and the two words in his own ears.

That is all of it. That is everything eight years of keeping one thing shut on purpose bought, in both directions, and she has to hold both halves at once, and nobody on nine days of road holds both halves for anybody except the person holding them.

She wanted somebody to come back to her and tell her it was a good thing to do.

There is nobody to come back to. She stands in it.

## What She Decided Before She Left The Wall

She stood there after that for an hour and forty minutes, on dressed stone, on her own side, and she worked out the one thing left and it took her nine minutes.

She is going to find him herself.

Not by asking. She has four questions standing in a book nine hundred years deep and she is not allowed a fifth, and the first one is holding the other three shut and it has been standing for a hundred and twenty-one days.

And she has just been told, in daylight, by the only man in the world who cannot be stopped writing her, that a name said at that wall stays with the one who says it.

So there is nothing in nine hundred years for her to ask.

She put her hand flat on the dressed stone and she said the name out loud one more time, to herself, at a wall, with a boy and a carter and a man in it, and it cost nothing at all, and that was the worst part and she has not got over it.

Then she went west, and her arm was open, and she had fifty-four days.
