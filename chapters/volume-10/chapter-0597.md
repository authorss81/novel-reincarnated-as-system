# Walking Him To The Turn

He shut his own door at the seventh hour of the twentieth of the month beginning 1,784 and it is eleven feet of boards on three hinges with a bar across the inside, and he did it in the time it takes to swing a hand and he did not lean on it.

Then he came out and stood on the flat in daylight with a bag over his shoulder and no brush in it.

## The Road West

There are thirteen miles between that wall and the turn and she has walked them nine times this year with a bar on her shoulder and it has never once taken two days.

It took them five.

He was nine hundred years old and he walked it. He did not ask her to stop and she did not stop him.

On the second day she worked out that she was three hundred yards ahead of him and going too fast, and that was the only time in nine years she had ever had to think about another person’s legs.

She dropped back and she did it without a word and she has gone over how she did it every day since.

It is the same thing you do with a bar that is too heavy. You do not put it down in the road and you do not say anything about it. You take more of it on the good arm.

## The Wall Behind Them

There is a wall in this ground and it stayed open.

She looked back at it at the third hour of the twenty-second of the month beginning 1,784 from four miles west and there was an opening in it eight feet high with a plank table in it and nothing on the plank table, and there was no light in a window a hundred yards east of it, and there was a gate standing open two miles west of that with nobody at it.

Nobody shut it. Nothing shut it. She was not there when anything was closed and she has worked out since that this is not a mercy and it is not a reward.

Walls stay. That is what a wall is and it is all of what it does, and a man walked out of one at the seventh hour of the twentieth of a month and it is open behind him now and it was open behind him when he was dead or whatever a man of nine hundred years is, and it will be open tomorrow.

## What They Said On The Road

They said very little and it took five days.

She asked him one thing, on the third day, at the second hour, coming down the made road.

“Is the long way round the cut open at night.”

“It is four miles and it is flat and there is a ditch on the north side of it after the first half mile.”

“That is not what I asked.”

“That is what there is,” he said. “The other answer is no, because I keep the chain and not the road.”

She thought about that for a mile and she has thought about it every day since.

A man who keeps a crossing does not keep the road, and a road that is not kept is open to anybody who wants to walk four miles round a cut in the dark, and on this ground there is exactly one person who would ever want to, and she is walking with him.

He asked her nothing for five days.

Not one question. Not what she was going to do, not whether she had eaten, not where she slept, not whether the thing in her head had been right about anything.

She has been asked things by men, and by clerks, and by a woman at a bench. She has never once been left alone on a road for five days.

## What She Worked Out About Him

She worked it out on the second day and it took her most of a mile, and it is the last thing she takes from him.

He is not walking west.

She has been telling herself for five days that she was walking a man of nine hundred years to the turn because there is nobody on this ground who could walk him. That is true.

It is also true that a man of nine hundred years does not need to be walked to a bend in a road. A man who has kept something for nine hundred years knows exactly how long the road is and what is on it. He could walk it in two days standing up.

He is walking west because she is going west.

And after the turn he is going to turn round and walk back twenty-two miles to a shut door on eleven feet of boards, and he is going to do that on purpose, and no one has asked him and no one is going to thank him, and he is nine hundred years old.

She did not say it out loud and she did not ask him and she did not say thank you, and she is going to be carrying all three of those for a long time.

## The Night On The Made Road

They slept in the broken ground north of the made road on the second night and on the fourth, and there is nothing between the gate and the wall to lie down on at all, which is why it is nine miles west of a gate and not one.

She had a bar and a bag and two hours and forty minutes. He had a bag and sat with his back against a bank and did not lie down at all, and she noticed that and did not comment on it and he did not either.

At the second hour of the third night she woke and he was not asleep and he was looking east down the made road, and she said, “You will not sleep.”

“I have not needed to for a very long time,” he said. “Do not stop for it.”

So she did not stop for it. She lay in the broken ground with her bag under her head and a bar across her and she did the count, because she counts and because it is what she does when the ground is bad.

Four days since the book shut.

Sixteen miles to the turn.

Six people on a shut page.

And then she did the one she had been not doing, which is the reason she has not slept more than two hours and forty minutes in nine years, and she got as far as starting it and stopped, and she has not been able to start it since either.

## The Turn

He came onto the stones of the turn at the seventh hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 1,784 with a bag over his shoulder and no brush in it and he stopped there.

The bench is at the turn and the woman of sixty was on it and she did not look up, and there was a bar on two posts four hundred yards east with a board nailed to it and nothing chalked on the board.

He put his bag down on the stones and he stood up straight, which took a while.

She wanted to say one thing and she had it ready and she did not say it.

She wanted to say *what was in it*. Six words, in the ordinary voice, to a man of nine hundred years on a road with a woman of sixty thirty feet off. That is the smallest thing a person can hand anybody. It would have taken two seconds and he would have answered her, because he has answered her twice about a window.

She did not say it.

If she asks him what six means and he tells her, then she has an answer about six people, and she will still not know where her brother is, and she will have spent the only book in the world that had a question in it on a number of six strangers, and there will be nobody left in the world to ask.

So she stood on the turn with a bar on her shoulder and a man of nine hundred years in front of her and she said nothing at all, and it is the third time she has done that to him and the first one that cost her something she will not get back.

## What He Did

He picked the bag up.

“East,” he said.

“There is a crossing at nine miles east of the post and the chain is down in the dark.”

“There is a crossing at nine miles east of the post and the chain is down in the dark,” he said, “and I know, and I have been doing it for nine hundred years and I will be doing it on the twenty-fifth of a month beginning 1,784 as well.”

Then he looked at her for two seconds, which is the longest he has ever looked at her, and he said the only other thing he said in five days.

“You do not have a road,” he said. “Go east.”

And he went east.

She stood on the turn and watched a man of nine hundred years go up the nine miles of strip road with a bag over his shoulder and an arm that is not as good as hers.

She did not call after him. She did not follow him. She did not say thank you, and she has not said thank you to a man of nine hundred years once in nine years, except for four sentences in the month beginning 1,694 that she did not expect to buy anything.

## What She Was Left With

Nine coppers she cannot count. A bar nine feet nine. A book of eight leaves with six of them hers and blank.

Six people on a page that is shut.

And a line in wet ground east of a bar on two posts, eleven miles across open country, that came out on the far side of a wall a quarter of a mile past an opening.

She could not find it in her head.

That was on the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 1,784 and she had been off it for six days and she knew the direction and she knew the length and there was not one rut in her anywhere between the turn and the gate.

She stood on the turn at the eighth hour with the light going and a woman of sixty thirty feet off who had said nothing at all, and she was nineteen, and there was no list anywhere east of her with either of their names in it where anybody could ask after them, and there was no book left in the world she was permitted to ask.

She went and picked up her bag.
