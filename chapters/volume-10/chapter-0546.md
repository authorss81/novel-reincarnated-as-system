# The Question In Her Own Voice

She found out at the ninth hour of the ninth of the month beginning 1,604 that she did not know whether she was able to ask a man a question, and she had wanted to know that for four months, and there was nobody on nine days of road she could have found out from.

## Forty Feet Of Kerb

Forty feet of kerb was all that was left of the work and the foreman measured it at dusk with a cord and a peg, the way he had measured everything for nineteen years, and she was on her knees at the drain end of it from the fourth hour.

The nine men had the bar between four of them. She had the shovel and the packing and forty yards of a drain that would take nine days if she left the kerb to them, and she was not going to leave the kerb to them.

Nothing came out of her. That is worth setting down: four days of listed work cleared past a line she cannot see, and on the ninth the small open place low behind her fourth rib stayed exactly as it was, and she stood in the dust at the seventh hour and checked twice and then a third time, because a thing that stops is a thing she has never had before and she did not trust it.

She did not think about it for more than a day.

## What She Wanted To Know

The answer was not the thing. She had been carrying the wanting since the twenty-fifth of a month that has gone and she could have put it down anywhere on nine days of road and it would have stayed where she put it.

What she wanted to know was whether she could do the other part, which is the mouth.

There is a whole of her that has never been used. Four fingers and a thumb, and a hand that will not write a line that is not true and will not take a thing out of a place and will not put a thing into anybody else’s hands, and eight years of walking up to a man who keeps something and finding a way of leaving the room with her mouth shut.

She has said no to men who were owed nothing. She has said it in daylight in front of people, four hundred times, and been right every time, and she has built the whole of herself on that.

She has never once gone to a keeper and said what do you keep.

## The Boy At The Bottom Of The Ladder

She did the small one first, in daylight, at the seventh hour, with a shovel in her left hand.

The boy of eleven was holding the near end of a board while the paste went on, and she had looked at that ladder forty times a day for four days and worked out that the fourth rung moved.

“Is the fourth rung loose,” she said.

He did not turn round. He put his palm flat on it and pushed down with as much of himself as a boy of eleven has, and it gave a quarter of an inch, and he took his hand off.

“Only when it is wet,” he said.

“Thank you,” Ning Yan said.

He did not answer that either. He went on holding the board.

She stood there with the shovel in her left hand and her right arm open, and she had asked a question and a boy had given her a better answer than the one she wanted, because it came with the condition on it. She said thank you to him and meant it and it had cost her nothing at all.

Her arm was open. She had asked with it open and it made no difference whatever.

## The Foreman And The Drain

She did a harder one at the eighth hour, standing in front of a man who decided what she ate.

The foreman was sitting on the tail of the cart with his cord and peg in his hand and the nine men were drinking out of a cup on a wire, and she had been putting a bar of dressed stone up onto a sill end for four days.

“Can I finish the drain,” Ning Yan said, “and leave the last of the kerb to the men.”

He looked at her for four seconds.

“The drain is length,” he said, “and so is the kerb, and the road is measured once at dusk and not twice. You can have whichever one you want and I will mark off the same figure either way.”

“All right,” Ning Yan said, and took the drain.

That is a man who has the right to say no to her about bread and did not say it, and it went through her harder than anything he could have put in his mouth, because she had gone at him with a question about her own food and he had treated it as a question about the road.

She got eleven more yards of drain out of the last two hours of that day.

## The Morning She Cannot Find

Then she sat on the rail at the post at the ninth hour with the bar across her knees and went looking for the morning she had decided not to ask, and she could not find it, and that is where the anger came from.

There was a morning. She is certain there was one and she is certain she was standing in it, and she came out of it having worked out the way a person stands in a room they have already decided about. It is not in her anywhere.

She has gone back through eight years looking for it twice, once in the fourth month of a year she could name and once in the month beginning 1,544, and both times she found the day after it.

## The Reason She Gave Herself

The reason she gave herself at the ninth hour is not the reason, and she knows it is not the reason.

The reason she gave herself was that a person who asks can be refused.

And a person who is refused has to go away.

That is clean and it is true about other people and it has kept her out of nine hundred rooms in eight years, and she sat on a rail in the dark and was angry about it, because she picked it. Somebody would have said no to her and she would have had to stand there and take it and then go out of the room, and that was too much to arrange on a day when she was already tired.

Four hundred refusals of her own choosing, and a thing she is owed that she has never once put out in the open, and she is nineteen.

She put the shovel down in the dirt hard enough to leave a mark in the road.

## The Four Sentences In Her Own Book

The second leaf of the eight has four sentences on it in her own hand and she wrote them out of a blanket in nine days and she knows every one of them by heart and she has never once read them back to the person they are about.

They are about why a thing is kept.

That is all they are. She went into that room with nothing to keep and everything taken, and she wrote down four sentences about the reason a person is held in a place, and she put them in a book of her own with six empty leaves that are hers, and she put the book on a flat stone under a ladder at a post because keeping is what the man under the ladder does and there was nowhere else on nine days of road to put a thing down.

Here is what she has not said to herself in four months.

The four sentences are half of an argument and the asking is the other half. A keeper who will not say what he keeps is a keeper who keeps people in the dark, and she has known that since she was eleven years old and had her own reasons for it, and it has been sitting on a stone nine miles west of a turn ever since with nobody on this ground who could read it and act on it.

She is not going to put it that way to him. She does not know what her four sentences have to do with a list and she is not going to find out by talking to herself in the dark.

## Saying It To A Trough

She said it out loud at the ninth hour, to the trough, once.

“What else is on it,” Ning Yan said.

Four words, in the ordinary voice, standing up, in daylight, on a strip road with a post on it. There was nobody within four hundred yards who could hear it. The trough did not answer and the wood of the post did not answer, and the flat ground under her feet is nine hundred years old and has never once answered a question of hers that was not about a weight or a length.

Her mouth did it.

She had been certain for four months that it would not. She had arranged the whole of the rest of her life around not doing that, and the thing that proved her wrong was four words said to a piece of hollowed stone in the open.

She said it four more times, which she had not planned, standing at the trough with her eyes shut and her hands on the rail, and it did not get easier and it did not stop working.

## What She Is Going To Do

Here is what she found out and she found it at the ninth hour and she is not going to un-find it.

She is going to ask him what else he keeps. Not to a trough. To a man, in daylight, at the place where it is done, and she is not going to begin with the first word of it.

She has not asked yet.

That is the whole of the ninth of a month beginning 1,604. Forty feet of kerb and forty yards of drain, a shovel and four fingers and a thumb, and a question she has carried for four months that is four words long, said six times to a piece of hollowed stone at a post where nobody was.

She filled the skin at the tenth hour because the trough had a skin on it, and she did not sleep, and she lay on the flat stone with the bar under her head and her right arm open and worked out that the six times were the easy part.
