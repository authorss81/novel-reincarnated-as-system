# Three Names The Miller Cannot Give

She went back to the mill at the seventh hour of the fourth of the month beginning 1,784 because the mill is one mile east of the store and she was going east, and because there is one man on nine days of road who has not been asked anything since the eighth of the month beginning 1,694 and it turned out that she was the one who spent that.

## What She Had Spent

She worked it out at the store step on the third and it took her a minute.

A mill that has stopped does not forget, because there has not been anything newer to get in the way. That is what a dead mill is for, and it is the only free thing she has been given this year, and she wrote it in the dust and then scratched it out.

She spent it on the eighth of the month beginning 1,694 in a yard with a dog at her feet.

She is the only person on nine days of road who has ever gone to that mill to ask a question, and she has gone twice, and a man of seventy who had not been asked anything in nine years has now been asked something twice by the same person, and it will not come back.

There is no version of that mill where she is a stranger again.

She said that to the dog when she came through the gate at the seventh hour, and the dog was old and got up and lay down again.

## What She Asked Him

She sat down on the bench by the door with the fire ring and the pot on three stones and she did not waste any of it.

“Who else was on the board.”

The man of seventy looked at her for a while.

“The carter chalked it in front of me,” he said. “I stood there while he did it. He had the board on his knees and the chalk in his thumb and he did not ask me anything and he did not tell me anything either, and I read it, because it was in front of my face and it is four inches high.”

“What was on it.”

“Four.”

“Four names.”

“Four names,” he said. “And I have not got three of them and I have never had three of them, and I am telling you that at the seventh hour instead of the ninth because you have walked a long way and you should not have to sit in a yard for it.”

## Why He Has Not Got Three Of Them

Here is what the miller has, and it is everything the mill has, and she asked for it and got it and it took him four minutes.

He does not know the four who lived in the cottage and worked the lime.

He has known them for longer than nine years by their work. One could not count. One could not lift the far end of a barrow. One had a bad leg and did the loading and sat in the sun. And the fourth was the one who counted the loads out loud, and that one was a boy, and that one came with the lime and stayed.

And the miller said this, and he said it in the ordinary voice, and he did not put a name on any of it.

“They were not people with names in this ground,” he said. “They were the ones at the lime and the one that counted. Nine years and I could not tell you a single one of them and I have never once wanted to.”

She sat on that bench with a bar across her knees and understood that she had walked a mile to be told by a man with a dead wheel that he did not know three names, and that he was right, and that it was not going to come from anywhere.

There is no record of that board on nine days of road. There is a wheel that has not turned, a wheel that has turned, a woman of fifty who does not remember a face, a boy of eleven who carries boards, and a carter who is not the next one back.

## The Thing He Had Not Said In A Year

And then the man of seventy said a thing he said afterwards he had not said in a year, and he said it the way a man says a thing he has had sitting in a yard with him.

“He came back for it.”

“The board.”

“Eleven days after. He came down the road with an empty cart and he stopped at my gate and he asked me for the board.”

“And you said.”

“I said I never had it.” The man of seventy put his hands on his knees. “And he asked me twice, and the second time he was not asking me, he was telling me he had not got it. And he said that a board does not come back and a man does not either, and he went off up the road, and that was nine years ago and I have not seen him since and I have not asked anybody about him.”

She wrote all of it in the dust of his yard with a finger while he said it, because she does not trust herself to keep a thing she has only heard once, and it took her four minutes to write and he let her.

## What She Worked Out Sitting On The Bench

She sat there until the eighth hour and she got it in the end, and she is nineteen, and it came out of four things she had been carrying since the month beginning 1,694.

A board on a cart is the same instrument as a book nine hundred years deep.

She worked that out on the eighth of the month beginning 1,694 in this yard and she has been carrying it since, and everything anybody has told her since then has agreed with it.

Here is the half she did not have.

A board does not keep a person. A board carries one. That is the whole work it does and it is done in chalk on the flat of a cart, and when the cart goes east the person goes east, and when it stops the person has stopped somewhere else, and the board does not know where and neither does the carter and neither does anybody at a mill.

A book nine hundred years deep does not keep a person either. It does the other thing. It writes one down and it goes on writing it down.

Both of them take a name off a person and carry it somewhere else.

Not one of them on nine days of road has ever given a name back to anybody, and she has been walking on that ground since she was eleven years old with a bar.

## What That Leaves Her

She said it out loud on the bench, to a man of seventy and a dog, in the ordinary voice, because she wanted to hear the shape of it in the air.

“There is nothing on this ground that can tell me where he is,” she said. “A wheel can. A woman can. A book can tell me what is on it and a wall can tell me when it shuts and a board can take me east and put me somewhere. Not one of them goes and looks for anybody.”

The man of seventy did not say that was not true and he did not agree with it.

He said: “You have been east twice in a year. Go east.”

## The Two Questions She Had And Did Not Ask

She had two questions in her mouth for the whole hour and she did not ask either, and here is the second one, because it is the reason those two days cost what they cost.

The first was whether the boy is alive.

The second was whether the boy was on the board.

She had the second one in her hand at the sixth hour of the fourth of the month beginning 1,784, four miles east of the turn, at the foot of a made road with a bar on her shoulder, and she had it in her hand all the way into the mill yard, and she put it down when she got to the bench.

Here is the arithmetic and it took her four minutes.

A man who has not been asked anything in nine years will tell you everything he has. She used that on the eighth of the month beginning 1,694 and it is the only free thing she has been given this year.

There is no version of this yard where she is a stranger again, and a man of seventy who is going to be asked about a boy by the same person twice running is going to think about it afterward, on his own, at night, with a dog, for years.

And she is going to ask him a third time.

That is the cost of the free thing, and it came due nine months late and in a yard she had already been in.

She has fifteen days. She cannot spend one of them on a man of seventy deciding how much he remembers.

## The Count She Comes Back To

She got the bar off the wall and put it on her shoulder at the eighth hour of the fourth of the month beginning 1,784 and stood in the yard of a mill with a wheel that has not turned for nine years.

There is a count she has never been able to put anywhere. It came out of him in the early months of this year and it has been sitting at the back of her head for nine months and she has never once got it to be a length of anything.

Twenty-two days.

She has written it down and scratched it out more than once and it has never been a month and it has never been the distance to a gate and it has never been the time a thing takes to come back on a cart.

It is a count out of his keeping, and it is older than her counting, and she worked out on a bench in a mill yard at the eighth hour of the fourth of the month beginning 1,784 that it is not going to be a count of anything she can stand in.

She has fifteen days to the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784.

She is going east, and she has nine coppers she cannot count, and there is no instrument on nine days of road that will put a man in front of her, and the only thing left is four days of her own legs on ground she has already walked.

She went out of the gate and up the four miles in the ordinary way, with the bar on her left shoulder and her arm hanging.
