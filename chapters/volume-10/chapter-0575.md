# What A Loaf Is Not

She got back to the head of the lane at the second hour of the tenth of the month beginning 1,694 and there was a loaf on the sill of the shut door with a cloth over it, and the woman of fifty was inside with the bar across.

## Forty Feet

The lane is a cart track between two banks and it runs four miles west to a face, and Ning Yan had walked past the first forty feet of it six times in ten days without looking, and on the tenth of a month she walked forty feet of it and stopped, because there was a loaf on a sill.

The cloth was wet on the underside where the night had come through it. The loaf was cold in the middle and not stale at the crust, which meant it had been put there inside twelve hours.

There was no stone on it. There was no mark on it. There was no string, no ticket, and nothing chalked on the sill, and nobody had come out and stood there while she found it.

She stood in front of a shut door with a loaf in her hand and could not find anywhere to put it.

She put it down once and picked it up again and put it down again, and she wants that written down, because it is everything the tenth of the month beginning 1,694 was and she is not going to make it look better than it was.

She could not find anywhere to put it because she did not know what it was.

A thing she can name can be put somewhere. Wages go against a board. A loaf a woman sells at a rate said out loud goes on a counter and the woman has it in a book. A gift has a shape too, and she has refused a great many gifts in eight years and she knows exactly where a gift goes.

She had it in both hands on a step in front of a shut door and she had nowhere at all.

## What She Did Not Do

She did not go down the lane.

That is all of it and she has been turning it over for a day and a half, because there are four miles of lane past that door with a stone face at the end of it and six men on it and a man with the chalk, and the woman inside had not asked her for anything and had not told her to go.

She stood forty feet in and she turned round and went four miles to the face and worked the tenth, the eleventh and the twelfth of the month beginning 1,694 from the second hour to the seventh.

And what the turning round was, when she got it, is this. She has spent eight years refusing documents, refusing readings, refusing keepings, and every one of those was a person saying give me something and I will give you something back.

Nobody at that door had said any of that. There had been a loaf and a cloth and a woman who had gone inside and barred a door.

## The Three Days On The Face

She filled eleven barrows a day on the tenth and the eleventh and the twelfth and the man with the chalk said every figure out loud as he wrote it.

The arm was open for the best part of the second day and she did six of the eleven with it hanging and her back and her knees.

Her fourth count went for four inside a loaded reach nine times a day and came back in four seven of the nine, which is what it has been doing since she took the fourth stage and which she has stopped being frightened of.

And on the second of those three days she found out the thing all three days came down to, and she found it out with a loaf in her bag and her hand on a barrow handle and nowhere to have put it.

## The Arithmetic

She did it at the seventh hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 1,694, on the end of the bar at the head of the lane, with the last of that loaf in her hand and her finger in the dust, and it took her four minutes, and it is the shortest piece of arithmetic she has ever done.

A keeping is a thing that holds. You can be in it and you cannot get out of it by deciding, and the test is whether there is a door you would have to argue with.

A debt is a thing that can be called in. Somebody has to be able to ask, and the test is whether there is a person who holds the other half of it.

This loaf is neither.

She can put it down. She could have left it on the sill. There is no board anywhere on this ground with her name against a loaf on it, no counter, and no rate, and the woman at that door has not asked her for one single thing in four meetings and has not said the word bread once.

And the woman is not keeping her. A woman of fifty behind a shut door forty feet off a store road is not keeping a girl of nineteen with a bar on her shoulder. There is no arrangement on this ground by which a loaf on a sill holds anybody, and if it did hold anybody it would have held the four at the lime cottage too.

## The Part That Put Her Out

And here is what she could not put anywhere, and she has been carrying it since the eleventh and it is the reason she did not sleep on the twelfth.

She has been counting since she was a child. She has four questions standing in a book nine hundred years deep that she cannot close, and four days of stone in the last four, and a body that has been lighter every single day since the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,544, and she has not got anywhere by counting.

And then a loaf comes over a sill with no string on it and her whole method has nothing to say about it.

It is not a keeping. It is not a debt. It is not wages. It is not a rate said out loud by a woman behind a counter to a girl and to a man at the end of the counter and to nobody in particular, which is what a rate is and which she has been able to stand in front of for two years.

It is just a loaf.

And she had two loaves on the twelfth, one from the seventh hour of the fourth and one off a sill in the dark, and the second one was the best thing she has eaten in two months, and she ate a quarter of it and gave the rest of it away to a dog at a mill who had not been given anything by anybody in nine years.

## What She Decided Not To Do About It

She decided two things on the twelfth of the month beginning 1,694, sitting on the end of a bar in the dark, and she wrote them in the dust so they would be there in the morning.

She is not going to say thank you for it, because a thank you is a debt you hand to somebody else, and she has not asked that woman for anything and is not going to start by taking something and then telling the giver what it was worth.

And she is not going to work out what the woman knows.

That is the second one and it is the hard one, and she wrote it down in nine words and then sat looking at it.

She knows what the lime cottage was. She knew a boy counted loads out loud ten years back. She put a loaf over a sill in the dark so a girl going past at the second hour would find it.

And she has never once said how she knew, and she is not going to be asked twice by a girl who cannot ask once, and she is not going to go down that lane.

## The Four Miles She Walked Back Twice

She walked the four miles east twice in three days and neither time was for the face.

The first was on the tenth, with a loaf in her bag, and she walked past forty feet of lane and did not turn in.

The second was on the twelfth, at the sixth hour, with nothing in the bag at all, and she walked past it again and stopped at ten feet and stood for four minutes and then went on, and she wants it on the record that she stopped at ten feet the second time and not at forty, because she was already moving at the rate she moves at a shut door, and a person who is already moving at that rate is not going to turn round twice.

And then she went four miles to the face and did eleven barrows with an arm that was shut and a count of four that went to five inside a loaded reach on the seventh trip and came back in four on the eighth.

She got up off the bar in the dark, picked up nine feet nine of it, and walked four miles east to the kerb at the turn, and she slept on the stones with her bag under her head, and she had ninety-seven days.
