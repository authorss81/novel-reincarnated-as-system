# What She Puts Down For Him

She stood four feet into an opening on the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,784 at the ninth hour with a bar on her shoulder and nothing in her hands, and she worked out by the second hour of the seventeenth what she was going to do about it, and it took her two days to be sure of it and two days is a long time when there are three days left.

## What She Had

Here is all of what she carried into that opening, and she laid it out on the flat ground at the fourth foot of it where a man of nine hundred years could not reach it, and she counted it, because she counts.

A bar nine feet nine. A skin. A bag with a dish and a lump of clay and a ring a finger’s width in it and a stub of a nib and a cake of ink she has not used in two months. Nine coppers she cannot count.

Two things against her ribs. A sheet of paper she opened with her own two hands four years ago and has never once called anything. And a book of eight leaves made out of a blanket with a date and a place on the first leaf, four sentences on the second that she has read out loud once and will not read again, and six leaves that are hers and that she has decided twice already not to write in.

And four questions.

That is the inventory. She is nineteen years old and she walked four hundred miles in a year with all of it and there is not one object in it that a man of nine hundred years would have to look at twice.

## What She Wants To Hand Over

A man of nine hundred years gave her four sentences in the month beginning 1,694 about a house four miles west of a turn with a standing stone in the yard, a boy who counted loads out loud, and a carter with a board.

He took them and did not write them down and did not put them down.

He has never told her what that is for and she has never asked him and she worked out in ninety seconds standing at a bar on two posts that a keeper takes a thing and does not let it go stale, and that what she handed him would be true on the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784 whether or not it was true on any day in between.

She has been carrying that for a hundred and twenty-eight days and it is the most useful thing anybody has ever handed her.

And she has been waiting, without knowing she was waiting, for something to put in front of him in the same shape, and on the seventeenth of the month beginning 1,784 at the fifth hour she went a hundred yards east and stood under a sill.

## The Sentence She Gave Him

Here is all of it and it is one sentence and she said it out loud, on her own side, with a boy of fifteen four feet off behind her and a man of nine hundred years on the other side of a plank table.

“There is a window a hundred yards east of your wall and it has one pane in it and there is a room behind it with a bench in it, and I have not been inside and I am not going to, and I am telling you because I found it and I cannot keep what I find.”

He did not write it down.

He did not take his hands off the plank and he did not put his hands on the plank either, and he looked at her for about a minute, and then he said: “I know the one you mean.”

And that was all of it, and she stood there afterwards and worked out that he had just told her the window is worth telling somebody about, which is more than that book has ever given her about anything, and it cost her nothing, and it was the last thing on this ground she would have been able to give away.

## What It Cost

The sixth stage, which is that a finding is not a keeping and that holding is somebody else’s work.

She gave away the only thing she had found east of a wall in eight years. She gave it away on purpose, in daylight, to a man who cannot be thanked and has never once asked her for anything, and she had been carrying it since the ninth of the month beginning 1,784 and it was already going out of her, and now it was gone by choice.

She did not regret it for a minute and that is the part she will have to carry.

A woman of fifty handed her a place at the end of a lane and did not ask for anything and that is the only kind of gift she has ever had. A man of nine hundred years gave her four sentences. She has just given him one, and it is the first thing she has ever handed to anybody that she did not have to say thank you for afterwards, because there was nothing to pay and she is not going to pay.

## The Boy Of Fifteen And Why He Is There

She asked him for it on the sixteenth, at the gate, before she had decided anything else, and he came without asking her one question about it.

That is what he has done for five months. She has said the same three sentences at that gate perhaps forty times and he has come every time and has not once asked her why, and she worked out at the fourth hour of the seventeenth, standing in the opening with him four feet off behind her, why she keeps asking him.

It is not the walking. Nine miles of strip road and eleven of made road is nothing to a boy of fifteen and he has done it fifty times.

It is that he is the only person on this ground who has ever seen her ask for something and come back with nothing and not treat her differently the next morning.

A carter at four hundred yards saw a girl say two words at a wall on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,724 and he did not hear them and will be on that road again and will not know. A woman of fifty saw a girl take a place she did not ask for. A man of seventy saw a girl ask him a question she had already worked out the answer to, on a bench, and she will never do that twice.

The boy of fifteen has seen her stand at a post and say nothing out loud in front of him, on the eleventh of the month beginning 1,754, with four things ready and none of them asked.

That is all of what he is for and he does not know it and she has never once told him.

## The Lever She Did Not Pull

She wrote it in the dust on the flat ground at the fourth foot of the opening on the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,784, at the sixth hour, the way she writes it every day, and then she sat there and looked at it for about a quarter of an hour.

Asking twice.

That is all it has ever been. One asking put into the air twice, so that both go in and neither gets answered, and the first question gets answered sooner or it never gets answered at all.

She found it on the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,634 and she has carried it for a hundred and forty-eight days, on purpose, every day, including on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,724 in the best hour of her life with a name in the air and a man nine hundred years in front of her.

It would work. That is the honest part and she has never once pretended otherwise to herself.

Three days from the closing, with the first question standing a hundred and seventy-four days, it is the only instrument in the world that would make him get to it sooner.

And it is the only instrument in the world that would make the answer a way of getting rid of her rather than an answer.

She rubbed it out of the dust with her hand and she did not write it again.

He watches her do it. He is on the other side of a plank table and he does not have to ask what she is rubbing out, and he said one thing.

“I would not have stopped you.”

“I know that.”

## The Three Days She Stood There

She stood in that opening at the seventh hour on the sixteenth, the seventeenth, the eighteenth and the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,784, four feet in, on her own side, in daylight, with a bar on her shoulder.

Twenty-one feet of it in three days. That is all there is to say of what she did with the time and she did it standing up, because there is nothing between the gate and the wall to lie down on and the opening has a kerb on the near side that she has used twice.

On the sixteenth she did the arithmetic of what she had and it came out at nothing.

On the seventeenth she went and gave him the window.

On the eighteenth she wrote the lever in the dust and rubbed it out and did not write it again.

And on the nineteenth, at the seventh hour, she is going to ask him the first thing she ever asked.

There is no page of that book read out loud. There will not be one. She does not know how much of it there is and she is not going to find out by having somebody read it at her, and she has never once in nine years wanted that, and there is not one hour of this in which to want it.

## What She Is Going To Ask

One at a time. At the wall. In daylight.

Forty-four miles and two days a question, and he gave her those three terms at the seventh hour of the month beginning 1,604 and she has not broken one of them since and she is not going to break one of them on the last day.

She is going to ask him the question she asked first.

She has four and three of them are unanswered and one of them has an answer in it that is a day and not a fact, and the first one is the one that is holding the other three shut, and she has been walking toward it for one hundred and seventy-four days without knowing that was what she was doing.

She is not going to ask it about a man who counts loads out loud.

She is going to ask it because it is owed and because the terms are the terms, and she is keeping them the way he has kept the other half of them for nine hundred years, which is without being asked.

She does not know what he will say. She is going to walk four feet into an opening on the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784 at the seventh hour with a bar on her shoulder and a boy of fifteen behind her and ask it in daylight, and it will go into a book that shuts at the end of that day.

That is all of it. She has three days and nothing to hand him that is not a question, and she is going anyway.
