# Forty Feet Of Kerb

She finished a road gang on the second of the month beginning 1,634 by putting down the last of forty feet of dressed kerb with her own two hands, and she was the strongest person on it, and there was no more road within nine days of her that anybody was repairing.

## The First Of A Month

She went out at the second hour because the gang does the loading at the second hour and the kerb at the fourth.

Nine men and a foreman and ten people on a mile of strip road. Thirty-one yards of bed still to pack, and forty feet of dressed kerb to set along the north side where the thaw took it out.

Forty feet of dressed kerb is nine stones. Each one is two hundredweight and a bit and you cannot get your arms round one, which is why they came off the cart on a bar with four people on it or a bar with two and a roller.

She went on the light end of every stone for two hours and she did the packing and she did thirty-one yards of bed with a bar and a shovel and she was faster than she had ever been on that mile.

Her arm opened at the fourth hour and shut at the seventh. She went through the fourth count inside a loaded reach eleven times on the first day and it came back in four ten of them.

## The Thing She Had Started Doing On The Twenty-Sixth

She took the heavy end.

Not on the first day. On the second, at the fourth hour, when the foreman put the bar on the stone she put her right hand over the top of it and her left under the near end and took the weight up off the ground before the man on the other end had got his hands on it.

The foreman looked at that for four seconds and then he put his own hands on and they set it.

Nobody on that mile said one word about it. They have not said one word about what she carries on the other days either, and after two hours of it on the second they stopped stepping round to the light end out of her way.

Here is what happened to her, and she is putting it down because it is the only thing in nine volumes that has gone the other way.

She has been lighter every day since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 and something came out of her on the eighth of the month beginning 1,604 and something came out of her again on the twenty-sixth, and on the second of a month that is going she was quicker than she has been since she was eighteen years old.

A body past the ceiling of a realm that nobody holds the top of is not the same thing as a body that can carry.

## Nine Stones In Two Hours

She wrote it out on the flat at the ninth hour of the second of the month beginning 1,634, sitting on the flat stone with the book nine inches off her left knee, because she counts things and because this was a thing that needed a number in it.

Nine stones on the second of a month, each two hundredweight and a bit, on the heavy end, with a man taking the far side.

Thirty-one yards of bed packed and metalled. Two hundred yards of shovel work. Nine times through the fourth count inside a loaded reach, four of them on the heavy end.

There is no board on nine days of road with a figure against her name for any of that and there never has been and the foreman marked forty feet at dusk and said the number out loud and went home.

## The Ninth Hour Of The Second

The ninth stone went in at the seventh hour and the foreman came up with his cord and his peg and he did what he does at dusk on every day he has worked this mile.

He stretched the cord from the peg at the head of the new run to the one at the foot and he got it out to the length he wanted and he drove the peg and he marked off against the stone.

“Forty,” the foreman said.

Nine men on a mile of strip road, nine stones set, and the longest straight run of dressed kerb on nine days of road, and the man who measured it stood there with the cord in his hands and said one number.

She has been on a road gang for four days and everything it has taught her is that a thing you can do and a thing that gets marked off are two different things, and that only one of them is done out loud.

## What She Felt About It

She was angry for most of that evening and she let herself be, because there was nowhere to be angry that was not useful.

She was not angry at the foreman. He counted nine and he marked forty feet and both of those were right and both of those had been right every day of a year before she came to that mile.

She was angry at the arrangement, and there is no arrangement on this ground to be angry at that is bigger than a finger and a cord, and she has been angry at bigger things for eight years and none of them ever came out.

So she went and did the stupid thing, which is what she does.

## The Stupid Thing

She went back out at the ninth hour, alone, on a mile where there was nothing left to do, and she got a bar under the ninth stone and she had it up off the bed and she had it turned and she walked it eleven feet and set it down.

Two hundredweight and a bit on one arm and a bar, on loose metalling, in the dark, by herself.

Her fourth count went for four on the sixth step and it did not come back, and it did not come back at nine, and she was on her knees in the stone dust with two hundredweight of dressed kerb coming down on her left forearm and her right arm open and nothing anywhere on nine days of road.

It came back in twenty-two.

She lay on the stone with the kerb off her arm and her hands open and the sweat going cold on her, and she did the count out loud twice, and then she got the bar under it and brought it up in three goes and stood there with the peg in her other hand.

And the thing she found out lying on the stone is that she would have got up if she had had to, and that she had got up, and that there was not one person within nine days of road who would have known it either way.

## What The Nine Men Did At Dusk

They did not do anything.

That is the part she wants recorded, because she had gone at it all day expecting the last stone of a gang’s road to be a thing the nine of them would mark in some way, and it is not.

The foreman marked off forty feet with a cord and a peg and said the number out loud, and the nine men put their bars down on the cart track and stood about for a minute, and one of them lit a pipe, and then they went east along the strip road to the post at a quarter past the eighth hour and their day was over and there is no arrangement on nine days of road for a gang to say anything about finishing.

None of them said one word to her and none of them said one word at the ninth stone, and none of them looked at the place where she had put it down eleven feet off the line.

That is nine men and it is the whole of what she got out of four days of being the strongest body on a mile of strip road.

## What There Is Left To Do

She asked him at the eleventh hour, on the flat, with the cord still round his waist.

“Is there more road past this mile.”

“No,” the foreman said.

“Past the turn.”

“There is no gang past the turn,” the foreman said. “There has not been one on this stretch since before I had the cord.”

“Then where.”

He looked at her and he was not being kind and he was not being unkind either, and he gave her everything he had, which was very little.

“The store road has a lane off it four miles west of the turn,” the foreman said, “and there is stone coming out of a ground at the head of it all summer. That is the only thing I have heard of in nine days. I have not worked it and I do not know anybody who has.”

## The Reversal

Here is what it is and she worked it out on the flat at the second hour of the third of the month with the bar across her knees.

There are nine men on nine days of road who could not lift one of those nine stones on the heavy end, and one person who can lift all nine, and a foreman with a finger who cannot say she was there.

She is the strongest body on nine days of road. There is no work on nine days of road for the strongest body on nine days of road, because a gang that has forty feet of kerb left in it is a gang that is finished.

And the gang that would have fed her is a gang that cannot count her anyway, so she did not lose the gang’s bread tonight and she did not have it either.

She got up at the second hour of the third of a month and she did not go to the mile. She went east nine miles of strip road and then west four miles of store road, to the head of a lane, to ask a man for work that is measured by the weight and the length.
