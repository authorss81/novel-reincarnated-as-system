# What He Took And Kept

She left the post at the second hour of the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 1,694 with a bar on her shoulder and nothing in the bag, and she was at the wall at the seventh hour of the twenty-seventh, and she stood in the opening for two hours and said four sentences and not one of them was a question.

## Twenty-Two Miles With Nothing In The Bag

The strip road is nine miles and it took her from the second hour to the ninth because her right arm went open on the third mile and stayed open for the whole of the rest of it, and there is nothing between the gate and the wall to lie down on.

She lay down in the broken ground north of the made road at the third hour of the twenty-seventh and slept for eleven minutes, and she woke up with her hand in the dirt and did the count, and it came back in four.

She was lighter than she had been on the twenty-fifth. She has been lighter every single day since the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,544 and there was nothing in the bag to stop it and no wages to stop it with, and it is seventeen days since a loaf came over a sill forty feet off a lane.

She was not hungry. She has not been hungry since that eighteenth and she has worked out that that is finished.

## What She Said

She stopped four feet into the opening at the west jamb, on dressed stone, with the metalled road at her back, and the plank table in front of her with nothing on it, and a man on his own side of it.

She had come twenty-two miles to give him four sentences and she had them ready before the gate.

“There is a house four miles west of a turn with a standing stone in the yard and the door shut on a bar. A woman at the end of a lane told me there was a boy at it ten years back who counted the loads out loud so the men could hear him do it.”

“A man at a mill one mile beyond it says a carter came down the road with a board when the lime stopped and put four on it and took them east, and the carter came back without them and was not back for eleven days.”

“He says the boy went with them. He says he walked behind the last barrow because he would not get up on one.”

That is all of it. She said it at the seventh hour in daylight on her own side with the wind coming off the top of the wall, and she said none of it as a question, and she was watching his hands the whole way through.

## What He Did

He did not write.

That is the first thing, and she would have given four minutes of her life to see him reach for the brush, and his hands stayed on the plank the whole of it and at the end of it they were exactly where they had started.

He did not say it was true and he did not say it was not.

He said three sentences.

“That is a place,” he said.

“Yes.”

“That is not a name.”

“No,” Ning Yan said. “It is not.”

And then he waited, and she understood that he was waiting to be asked the next thing, and that there was no next thing, because she had not come with a next thing and had not come for anything at all, and a man of nine hundred years cannot fill a silence that was not built for him.

## What She Had Brought And Why

It is worth putting down why she chose four sentences, because she chose them on nine miles of strip road and four of made, and she had a longer list and she cut it down.

She did not tell him about the woman of fifty. A woman of fifty has not given her permission to be put in a book nine hundred years deep and Ning Yan did not ask and would not have taken it.

She did not tell him about the standing stone being only a stone, because that is her finding and he has never once asked her for a finding.

She did not tell him that she walked past the door.

What she told him was the place, the stone, the bar, the boy, the year, the carter, the board, and the walking behind the last barrow because he would not get up on one.

That last clause was not asked for. She put it in because she had carried it a mile and a half and because a sentence about a boy that does not say what he did is not worth the forty-four miles it cost.

## What He Worked Out There

She worked it out standing on dressed stone at the eighth hour of the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,694, with her arms down at her sides, and it took her a few seconds, and it is the only thing anybody has ever told her that she did not already know how to count.

He is a man who takes a thing that is offered and does not put it down.

She had assumed, for eight years and for ninety-three days, that giving and asking were two ends of one thing. She had come twenty-two miles with four sentences to put something into that book, and she had worked out on the made road at the ninth mile that it was free, and it is not free, and it never was.

A man who keeps things faithfully does not let them go stale.

That is not a virtue. That is what keeping is. A name that was true in the ninth year back is true in the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784, because in a book that deep nobody has ever come to take one out and say this is out of date now.

And she handed him a place, on purpose, in daylight, knowing the terms, and the terms say nothing about giving because the terms were made by a man who only ever expected one kind of person to come to a wall.

## The Part She Was Not Ready For

The cottage was twenty-three days old when she gave it to him.

The last day the miller described was nine years old. The carter and the board were nine years old. The boy who counted loads was ten years old, or near it, and may be somewhere now, and there is not one figure in any of it that has a date on it.

And she put four sentences about ten years ago into the keeping of a man who does not let a thing go stale, and he did not put them down, and he will not, and on the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784 that will be a true thing in a book nine hundred years deep about a place where a boy was once.

She wanted to take it back.

She stood there and wanted it back with everything she has, the way a person wants back a thing they have said, and there is no door on this ground that a keeper opens from his side.

And then she worked out the second half of it, standing on dressed stone with the wind off the top of the wall, and it took four seconds and it is worse than the first half.

He will not put it down. But he also cannot put it in.

She watched his hands for an hour and twenty minutes before she said one word, and his hands did not move, and the brush was in a bag eleven feet behind him, and he did not turn round and get it.

So the place is not in the book.

It is in him. That is a different instrument and she does not know a single thing about how it works, and she has just handed four sentences about a boy she cannot ask after to a man she cannot ask anything, and there is no term anywhere on this ground that covers what she has done.

## The Two Rules She Broke And The One She Did Not

There are three terms. One at a time. At the wall. In daylight.

She broke one of them. She said a thing at the wall that was not a question, in daylight, on her own side, and he received it, and there is no term that says she may not.

She kept the other two exactly, and she kept them because she chose them, and she has been keeping them for ninety-three days out of a habit and not out of a rule and this is the first time that has been worth anything.

One at a time. She said one thing. Not two. She had four questions standing and she did not put one of them in on the back of four sentences about a boy, and it took her four seconds of will at the west jamb and she will be able to do it again.

At the wall. She was at the wall.

In daylight. It was the seventh hour and the sun was off the top of the wall.

She has been so careful with those three for ninety-three days that she had stopped believing they were hers, and it turns out they are.

## What She Did Instead

She said thank you for it and she meant it, and she has not said thank you to a person on this ground in eight years.

She has not said it to the woman at the end of a lane. She has not said it to the woman on the bench. She said it here on dressed stone at the eighth hour of the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,694, to a man of nine hundred years standing on his own side of a plank table with his hands where they were.

It bought nothing and she did not expect it to buy anything.

Then she went west, and her arm was still open on the strip road at the sixth hour of the twenty-eighth, and she was lighter again at the ninth hour than she had been at the sixth, and she walked the whole nine miles working on one thing.

She has given him a place. That is the first thing she has ever put into him on purpose that was not a question, and she cannot take it out, and she is going to have to work out before the twenty-fifth of a month beginning 1,724 what it means to hand a man who keeps things a name.
