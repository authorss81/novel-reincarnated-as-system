# Five Days of Margin

The channel went thin the way late winter goes thin, still covering and less warm.

Ning Yan felt it in the mornings first, the way you feel a blanket gone thin, reaching past her skin for breath and finding less. Yuan spoke little and plain, present as a weight with no hands, and what he spoke was short. Ordinary use, week on week, stamp days and tower readings and carrying loads in thaw mud. The spike at the gate weeks ago had cost months. What was left was weeks, and weeks had gone to days while she carried.

She did the arithmetic on the mat at night against the calendar, the way she does tallies, without flinching.

About five days of ordinary margin. Past nine-tenths and holding. Without a listed kill he ends inside the next chapter. With nothing else to spend, because Ledger is refused permanently and he orders himself nothing and there is no trial in this volume to clear.

She did not write the number. Numbers in ink travel upward. She held it the way she holds the tablet, against the skin.

“You count it,” said Yuan. “You see me thin and you do not look away.”

“I count it,” said Ning Yan. “Five days at this rate. I work still. You rest and you watch.”

“I rest,” said Yuan. “I watch with you and I touch nothing.”

She worked still. Stone for the wall in the morning, water at the seventh hour, paper after the fifth. The loads sat heavier in thaw mud that took the sole and gave nothing back. Breath pulled short at two counts. Her arm served eleven minutes and went, a third and no more. She drilled the left-hand sequence once and stopped early to save breath, hook and turn and settle, unwritten, hers.

Du Hong watched her stop early from the tower door with soup in his hands.

“You save breath,” he said. “Since when.”

“Since the calendar,” said Ning Yan. She did not tell him the number. Telling a man the cost of a thing is not the same as taking the thing away from him, and she knew which one she was holding. “Walk entered. Walk with the cart. No upper lane after dark.”

“The soup cools fastest there,” said Du Hong, and grinned, and walked the lower lane that night with the cart, entered, because he had heard the asking as an order and answered it with the whole of himself.

She attended the stamp day with him at the tenth hour, stood through the reading, signed, took the mark. The filing copy thickened. The buyer paid at three desks. The younger man watched the tower door. The counting room stacked paper by household.

In the evening she sat in the loft alone with the tower volumes below and the tax sequence in her own body, and the safe-side sentence ticking behind her eyes, and one word with nobody to hold it, and five days of margin.

She did not ask to be forgiven. She did not forgive anybody. The middle of the pursuit held, grinding, claustrophobic, a room getting smaller every week: the terrace, the quarters, the furnace floor, the tower loft, a counting room she would never enter.

“You fear,” said Yuan. “You want more time and you have days.”

“I fear,” said Ning Yan. “I keep the hours. You stay.”

“I stay,” said Yuan. “I am with you through the count.”

She lay on the mat with the shutter tied still and the square noise thin through the gap. Below, Feng coughed and copied, column at five, four copies walled, none upward. On the square the desks closed. In the lane the broker stacked paper, and the employed man reported, and feet waited behind coin for a body to walk unentered.

Five days. That was the whole of what was left between ordinary use and dry, and she had spent the day keeping county hours instead of spending him, which was the only mercy she had left to give.
