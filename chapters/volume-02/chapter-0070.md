# The Refusal Filed

There was running water on the floor of the Assembly Hall of Qinghe at the ninth hour of the morning of the twentieth day, and it came in at the open west door where the roof came off and it went across two hundred years of stone floor and found the low corner at the north end and stood there.

Nobody swept it. Nobody swept it for a week. There was a bucket under it by the sixteenth day and there was not a bucket under it on the twentieth, and the water stood in the corner of a building with no roof and made the shape of a small dark pond about a foot across, and every clerk who came up the landing had to walk round it.

She came up the landing at the ninth hour with four documents and stood at the end of the line.

There were nine of them.

The filing desk at Qinghe is a folding table on the landing at the north end, because the Hall has no roof and the landing does, and it is open two days in the month, and on those two days a clerk comes up from the county office at Lowwater to receive the filings into the county queue and takes them down the eleven days of road at the end of it.

The clerk at the table was a woman of about fifty with grey in her hair and the particular stillness of a person who reads all day. Behind her, against the wall, there was a second table and a bound book on it and the book had a queue in it.

The nine of them were people.

That was the part she had not prepared for. She had prepared for a clerk and a book and a queue in the sense of a place where a name waited. She had not prepared for nine actual bodies sitting on a landing in the weather, four of them servants, two of them inner rank, one of them from another county with a bad cough and a sealed packet, and two of them the kind of older person who has been somewhere before and has brought a chair.

The servant in front of her had a stage four and a schedule that was going to be certified in the spring and she had been to that office twice before. The one after her had a stage two and was crying silently at the left eye. The one from the other county had a chest like a barrel and had walked eleven days.

They had all of them sat down at the seventh hour on a landing in the weather because the landing has a roof over part of it and somebody in the county office had decided two days in the month was enough.

Two of them looked up at her when she came to the end of the line.

One of them looked at her for about three seconds and then at the iron token she had taken out of her coat and not put back, and then at her face, and then away.

The servant in front of her had noticed nothing at all, which she had earned four months ago and had not known she had earned.

## The Man From the County

The clerk at the table did not say anything to anybody until she had put the four documents down.

“Clause Four,” she said. “Read it, because I read it to every applicant and I am not going to start making exceptions on a landing in the weather.”

There was no expression on her face at all. There was not one on the landing either. She had the book open and she had it propped on the wall, and she read in the voice of a woman reading out the price of lamp oil.

*A servant-born who has broken in the open, upon the hearing of the bound and in the presence of the Hall, may draw sect qi, may take paid work outside the crew, and may be taught past her stage — only under licence, countersigned at the county. The licence is of three parts, and no part is the licence. Sponsors' seal. Quota clearance. Countersignature at the county.*

“That is the clause,” she said. “Nine names before yours. Sit down if you have a chair.”

“I have a floor.”

“You have a floor,” she agreed.

## Filing

The service was: name, sect, and one of nine headings. Name. Sect. Schedule attached. Applying under the instrument of the fifth day at the second hour and thirty-one minutes.

“Together or apart,” said the clerk.

“Together.”

She stamped nothing and initialled nothing and put the two sheets on the left with the seven before them. And the two men on the landing behind Ning Yan had seen the seal, because a seal is the one thing on that table any person in a queue can identify from four feet away, and that was the moment her name stopped being a thing she was carrying.

She stood in the queue for an hour and eleven minutes and did not sit down.

## Refused

“Quota clearance,” said the clerk.

“I do not have it.”

“Then I cannot take it past the third part.”

“You can take the application into the queue,” said Ning Yan, “and you can refuse it at the desk, and those are two different acts and I would rather have the second one in writing than be sent away to wait.”

The clerk looked at her for about two seconds.

“You want it refused.”

“Yes,” said Ning Yan. “Not dropped.”

The second woman at the wall wrote something in the queue book and then wrote it again on a slip, and the slip went down the table and got read and came back with a second line on it, and the clerk at the table read that line and then wrote the rest of it herself, slowly, in front of her.

It took her a while.

*Application refused. Quota clearance not produced. The sponsors' seal is of no effect without it and is not opened. A further application may be made upon production of the said clearance. The application is returned entire.*

“Read it back to me.”

The clerk read it back.

“Enter the hour,” said Ning Yan.

“It is entered.”

“Enter the county,” said Ning Yan, “because the county is the office that refused it and I will be carrying it to eleven days of road and I want to know who to carry it to.”

There was about a second and a half of a silence on a landing in the weather.

“The county office at Lowwater,” said the clerk, “by my hand, under the registrar. I will write it. You are the first in two years to ask and you are not the first to want it.”

She wrote it.

## Returned Entire

She came away with three documents instead of four.

The clause of the sponsorship with three names cut into it and a counter of an hour and a date. The certified schedule with her hours on it and two men's hours beside hers, standing against it. And a slip with nine lines on it and an hour and a county and a hand.

Three sponsors' seals, on a piece of paper, visible to nine people in a queue, on a landing, in the weather, at about the eleventh hour of the morning.

And underneath them a sentence that said they were worth nothing.

She stood on the landing with the wet corner of a roofless hall four feet away and made herself read it four more times, because she had been carrying a goal for four days and the goal had just been made into a shape with edges and a face, and there was no version of that as anything else.

She did not tear it.

She wants that written down as well, because it is the most obvious thing in the world to do and she has been asked about it by people who were not there.

She stood on a landing with a thumb on the corner of a piece of paper for a minute and a half, and she did not tear it. A refusal on paper is worth more to her than the application was, and there is no sense in destroying a document that has a county clerk's hand and an hour on it.

## Once

She did not go down the landing and put up a second one.

That was the entire discipline of the twentieth day and it took her about four seconds to decide, standing on the landing, wet to the knee on one side, with nine people behind her who had all now looked at her.

There was a rule, and it was not written anywhere: an application is a thing you do once and then you keep. A second application is a different application, and the first one is then in the file.

A refused application put up in the county queue in the winter was a document about a woman who applied and was refused. It would be in the book at Lowwater and in the book up here, and a clerk would read it again in four years.

Two applications meant two refusals in a book.

She had exactly one. She was going to spend it at an hour of her choosing, in the same week she had the clearance, and not in a week in which she had a dead arm and no certificate.

“It is done, then,” said Yuan, from a very long way off, and sounded tired.

“It is done.”

“You could go back down the landing and ask her whether a refusal can be re-opened.”

“I could go back down the landing and ask her about nineteen things,” said Ning Yan. “And she would answer all of them correctly, and none of them would be the third part.”

## The Log

She wrote the wording in the log at the second hour of the afternoon, in the wash code, in the hooked strokes, in full, with the hour and the place and the nine lines, because she had been refused a thing in writing in a county office and the whole of her life had been spent on the fact that in four years a thing that is in writing is the only thing anybody can be made to look at.

*Refused at the receiving desk of Qinghe for want of quota clearance. The seal is of no effect without it and is not opened. Returned entire.*

*A second application is a second refusal in a book. I file once.*

*Seven hundred and twenty-nine hours. Middle of spring. He did not say please and he did not say no.*

Then she sat with her right arm out of its own weight and looked at the four lines for a while, and the fourth of them was the one she did not write but understood.

*She said the county out loud because I asked her to and nobody had asked her to in two years.*

She put the brush down.

## The Cold End

She went down on the cold end at the ninth hour of the night because the frost had gone out of the slag heaps and there was nine hundred tons of it up there to be worked before the melt, and because the cold end was where she was worth something.

There were four of them up there and not six.

Six had been the strength of that job since the autumn, and it was four on the twentieth day, and the foreman had put four on the board and had known about the four since the sixteenth. The two who were missing from that hole were at the lip, working the channel she was working, taking the ninth hour to the second night hour, on a double, because a roster had two men's marks on it.

Cao Lei had his back to her and had a bar in both hands and was standing about where he stood every night, and Xu An was nine feet along and had a bucket and was counting.

The beam over the cold end was where the beam had been since the autumn. A slag beam is not wood and does not crack like wood, and it is held up by four shoring posts that are inspected at the first hour of the week by a man who has forty-one of them.

She looked up at it because you look up first, at the cold end, on a channel, always. There was a hairline in the near shoring and she had seen it before and had not thought about it, and she thought about it now.

Then she went back to the channel and worked, and held the wall straight for eight hours with the cold stripe growing along her left ribs, and she could not get to the far end of that lip in time if anything came off that beam, and there were two men on it and eleven below her and a foreman who had put four on a board.

The melt was coming down off the slag in a warm rain on the back of her neck.

Nobody shouted. Nobody fell. Nothing happened for about four hours, which was the most anybody in that building was entitled to expect out of the twentieth day of the winter.
