# Stands to Me

The county office read it out to her because a county reads a line out when the line has been entered since the last time she stood in the room.

She had asked for it in the morning, at the third desk first, and been sent to the book.

“A transfer has been entered against your filing,” said the clerk at the side, “in the position of the surety. I will read the position.”

“Read it.”

He read it.

*The surety entered at this office on the day of the filing, under his own name and at his own voice, is dead. The surety is void. The debt stands to Ning Yan, the undersigned.*

“That is what it says.”

“That is what it says.” He turned the book a quarter turn so she could see the line without leaning over it. “It is the same hand as the filing and it is stamped with the filing stamp. It is not a copy. It is in the book.”

“I know. I asked for it to be written that way.”

He looked at her then properly, for the first time that morning.

“You asked for it to be written that way,” he said.

“Yes. Write debt however the office writes debt. I did not want it in the county book in a shape a reader would have to stop and think about.”

He wrote the reading in the ante-room margin where readings go, with the hour on it, and said nothing more about it.

She understood what had happened there. She had handed a clerk a sentence and he had understood all of it, and a clerk who understands a thing at the second hour of the afternoon is a clerk she can work with for the next eleven weeks.

Then she asked the question she had come for, in the plain words, because a plain question gets a plain answer out of a clerk and a shaped one gets a shaped answer.

“What did his being alive let me do.”

The clerk stopped with his hand on the book.

“That is not a thing anybody has asked me.”

“It is a thing I need,” said Ning Yan. “I attended every county hour for four months with a man whose attendance stood beside mine. I want to know what his death has taken off the filing, in terms.”

He thought about it. He was about thirty and he had ink to the wrist and he did not have the look of a man who decides things quickly.

“You had a surety,” he said. “A surety stands in the room. It is not attendance; it is standing. Where the filing requires attendance and the applicant is present, the filing stands. Where the applicant is not present, a surety present in the same hour stands for her until the county says otherwise.”

“That is what it was for.”

“That is what it was for,” he said. “He was not in that room to be your name on a sheet. He was in that room so that a day could be a day. If you are ill, or on a road, or carried, or dead, and a man whose name is in this office stands in the ante-room at the hour with your sheet in his hand, the hour counts.”

“It is the whole instrument. It is why they cost what they cost.”

“It is the whole instrument,” said the clerk.

She put her left hand flat on the table and felt the wood under it and made herself go on.

“And now.”

“Now there is a line in my book that says the surety is dead and the debt stands to you.” He turned the book so the line was under her hand. “So if you are not in this room at the hour, there is nothing else in this room, and the hour is a gap.”

“And a gap in a filing is a break in a filing, and a filing that is broken is a filing that is refused.”

“That is the rule. I am not the registrar and he can read it different on a bad morning.”

“So I cannot be ill.”

“You can be ill inside this room.”

“I cannot be carried.”

“You cannot be carried,” said the clerk, and then, after a moment, in a lower voice, as though it were a thing he had been turning over since the line went into the book: “He told you.”

“He told me. Before he said it.”

“Then he was a fool with good sense in him,” said the clerk, “which is most of the men in this office.”

He wrote the reading down and put the sheet back and did not offer her anything else. She understood there was nothing else there to offer, and that the county had given her nine words of truth for nothing, the way a county does when the truth happens to be its own position.

## What She Asked For

“I want it in writing,” said Ning Yan. “On the filing. That no surety may stand for me again without relicensing.”

“That is a term on the licence, not on the filing.”

“Then put it on the filing as well, because the licence does not exist and the filing does, and I am not going to hold a county up at a table in December and say a word I have not got on paper.”

He looked at the form, and then at her, and then he wrote it in the position under the transfer line where a county writes the things an applicant has asked to have written, with the hour on it.

*At the third hour the applicant requested that no surety be admitted against this filing thereafter, save upon relicensing.*

“Read it back,” said Ning Yan.

He read it back. She signed under it with the bad hand that had a hole in it and the clerk stamped it with the filing stamp, which was a stamp that had now gone onto three pieces of paper with her name on them and none of them about her advancement.

“There is no charge for that,” he said.

“I know there is no charge for that,” said Ning Yan. “That is not why I asked.”

He put the brush in the wash and did not ask her why, and she did not tell him, because a person who has been on the floor of a furnace and has come to a county office alone learns early which questions are worth answering.

## The Road

She walked the road back up to the tower alone, in the market, in daylight, past the three desks.

A man she did not know came along behind her for thirty yards out of nothing more than Lowwater habit, and then stopped in a doorway to light something. She understood the whole of it standing there. A guard is a place. A place on a road with an eight hundred jin claim under it is a line a man can read.

She entered the walking. Both ways. Hours, and the hour it was walked, and the lane it was walked in.

She did the sum on the stones at the bridge, standing at the trestle where Old Gao put his hand on the tailboard. Four days a month to a county office four miles down a road that shuts in the weather, alone, at the second hour, sixteen times, for the rest of the season and probably for the rest of whatever came after the season.

She had done it four times with a man. She was going to do it sixteen times without one, and there was no version of the winter in which a cart came up for her at the second hour, because a cart is a place with a name on it and she had one now, and it was written on a board at the fourth bridge in a captain's own hand.

That night she did the sum on the loft mat with the coat for a blanket and the arm out to the side, and found what a surety had actually been for, which was not what she had paid for.

Du Hong had bought her a hundred and something days of being sick.

That was what his name in the book had been for, in the only sense that mattered. He had stood in daylight before a queue and taken it without being asked, and had told her the cost first so that she could not be the one who did not know, and he had been beaten into thaw water for it at the foot of a tower a month ago.

She could not be late. She could not be three miles down a muddy road at the second hour because the market had held a carrier. She could not be stopped at the bridge with a fever on her, and she could not be carried in by a man in a good coat who wanted a place, and be alive for it the next day.

She had gained a licence to practise and lost the right to fall over in front of a county clerk.

“You carry it alone now,” said Yuan.

“I carried the hours before him alone,” said Ning Yan. “I carried them the first month with nobody standing in the room at all. I know what they are like.”

“They bought you days.”

“They bought me days and I spent them being at the hour,” said Ning Yan. “So they are the same thing. I am a woman who has to be in a room at the hour, and the reason is a dead man in a book, and the book is stamped.”

She did not forgive him and she was not going to. Four months of going down the mountain to Lowwater and up it again had already cost him eleven weeks, and about six of them were left.

“I do not stop,” she said.

“I know you do not stop,” said Yuan. “I hold it here and I do not decide anything about you and I do not get to arrange your days.”
