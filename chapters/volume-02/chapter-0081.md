# The Counting Room's Employee

She wrote the reading down the way she writes everything that matters, in wash code on the back of the docket that holds Clause Four.

The loft shutter did not shut and the night air got in. Feng coughed on the copying floor below, steady through the boards. She sat on the mat with her knees up and the brush in her left hand, and made the hooked strokes that turn a letter into a mark no clerk reads quickly: a broker keeps a counting room, the man in the good coat works for him, the trade is records, the place unbought, the carriers' names unanswered.

She did not write the cost. The channel stood thin at about six and a half weeks, and her foundation held the absence where the spike had gone, and both facts sat in the room without needing ink. Yuan had held Ledger less than a day and put it down the same day and refused it permanently, and the refusal was the shape of the evening.

“You sleep little,” said Yuan. “You eat little and you write late.”

“I write what you bought,” said Ning Yan. “I eat what the loft has. You rest and you watch.”

“I rest,” said Yuan. “I watch with you. I touch nothing.”

In the morning she took the docket to the tower yard and burned nothing, because burning is a statement, and folded it back into the lining. Then she went to the square with a carrier's load on her shoulder to see the desks the man pays.

## Three Desks

The Lowwater square holds the county office on its north side with shutters open and a queue, and three county desks under an awning to the east where clerks copy advancement notices for any hand with coin.

She had seen the man work one of them from the water cart. Now she worked past all three with stone on her back, and heard what a square hears in daylight.

He came at the eleventh hour with his hat in his hands, polite to each clerk in turn, asking which hand keeps the copies of the Qinghe advancement and what carriers carried packets unsigned in eleven weeks. He put coin on each desk and paid for the writing as well as the answer, so no clerk wondered after whether he was paid. He asked for the place where she sleeps the way a man asks after a debt, without heat, and got nothing he could write down.

He is employed. That was the reading's whole gift, plain through her eyes. Not the broker but the broker's man, with a counting room behind him and paper stacked by household. The broker holds paper on three struck households and cites the maintaining members' ledger by name. The man does not threaten and does not whisper. He buys records instead of wordings because wordings go wrong by nightfall in two counties and records sit in files.

She carried stone past his back without breaking stride. He did not look at her. A body is the proof that cannot be bought at a desk, and bodies walk through squares every day.

At midday she wrote a letter in her own unreadable hand and gave it to Old Gao's boy for the carts: copy nothing for him. Four words that Feng already lives by, with the column at five and none upward. The boy took it without asking, because tower letters go free.

“You hate him,” said Yuan. “You want him gone and you hold it.”

“I want my hours,” said Ning Yan. “I want clearance and a stamp. Hate takes hours. Work takes them first.”

## The Book Moves

The household passed four hundred and twelve worked by the week's close. Five hundred and twenty-eight standing. Fifty-eight in her own hand, continuous from the day the book opened.

She rode up with the water cart at dawn on crew days and worked the channel left-handed, breathing two counts, holding the wall straight while the leak ran cold. The shift leader ordered the bench when the breath went, and she took it as tactics. The foreman signed six and posted six, name on the line, timber sound. The clerk wrote her blocks slow, four and a half and four and a half, and read the week back.

On tower days she carried loads. The breath stayed short the way Yuan had said it would, a month of it and then some never leaving. She climbed the tower stair once instead of twice and slept in the loft with the shutter open to the cold, because cold air is easier than close air when the chest pulls short.

Du Hong came to the loft on the fifty-fifth evening with oil on his sleeves and news in his face. He had been noticed for carrying her copies, as she knew, and he carried it the way he carries everything, badly hidden and entirely steady.

“You look thin,” he said.

“I am thin,” said Ning Yan. “The book does not weight for thin. Sit.”

He sat on the boards. “The county asks for a surety on licences like yours. A living name for attendance at every county hour. I asked the clerk what it means.”

“What does it mean.”

“It means my name in the county book for your attendance. If I die it voids and the debt comes to you.” He said it plainly, the way he says things he has decided. “I am telling you because you do not take names without telling what they cost.”

She looked at him for a long time. He was eighteen and ambitious and bad at lying, and his file carried her hand twice, and he was the cheap door into her and had said so before she did.

“I have not asked you,” she said.

“You have not,” said Du Hong. “I am telling you what the county wants before you carry clearance into it. That is all.”

“Then I know it,” said Ning Yan. “When the hour comes, you decide it then.”

He nodded and went down the stair. She lay on the mat with the arithmetic for company and the thin channel quiet in her head.

“You keep him out of it,” said Yuan. “You want him safe and you cannot make him so.”

“I keep nothing,” said Ning Yan. “I tell him true and he decides. That is the whole of what I have.”

“It is much,” said Yuan. “I see it and I hold it with you.”

Four hundred and twelve. Five hundred and twenty-eight. The man paid at three desks for records. The broker counted behind him. She slept with the docket in her lining and the thin weeks ticking, and the work went on.
