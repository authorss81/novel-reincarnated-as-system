# The Bounty Man

The thaw started below the terrace at about the second hour of the eighteenth day and she saw it from the ninth step before she ever went down.

The eaves dripped. That was the first thing and it was such an ordinary thing that she stood on the step for a moment and could not place it. Eleven days of ash, then a clear sky, then ice on every shaded step, and now the eaves of the wash house were running at four in the afternoon and there was mud in the ditches by the outer wall, in the first running water of the year.

Everything on that mountain was going to thaw at once over the next three weeks and the middle of it was going to be mud and rot and nine days of nobody able to get anywhere.

Below her, the outer yard was full.

It was full the way it is full at the third hour past midday in midwinter, when there is nothing else to do and a furnace is running, and there were about forty of them in it and every one of them knew her name. She had been a number in a column in the autumn and now she was the reason the eaves dripped on this particular mountain.

## The Gate

The Qinghe gate is at the bottom of the nine steps and it is a gateway with a yard on both sides of it, and the road comes at it out of a hundred feet of stone.

There were two men on it who had not been on it on the sixth day past, and she had walked past both of them eight times in a fortnight and had learned the shape of one — a young man with a good coat and a leather tube on his belt, who had nothing to carry and had been on a cold gate for a month and had not once been sent for anything.

The other one was standing at the gate clerk's table with his hat in his hands.

He was about fifty. Grey at the temple, not thin, a coat that had been good in Hanhui eleven days of road ago and was good still. He was not a servant and he was not a sect man and he was not selling anything. He had arrived in a cart with three others and had paid at the outer gate without haggling, which was how you tell.

She came down the nine steps at the third hour past midday with about forty people in the yard and the intention of looking at nobody.

She got as far as the sixth step before she understood that she was not going to manage it, because the gate clerk had looked up, and because the man at the table had also looked up, and because those two things happening in the same half second was the most interesting thing that had occurred in that yard in a fortnight.

## The Asking

The clerk was about twenty-two and had a blotter and a bell and the expression of a man who has been posted to a gate for eleven weeks.

“Sir,” said the man. “I will not take much of your morning.”

“State it.”

“I am looking for a person,” said the man. “Not a person I am looking for. A person I am asking after, and I am asking after them politely and I am asking after them in daylight, in front of these gentlemen, because I would rather a hundred people hear me say it than have it said afterwards that I whispered it.”

The yard did what a yard does.

“You will forgive me,” he said, “if I do not use the word. It is a word in a sentence that came out of the machinery four counties over, and every man in this yard has heard it in his own ears and half of them have it wrong, and I have no wish to add a fifth version.”

Forty people.

“I want to know two things,” he said. “The first is the place where that person sleeps. Any of them. The city bunks, the outer terrace, the inn below the weigh-house, a room off this court. I do not want to search it. I want to know where it is, because a person who has somewhere to sleep is a person, and a person who does not is a thing on a road.”

Nobody in that yard moved.

“And the second,” he said, “is the name on the carriers' papers. That is a smaller thing. Any cart that has gone up or down that road in eleven weeks with a packet somebody did not sign for. Any carrier who has been paid to take a paper and not to read it. I will pay for both and I am telling you that I am paying so that you will not have to guess whether you are.”

He put a coin on the table. Not a lot. Enough to be serious and not enough to be a scandal.

The clerk looked at the coin for a long time.

“This is not a county matter,” he said.

“It is not,” said the man. “That is why I am asking you and not a county clerk. I would like to write down that you were asked and you said you did not know, and I would like to write down both, and I will pay for the writing.”

“Wait there.”

He wrote. It took him the time it takes to write three lines and it took the yard about forty seconds to say nothing at all.

She stood on the sixth step of the Nine Steps Terrace with a dead right arm inside her coat and forty people in a yard below her and a coin on a gate table, and she watched a man ask for where she slept and the name on her papers, out loud, in daylight, in front of forty witnesses, in a tone of voice like a man buying lamp oil.

Two of three.

There was no third thing he had asked for and she understood that he was not going to ask for it out loud, because the third thing was the whole of the prize and the whole of the prize does not get said in a yard with forty witnesses in it. The third thing was a body, and it walked about inside the wall, and it had just come down nine steps in front of him and stopped.

## The File

He thanked the clerk. He put his hat on. He turned round to go up into the yard because the road to the square was through it and everybody knew it, and he came ten feet and stopped.

He did not look at her.

He stood ten feet away with his hat on and looked out through the gate at the road, and she had four seconds in which to decide whether to look at his face, and she made all of them, and she used three of them doing it.

He was not young. He was not frightened. He had the face of a man who has been doing a thing for a long time and does it the same way every time, and he had the specific tiredness at the corner of the mouth of somebody who has been spending other people's money for a month and would like to stop and cannot.

Then he went up into the yard and down through it and out at the far gate, and nobody stopped him, and forty people watched a stranger walk through a sect's yard and out the other side of it.

She went back up four steps.

## The Thing That Is Legible

“Read him,” said Ning Yan.

She said it out loud on the fourth step of the Nine Steps Terrace with forty people in the yard below and the eaves dripping and her hand on a cold rail.

“I cannot,” said Yuan.

“Try.”

“No,” he said. “Not because I will not. I cannot. I read you and I read nothing else on this mountain since the sixth day of the winter, and the thing I traded for that is the ability to see through anything you are not wearing. He is a man in a yard nine steps below you and I cannot see one line of him.”

He said it flat, and he did not soften it, and he did not apologise for it either, which was the correct way to say it and which she noticed.

“Then say what it costs,” said Ning Yan.

And he told her.

Not quickly. He told her the way he told her everything, in order, with the numbers in the right places, and she listened to the whole of it standing on a step in the drip.

“To reach past your skin I take something back. It has a name and I have not used it, and the name is not the point. The point is that when I took it the last time it went up hard and it took four and a half weeks of ordinary use with it, and it will do that again, and there is no version of taking it that does not.”

“You have used four and a half weeks of it before.”

“I have spent it twice and never told you and that was on purpose and I would have gone on not telling you.”

“How long have you got.”

“About four months,” said Yuan. “And the spike takes a half of it, and it does not come back, because it does not come back out of me. It comes back out of you. That is where I get it. I have never told you that either and you have never asked.”

She stood on the fourth step with the rain coming off the eaves.

“Say the last part again.”

“The spike is paid out of your foundation,” he said. “Not out of mine. Not out of the weather.”

“Every time you spend something like that it comes out of the thing that is holding you together, and the thing holding you together is the only thing I have ever had a way of getting at. That is the deal. It was the deal on the first night and you have been paying it for four months and there is a clause in it you did not know about.”

The drip went on at the edge of the eaves.

“Four and a half weeks out of four months.”

“Yes.”

“That is a third of what you have.”

“About a third of what I have, for one morning, on a man in a yard,” said Yuan. “And I have been telling you all of this in a yard because you told me to, and I want you to know that I told you the price before you gave the word, because of a rule you made me in the middle of the winter, and I am not going to be the one who decides this one.”

## What She Said

She did not say it then, and she did not say it on the step above the yard, and there is an hour in the record that agrees with her on both counts.

There was a rule and she had made it on the ninth step of the terrace on the ninth day of the ash in the voice she used for tallies: nothing decided about her again without telling her first. He had told her. He had told her first, in order, with the number in it, on a step, in the drip, and he had then stopped and waited.

She stood there for a long time.

Twenty-three days of thaw. Two men on a settling floor. A paper in a clerk's tray with a fair round hand at the top of it. A refused application in a coat lining. And a man in a yard who had two of three and who had said in front of forty people that he was not here to search anything, and would walk up and down this mountain asking politely, at a fair rate, until somebody told him.

“Tell me the other thing,” she said. “The one you have been not-saying since the fourth hour past midday when I wrote four lines in a docket.”

He was quiet while she waited.

“A claim in the bound is entered by the layer,” he said. “Every one. That is what an entered act is; it is not a rumour and it is not a rumour's echo, it is a line the layer put in four counties with the hour on it, and it is against something, because the layer does not enter an act against nothing.”

“Against me.”

“Against you. That is what it is about.”

“And a man who takes up an entered act.”

“Is inside it,” he said. “For as long as he takes it up. There is no clean way to work one from outside and everybody who has ever tried has found that out. He is inside a thing you are inside of, and the layer keeps the inside of an entered act the way it keeps the inside of anything a Chosen One touches, which is to say it keeps it, and it does not need your agreement, and it does not ask you.”

She did not say the word. She had the word and she held it in her mouth and she looked at a man walking out of a gate, and she did not say it.

“Say the word,” said Yuan.

“No.”

“It is a word I have never used about a man and I am not going to use it about one now. I am telling you that there is a file with him in it that you cannot read because you cannot read me and I cannot read him. That is the whole of it. That is what I have got for you this afternoon and it is better than what I had at the ninth hour of the fourth day and it is not enough.”

“Then I am not ordering you to take it.”

“I know you are not.”

“If I gave the word you would do it.”

“Yes,” said Yuan. “That is the part that is going to cost you, and you have not given it, and I am not going to pretend that you were ever going to, because you are the only person inside the bound who has ever made me ask first and you have made me ask first six times and this is the seventh.”

She went up the remaining five steps with the drip going off the eaves on her coat.

Down at the gate the clerk was still standing at his table with a coin in front of him and a blotter, and a man in a good coat was walking away down forty feet of churned white at the bottom of a hundred and forty feet of stone road, with the place she slept and the name on her papers not yet bought and a body neither of them had said out loud.

Eight hundred jin. Two of three.

The hunt was on the mountain now, and it had a rhythm to it, and it walked.
