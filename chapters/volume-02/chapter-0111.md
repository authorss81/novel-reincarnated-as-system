# Struck Wherever Copied

The county struck it at the second hour and entered the hour, and she was standing in the ante-room at the time and was not asked to leave and was not asked to stay and was not, at any point, consulted.

The clerk at the side had a thin brass rule laid along the second position of the sheet of her own advancement, and he drew it once, cleanly, and turned the rule over in his hand and set it down at the edge of the table.

The hour went in the margin. Ordinary county hand. The mark of the office on the corner of it, the same mark as on the pending filing.

It is a strange thing to watch a word come out of your own record.

She had thought for eleven weeks that she was carrying a piece of paper that a clerk in a bad hat had written out of a book six hundred years old into the one place in nine counties where it had been set down in a second position where nobody reads, and she had been carrying it the way a woman carries a stone in a shoe: not comfortably, and not without knowing it was there.

Now it was out of the sheet, and out of the tower's four copies, and out of the shelf, and the county sheet of her advancement read exactly like the county sheet of any other advancement in this bound.

Nothing in it was false. That was the part she could not get round. Every mark on that sheet was still true — her county, her stage, her advancement, her hours. And the second position was blank, and it had not been blank at any time before the second hour of this afternoon, and the office that had put a word there had gone on using it in the ordinary course for three months and had now been instructed by somebody outside the bound to take it back out.

“Do you want to see the original,” said the clerk.

“No,” said Ning Yan.

“It is kept. It is not destroyed. It is struck on the copy that goes out and on the copy that stays, and the original is in the press with the county mark on it, and you may see the original if you ask.”

“I am not asking.”

“Most people ask.”

“Most people have not had a thing taken off them by an office that is not this one,” said Ning Yan. “I would like the original left exactly where it is, and I would like the striking entered with the hour on it, which you have done, and I would like to go back to the queue.”

The clerk looked at her for a moment with the brass rule in his hand.

“There is no queue,” he said. “It is the second hour and they have all gone.”

“Then I will stand where I stood.”

She had two arms and one of them did not work, and she had a filing that required her to be in a room at an hour, and she had known since an afternoon in the spring that the sheet would be struck and had done nothing about it, because there was nothing anybody could do about it and she had gone down to the wash house instead.

She stood in the ante-room for the length of it and let the word be gone.

And then, because she was that sort of woman and had been that sort of woman since she was eleven years old, she did the other thing, which was the thing she had actually come for.

“What does the filing need to be countersigned,” said Ning Yan.

The clerk set the brass rule down square with the edge of the table.

“The legs,” he said. “The seal stands on the application and the clearance stands in the quota book and neither has moved. What is missing is the labour. A filing is held open until the applicant produces the schedule the hours are worked on, certified by the office that keeps it, with the season's hours on it to the date you sign. Your hours go on a furnace schedule at Qinghe. The county does not keep furnace schedules. The county reads them when they are certified and sent.”

“Where do I get one certified.”

“The Hall's clerk at Qinghe. It goes up the quarter with your hours on it and the Hall's mark beside them and then it comes down here and goes on your filing, and the registrar has a complete paper in front of him and countersigns it.”

“How long.”

“The season's labour closes at the last week of the thaw and the return goes at the quarter after it, and then the county has it, and then it is a matter of the registrar's hand.”

“Then the registrar's hand is in the middle of the last week of the thaw.”

“The registrar's hand is at the end of it and he does not hurry.”

She had been told that about this office before by a different man in a different book about a different number, and she had thought at the time that it was a piece of craft and now she thought it was a piece of weather.

She wrote it in the docket in the lining, in the wash code, on the back of the line she had written about the two men on the wall.

*Gone from the sheet. Second hour. Entered. Not asked, not left, not consulted.*

*Paper for a licence: schedule, Hall clerk, certified, season's hours, via quarter.*

*Closes last week of thaw.*

And then, because the log was in the lining and the wash code was in the log and the log had been a laundry ledger's back pages for a year and she had been so careful about what went into it for so long that she had forgotten what it was for, she stood in the middle of a county ante-room at the second hour and wrote a line of plain text, without code, at the foot of the docket.

*A word six hundred years old is gone out of the only paper that had it and a man I did not ask is holding what is left of it. I have it in my head. I am not writing it down again.*

Then she put the docket away, because a thing written in plain is a thing that can be read by anyone who ever handles her coat, and there are four hundred people in Lowwater who handle coats.

## Four Copies, Struck

She went up the copying floor before the dark came and looked at them where they stand walled behind the fifth item, and it took her a moment to find the one with the word in it.

There were four county hand-copies and three of them had never carried it, and the fourth was the Lowwater one. It was a fair sheet in a fair hand with a dry line drawn clean through the second position from top to bottom, and no smudge, and a seal under the striking.

The striking looked like nothing. That was the thing about it she had not been ready for. A line through one cell in a second position reads as a clerk tidying a sheet, and there are a hundred thousand sheets in this bound with a line through one cell in them, and not one of them means what this one means.

Six hundred years. Eleven entries. Eleven status words. And now one of them was a fair sheet in a tower with a dry line through it, and the whole of them were still in the volumes on the high shelf, which nobody goes up to, because the request had said *wherever it is copied* and not *wherever it is bound*.

She stood in front of the shelf and worked out whether the volumes counted.

She went up on a stool with the cloth in her hand and looked at the third rank, where the open one sat with the ruled pencil line and nothing in it, and it was not struck, because it was a volume and not a copy, and nobody was going to climb four flights of a tower to find out.

That was the whole of what the door closed on. Not a silence. A shelf.

The square was outside in the rain that had started at noon and gone on all afternoon, the three desks shuttered, the counting lane barred, the tower lamp already going in the middle of the day with the old man coughing on three floors.

She went up the lane and past the two men on the wall, who got up out of the way of the boards she was carrying and said good afternoon, in the way of two men who have been sitting on a wall for six weeks and have made no arrangement with anybody at all.

Four days up and four days down, and she had known since that afternoon at the wash house, and she had been at the wash house for no reason she had given anybody.

She went up the tower and got the coat out of the lining and the filing copy and the docket and the last slate of carriers' sheets for the month, and put the slate down in front of Feng without a word.

“You are going up,” said Feng.

“I am going up.”

“Cart day is in two days. Road is open to carts before the ninth hour and shut by the eleventh and the pass is clear to the fourth bridge now it has not been all winter.” He wrote a line on a small sheet and gave it to her, which was a pass for the water cart, which he could write and she could not, and he did not ask for it back.

“Feng.”

“Do not,” said Feng. “I have been a keeper for twenty-two years and I write a great many things that are not asked for and I do not collect them. Go and get your schedule. And when you come down the road again you will want to be four days inside the quarter, and I will be here, because I am always here, and because there is no version of this tower in which I am not here.”
