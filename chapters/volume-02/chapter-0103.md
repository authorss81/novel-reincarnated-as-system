# Four Causes

Lowwater buries its dead in mud in thaw season, nineteen minutes and no questions, unless somebody stands to give causes.

Ning Yan stood to give them.

She had not been asked to. That was the part she kept turning over on the walk out, with Feng's lamp held high in daylight and the ground half under water: the clerk had read the four terms of a killing and a burial, and read the third of them aloud, and then stopped and waited, the way a man waits when he has learned that the person in front of him will fill the silence and that what she fills it with goes in a book.

They had come out past the counting lane in a cart she had not wanted and could not refuse, because a hand carted in the town's cart is a statement about how much a body weighs, and a tower that put a hand on the shaft would have been read all morning. Two clerks from the copying floor walked at the rear with their hands empty. The girl of twenty-four had sent a slate with a correction on it that she had not been asked to correct, and it lay in the cart on Du Hong's coat.

The burial ground lay past the tower on the town side, thaw water standing in the ruts, sky low and grey. Feng held the lamp though it was daylight, because lamplight is what the tower holds for its own. The clerk stood with his book. Two carriers stood with shovels. The younger bounty man lay buried elsewhere, unclaimed, unmourned, proof failed. Du Hong lay shrouded on the boards between them.

She stood with her left hand on the shroud and her right hung dead, breathing two counts, stage three sitting crooked in her without its order, channel whole at roughly two-thirds where days had stood.

She gave the causes in the tally voice, all of them, where nobody could soften them.

“First,” said Ning Yan. “My hand in his file. Twice. The advancement on my recommendation and the direction never filed. I put it there on the sixth step and on the sponsorship sheet. Any man cashing eight hundred jin opens him first.”

The clerk wrote. Feng held the lamp steady, coughing into his sleeve.

“Second,” said Ning Yan. “My copies in his coat down the road. Four sheets in my own hand with no name on them, sent to the tower weeks ago. He carried them and was noticed for it. A clerk asked what he carries and has a file to keep it in.”

Thaw water ran in the ditch. The counting lane stood barred beyond the roofs. No elder came to the ground. Feet that lose a man know the cheap door is gone and do not stand where it closed.

“Third,” said Ning Yan. “Letting him stand surety. Living name for attendance at every county hour. Death voids and debt transfers. He told me the cost first so I could not say nobody told me. I let him stand in daylight before a queue. I carry what he decided.”

She paused, because the fourth is the one that finishes it, and finishing is the whole of burial.

“Fourth,” said Ning Yan. “Killing one of them when I came down the road. Habit, not technique. One working arm. I stopped the beating and the beating had done its work, and feet that lose a man do not forgive the road. He died of it before the night was out with Feng holding the lamp.”

Four causes. All hers. Her hand in his file. Her copies in his coat down the road. Letting him stand surety. Killing one of them when she came down the road.

The younger man had been two feet from the elder with the file open and reading out of it, and the elder had had Du Hong's arms pinned the whole time he was reading, and she had put her elbow into the throat of the man who had asked for the place, and everything after that was arithmetic performed on a body in a ditch. She said that too, because a clerk who writes four causes and stops where the story gets comfortable is a clerk who has been softened, and she would not be the reason a book was.

“Fifth,” she said. “If the second man had not been holding a file and reading it out loud over a boy in the water, he would not have stood where I could reach him, and I would have got to Du Hong later.”

The clerk wrote that one too. He did not ask her to leave it out, which she had prepared for, and which would have gone in the book as an act of mercy.

The clerk entered them unasked and said he had never once written *fell* in that column. She signed the burial line in her own left hand, the second document she has ever written naming her as the cause of anything, and ended it *and that the undersigned caused all of these things and entered them herself and asks that this entry be read whenever the roster is read.*

She took the transferred debt standing in mud with lamplight on her face. The pending filing stood, capped at five, defect entered, stages recorded. The claim stood, unclaimed, proof failed on the body. The sponsors' writ stayed dead. Settlement stood at roughly two-thirds, about eleven weeks. *Kiln-born* stayed in two heads, no paper. Tower column at five. Feng alive, unaudited, no Lung.

“You bear it,” said Yuan. “You name it and you do not soften it.”

“I bear it,” said Ning Yan. “I do not forgive and I am not forgiven. You stay.”

“I cannot lift it,” said Yuan. “I can be in the room with it, and I am.”

They walked back toward the tower with Feng holding the lamp though daylight held. On the square the three desks ran their trade. The man in the good coat stood at the first desk with his tube closed, paying for nothing, because the cheap door was gone and every hand that had fed her would now be a hand sheltering something the whole bound could name, and bodies cost more than records.

She did not stop at the desks. She had four hours before the schedule and she went up the tower stair and took water for the kitchen instead, because a butt standing by a stair goes bad and the copying floor will blame whoever they can reach.

Feng watched her go and said nothing about it.

At the county office at the second hour she handed the burial line in for the record, and a clerk read it through twice and asked her, in the flat voice of a man doing his duty, whether she was a member of the household.

“I am the undersigned,” said Ning Yan.

“That is not what I asked.”

“No,” said Ning Yan. “It is what I entered.”

The clerk wrote her answer in the corner of the sheet and stamped it, and the stamp was the same stamp the pending filing had been marked with on the ninth day, and she stood there while he did it and understood that the county now held two documents with her hand on both of them and neither of them was about her advancement.

Then he asked whether the surety was still entered.

“The surety is dead,” said Ning Yan. “Entered in this county, under his own name, at his own voice, in daylight, for my attendance.”

“And the debt.”

“Stands to me,” said Ning Yan. “Write it that way. That is what death does to it and I would rather it went in your book before it goes in mine.”

The clerk wrote it, and turned the book so that the line sat where a county keeps a line, and did not say anything to her about it, which was the correct thing and which she would remember.

She climbed once to the loft and lay on the mat with the filing copy in her sleeve and the burial entry drying in the county book. Breath short at two counts. Arm a third and staying there. Left-hand order gone and not returning.

Below, Feng coughed and copied. The tower lamp burned in its cage. The market knew where she sleeps and had known it since the announcement, and now the bounty men knew the road to it runs no longer through a boy with flour on his sleeves.

That was what the loss bought and it held because she had named it herself where no one could file it away.
