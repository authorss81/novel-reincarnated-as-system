# The Safe Side

The loft held the day’s heat after the market quieted, shutter tied still, square noise thin through the gap.

Ning Yan lay on the mat with the third stage sitting in her like a stone set down crooked. Breath pulled short at two counts. The stripe ran cold. Her arm had served eleven minutes at the wall and gone, a third and no more, and she had stopped expecting it to be otherwise.

Below, Feng’s brush went fair and steady. Du Hong’s soup cooled on the boards beside her. The filing copy sat in her sleeve with the day’s mark and the stage entered.

“You are quiet,” said Yuan. “You think much and you say little.”

“I think about the noise,” said Ning Yan. “Four months and the bound is told again. The buyer knows the loft. The lane knows my loads. I ask why the claim cannot take me quietly.”

“I tell you,” said Yuan. “I tell you true and I tell you plain.”

She waited, the way she waits for a clerk to finish writing, without moving her face.

“You are Chosen,” said Yuan. “That is why no hand takes you without a record. I mean it and I hold it.”

“Many are claimed,” said Ning Yan. “The bound takes people nightly.”

“Not like you,” said Yuan. “You stand in eleven. Six hundred years, eleven bodies the bound had to account for. The writ is why there is a record of any of them at all.”

The writ, not a reward. He did not unsay what he had told her on the ninth step. She marked that he had kept it, because he lies to her the way he always lies, by taking the half-truth she has already drawn and hardening it into a floor.

Her own conclusion from the winter past had been that the claim cannot take her quietly. He took it now and set it down entire.

“The protection and the danger are the same line,” said Yuan. “You stand on the safe side of it. I see it and I say it.”

She sat up on the mat with her coat round her shoulders and looked through the shutter gap at the tower lamp burning below. A record is why a person cannot be taken without one. Eleven bodies, every one accounted for because the designation required accounting. That was his evidence, not the claim. The claim was the first in the bound’s history and she knew that better than he did.

“Safe,” said Ning Yan.

“Safe,” said Yuan. “I am with you and the line holds.”

She did not agree aloud. Agreement is a thing a person spends, and she needed the breath for the stair. She drank the soup left-handed and set the bowl aside, and logged nothing, because logging a sentence makes it paper and paper travels upward.

“You want me easy,” she said at last.

“I want you alive,” said Yuan. “I want you recorded and held. I want little else.”

The words sat between them with the lamp smoke and the coughing under the boards. Comfort offered, entire, kind in the way that spends her. She felt the want in it to be believed, and the fear under the want, and the clerk’s habit of calling omission a kindness.

She kept the three terms standing without speaking them. Employed, not forgiven. The work is her brother. Nothing decided about her again without telling her first. They stood, and she did not set a new rule, and she did not say the sentence back to him.

“I hear you,” said Ning Yan. “I sleep on it. You watch.”

“I watch,” said Yuan. “I stay with you through the night.”

She lay down with the docket in her lining and the filing copy in her sleeve, and the safe-side sentence ticking behind her eyes like a second shutter. Eleven bodies in six hundred years. A record for each because the writ required one.

It was a floor. He had given her a floor to stand on, and the floor was her own conclusion made solid under her feet.

She did not sleep for a long time. When she slept the breath went short and stayed short, and the tower held her the way a tower holds paper, steadily and without comment, and the market below knew where she slept and had known it since morning in the layer’s own voice.
