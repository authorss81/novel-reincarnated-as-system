# The Safe Side

She carried the day in public and thought in private, which is the only arrangement a servant gets.

The morning after the announcement the square talked about the loft over the copying floor the way squares talk about weather. Ning Yan went out with a water yoke at the seventh hour and heard her sleeping place named twice before the ninth, once wrong and once right. A carrier she knew told her that the layer had said *tower* and that three towers kept books in Lowwater and only one of them had a loft over it, and she said yes, and thanked him for the arithmetic, which he had not expected.

Two of the three desks said nothing to her at all, which was the worse of the two answers.

She took her stone to the wall and set it down and her arm came back eleven minutes into the block and served and went. Then she sat on the wall footing and did the sum she had not done yesterday, because a stage that has been announced changes what a body is worth and she wanted the number before the market made it for her.

Eleven designations. Six hundred years. The claim had a proof written into it in four county books: a body, a place, a name. She had been carrying the protection of that proof since the autumn and had never once costed it. A claim cannot take a person quietly because a claim is a document and a document needs a body to attach to. That was the whole of what she had understood at fifteen, and it had been enough to keep her alive in a hall full of men who wanted her finished.

But a document needs a body to attach to, and she had just made herself a body that nine counties could name, with a tower, and a market that knew what a tower was.

“You are working something out,” said Yuan. “You work it while your hands are busy.”

“I am working two things,” said Ning Yan. “I am counting what I am worth now, and I am counting what you are about to tell me.”

“I will not tell you anything you did not ask for.”

“No,” said Ning Yan. “You will not.”

In the evening the loft held the day's heat after the market quieted, shutter tied still, square noise thin through the gap.

Ning Yan lay on the mat with the third stage sitting in her like a stone set down crooked. Breath pulled short at two counts. The stripe ran cold. Her arm had served eleven minutes at the wall and gone, a third and no more, and she had stopped expecting it to be otherwise.

Below, Feng’s brush went fair and steady. Du Hong’s soup cooled on the boards beside her. The filing copy sat in her sleeve with the day’s mark and the stage entered.

“You are quiet,” said Yuan. “You think much and you say little.”

“I think about the noise,” said Ning Yan. “Four months and the bound is told again. The buyer knows the loft. The lane knows my loads. I ask why the claim cannot take me quietly.”

“I tell you,” said Yuan. “I tell you true and I tell you plain.”

She waited, the way she waits for a clerk to finish writing, without moving her face.

“You are Chosen,” said Yuan. “That is why no hand takes you without a record. I mean it and I hold it.”

“Many are claimed,” said Ning Yan. “The bound takes people nightly.”

“Not like you,” said Yuan. “You stand in eleven. Six hundred years, eleven bodies the bound had to account for. The writ is why there is a record of any of them at all.”

She did not answer straight away. The writ, not a reward. He did not unsay what he had told her on the ninth step. She marked that he had kept it, because he lies to her the way he always lies, by taking the half-truth she has already drawn and hardening it into a floor.

Her own conclusion from the winter past had been that the claim cannot take her quietly. He took it now and set it down entire, with a number attached to it, and a number is the one thing she has never been able to argue with because a number can be counted.

And underneath that there was a second thing she had not said out loud in four months, which was that she had wanted it to be him.

That was the shameful part, and she looked at it plainly, the way she looks at a tally that does not balance. From the terrace on, some part of her had been waiting for him to say that the claim could not touch her, because it was easier to owe your life to a voice than to a schedule. Every time she had told herself she was working, some part of her had been waiting for the sentence that made the working optional.

It had come. It was not a sentence that made anything optional. It was a sentence with six hundred years and eleven bodies behind it, and eleven bodies is a number, and a number does not care who is speaking it.

“Six hundred years,” she said. “Eleven. So one every fifty-five years, roughly.”

“Roughly. There are long gaps. There are gaps that are not long.”

“That is thirty-nine bodies in six hundred years, by the county's own arithmetic, if the claim has stood the whole time.”

“It has not stood the whole time,” said Yuan. “It stood once. What I am telling you is what stood once.”

“Then tell me what stood once,” said Ning Yan, “in numbers.”

“No,” said Yuan.

She lay still with that. He had refused her a number, which he had never done, and the refusal was more use than any number would have been, because a man who will not give you the figure is a man who knows the figure is the wrong size for the question.

“Protection and the danger are the same line,” said Yuan. “You stand on the safe side of it. I see it and I say it.”

She sat up on the mat with her coat round her shoulders and looked through the shutter gap at the tower lamp burning below. A record is why a person cannot be taken without one. Eleven bodies, every one accounted for because the designation required accounting. That was his evidence, not the claim. The claim was the first in the bound's history and she knew that better than he did.

“Safe,” said Ning Yan.

“Safe,” said Yuan. “I am with you and the line holds.”

She did not agree aloud. Agreement is a thing a person spends, and she needed the breath for the stair. She drank the soup left-handed and set the bowl aside, and logged nothing, because logging a sentence makes it paper and paper travels upward.

“You want me easy,” she said at last.

“I want you alive,” said Yuan. “I want you recorded and held. I want little else.”

The words sat between them with the lamp smoke and the coughing under the boards. Comfort offered, entire, kind in the way that spends her. She felt the want in it to be believed, and the fear under the want, and the clerk's habit of calling omission a kindness.

She kept the three terms standing without speaking them. Employed, not forgiven. The work is her brother. Nothing decided about her again without telling her first. They stood, and she did not set a new rule, and she did not say the sentence back to him.

“I hear you,” said Ning Yan. “I sleep on it. You watch.”

“I watch,” said Yuan. “I am here through the night.”

She lay down with the docket in her lining and the filing copy in her sleeve, and the safe-side sentence ticking behind her eyes like a second shutter. Eleven bodies in six hundred years. A record for each because the writ required one.

It was a floor. He had given her a floor to stand on, and the floor was her own conclusion made solid under her feet.

She did not sleep for a long time. When she slept the breath went short and stayed short, and the tower held her the way a tower holds paper, steadily and without comment, and the market below knew where she slept and had known it since morning in the layer's own voice.
