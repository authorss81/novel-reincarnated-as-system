# The Pending Filing

She walked into the county office on the square on the eighty-first day certain the licence was hers, with three legs under her arm and Du Hong beside her.

The shutters stood open and the queue ran out the door into the market noise. Sun He was at the far table with the county book open and the stamp at his hand, and nineteen years of that book were in the lines of his face. She took her place fourth and stood in it, because a queue is where a body in a county office is least worth looking at, with the sponsors' seal, the quota clearance signed without a look, and the surety name fresh in the book.

The application was complete. Seal, clearance, surety. No part is the licence, but all parts together the county countersigns. She had worked four months of tithe for it, two hundred and eleven to nine hundred and forty, fever and short breath, a stage the whole bound could recite. Up the mountain the three-sealed refusal still lay under the cover of the tithe book where no clerk had been told to look for it and no clerk had. This paper would replace it.

“You want it,” said Yuan. “You carry it in and you do not know what it says.”

“I carry it in,” said Ning Yan. “I walk in entered. That is all I have.”

## The Refusal

Sun He called her at the tenth hour, and she stood up out of the queue with her hands at her sides before he had finished saying her name.

She laid the papers on the table in order: the sealed application, the clearance with Shen Xiu's hand on it, the surety entry. The three seals stood above the old Hall refusal the way they had stood before nine people in a queue. The anteroom went quiet the way rooms go quiet when a stamp is about to fall.

He read the seal. He read the clearance. He read the surety. Then he turned pages back in the county book to a page with a mark on it, and read that longer.

“Application refused,” he said. “The sponsors' seal stands. Quota clearance stands. The surety stands. The bounty standing voids them.”

The words landed the way the burial clerk's brush lands, without hurry. She had asked a registrar about this exact wall in a queue three weeks ago and had been given the shape of it for nothing, and here it was, with three legs laid out on the table underneath it.

The man in the good coat was buying a page in a county file that said no household in this town had her entered against it, and she had come down four miles on a dawn cart to get onto a page with a county hand on it.

The county had looked at everything she carried and refused to countersign a name the layer held. So the page she wanted would have to be a page about her body instead of her name, and there was one, and the registrar was already turning to it.

She took it standing with her hands at her sides. It rhymed the Hall refusal without repeating it, same shape and different book: want of standing instead of want of clearance, county instead of Hall, bounty instead of hours. She had filed once at the Hall and been refused. She had brought three legs to Lowwater and been refused again, in the same hour as the paper that would save her.

“No plea moves it,” said Sun He. “No sponsor moves it. The standing is not mine.”

“I know,” said Ning Yan. “I ask what the county gives instead.”

He turned to a fresh sheet in the county book. The brush sat ready. The queue waited.

“The county gives a pending filing to hands that work,” he said. “Hours, stages and defect on the county's own sheet, counted from the day the tithe book opened. It carries a ceiling where a cracked aperture stops. It is worth everything except the Foundation. It is refused as a licence and kept as a filing.”

He read the terms plainly, the way Shen Xiu reads figures. The sheet counts every hour worked and the days not worked, continuous, no gap. It records stages as announced. It enters the defect in the applicant's own hand. It caps practice at the fifth stage of Condensing. It bars any Foundation petition without relicensing. A refused application is worth nothing. A pending one is worth everything the body can still hold.

She heard the ceiling for the first time with a brush behind it. Fifth stage. Where a cracked aperture stops. Her mouth for an aperture, stabilised at the crack, would take her to five and no further. Beyond that the paper stops and the practice without paper is breach.

## The Defect

He set the sheet before her with the brush on it.

“Defect in your own hand,” said Sun He. “As the body says.”

She took the brush in her left hand. The county office went quiet round her, clerks and queue, Du Hong at her shoulder. She wrote in the ugly slant the county had already seen on the tithe sheets, three clauses that were the truth of her chest since the sixth day of the winter.

*aperture cracked through; stabilised at the crack; permanent.*

She read it back without being asked, the way she reads tallies, so no clerk wonders after whether she wrote it.

Then she signed the ceiling down on herself. Practice capped at the fifth stage of Condensing. No Foundation petition without relicensing. Sponsors' seal and clearance and surety carried on a pending sheet instead of a licence. The brush moved and the ink dried, and it could not be undone.

She worked out the writ as the ink dried, the way she works figures standing up. Foundation within two years, outside the schedule, on the sponsors' signature. Foundation on that writ reaches only through a licence. Without one, Foundation on that writ is practice under a schedule it does not appear on. The pending page kills it dead on the same sheet that gives her room to climb.

Three men knew and had said nothing in the corridor. They know now, with the county's ink on it. The leash, the lever and the room, all three, taken together while refused.

“You sign it,” said Yuan. “You know the writ dies and you sign still.”

“I sign it,” said Ning Yan. “A refused paper is worth nothing. A pending one is worth five stages. I take five.”

“You take it,” said Yuan. “I see you and I hold it with you.”

## The Same Hour

Sun He entered the surety alongside, Du Hong's name in the county book for her attendance at every county hour, told and standing. Death voids it and the debt transfers to her. Du Hong watched it go down without flinching. Then the registrar set the county mark on the pending filing, not the licence stamp but the filing mark, and the paper was worth something.

Refused and signed in the same hour. The licence refused on the bounty standing. The pending filing signed with the ceiling in her own hand. Capped at five, barred from Foundation without relicensing, sponsors' writ dead on the page, surety living beside it.

She walked out into the square with Du Hong and stood a moment in the market noise. The three county desks ran their trade. The man in the good coat stood at the second one with a coin on the wood, employed by a broker with a counting room, buying records in daylight. The tower stood above the roofs with four copies walled behind the fifth item, column at five. The county office held her defect and her ceiling behind her in its book.

Every hour she had worked, and the days she did not. That was what clearance had cost, and she had paid it. Who bought proof in Lowwater now the road was open was answered too: a man in a good coat, employed, polite, paying for the place unbought and the carriers' names. The claim stood and the paper stood, side by side, one refusing the other and both hers.

“You carry it,” said Yuan. “You bear the ceiling and you walk still.”

“I carry it,” said Ning Yan. “I climb to five and I stop where the paper stops. That is the room.”

She went up to the loft with the filing copy in her sleeve. The breath pulled short at two counts on the stair. The stripe ran cold. The arm hung dead and she did not test it a third time. Below, Feng coughed and copied. On the square the desks closed. In the counting lane the broker stacked paper by household.

The licence was refused. The pending filing was signed. The claim stood unanswered, and it was why the county had refused her. The writ was dead, and three men knew and said nothing. Du Hong's name stood in a county book for her. She lay on the mat in Lowwater with a tower, a square, and a man hunting proofs round her, capped and grieving and legal to five.

She did not forgive anybody. Nobody forgave her. The middle of the winter held.
