# Feng Holding the Lamp

They got Du Hong inside before the light failed, Feng with the lamp, Ning Yan with his shoulders, a carrier with his legs.

The copying floor ran lamplight and coughing. Brushes stopped. Clerks cleared the intake table and stood at the wall and did not ask anything, which was the only kindness a room had to give and it was given without anyone deciding to give it. She laid him on it in his wet coat with her copies still in the lining, her hand twice in his file where the file lay torn on the road, and held pressure on the mouth bleed with her left hand while Feng cut the coat away.

“You press,” said Feng. “You hold here and you do not move.”

“I press,” said Ning Yan. “I hold. You work.”

He worked the way he copies, fair and steady and thinning, hands shaking on the shelf edge and then steady on the body. Ribs broken. Face cut. Cold water in the lungs from the ditch at the tower foot. Beaten for the place and left in thaw water, refused and burning.

He cut the coat off with a knife he used for leather, which was wrong for the work and the only one on the floor, and he did not stop while he did it. He asked three questions in the order a clerk asks them and he wrote nothing down, because there was no book on the copying floor for it and he was not a man who wrote things down that would travel.

“Seven broken,” he said, at the fourth minute. “Two of them go in. I cannot put two back in and I am the only hand in this tower.”

“Do it anyway,” said Ning Yan.

“I will do it anyway,” said Feng, “but you have heard me say it and you do not get to say you did not.”

The burned sheet was gone, blackened and broken on the road. The place refused. His own name given instead of hers. That was what he had carried into the beating, and it had held through it.

Du Hong breathed ragged on the table with lamplight on his face. Eighteen, generous, bad at lying. He opened his eyes once and looked past the lamp at her.

“Entered,” he said. Blood ran from his mouth. “Walk entered. I told you.”

“You walked it,” said Ning Yan. Her voice came out plain, without tally. “You gave your name. You burned one sheet. You refused the place. I saw it.”

He smiled, short, bloodied. “The cheap door holds.”

Then the breathing changed. Feng held the lamp closer, coughing into his sleeve, old and unbowed, and shook his head once without speaking. No healer in time. No county book to sign. Only heat, pressure, and a lamp.

The water in the lungs was the thing. She knew what it looked like because she had sat on a furnace floor in the winter watching two men with exactly this breathing, and she had washed their faces afterward left-handed, and she was going to wash this one too and there was nothing in the room that would make it different.

“You stay with him,” said Yuan. “You do not leave him and I do not leave you.”

“I stay,” said Ning Yan. “You stay with me. That is all.”

He died before the night was out with Feng holding the lamp over him, coughing steady through the boards of lamplight. No speech. No plea. Only breath going short and then stopping, the way breath stops when cold water and beating have done what they do.

It took a long time and there was no moment in it she could have called the moment. She watched the lamp flame lean twice, and the second time it did not lean back.

She washed his face before she could speak, the way she had washed two faces on a furnace floor weeks ago, left-handed, without hurry. She washed the blood out from under his eyes and off his jaw and she did not do the mouth, because there was nothing in the mouth to wash and she had read that in a book once in a winter she did not want to think about now.

Feng set the lamp on the table and stood with his hand on the shelf edge till it steadied.

Proof failed on the body. The beaten man gave no place. The burned sheet gave no name. The dead man on the road edge gave no testimony, ended by her hand in a listed kill the layer had already written down. The elder had backed toward the counting lane shouting that the place was refused, and the only man alive who had heard him say it was standing on a stair in a wet coat, and the bounty stood unclaimed with its three proofs unbought on a body that proved nothing.

She sat on the copying floor with her dead arm under her and her living hand on his chest after breath stopped, and understood what she had done when she came down the road. Killed one of them. Habit, not technique. One working arm. Very nearly killed herself doing it. And it was the killing that finished it, because feet that lose a man do not forgive the road that took him.

The four copies sat walled behind the fifth item, entered none upward, column at five. The filing copy sat in her sleeve with the day's mark. Settlement stood at roughly two-thirds, about eleven weeks. The left-hand sequence was gone and cannot be relearned.

The clerks came back in at the last hour and found the table bare and the floor washed and a lamp burning, and Feng told them a tower hand had taken a fall on the stair and had been carried out to the wash house before the cold got into him, and every one of them wrote that down because it was easier than the other thing, and Ning Yan let them.

The Watch came at the ninth hour with two men and a book, and did what a Watch does in thaw, which is to write down what is lying in the road and the names of the men who are standing near it.

The elder man had gone. The younger one was still lying on the road edge under two boards the tower had sent down at the fifth hour and nobody had come for him, and two carriers from the tower lane stood well back with their hands visible, and that was the whole of the crowd.

The Watchman wrote a body, a road, and a ditch. He wrote that a man had been shouting about a refused place and that the shouting was finished when he arrived. He wrote that one man was carried up the tower stair alive at the fourth hour and that a lamp was carried up with him.

He did not write that the corpse was answering to an act entered in the layer, and he could not have, and the reason he did not write it was that no office in Lowwater had ever had a form with a column for it.

He asked her whether the man carried up had said anything.

“He said *entered*,” said Ning Yan. “He was telling me he had walked entered.”

The Watchman wrote that down as a statement made by the injured party and no more than that, and closed the book, and went, because there was a road to clear and it was going to be impassable by morning.

That was the whole of the county's half of it, and it was the reason the eight hundred jin still stood unanswered at dawn.

Feng picked up the lamp and held it over the table a moment longer, fair hand shaking, coughing wet and deep.

“He stood surety,” he said. “The county book holds his name. Death voids it.”

“I know,” said Ning Yan. “The debt transfers. I carry it.”

He looked at her for a while with the lamp held out in front of him and did not ask her whether she was going to be all right, and she understood that he had decided not to and that the deciding had cost him something, because he was a man who asked.

She stayed till the lamp burned low. No forgiveness asked. None given. The tower holds paper the way the furnace holds heat, steadily and without comment, and the hands inside it fix shutters and copy fair and hold lamps for the dying.
