# The Ash-Flats

The Verge keeps its ash on the flats east of Lowwater and in the thaw it turns to mud that takes a boot to the ankle, and on the tenth day after they put Du Hong in the ground she went out onto it at the fifth hour and did not come back till the light went.

She had been three days without speaking to a person who was not paid to be in the room. She needed ground. A stage needs ground and a grief needs something to be heavy with, and the tower had a floor with a copying business on it and a loft with a shutter that did not shut, and the flats had eleven acres of nothing on them and a wind that came off the Verge hard enough to take the breath out of her at two counts.

She carried a stone. Not a load. One stone, four pounds of it, picked off a scree line and carried out to a marker post thirty yards down and set down and walked back and picked up again, all day, in mud, with her right arm hanging and her left arm doing the lifting, until the left arm was the arm she had.

That was the grief, worked into something. She had learned it at eleven. Feeling is a load. You carry it until it is set down or until your back changes, and there is no third thing, and nobody has ever put that in a book.

The fourth morning out she stopped at the post and stood in the wind and worked out that she had been carrying the same stone thirty yards for three days and had not put it down once, and had walked about four miles of flat ground in it, and that the same four pounds was going to be the same four pounds at the fourth hour of the afternoon, and that was the day, and that was how she found out what her body had decided.

She sat down on the marker post because a marker post is a thing a person is allowed to sit on.

## What the Sheet Said

She took the filing copy out of her coat with her left hand and worked it against her knee in the wind, because she had been carrying eleven weeks of paper in a lining and had never once read the middle of it.

It was in the plainest hand the county has, which is a hand that does not want to be remembered. Hours, stages and defect, on the county's own sheet, counted from the day the tithe book opened. It recorded stages **as announced**. She had read that line at the desk on the eighty-first day and had taken it for a formality, the way a woman takes the small print on a sheet she is signing under a brush somebody else is holding.

There were five words in it that she had not looked at.

A stage that is announced goes into four county books and lands in the county sheet with a hand on it and an hour. A stage that is not announced goes nowhere. It is not that it is hidden. It is that it does not exist in any office in nine counties, and a filing is a chain of stages, and a chain with a missing link is not a shorter chain.

He was wrong about the second half of it, or she was. A chain does not stop being a chain because a link is gone. But it is a chain a registrar has to look at, and a registrar who has to look at it is a registrar deciding something, and she was seventeen and capped and she did not have a leg in this court.

She put the filing copy away.

“You have one in you,” said Yuan.

She did not startle. After eight months you do not startle at a voice that arrives without air.

“You have had one in you since Tuesday,” said Yuan. “I have watched it come. It is ready and it is not released and it is mine to release and not yours, and I do not do it until you say.”

That was the term. The third one, standing since a winter step on a terrace, in the plain words she had set for him and he had broken once and been stopped once: nothing decided about her again without telling her first. He had not held it four seconds this time. He had held it three days and had come and found her on a mud flat east of town and asked her before the light went, and had set out what each choice did while he asked.

He had never asked her properly before. He had told, and acted, and been sorry. This was a question with its consequences lying beside it, and he waited for the answer with the whole of it, and he did not fill the silence with a recommendation.

“If you let it go,” said Yuan. “It goes to every ear inside the bound at once. It names this town, because I do the naming and the town is where you are. Four months past, nine counties learned where you sleep off a sentence that goes out when I say it, and this one goes out tonight.”

“And if you hold it.”

“Then it goes out never, or on a day I pick, and it will go out on a day I pick if you ever tell me to let it go, and it does not rot while I hold it. It does not spoil. It is in me and it stays in me.”

“A month from now. A year.”

“A year. It will not change by waiting. It is a body that has made a place for it and the place is there.”

“And the filing.”

“The filing counts stages as announced.” He said it without weight, the way he reads figures, and then he stopped and said the rest of it, because he had learned at the fourth step of a terrace that a fact without its cost is a knife with the handle toward somebody else.

“The county sheet will hold one, two and three. It will not hold four. When you reach five and it is announced, your own record reads one, two, three, five.”

“There is a hole in it where you are standing.”

“There is a hole in it where you are standing,” said Yuan.

“Does a hole break it.”

“I do not know.” He said it plainly and did not soften it, which was the whole of what she had asked for and the reason she was standing in mud instead of a warm room. “A filing fails when it is broken and a registrar decides what is broken.”

“He may read an absence as an absence. He may read it as a body that advanced and left the record out of order, and then it is a question asked at a table with a queue behind it.”

“I cannot see his book and I will not tell you it is safe.”

The wind came off the Verge and put the cold in the left ribs where the crack ran, and she stood with a four-pound stone in her left hand and let the cold finish its run.

“What would you have done.”

“I would have let it go,” said Yuan. “On the twelfth day, in the yard, on my own hour. That is my shape. I do it at a terrace in the frost and I do it in a yard behind a tower, and every time it costs you something, and every time you have not asked.”

“You would not have asked.”

“I would not have asked,” said Yuan, “and you would have found out about it from a clerk or from a market or from your own body, and you would have been right, and I would have had nothing to say to you at all.”

She stood in the mud with that.

“Today,” said Ning Yan. “Today would be the day I buried him.”

“That is your reason.”

“That is my reason and I do not need a second one.” She set the stone down in the mud. “Nine counties have my stage three and my town and a date they can put against a burial if they walk to a churchyard and read the ground. I am not going to be a number on the morning they put Du Hong in. Hold it.”

“You say it and I hold it.” He said it the way he says it when she has given an order and there is nothing left to argue with. “I hold it now. It is held. Nine counties do not have it. You do.”

## What It Cost

She walked back up off the flats at the ninth hour with the wind behind her and the stone still in her hand, because she carried it to the trough and set it down where a stone belongs.

Feng was in the lane with the boards half in. He looked at her boots and looked at the sky and did not ask where she had been, because he has not asked her one question in twenty-two years of copying for her and he was not going to start in the spring.

That night she sat on the loft mat with the filing copy out of her sleeve and the coat for a blanket and the arm out to the side, and she did the arithmetic she had been doing for eleven days and found the same figure at the end of it.

She had bought a burial morning with the fourth stage of her life.

The price had been entered nowhere. Not in the county book, not in the tower's four copies, not in the schedule, not in a letter to a man she owed nothing. It was in a mud flat east of a town, in the body of a woman carrying a stone, and it would be in no office in nine counties for as long as there were offices in nine counties.

She wrote nothing in the log about it, because a thing nobody else holds a record of is the only kind of price that is entirely hers, and there had not been many of those in a year.

She was not hungry. She noticed that the way a woman on a furnace floor notices a wall leaning, and she did not think anything about it until the fourth day, when Feng put a bowl down by the mat at the eighth hour and looked at her while he did it, and she understood that the four pounds of stone had been the last thing that had happened to her that her body had agreed to.
