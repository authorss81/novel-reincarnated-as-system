# The Roofless Hall

The clerk had her name on the table in the wrong order. He had been the Hall's copyist since the twelfth day of the winter and he wrote it that way because an office had sent him a version and he had no other, and she had asked him to write it in the wrong order and he had obeyed her, and neither of them had ever once mentioned it since.

She did not mention it now. She put her left hand flat on the table and pushed three sheets across it.

The filing copy, worn through at the fold from eleven weeks in a coat lining. The schedule, certified, the Hall's mark on it and Shen Xiu's hours and her hours in a column of their own with the last week of the thaw closed on it. And the defect line, in her own hand, in a slant no clerk in nine counties can read.

*The undersigned requires the schedule attached to her filing to be read and received by the county office at Lowwater at the return of the quarter.*

The clerk read it twice, which is more than she expected, and then he stopped with the second sheet in his hand and looked up at the rain coming through the beams four feet over his right shoulder and coming down in a line onto the stone floor he had been carefully not standing in.

“That is not an ordinary request,” he said.

“No.”

“The ordinary request is a form and a fee and it goes up with the crate. This is you standing under my awning in the rain asking me to put a sheet on a thing that goes up ahead of a crate.”

“Yes,” said Ning Yan. “I am asking you to put it on it.”

He looked at the filing copy, and then at the schedule, and then at the marks on both of them, and she watched him do the arithmetic that any man who has ever sat at a table in this Hall does with a sheet he is being handed twice, which is to work out who is holding it and what it is for.

“There is no signature on the wanting of it,” he said.

“No. There is a defect line in my hand and a ceiling in my hand and a county stamp on a filing that is the county's own paper, and you do not need me to want it. The registrar may look at it or he may not. I want it to be in the crate when the crate goes, and I am telling you that plainly, and I am not asking you to carry anything.”

“I am not carrying anything,” said the clerk. “I am a copyist and I am wet and I have eleven days.”

“You have eleven days and the crate goes at the quarter and the county needs it in the spring and I know what a registrar's hand is and so do you, and I am a woman who is capped at five and I have eight days of road left in me before the road shuts for the season, and if that sheet is not in that crate it will not be in the county at all this year.”

She said it all in one breath, and then she stopped, and she put the twenty-two hours of carrying on the last three days in where it belonged, which was underneath.

## What the Sponsors Did

The schedule belonged to three men and to nobody else, because it was their paper, and it was certified by their Hall's clerk, and it carried a column of nine households struck over eleven years that nobody was supposed to read, and a servant-born who advanced inside it was the only thing on it in eleven years that ever worked.

They had known since the county had put a stamp on a filing with a ceiling on it, and they had said nothing, and there were three men in a corridor behind a Hall clerk and she was going to walk past them with a sheet on a table.

Elder Lu Kang was at the west end under a dry awning of his own with his hands behind his back.

Ning Yan came along the table and put the schedule down in front of him with the Hall's mark on it and the hours on it and the season closed, and she stood there with her left hand flat on the wood.

He did not ask what it was. He read it — the whole of it, the header and the column and the hours and the closing date — at the speed of a man who has read a document like this eleven thousand times.

He did not turn it over, and he did not look at the nine households, and when he came to the end of it he took the Hall's stamp off the wall behind him and set it on the sheet himself and pressed it and lifted it and set the stamp back.

He did not say one word.

“You are certifying a schedule for a licence that has killed your writ,” said Ning Yan.

“I am certifying a schedule,” said Lu Kang.

Deng Chao and Pei An were behind him and neither of them came forward, and she said what she had come to say, in the plain words and in front of the Hall clerk, because she was not going to let a thing like this be said in a corridor where only one man heard it.

“The Foundation writ died on the county sheet the hour I signed the ceiling. Foundation on your signature is unregistered practice under a schedule it does not appear on, and if I go to Foundation on it, the sponsorship that issued it is breached, and the breach is mine and yours and on one page.”

“The write has not been revoked,” said Deng Chao.

“It has not been revoked,” said Lu Kang, from the awning, “and it has no road left to anywhere. You may both write to your own rooms about that at your leisure.”

He turned and went along the west table and out into the rain, and Pei An went with him, and Deng Chao stayed where he was for a moment with his hand on the back of the bench and then went too, and none of the three of them said anything else, and the Hall clerk copied his own name into the certification column and did not look up while he did it.

She would have said thank you to a man who had helped her, and he had not helped her. He had stamped his own paper, which was the only thing left in the building he could still lawfully do, and the three of them had known what it would cost her since the day they signed it and had let her sign anyway, and there was no arrangement on any side of this in which one of those men was owed anything.

That was the leash and the lever and the room, all three, in one stamp, in the rain, under a roof that had been gone since the autumn.

## The Cold End

She worked the cold end at the ninth hour that afternoon because there was nothing else she was going to do with a day that had cost her nine men and gained her a stamp.

The stage was in her at the fourth and had been in her at the fourth for eleven days, and the stage above the fourth was where the practice stopped and her own hand in her own hand had written down that it stopped, and everything between the two of them was nine days of load and a mud flat and a held sentence, and the ceiling was the thing that had made the whole of the winter survivable, and it was one stage away.

The crew came down at the ninth hour and worked around her and worked the cold end on the other side of the room, and two hundred yards of timber and four men were between her and the next man, and they were doing it because she was a woman on a shift that none of them would sign and they had worked out where she would be on the floor and they had moved to the other end of it.

“You are at the edge,” said Yuan.

“I am at the edge.”

“You are at the edge and I hold a sentence I have held for eleven days, and it does not spoil, and it will go out on whatever day you say, and I have not asked you and I will not ask you again until you are ready. I say it once because you have not heard it from me since the mud flat and a man should not leave a thing like that unsaid for eleven days.”

“I heard it at the mud flat,” said Ning Yan. “And I have thought about it every hour since, and I have not turned my mind from it once, and that is not a woman who has forgotten what a thing costs.”

“No,” said Yuan. “It is not.”

She held the wall on the cold end with the left hand flat on cold brick and let the channel run the way it had run without asking her since the second of the month, and the crack sat open as a mouth and did work and paid at the rim, and she let it, because the filing wanted the hours and the hours were all she had.

Then she went up the stair and lay on the mat in the second bunk of the quarters with ten men breathing round her, and the arm lay out to the side because it did that, and she put the filing copy out of the lining and looked at the stage column of it in the lamplight.

One. Two. Three.

And then a space, the width of two ruled lines, where the fourth was not, and would not be, and could not be put in now by anybody including her, because a county that has entered a striking on a sheet at the second hour this morning does not take a line back before the quarter at the earliest, and she had known that when she said it in the mud, and she had said it anyway.

Six weeks from the last day of the thaw for the return, and the road open to carts before the ninth hour and shut by the eleventh, and four days up and four days down.
