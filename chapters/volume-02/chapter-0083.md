# Weight of the Market

The Lowwater square at the eleventh hour is a thing you learn by weight, not by sight.

Ning Yan learned it carrying stone for the tower wall, load on her shoulder, breath short, eyes open. The county office on the north side with its queue and its stamp. Three county desks under the east awning where clerks copy advancement notices for coin. Carriers shouting loads, oil sellers, a knife grinder, children underfoot. And through it all a man in a good coat moving the way a brush moves in a fair hand, desk to desk, polite and unhurried.

She set the stone down at the wall and took a carrier's strap for the afternoon, because carriers hear more than stone carriers and the hours count the same. The strap sat across her chest over the tablet, and the log rode in her lining, and the defect in her chest pulled short every third breath.

“You watch him,” said Yuan. “You learn him and you do not touch him.”

“I learn him,” said Ning Yan. “I carry and I listen. You watch with me.”

“I watch,” said Yuan. “I touch nothing.”

## The Desks

The first desk kept the Qinghe advancement copies. The clerk there was young with ink to the wrist, and he told the man for coin that the copies sit with the county office and not with him, and wrote down that he was asked and did not have them. The man paid for the writing and thanked him.

The second desk kept carriers' papers. The clerk there was older and tired, and he turned his book for coin and read out three names of carriers who had taken unsigned packets up the road in eleven weeks. None was the name the man wanted, because the packets that matter went in Du Hong's coat with no name on them. The clerk wrote the asking and the not-knowing, and the man paid for both.

The third desk kept household lines. The clerk there shook his head before the coin came out. The place where she sleeps is not in his book, he said, because sect bunks are not county lines. He wrote that too when the coin sat on the wood, because the man pays for records of refusals as well as answers.

Two of three, worked politely in daylight before a square. The third unasked because the third is a body and bodies walk through squares with straps across their chests.

She carried loads past all three desks four times in the afternoon and heard it all without seeming to hear. The restraint sat in her like a second load. On the mountain she had stood on a step and given an order that thinned the channel to weeks. Here she carried and listened, because confrontation buys nothing and costs the place she sleeps.

At the fifth hour the man closed his tube and walked to the counting lane that runs off the square toward the broker's quarter. She did not follow. Following is a statement, and she had made her statements for the season in a burial register. She marked the lane and the hour in wash code on the Clause Four docket and went back to the tower.

“How does it stand,” Du Hong asked in the loft.

“Five hundred and twenty worked,” said Ning Yan. “Four hundred and twenty standing. Eighty-five in my own hand.”

He did the subtraction on his fingers the way carriers do. “Spring holds.”

“Spring holds if the book squares,” said Ning Yan. “The book squares on five estates not mine. Shen Xiu carries those.”

## The Counting Lane

In the evening Feng sent her to the counting lane with a sealed copy for a merchant, because tower copies go by hand after the fifth hour and she was a hand.

The lane held counting rooms with barred windows and brushes going behind them. She delivered the copy to the third door, took the receipt mark, and walked back without hurrying. Halfway down she passed an open door with paper stacked by household and a brush that never stopped, and a clerk calling figures to a back room. The man in the good coat stood at the tall desk with his tube open, reporting in a low voice, employed the way clerks are employed, unhurried even indoors.

She did not stop. Stopping is a statement. She walked past with the receipt in her hand and the stripe cold under her coat, and heard enough to know the room counts by household and the broker holds paper the way the sponsors hold schedules.

“You see the room,” said Yuan. “You know it and you keep it.”

“I see it,” said Ning Yan. “I keep it in code. I do not enter it.”

Back in the loft she added to the docket: counting room, stacked by household, employed man reports. She did not write the broker's name. The name on the debt paper up the mountain was got upside down over a clerk's tray, and she keeps what she gets the way she got it, unreadable and exact.

“You do well,” said Yuan. “You carry stone and you carry knowledge. I see both.”

“I carry hours,” said Ning Yan. “Hours are what the county stamps. The rest is weight.”

In the morning the frost held late and the desks opened slow. She took a water yoke for the tower kitchen because hands carry what the tower needs and the hours count the same. At the well she heard two carriers talk about the good coat the way carriers talk about weather, without interest. He pays, he writes, he thanks.

One said the man asks after packets unsigned and always pays for the writing even when the answer is nothing. The other said a man who pays for nothing is saving to pay for something.

She carried the yoke up the tower stair with the wood knocking her shoulders and set it down without spilling. The breath pulled short at two counts and settled. Feng nodded to her from the fair-hand desk without stopping the brush. The intake clerk entered the water as labour on the schedule sheet, and the sheet would go up by the carts on the third evening, and the clerk with the broken finger would write it slow.

She lay on the mat with the square noise coming through the shutter gap. Five hundred and twenty. Four hundred and twenty. The man paid at three desks for records. The counting room stacked them by household. She slept with the strap marks on her shoulders and the arithmetic ticking, and the market's weight settled over her like a blanket that does not warm.

In the morning she went down for another strap. The desks opened. The man came at the eleventh hour. The coin sat on the wood. The writing went into files. And she carried past with stone and paper, a body unbuyable at any desk, working her hours toward spring.
