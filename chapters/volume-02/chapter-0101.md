# The Listed Kill

The Lowwater road at the foot of the Lantern Register tower runs mud to the ankle in thaw, ditch water high on the town side, tower shadow long across it.

Two men held Du Hong between them where the road meets the tower stair. The elder had his arms pinned. The younger had his file open, her hand twice on its pages, and her copies in his coat where the coat hung open. A burned sheet blackened at their feet in thaw water.

Ning Yan came down the road with soup still warm on her hands and stopped ten feet off, breathing two counts, with one working arm and short breath and habit in her legs.

“Give us the place,” said the younger man. He spoke the way men speak when they are done being polite at desks. “The loft, the bunk, the room off the court. Any of them. Say where she sleeps and we walk.”

Du Hong stood in mud with his face bloodied and his flour sleeves torn. He looked past them at her once, and then away, the way carriers look at weather they have decided about.

He had decided. She could see the deciding sitting on him like a weight.

“I am Du Hong,” he said. “Furnace crew, tower hand. My name is in the county book for attendance. That is the place I give. Mine.”

“The other place,” said the elder. “The woman’s place. The Chosen’s sleeping place. Say it.”

“I carry sheets with no name on them,” said Du Hong. “I carry letters. I carry soup. I burn what is mine to burn.” He nodded at the blackened sheet in the water. “That was mine. The rest is entered. Ask the county.”

They beat him for the place. The elder struck him across the face with the file. The younger kicked his legs from under him into thaw water at the tower's foot. He went down without crying out, because crying out is for rooms where somebody might save you.

The younger man stood over him with the file open and read from it, in the flat voice of a man doing his work. *Advancement on the recommendation of Ning Yan. Direction to report, made by the Elder Hall, never filed. Two entries in one hand on the same sheet.* He read all of it while Du Hong was in the water, because a file is read to a man in the water and not to him later.

Then he said the thing she had been afraid of for four months, which was not a threat but an account.

“You are standing at ten feet in a lane with a county stage entered against your name and a tower above you with the lamps lit. We have all three. You have not made anybody poor by saying it.”

Ning Yan moved before the water settled.

She had one working arm, short breath, and the Nine-Step as habit, not technique, from a laundry manual's back page remembered in her legs. No aperture opened as channel. No crack worked as bill. Only weight and turn and settle, built out of four weeks of stone.

Hook, turn, settle. Left leg under the younger man's knee. Hip through his stance. Elbow to the throat where the breath lives.

The second man went down hard on the road edge and did not get up. The first released Du Hong and came at her with the file raised, and she turned into him with the same habit, shoulder reading done before thought, and put him in the ditch with a carrier's yell behind her.

Then the channel tore. Breath broke to one count. Cold ran up the left ribs. The body she had built to stage three gave under the killing the way a wall gives under a beam, crooked and entire.

The cold in her left ribs opened the whole way down while the killing was still in her legs. It was the crack she had never used on anybody, working without being asked, paying at the rim for something she had not spent and could not stop spending, and for one whole breath she was aware of every person in the tower above her standing in lamplight with the crate going up the stair.

She knelt in thaw water with her hands on Du Hong's coat, pulling him clear of the ditch, while the younger man lay still on the road edge and the elder scrambled up shouting for the Watch.

Merit settled the way heat settles, all at once and without noise. A man working an entered act, working inside it from the hour he took the claim up, ended by her hand on a road the layer watches. It was not something she had arranged and it was not something she could have bought. Listed long before this hour, read off an announcement weeks ago and filed, and the only thing her killing did was settle a thing that had been standing in the bound since the autumn.

“You feel it,” said Yuan. “I am here and I do not act without you.”

“I feel it,” said Ning Yan through blood in her mouth. “Tell me before you spend. That is the term.”

“I tell you now,” said Yuan. “It costs you and it buys me. It comes from your foundation and it does not return. I ask you and I wait.”

“What does it take,” said Ning Yan. “Say it plain.”

“The left-hand order,” said Yuan. “What you build in the cold. Hook and turn and settle. I take the order and I leave the strength. You say yes or you say no.”

She knelt in thaw water with Du Hong breathing ragged under her hands and the dead man cooling ten feet off, and understood what he asked. Sequence, not substance. The technique and the order, not the body's capacity. Practice rebuilds strength and cannot rebuild order.

She thought about four months. Four weeks of stone and water and paper, the lath across her knees in the mud, the sequence running cleaner each night, the night she had built it out of load the way stage eight had been built, and the two minutes at a stage she had said yes to knowing that the one thing she had made out of the whole winter would go out of her legs to pay for eleven weeks of a man she could not stop dying.

“Yes,” said Ning Yan. “Spend it. I say yes.”

He spent it. The left-hand sequence rebuilt over winter went out of her the way order goes, entire, unrelearnable. Hook, turn, settle — gone as order, held as ache. Nothing she loses comes back.

It did not feel like losing an arm. Her legs were exactly as strong as they had been that morning and exactly as capable, and she sat in the water and found that she could no longer remember which of the two men was the one she had to turn. Not the shape. The order. The first move of it was gone and what was left was four years of a furnace floor that had taught her to move before she thought and had not taught her a single thing she could write down.

The channel steadied. Thin to whole. Past nine-tenths back to roughly two-thirds. About eleven weeks of ordinary use where five days had stood that morning. Third term paid in full, four months late. First time he asks instead of acting.

[The road at Lowwater. One of the entered is ended by her hand.]

She sat in thaw water with her dead arm under her and her living hand on Du Hong's chest, and felt the rebuilt winter leave her legs while strength stayed in them, useless without its order.

Feng came down the tower stair with a lamp, coughing, running, old and unbowed. Carriers shouted. The elder man backed toward the counting lane with the file still in his hands, shouting that the place was refused and the body proved nothing.

Du Hong breathed. Ragged, wet, alive. Blood ran from his mouth into thaw water. His burned sheet blackened and broke. Her copies stayed in his coat, wet through.

She had killed a man to stop him killing another, with habit not technique, one working arm, and it had very nearly killed her. That was the whole of it, and it had bought eleven weeks.
