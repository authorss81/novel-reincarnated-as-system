# The Conditions

She stood in the middle of the square with it in her coat and let the paper be a paper for the length of it, which in Lowwater is about the time it takes a clerk to cross a wet yard.

Then she took it out and read it in the open, on the benches by the well, in the rain, because a thing that is a secret on a first day is a thing somebody else finds out about on the second, and she would rather a market read it off her hand than go and buy it.

Four of the six were the shape of every licence that had ever come out of that office. The ceiling. The Foundation barred. The defect entered. The hours on a schedule returned at the quarter.

The first was the one Du Hong had bought with his name, and she had gone and got it back in her own hand at the county office at the third desk six weeks ago, and read back to herself, and she had never once thought it would come back on the front of a document in words like that.

*No surety may stand for the applicant again without relicensing.*

Fifteen years, maybe. A woman who is in a room at every hour of every month for fifteen years does not die in a bed. And the only man who had ever offered to stand in a room for her had been beaten into thaw water for it two months ago at the foot of a tower four miles down this road.

The sixth one was on the back in the county's own hand and it was eight hundred jin.

“It is the price of the stamp,” said Yuan.

“It is the price of the stamp,” said Ning Yan. “He said so. He put it in writing on the day he gave me the thing it was refusing me for.”

“You are carrying a bounty on a licence.”

“I am carrying a bounty on a licence in the county's own hand, in the sixth condition, entered under a claim that has been standing in four counties since the autumn and that has never once been answered, and it is going up the quarter in the crate with everything else on it.”

“That is the sixth condition,” said Yuan. “Read it again.”

“No surety may stand for her again. Hours on one schedule only. Practice at or below five. No Foundation without relicensing. The defect restores nothing. The claim travels with it.”

“Nothing in those six puts you at a door that a man with a tube can use, until the next one,” said Yuan. “The next one is a licensee taking paid work outside the crew. That is in the scope line and it is the whole of what this licence buys.”

“And a woman who takes paid work outside the crew is a woman who goes where the work is, at the hours the work is, on the road the work is on. She will be seen doing it, by people who know what she is, in a town with a counting lane in it.”

“Then I take the work on the schedule.”

“You take the work on the schedule. And in nine weeks’ time when you are in a lane at the third hour carrying somebody else’s stone, there is a licensed practitioner with a paper in her coat and a standing attached to it, and the counting room has both halves of you in one hand for the first time since the market day.”

She folded it and put it in the lining, in the place the filing copy had worn a soft patch in eleven weeks, and it went into the same soft patch, and the coat had two documents in it now instead of one and there was a third place where a word used to be that was not a document and was not going to be ever again.

The good coat was at the second desk with the boards half in.

He was not hurrying and he was not leaving. He stood at the second desk with his tube in his hand and not a coin on the wood, in the rain, at the end of a day when a queue had gone past that desk and out of that door and through that office and had walked back out of it holding a county document, and he had been in the square the whole time.

He had seen her come out. He had been forty yards away at a shuttered board and he had not come over and he had not gone away, and he had not gone away all afternoon, and she understood standing there in the wet that this was a man who had watched a thing happen and had been patient enough not to be in the room for it.

He had had the two cheap ones since the ninth day of the winter. He had a place, and he had a name, and every hand that fed her or gave her a mat had been sheltering something the whole bound could name.

What he had never had was the third.

Every month since the autumn she had been a woman in a room, a woman at a table, a woman at a trough, and every one of those things is a description of a woman and not a piece of paper. A piece of paper is what eight hundred jin is bought with, because a broker does not buy a body in a lane. He buys a county line with a county hand on it.

And at the second hour of that afternoon a registrar had put one in her hand.

“Six weeks of the season's road is the last of it for this year,” said Yuan. “The pass shuts in the weather. The cart days are counted and there is no carrying after the last one without a writ for the pass, and a woman with a licence does not get one, because the licence says practice and not travel.”

“Good,” said Ning Yan.

“It is not good.”

“It is not good and it is mine,” said Ning Yan, “and I have been in a house with a tower over it for three months, and I have not had to think about where I will be in the autumn, and that is not nothing, whatever else it is.”

She looked at him across the wet square and he did not go back to the wall. He stood at the second desk with his tube in his hand and put a coin on the wood, which was a waste, because the desk was shut and the clerks were gone, and then he picked the coin up again and put it back in his pocket, which was the first thing she had ever seen him do that was not part of a trade.

Then he went home, and there was nothing to do about him, because a man who has been handed the paper does not need to stand on a wall about it.

That was the bargain and it was the shape of the whole winter: she had bought her own advancement with a licence that named her to the one man in nine counties who was paid to be able to name her, and the piece of paper that said she could practise above a servant was the piece of paper that told a broker where to put the rest of his money.

She went down the lane past the copying floor where Feng was working late, in a good coat bought in Hanhui, and she went up the tower stair to the loft and put the licence in the lining with the coat for a blanket and lay on the mat and worked out the whole of what she was.

## The Count

There was a calendar on the wall of the loft and she had been keeping it since the ninth day of the winter in a hand nobody can read, and it was a laundry-man's calendar, a month at a time with the days in squares.

She did it on her back with the lamp turned away.

The hours: returned to the county at the quarter. That is four weeks off the last day of the thaw, and the clerk takes what comes in the crate in the order the crate is stacked, and the crate goes up on the day and not the one before, so the working count is five weeks, and Sun He reads a return in the first week after it and does not hurry and has told her so on two occasions in the last hour.

The condition on the ceiling: she is at five and the licence says nothing above it, so there is nothing above it to count.

The Foundation: barred without relicensing, and relicensing is a new application, and a new application needs a schedule and a schedule needs an hours column and her hours are the furnace's hours, and the furnace does not stop.

The claim: standing, unanswered, travelling with the paper, and no office in nine counties has a form with a column for a thing that happened to a man in a ditch because of an act entered in a layer, and it will be standing at the first return and at the second and at every return after that until somebody puts a body and a place and a name on it, and there are eight of those left and every one of them is further away and dearer.

The first return is the fifth week of summer.

The season's road shuts with the weather and there is no carrying after the last cart day without a writ, and a licence does not carry a writ, and the pass is four miles up and iced shut by the first snow and open again by nobody until it is open.

She lay on the mat and put the arithmetic against the calendar and it came out the way arithmetic always comes out when you do it honestly, which is that it is very tight and it is not going to be got by hurrying.

“You have got what you wanted,” said Yuan.

“I have got it.”

“Say it.”

“I have got what I wanted,” said Ning Yan. “From the sixth of December to the last day of the thaw, and I got it with a stamp and a seal and a dead man’s name crossed out and my own defect in my own hand and a bounty in the county's writing as the last thing on the back of it, and the man who was going to buy me was standing in the rain watching the door I came out of.”

“That is what it is.”

“Yes,” said Ning Yan. “That is what it is. And I would have signed it in the same hour at that table in the same weather, and I would sign it again tomorrow, and I am not going to pretend to you tonight that I have got something I can put down.”

“Then what have you got.”

“I have got a room,” said Ning Yan. “Five stages, a schedule, a quarter, and a document that says I may practise in the open for the first time in my life.”

“I am seventeen years old and I am lying in a tower above a town with a man on the wall who has just been handed the best piece of paper I will ever own. I buried Du Hong two months ago, and the fourth stage of my life is in nobody's book, and it cost me nine days of not eating, and I have not forgiven you.”

“You have not forgiven me.”

“No,” said Ning Yan. “Not yet. Not for a long time.”

“And I am going up that road in four days with a licence in my coat and three hundred and eleven hours of a furnace's schedule in my hands and a crew that will not sign my shift. I am going to be a legal practitioner for the first time in my life at the age of seventeen, in a room with a roof off.”

“Nobody in nine counties is going to be glad about it. Including me. Including him.”
