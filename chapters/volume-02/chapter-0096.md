# Kiln-Born

The tower keeps volumes the county never sees, bound in oilcloth, stacked by year where the stove never quite warms them.

Ning Yan learned them carrying paper for Feng, setting loads on the intake table and taking sealed copies away. On the ninth morning after the stage the copying floor ran short-handed, and Feng set her to dust the high shelf where the old registers sit, because a hand is a hand and dust is dust.

“You dust,” said Feng without looking up. “You do not copy. You do not take.”

“I dust,” said Ning Yan.

She dusted with her left hand, breathing two counts, moving bound spines without opening them. It is a strange thing to be trusted with and she understood at once that it was not trust. It was that Feng had two clerks who could copy and one pair of hands that could lift, and a woman who could be watched, and a tower where a watched pair of hands was worth more than an unwatched one.

The high shelf was warmer than the floor. The stove sat at the far end with its door shut and the copies going up in a crate by the stair, and the old registers stood where the heat had never reached, in nine ranks by year, bound in oilcloth gone the colour of weak tea.

The third volume lay open where a clerk had left it for the light, pages thin as lamp skin. She did not mean to read. Reading is a statement, and she had made her statements for the season.

She looked at it while she dusted the shelf it sat on, and then she put the cloth down and stood entirely still.

The page held columns. Name, county, stage, year. And a fifth column with no heading, narrow as a finger, carrying one word per line.

She stood with the dust cloth in her hand and looked, because looking is not taking.

Eleven lines across the pages she could see, spaced across years she could count on her fingers and lose. Six hundred years. Eleven entries. Each with a status word in the fifth column. Each word in a copying hand nobody living uses, strokes hooked the old way, ink brown at the edges.

She did not touch the page. She counted what she could see from where she stood, and then she went along the shelf and counted the gaps between the ranks of years, because a volume bound by year and left open in a light is a book somebody uses and the marks of use are in the bindings and not on the pages.

Four ranks stood out of nine. Four volumes had been opened often enough that the oilcloth had gone back at the corners.

Eleven entries. Four volumes opened. Six hundred years.

Then she understood that the eleventh line was hers, and that it was not in ink.

It was a line ruled across the page in pencil, pressed hard, with no name on it and no county and no stage and no year, and it sat at the end of the run of years where the last entry was four hundred years back. Somebody had ruled a place for a next one and ruled it in pencil because pencil is what you use for something you expect to erase.

That line had been there before the sponsorship. It had been there before the claim. It had been there in a tower nobody had thought to lock against a girl with a cloth in her hand, in a year not written down.

She moved the cloth and did not turn a page. Turning is taking.

The nearest line before hers — a name she did not know, a county she had never walked, a stage five hundred years gone — carried two words in that narrow column where the others carried one line each.

The second word stopped her breath worse than the stair.

*kiln-born.*

She stood there until the coughing under the floorboards moved and made the floorboards move and she had to hold the shelf.

Then she did the thing she had trained herself to do, which was to finish the work she had been given. She dusted the rest of the high shelf, rank by rank, and she counted the spines while she did it and there were four hundred and some, and she did not open one, and she did not read a word she had not already seen.

She set the cloth down and took sealed copies to the town without hurrying, delivered to the second door, took the receipt mark, walked back. At the well she stood a long time with the yoke across her shoulders, working the word the way she works tallies, without moving her face.

*kiln-born.* Not kiln-made. Not kiln-born as the nine households were born into debts that were not theirs. A word in a status column in a dead hand, on the line before hers, and a ruled pencil line with nothing in it waiting for hers.

In the evening she asked Feng for the county sheet of her own advancement, because the filing needs stages recorded and the county sheet is the record.

He gave it without comment. Four counties' sheets lay in the tower as hand-copies behind the fifth item, walled, entered none upward. Lowwater's sheet of her stage three carried the advancement in the county hand, fair and final. After it, in a second position no other county's sheet carried, in a smaller hand, one word.

*kiln-born.*

Of the four hand-copies in the tower, one carried it. Three did not. The one that carried it was the Lowwater copy, in the same brown-edged strokes as the volumes.

She stood in the copying floor among five tables and understood what she held. First new fact about the eleven, on paper. Eleven entries, eleven status words, six hundred years, a copying hand nobody living uses. The nearest before hers named the same word the county had set beside her own advancement where no other county set anything.

The county had put it there. Not the layer. Not the sponsors. A clerk at a desk in Lowwater, in a second position under an advancement that had been standing three months, writing a word out of a book six hundred years old in a hand that had not been alive since before the county kept books at all.

She thought about the first desk on the square, where a young clerk with ink to the wrist had looked up at the good coat and put his brush down flat. Somebody in this town had written that word. Somebody had been handed it, the way a clerk is handed a thing to write, and had written it small in a second position below the one anybody reads.

She climbed to the loft with the word ticking behind her eyes. Du Hong slept below through the boards. Feng coughed and copied.

She spoke it once, aloud, alone, to him.

“*Kiln-born*,” said Ning Yan.

Silence. The weight with no hands, listening.

“What is it,” said Yuan. “I hear it and I do not know it.”

“You do not know it,” said Ning Yan. It was not a question.

“I do not know it,” said Yuan. “I have no word for it and I do not hide one. It is new to me.”

She lay with that for a long time. He does not recognise it and says so. No shelter in it, no danger yet, only paper older than pursuit with a word on it in a dead hand, and a word that a living clerk copied out of it into a county sheet three months after she was named in it.

“He does not know it,” she said aloud, to the boards. “And a man who has been in the bound since before the county kept books does not know it. That means it is not in the layer. It is only in the paper.”

Nothing in the room answered her.

She wrote nothing of the word anywhere. Writing makes paper and paper travels upward. She added to the docket in wash code only: looked, eleven, column, one word, Lowwater second position, one of four, four volumes opened. No word itself. Two heads hold it and no paper does.

“You hold it with me,” said Yuan. “You carry it and you do not spend it.”

“I carry it,” said Ning Yan. “We hold it. That is all.”

Below, the brush went fair and steady. The column stayed at five. Four copies walled behind the fifth item, entered none upward. No audit. No Lung. The hand nobody living uses stayed unrecognised, and she did not ask Feng to recognise it, because asking would spend what paper had just given her for nothing.

The word sat beside the safe-side sentence and did not answer it.
