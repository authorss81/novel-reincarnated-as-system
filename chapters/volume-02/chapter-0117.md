# Nailed Above the Second Bunk

The second bunk in the crew quarters had a board over it four feet long and the quarters had eleven other boards like it, and the foreman did not take the one down, and she nailed the licence up over the foot of her own bunk on the sixth evening with a nail she bought off a boy in the lane and it went in crooked because she has one arm.

It went up crooked and it stayed crooked. It was four feet of oilcloth-backed paper with a county stamp on it and six conditions written on the back and it was the first thing any of them had seen on a wall in that room in three years, which was the tithe-sheet a woman had entered into the cover of a book in the winter and nobody had looked at that either.

Ten slept in twelve bunks. Nine of them worked the furnace and the tenth did not, and she had been on the settling floor that week and for a month before it because the floor was short again, and nobody had any idea that this was connected to anything.

The crew came in at the second night hour and stopped.

Nine men in a coal-smelling room in twelve bunks looking at a sheet of county paper nailed crooked over a servant's bunk, and one of them said, out loud, in the plain voice of a man reading a thing he cannot make sense of:

“Licence to Practise Past Registration.”

It got to the middle of the room and stopped there.

“It is hers,” said the foreman from the doorway. “You can read it. It is nailed up where a man can read it. That is what nails are for.”

“Condensing five,” said one of the men. “It says five.”

“It says five.”

“We were not done with three,” said the same man, and he was not talking about the paper, and there was a silence in the room after it that went on for a count and a half.

Ning Yan came in at the eighth hour with the coat open and a splash of mud up the front of it and put her hand on the frame of the bunk and looked at the nine of them.

“Three of the four you are not done with was mine to tell you about and I did not,” she said, “and you are all standing in a room with a piece of county paper in it now, and it says what I am and not what I am worth, and that is exactly what paper is, and it is what I wanted for four months and it cost a man his life and I put him in the ground myself and wrote the causes down in my own hand.”

The foreman did not move out of the doorway.

“You are not signing anyone's shift,” he said.

“No,” said Ning Yan. “I am not asking and nobody here is going to and we can all stop standing about it.”

She got up into the bunk. The lamp was at the foot of the stair and ten slept in a room that had held twelve three winters ago, and the quarters had not had a roof issue and had not had a beam issue and had had two funerals and a killing, and the boards over the bunks were the only thing in the room that had not changed.

Up the mountain at the same hour, the Hall had no roof and the rain went through it in four places onto a floor with a table at the west end, and the water in the low corner had joined another pool and the second pool had joined the wall.

Down the road at Lowwater, the Lantern Register tower was lit from the inside, and a fair hand was copying upward in a bundle that went at the quarter, and in a bundle that did not go anywhere at all, on the last page of a fair volume taken down off a shelf that was warmer than the floor, was one line in a small hand.

*Stage five, at Qinghe, announced. Witnessed a countersignature at the county office at Lowwater. Copy of the announcement struck under request from without the bound. Entered upward with the quarter. Entered here. Not entered in the column.*

The column stood at five where it had stood since the market day. The four county copies stayed walled behind the fifth item. Nobody in this bound had audited this tower in twenty-two years and was going to start, because a licence got stamped in a county office with a man's name at the bottom of the witness line.

That was the whole of what Feng Lushi had done in the spring, and there is a line in a fair hand at the back of a fair volume in a tower four miles down the road, which is the only place in nine counties where any of it is written down.

She lay in the second bunk with the arm out to the side and the lamp at the foot of the stair and the licence crooked above her feet.

The plate was against her chest. It had been against her chest since the sixth day of the winter and it would be against her chest when she died.

The four counties' registers of Qinghe and three other counties still read *reconciled* for a brother who was thirteen when he was taken for the kiln, alive, on a list four days' road beyond the Wall. The truth of him had not changed by one letter, and there was a plate on her ribs that would outlast her.

Du Hong was under the ground at the tower's foot on the far side of town. Cao Lei and Xu An were under the ground at a quarter past the furnace in the winter mud, with nine lines in a burial register written by a clerk of about seventy who had never once written *fell* in that column.

Four of the nine who slept in this room had signed the sheet that had put two names onto a doubled shift, and two of those four were dead, and the ninth man in the doorway was one of them. He was standing there on her account and he knew it and she knew it and neither of them said it, and that was the arrangement.

The three sponsors held a Foundation writ that a stamped sheet had killed, in a corridor with a dry awning at the west end, and they knew, and they had said nothing at all, and they would go on saying nothing at all, which was the safest thing three men in nine counties could possibly do.

Shen Xiu had closed the winter book on the last day of the thaw with three hundred and eleven hours entered in a column of his own and eleven days of her absence in the gap between the rows where a clerk's own absences go, and he had signed it looking past her and had not asked her a single question, and would not for the rest of his life.

Hou Sanliang was sitting in Lowwater with a piece of county paper he had not paid for, at a desk he owned, holding paper on three of nine households, and he was not in a hurry, and he was not owed by anybody and no one was going to come and tell him anything. His man had not been paid in a month and had gone home and would go back. The eight hundred jin stood and answered nothing.

The girl of twenty-four was in a wash house four miles down the road with a leg that had set crooked, copying lists for the eighth estate, charging a county request to her own hour in a return sheet, and she was alive, and she was the safest thing in that town and had been since a winter she did not describe.

Two heads held a word six hundred years old. One of them had said it out loud alone on a loft floor in the spring and had been told by a man with no hands that he had no word for it and never had, and it was now on no paper in nine counties at the instruction of an office in a bound they could not read the name of, and would never be on paper again.

Yuan's channel stood a little under three-quarters with about six weeks of ordinary use in it.

She had counted that on the mat at Lowwater, out of a clock she cannot see and cannot check, against a calendar on the wall, and she had done it twice, and the figure had not moved, because it is the only number in this year that has not moved once when she looked at it.

“You are not sleeping,” said Yuan.

“I am listening to nine men breathe.”

“That is not a reason and you know it is not a reason.”

“It is the reason I have,” said Ning Yan. “Go quiet. You have been talking since the mud flat and I have not asked you to stop and I am not going to, but I am asking you to go quiet, because I have not had an hour in this year where nothing wanted anything of me, and I would like one before the first return.”

He went quiet. She lay in the second bunk in a room with ten sleepers and a crooked licence over her feet and the rain going through a roof four hundred yards off, and she slept, and it was the first whole night she had had since a man died at the foot of a tower.

Down the mountain in the dark the tower lamp went on burning in its cage, and the crate went up the stair at the quarter, and the season's road shut with the weather behind it, and there was a thing she still had to count before the rain stopped.
