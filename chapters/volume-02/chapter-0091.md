# Four Weeks of Stone

The next three weeks went into her legs the way the flats had once gone into stage eight, out of weight.

Ning Yan carried stone for the tower wall, oil for the lamp, paper for the copying floor, water for the stove. The loads built out of the schedule, two a day entered as labour, and the intake clerk entered them without comment. The left-hand form settled into the hips under weight, and the leak ran sideways into the pattern instead of out, cold at the rim, familiar as a coat seam.

Her arm came back eleven minutes in the middle of a block and served and went. She stopped testing it more than twice a day, morning and night, the way a person stops knocking on a door that opens a third of the time. A third was what it was. It stayed a third through frost and then through thaw mud, and she built the working rhythm round it and did not ask it for more.

“You build well,” said Yuan. “You carry much and you complain little.”

“I carry what the schedule carries,” said Ning Yan. “You watch the edge and you tell me true.”

“I watch,” said Yuan. “I tell you true and I hold nothing back.”

The breath stayed short at two counts. The stairs cost one stop. Night drill in the yard behind the tower went to the lath for a hook, frost on the boards, then mud sucking at the soles. The rebuilt sequence from the winter — hook, turn, settle, carry — ran cleaner each night, unwritten, unshared, remembered in the legs where no clerk looks.

Feng coughed worse through the boards. The copying floor ran quiet with brushes. The four county copies sat walled behind the fifth item, entered none upward, column at five. The buyer came once to the tower stair and asked which hand keeps the Qinghe advancement, and Feng told him the county office keeps it and the tower copies upward, both true, and took no coin because he was asked nothing he writes.

Du Hong carried tower letters and furnace sheets openly, noticed and unbowed. The younger man with the good coat walked behind carriers twice, asking after hours by description rather than by name. A hand with a file, tower letters, flour on the sleeves. Ning Yan changed her carrying hours and walked no lane twice, and told Du Hong to walk the upper lane after dark no more, and he laughed short and said the upper lane is where the soup cools fastest.

The girl of twenty-four sent willow tea up with the water cart and a slate copied fair with four carrier lines corrected. Alive, splinted, off the floor for the season, holding the bunk secret with both hands. Ning Yan drank the tea left-handed on the mat and did not write thanks, because thanks between people who know the cost is done by not saying it.

Stamp days came and went. She attended every one with Du Hong beside her, stood through the void-and-transfer reading without flinching, signed the hours, took the mark. The filing copy thickened with marks. Hours, stages, defect, all on the county sheet from the day the book opened. The paper was used, not admired.

On the last night of the fourth week she drilled the sequence in mud that took the sole and gave nothing back, and felt the edge come up under the feet the way it had come on the settling floor before stage two. Heat held past comfort. Breath held at two counts and did not break. The crack sat as a mouth, open and shut, permanent.

She stopped with the lath across her knees and sat in mud till the chest settled.

“It is here,” she said quietly.

“What is here,” said Yuan.

“The third,” said Ning Yan. “Built out of load, as the eighth was. Four weeks of stone and water and paper. I take it at my order or I let it take me. I take it.”

“You want it now,” said Yuan. “You say it and you mean it.”

“I want it announced,” said Ning Yan. “I pay for the noise. The market learns where I sleep and I sleep here still. That is the price and I pay it.”

“You pay much,” said Yuan. “I am with you and I let it go at your word.”

She washed the mud off at the trough behind the tower with her left hand, hung dead right in the sleeve, and climbed once to the loft. The shutter was still where Du Hong had nailed it. The square noise came thin through the gap. Below, Feng’s brush went fair and steady.

She lay on the mat and made the decision the way she made all of them, out loud, alone, in numbers. Third stage. Her order. Announced. Capped at five, defect entered, surety living beside it. The bound told again what she has become, four months after it was last told where she sleeps.

Fear sat beside her with the arithmetic, and she did not send either away.
