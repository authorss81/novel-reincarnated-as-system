# Four Weeks of Stone

The next three weeks went into her legs the way the flats had once gone into stage eight, out of weight.

Ning Yan carried stone for the tower wall, oil for the lamp, paper for the copying floor, water for the stove. The loads built out of the schedule, two a day entered as labour, and the intake clerk entered them without comment. The left-hand form settled into the hips under weight, and the leak ran sideways into the pattern instead of out, cold at the rim, familiar as a coat seam.

Her arm came back eleven minutes in the middle of a block and served and went. She stopped testing it more than twice a day, morning and night, the way a person stops knocking on a door that opens a third of the time. A third was what it was. It stayed a third through frost and then through thaw mud, and she built the working rhythm round it and did not ask it for more.

“You build well,” said Yuan. “You carry much and you complain little.”

“I carry what the schedule carries,” said Ning Yan. “You watch the edge and you tell me true.”

“I watch,” said Yuan. “I tell you true and I hold nothing back.”

The breath stayed short at two counts. The stairs cost one stop. Night drill in the yard behind the tower went to the lath for a hook, frost on the boards, then mud sucking at the soles. The rebuilt sequence from the winter — hook, turn, settle, carry — ran cleaner each night, unwritten, unshared, remembered in the legs where no clerk looks.

Feng coughed worse through the boards. The copying floor ran quiet with brushes. The four county copies sat walled behind the fifth item, entered none upward, column at five. The buyer came once to the tower stair and asked which hand keeps the Qinghe advancement, and Feng told him the county office keeps it and the tower copies upward, both true, and took no coin because he was asked nothing he writes.

Du Hong carried tower letters and furnace sheets openly, noticed and unbowed. The younger man with the good coat walked behind carriers twice, asking after hours by description rather than by name. A hand with a file, tower letters, flour on the sleeves. Ning Yan changed her carrying hours and walked no lane twice, and told Du Hong to walk the upper lane after dark no more, and he laughed short and said the upper lane is where the soup cools fastest.

The stamp days came on the ninth and the twenty-third, and she attended both with Du Hong beside her and stood through the void-and-transfer reading without flinching and signed the hours and took the mark, and the filing copy thickened with two more dates in the registrar's hand.

On the ninth the reading had gone over a man she had carried for in the autumn, whose entry was struck in the third column and whose hours were transferred to a household on the far side of the water. She had put a hand on that house before she thought about it and taken it off again. The registrar read the whole column before he read the terms, which took four minutes and gave a queue time to think about a fourth man.

On the twenty-third the transfer was a whole column of them, nine entries at the county's own pace, and she stood through all nine and learned that the county could empty a household out of a book and put it somewhere else without asking the household.

That was not new information. It was the first time she had watched it happen to people she had carried for.

The girl of twenty-four sent willow tea up with the water cart and a slate copied fair with four carrier lines corrected. Alive, splinted, off the floor for the season, holding the bunk secret with both hands. Ning Yan drank the tea left-handed on the mat and did not write thanks, because thanks between people who know the cost is done by not saying it.

On the sixteenth she went down to the wash house herself instead of waiting for the cart, because there was a thing she wanted to see with her own eyes.

The girl had her leg up on a stool and the lath-and-cord splint gone hard and grey, and she was copying a carrier's list onto her slate with the wrong hand, because the right one was the one that had gone. She did not stop copying when Ning Yan came in.

“You have four hours twice,” said Ning Yan, over her shoulder. “Both on the fourth. The cart went out at the seventh hour and came back at the eleventh.”

“They go out at the seventh and come back at the eleventh,” said the girl. “That is twice. You wrote it as four, and a four is a thing somebody has to answer for.”

“Write two.”

“I wrote two,” said the girl. “I am copying it fair for the eighth estate so that nobody answers for it twice.”

Ning Yan stood a while in the smell of the wash house with the steam up off the trough. “The gate man came asking again. Before the market.”

“He came asking,” said the girl. “I was in the water up to the knee and I am slow getting out of it. I told him the mat is on the fourth step and the fourth step is where everyone in this town sleeps. Then I went back in the water.”

“That was the right answer,” said Ning Yan.

“It was the true one,” said the girl. “There is no other kind now that the layer has said it.”

On the last night of the fourth week she drilled the sequence in mud that took the sole and gave nothing back, and felt the edge come up under the feet the way it had come on the settling floor before stage two. Heat held past comfort. Breath held at two counts and did not break. The crack sat as a mouth, open and shut, permanent.

She stopped with the lath across her knees and sat in mud till the chest settled.

“It is here,” she said quietly.

“What is here,” said Yuan.

“The third,” said Ning Yan. “Built out of load, as the eighth was. Four weeks of stone and water and paper. I take it at my order or I let it take me. I take it.”

“You want it now,” said Yuan. “You say it and you mean it.”

“I want it announced,” said Ning Yan. “I pay for the noise. The market learns where I sleep and I sleep here still. That is the price and I pay it.”

“You pay much,” said Yuan. “I let it go at your word and not a moment before.”

She sat in the mud with the arithmetic standing beside the arithmetic, because there were two sums and they did not agree. Four weeks of carrying would be in the county sheet as hours. The stage would be in the county sheet as a stage, and a stage has to be written before it is entered or the entry is a report of something that never happened. She could have him let it go and take it into the crack unannounced, and the stage would sit in her body with no paper on it, and the filing would go on counting hours like a book with a page torn out that nobody had asked for.

That was the whole of what she had to decide, and it took her longer than the stage would.

She washed the mud off at the trough behind the tower with her left hand, hung dead right in the sleeve, and climbed once to the loft. The shutter was still where Du Hong had nailed it. Below, Feng’s brush went fair and steady, and the coughing under the floorboards had a new note in it that she did not like.

She lay on the mat and made the decision the way she made all of them, out loud, alone, in numbers. Third stage. Her order. Announced. Entered before the stamp, not after. Capped at five, defect entered, surety living beside it. The bound told again what she has become, four months after it was last told where she sleeps.

Fear sat beside her with the arithmetic, and she did not send either away.
