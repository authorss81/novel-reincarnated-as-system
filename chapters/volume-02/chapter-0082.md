# The Barrier Escort

She went down to stay on the fifty-eighth day, with the tithe sheet signed through the week and the winter book at four hundred and sixty-eight worked.

The barrier at the fourth bridge stood in a cut full of morning shadow with frost on the trestle. The board carried the captain's line in its roundhouse hand, no order on it, no clerk's name against it. The Watchman at the rail knew her by sight the way the whole mountain knew her, and nodded her to the water cart without ceremony.

“On the sheet,” he said. “Tenth hour. Daylight. The Watch watches the cart to the town.”

“On the sheet,” said Ning Yan. “I ride the boards.”

Old Gao had the barrels loaded and the sheet open on the rail. He entered her as a carrier's hand with his own mark beside it, in daylight, before the Watchman. The grey-templed captain's man stood up the slope with his hat on, not looking, the way he never looks. She felt his not-looking and set it aside, because a sheet with her name on it going down at the tenth hour is a thing he can buy and cannot lift her off.

“You ride down and you stay down,” said Old Gao quietly. “The town has ears and the tower has a mat. Come up for crew days entered, go down entered. Nothing unrecorded.”

“Nothing unrecorded,” said Ning Yan. “I learned that in a burial register.”

The cart went through the cut with the Watchman walking the rail above it to the town boundary. Four miles of white rutted road, ice at the shaded turns, mud where the sun hit. She rode the boards with her feet braced and her coat shut, breathing two counts against the cold, watching the mountain fall away.

“You fear it still,” said Yuan. “You want it and you fear it.”

“I fear it,” said Ning Yan. “I ride it. That is the difference between fear and stopping.”

“You ride it well,” said Yuan. “I see you and I stay with you.”

## Lowwater Ground

The town came up with its smoke and its noise. The square ran full with market carts, carriers shouting, clerks at three desks under the awning. The county office stood on the north side with its shutters open, queue forming, stamp going down steady as a hammer. The Lantern Register tower stood above the roofs with its lamp cage dark at midday, copying floor inside where Feng writes.

She got off the boards in mud for the first time to stay, entered on the cart sheet, watched but not guarded. The Watchman turned the cart without looking at her again. Water had gone down. Water went back up. That was all it was.

Du Hong met her at the tower door with a spare mat under his arm and flour on his sleeves.

“Loft,” he said. “Feng says the stair costs breath. Climb once.”

“I climb once,” said Ning Yan.

She climbed once, with stops, and lay on the mat till the chest settled. The loft held a pallet, a shutter that did not shut, and a view of the square through the gap.

Below, the copying floor ran quiet: brushes, paper, Feng's cough. Four of her county copies sat walled behind the fifth item in that room, entered none upward, column at five. The county office on the square kept a different book with a different hand, where Sun He stamps. She held the distinction the way she holds tallies, because merging them is how men get lost.

In the evening she went to the wash trough behind the tower and washed the road off. The girl at the wash house up the mountain copied slates with a lath splint. The thought came and she set it aside, because carrying everyone up the mountain down to Lowwater is how the arithmetic breaks.

“How does the household stand,” Du Hong asked over soup.

“Four hundred and sixty-eight worked,” said Ning Yan. “Four hundred and seventy-two standing. Seventy-two in my own hand.”

He whistled low. “Spring, then. As he said.”

“Spring,” said Ning Yan. “If the hours hold and the book squares. The book squares on estates not mine.”

She worked tower loads the next three days, stone and paper and oil, entered on the schedule as labour. The hours went up by the floor sheet every third evening, and the clerk wrote them slow. She climbed the stair once a day and drilled the left-hand form in the yard at night, two counts in, weight low, hand leading. The breath stayed short. The stripe stayed cold. The arm came back eleven minutes and served and went.

On the fourth evening she stood in the square with a load on her shoulder and watched the man in the good coat pay at the second desk for the name on carriers' papers. He got a shrug and writing. He paid for the writing. He went to the third desk and asked after the place, and got nothing he could write. He thanked both clerks and walked to the counting lane with his tube on his belt, unhurried, employed.

“You see him,” said Yuan. “You know him and you do not move.”

“I see him,” said Ning Yan. “I know his master keeps a counting room and he keeps hours. I move my hours. That is my answer.”

“Good,” said Yuan. “I hold it with you and I add nothing.”

She shifted the load and went on to the tower with the receipt in her hand. The copying floor took the paper without looking at her, the way it takes all paper, and Feng entered it in the intake list in his fair hand. His brush moved steady and his cough moved under it, wet and regular, the way the furnace breathes under the work.

“How does the stair take you,” he said without looking up.

“Once a day,” said Ning Yan. “I climb once and I do not climb again.”

“Then climb once,” said Feng. “Loft hands that climb twice breathe twice as short.”

She climbed once and lay on the mat till the chest settled. Below, the brushes went on. The four copies sat behind the fifth item in the wall, unread by any office but his, entered none upward. The county office on the square held a different book with a different hand. She kept the two rooms apart in her head the way she keeps tallies apart, because the day she merges them is the day she files in the wrong one.

Four hundred and sixty-eight. Four hundred and seventy-two. On the ground at last, with a tower that copies nothing upward and an office that stamps everything it sees, and a man hunting proofs between them in daylight.

The road behind her ran white up to the cut where the board said held. She had come down entered and in daylight, on a sheet, watched. The claim stood and the body walked. That was the whole of the descent, and it held.
