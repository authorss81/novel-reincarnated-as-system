# Eleven Bodies

She tested his sentence the way she tests tallies, by walking it past paper she could touch.

The morning after the announcement the square talked about the loft over the copying floor the way squares talk about weather, without interest and without stopping. Ning Yan carried water for the tower kitchen past all three desks with her strap across her chest, breathing two counts, and heard her sleeping place named twice before the fifth hour, once wrong and once right.

The buyer stood at the third desk with his tube open, paying for the writing of the absence again, dated, filed. The younger man with him watched hands, not desks, and marked Du Hong's flour sleeves from nine feet.

She did not confront them. Confrontation buys nothing and costs the place she sleeps, and the place was now bought by the layer itself.

“You walk known,” said Yuan. “You carry still and you do not hide.”

“I carry,” said Ning Yan. “I attend. You stay with me.”

“I stay,” said Yuan. “I do not trouble them and you do not either.”

She went down to the wash house at the sixth hour because she had a sentence she wanted to put next to somebody who was not her, and there was one person in Lowwater whose whole situation had been made by a sentence like it.

The girl had her leg up on the stool and was copying a carrier's list with the wrong hand, because the right one was the one that had gone. She did not stop when Ning Yan came in.

“Did you hear it,” said Ning Yan, “over the kettle on the tower?”

“Heard it,” said the girl. “Eleven times since I have been down here. Nine counties, and my sister is in two of them.”

“And?”

“And nothing,” said the girl. “That is the part I have not got used to. Nine counties have said out loud that there is a woman in a loft over a copying floor in Lowwater, and nobody has come down the lane.”

She put the brush down.

“You are asking me whether it is safe,” said the girl. “I have a splint on my leg and no floor to go back to and a household that charges me for the water. I am the safest thing in this town and I have been since the winter you do not know about. Ask me whether it is safe for you. I will answer that and I will be honest, because you did not have to ask.”

Ning Yan stood in the steam off the trough.

“It is not safe,” she said. “That is what I wanted tested.”

“I know it is not,” said the girl. “You told me the whole of it on the first day and you have not once told me a thing that made you sound better. Go on with your day. I have four hours of carriers to correct and I have to be slow at it or the estate gets them wrong.”

“You are,” said Ning Yan.

“I am,” said the girl, and picked the brush up again.

The test she wanted was paper, and paper in Lowwater sat in two buildings that most people confused. The county office on the square kept records of persons and made them plain. The tower kept records of things and made them fair. Nobody confuses them on purpose; the county hand writes large and legible and the tower hand writes small and thinning, and a person in a hurry takes whichever of the two is nearer the street.

She went up to the copying floor at the ninth hour with paper for the intake table and stayed longer than paper required.

Feng sat at the fair-hand desk, coughing into his sleeve, copying steadily. She set the load on the table and stood a moment watching him write, fair and thinning at the edges, twenty-two years of it. Behind him the tall shelves held the volumes bound in oilcloth by year, and above the shelves the stair went up to a loft that nine counties could name.

She wanted to ask about volumes with old hands. She did not ask. The day she asked it sideways would be the day he answered it sideways too, and the sentence she carried needed paper, not asking.

Instead she asked him something she already knew, which is a thing she does when she wants to hear how a man answers when nothing is at stake.

“Does the tower keep anything on the Chosen?”

“The tower keeps what the tower is given,” said Feng, without lifting the brush. “Once a year the offices send up a list of what a person has done. Nine counties send what they have. It goes up again the same day.”

“So the tower is not the record. The tower is the copy.”

“The tower is the copy,” said Feng. “That is the work.”

She left him with that and went and attended the stamp day with Du Hong at the third hour past midday, entered and in daylight.

The queue moved. Sun He sat at the far table with the county book open. When her name came she stood before him with the filing copy, and Du Hong gave his name aloud for the attendance line, and the brush moved.

The registrar read the void-and-transfer terms the way clerks read weather. Du Hong said he knew. The brush moved again.

“Stage three is entered,” said Sun He, not looking up. “Read at the ninth hour. The mark is good.”

“I have read it,” said Ning Yan.

“Then the filing is continuous,” said Sun He, “and the filing only fails when it is broken. Keep it unbroken and I do not need to see you again until spring.”

It was the longest sentence the registrar had ever given her, and it was not kindness. It was a clerk telling a holder how to keep a record clean, and she understood that he had said it because a broken filing would come back to his table and a continuous one would not.

They walked out into market noise with the mark wet. The younger man stood at the lane mouth watching the tower door, unhurried, paid by the week.

“He watches the door now,” said Du Hong quietly. “Not the desks.”

“I know,” said Ning Yan. “Walk the upper lane after dark no more.”

“The soup cools fastest there,” said Du Hong, and did not promise, and she did not ask him to promise, because promises between people who know the cost are done by walking differently, not by saying.

In the evening she carried a sealed copy to the counting lane, delivered to the third door, took the receipt mark, walked back without hurrying. Halfway down she passed the open door with paper stacked by household. The broker's brush never stopped. The man in the good coat reported low, employed, entire. She walked past with the receipt in her hand and heard enough to know the room counts by household and holds paper the way the sponsors hold schedules.

She stood three paces past his door before she understood what she had just heard, and she went back two paces and stood in the lane and listened properly, the way you listen at a furnace door.

Two voices inside. One the good coat's, reading. One younger, repeating. They were going down a list of households and asking, for each, whether any person in that house had come up the road from Qinghe inside the year. Nine households. Two answers of yes so far, both about other people, neither about her.

Then the good coat said a name she did not know and asked what was owed on it, and the younger one answered with a figure and a date, and the brush went on.

So the room was not only hunting her. It was doing the ordinary business of the claim on all eight hundred jin of it, on nine households at a time, and her name was one line in a ledger of nine that had been running since the autumn. That made her smaller, which was a relief, and it made the thing she had overheard a door she could stand in for a while longer.

Back in the loft she added to the docket in wash code: known, watched at door, lane reports, nine households walked, two yes. She did not write his sentence. Writing a sentence makes it paper.

“You hold it,” said Yuan. “You turn it and you do not spend it.”

“I hold it,” said Ning Yan. “Eleven in six hundred years. A record for each because the writ required one. Protection and danger the same line. I keep it without using it.”

“You keep it well,” said Yuan. “I see what you are doing with it.”

She lay on the mat with her coat over her and the wind working at the gap, and the third stage sat crooked in her and the breath pulled short. A floor to stand on, made of her own conclusion hardened under her feet. She stood on it because there was nowhere else to stand while pursued, and she did not trust it, and both were true.

She had attended, marked, been seen, and been written about. Nothing had been answered. The sentence held, and she held it without stepping harder on any board, waiting for paper older than pursuit to answer it or not.
