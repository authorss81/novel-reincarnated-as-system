# Where She Sleeps

She did it in the yard behind the tower where the mud holds footprints till morning, because a stage needs ground and witnesses are a luxury she could not afford.

The night ran cold after thaw rain. Feng’s lamp burned below in its cage. Du Hong slept under her through the boards with his file on the table. The square lay quiet beyond the roofs, desks covered, office shuttered, counting lane barred.

Ning Yan set the lath aside and stood with her feet in mud, left hand loose, right hung dead. She ran the rebuilt sequence once through, hook and turn and settle, and felt heat hold past comfort the way it holds in a furnace child. The crack sat open as a mouth. Breath went to two counts and stayed there.

It was not like the furnace, where a body went up with a witness on every side and somebody's hand could be found afterwards to say it had. Here there was a yard, a wall, a boy asleep under her own floor, and a market eleven streets off that would hear about it by morning and not one of them would have seen a thing.

She had thought about that for four weeks, on every load, and there was no version of it where the noise stayed in the yard. The only choice was whether it went out under her word or under his, and four months ago he had let one go under his own word and she had not forgiven him for it and would not do it to him now.

“Now,” said Ning Yan. “I say it as an order. Let it go.”

“You say it,” said Yuan. “I hear you and I let it go now.”

It went the way stages go, small and unpleasant, a knuckle cracking through a wall. Heat moved and held. The leak ran sideways and did work. Her knees went watery and did not fold. Four weeks of stone and water and paper settled into the body all at once, and the body took it.

The cold in her left ribs stopped being a cost and became a thing she was using, which is a thing the crack was never supposed to be for her, and she felt it happen and did not stop it, because it was the fourth week of a stage and the stage did not care what a thing was meant for. Her right arm came back whole for the length of it. She carried it twice across the yard before she understood she was carrying it, and it weighed nothing, and that was the most frightening part of the whole night.

She sat down in the mud on purpose. Letting it put her down would have taken an hour longer and bought nothing.

She stood in mud with her hands empty and breathed two counts against the cold, and the third stage sat in her the way a load sits after it is set down.

She did not thank him. Thanks was not the shape of the hour.

The sentence went round nine counties while she stood in mud behind a tower. She did not hear it and never would; it was said once, in the place where it is said, and every county took its own copy of it in its own time, in clerks' rooms and market stalls and forge floors, in the words of whoever read it out.

In Lowwater market it arrived with the morning carts, audible, entire, naming the town where she sleeps. The first thing in four months to tell a market where she sleeps.

It named the tower as well. Not the loft, not the bunk, the tower, and there are three towers in Lowwater with a copying floor under them, and one of them keeps a column at five that has not gone up in a month. She had known that when she ordered it. She had known it for four weeks.

She washed at the trough with her left hand and went up to the loft and lay on the mat till the chest settled. The arm hung dead and she did not test it a third time. A third was what it was. It had settled and it stayed there, through the stage and after it.

She slept about two hours with her coat for a blanket and her left hand open on the mat, and woke to the sound of the market saying her name in a tone she had not heard before, which was not curiosity and was not fear and was the sound of a market repricing something it had not known it was holding.

At the eleventh hour she carried stone past the three desks with her face doing nothing. The clerks had heard. The carriers had heard. The man in the good coat stood at the second desk with his tube closed, listening to the square talk about the loft over the copying floor the way squares talk about weather. He did not look at the tower. He did not need to. The layer had looked for him.

Two carriers she had carried for in the autumn nodded to her at the second desk and one of them said, out loud and not unkindly, that he had not known. She said she had not either. Both of those things were true and neither of them helped.

Du Hong met her at the tower door with soup and his face set.

“You ordered it,” he said. “The whole market knows the loft.”

“I ordered it,” said Ning Yan. “Capped at five, defect entered, surety beside it. The filing needs stages recorded. Being legal is the point.”

“You pay for it,” said Du Hong.

“I pay for it,” said Ning Yan. “I sleep here still.”

He put the bowl down on the step and stood there with his hands hanging.

“I want to say one thing,” he said, “and then I will carry water for a week and not say it again.”

“Say it.”

“My name went into a county book in the autumn for a thing you did on a floor. Yesterday a man asked a carrier what the tower hand keeps in his coat. This morning the layer told nine counties where you sleep, in my hearing, over the kettle.” He turned the bowl a quarter turn on the step. “I do not mind the tower. I mind that every one of these is a line with your name at the end of it and I am standing on all of them.”

“I know,” said Ning Yan.

“I know you know,” said Du Hong. “That is the part I cannot get past and I have tried, so I will stop trying.”

He picked the bowl up and put it in her hands and went down to the copying floor without waiting, which was his way of finishing a thing, and which she had learned not to interrupt.

She attended the stamp day that afternoon with him beside her, stood through the reading, signed the hours, took the mark. The filing copy carried the stage now, announced and entered. Sun He wrote it in the stage column with the hour on it and did not look up, and the clerk at the side wrote down that he had read the terms, and eight brush strokes went into a name that had cost a market a morning's work.

The county examined the paper, because that is what a county office does, and the paper was excellent.

In the evening she sat in the loft with the docket in her lining and the square noise coming through the gap, and the arithmetic ticking, and one new fact on top of all of it that no clerk was keeping: every hand that had fed her or given her a mat was now a hand sheltering something the whole bound could name.

“You are known,” said Yuan. “You bear it and you do not hide.”

“I bear it,” said Ning Yan. “I wanted the floor and I have it. You let it go at my word and I carry what it bought.”

“I let it go,” said Yuan. “I stay with you in the noise.”

She slept with her coat for a blanket and her left hand open on the mat. Below, brushes moved and Feng coughed and copied. On the square the desks closed. In the counting lane the broker stacked paper by household, and the employed man reported that the place unbought was now a loft over a copying floor, heard that morning in the layer’s own voice.

That was what the third stage cost. That was what she had ordered.
