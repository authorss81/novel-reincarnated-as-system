# The Safest Thing Here

The girl of twenty-four sat at the end of a bench in the wash house with her leg up on a bucket and forty carriers' sheets under her hand, and she had been told by two separate people that she was not to be working.

“Sit up,” said Ning Yan. “I want the list for the ninth hour and I want it now, and I am not going to stand over a girl who is not supposed to be on her feet.”

“I am on my feet,” said the girl. “I am on them four hours a day and the eighth estate pays for them, and it is not your hours and it is not your sheet. Sit on the other end.”

She sat on the other end. The splint was a lath and a piece of cord and it had been made in a wash house by a washerwoman out of the leg of a bed frame, and it had been on the leg since the thaw before last, and the bone had set crooked and would set no straighter and everybody in the room had made her peace with it in the specific way a room does, which is by never once mentioning it.

“Twenty-two names for the ninth hour and nine for the fifth,” said the girl. “The fifth is a different thing now. Feng changed it. I did not know why and I am not asking.”

“Nobody is asking,” said Ning Yan.

“There is a woman on the fifth hour who used to set loads down slow,” said the girl, “and I have been watching the fifth hour and the ninth for a fortnight, and there is a woman on the ninth who sets them down slower, and there are eleven other women on the ninth, and I counted them twice.” She put the brush down flat. “That was worth telling you. That was the whole of why I got up off the bench.”

She said it the way she said everything, which was as a fact with a number in it that she had checked, and there was nothing else in the room at the time.

“Thank you.”

“Do not thank me,” said the girl. “You are at the eighth hour and I am at the eighth estate and thank you is a thing that goes in a mouth and comes out somewhere.” She pulled the next sheet across. “The county asked for the furnace schedule.”

Ning Yan did not move.

“Asked in writing,” said the girl. “Twice. A county request on the ordinary form, the one any applicant can fill in, asking for the current season's furnace schedule with its hours certified, for attachment to a filing already standing at this office. It came down the road with the quarter's crate and it has been sitting on the shelf in the third room ever since, because nobody in this building knows what a furnace schedule is and nobody wanted to be the one who sent it back wrong.”

“Nobody can send it back wrong. It is not theirs to send.”

“That is what I said,” said the girl. “So I have written it up on the return sheet with the hour on it, and the hour is my hour and not yours, and it goes up the quarter like everything else goes up the quarter, and you cannot stop it and you would not want to.”

She did not look up while she said it.

“Who asked,” said Ning Yan. “The office that asked.”

“The county office,” said the girl. “It does not say which desk. There is no name on the form, because there is no name on the form, because the form does not have one.” She turned the sheet over and showed her the back of it, which was blank, in the ordinary way of the backs of forms. “It came in with the crate. It is not a person. It is a room asking a room for a number.”

She put her hand flat on the return sheet and then took it off, because she was careful about that in a room where sheets went up a stair.

“You have not asked me for anything,” said Ning Yan.

“You have not asked me for anything,” said the girl, “and I have not given you anything. I copy lists. You carry water. Two people in a room going along the same road at the same hour in the same weather is not an arrangement, and I have one of those with the third estate's carrier and I have had it since the autumn and I have never once told anybody in this building about it, and if it were an arrangement I would not have told you either.”

She said that last part looking straight at her, and Ning Yan believed her, which was inconvenient, because a woman who is entirely honest about her own arrangement is a harder kind of person to work with than one who lies.

## Four Hours

“Twenty-two names for the ninth and nine for the fifth,” said the girl, “and the ninth is mine because the ninth is the carrying and I copy what the carrying leaves. Four hours a day. Not six. The eighth estate pays me for four and the estate’s own carrier thinks I do six, and I copy all six for them and get paid for four, and that is the arrangement and it is a good one and I would not do it for a wage.”

“Why.”

“Because the eighth estate cannot check the fifth hour and the carrier cannot check my hours, and both of them would rather have a girl with a leg on a bench than a girl with a leg on a floor asking to be moved.” She turned a sheet over and did not look up. “That is what four hours buys. It is not a kindness and I did not do it for anybody.”

“No,” said Ning Yan. “You did not.”

“And I am not going to be thanked for the return sheet either,” said the girl, “because it took me four minutes and it is my hour.”

“You already told me not to thank you.”

“I tell everybody not to thank me,” said the girl. “It saves the time.”

She had a slate under her arm with the first of the day's carriers on it, and she squared it against the edge of the bench before she went, because everything she touched got squared, and Ning Yan had watched her do it at a desk a month ago and had thought about it since.

“You are at the eighth hour,” said the girl, at the door. “You are always at the eighth hour. It is in the carriers’ sheets and you do not need to tell me anything about yourself, because you write yourself into this town every time you walk through it and you do it in five columns.”

“You are the safest thing in this town,” said Ning Yan.

“I am,” said the girl. “I have been since a winter I do not describe and I am not going to describe it now.”

“Why.”

“Because everyone who comes through that door wants something off a carrier's sheet and nobody in nine months has wanted anything else off me,” said the girl, “and that is worth saying out loud once, and I have said it, and now I want you to take your ninth hour and go.”

She went. At the lane mouth she turned and looked back and the girl had already picked up the brush and was writing a name she had copied fair for the eighth estate because nobody was going to answer for it twice.

Outside the wash house door she stood a moment in the wet and found that she wanted to say thank you and knew exactly why she was not going to.

The girl had told her the county wanted a furnace schedule, and she had put it in the quarter's crate on her own hour, and she had told her in advance that she was not going to be thanked for it.

She had done that so that she would never have to say no to being thanked. Which meant that if Ning Yan said it now, she was taking the one thing the girl had set up to protect.

So she walked to the end of the lane with the sentence still in her mouth, and the girl was still inside, and neither of them had been thanked and neither of them ever would be.

The schedule had to be certified at the Hall's own clerk. That meant Qinghe, which meant four days up and four days down on market days, which meant the barrier at the fourth bridge where a captain who owed her nothing had written her name on a board in his own hand with no order on it and no clerk's name against it, and a road with two men from a counting lane sitting on a wall at the bottom of it and the market opening before the ninth hour.

She could get it certified in the third week of the thaw. The county's return closed at the end of the season's labour and the season did not close until the last week, and a filing with a schedule on it that came in late was a filing that came in late.

That night she did the sum on the loft mat with the arm out to the side and the coat for a blanket, and she made herself write the number down and look at it, which was the only cure she had ever found for a thing she did not want to carry.

Four days up. Four days down. Eight days of road with an eight hundred jin claim standing and a girl in a wash house who had just put a county request in the quarter's crate on her own hour and would be owed nothing for it and had asked for nothing.

And a schedule she could not get in Lowwater, on a furnace floor she was not going to own for the rest of her life, in front of nine men and three men and a clerk who wrote her name in the wrong order because he had no other version.
