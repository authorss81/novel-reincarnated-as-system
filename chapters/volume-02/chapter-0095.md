# What Walks Behind Coin

The pursuit stopped paying for pages and started paying for feet in the week after she was known.

Ning Yan learned it carrying oil for the lamp at the seventh hour. The younger man no longer stood at the desks with the good coat. He walked the lanes carriers walk, asking after hours by description, paying for answers about a hand with tower letters and flour on the sleeves. A hand with a file. A boy eighteen who cannot lie and does not try.

She set the oil down at the tower kitchen and took a paper load instead, because paper loads go by hand after the fifth hour and hands hear more than shoulders.

“You see them,” said Yuan. “You mark them and you do not meet them.”

“I mark them,” said Ning Yan. “Two now. The elder pays. The younger walks. You watch the door with me.”

“I watch the door with you,” said Yuan. “I do not open it and I do not stand in it.”

She saw the younger man work for herself, at the fourth hour, from the roof of the intake shed where she had gone to get the weight of two bundles off her shoulder.

He was standing at the end of the tower lane with a carrier he had bought, and he was not asking about a person. He was asking about a schedule. Which hour the water went up. Which hour the paper went down. Which hour a hand came out of the tower door with nothing in it and went back in with something. He paid for the hour, and the carrier told him, because a carrier is paid by the hour and eleven pence for one hour of somebody else's day is more than that hour is worth.

And then he said a thing that stopped the woman on the roof of the intake shed from moving for the length of a breath: *there is a woman sets the loads down slow.*

He had described her by her pace. That was all he had bought. There were four women in Lowwater who set loads down slow and he had not yet found out which of them slept over a copying floor.

She stayed on the roof until he had gone, and then she came down and did not hurry and did not look up the lane, because a woman who has just heard that she is described by her pace will turn her head.

The change of hours cost her the first two days of it. The schedule is entered a day ahead, so a woman who moves her loads at the seventh hour has to move them a day ahead of herself, and the intake clerk who wrote it was the man with the finger set an inch too long four feet from the furnace mouth three hundred miles behind her, entered her as labour off a sheet she never saw and dated the day before she carried it.

For two days she carried water at the fifth hour instead of the seventh because that was the hour on the sheet, and the fifth hour was the hour the paper went down the lane, and for two days she walked a lane she had walked for nine weeks with a man in it.

She did not change the hours back. She went to Feng and had the intake line moved to the ninth hour, where it was already thick with other hands and where a woman setting a load down slow is one of forty, and she had it entered as a tower change and not a request, because a request has a reason attached and a change has a sheet.

Feng looked at her for a moment over the top of the fair-hand desk.

“They have you at the fifth hour,” he said.

“They had me at the fifth hour.”

“They will have you at the ninth hour from tomorrow and the difference will be on a sheet that goes up the stair,” said Feng, “and I will enter it, because I am the hand that enters it. Do you understand what you have just spent.”

“I have understood it since the autumn,” said Ning Yan.

Feng wrote it in, and did not look up again.

The elder still bought records at the desks, daylight, before witnesses. Which clerk keeps the Qinghe advancement copies. What carriers carried packets unsigned. What hours stand beside which names. Polite, unhurried, coin on the wood before the asking finished.

The younger bought bodies. Where the tower hand sleeps when he is not in the loft. What road he walks after dark with soup. Whether he carries sheets in his coat and whether the sheets bear a woman's hand. He paid a carrier twice for an hour and a lane, and the carrier took it because coin is coin and hours are hours.

She changed her carrying hours and walked no lane twice. She logged both faces in wash code on the Clause Four docket, the elder's grey temple and the younger's quick hands. She told Du Hong to burn nothing and carry nothing unentered, and to walk with the water cart after dark and not alone.

“You order me like a foreman,” said Du Hong, flour to the wrist, soup in his hands.

“I order no one,” said Ning Yan. “I ask you walk entered. The file on you grows while the claim stands.”

He set the soup down and looked at her across the mat, and his face did the thing it does when he has worked something out and wishes he had not.

“My file carries your hand twice,” he said. “The advancement and the direction I never filed. The county book carries my name for your attendance. I am the cheap door and we both said so before you did. Now feet follow me to find your sleeping place, which the whole bound already knows. What do you want me to do, walk less?”

“I want you alive,” said Ning Yan. It came out rougher than a tally. “I want you walking entered in daylight with the Watch on the rail. That is all I have.”

“That is much. I walk it.”

He picked the soup up again and drank half of it standing, which he had not done since the mountain, and she understood that he was not hungry and that saying so out loud would have been a thing she would have had to answer.

In the evening Feng called her down after the clerks left and stood at the wall with a lamp, coughing into his sleeve.

“The buyer asked after tower hands today,” he said. “Not desks. Hands. I told him hands carry what the tower needs and the hours count the same. Both true.”

“He pays for feet now,” said Ning Yan.

“He pays for what the desks cannot sell him,” said Feng. “The desks sold him refusals. The loft was announced. What is left is who walks to it after dark.”

She nodded. The distinction stood in lamplight, plain as brushes. Records to bodies. The counting room stacked paper by household behind both, and the broker held paper the way the sponsors hold schedules, and she would not meet him in a room and would not let a room be built where she had to.

“The young one described me to a carrier on the tower lane,” she said. “By my pace.”

Feng looked at her for a while with the lamp in his hand and did not say anything at once, which from him was a long answer.

“They will have it from somebody in a month if they do not have it now,” he said at last. “The men who set loads down slow are mine and they are not yours to discipline. Do not go at them. A hand that is shouted at sets its load down fast for a week and a week is how a man gets counted.”

“Then what do I do.”

“You make the counting expensive,” said Feng. “You are attended, marked and entered. That is the whole of what there is to do and you are doing it.”

He turned back to his desk before she could ask the other thing, which was whether a tower that copied up the same day was a wall or a door, and which she had wanted to ask since the morning she had learned that the tower was the copy and not the record.

She went up to the loft and added to the docket: feet, two men, Du Hong described, upper lane watched, pace. She did not write the broker's name. What she got upside down she keeps unreadable and exact.

She lay on the mat with the arithmetic ticking. Settlement thinned by ordinary use, week on week, thin and clear. About weeks now, not months. The channel covered and less warm, the way a blanket goes thin.

“You count it,” said Yuan. “You do the sum and you do not flinch.”

“I count it,” said Ning Yan. “Weeks, not months. I work still.”

“You work well,” said Yuan. “I see it, and I do not tell you to stop.”

Below, brushes stopped and Feng coughed and wrote and coughed. On the square the desks closed. At the lane mouth the younger man waited till the tower lamp lit, then went toward the counting lane with his report, unhurried, paid by the week, walking behind coin the way coin walks behind paper.
