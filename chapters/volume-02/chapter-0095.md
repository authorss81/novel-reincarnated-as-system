# What Walks Behind Coin

The pursuit stopped paying for pages and started paying for feet in the week after she was known.

Ning Yan learned it carrying oil for the lamp at the seventh hour. The younger man no longer stood at the desks with the good coat. He walked the lanes carriers walk, asking after hours by description, paying for answers about a hand with tower letters and flour on the sleeves. A hand with a file. A boy eighteen who cannot lie and does not try.

She set the oil down at the tower kitchen and took a paper load instead, because paper loads go by hand after the fifth hour and hands hear more than shoulders.

“You see them,” said Yuan. “You mark them and you do not meet them.”

“I mark them,” said Ning Yan. “Two now. The elder pays. The younger walks. You watch the door with me.”

“I watch,” said Yuan. “I stay near and I touch nothing.”

The elder still bought records at the desks, daylight, before witnesses. Which clerk keeps the Qinghe advancement copies. What carriers carried packets unsigned. What hours stand beside which names. Polite, unhurried, coin on the wood before the asking finished.

The younger bought bodies. Where the tower hand sleeps when he is not in the loft. What road he walks after dark with soup. Whether he carries sheets in his coat and whether the sheets bear a woman’s hand. He paid a carrier twice for an hour and a lane, and the carrier took it because coin is coin and hours are hours.

She changed her carrying hours and walked no lane twice. She logged both faces in wash code on the Clause Four docket, the elder’s grey temple and the younger’s quick hands. She told Du Hong to burn nothing and carry nothing unentered, and to walk with the water cart after dark and not alone.

“You order me like a foreman,” said Du Hong, flour to the wrist, soup in his hands.

“I order no one,” said Ning Yan. “I ask you walk entered. The file on you grows while the claim stands.”

He set the soup down and looked at her across the mat, and his face did the thing it does when he has worked something out and wishes he had not.

“My file carries your hand twice,” he said. “The advancement and the direction I never filed. The county book carries my name for your attendance. I am the cheap door and we both said so before you did. Now feet follow me to find your sleeping place, which the whole bound already knows. What do you want me to do, walk less?”

“I want you alive,” said Ning Yan. It came out rougher than a tally. “I want you walking entered in daylight with the Watch on the rail. That is all I have.”

He laughed once, short. “That is much. I walk it.”

In the evening Feng called her down after the clerks left and stood at the wall with a lamp, coughing into his sleeve.

“The buyer asked after tower hands today,” he said. “Not desks. Hands. I told him hands carry what the tower needs and the hours count the same. Both true.”

“He pays for feet now,” said Ning Yan.

“He pays for what the desks cannot sell him,” said Feng. “The desks sold him refusals. The loft was announced. What is left is who walks to it after dark.”

She nodded. The distinction stood in lamplight, plain as brushes. Records to bodies. The counting room stacked paper by household behind both, and the broker held paper the way the sponsors hold schedules, and she would not meet him, not in this batch, not in any room.

She went up to the loft and added to the docket: feet, two men, Du Hong described, upper lane watched. She did not write the broker’s name. What she got upside down she keeps unreadable and exact.

She lay on the mat with the arithmetic ticking. Settlement thinned by ordinary use, week on week, thin and clear. About weeks now, not months. The channel covered and less warm, the way a blanket goes thin.

“You count it,” said Yuan. “You do the sum and you do not flinch.”

“I count it,” said Ning Yan. “Weeks, not months. I work still.”

“You work well,” said Yuan. “I see it and I stay with you.”

Below, brushes stopped and Feng coughed and wrote and coughed. On the square the desks closed. At the lane mouth the younger man waited till the tower lamp lit, then went toward the counting lane with his report, unhurried, paid by the week, walking behind coin the way coin walks behind paper.
