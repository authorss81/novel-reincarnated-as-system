# Countersignature

The queue ran out of the door and down the wet stones in the middle of the afternoon, and she stood fourth in it with a certified schedule under her arm in oilcloth and the filing copy in her coat, and for eleven weeks that was all she had ever been able to do with it.

“Fourth,” said Sun He, without looking up. He had said her name and the number in one breath the way a man says a thing he has said a hundred times, and nineteen years of the county book were in the lines of his face and there was no more in them than there had been at the first hour of the thaw.

She laid the papers on the table in the order of the filing.

The sealed application, with the three sponsors' marks above it. The clearance, signed in a back room four months ago by a man looking past her. The pending filing, worn through at the fold, with a county stamp on it, and a defect line in her own hand on the second page, and a stage column with one, two, three and then nothing.

And the schedule, certified, the Hall's mark on it, the season's hours on it, nine households' column beside hers, closed on the last day of the thaw.

Sun He read the schedule for longer than he read anything else, which she had expected and had not wanted.

He read it twice.

“This is the season's labour.”

“It is the season's labour.”

“Certified at the Hall's clerk and stamped by a maintaining member of the Hall.” He set it down square. “Three hundred and eleven hours before the last week and the last week closed. Nine households on the column and yours in it in your own hand and not as a marked-up for a shift.”

He turned the sheet a quarter turn and looked at it from the side.

“This is a real sheet. Most of what comes to this table is a list of hours. This is the thing the hours were done in.”

“Yes,” said Ning Yan. “That is the argument.”

“That is the argument,” said Sun He, “and the registrar does not argue. The registrar counts.”

The queue waited. Somebody at the back said a word about the rain and the door was open and the sound came in off the square.

## The Gap

He got to the stage column at the fourth minute and stopped.

Ning Yan watched him read it. She had been waiting eleven weeks for a man to stop on that sheet, and she had got it in a county office in the rain with a queue at her back, and she had known for eleven weeks what his face would do and had not been able to make it do anything else.

“This reads one, two, three,” said Sun He.

“It reads one, two, three.”

“And then five.”

“It reads one, two, three, and then five,” said Ning Yan. “I reached the fourth stage on the twelfth day after the burial and it was not announced. It is not on the county sheet. It is not on the tower's copies. It is not in any book in nine counties, and it cannot be put in one now, because this office struck this sheet at the second hour and entered the striking, and a county does not take a line back off a struck sheet before the quarter.”

She said it without stopping, because it had been in her for eleven days and she had walked it through four hundred times and there was not one part of it she was going to soften in front of a clerk.

“The applicant states that she reached the fourth stage and did not announce it,” said Sun He.

“The applicant states that she reached the fourth stage and did not announce it, and that no office has the number and that no office can have it now.”

“Why.”

“Because I did not want nine counties to be told what I had become on the morning they buried a man I killed.” She said that in the flat voice of a woman reading a line out of a book. “Because it cost me the stage and I took it anyway, and it is entered nowhere, and I would not enter it now if the office were in front of me with the book open.”

The room did that thing a room does at a sentence like that. Not quiet. Waiting.

Sun He looked at her for the length of three counts. Then he wrote, in the margin of the county book, in the ordinary hand of a man doing his duty, at the fourth hour of the afternoon.

*At the fourth hour of this day the applicant states of her own motion that she reached the stage fourth of Condensing at this office's county on the twelfth day after the burial, and did not announce the stage, and no copy of it exists. The county has counted the fourth stage not.*

Then he read that back, because he read everything back in this office.

“Enter it,” said Ning Yan.

“It is entered,” said Sun He. “Now you will hear the ruling and you will hear it read, because a ruling in this office is read.”

“An absence is not a breach,” said Sun He. “A filing fails when it is broken. It is broken when a term of it is failed, and a term is an hour attended, a sheet signed, a schedule certified, a defect declared.”

“The count on this sheet runs one, two, three and five. That is an absence and not a failure, because there is a line in this office for every stage of the term and the line for the fourth is empty and not struck, and an empty line in a book is not a broken book.”

“I have written the distinction on the sheet so that the next man to read it does not have to make it himself.”

“And the count.”

“The count stands as the county has it, one, two, three, five, and the count is the count. Your licence reads to five and the ceiling holds at five, because the ceiling is entered in your hand and not in mine.”

He turned the page.

“I will say this once and let it stand in the book. The fourth stage you did not announce is not on this sheet and is not on any sheet in this bound, and it is not going to be on one. I cannot restore it to you. You have not asked me to, and I would not have done it if you had.”

“I would not have wanted it done,” said Ning Yan.

“I know,” said Sun He. “Write that down yourself if you want it written down. I am not going to put a hurt of yours into a county book for you.”

## The Stamp

He read the terms aloud. She had heard them once before and had signed them in the same hour, and she heard them again in a different voice with a stamp about to come down on them.

A pending filing, with its hours continuous from the day the tithe book opened, and its defect declared in the applicant's own hand, and its ceiling entered at the fifth stage of Condensing, and no petition for the Foundation without relicensing, is countersigned and becomes a licence.

She listened to every word of it and she did not ask him anything, because there was nothing in it she had not signed and there was nothing in it she wanted.

Then he took the stamp.

*Licence to Practise Past Registration. Clause Four of the Servant Advancement Settlement. The Bureau of Records, county office at Lowwater.*

She watched his hand go down and come up. The ink was worse than the county's and she knew that from four months of the filing's mark, and the sound the table made was the sound a table makes.

The scope line first, which she had been reading about since December and had never once seen.

*Servant-born. Granted to draw sect qi, and to take paid work outside the crew, and to be taught past her stage, under countersignature at this county.*

Paid work outside the crew. That was the whole of four months and it was one line and it did not have a stamp on it particularly.

Then he wrote the six conditions on the back of it, in order, in the ordinary hand of a man doing his duty, and read them out to the queue because that is what the conditions on a licence in this office are for.

*First. At every county hour of this filing, in person, and in her own hand. No surety may stand for the applicant again without relicensing.*

*Second. The hours are those of the furnace schedule of Qinghe, certified by the Hall's clerk and returned to this office at the quarter. No other schedule counts.*

*Third. Practice at or below Condensing five. Nothing above it is licensed.*

*Fourth. No petition for the Foundation without relicensing.*

*Fifth. The defect is entered and is permanent. This licence restores nothing to the aperture.*

*Sixth. There is entered against this filing the standing claim of eight hundred jin entered in four counties against the Chosen One. That claim travels with this licence and is not affected by it.*

The room was very quiet.

“The sixth,” said Ning Yan.

“The sixth,” said Sun He.

“That is the claim that refused this application.”

“That is the claim that refused this application,” said Sun He, “and it is in this office's book, and a book is a book, and a claim that is in a book cannot be unlisted by a man stamping a different document. I am required to enter it. You may read it.”

She read it. It was the ordinary shape: eight hundred jin, entered in four counties, proof by body, place and name, standing and unanswered.

“You could have refused again,” said Ning Yan.

“I could have refused again and I would have had to write down again why, and the reason would be the same reason, and there is no version of this office in which a registrar refuses the same paper twice for the same line without somebody upstairs asking him what has changed.”

He set the stamp down square with the edge of the table.

“What has changed is that the labour is now on the sheet. That is what has changed, and I will tell you exactly what it is worth to you. It is worth exactly a stamp. It is not worth a thing you have to be careful about on a road.”

He turned the licence round and put it face up in front of her, and then he did the thing that she had not been ready for and would think about for the rest of her life, which was that he stopped looking at the paper and looked at her.

“You will be careful on a road,” said Sun He.

“I will be careful on a road,” said Ning Yan.

“Then you are licensed,” said Sun He, “and I never asked you a single question about what you intend to do with it, and I want you to understand that I could have, and that a registrar in this office could have, and that in nineteen years the number of people who have stood in front of this table and been examined about their intentions is a number I could put on one hand.”

## The Witness

Feng Lushi was standing four feet behind her with his hat in his hands and had been standing there for the whole of it.

She had not known he was there.

She had asked him at the tower door at the eighth hour, in daylight, in front of two clerks, and had said *I am going to ask you to stand in a county office for four hours and I am telling you now that it will be the shape of your last year and I cannot stop you asking me why*, and he had said *I have copied in that building twice in twenty-two years and both times a man came in afterwards and asked me what I had seen*.

She had said *then come and see*.

He had come and seen.

He had read nothing. He had stood at her shoulder and had not read one line of the paper that was going down on the table, and when it was stamped he had not looked at the stamp either, and the two clerks from his floor were not there because he had told them where he was going and not asked them to come.

He wrote one line in the witness column at the bottom, in fair hand, with a fair hand's care, and he did not write a great deal in it.

*Present at the countersignature of a Licence to Practise Past Registration, at the county office at Lowwater, on the day and hour entered on the licence. W. Feng Lushi, keeper, Lantern Register, Lowwater.*

That was the whole of it. There was no sentence about what he had seen, and there was nothing in it that could be carried upward by anybody, because a keeper of a tower standing in a county office is the most ordinary thing in this bound and there is nothing in that line that an office upstairs could use except the office it is in, which is a room he is paid to be in.

He put the hat back on.

“You read nothing,” said Ning Yan.

“I read nothing,” said Feng.

“You stood for four hours.”

“I stood for four hours,” said Feng, “and I have been in this trade twenty-two years and the longest hours of my life are all standing in rooms where somebody else is being stamped, and I want to tell you what it was like, and then I am going to stop telling you.”

“Tell me.”

“It was like copying,” said Feng. “That is all. Four hours of a man being extremely careful about a piece of paper in front of a queue. It was the most like copying any hour I have spent in my life.”

They went out into the rain on the square together and the queue went past them to the table and the next one was called.

The market was shutting. The three desks had their boards in and the second desk had a coin on the wood that was a day old, and the counting lane was barred, and out over the roofs of Lowwater there was nothing to see and nothing to hear.

No man on any square in nine counties was saying anything about the ceiling of the most famous woman in the bound. An announcement is audible inside the bound, and Lowwater is inside the bound, and she had heard it in the winter, and it had cost her eight years on her father's column.

She had been told her own number in a market in the middle of the ash fall, and it had told a man with a good coat where she stood, and five weeks ago two men had sat on a wall and bought nothing.

The claim was on the licence. It was the sixth condition and the last thing on the paper and it was in the county's own hand and it was going up the quarter with the crate and down to nine counties with everything else, and a man with a counting room had just been handed, by a registrar doing his duty, the one page he had come four hundred miles down the road to buy.
