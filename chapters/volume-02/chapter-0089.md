# The Filing Copy

She went back to the county office on the second morning after the refusal with the filing copy in her sleeve and Du Hong walking beside her, because a pending filing is not a paper a person admires.

The square ran wet with thaw. Carts stood axle-deep before the ninth hour and carriers shouted over each other, and the three desks under the east awning had their covers off early. The man in the good coat stood at the first desk with his tube on his belt, paying for the writing of a refusal he had already bought twice.

Ning Yan did not look at him longer than a carrier looks at weather. She took her place in the county queue fourth, breathing two counts, with her left hand free and her right hung dead in the sleeve.

“You attend,” said Yuan. “You stand it and you carry it.”

“I attend,” said Ning Yan under her breath. “I stand the hours. You watch with me.”

“I watch,” said Yuan. “I am close and I keep my hands to myself.”

The county office was one long room with a raised step at the back and a smell of wet ink that did not change with the season. The list of names called that morning was a single sheet on a board by the door, and the brush moved down it while the queue made its own small weather behind her. Sun He sat at the far table with the county book open and a second book under it, and neither book had a cover worth the name.

The fourth man ahead of her was carrying somebody else’s copy and had not troubled to learn what was in it. The third had come in from Beihe with the road still on him and stood with his shoulders inside the doorframe. She used the time the way she used all waiting time, which was to count what the room was doing. Four names to the hour. Eight brush strokes to a name, because the registrar reads the terms aloud before he writes and a clerk at the side writes down that he did.

Sun He called names from the list without hurry. When hers came she stood before the far table and laid the filing copy flat. The defect stood on the county sheet in her own left hand, ugly and exact, and the registrar read it without pausing on any word.

*aperture cracked through; stabilised at the crack; permanent.*

He turned to the hours page. The brush sat ready. The queue waited the way queues wait, with no interest and complete memory.

“County hours,” he said. “Stamp days. Tower readings. The filing counts what the schedule carries.”

She understood him the way she understands tallies, which is all at once and without needing it said twice. The pending paper was not a licence kept in a sleeve. It was attendance, week after week, in rooms where a clerk could see her breathe.

The clerk at the side wrote that the registrar had read the terms. Du Hong stepped forward when the clerk nodded and gave his name aloud for the attendance line, steady, badly hidden, entirely himself. The brush moved. A living name for every county hour.

Going out, the man in the good coat stood aside of the door and let her pass, which a man buying refusals has no reason to do. Up close he was smaller than his coat and older than his hands. He said, without any weight in it, that the thaw had come early this year.

She gave him nothing back. Not her name, not her county, not the loft. She looked at the string of his tube where it hung from his belt and she kept walking, and she felt him decide not to follow, and the deciding was the whole of the information she got.

Outside, the mark was still wet on the copy. Du Hong walked with her into the noise and did not ask what the man had said, which was how she knew he had heard it.

“Nothing worth carrying,” she said.

He carried it anyway.

She carried stone for the tower wall till the fifth hour. The load sat across her chest over the tablet, and the log rode in her lining, and the stripe ran cold under the coat every third breath. Her arm came back eleven minutes in the middle of a block and served and went, and she built the rhythm leftward round it the way she had learned on the furnace floor.

At the well she heard two carriers talk about the buyer without interest. He paid, he wrote, he thanked. One said the man always paid for the writing even when the answer was nothing. The other said a man who pays for nothing is saving to pay for something.

She carried the yoke up the tower stair with the wood knocking her shoulders and set it down without spilling. Feng nodded from the fair-hand desk without stopping the brush. The intake clerk entered the water as labour on the schedule sheet, and the sheet would go up by the carts on the third evening, and the clerk with the broken finger would write it slow four feet from the furnace mouth.

She went back down and did the sum herself before she went up again, because a county counts differently from a furnace. The county wanted days she had stood in a room. The schedule wanted loads she had set down somewhere. In the furnace a man was paid for what his body did and the book could not see a single hour of it. Here the book could see nothing but the hour, and wanted it in one hand, in one column, week on week, with nothing in it that a person could do twice.

She had forty-one days of the schedule behind her and one stamp day entered. Nineteen weeks to the end of the year on the county's own paper. She could carry stone on all nineteen. There was no arrangement of days that made nineteen weeks go faster, and there was no version of the filing where a single day counted for more than one hour, so the only thing left to decide was whether she would be standing in the room on all of them.

She decided yes, and wrote nothing down, because a decision written is a decision somebody else can read.

“How does it stand,” Du Hong asked in the loft at the eighth hour over soup.

“Attended,” said Ning Yan. “Marked. Hours, stages, defect, all on the county sheet from the day the book opened. The schedule carries what I carry.”

He did the subtraction carriers do on their fingers. “And the ceiling.”

“Five,” said Ning Yan. “Where the crack stops. No Foundation without relicensing. The writ is dead on the same page.”

She did not say it bitterly. Bitterness is a thing a person spends, and she needed the breath for the stair.

In the evening Feng sent her to the counting lane with a sealed copy, because tower copies go by hand after the fifth hour and she was a hand. The lane held counting rooms with barred windows and brushes going behind them. There were four doors open out of nine, and behind the fourth a man was reading numbers aloud to nobody in particular.

She delivered to the third door, took the receipt mark, and walked back without hurrying. The mark was a small stamped ring with the door number in it, and she put it in her lining with the others, because a receipt is a receipt and one day it would be a thing she could show somebody.

Halfway down she passed an open door with paper stacked by household and a brush that never stopped. The man in the good coat stood at the tall desk with his tube open, reporting low, employed the way clerks are employed. She did not stop. Stopping is a statement, and she had made her statements for the season in a burial register.

Back in the loft she added to the docket in wash code: attended, marked, buyer at three desks, counting room reports, one courtesy at the county door. She did not write the broker’s name. What she gets upside down over a tray she keeps the way she got it.

“You carry well,” said Yuan. “You sleep little and you work much.”

“I carry hours,” said Ning Yan. “Hours are what the county stamps. The rest is weight.”

“You know it,” said Yuan. “I see it and I hold it with you.”

She lay on the mat in the coat she had not taken off and put her left hand flat on the boards, and the wind worked at the shutter gap where the cord had gone slack, and the breath pulled short at two counts and settled.

Below, brushes moved and Feng coughed and copied.

The filing copy sat in her sleeve with the day’s mark on it. Refused as a licence and kept as a filing. Worth everything except the Foundation. That was the first week of it, and it held because she had stood where the book said to stand, with a boy’s name beside hers in the same book.

She did not forgive anybody for the shape of it. She did not expect it, which was the cheaper way to carry the same weight.

The last thing she heard before sleep was the tower lamp lit below in its cage, and Feng’s brush going fair and steady under a cough that had got worse since the market opened.
