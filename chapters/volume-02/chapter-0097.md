# A Word With Nobody

She carried the word the way she carries the tablet, against the skin and without showing it.

The morning after, Ning Yan attended the stamp day with Du Hong, stood through the reading, signed the hours, took the mark. The filing copy thickened. The county examined the paper, because that is what a county office does, and the paper was excellent. No clerk mentioned the second position on the Lowwater sheet. No clerk reads what a copy carries in a smaller hand unless paid to read it.

The buyer stood at the first desk, paying for the writing of a refusal. The younger man stood at the lane mouth, watching the tower door. She walked between them with a water yoke, breathing two counts, and neither touched her, because attendance is a room and she was inside it.

“You carry much,” said Yuan. “You say little and you hold much.”

“I carry it,” said Ning Yan. “You hold it with me. That is enough walking for today.”

“I hold it,” said Yuan. “I stay with you and I ask nothing.”

In the afternoon Feng sent her to dust again, and she dusted and did not open another volume. Opening twice is a habit, and habits are read. She wanted to ask what the word meant. She wanted to ask Yuan again, though he had said he has no word for it. She wanted to write it in wash code where no clerk reads quickly.

She did none of it. Wanting is not spending, and she had spent enough that season to know the difference.

Instead she washed at the trough behind the tower with her left hand, hung dead right in the sleeve, and mended the shutter cord where it had slipped. Du Hong came up with a nail and a stone for a hammer without being asked, fixed it in ten minutes, went down again with the stone in his hand.

“You climb for quiet,” she said after him, the way she had said it weeks ago, and he grinned up the stair without stopping.

Feng called her down after the clerks left and stood at the wall with a lamp.

“You looked,” he said. It was not a question. He looks at paper directly and without comfort, and he looked at her the same way.

“I looked,” said Ning Yan. “I dusted where you set me. I turned nothing. I took nothing.”

“Good,” said Feng. He coughed into his sleeve, wet and deep, worse than the winter past. “What the tower holds behind the fifth item is not for carrying. What the volumes hold is not for copying. You know the difference.”

“I know it,” said Ning Yan. “Four walled, none upward. Column at five. I copy nothing for him and I take nothing for me.”

He put the lamp on the table and looked at her the way he looks at fair hand going thin at the edges.

“Then we hold,” he said. “Do not thank me for it.”

“I do not,” said Ning Yan.

She went up to the loft and lay on the mat with the word ticking behind her eyes and no paper under it. *Kiln-born.* Held in two heads. A status column with eleven lines, six hundred years, a dead hand. A county sheet with a second position no other county carries. One of four copies carrying what three do not.

It meant nothing she could use. That was the horror and the mercy of it. A discovery that changes nothing she can touch, and therefore cannot be taken from her by any hand that takes things.

“You are lonely with it,” said Yuan. “I am with you and you are lonely still.”

“I am,” said Ning Yan. “You have no word for it and I have no one to ask. We hold it together and it is still lonely.”

“I hold it,” said Yuan. “I do not leave it and I do not fill it.”

She slept with the docket in her lining and the filing copy in her sleeve, and the square noise thin through the gap, and one word with nobody to hold it except the two of them, both unable to read the hand that wrote it and both unwilling to ask anyone who might.

The wrong-order name stayed unresolved in the Hall with no roof. The twelve-sheet frame stayed logged in wash code, untouched. The office with a name he cannot read stayed unopened. Paper older than pursuit sat behind a loose spine and said one word, and the word stayed where paper cannot follow.
