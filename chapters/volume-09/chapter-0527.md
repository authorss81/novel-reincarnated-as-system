# Nine Coppers A Head

She went out of the opening at the fourth hour of the twenty-eighth of a month with the bar on her shoulder and three square sheets in the bag against the place low behind her fourth rib, and she came out of the far ground past a bar on two posts on her own two feet.

## The Gate

The boy of fifteen was on the chain at the seventh hour with the cup on its wire and he put the chain down when he saw her and asked her the question he asks everybody.

“No,” Ning Yan said.

“All right,” the boy said, and put the chain up for a barrow behind her.

She has been past that gate nine times in six weeks and it is the same every time, and there is nothing in it for her, and she has noticed that the reason it works is that he is fifteen and does the same thing to everybody.

Four hundred yards west of him there was a team on the long road with boards on the cart, going east, and it had been going east since the second hour and had a long way still to go.

## Nine Coppers A Head

It was Wei Ran and it was one of the two women on the front bench and the driver had his feet up on the rail.

“Where,” Wei Ran said.

“West, through the crossing and along the lane, and then nine miles on the strip road, and back.”

“Nine coppers a head,” Wei Ran said. “And the bar goes in the cart and it is not in the rate.”

She said it at the same speed she has said it to every person on this road for four years. Then she said it again to the man on the front bench, who is forty and has been on that team six years.

“That is her,” the woman on the bench said.

“That is her,” Wei Ran said, and put her hand on the side of the cart.

Then she said it a third time to the driver, who did not look up, and a fourth time to a man with a goat who had stopped forty feet off and wanted to know whether the chain would be up or down.

That is what a rate is. She said the same figure four times in eleven minutes to four people who were not the one she was talking to, and if she had said it different to Ning Yan than to anybody else it would not have been a rate and everybody on that cart would have known it inside a day.

## The Fourth Arrangement

This is the fourth arrangement in eight years that has put a figure on her and she has got into the first one.

There was a shed with a man in it at the end of a lane in the second month. There was a board outside a room with six day books and a figure against a line on it in the third month. There was a rate offered across a counter in the fifth month for carrying and she said no out loud in nine words.

This one is different in one respect only and it is the respect that matters.

She has been in the cart.

## The Thing She Cannot Do

Her hand will not take money.

That has been true since she was fourteen and it has cost her four arrangements and it is not going to cost her anything this morning, and she is not going to stand in a cart at the seventh hour feeling bad in it, because a person cannot feel bad about a thing they did not choose and cannot undo.

What she can do is the other half of it.

Eight coppers have been on Wei Ran’s book against her name since the second month of a year she could name, and Wei Ran has said out loud, twice, in front of a woman on a bench, that she is not going to chase them and never will.

Nine coppers is what this morning would be worth and it is not going on any book, and it is not going to be chased, and the reason is not generosity. It is that a rate is only a rate if it is the same to everybody, and a woman who waives it for one person on the ground has a second rate and then she has a rate for herself.

## The Twenty-Two Miles

She got in at the seventh hour on the twenty-eighth and she sat on the boards of the cart with the bag between her feet and her hand flat on it.

Eleven miles of made road from the gate, two miles of lane, and nine miles of strip road, which is twenty-two, and they did it in two days and a half with one night in a siding at the sixth mile.

She has not ridden with her hands on her knees for two days since the second month of the fourth month and she found that she had forgotten the shape of it, and that the thing she had forgotten was that somebody else chooses the speed.

The cart did not know it was carrying the strongest person within nine hundred yards of it. The boards did not shift for her and the driver did not take his feet off the rail for her and the boy on the front bench did not offer her the end of the bench, and all of that was the single most ordinary two and a half days she has had in four months.

## What The Body Did

She has eaten nothing since the sixteenth of a month that has gone, and she is not hungry, and that is finished, and she is thinner than she was on the eighth of one that has gone.

A cart is the only way of covering twenty-two miles she has had in five months, and it took four days off her, and she gave it back on the second night on the boards of the cart with her right arm out of the way of the bag because the arm had been open since the sixth hour of the twenty-eighth and had not shut once in two days.

Her fourth count on getting down off the boards at the second hour of the thirtieth went for five seconds, and she did not measure it twice, because she had measured it twice at the fourth hour on flat boards for four days and it does not improve and she knows it does not improve.

She carried it herself up onto the boards of the cart from the tailboard. The boy on the front bench was not asked and was not offered and did not come back four feet to help, and she did not want him to and she is not going to pretend to anybody that she minded.

## What Wei Ran Said Going

She said it at the eleventh hour on the second day, at the place where the lane meets the store road, without turning round, the way a person says something to a cart.

“I have had eleven people on this road in four years who want to go somewhere and cannot pay and one of them is on my cart.”

Wei Ran did not look round either.

“Yes,” she said.

“That is the opinion,” Wei Ran said. “Nine coppers a head is what I say. You are on the cart for nothing and I am not going to chase you for it, and I would have said the number just the same if you had money in your hand.”

“And if you ever have money in your hand I am going to take it,” Wei Ran said, “and I would rather you knew that now than worked out that you were the one person I never asked anything of.”

“All right,” Ning Yan said.

“I am not being kind to you,” Wei Ran said. “I am being the same to you. It is the only arrangement in this world I have ever found that has never once tried to keep anybody.”

## What She Told Nobody

She told the cart nothing. That is the fourth hour on the twenty-eighth and the reason for the whole of the twenty-two miles, and it is worth putting down before the rest of it because everything after the gate is a cart.

She wants one sheet of good paper from a man who has a paste pot and a rag and a board a foot square that gets filled off a cart every nine or ten days, and a rail, and a trough.

She has told nobody on nine days of road where she is and she has told nobody what she is making. She will tell a man with a rag nothing at all about a thing that has to be found, and if he says yes she will not be able to take it, and if he says no she will be two days further from a room with a table in it.

She has told him none of that. She told him west, nine miles, and back, and the bar, and that is all she is going to tell anybody until she is standing in front of him.

## The Road In

They came onto the strip road at the fourth hour of the thirtieth, and boards go down that road and everything goes down that road, and the post was on the low side with a ladder against it that went up to the board and not further.

There was a cart at the trough and a man with a rag over his shoulder and a paste pot on the board, and there was a foot of writing on the board, and there was a flat stone under the bottom of the ladder with a book on it.

She had stood there twice in two years.

It is not a large post. It is a post with a board on it and a rail and a trough and a ladder that does not go past the board, and a man has stood at the rail of it with a paste pot, and it will be twenty years in the spring coming, and there is nothing else at it.

She got off the cart forty feet short of the post and did not say anything to the driver, because there was nothing to say, and she stood on the low side of a strip road at the fifth hour with a bag against her ribs and twenty-two miles behind her and worked out, in nine seconds, that she was not going to get a sheet of paper off that man.

She had known that at the fourth mile. She had got on the cart anyway.

## What She Was Not Going To Do

She was not going to say what the paper was for.

That is the decision and she made it in the cart on the second day and it took her four minutes and she has not gone over it since.

If she says nothing, and he gives her nothing, she goes back east twenty-two miles with three square sheets and a table and gets on with it, and there is nothing in the world she can say afterwards.

If she says what it is for, and he is the man she thinks he is — and she has spoken to him twice and both times he has told her a true thing he had no reason to tell her — then there is a piece of paper on nine days of road with a wall on it.

And there is a man with a rag who has been told, and that is one more person in a place where there is already a man with a light on in his window.

And she is not going to do that to a man she has met twice for a sheet of paper she has not got.

So she got her bag off the cart onto the rail at the fifth hour and she went up to him and asked him for a thing, and what she asked him for was the plainest possible version of it and it had no reason in it at all.
