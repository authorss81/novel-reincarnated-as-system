# She Does Not Ask Him Back

She went back east on the twenty-ninth of a month and was at the wall by the second of the next one, and she stood on her side of it at the fourth hour of every morning for twelve days, and she did not ask him to come back once.

## Going Back East

Eleven miles of made road and a gate and four hundred yards of flat, on four fingers and a thumb, with a bar at the light end and a skin and nothing to eat since the second of a month.

She is not hungry. That finished in the fourth month and she worked out at the time that it was not a gift, and she is thinner than she was on the eighth and she has been thinner every day since, and the only thing she has for it is a jug and a shelf and a skin at the gate.

She did it in two days and she slept in the ditch at the sixth mile both nights and she did not go through the bar on two posts until the second hour of the second.

The boy of fifteen was on the chain at the fourth hour on the third and he asked her the same question he asks everybody and she said no and he said all right.

She walked three hundred yards east to the wall and put the bar down and she did not go through the opening.

## The Fourth Hour

She got there at the fourth hour because that is the hour she has done everything in for eight years and it is the only hour in the day that belongs to her.

There is a light in the window of the hut a hundred yards behind that wall and it has been on continuously since the ninth hour of the eighth of a month and she has watched it from the opening in the wall at the fourth hour every morning since the seventeenth.

She stands in the opening with her left hand on the jamb, because the jamb is cool at the fourth hour.

There is a chair by the light in that window. There is a man in the room some mornings. There is nothing else in that hut that she has ever been able to see and she has not gone a step nearer than this in twelve days.

She does not shout. She said his name once, into a hundred yards of flat ground, on the twelfth of a month at the second hour, and got nothing for nine hours, and she has not done it since.

## What There Is To Say

She worked all of it out on the flat at the west side of the wall on the fourth of the second month, standing up, with the light behind her in her eyes, and it took forty minutes and it is the longest thinking she has ever done about one sentence.

There is a way to ask him.

That is the finding and it is the whole of what she got out of forty minutes on flat ground, and she wants it said without anything around it.

There is a way to ask him and there is no way at all for the sentence to arrive.

She cannot say it to his face. He is on the far side of a wall he opened for her and the opening is four feet wide with no door in it and no bar on it and it does not cost her anything, and it is not a gap in a wall any more. It is the other side. A man who goes through that goes through it.

And she cannot write it. Her hand cannot take a gift and it cannot put a line down that is not straight and there is nobody within four hundred yards who would carry a thing for her, and she has not told anybody where she is since the third of a month.

And she cannot send for anything. There is nothing on nine days of road that would come.

So she can stand in an opening in a wall at the fourth hour of every morning and say any sentence she likes into a hundred yards of flat ground, and there is not one arrangement in the world that carries it the last hundred yards.

## What A Wall Is When It Is Opened

And she has got the other thing too, and it is the same thing seen from the other side, and she worked it out on the fifth.

A wall opened from the inside is a document read from the inside by the person it concerns, and there has to be a reader in the room, and there was nobody qualified in that room but him.

So he read it.

That is the whole of it. She said a number of one out loud on the boards with the door shut and he did not tell her whose it was, and there was one person in the room, and it was him, and he went and read it, and reading it put him on the other side.

She does not know how a rule that old gets hold of the person who does the thing. She has not got to it in twelve days and she is not going to in a year.

She knows the shape of it and she knows where it came from and she knows it was her mouth on the boards with the latch down.

## She Could Un-Say It

And here is the part that is left, and she has gone over it every morning for twelve days, and it is the whole of what there is for her to decide.

She could go back to that room and stand on those boards and say the sentence again the other way round. Withdraw it. Take it back. Say that she withdraws the only permission she has ever given anybody and she does not want it and she is sorry and she will not be doing it again.

She could say all of that.

It would change nothing at all.

A wall opened from the inside is not a door that can be shut. It is not a document that can be withdrawn. She opened a sheet of paper against her own ribs in the fourth month of a year she could name and it could not be un-opened, and she said that out loud on the boards that night, and this is the same mechanism at four hundred times the size and there is not one word of it that has changed.

## She Does Not Ask Him Back

So she did not ask him to come back.

Not on the sixth of the month at the fourth hour, when her right arm was open and she could not get the fourth count back for nine seconds on flat ground.

Not on the ninth, when it rained and the light in the window was behind the rain and she could not see it at all and stood there anyway.

Not on the eleventh, which was the eleventh morning, and she counted them.

She said nothing at all, out loud, into a hundred yards of flat ground, on any of the twelve mornings, and the reason is not magnanimity and it is not a decision she is proud of.

She does not ask because she has no way to ask and because there is no way to ask, and she would be saying a sentence to a lit window in order to feel that she had said it, and she has not got to be able to do that.

## The First Person

This is the thing underneath all of it and she put it out loud once, on the twelfth of the month, at the fourth hour, standing in the opening with her left hand on a cool jamb, in the ordinary voice, to a wall.

She has hurt people on purpose before. She said so in a room with the shutters up when she was fourteen and she was right and it is in a book on nine days of road and it is the best thing anybody has ever done with her.

Every one of those people went somewhere.

That is the whole of the difference and she has not been able to get past it for twelve days.

A woman behind a counter went on running a counter. A man with a brush went on carrying a book. A leg man went on scraping moulds. An office went on issuing things at a rate.

She did that to all of them, in her own words, once, and they all of them went somewhere else to get on with being alive, and every one of them is alive, and she has never once had to look at one of them and know what she took.

He did not go anywhere.

There is one hundred yards of flat ground and a wall and a hut with a light on in it and a man in a chair, and he is alive, and he is not answering, and she put him on that side of the wall with her own mouth at the ninth hour of the eighth of a month with the latch down.

## The Guilt She Is Not Going To Make A Virtue Of

She is not going to call it guilt and she is not going to make it into anything.

She is going to carry it as a fact, the way she carries the open place behind her fourth rib and the four fingers and a thumb and the five seconds on the fourth count, and she is not going to get any comfort out of it, because a person who has decided to hurt somebody in order to get somewhere is not a person who deserves to sit in a wall feeling bad about it for twelve mornings.

She said that out loud, once, at the fourth hour of the twelfth, and it took four sentences, and she has not said it since.

## The Morning

There is a light in the window of the hut behind the wall and it has not gone out once since the ninth hour of the eighth of a month, and she has stood at the fourth hour of twelve mornings and watched it.

She goes back in the morning.

That is the whole of what she has and she does it at the fourth hour: she goes back through the opening in the wall into a nine-foot room with boards on the floor and a chair and a table and a book on it, and she sleeps on the boards with her clothes on, and in the morning she walks out through the wall and stands in eleven miles of made road with a bar on her shoulder.

Past the bar on two posts. Past the gate, where a boy of fifteen works a chain and a cup hangs on the wire. Down the long road, all of it, in daylight.

And then the lane, and the cut, and the frame, and the store road, and the turn.

She has to walk all of it. There is nothing else on nine days of road that she has to do.
