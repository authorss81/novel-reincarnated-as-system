# What She Told Him To Do

She went through the bar on two posts at the ninth hour of the eighth of a month and she did not go back through it, and that is the whole of what happened at the turnstile.

## The Far Ground

There are two hundred yards of flat between the bar and the wall and she walked all of it with a bar on her shoulder and nothing behind her.

The wall is set in the ground the way a footing is set in the ground. Pale stone, four feet high, running north and south further than the flat goes, and there is no gate in it and no gap in it and nothing about it that would tell a person walking past that it was anything except a boundary somebody built.

A hut is a hundred yards east of it with a low roof and one small window, and there was a light in the window at the ninth hour of the eighth of a month in the daytime.

And there is a room built against the west face of the wall, four feet by six, with a door in it and a window and a chimney, and the door is shut.

She had never been in it. She had never been within three hundred yards of it. It is the only building on the last four miles of eleven and she found it by walking at it.

## The Room

She knocked on the door with the back of her hand because her right arm was open at her side.

He opened it and she went in and he shut the door behind her and stood with his hand on the inside of it and waited for the latch, and she heard the latch go, and it is the smallest sound anybody has ever heard her make.

There is a chair, and a table, and a book on the table, and boards on the floor.

That is all of it. It is not a hall and it is not an office and there is nothing in it that is called anything. Nine feet by eleven, one window facing west onto two hundred yards of flat ground, a chimney, a chair, a table, a book, and a bar of a door across the back of the room that is not a door and is the wall.

He stood nine feet off with his weight on his good leg and he did not say one word.

## The Count

She did the count while he was shutting the door and it took nine seconds.

Day one thousand two hundred and fifty-six is the day she made him put a thing down and he did not pick it up again.

Day one thousand five hundred and twenty-one is this one.

Five hundred and twenty-one less two hundred and fifty-six is two hundred and sixty-five days.

Two hundred and sixty-five days of a man who has had nothing to spend since before she was told what he was. She has said his name into four rooms and a shed and a field in that time. She said it into a shed with boards on the floor in a month that has gone and waited anyway, and nothing came back, and that is the whole of the instrument she has had for four hundred days.

She wanted to publish that at him. She had wanted to for a hundred days.

She did not, because she had a figure on a bench in her head and he had put it there on purpose and she does not hand a man his own instrument back to him.

## Who

She asked it once, standing up, in the ordinary voice, in a room with the door shut and the latch down.

“One,” she said. “One person stays on the wrong side of it and it is not me, because I said the words on a bench at a crossing on the third of a month and there is no name on the page and you are not going to write one, and I have thought about it for five days on eleven miles of made road.”

He did not answer.

That is his whole answer and she has been over it since. He did not say no. He did not put his hand on the page again. He did not shake his head, which she watched for.

He stood nine feet off in a room with the door shut and he did not tell her who.

## The Permission

She said it out loud once, on the boards, with the door shut and the latch down and a bar of a wall across the back of the room.

“Open it,” Ning Yan said.

And then, because she says everything out loud exactly once and because she wanted the whole of it over with in nine seconds and did not want to be asked one question about it afterward:

“I know the number. It is one and it is not me and I do not know whose it is and I am telling you to do it anyway, and I have said that out loud with a shut door behind you, and that is the whole of what I came here for.”

He did not say one word.

He went to the back of the room.

## What Was Not In The Room

She is on the far side of that shut door from this and she cannot hear one word of it, and what follows is not in the room with her, and it is not in the world she is standing in either.

It is not a field and it is not a list and it is not a strip of paper on a floor.

It is a doorway.

It stands in a space with nothing under his feet and no light in it that comes from anywhere and no shadow, and it is about four feet wide and six feet high, and it is made of pale stone, and he has been inside this four times in eleven hundred years of practice and he started by working out what kind of place it was.

## The Question He Goes Looking For

He has one question and he has had it since the thirtieth of a month, and he came in here on the eighth to answer it before he does it.

“Does a wall open from the inside without somebody staying on the wrong side of it.”

He goes and looks for the answer the way he looked for the morning and did not find it.

The doorway has a lintel and two jambs and no door in it and no threshold and no step. He walks through it. He comes back through it. He walks through it again and comes back.

There is nothing on either side.

## The Answer

There is one figure standing on the far side of the doorway and it is nine feet tall and it is not a person and it is the layer’s own way of putting a price on a page, and he knows it because he has seen the same shape stand in three other rooms in eleven hundred years of practice.

He goes and stands next to it and looks at it for a long time.

The rule has nothing to do with whose wall it is. He believed for nine hundred years that it did, and it does not, and that is the whole of what this space is for.

A wall is a document. A document opened from the inside has to be read by somebody, because that is what a document is, and the layer does not keep a distinction between the person a thing is about and the person who reads it. It has never had to. There has only ever been one pair of hands in the room.

So whoever reads it is whoever opens it, and whoever opens it goes through, and there is no version of this in which the one who opens it stands in a nine-foot room on this side of a doorway and watches somebody else walk out of it.

He looks for the exception. He looks for a door that works both ways and there is not one. He looks for a second pair of hands and there is not one. He looks for a rule that lets whoever opens it stay where he is, and there is not one, because there has never been a rule that says that.

“A wall opened from the inside always leaves the one who opened it on the wrong side of it.”

That is the answer, and it is the figure he put on a page on the first of a month and slid across a bench and did not say a word about.

He knew it when he wrote it. He wrote it anyway, because she asked him a good question and nobody had asked him one in nine hundred years.

## What He Does About It

He stands in a doorway with nothing under his feet and takes the eighth of a month and goes back out.

She told him to.

That is the whole of the reason and it is the first reason any of the four of these has ever had, and it is not a rule and it is not a price and it is not nine hundred years of anything. A woman stood in a nine-foot room with the door shut and the latch down and said out loud, in the ordinary voice, that she knew the number and did not know whose it was and was telling him to do it anyway.

Nobody has ever said that to him.

The other three people who came near a door in nine hundred years each asked him whether it was possible. She told him to do it.

## The Wall

She saw him go to the back of the room and put his two hands flat on the stone about four feet off the boards, shoulder width apart, the way a man puts his hands on a thing he has decided about.

She watched his shoulders move once.

There is no sound. She was standing nine feet off in a room with boards on the floor and there was no sound at all, and she has heard that hand write forty seconds of brush at forty yards across a made road and she knows exactly what that hand sounds like, and the wall did not make a sound.

The stone did not move.

The ground inside the wall moved. It went down about four inches and came up, once, the way clay comes up out of a dish, and there is an opening in the wall of a room in the ground four feet wide and six feet high, and it is not a hole and it is not broken stone and there is nothing loose anywhere on it.

And he stepped through it.

## What Is In The Opening

She went up to it at the tenth hour and stood in it and put her left hand flat on each side of it.

The stone on the left of the opening is the stone on the left of the wall. The stone on the right is the stone on the right of the wall. There is a jamb on both sides that was not there at the fourth hour and is exactly like the jamb that was there at the fourth hour.

It is four feet wide and six feet high and there is no threshold in it and no step and no door and no bar across it.

She stepped west through it into the room and stepped east through it back onto the boards, and it cost her nothing, and the boards of the room were the same boards on both sides of it.

She can go through it as many times as she likes.

That is the shape of what he did and it is the whole of the mechanism, and she worked it out standing in a doorway in a wall at the tenth hour of the eighth of a month with a bar against the outside of the wall.

There is one opening in this ground and it is not counted for her, and it is not counted against him either, because he is on the far side of it and the far side is the other side.

## What She Watched

He went through at the ninth hour of the eighth of a month in daylight and he did not hurry and he did not look round, and he walked east across a hundred yards of flat ground toward a hut with a low roof and one small window and a light in it.

He got there at about nine minutes past the hour.

He went in through the door of the hut and she could not see whether he shut it, because at a hundred yards, in daylight, with the light behind the window, she could not see a door.

She stood in the doorway of a nine-foot room with a chair in it and a table and a book on the table and she did not go through the opening.

Not then.

That is the whole of the eighth of a month and she has not said any of it out loud since, and she is not going to for some time.

## The Chair

The chair is against the wall of that room and it has a straight back and no cloth on it and it is not a good chair.

At the eleventh hour of the eighth of a month it was empty, and at the fourth hour of the ninth it was empty, and it has been empty every time she has looked at it since.

There is a light in the window of the hut behind the wall and there has been a light in that window continuously since the ninth hour of the eighth, and it is burning in the daytime, and nobody has come out of the hut.

She put the bar against the outside of the wall and she did not sleep, and she sat down on the boards with her back against the chair and she stayed there until the light went off the flat, and then she got up and went to the opening in the wall and stood in it and looked east at a lit window a hundred yards off.

One person stays on the wrong side of it, permanently.

It is not her.
