# He Asks The Carrier

It took him five days, and the five days are the shape of the whole of this, and she worked out what had happened on the thirtieth in four seconds standing in a ditch with rain coming into it.

## Five Days

She came back west off the long road on the twenty-second and she was at the trough road from the twenty-fourth.

She worked the cut in the day and filled the skin at the trough at the sixth hour and the tenth hour and slept on flat ground in the lee of something with her clothes on, and the space with a line on it was still empty on the twenty-fourth and on the twenty-fifth and on the twenty-sixth.

On the twenty-seventh the man who left bread on the stone wall left bread on the stone wall and walked off up the track.

And on the twenty-fifth, the twenty-sixth, the twenty-seventh, the twenty-eighth and the twenty-ninth, nothing came for anybody from anybody.

It is five days and she was in the middle of a stretch of them, and she is putting that in the page because every one of the five of them has been a thing arriving on a date somebody else chose.

Four months ago she bought the right to be nobody on this ground for the price of a refusal and a silence. That trade held for a hundred and twenty-nine days.

On the third of a month she gave a page away on a bench in daylight and the trade ended in eleven days, and the price of ending it was not being found.

It was being indexed. There is a difference between those two things and she had not known there was one until the twenty-eighth, and she has been thinking about it in a ditch ever since.

A person who is careful with a woman watches her. A person who is frightened for a woman watches her closer. Eleven people on nine days of road have been watching a girl with a bar since the ninth of a month because they believe something has come for her, and they have not been wrong to watch, and every single thing they have seen has gone somewhere.

They could not have told you what it added up to.

They did not need to.

## What A Page Carries

She had it worked out by the twenty-eighth and it took a quarter of an hour sitting on a stone at the mouth of the cut.

He told her on the seventh of a month that he used the date and not the place. The third of this month, one person on nine days of road got to the top of a realm, and there are not eleven people on this road who could be it, and he did not have to ask anybody anything.

The date was the third. The thirtieth is twenty-seven days after the third.

Twenty-seven days is a long time for a man with a book to walk about asking after a girl with a bar on a road he has never been west of. He had not walked the lane four times and found her. He had gone somewhere and started asking, and asking takes longer than walking, and she had given away a shed.

So he asked people.

## The Man Of Fifty With Four Boards

He has carried boards along that lane and up the long road for nine years and he is fifty and there are four boards on a bar and he has never once put them down anywhere anybody could see him put them down.

He stopped saying good on the ninth of a month and she worked out that it was not a decision about her, it was a decision about a thing he could not see the size of, and she has been right about that ever since.

And on the fourteenth he said it again on purpose, and she watched him decide to, and she did not thank him.

Here is what he did on the twenty-eighth of a month, and she has got it exactly and she put it out loud once on the made road at the sixth hour and it is the sentence that decides where she goes next.

A man she has never spoken to asked him, in the ordinary way, at the crossing with the painted board, whether there was a girl on this road with a bar she carried at the light end, and whether he knew where she was.

And a man of fifty who has been careful with her for nineteen days because something has come for her and he could not work out what, sat down on a stone, and said that there were four boards carried up this lane every day by a girl who carried the heavy end of them herself and could not take anything for it and was thinner than she had been.

He gave three facts and he stopped, and he would not give a fourth, and the fourth would have been the one that mattered.

She was not paid. She could not be paid. She was at the cut on the working days and she carried the heavy end of four boards up a lane every morning for two years and got nothing for it and had got thinner.

Then he put his bar down across his shoulder and said good to the man, and went on up the lane, and he did not look back.

He is fifty and he has carried boards on that lane for nine years and he is one of eleven, and what he did on the twenty-eighth of a month was the single most ordinary act of the kind that has ever been done on nine days of road. He was asked a plain question and he answered it, because a plain question is the only kind anybody has asked him since the ninth of a month and because he could not carry it by himself any more.

## The Trough Shed

There is a roof over the trough at the east end of the trough road on posts, and three sides of board and a door with a latch, and men have sat in it in the rain for thirty years.

He came up the trough road on the thirtieth at the seventh hour in the rain and he was not hurrying, because a man with his weight on one leg does not hurry in the rain at the seventh hour.

She was at the trough with a skin in her hand.

He went into the trough shed and shut the door behind him and stood with his hand on the inside of it and waited for the latch to go, and she heard the latch from eleven feet off, and then he came out and stood nine feet off her in the wet and did not say one word.

He did not ask her anything. He did not tell her anything. He did not explain that he had been to the crossing and the gate and the frame and the store in twenty-seven days.

He stood there.

## She Did Not Go To Him

She wants that said, because it is the whole of the twenty-ninth and the thirtieth and everything else in this is secondary to it.

He came to her. He has never once asked her to come to him and he did not ask her now, and she did not go.

She stood at a trough with a skin in her hand and a man of nine hundred years nine feet off her in the rain and she waited for him to begin, and he did not begin, because there was nothing to begin. He had come and found her and that was the whole of what he had come for, and the rest of it was hers.

Two hours in the wet with the sound of rain on a board roof and neither of them saying anything.

And what she felt for the two hours was not one thing. It was two, and they arrived at the same time and they are the same size.

Vindication, because he had spent twenty-seven days and she had not made it easy and it had worked, and everything she had worked out about a page and a date and a place was correct and she had counted it right.

And shame, because the only reason it had worked is that a man of fifty with four boards could not stand it any more and said so to a stranger on a made road.

## What She Said

She said it out loud once, at the ninth hour, in the ordinary voice, standing in the wet by a trough with a man of nine hundred years nine feet off her.

“You have asked eleven people about me in twenty-seven days,” she said. “And every one of them has been watching a girl with a bar since the ninth of a month because they think something has come for her, and you are the reason I have been found.”

He did not answer that either.

She put the skin down in the mud and straightened up and she asked her question.

“A man told me once that the door is the thing you do when you get to the top of a realm and there is no document to take you past it,” she said. “I have got to the top of a realm and I have got past it twice and I have opened a piece of paper with my own two hands, alone, with a bar under a door. So the door is the other thing. The only other thing.”

“And there is a bar on two posts at the end of the long road and a wall in the ground past it, and I have been standing in front of that wall for five days and I have not got past it and I am not going to be able to get past it on my own.”

“What is left on the far side of it when the person it concerns opens it and there is nobody qualified in the room.”

## What He Did

He did not answer her.

And he did not say no. That is the thing and she has thought about the difference for a long time after.

He stood in the rain and he looked at her for as long as it takes to count to forty, and then he reached down into the cloth bag at his side for the book, and stopped with his hand on the strap.

And he left it in the bag.

He did not say she could not have it. He did not say it was not his to give. He did not say the question was badly put or that she should come back in a week.

He took his hand off the strap and he shut his hand and he stood there in the wet for another count of forty, and then he turned and went up the trough road at the speed of a man with his weight on one leg, and he did not look back, and she watched him go a quarter of a mile.
