# It Does Not Come Back

She went back to the cut at the fourth hour of the fifteenth of a month to find out whether the stone had been worth anything to anybody, and she stood in the working part of the ground and waited for it to say so, and it did say so, and she spent two days learning that she was not going to be able to spend it.

## The Boards

There is a board outside the room with the six day books and the board is four feet by three and it is ruled in a hand she can take, and it carries every line of work anybody on this ground has offered in eleven weeks.

Forty-one lines a month, for two years, and she has read the whole of this one twice and can take none of it, and she read it again on the fifteenth with her finger under the ruled lines going along, and the finger stopped four times.

Four things a person on this ground has wanted done past the line. Stone off the cut up over the eighth foot. Two lengths of sill, forty yards, by the length. A chain stiff on a gate, and oil for it. Nine hundred feet of ditch, cut side, by the yard, on the north end.

Every one of them is still on the board and every one of them has been on the board since she first read it.

And on the fifteenth there was a fifth thing and it was not a line, it was a space, and there was a line on it, and there was nothing written on it, and she stood in the road outside and read it for a quarter of an hour.

## A Space With A Line On It

A space on that board means a thing is wanted and has not been taken.

It is the only kind of writing on four feet by three that she can do anything about at all, because every other kind of it is a line somebody has already put themselves under.

It had been there since the twelfth, which is the day she carried the stone, and it had been put there by a hand she could take, and it said nothing about who wanted it or what it paid.

And it stayed empty for three days, and there are eight men who work that cut every day and not one of them has touched it, and she stood in the road and looked at it and worked out the reason in four seconds and it is the same reason as the six books and the nine men and the eleven people on this ground.

They would have to ask her.

## The Second Time

It came up at the fifth hour, under her boots, on the working part of the cut, with eight men on the frame and the light going.

It came the way it came the first time and the way it has come four hundred times, out of the place low behind her fourth rib, and it went up through the soles of her boots and out into the open about a foot and stood there.

She watched it go up. She watched it go the other way, which is the part she had not seen before, or had not looked at before.

It did not come back into her.

On the twelfth of a month, a mile down a flat, she had watched it come and go and had put a boot on the place where it had been standing and found the ground warmer. She had thought that was the end of it.

It is not the end of it. It goes down. It goes into the ground and it keeps going, and the ground of a cut that nine men have been working for four hundred years takes it the way a room takes a figure.

And then it is somewhere else, and she has no idea where, and she can stand on that ground until her boots come through.

## Subtraction

She did the arithmetic standing up at the mouth of the track with the light going off the cut, and she has never in eight years had a sum come out like this one.

On the twelfth, the thing came up. On the thirteenth and the fourteenth she did not get up off the ground at all. On the fifteenth it came up again.

In between: nothing that anybody could point at. Nothing in a book. Nothing on a board. Nothing in a room.

She was no less hungry. She was thinner. Her arm was open at her side for the whole of the fifteenth and it was going to be open on the sixteenth and the seventeenth and the day after that, at four days in five, and she is writing that down because she wants it in the page and not as a mood.

So: a thing came up under her feet on two days and neither of those days had anything at the end of it.

She stood in the road and worked out the third line of it and the third line is the one she has not been able to put anywhere since.

There is one more thing in the world that pays a man she cannot see.

## What A Settlement Is

She has seen one land. That is the whole of her experience of this and she has been carrying it for a month and it is worth setting down properly because it is the only evidence she has.

A man of thirty-four came off a frame four hundred years old with his leg wrapped to the ankle. She fitted it on a low bed with a leg-man’s sheet and a mould and a scraper and her own two hands, and she could not be paid for it and the man could not pay her.

Two days later a piece of clay came up out of the ground under her boots on that frame.

And nine days after that, in a room nine feet deep with a rate at the head of the book in a hand that is not hers, a woman wrote a figure on a line with the fifteenth against it, because a man had come down a lane and said he had done a thing.

That is what a settling does. It moves a thing from the ground into a place where a person can be paid for having done it.

And on the fifteenth of this month it did that, and there was no room, and no rate, and no woman, and no man who had done anything, because she carried a stone up a stair on her own account and it is not in any of the six books.

## Where It Went

So it went into the ground’s own record of work, which is a thing any hand anywhere can go looking for work in, and which is not a room and has no woman in it and does not know that she exists.

That is the finding. She got it out on flat ground a mile west of the cut at the eighth hour, sitting on a stone, and it took eleven minutes and she said it out loud once, in the ordinary voice, with nothing within nine hundred yards.

“The work went in and the man did not,” she said. “It is written down somewhere that a person did a thing and there is no person, and no rate, and no woman, and nothing can ever be found out about it.”

Then she said the other half of it, because she says everything out loud exactly once and she was not going to have the second half sitting in her unsaid.

“It is the same shape as a page,” she said. “Except a page has a man behind it.”

## The Cold One

The anger had been clean and cold for six days and on the fifteenth it went out of her entirely, and she noticed it go, and that was worse than the anger.

She cannot spend it on the nine men. They were correct. She cannot spend it on the man who left bread on a stone wall. He gave nothing.

She cannot spend it on a woman of forty-one who writes names in a book and did not write hers, because the woman did not refuse her anything. There was nothing to refuse. A stone went up a stair and there is no line in six books that it fits into and the woman has no more say in that than the ground has.

And she cannot spend it on the thing itself, because she cannot see it and does not know where it has gone and cannot get at it.

That is a new shape and it is the first one in eight years. She has been angry eleven times that she can count in eight years and every single one of them had a person in it.

## The Open Place

She got her hand up under her own ribs at the ninth hour on the flat and found it, out of habit, because she has been finding it since the eighteenth of a month in a year she could name.

Open. The size of a coin. The place where the thing comes out of, and it came out twice on the twelfth and the fifteenth and it does not close and there is nothing above her to close it with.

That is all she has to show for it. A hole the size of a coin in the side of a person of eighteen that opens into whatever it is that pays a man she cannot see, and eleven feet of stair, and nothing else.

## The Only Instrument

She was lying on flat ground a quarter of a mile west of the cut at the eleventh hour with the bag under her head and the bar across her knees and she worked out the last of it, and it took four minutes and it is the reason she got up the next morning and walked.

Everything on nine days of road is one of three things. It is a favour, and a favour lasts until it costs somebody something. It is a book, and a book takes a name and a man and a figure. It is a hand out of the ground, and a hand out of the ground does not know where she is and cannot write anything.

One of those three can find her.

There is a book in the world with a man behind it, and she is in it in forty words with a date and a place on it, and the man walked four miles up a lane and sat against a shut door for two hours because of what she said on a bench.

She has been spending four months making sure nothing could find her. She has one instrument left and she has had it in her hand since the third of a month, and she gave it away on purpose, and she is going to have to go and ask a man of nine hundred years for the use of it.
