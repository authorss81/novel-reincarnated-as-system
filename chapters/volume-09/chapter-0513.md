# The Only Instrument She Has

She came back to the cut on the eighteenth of a month at the fourth hour to look at a space on a board, and it was still empty, and she looked at it on the nineteenth, and it was still empty, and she did not go up it on the twentieth either.

That is three days and it is the whole of what three days came to.

## Three Days Of A Space

A person who wants a thing done goes and stands in front of the board and reads down it and then goes and gets whoever is going to do it, and that is the whole of the arrangement, and it has been the arrangement for four hundred years.

She stood in front of that board for two hours on the twentieth, in the rain, reading down forty-one lines with her finger under them, and she could do every one of them.

Stone off the cut up over the eighth foot. She did that on the twelfth and four men could not.

Two lengths of sill, forty yards, by the length. She has measured a sill with her own two hands since she was fifteen.

A chain stiff on a gate, and oil for it. Nine hundred feet of ditch, cut side, by the yard.

And a space with a line on it and nothing written on it, put there by a hand she can take, on the twelfth of a month, by somebody who wants something done on this ground and cannot get anybody to do it.

Eight men come out of that cut every morning and nine men go up a frame four hundred years old and not one of them has put a name under that space in three days.

And a space with nothing in it only ever gets written on by the one who comes and asks for it.

## Six Days

She had eaten on the third of a month and on the twelfth, a third of a loaf standing up in the rain both times, and there had been nothing since.

That is six days since the twelfth and fifteen since the third, and it is not hunger any more. That finished in the fourth month and she worked out at the time that it was not a gift.

What six days is: the fourth count of breath does not come back on the first go. It comes back in four seconds on an empty reach and nine on a full one, and she has measured both, and she has a stone off the cut still up over a painted mark because she has not been back up the stair since the twelfth and does not know yet whether she can.

Four fingers and a thumb. The right arm open four days in five. Eight of eleven holds, and she went through them on the eighteenth on a level stone floor and got eight.

The open place behind her fourth rib has not changed since the eighteenth of a month in a year she could name and it is what the thing comes out of and it does not close.

She is thinner than she was a month ago and thinner than she was in the fourth month. She is eighteen years old.

## The Count She Is Keeping Now

She stopped counting toward something in the fourth month and she wants that written down, because it is the only thing in eight years that has changed in the way a person changes.

For two years she had a line in the world and she wanted it. Forty-one lines a month. A rate behind a counter. A bench outside a store with bread on it one day in four. Forty-one lines a month for two years and every one of them was a line going towards something.

Since the fourth month of that year she has been counting what is, and it is a different job and it does not have a direction in it.

Six things, on the twentieth, in the rain, at the mouth of a track.

She can carry anything on this ground. Nobody will give her anything to carry.

She can do the work of one man and most of a second. Nobody will put her on it.

She can fit a leg and a sill and a chain and nine hundred feet of ditch. Nobody has asked.

She cannot be paid. That has been true since a room in a month she could name.

She is the strongest person within nine hundred yards of every point on nine days of road, and there is not one thing on nine days of road that wants a strong person.

And the sixth thing.

## The Sixth Thing

A page carries a date and it does not carry a place, and there is exactly one page in the world with her words on it, and it is in a book, and the book is with a man who walked four miles up a lane and sat on the ground against a shut door for two hours because of what she said on a bench.

That is the whole of it. That is the sixth thing in the count and it is the only one with a person behind it.

And she has not seen him since the seventh of a month and she does not know where he is, and there is not one office on nine days of road that could look, and there is not one person on nine days of road who knows that a man of nine hundred years exists.

She could go and stand in front of a woman of fifty at the end of this lane and ask her where the man who wrote on my bench is, and the woman of fifty would not know, and she would remember the asking, and eleven people would be right about the shape of it.

## What Going To Him Means

She got it out on flat ground half a mile west of the cut at the seventh hour, in the rain, standing up, and it took four minutes, and she said it out loud once.

She was not afraid of the asking. She has asked four things of strangers in eight years and got nothing every time and it has never once cost her anything.

She was afraid of the sentence that goes with it.

If she goes and stands in front of him she has to say out loud that there is nothing on nine days of road that wants her, and that she has had a third of a loaf twice in fifteen days, and that she cannot be paid.

She has to say that her arm is open four days in five, and that the only thing in the world that can find her is a page she gave away on a bench, and she has to say it to a man who does not need any of it.

He wants one line in a book. As kept or as lost, and he does not prefer one. He has said in his own words that the writing does not save one of them.

He has never needed her. He walked the lane four times and he sat against her door for two hours and he did not knock, and he has not sent for anything, and he is not going to.

And she is going to go and stand in front of him and ask him to be the only instrument she has left.

## She Said It

She said it out loud once, on the lane, at the seventh hour of the twentieth, in the ordinary voice, with rain coming down and nothing within nine hundred yards.

“I am going to go and find the man with the book,” she said.

Then she stood there in the lane and let that be a thing for a quarter of an hour, and then she worked out what she actually had decided, and it was not the sentence.

What she had decided was the recklessness of a permission.

She has been saving something for four months. Not a thing anybody can take off her — a silence, bought with a refusal and a sheet of paper folded in four against her ribs, and it was the best thing that had ever happened to her and she has said that out loud on a made road once.

She is going east to spend it. Past everything. To a gate she has never been through, on a road she has walked two miles of and turned round on.

And she wants it in the plainest words available, because it is the last thing on this ground she cannot get back.

## Ning Wu

There is one more count and she has been keeping it for eight years and she did not do it on the twentieth and she thought about doing it.

Ten years is a line on a page in an office and it has no mark on it and it has had no mark on it since she was ten years old. She has wanted it in a book she could get at ever since and there has been exactly one book in eight years that a person could be found in, and she gave that one away on a bench.

She did not say his name out loud. She says everything out loud exactly once and she had not said that name in eight years and she was not starting on a lane in the rain.

And that was the whole of the wanting and it took nine seconds, and she put it down beside the other five things in her head, where it has been since she was ten.

## East

She did not tell one living person.

She went back past the frame and up the track with the bar at the light end and a third of a loaf in a bag and nothing at all in her, and she came in at the lane end at the second hour of the twenty-first.

She went past the shed at the end of it without looking at it, and past the crossing with its painted board and its chain and the hut with a light in it, and on to the long road.

The long road is made and flat and goes east. She walked it four miles on the fourteenth of a month in a month that has gone, stood in the middle of it at the ninth hour with nothing behind her and nothing in front of her, and turned round, and she has thought about that four minutes on and off ever since.

The boy at the crossing was on the chain and he did not ask her a question and she did not give him a look, and that has been the arrangement between the two of them since the third of a month, and it is the only place on nine days of road where nothing has been decided.

She did not turn round this time.

She walked it all the way, and at the end of it there is a gate with a chain on it and a cup hanging off the chain, and past the gate there is a bar on two posts that people go through, and she has never been past either of them in her life.
