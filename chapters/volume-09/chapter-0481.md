# Forty-One Lines Twice

She went down the lane at the sixth hour on the seventeenth and stood at the rail outside the lifting floor, which is the fourth time in two years she has stood at that rail with a bar against her leg and the whole of a morning in front of her.

The sun comes up off flat ground half an hour before that and by the sixth hour it is over the whole of the yard and the board is in full light, and she has never once read that board in the dark and she is not going to start.

There were four people on the floor. That matters and she will come back to it.

## The Board Outside The Floor

Forty-one lines. It was twenty-nine when she came to this ground two years back and it has gone up twelve since, and it is full, and a full board is a board that cannot take another line and is not going to and will not be at the end of a month or the middle of one.

Every line on it is measured by the weight of what comes out of the ground or by the length of it. Loads by the load. Dressed stone by the yard. A chain that wants oil. Nine men and a frame that is four hundred years of oak and comes down the wrong way. A man of thirty-four who lost a leg on the fifteenth of a month and wants it dressed.

Forty-one lines, in a hand she can read, on a board that is full.

That is the trade she has got. She has had it for eight years and it has never once wanted her, and she can prove that in nine seconds.

## Forty-One Down

She read them.

She read them the way she has read every board for eight years, which is down the left column first and then the right, and then the notes underneath, and then the date, and then the whole thing again from the top to be sure of the ones with two figures against them.

Loads. Stone. The chain. The frame. The leg. Four hundred and eleven pounds of dressed stone for a man who wants it inside a month and will pay what it costs. Nine men, four days, the frame coming down on the third. Four hundred yards of paving at a rate she can say out loud.

And forty-one of them.

It took her four minutes, which is what it has always taken her, and the hand on the board has not got worse in forty days and has not got better either, and there is a man who can read it and there is a woman who can read it and there is a boy on the stair who can read a third of it.

## The Same Twice

Then she read all forty-one of them down again, and she got them the same twice.

That is not a small thing to be able to say.

Eight years ago she could read a hand that four people could not read, and there is still not one figure in forty-one that comes out different twice. She has that by doing it in bad light and in good light and on a board in the rain and on a board with somebody’s elbow against the end of it.

Forty-one lines read down twice. Forty-one lines taken.

None.

She stood with her hands open at her sides in the middle of the working part of a floor at the sixth hour and read forty-one lines of work twice and took none of it, and she did that on purpose, in daylight, with four people within thirty feet of her who could have watched her do it.

She did it because the last time she did not do it on purpose was a morning in a winter four years back, and she had spent the eight months since discovering that she had not been able to read forty-one lines and refuse them.

That is a different thing from a reach that will not act. A reach that will not act is a rule about a body and she has had that since she was fourteen and she can live inside it. What happened on the seventeenth of this month at the sixth hour is that she stood in front of forty-one lines she could have read out loud in four minutes and she wanted none of them, and there was not a single moment in the four minutes where she could have pointed at the wanting and called it a rule.

She was not stopping. She was not being stopped.

She had simply run out of reasons, and she had done the reading first and worked that out afterwards, which is the opposite of the order she does everything else in.

## The Arithmetic Under It

She had four fingers and a thumb and she brought the bar up off the ground with them and set it down again on the boards of the rail, and she waited for the thing that has waited four hundred times in eight years in a room with a man in it and has never once waited for nothing.

The reach answers a question. That is the whole of it. She learned that at fourteen in a room with a man of thirty in it and she has not been able to break it since, and it does not go out and take the fourth load because she is standing next to it. It does not go out and take the chain because the chain is what she can see. It goes out when she has asked one thing out loud in the ordinary voice, and it takes exactly that, and it will not take a second thing in the same hour.

She stood at the rail and she asked nothing.

That was the whole of the work she did that morning. She stood there at the sixth hour with a full board and four people on the floor and she asked that rail for nothing, out loud, on purpose, with her mouth, and it did what it does when she is asked nothing, which is nothing.

She had done it twelve times in two years before this one, and every one of the twelve had cost her something, and this one cost her four minutes and a decision.

## The Four Coppers She Did Not Take

The other thing that happened on a floor like this one was eight months ago and it is the reason she does not read for anybody any more, and it has nothing to do with wanting to.

A man put four coppers on the wood after he had asked her three questions about a rate over four months, and he put them down and did not move, and she stood there with her hand half an inch over the wood.

She cannot take anything that is given to her. That is not a choice and it is not modesty. It is a thing her hand does, the way her breath does four counts and not three, and there is nothing above her in the world that can change it.

So the hand hung in the air over four coppers and would not close, and a man of forty swept them back across the counter, untaken, and said nothing wrong about it, and went on working.

That was eight months ago and it is the reason a floor stopped asking her a question a month and the reason her name is not on any board on nine days of road, and she has never once said out loud that she would have taken them if she could have.

She would have. That is the part she carries. She would have taken four coppers off that counter in eight months and it would have been the right thing to do and there is no version of her hand that would have let her, and that is the exact shape of the last eight months.

## With People Standing There

What the four people did is the part she wants.

The boy from the stair came down and went back up and did not look at her. A carter came in with two things on a hand bar and set them down and went out. The man with the brush came out of the office, looked at the board, went in, and came out again nine minutes later with the brush wet.

The man behind the counter asked her a question about a rate.

He asked it the way he asks it. He has asked it four times in eight years, and it has not been four times in eight months, and it is not a greeting and it is not an invitation and it is not interest in her.

It is the ordinary way that man talks to anybody who comes within reach of his voice.

And she answered it. She said the rate, which is four, and he said thank you, and that was the whole of the exchange, and none of it had anything to do with her, and none of it would have happened if she had not been there, and none of it would have happened differently if she had been somebody else.

That is what being left alone looks like from the middle of it. There is no difference at all between a floor that has forgotten you and a floor that has never had you.

## The Question She Has Not Asked

She walked back up to the shed at the seventh hour and she sat on the boards, and she worked out the day, which is what she does instead of a great many other things.

Four fingers and a thumb. Forty-one lines read twice. Four people on a floor and not one of them asked her anything she could have been useful at, and one of them asked her a question and it was not about her.

And then she worked out a second thing, which she had not worked out before, and which took her the rest of the morning.

In two years there is exactly one person on that ground who has never asked her a single question, and has never once been asked one by her either.

The woman in the store has said “good” to her two hundred times. That is the whole of the two years. She has never asked where Ning Yan sleeps, or what she does, or why she is carrying a bar she cannot hold at the heavy end, or where a girl of eighteen came from, and she has never once asked her a question at all.

Ning Yan has never asked her one either.

She has stood at that counter and bought bread and said nothing and it has been the easiest thing in the world, and she has never noticed that she has done it, and she noticed it on the seventeenth of a month at the seventh hour sitting on the boards with her hands round her knees.

And she is going to go and ask her something.

Not a real question. She is not ready for a real question and she knows exactly what she wants and it is the easiest question there is.

She wants to know what happens to bread nobody buys.
