# The Record Is Accurate

She asked him what the book was for at the second hour of the tenth of a month, standing on her own side of the jamb, and he answered it in six sentences over seven hours, and the sixth of them is the only wrong thing anybody has said to her in eight years.

## The Question

She said it in the ordinary voice and she did not ask it nicely and she had spent three days getting it down to five words.

“What is the book for,” she said.

He put his weight on the other leg and looked at her for four seconds.

“It is not a place to join,” he said.

That is the first one and it is seven words long and it is not the answer.

## Nine Feet Off

There was a man nine feet off her left side with a notebook shut under his arm and his weight on one leg and his face doing nothing at all.

She had not seen him come and she did not start. He has never once come nearer than nine feet and he was not going to start, and he had been on the wrong side of a bar on two posts since the seventh month of a year she could name, which is the second time in his life he has been somewhere she had not already been.

He said nothing. He does not begin. She has to ask him and she asked him at the fifth hour of the tenth, in the ordinary voice, without turning round, because turning round would have been an answer to something he had not said.

“Bai Zhu.”

Then she asked him the question and it came out in fourteen words and it is the only thing she asked him for in the whole of that ground.

“Is a person written as lost safer than a person not written at all.”

## The Second Answer

He was quiet for nine seconds, which is longer than the four or five he takes to decide whether a question has been asked properly.

“I do not know,” he said.

That is the first answer and it is all of it and she had expected anything except it.

Then he said the other thing, in the ordinary voice, and it is the second thing he has ever said to her that was not an answer.

“You asked me,” he said. “That is the second question anybody has ever asked me that I would rather not answer.”

He did not say what the first one was and she did not ask him.

He did not ask her for anything. He has never asked her for anything, not once, and he did not ask her for anything at an opening in a wall with a book open on a table behind her, and whatever it is he has come this far for is still where it was in the ninth month of a year she could name, and it is still pending, and nothing that happened on that flat moved it an inch.

## Back To The Man In The Wall

She turned back at the ninth hour and he was where he had been, on his own side, with the book open on two feet of plank table.

“What is the book for,” she said again, because he had answered seven words in forty seconds and she wanted the rest of it.

“It is not a punishment either,” he said.

Then he was quiet for a while, and she stood there, and the wind came across a hundred yards of flat and she waited for it.

“It is a record of the ones the bound took,” he said.

That is the third one and he said it in the ordinary voice, and he did not say anything after it for a minute, and she understood standing there that he had said three sentences and there were two more and that he was going to say them whether she asked or not.

## The Record Is Accurate

“The record is accurate,” he said.

That is the fourth one and it is four words and it is the plainest sentence anybody has said to her in eight years, including four said out of the dark on the sixteenth of a month at a hundred yards.

“The accuracy is the whole of it,” he said.

Five sentences. He shut the book and picked up the strap and she counted them on her fingers on her own side of the jamb and did not ask him for a sixth, and that is the whole of what nine hundred years of writing down every person the bound has swallowed came to when a woman finally asked in daylight.

## The Sixth

He put the book under his arm and then he stopped with his weight on the leg he had been using and he said one more thing, and it is the reason this is the chapter and not an ordinary one.

“And a person on my book is better kept than a person off it.”

That is the whole of it. He said it once, in the ordinary voice, to a woman standing on the other side of a jamb, and then he picked up the book and went back through the opening into the far ground, and it cost him four steps and he shut nothing behind him because there is nothing on that side to shut.

He does not know he is wrong about it.

That is on the page and it is not a thing she is going to have to argue with anybody about for nine hundred years, because he is not going to be argued with. He is correct about all the rest of it.

## She Cannot Correct Him

She wanted to be angry for eleven minutes at the ninth hour of the tenth, standing on flat ground with the bar against her shoulder, and she got four minutes of it and then it turned into something worse.

He is right about the bound taking people. He is right that there is a ceiling and it is real. He is right that an unrecorded taking is worse than a recorded one, and she believes that, and she has believed it since a room with the shutters up in the second year of the realm.

And he is right about one more thing that she has never once been able to say out loud to him, which is that a person no one has heard of is easier to kill. She has thought that at the ninth hour of nine hundred nights. He has thought it for nine hundred years and written it down nine hundred years of times.

He is wrong about one sentence and he is wrong about it because he has nine hundred years of evidence and she has four minutes of standing on flat ground.

And she cannot correct him, and she is not going to try.

She has never in eight years gone back to a person and explained a thing to them after they had already made it up. She says no and she goes, and it has worked eight years, and the reason it has worked is that she has never had to do anything else.

She is not going to argue with a man about the meaning of the word kept. She is not going to give him a reason. She does not have a reason; she has a thing that happened to her in the fifth month of a year she could name and four fingers and a thumb and an open place behind her fourth rib, and that is not a reason, it is a set of injuries.

## What The Six Sentences Added Up To

She said it out loud once, at the eleventh hour of the twelfth, sitting on the boards with her back against a wall four feet high that does not go over her head.

So I am on a book, and he has written nine hundred years of the ones that came, and he says it is accurate, and the accuracy is the whole of what the book is for.

And he says I am better kept.

That is what she asked for and that is what she got and it took three days of five words and seven hours and a man nine feet off who did not know any of it.

## What Five Sentences Come To

She was not able to leave it alone and she did not try.

Three days. She went over the five of them the way she goes over a figure, one at a time, on the boards, at the fourth hour and the ninth hour, and she found that the third one was doing all the work.

It is not a place to join. That is a sentence for a person who has heard about it. She has never once thought it was a place to join and she has never once been told it was one.

It is not a punishment. That is a sentence for a person who has done something. She has done things. She is not sure that one of them was not a punishment and she is not going to find out and he is not going to tell her.

It is a record of the ones the bound took.

That one is about the taking and not about her, and it is the only one of the five she has never had a version of from anybody, and she sat with it for a long time on the eleventh at the second hour.

A record is not a thing you keep people in. A record is a thing you write down so that it happened.

She has been entered four times in eight years and every one of those four times was somebody making a record, and every one of the four had a use for her afterwards. She has worked out in four months that a record and a use are two different things, and that this is the first person in eight years who has offered her the first one without the second.

## What She Is Not Going To Do About The Sixth

There is an argument she could make and it took her nine minutes on the eleventh and she made it to herself once, out loud, standing at the opening, and then she threw it away.

She could put it in a book of her own. Four sentences in a hand that cannot write a false line, on paper she made out of a blanket, and hand it to him or leave it where it can be found.

That is a good argument and it is the best one she has got and it is eleven words long.

She is not going to make it, and the reason is that the four sentences are not written yet, and she has one line in a book of her own for the exact purpose of being the one that does say what happened, and she is not going to muddy it before it is written.

He is wrong about one sentence out of nine hundred years. She is not going to be the person who tells him why and gets the four sentences wrong instead.

## Two Words And A Table

There are two words. She has had them since the second month of a year she could name and she can say them both back in the ordinary voice whenever she wants to.

One of them has to be written in his hand on that table, while the book is open, while he is standing on his side of the jamb with the light coming off the flat, and it cannot be written anywhere else and it cannot be written at another hour, because the table is in the opening and the table is in the opening from the fourth hour to the eleventh and there is no other table in nine hundred years with that book on it.

She worked that out on the twelfth at the sixth hour, standing in the opening, and it is the first thing she has understood about the demand that is not the words at all.

It is a day and an hour and a man with a brush.

She has twelve days of it and she does not know what she is going to do with any of them.
