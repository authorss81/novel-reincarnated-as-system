# A Line On No Board

She went back to the cut at the fourth hour of the twelfth of a month with no bread in her since the third, and she stood on the low side of it for a quarter of an hour and worked out what she was going to do there, and it took her nine minutes and the answer was not good.

## The Line On The Upright

The frame is four hundred years old and it is going up eleven feet in six weeks and there is a mark painted on the upright at the eighth foot.

Below the mark a man carries one side of a thing on his own and another man takes the other side and the two of them go up a ladder that is not a ladder, which is a stair cut into the frame itself with a rail on it.

Above the mark, one man carries it.

That is the line and it is the whole of what the line is. Four men have gone above it in six weeks. Two men went up with a stone out of the cut at noon on the twelfth and got it to the eighth foot and had to bring it down again because the frame moved when the left one turned, and they stood at the bottom of the stair for a quarter of an hour looking at it.

The mark is not a rule anybody made. It is where the frame stops holding itself still and starts holding a man.

## What She Was Going To Do There

She worked out all of it on the flat west of the cut at the fourth hour, standing up, with nothing to sit on.

She was not going there to be paid. Her hand will not take it and it has not taken it since a room in a month she could name and there is no sense in a woman standing in a cold room for a figure at the end of a week.

She was not going there to be asked. Nobody has asked her for anything in eleven days and she has not made it happen and she is not going to be able to.

She was going there because it is the only place within nine days of road where a thing has ever been asked of her that she is the only person who can do, and there is a stone at the bottom of the stair that four men have brought up time and again and put down again.

That is the whole of her reasoning and it is not good and she went anyway.

## Above The Mark

She went up at the sixth hour in the afternoon with nine men on the ground and four of them watching from the top of the frame, and not one of them asked her to.

That is the first thing and it is the thing she wants in the page: she went up the stair without being asked to and without asking. She stood at the foot of the stair for four minutes and four of them looked at her and not one of them said a word, and she said the sentence to herself in the ordinary voice before she put her hand on the rail.

“You do not have to do this,” said a man of forty at the top of the frame, from nine feet up, in the ordinary voice.

“All right,” Ning Yan said.

And she went up.

## The Reach

The stone was off the cut floor and it was the weight of a fullgrown man and there is no name for it on nine days of road because there is no man to put it against, which she worked out afterwards and not at the time.

She got it up off the ground with her left hand under it and her right hand on the edge, and her right arm went open on the second foot of the stair, the whole way, not halfway and not at the end.

She did not stop. That is the thing with a reach that answers a question and only a question: it does not stop when the arm goes, and it does not take advice from anybody, and four counts of breath is what she has everywhere including inside one.

She went up the stair at the speed of a woman with a bar on her shoulder. Eight feet. Nine. The frame moved under her when the left one turned at the sixth foot and she felt it go and she kept her weight over the rail and went on, and at the tenth foot her fourth count went and did not come back.

It came back in four seconds. She has measured it and it has never once taken longer than that in eight years and there is nothing in the world she can do with four seconds.

Then she got it over the mark.

## What It Cost In The Body

It came off her left shoulder at the mark and went down onto the frame and she took her hand off it and her left arm went out straight in front of her and stayed out straight in front of her for nine seconds, which is what four fingers and a thumb does when the four fingers have been holding a stone for eleven feet of stair.

Her knees went. Not far. About four inches, and one boot forward, and her left hand went flat on the frame rail and she stayed on it and she did not go down.

The frame stood. That is the first fact. Four hundred years of it and it stood with one woman on it at the mark and one stone on it and four men at the top who had both hands on the rail and did not need to.

The second fact is that she was nine days without bread and it came out of her at the tenth foot and not at the first, because a loaded reach is a thing she does with the front of her and there is nothing left in the front of her after the second day of nothing to eat.

She came down the stair on her own two feet. Nothing was carried for her and nobody came up behind her.

## Nobody Wrote It Down

That is the part that is left.

Four men were on that frame above the mark. Four men had watched a woman of eighteen carry a stone up eleven feet of stair past a mark that four men could not get a stone over in six weeks.

And at the seventh hour they all came down and washed their hands and went to the room with the six day books and eleven men said their names and said what they had done, and not one of the four said a word about the stone.

She went and stood in the road outside and waited, because she is a person who has been written in a day book twice in eight years and she knows what it looks like, and nothing came out.

She was not owed anything. She was not refused. There is no line on any board on nine days of road that a stone carried up on no person’s order can go into, because a day book takes a name and a man and a figure and there was no man and no name and no figure.

She went and stood in the door of the room with the six day books and read the last page of the second book upside down from six feet off, and she reads a hand four people cannot take, and she read eleven names and eleven figures in nine seconds.

Not one of them was a stone.

## The Stone Wall

There is a low stone wall at the mouth of the track down into the cut and it is four feet high and it is where the men have put their things for thirty years.

At the seventh hour a man she has never spoken to put a loaf down on it, squared it up with his hand, and went off up the track without looking at it and without looking at her.

That is the arrangement. That has been the arrangement for two years and it is the only one that has ever worked: a thing left out on a surface and not handed to anybody.

She ate a third of it standing up and put the other two thirds in the bag, because that is what she does, and she did not say thank you, and she is not going to, and she stood there for a while afterwards with her hand flat on the stone.

She has not been given anything in eight years. A man of thirty she has never spoken to left bread on a wall and walked away and that is the whole of what came back to her for the stair, and she does not know his name and he does not know hers.

## What She Took Up On Purpose

She walked four hundred yards east on flat ground at the seventh hour with nothing in her and the arm open at her side and she said one thing out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, standing in the middle of a made road with the frame coming down behind her at the wrong end.

Then she stood there and she worked out why she had done it, and it took four minutes, and the finding is the finding.

It is the oldest thing she has and it goes back further than any of it.

Eight years ago a man in a room with a brush told her that a person who can carry anything is a person who is useful, and she has been carrying things ever since — four hundred loads, four hundred times, for eight years, and every one of them for somebody, and every one of them inside a shape that somebody else had made first.

That is what she took up on purpose, on the twelfth of a month, above a painted mark, in daylight, on a body at half.

She carried a stone up a stair for nobody at all, past a line nobody drew for her, and there is not one word in the six books, and she is not going to be able to spend that on anybody either, and she would do it again tomorrow.

## What Came Up Under Her

She got a mile down the flat before she stopped.

Not fast. She had not been going fast since the mark. She went at the speed of a woman with four fingers and a thumb and nine days of nothing in her and an arm open at her side, and a mile later her boots came down harder than the ground asked for, twice, and then a third time, and she stopped and stood in the road.

It came up out of the ground under her feet.

It came out of the place low behind her fourth rib that has been open the size of a coin since the eighteenth of a month in a year she could name, and it went up through the boots and the flat and a foot into the air and stood there, and it was there for nine seconds.

She watched it come and she watched it go.

There was nothing to weigh. There was nothing to measure. She put her boot flat on the place where it had been standing and the ground was ordinary ground, and it was warmer under the boot than it had been nine seconds before, and that was all of it, and she stood in a made road for a quarter of an hour waiting to find out what she had just been paid for.
