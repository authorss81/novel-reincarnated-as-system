# Twelve Feet Off

He did not move and he did not speak, and he had been standing there for four minutes before she came round the bend and saw him, which she worked out afterwards by the light.

She came four miles up the cart track on the seventh of the month with a bar on her shoulder and nothing in her, and it took her two hours, and she went out at the fifth hour because she did not want to arrive in the dark.

There has been no bread on that ground since the twenty-first of a month. She has been down the trough road once in two days and has been living at one day out of seven out of it, and she has not been hungry for a hundred and forty-four days and that is finishing now, on a track, in a wind.

The wind came up out of the west and she had to work at it, and her right arm was open at the side the whole way and she put it into the strap of the bar for two hours of it, which is two hours of a body at the top of a realm doing nothing at all.

He was nine feet off the side of the track with a notebook shut under his arm and his weight on one leg and his face doing nothing at all. He has been nine feet off for four volumes and she has never once got closer and he has never once come nearer, and both of those are rules of his and not decisions he makes in front of her.

She stopped twelve feet off, which is three feet further than the last time.

“Bai Zhu,” she said.

That is the first time she has said it to him in person. She has never called him Hunter to his face and she was not going to start now, and a name is a thing you use when you have come four miles with a question.

He waited.

She had the whole of it ready and she got nine words into it and threw the rest away, because he has never once in his life responded to a thing she prepared.

## The Question

“Can a thing that is written about a person, without being read to them, be answered.”

He did not answer straight away, which she had not expected, and she found out afterwards that the four or five seconds were him deciding whether she had asked it properly.

“Yes or no,” she said. “That is all I have got.”

“No,” he said.

The wind took about half of it, the same as it takes half of everything out here on flat ground.

“It cannot be answered,” he said. “It is not a question. Somebody writes a thing about you and you never see it and there is no point at which you could have done anything about it, and then afterwards nothing you do reaches it.”

He put his head slightly over.

“I have watched it happen eleven times,” he said. “That is the whole of my experience of it and the method is me standing in the room and watching, eleven times, and each one of them was a person who did everything correctly for years afterwards and it made no difference whatever.”

## The Second Question

“And what happens to a person it happens to.”

He looked at her for a moment.

“They do not know it happened,” he said. “That is the part that does not get better with time. Eleven times, and not one of them has ever found out, and every one of them has gone on being a person somebody had written a true thing about.”

“That is not fair to say.”

“It is the accurate version,” he said. “There is a kinder version, which is that nobody had a reason to write it, and I have not seen that one either.”

And that was all of it, and he did not add anything to it, and she did not ask a third question, because there was not a third one in her.

## What She Did With It

She stood on a cart track with the bar against her leg and let that sit for nine seconds, and then she said the thing she had come four miles to say, which was:

“I have read mine.”

That was the whole of what she gave him and she gave it to a man standing nine feet off a road.

He did not ask what she meant. He did not ask where, or when, or what was in it, or who had put it there. He has never once asked her a question in eight years and she has got used to it in about the way a person gets used to a wall.

“You read it yourself,” he said.

“Yes.”

“Then it is not that.”

That was three words and it went through the whole of eight months like a bar through a ring, and she stood there and let it, and she did not make a smaller or a bigger thing out of it in front of him.

## The Notebooks

She looked at the notebook under his arm.

It is shut. It has been shut every time she has seen it in eight years, and he has not opened it in front of her once, and he has not offered to show it to her once, and he has not told her what is in it once.

She did not ask. That was a decision and it took her a second and a half and she made it standing in the open on a cart track.

“You have something pending,” she said. “You have had it pending for a long time and it is not raised and it is not going to be raised today.”

“No,” he said.

“Good.”

That was the entire exchange about it and there was not one word about what it was, and he had not brought it up and she had not asked and both of those were the correct thing to do and both of them cost something.

## Neither Of Them Wrote It Down

And then there is the other half of it, which is that neither of them wrote a word down.

He did not write it. She saw him not write it. He stood nine feet off a cart track with a notebook shut under his arm for nine minutes and did not take it out, and she has known him for eight years and she would stake a bar on the fact that he did not write it afterwards either, and she is right.

She did not write it either. She has an instrument that four people cannot take and she has had it for eight years and she used it nine hundred yards behind him on the track in four seconds, and she put two things on a board in a shed and that is all she did with it.

It is the first time either of them has had a conversation and there has been no record of it, and she has never before known what that feels like from her own side, and it felt like setting a bar down.

Then she said, “Thank you,” which she has not said to him in eight years and which is four coppers of bread and is the first thing she has said to any person in nine days.

“It is not a thing anybody asks for,” he said.

That is the whole of the man. She has known that since a room four years ago and she has never had it put to her as plainly as that, in eleven words, by somebody who is nine feet off and has never taken a step towards her.

And then he said the other thing, and it was not a question, and it was the last thing he said on that track:

“You are the first one who has come and asked me that.”

She did not ask him what he meant by first. There were forty people on nine days of road who had been entered four times apiece by things that read to them in rooms, and she was not going to stand on a cart track and make him account for the difference between them and her.

“Right,” she said.

He moved nine minutes after that. He went down the track at the ordinary speed, with the notebook under his arm, and he did not look back, and that is how she knew it had been the whole of it.

## The Shed That Night

She was up to the shed at the eighth hour and she did the ring and she lay down with the wind coming in under a door she had barred shut with a piece of bar.

The day had been four miles out on a wind with no bread and she slept the whole night through, and she woke at the third hour and could not get her breath for nine seconds and lay there until it came, and then got the fourth count and lay still and let it be four.

And what she came back with was not the answer, because it was only ever going to be the answer to one question.

What she came back with was a thing about the shed.

She lay on the boards and worked out what it was.

It was that she has been in this shed two years and has never once put a name on it, and that this month she has barred the door with her own bar and opened a paper on the boards and slept nine hours in it, and that she has begun, somewhere between the fifth and the sixth of this month, to think of it as a place where she is.

Not hers. Not yet. A place where she is.

She turned that over on the boards for nine minutes and it did not go anywhere and it did not turn into anything worse, and she let it lie there, which was new.

And then she worked out that she has nine days of it, and that the nineteenth of the month is the tenth day of them, and that there are things about her own body she has never once found out on purpose, and that the paper does not tell her how.

It opens once. She has read it. Reading is not opening.

Nine days.
