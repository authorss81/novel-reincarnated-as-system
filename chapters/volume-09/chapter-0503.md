# In His Own Hand

She told nobody for four days, which is not a decision she made. She got up on the fourth of a month and worked the ring in a dish and went to the cut at the second hour and stood at the frame while nine men took it down wrong twice. She went west to the store and did not buy anything and came back east.

There was not one person within eleven miles who knew that an old man had written forty seconds of brush on a bench at a crossing.

That is the first change and it is not a small one and it happened before anything else did.

For eight years every single thing that came for her came out of a document somebody else made. And the sentence she said on a bench at a crossing is the first thing she has ever handed anybody that was not read out of a book at her by a person who was qualified, and she handed it to a man with a brush and a rule and no staff and it took nineteen seconds to say and forty to write.

## The Seventh

He came up the lane on the seventh at the second hour in the afternoon and she was on the bench at the shed at the end of it with a leg-mould in a box beside her that she had gone and got from the cut at the fifth hour because it needs scraping and there is no stone on this ground that will do it.

He stopped nine feet off.

“You used it,” she said.

“I used the date,” he said.

She put the scraper down.

“There is a place on that page,” she said. “I gave you a place.”

“There is a place on that page,” he said, “and I have been up that lane four times since the third and I have not once used it.”

“Then what did you use.”

“The date,” he said, “which is the third of this month, and the rule is that a name goes in the book when a person gets to the top of a realm, and a person got to the top of a realm on that date, and there are not eleven people on this road who could be it, and I did not have to ask anybody anything.”

He put the cloth bag down on the ground beside him, which he had not done before.

“So a date in a book is worse than a place,” Ning Yan said.

“A date in a book is a person who is looking for one stage in one month,” he said. “A place in a book is a building with a door in it. I do not want a building. I have never wanted a building. I have wanted a person since the seventeenth of a month and it has taken me nine days to get one, and I would have walked another nine.”

## What She Had Bought

She sat on a bench with a scraper in her hand and worked out what she had actually done on the third of a month, and it came out different from what it had looked like at the second hour in the morning.

She thought she had given him a room. She has given him a date that means there is one person on nine days of road he does not have to be polite to.

And it was worth exactly as much as the walking and no more, and she had known that at the time, and she had done it anyway, and the thing she cannot get round is that she would like the walking to have been the thing that worked.

She said that out loud, once, on the bench, at the second hour of the seventh of a month, and she has not said it since and she is not going to.

## The Door

“You said a name goes in when a person gets to the top of a realm,” she said, “and there is no document to take them past it.”

“There is.”

“And you said a name goes in once, and I do not know why once.”

“Once is the rule and I did not make the rule,” he said, “and I have never been able to find out what once is for, and I have been doing it for nine hundred years, and it is the one part of this I would answer if anybody asked me a good question about it, and nobody has asked me a good question about it.”

She had one and she had been keeping it since the seventeenth.

“Is that the door.”

He did not shut the door. He waited four seconds, which for him was a long time.

“No,” he said.

## What She Said Then

She stood up off the bench and her right arm went open nine seconds before she got the words out, and it stayed open while she said it, and he stood nine feet off and watched her do it and did not come nearer.

“That is the only door in the world,” Ning Yan said. “It has been against my ribs for four years and I have opened it, alone, on the twenty-second of a month, with a bar under a door, and there are seven lines on it and four marks and four sentences at the foot of it and it is a door that opens for the person it is about and for nobody else, and I have read every word of it twice, and you have just told me it is not the door.”

“It is a document,” he said.

“It is a door.”

“It is a sheet of paper with four sentences on it in a hand half an inch tall and it has been folded in four for four years, and it opens for the person it is about, and it is not the door and I will not have it confused with the door, because I have been carrying it since I was sixteen and if it is only paper then I have been carrying paper.”

He did not answer for nine seconds.

“What is the door,” she said.

“It is the thing you do when you get to the top of a realm and there is no document.”

“You do not know what it is.”

“No,” he said. “I have kept nine hundred years of a rule about a door and I have never once seen it open, and I have two people in nine hundred years who came near it and neither of them finished.”

He picked his bag up off the ground.

“That is not a thing I can put in front of you and I am not going to try,” he said. “When you are stood in front of it, you will know what it is. Everybody has said that to me and I have believed every one of them and not one of them was wrong.”

## The Cut, On The Fifth

She went out to the cut on the fifth to scrape the leg-mould and Zheng was on the frame and his leg was out in front of him on the wall and the wood was holding.

He came down off it at the ninth hour and sat on the wall two feet off her, the way a man sits who has been on his feet since the fourth hour and has found the one place on that ground with room on it.

“You have been east,” he said.

“I have been east.”

He looked down the lane for a while with his hands hanging between his knees.

“I am not going to ask you what is at the end of it,” he said. “The last person who asked me where I got to was a woman in a doorway about a month ago and she asked it in the middle of a job, and I told her, and I have not been able to get anything finished since.”

“That is not what I would have asked.”

“No,” he said. “I know that. You have never asked me one single thing about where I get to, in eight years and about nine hundred miles, and I have had four months to notice it, and it is the reason I put that strip on the bar myself.”

He got his weight onto the frame and went up on it.

“There is a thing on that bench about a third of the time,” he said, “and you have not been at it once since the third.”

“I have been at it twice.”

“Twice in ten days is not a thing a body does that walks nine miles a day.”

“I know that,” Ning Yan said.

He looked at her.

“I am not going to say one word more about it,” he said. “I have nine men on this frame and a leg that is holding and a rate that has not moved since a month that has gone, and you are the only person on this ground who fitted that leg, and if I stand up here and lecture a girl about eating I will hear myself.”

He went the length of it and came down off the end and did not look back at her, and she sat on the wall with a scraper in her hand for a quarter of an hour afterwards and did not do anything with any of it.

## The Thing She Went Away With

She did not sleep on the seventh and she did not get up to wait for anything, and she lay on the bench with her clothes on and worked out four things in the dark, in order, and she is not going to say the fourth of them out loud to anybody for some time.

A piece of paper is not a door.

Nobody has read a page to her since the third of a month two months back and nobody is going to and she has been carrying the reason for that against her ribs ever since.

He cannot write her down until the door is open. That is not a mercy and he did not offer it as one; it is the shape of the rule and it is the same for everybody, and it means there is a piece of paper she is carrying that is worth nothing to nine hundred years of a man’s rule, and that she said out loud, once, on a bench, to his face, on the seventh of a month at the third hour.

And the fourth thing.

He was never going to write her down while she was off the page. That was nine days of a lane and a shut door and two hours of a man sitting against four inches of oak. She knows that now and she did not know it on the third, and she said a sentence about the shed at the end of the lane on a bench at a crossing in daylight, and the sentence was about a piece of paper.

The paper got him nine days and it got him two hours against a door.

The door gets the name.
