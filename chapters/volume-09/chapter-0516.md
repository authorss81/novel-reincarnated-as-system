# One, And Not A Word

She got to the crossing at the ninth hour of the first of a month, and he was already on the bench, and the book was shut on his knee, and he did not say one word to her for as long as she stood there.

That is the whole of how the price was given to her and she wants it in the plainest words available.

## The Crossing

There is a painted board on a frame at the crossing and a chain and a bench and a boy of seventeen who puts the chain up and takes it down and does not want to know anything.

He was sitting on the bench with his weight on his good leg and the cloth bag on the ground at his feet and the book shut flat on his knee, and he had been there long enough that the dew had got into the cloth.

She came up the road from the trough road at the ninth hour after walking fourteen miles in two days and a night, and she came up the made road from the west and she did not hurry and she did not slow down when she saw him, and the boy was on the chain.

## The Count

She did the count while she was walking in and it took nine seconds and she has not been able to think of anything else properly since.

Day one thousand two hundred and fifty-six is the day she made him put a thing down and she has never once asked him to pick it up.

Day one thousand five hundred and fourteen is this one.

Five hundred and fourteen less two hundred and fifty-six is two hundred and fifty-eight.

That is what she got. Two hundred and fifty-eight days of a man who has had nothing to spend since before she was told what he was, and she has said his name into four rooms and a shed and a field in that time and got nothing back from any of them.

She had wanted to publish that figure at him on a bench at a crossing. She wanted to hand it to him the way she handed him forty words on the third of a month, and she has wanted to do that for a hundred days.

And she got nine feet off him and looked at the book on his knee and did not say one word of it, because a woman who has been given a number for the first time in eight years does not walk up and give one back.

## What She Said

She said it out loud once, standing in daylight in front of a bench, in the ordinary voice, with a boy of seventeen forty feet off working a chain.

“You have a thing to tell me concerning a door,” she said. “You have had it since the thirtieth of a month that has gone and you have not said it and you are not going to, and you have brought the book.”

He did not answer.

“You could have written it on the bench and gone,” she said. “You have not done that for nine hundred years of it and you are not going to start with me.”

He did not answer that either.

She stood there for the length of a count of a hundred and forty, which is a little under two minutes, and she did not ask him anything else, and he did not say one word.

## Forty Seconds

He put his hand on the book and did not open it, and then he opened it, and he went to the back of it and took out a page that was clean, and he put the brush in his right hand.

She has watched a hand write three times in eight years.

A clerk at a counter, nine seconds, a good fast hand that did not care.

A man of sixty-one in a long low room with the shutters up, nine seconds, and it was the same hand in the same world nine days later in a different room.

And this one.

He wrote for forty seconds and she heard every stroke of it, and the sound of a brush on paper in the open air at forty yards is not a sound anybody forgets, and the boy of seventeen stopped working the chain while it was going on and started again afterwards without a word about it.

## What He Put Down

Then he shut the book.

He turned it round on his knee so that the cover faced the bench and not the road, and he took the page off the top of it and put it down on the bench, face up, in the middle, a foot and a half from her boot.

And he took his hand off it and put both hands in his lap and waited.

She did not pick it up.

Her hand will not take a gift. That is the eighth cost in eight years and it is the one that has kept her off every floor that could have used her, and it is not fastidiousness and it has never once been a choice.

So she stood over a page on a bench at nine feet from a man of nine hundred years and she leaned down and she read it without touching it, the way she reads four feet of writing off a board, and it took her four seconds.

There was one figure on it.

## One

It was a single figure. One stroke standing on its own foot with a small flag on the top of it, and it was in the hand she has watched write forty seconds twice, and it was not a date and it was not a place and it was not a name.

It was the figure for one.

That is all there is on the page. One figure, in the middle of a clean sheet, and nothing else on it, and the sheet had no second line on it and no heading and no date and nothing at the bottom, and she stood there and looked at it for the length of a count of two hundred.

She did not touch it. She did not ask a second time. She did not say the word out loud on a made road in daylight because she says everything out loud exactly once and she had not used that one yet and was not going to waste it on a figure on a bench.

## A Price Instead Of A Rule

She stood up out of the lean and worked out what had just happened, and it took eleven seconds, and it is the largest thing that has been done to her in eight years.

For eight years every person who has ever come at her with a piece of paper has come with a rule.

The terms have been read to her at the fourth hour in the room. A paper has to be opened by the person it concerns. Nothing has been asked of her twice. Forty-one lines a month, read aloud, and take none. A place to stand that somebody else keeps open.

Eight years of rules, and every one of them is true, and every single one of them has been a thing she must do before anybody will give her anything.

And a man of nine hundred years has put a figure on a page and slid it across a bench and shut a book and waited.

That is not a rule. That is a price, and he has told it to her before he has spent it, which is the one thing no office on nine days of road has ever done.

## What It Is Not

And it is one, and she does not know whose.

She knows what one is. She has had it worked out since the fourth hour of a month in a year she could name: a wall opened from the inside is a document read from the inside by the person it concerns, with nobody qualified in the room, and a person cannot be in two places.

She knows it is not her. He does not prefer kept to lost and he has never wanted to put her anywhere, and she said the words on a bench on the third of a month to make sure of it.

And the page has no second line on it, so there is no name on it, and he is not going to say a name out loud on a bench at a crossing in daylight, and he is not going to write one.

So it is one, and it is not her, and it is one person, and she is going to have to decide to say yes to a number without knowing what she has said yes to.

## She Left It There

She left the page on the bench.

She did not fold it and she did not put it in the bag and she did not tear it, and she stood there for another count of a hundred with her hands at her sides while the dew got into it, and then she turned round and walked east.

Behind her a boy of seventeen put a chain up and took it down, and a man of nine hundred years sat on a bench with his hands in his lap and did not get up and did not look after her.

She did not say thank you. She did not say anything. She has not said thank you to one living person in eight years except once, to a man nine feet off a cart track, for four coppers of bread, and she is not going to start at a man who has told her the price of a thing and will not say whose it is.

She got four hundred yards down the made road before she had to stop, and it was not the road.

She is eighteen years old and she has eaten four times in a month and she has walked fourteen miles in a day and a night on four fingers and a thumb with her right arm open, and she stood still in the middle of eleven miles of nothing and waited for her fourth count to come back.

It came back. It takes four seconds on flat ground now and it took six.

Then she walked the rest of it.

## East

She walked east.

There are eleven miles of made road between that bench and the end of it and she has been over it once in her life and turned round in the middle.

At the end of the long road there is a chain on a gate and a cup hanging off the chain and a boy of fifteen who has never once asked her a question.

Past the gate, four hundred yards, there is a bar on two posts with nothing on it at all, no chain and no cup and no boy.

And past the bar, three hundred yards east of that, in the flat middle of the last of the eleven miles, she has never once been told and has never once looked: a wall set in the ground, running north and south as far as the flat goes, with a hut behind it.
