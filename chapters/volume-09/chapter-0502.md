# Copied By Another Hand

She did it on the third of a month at the seventh hour at a crossing, in daylight, on a bench, with a boy of seventeen forty feet off putting a chain up and down.

That is the shape of it and she has not been able to make it into anything else in the three days since. The whole of the most important sentence of her life so far was said out loud, in the open, sitting down, to a man standing nine feet off a lane.

He did not ask her to sit down and he did not ask her to stand up. He asked her nothing. That was the arrangement and she made all of it herself.

“You said you would write down what I said and I would hear it,” she said. “Say that back.”

“You will say it and I will write what you say and you will hear me write it,” he said. “That is the arrangement. I will not stop you in the middle and I will not ask you a question while you are doing it, and if you stop I will put the brush down.”

“And if it is not what is in the book.”

“Then I will write what you said,” he said, “because it is your account and not mine.”

## The Reason She Gave Him

She gave him the reason first, out loud, once, because she says everything out loud exactly once, and she gave it sitting on a bench at a crossing in the ordinary voice.

“I am not doing it because I want to be found,” she said. “I have been unfindable for four months and it is the best thing that has ever happened to me and I am giving it up on purpose and I want that said out loud before I say the rest of it.”

He did not say anything.

“I am doing it because it is the only thing I have ever said in a room that was mine,” she said. “And because there is one true sentence about me in the world and it is nine days of road west in a book I cannot reach, and I have wanted it in front of me in some form since I was seventeen.”

She had her hands on her knees.

“And I am doing it because I have never once in eight years been able to find out what it is to be findable. I have been kept. I have been wanted. I have been read to. I have never once been able to stand at a thing and watch it come to me.”

## The Sentence

Then she said the sentence, and she said it in the same words and the same order and at the same speed, because that is what it was and she did not improve it.

“Ning Yan. Present, at the fourth hour, in the room, on the third of this month, brought in by the terms of her own asking. The terms have been read to her at the fourth hour in the room with the man who keeps the book at the end of the table. She has read the paper aloud to the room. She has declined it. Nothing has been asked of her twice.”

It took nineteen seconds and she has counted them and she counted them because she counts everything and because she wanted the whole of it over with.

And then she stopped, because that is where it stops. It stops at asked of her twice and it stopped there two months ago in a long low room with the shutters up and it stopped there because she stopped it.

He had the book open on his knee and the brush in his right hand and he wrote for forty seconds and she heard every stroke of it, which is what she asked for and what she had not known she was asking for until he was doing it.

## What He Wrote

He read it back without being asked, and it came out word for word, and then he stopped and waited, because there were three things underneath it and he was not going to put any of them in without asking.

“A date,” he said.

“The third of this month.”

“I have that.”

“And a place.”

She said, “The shed at the end of this lane,” and she heard herself say it, and that was the moment. She wants it written down that the moment was that sentence and not the forty seconds of brush, and she has been carrying both of them for three days and she is going to be carrying them for the rest of this.

“The shed at the end of the lane,” he said.

“That is where I am. That is the place you will have to come to.”

“I see,” he said.

And that was all. He shut the book and put the brush in the cloth and rolled the cloth and took the strap and did all of it in nine seconds, and he did not ask her one question on it, and he did not say that he was obliged to, and she has thought about that more than she has thought about anything else in eight years.

## What It Cost

She walked back up the lane to the shed at the end of it at the eighth hour with the bar on her shoulder and she did not sleep that night, and here is what she worked out, in order, on the bench, in the dark, one at a time, the way she does arithmetic.

A woman at a store five miles west of a mill has been good to her for two years and has never once asked her name, and she went five miles and spent her last coin on bread on the twenty-third of a month two months back rather than ask one question in a room.

She has never told anybody where she sleeps. Not in two years. Not to a shopkeeper, not to a floor, not to a man of thirty-four on a wall at a cut, not to a man with a brush who asked to see a slip three times.

And she said it out loud, on a bench, in daylight, to a man with a brush, for a sentence.

That is the price and she named it out loud once on the bench at the second hour of the fourth of a month, in the ordinary voice, and it was four sentences long.

“I have given a man where I sleep in exchange for a thing I said in a room two months ago,” she said. “It is not a trade and there is no other side of it. I did it and I would do it again and I am not going to be able to think about it for a while.”

## Why She Could Not Un-Let Him

And that is the part that cannot be walked back, and she did not know it was going to be that on the bench at the crossing, and she found it out at the second hour.

He wrote it in his hand. Nine hundred years of hands and one of them put forty seconds of brush on a page, and the brush was his, and the book was his, and there is no hand in the world that can take a line out of that book and keep it out.

She has spent eight years being entered by other people. Four times, in books, by offices, by men with brushes, and she has never once been able to take one out, and she has said out loud on a made road that she does not forgive and does not ask to be forgiven for any of it.

And on the third of a month she did the whole of it herself, with her own two hands, sitting in daylight, and gave it away.

That is the finding and she has it exactly and she said it out loud once, on the bench, at the third hour, and it took eleven words:

“Four times somebody else wrote me down. The fifth time I said it to him.”

## The Boy At The Crossing

In the morning the boy of about seventeen was on the chain and he had been on the chain the whole time and he had not come within forty feet of the bench and he had not asked her a question on it, which she noticed and which she has thought about since.

“What did the old man write down,” he said.

“A sentence,” Ning Yan said.

“Was it about you.”

“Yes.”

He put the chain up and took it down.

“He does not write people down here,” the boy said. “He has never once, in three years, and there is one board on this crossing and it is not for him.”

“That is all right,” Ning Yan said. “He is not writing people down here. He wrote a sentence.”

And the boy said, “Then that is different,” and went back to his chain, and she went up the lane with a bar on her shoulder, and she did not look back, and there was nothing behind her to look at except a boy and a chain and flat ground.

## Forty Seconds Of Brush

She heard all of it and she has been going back over it for four days.

It is not a fast hand. She reads a hand that four people cannot take and she can tell you the speed of a man writing from four inches of it, and this one was slow in the way a hand is slow when the person writing it has had nine hundred years of practice and no hurry at all.

Forty seconds is a long time for a sentence of forty words. A clerk at a counter does that in nine. A man of sixty-one with a brush at the end of a table did the same entry in nine seconds in a long low room with the shutters up and she watched him do it and timed it against her own breathing.

This one took forty, and he was not being careful over it, and she has been able to tell the difference since she was seventeen.

Careful is even. This was not even. It went uneven twice, at the eleventh second and at the twenty-ninth, and both times it was at the word asked.

He has written that word a great many times.

She does not know that and she is not going to be able to, and she has thought about it for four days, and it is the only thing about a man of nine hundred years that has ever made her want to ask him something she has no right to ask.
