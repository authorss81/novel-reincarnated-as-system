# She Reads It Back To Him

She read four sentences off a panel of a book she made out of a blanket, out loud, in the ordinary voice, at an opening in a wall on the twentieth of a month at the ninth hour, and then she said one word, and the word was the whole of what the last eight months had been for.

## She Put It Down

She went out at the ninth hour with the book inside her coat and she took it out and put it on the table.

Two feet of table stood on her side of the jamb. She squared the book with the edges of it, the way you square a thing on a table, and she pushed it an inch and a half from the middle and stopped.

His book was open at the far end of the same four feet of plank. The brush was on the table between the two of them and it was lying along the same line it had been lying along for three days.

She did not go through the opening.

## The Four Sentences

She read them out loud in the ordinary voice, at a table, in daylight, to a man nine feet off on the other side of a jamb, and she read them the way she read a page of her own back to four people in a room with the shutters up in the fifth month of a year she could name.

Not loud. Not for effect. At the speed a person reads.

People go past the end of what a county can carry, and something takes them there, and it does not tell anybody that it has.

There is a man who writes down every one of them, and he has been writing for nine hundred years and he has never once stopped.

He writes each name once, and he writes which of the two things happened to the person whose name it is.

He did not ask me for this and he cannot have it, because it is my book.

She got to the end of the fourth one and she did not add anything to it.

## What He Did While She Read It

He did not move.

That is everything she has on that and it is worth putting down exactly, because she was watching him out of the corner of her eye the whole way through and she was looking for one thing.

His hands were on the edge of the table and his right hand was four inches from the brush and it did not go to it. His face did not change at any of the four sentences. He did not interrupt her and he did not look away and he did not put his weight on his other leg.

He has read nine hundred years of records and a woman of nineteen read him one, and he listened to it the way a person listens to a thing that is about himself.

## What He Said About It

That is all he said about the four sentences and he said it after a minute and a half.

“You have not put anything in it that is not there,” he said.

“No,” Ning Yan said.

“All right,” he said.

That is nine words and one of them is the same word he has said to her on a lane and at a gate and in the opening, and it is the first time in nine days she has heard him use it and she is not going to think of that.

## The Word

She had it ready. She had it ready since the eleventh of a month at the seventh hour, sitting on the boards of a nine-foot room, and she had not said it out loud to anybody and she was not going to say it twice.

She said it in the ordinary voice, standing on her own side of a jamb, at the ninth hour, on the twentieth of a month, in daylight, with a book on a table and the light coming off the flat behind her.

“I am not going to be kept,” Ning Yan said.

That is all of it and it is seven words and she said it once.

## The Bill In The Same Breath

Here is the bill and it arrived at the same second as the win, in the same breath, in the ordinary voice, at an opening in a wall.

She has said no to kept. The rule has two words in it. She has been told in writing, on a page at a bench at a crossing, in a hand nine hundred years old, that a wall opened from the inside leaves one person on the wrong side of it, and she knows it is him, and she said it out loud at a gate in front of three people, and it saved nobody.

So the word is lost, and she does not know what lost does.

Not one word of what she has just given up was known to her. She refused the word a man of nine hundred years has told her is the better one. She has no evidence at all on the other one; she has one page and a figure of one on it and a man who said four words out of the dark, and none of those says what either of the two does to a person.

She has taken the one she knows nothing of, in exchange for refusing the one he told her on the tenth that she is better off with.

That is everything she got and she is not going to pretend on the page that it was a good exchange, and she is not going to pretend either that she would have taken the other one if she had known what it was.

## What He Said To That

This is the part she was not ready for and it is the reason the chapter is what it is.

“I will take either one,” he said. “I told you that on the fifteenth and I have not changed my mind about it.”

He did not say kept was better.

He did not say it once. He said it to her exactly one time, on the tenth of a month, in five sentences and a sixth, and he said it and went back through the opening, and he has not said it since, and he was not going to argue it now on the twentieth with a brush on the table.

“All right,” Ning Yan said.

He looked at the book of hers for four seconds.

“Then say the other one,” he said.

And that is all. He put his hand out and picked the brush up off the table and held it in his right hand and did not write anything with it, and he waited for her.

## Nine Hundred Years Of Evidence

He had nine hundred years of evidence and he did not use one day of it.

She stood there and understood exactly what that was, and it was the worst thing that has happened to her in four months, and she is going to write it the way it is.

If he had argued, she could have gone at it. She could have had it out with a man over the meaning of a word for nine hours and she would have won some of it, because she has three stages past the end of a realm and a hand that cannot write a false line and a mouth that has been reading documents since she was six.

He would have let her. He would have stood there and been argued with, because he does not prefer, and a man who does not prefer has all the time in the world.

He did not argue, so there was nothing on the table to fight, and the entire weight of nine hundred years of his belief came down onto one side and it was hers.

She is nineteen. She has been entered four times by other people’s hands and refused five arrangements and walked out of a shop with a loaf on a counter. She has never before had to hold up nine hundred years of somebody else’s certainty with nothing but her own word in a room where she has four fingers and a thumb.

And she did it, and it took eleven seconds, and the whole of it was one word: *lost*.

## The Reason For The Wall

She said one more thing and she said it in the ordinary voice and it is the last sentence she says at that opening before the twenty-second.

“What I read to you is in my book and not in yours,” she said, “and it is the only part of this that is mine, and I am the one who is carrying it.”

He did not answer it.

That is correct and she expected it and she is not going to be hurt by it: it is not a fact in his book, and a man who keeps a book for nine hundred years does not answer things that are not in it.

## What Stands

It cannot be un-written. It is not in his book and it is not on any board on nine days of road and no office has a copy and no reader exists who could be sent for.

It is in a book of eight leaves the size of a closed hand, on paper made out of a blanket, in her own hand, in her own ink, and it says what the bound takes and what a man does about it, and it is true, and it is somewhere she has not yet taken it to.

And she is entered, and she is not kept, and the distance between those two things is four sentences long and she wrote them herself on the eighteenth of a month at the ninth hour.

It is the first refusal in eight years that is not a refusal of being written down. She has said no four times to a document and once to a man with a brush and she has never once said no to the whole of a thing while agreeing to the part of it she could live with.

She has just done that, and it is not going to cost her the record, and it is going to cost her the road, and she does not yet know that.

## One Word Left

At the ninth hour on the twentieth of a month, at an opening in a wall, in daylight, there was a man of nine hundred years on the far side of a jamb with a brush in his right hand and his hand was not moving, and there was a book of hers squared up on two feet of plank table with four sentences on the second leaf.

There was one word left on the table and it was four letters long and she had said the other one out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, and it could not be said again.
