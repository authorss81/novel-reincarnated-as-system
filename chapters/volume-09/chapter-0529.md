# A Book Of Her Own

She came through the opening at the fourth hour of the fourth of a month and shut the door and waited for the latch, and the bar was on her shoulder and the bag was against her ribs and she had been out for four days and she had nothing to show for it.

## The Nine

She counted them at the fourth hour on the fifth, on the sill, in nine passes, one at a time, with her left hand, the same way she counted the three hundred and one.

Nine square sheets out of the whole of it.

She had gone on at the rate of a hundred a day on the days she was in the room and she had done eleven days of it between the twenty-fifth of the month that had gone and the sixth of this one, and out of nine hundred and six sheets, nine came out square by the standard she set on the second day and did not move.

She laid the nine out on the table with their edges squared to each other and looked at them for a quarter of an hour.

Then she took the bar off her shoulder and put it against the wall where it lives and stood in front of nine sheets of paper and started on the first of them.

## What A Sheet Of It Becomes

One sheet folded in half three times is eight leaves.

She folded it on the table with the flat of her left hand and the heel of her right, and it is a thing you can do with a splinter, and there was nothing hard in it at all.

It opens out into a strip of eight panels four inches by two, and you write on them in order along the strip, and then you fold it up and it is the size of a closed hand and the thickness of two of your fingers.

She made one on the fifth and she made a second on the sixth and she put both of them on the shelf beside the seven flat ones.

Then she took a fifth splinter out of the bed frame with the edge of the dish, and there are five holes in a bed frame with nothing on it now, and she cannot put them back.

## The Ink

Soot off the chimney breast, because the chimney draws and has drawn for as long as that wall has stood.

She wetted a piece of board and rubbed the soot into it on the wet side and left it to dry, and a dried cake of soot on a board is not anything you can buy and it is exactly enough. She wetted the tip of the splinter against it and it came off on the wood in a black smear the size of a grain of wheat.

That is the entire making of it. Four minutes for the cake and a splinter out of a bed frame and nine hundred sheets of blanket to get to.

## Where His Hand Cannot Reach It

That is the point of it and she worked it out on the fourth, before she folded anything, standing at the table in the dark with the bar against the wall.

There is a book on nine days of road with her name in it, in a hand nine hundred years old, in a room a hundred yards behind a wall he opened. Every word in it was written by him.

Every copy of it in the world was made by him or by somebody he sent for, and he does not send for anybody. It is the only account of her that exists in nine hundred years and there is not one line of it she is able to take out.

She cannot un-copy the page she handed over at a crossing in the fifth month. She cannot get her refusal out of his book and she has stopped trying to think about it.

This is the first writing about her life that is not in his book.

And when it is not in his book, it does not go where his book goes. She does not know where his book goes and she has decided she is not going to spend a day finding out.

She took it off the table and put it inside her coat, flat, against the open place low behind her fourth rib, on the other side of the sheet of paper that has been there for four years, and it is nine feet of nothing between her and the ground.

## Eight Years Of Things That Were True

She has been written eight times in eight years and not one of the eight said what happened.

That is not new and she is not going to make a thing of it here. It is a fact about the last eight years, carried the way the four fingers and a thumb and the road she cannot go back down are carried.

One of the eight is hers. Her name and the date on it and the place named in her own words, written by her, in a book on nine days of road, in the fifth month of a year she could name, and read back to her before it went in, which is the only time in her life a thing she said has been got right.

And one of the eight is on a bench at a crossing.

That page has a figure on it and a date and a place, and it is in a hand nine hundred years old, and it said one, and it did not say whose, and it was true when it was written and it was the truest thing anybody has ever said about her life.

It is also the only one of the eight that is not complete, and she knows now exactly what the missing part of it was, because a man said four words out of the dark on the sixteenth and it was him.

## The First Line

She wrote it at the ninth hour of the sixth, standing up, at the table, with her right arm open for the fourth day in five and her left hand holding the book flat.

A date. And under the date a place.

That is all that is on the first leaf and it took her eleven minutes because she got the shape of the figures wrong twice, and she has written in four hands in eight years and this is the first one that is hers and is not a hand somebody taught her.

She did not write anything else under it.

Not because she had nothing to say. Because she had four sentences and she had not yet worked out whether they were four sentences about him or about her, and she was not going to put that in a book of her own on the sixth of a month at the ninth hour.

## The Book On The Table

The blank book is still on the table across the room with sixteen clean leaves in it, square to the edge of the wood, where it was on the twenty-first of the month before.

She has not touched it in twenty-six days. She opened it once and shut it and put it down square and she has walked past it four times a day since.

It is right there. It is eleven feet from her and it is the exact size of the thing she needed and it would have had everything in it that her own has and four hundred times the making.

The difference between the two books is nine hundred sheets of blanket and three days with the flat end of a bar, and that is the whole of the difference and it has taken her eleven days and twenty-two miles and a refusal at a post to arrive at it.

She does not know who left the other one there. It has been on that table in the middle of the dust for longer than she has been in the room and she is not going to ask, because he has not asked her anything about himself in eight years and she is not going to start at twenty-seven.

## For The Record And Not For A Person

Here is the part she did not expect, and it is the only part of the eleventh day that cost her anything at all.

In four months she has not done one thing for a person.

She has refused a man behind a counter. She has refused a room with a bolt on a west door three times. She has let a woman put a loaf on a counter and walked out of the shop. She has said no to a leg man and a carrier and a carter and a bar on two posts and a line on a board and a chain at a gate.

Every single one of those was a person. Every single one of them had to be answered, and she answered, and the answer was a sentence with her in it and it cost something.

She folded a sheet of paper three times on a table at the fifth hour on the sixth of a month and made the first line of a record that is not about her, and there was nobody in the room.

She said it out loud once, to the boards, and it took her four sentences and she is going to leave it there.

A record is for whoever comes after you and not for you, and a book that is made for a person is not a record, it is a letter, and I have got eight of them and not one of them is worth the paper.

That is the first thing she has made in four months that is not a thing somebody else made first, and it is not a gift and she cannot give it to anybody and she has not got anybody to give it to.

## What The Book Is For

On the sixth at the eleventh hour she came to the end of it and she said it out loud once, standing in the middle of the floor of a nine-foot room with a bar of a wall across the back of it.

This is not about me.

It is four sentences about a thing that has been going on for nine hundred years, and one of the people in the middle of it is me, and I am in it and I am not the reason it is being written.

That is the decision and she made it before she knew what anybody was going to ask her for, and she wants it written down in that order, because on the seventh of this month at the fourth hour something is going to come to that opening and she has not the least idea what it is and she is not going to write a book around whatever it turns out to be.

## The Seventh

At the fourth hour on the seventh she was standing in the opening with her left hand on the jamb, which is cool at that hour, and she heard something come across the flat that was not a footstep.

It was the four legs of a table coming out through an opening in a wall, one at a time, set down on stone, and then a book being put on it.

A plank table. Four feet long, on four legs, carried out through the opening and set down in it so that two feet of it stood on her side and two feet stood on his.

And a man of nine hundred years put a book on it and opened the book.

She did not go back in.

She had a book of her own inside her coat with a date and a place on the first leaf and nothing under it, and she had four sentences that were not written yet, and there was a man on the other side of a wall who had come to ask her for something.
