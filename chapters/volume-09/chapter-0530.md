# He Does Not Ask Her

He came out of the far ground at the fourth hour of the seventh of a month with a plank table on his shoulder, and he set it down in the opening, and he put a book on it and opened the book, and she did not go through the opening and she did not speak for two days.

## The Table

It is four feet long on four legs and made of the same wood as the table in the room and it is old enough that the top of it has gone the colour of a piece of bark.

He set it into the opening so that two feet of the top stood on her side of the jamb and two feet stood on his, and the four legs stood in the dirt on both sides, and after that it did not move again for the whole of the rest of the month.

A thing goes through that opening in both directions. A person goes through it once.

She worked that out on the seventh morning in four seconds and she has not gone over it since, because it is a fact about an opening and not a fact about him.

## What Came Out Of The Hut

He came out with the book under his arm at the ninth hour on the seventh and put it on the table and opened it and did not say anything for a minute.

Then he said the whole of it in the ordinary voice, standing on his own side of the jamb with his weight on one leg, and it took him under two minutes, and she counted it.

A name goes in when a person gets to the top of a realm and has nothing to take them past it. It goes in once. It goes in as kept or as lost. It is not a thing I decided and it is older than you are.

That is all he said.

Then he stood there with the book open on a table in a hole in a wall and waited, and she did not go in, and he did not ask her why she had not.

## Two Days Of It

He came back at the ninth hour on the eighth and stood on his side and did not repeat it.

That is the correct thing to do with a thing you have already said once to a person who has heard it. She worked that out on the seventh afternoon and it is the first thing she has ever agreed with him about that she did not have to agree with him about.

On the eighth at the ninth hour he said one more sentence.

“I am not going to tell you again,” he said, “and I am not going to explain it.”

“All right,” Ning Yan said.

He looked up. That is the entire reaction and it took a second.

That is the first time she has got a word out of herself at that opening in two days and he has not asked her anything to get it.

## The Thing He Did Not Ask Her

He has not asked her a question about himself in eight years. Not one, in eight years, on nine days of road, in four rooms with shutters up, at a rail, on a lane, in a shed at the end of a lane, at a crossing, at a gate.

She got to the counting of that on the eighth of a month at the fourth hour and she got it exactly, in her head, one item at a time, the way she counts everything.

Four hundred and some answers in eight years and not one question about himself. Not what he is for. Not why the book. Not how he came to keep it. Not whether it is hard.

And on the seventh he put the price of a door in front of her in a figure and in writing and said nothing at all about it, which is the same behaviour from the other side, and she noticed that at the time and has not gone over it since.

He is not going to start today. She has decided that he is not going to start today the way you decide a thing about the weather.

## Being Told What She Is

Here is what the two hours on the seventh and the eighth actually were, and she put it out loud once at the ninth hour of the ninth, standing in the opening, in the ordinary voice, to a man nine feet off on the other side of a jamb.

Nobody in nine days of road has told her what she is.

Not in eight years. A man behind a counter told her what her labour was worth. A man with a brush read out a document. An office entered her four times. A woman told her she was thinner. A boy of fifteen asks her whether she wants the chain up or down and means the chain.

And a man of nine hundred years, who is the only person alive qualified to say it, stood on the other side of a table in a hole in a wall and told her in nine words that she is at the top of a thing and that there is no document in her possession that takes her past it.

Both of those are true and one of them is what she has been working towards for four months and the other is the bill.

She said the nine words out loud to him and he said nothing back, and the reason he said nothing back is that she was talking to herself and he knew it.

## The Book On Two Feet Of Table

It is larger than a house book. It is bound in something dark and soft that has gone shiny at the corners, and it is nine inches thick, and there is a strap and a flap on the outside of it and the strap has been done up and undone so many thousands of times that it has the shape of a thing that is always being undone.

She has seen it four times in eight months. On a bench at a crossing in the fifth month, upside down, with a figure on a page of it. In the room in the third month, shut, with a hand flat on it. In this opening, shut, when he came through the wall in the eighth of a month. And here.

When it is open there is one date at the top of a page in a hand nine hundred years old, and a line under it, and a great many lines under that.

She can read it.

There is not one person within nine hundred yards of this wall who can read a page of that book, and she can read every line of it, and she stood on her own side of a jamb at the ninth hour of the seventh and the eighth and the ninth and did not read one line of it.

She has not told him that either. She has told one living person in eight months that she can read it, and she is not going to tell him at an opening in a wall with his own book open on a table.

## The Thing She Asked For

Here is the finding, and she got it at the fourth hour of the ninth on flat ground outside with the bar against her shoulder, and she wants it said in her own words and not as a complaint.

On the twenty-second of a month she started eleven days of work in a nine-foot room on finding out how to be worth the price of one, and what came out of it was that she wanted one thing in the world and she had never had it.

Somebody who needs her to do something.

He came out of that hut at the ninth hour of the seventh of a month and asked her for a thing, and it is the only request anybody has ever made of her in eight years, and she stood on her own side of a jamb and could not do it.

It is not the thing.

That is the whole of it and it is not fair and she is not going to stand in a room and pretend to anybody that it is fair.

She wanted to be wanted for what she can do with two hands. She has carried a bar of nine feet nine up eleven steps of a stair past a painted mark. She has carried a stone that four men could not get over a mark. She is past the end of a realm and the end of a realm is not a place anybody hires for.

What came to an opening in a wall is a man who wants a line in a book.

## Two Hours A Day

The table is in the opening from the fourth hour to the eleventh.

He came at the ninth and he went at the eleventh with the book shut under his arm, and on the seventh and the eighth and the ninth he came out through the opening with it and put it on two feet of table and opened it and stood there.

Seven hours. Two of them with the book open.

That is everything available and she has not worked out yet what it is available for, and she has fifteen days of it.

## What She Is Not Going To Do

She has one question and it is not about herself.

She worked it out at the fourth hour of the ninth, in the opening, with her left hand on the cool jamb, and it took her nine minutes and she did not say it out loud because she wanted to have it in a hand before she put it in the air.

The question is what the book is for.

Not what the rule is. She has had the rule since the seventh and she can say all of it back to him at any hour of the day and has done it in her head forty times in two days.

What the book is for. Where the names go. What a person on it is. Nine hundred years of writing down every person the bound has swallowed, and he writes it, and she has never once been told what anybody is supposed to do with nine hundred years of it.

He will not tell her why. She has known that since the second month of a year she could name and she was not going to be told why by anybody this month.

He might tell her what it is for.

He is the only mouth in nine hundred years and he has said one thing in two days and she has got one question and she is going to ask it on the tenth, in daylight, out loud, in the ordinary voice, standing on her own side of the opening.

She is not going to go through the opening. She has gone through it eleven times in three days and she is not going to go through it with a table and an open book on it, because a person who sits down at that table has gone into the room, and she has not gone into that room and she is not going to.
