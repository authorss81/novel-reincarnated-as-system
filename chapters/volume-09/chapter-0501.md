# Without Being Read To

She said no to him on the twenty-first of a month at the ninth hour at the end of the lane, and she said it in the ordinary voice with the door open behind her, and then she said it again four days later, and he did not appear to mind either of them.

“You have not heard what I have,” she said. “You have heard what a book needs. Those are different.”

“They are different,” he said. “I am still asking for the first one.”

“Then I have nothing to give you and you have walked nine miles for it.”

“I have walked a good deal more than nine miles for it,” he said, “and there is no hurry, and I have told you that twice.”

## What Kept Means

She asked him what kept meant and she asked it the way she asks a floor about a rate, which is that she wanted the figure and not the feeling, and he gave her the figure in about six sentences on flat ground.

Kept means the person is written down and the bound goes around them.

Not loses them. Goes around. A list is a thing the bound goes around rather than through, and that has been true for as long as he has kept one, and it is not his arrangement and it is not his rule and he did not put it there.

“So the list is in the way of something,” she said.

“The list is in the way of the bound,” he said. “Yes.”

“Then the bound knows the list is there.”

“No,” he said. “The bound does not know anything. That is the difficulty and it has always been the difficulty. The bound goes around a list the way water goes around a stone, and it has never once stopped to look at the stone, and that is not because it is kind. It is because a stone is not food and it is not a door and it is not a person, and everything the bound has ever wanted is one of those three.”

She stood there and worked out what she had just been handed, because he had handed it to her without knowing he was handing it to her, which is the ordinary way everything worth having has ever come to her.

A list is a thing that is visible.

You do not go around a stone by accident. Water goes around a stone because the stone is there and the water knows where the stone is, and every single thing that has ever gone around his book in nine hundred years has gone around it because they could see it.

She did not say that out loud. She has had the sentence ready since the twentieth of a month at the ninth hour and she was not going to hand it to a man on a lane at the ninth hour of the twenty-second and she has not handed it to anybody in three days.

## The Sentence She Said Instead

What she said instead was this, and she said it out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, and it was the sharpest thing she has said to anybody in two years.

“You are not keeping me,” she said. “You are putting me behind a piece of paper. You have been putting people behind that paper for nine hundred years and not one of them was asked, and you have got so good at it that you have started calling it a record.”

He did not answer for nine seconds.

“No,” he said.

“No what.”

“No, I am not putting you behind a piece of paper,” he said. “I am going to write your name on a page and I have not got a page to put you behind, and if I stop writing for one day then in one day’s time the stone is not there and the water goes where it was going. That is what a record is. It is not a wall. It is four days, at most, of a wall.”

“Then it is four days.”

“Yes,” he said. “It has been four days for nine hundred years and I have written it every single day, and there is not one of those days in nine hundred years when I did not do it.”

That is what she got for the sharpest sentence she has had in two years, and it was an answer and not a defence, and he gave it to her standing up in the open with his weight on one leg.

## The Part She Had Not Planned

She did not leave because she had a plan. She left because her right arm went open in the middle of the ninth hour and would not close, and she was standing nine feet off a man of nine hundred years with her arm open at the side in front of him.

It took forty seconds and it was the fourth time in eleven days and it happens roughly every fourth day now, which is a rate she has been keeping since the twentieth of a month, and it is the whole of what the ceiling has done to a body in a month.

She stood there and waited for it with her left hand on the bar and the right one hanging, and he did not say anything and he did not look away and he did not come nearer, and after forty seconds she got it and put it in the strap of the bar.

She said, “That happens.”

“I know,” he said. “It has happened to me for about six hundred years and it stopped being interesting at about two hundred.”

She looked at him.

“I am nine hundred years old,” he said. “There is not a great deal that happens to me now that I have not seen before, and I am not going to make a ceremony of your arm.”

And then he said the thing that she was still carrying on the twenty-fourth of a month.

“I am going to ask you again on the twenty-fourth,” he said, “and again after that, and I am going to ask you the same way every time, and there is no version of this in which I come and do something to you, because I have no staff and there is nobody on this road who takes an instruction from me, and I want you to have that in front of you while you decide.”

## The Three Days

She worked out the rest of it on the twenty-second, at the shed at the end of the lane, on the boards, in the afternoon.

The rule has two sides and she has been thinking about the wrong one since the eighteenth.

Kept is not a reward. Kept is a stone in the water, and the water goes round it for four days at a time, and after the four days it does not.

Lost is not a punishment either. Lost is a page that says it happened.

So there is no good side to it and there is no side she would be able to choose if she had to choose, and she worked that out on the twenty-second and she was still not writing anything on the wall about it.

What she worked out on the twenty-second was that refusing costs her nothing.

That is the whole of the finding and it is the reason she had not said yes.

She has been unfindable for four months. There is not one line on nine days of road with her name on it that anybody can walk up and read. There is no column and there is no board and there is no rate and there is not one floor that could say where she slept on any night of it.

And the book is the only thing in this world that would put her back on a page, and refusing is the thing that keeps her off it, and she has been standing at a crossing for four days refusing in the ordinary voice and every refusal has been free.

She lay on the boards that night and worked out what it would take to write one line in a nine-hundred-year-old hand about a girl who has nothing, and it came to four seconds, and she did not do it for two more days.

## The Twenty-Fourth

He came up the lane on the twenty-fourth at the seventh hour in the morning and stopped nine feet off and did not come nearer.

“I am going to ask you again,” he said.

“I know you are.”

“No,” he said. “I am not going to ask you. I have come to say that I am not going to ask you, because you have not got a page and I am not going to put one in front of you that you have to refuse, and that is the last time you are going to be asked about it in a doorway by me.”

“That is a different thing from being asked.”

“It is a different thing from being asked,” he said, “and it is the only difference there is, and I am aware of what it looks like.”

He shut the door of the hut behind him and waited for the latch, and then he said the other thing without turning round.

“Your arm has been open four times in five days,” he said. “Go and eat something. There is a leg man at a cut four miles east of here and he has a bench and a loaf on it about this time every morning, and he is not a kind man and he is not going to ask you anything, and I have watched him not ask people for nine hundred years.”

He went down the lane at the speed of a man with his weight on one leg.

And she stood at a crossing with a bar on her shoulder and worked out, in eleven seconds, that an old man had spent his seventh hour of the morning telling her where there was bread, and that it had been the only sentence in four days that had been about anything except a book.

## The Body, Which Is Separate

She wrote that on the wall on the twenty-second because it is housekeeping and housekeeping has been the only thing on the wall since the sixth of a month.

Four fingers and a thumb. Right arm open about four days in five, for as long as it takes to come back. Eight of eleven holds, and she goes through them standing up, four times a month. Breath at four counts, and the fourth one goes and has to be fetched. A place behind the fourth rib, open, the size of a coin, since the eighteenth of a month two months back, and it is what the reach comes out of.

And one line under all of it, which is that every one of those five has stood exactly where it stood since the fifteenth of a month before last, and that she is past the ceiling by one stage, and that the stage has cost her nothing she can name, and that the cost of the last one was a shed.
