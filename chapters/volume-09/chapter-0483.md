# What The Bread Costs

The night of the twenty-seventh was the first one in four months that she was cold, and she lay on the boards and worked out why, and the answer took nine seconds.

It was not the season. It has not been the season for a long time and it is not going to be. It was that she had eaten a third of a loaf in thirty-six hours, and a body at the top of a realm is not a body that can be run on a third of a loaf, whatever else it is.

And the wind was coming in under the door at a foot a second, because the door does not shut properly, and she has not fixed it, and she is not going to.

## The Bar And The Strap

The bar goes across the boards at the head end, inside, and the strap round it is a strip cut off the lining of her own coat, four inches wide, and she bound it herself four months back because the one that came with the bar went at the buckle.

She gets the strap round the bar and the bar round the upright post and pulls it until the loop takes up, and then she puts her hand on it and pulls again, hard, in the way she does four times a month, to find out whether it will hold.

It holds. It has held every night for four months and it held tonight with more wind on it than any other night.

There is nothing on this ground to wake her. That has been true since the seventh of a month and it is true at the fourth hour of the twenty-seventh with the strap in her two hands and the bar not going anywhere.

She let go of it and lay down on her back with her hands behind her head.

## Four Counts

Lying down is the hard part and it has been the hard part for eight years and since the eighteenth of a month two months back it is a different hard part.

Breath runs four counts. It runs four counts standing, four counts walking, four counts with the bar on her shoulder, and four counts inside a loaded reach, which is the one that costs her. Lying on boards in a coat with the wind at a foot a second, it runs four counts and it will not run five, and there is a trick she has had since she was sixteen, which is to put her hand flat on the boards over the place behind her fourth rib and count against it.

The place is open. It came open on the eighteenth of a month two months back in a room with forty people in it and it did not ask her and she could not stop it, and it is a small open place about the size of a coin and it is not going to close.

What it does is take the fourth count.

She has tested it eleven times and it is always the fourth count, every night, lying down, and the fourth count is the one she needs to keep.

So she lay there and counted three, and the fourth one did not come for nine seconds, and then it came, and she took it, and she had a breath.

She did that forty times before the light came up off flat ground and found the boards.

## Not Hungry

She looked at the not-hungry in the dark on the twenty-eighth and put it down the way she puts a thing down once she has seen it.

She is not hungry. That has been true for four months and she spent them getting good at it, and she got good at it on purpose, because a body that wants things is a body that can be used, and she had been used by three separate things over eight years and had decided in a room that she would not be again.

She examined it once, on the twenty-eighth, and found what was under it.

What was under it was not a gift. It was taken out.

Somebody had put four months of not wanting into her at the fourth hour of the eighteenth of a month in a room with the shutters up, and it had not been offered, and she had not agreed to it, and it came the way a door comes open, and nothing she did that day or any day since has shut it.

And she had spent the four months being proud of it.

That was the part on the twenty-eighth, in the dark, with the boards under her and the wind coming in. She had spent four months walking a made road being proud of a thing that had been done to her. And then on the fifteenth of a month she had put two coppers down for bread because she wanted it, which was the first thing her own body had asked for in four months, and it had cost her the last money she had.

## The Thirtieth

The thirtieth came and went and she did not notice it, and she noticed on the morning after, on the board, because she keeps the day on the board and there is nothing else to keep it on.

Thirty days. The first day of that month was the eighth, and it was the day she said his name into the shed and got nothing, and she had written the eighth on the board then and had gone on counting forward from it every morning since, which is thirty days of a woman marking a wall because there is nothing else on the wall.

Six days of bread left, if she was careful with it. No money. A trough she had never once been to. And a door that does not shut.

She wrote the date and stopped, and then she sat with her knees up and looked at the board for a while.

There was one thing on that board that had ever cost anything to put there and that was not arithmetic, and it was a line about a door, and she had gone over it twice in two years and never once fixed it.

## The Door She Is Not Going To Fix

There is a piece of wood on the hinge side that has warped in nine years and a gap at the bottom a finger thick, and it is a four-inch job.

She has known that for two years. She has had a bar, four fingers, a thumb, and four hours of daylight in a day, and she could have done it in a morning when she had eleven coppers and a ring that was coming up.

She looked at it on the first of the month and decided again, and the decision was not about carpentry.

She worked it out properly on the first, sitting on the boards with the bar across her knees, and it took the better part of two hours.

A door she fixes is a door she is answerable to.

That is the whole of the finding and it is about that door and not about the world. If she fixes it then the keeping of it is hers, and the keeping of a thing is a thing she has been on the wrong end of since she was fourteen years old in a room with a man of thirty in it.

She has been kept. She has kept nothing that was hers in eight years, except eleven coppers she will not take and four coppers she was never handed and a loaf of bread she bought because she wanted it.

She was not going to stand in a cold shed and take on the keeping of a door.

And the wind went on coming in under it at a foot a second, and she slept in it, and that was what the night cost.

## The Trough Road

She got up on the first of the month at the fourth hour and walked the five miles with an empty skin and a bar, and she did not stop at the counter, and she went down the trough road at the back.

It is a cart track a mile and a half and it is in eleven inches of an old cart rut for four hundred yards and then it is not, and there are forty goats on it in two fields with a stone trough between them and a second trough at the bottom where the track comes out.

She got to the bottom of it at the seventh hour and the water there is cold and it comes off the stone at a foot a second and it is the best water she has had in four months and she drank for a quarter of an hour sitting on the stone with her boots off.

Then she filled, which takes nine minutes and which she had not done in a very long time, and she came back up the track with a full skin and no money spent, and eleven days of not having to think about water at all.

Eleven miles had been the figure on the board since the day she came to this shed. It was eleven miles round to the end of that road and back and a day of it, and she had believed the figure for two years and had never once been to the end of it.

Two years of that. Not one thing on this ground worth four miles.

She had been wrong about that since the fifteenth of a month, in a shop, over bread.

## You Are Back

She went into the store on the way back at the ninth hour with an empty basket and a full skin and nothing at all to buy, and the woman was behind the counter.

“Good,” she said, and then she looked up.

“You are back,” she said.

Ning Yan stood there with a full skin and a bar in her hand and did not say anything for four seconds, which had not happened to her before in her life.

Then the woman went behind the shelves.

She walked out into the sun having spent nothing and bought nothing and asked nothing, and she had been spoken to in a sentence by a woman who has not put one question to her in two years.

She stood in the road and worked out what she wanted, which took her the whole way home and four miles of flat ground.

She wanted to go back in and be asked one.
