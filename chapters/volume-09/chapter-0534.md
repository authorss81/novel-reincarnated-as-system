# In Her Own Hand

She wrote it on the eighteenth of a month in a nine-foot room against the west face of a wall, at the ninth hour, standing up at a table, with a splinter of a bed frame and a cake of soot off the chimney breast, and it took her two hours and eleven minutes.

## Eight Of Nine

She had nine square sheets at the sixth of a month.

Two of them were folded into books. Seven of them were flat on the shelf.

On the sixteenth, at the ninth hour, she had put one of the seven flat out on the table in the opening and stood a cup on it, because the book he opens on that table has no board under it and a page that lifts in wind cannot be written on, and she worked that out on the fourth hour of the ninth and nobody had ever told her to do it and nobody ever will.

The wind took it at the seventh hour and it went over the low side of the opening and lay on the dirt a yard from her foot.

She picked it up. It had a corner gone. She looked at it and she could not decide what to do with it and after nine minutes she put it on the floor of the room and sat on the boards with her back against the wall and did not think of it for a day.

So: eight square sheets in nine hundred and six, two of them books, one of them on the floor of a nine-foot room with a corner gone, and she is writing on the second book with two days to go.

## What She Cut Out

She went over it for two days before she wrote a line and she wrote down what she cut, because a person making a record has to know what they have left out or it is not a record, it is a case.

Out: everything about her. Her arm, her hand, the hole behind her fourth rib, the road, the four stages, the price of a wall in a figure of one on a bench at a crossing. All of it. Every one of those is true and not one of them is what she is making the record of.

Out: every complaint. She went over eight years in her head and there were four hundred of them and they are all true and she is not going to put one of them into four sentences.

Out: every question. He is not going to answer one. She asked him seven in three days and he answered none of them and she is not going to ask a ninth.

What was left was four sentences, and she had them by the evening of the sixteenth and did not write them down until the eighteenth, because she wanted to be sure that the four were the whole of it and not the four that suited her.

## What The Hand Did

It took two hours and eleven minutes and that is not the writing. That is six words.

The hand had to learn the soot. A splinter of ash out of a bed frame with a blunt point held against a wet cake of soot comes off in a blot and a blot on a sheet this size takes most of a leaf, and she made four of them on the first afternoon, and she learned to come in at an angle and to take the wood up off the cake rather than drag it.

The rest was drying the nib against the hearth between words and watching the black come off it and not stop it.

She wrote with the left hand flat on the panel to keep the paper from moving, because the right one is four fingers and a thumb and an arm that is open four days in five, and on the fourth day in five it was open, and the fourth count on a fine reach of that kind went and took nine seconds to come back, and she sat down on the boards and waited for it and started again and it did not go again inside the twenty minutes.

She says out loud, once, at the ninth hour of the eighteenth, to the boards: it is the only work I have ever done that my body was the wrong shape for.

And she wrote four sentences and the reason they are right is that the hand could not have made them otherwise, and a person with two hands and a full right arm would have been able to write four sentences that were nearly what she thinks, and hers are exactly what she thinks.

## The Four Sentences

She wrote them at the ninth hour of the eighteenth, standing up, with her right arm open for the fourth day in five and her left hand on the book, and the hand that wrote them was four fingers and a thumb and it could not write a false line.

These are the four sentences.

People go past the end of what a county can carry, and something takes them there, and it does not tell anybody that it has.

There is a man who writes down every one of them, and he has been writing for nine hundred years and he has never once stopped.

He writes each name once, and he writes which of the two things happened to the person whose name it is.

He did not ask me for this and he cannot have it, because it is my book.

## What Her Hand Cannot Do

She read them back off the panel to herself nine seconds after the fourth one and she stopped, and she has not got past it, and it is the whole of what the eighteenth of a month was.

She cannot write a false line.

That has been in her since a room with the shutters up in the second year of the realm and it has cost her four arrangements and a hand that does not make a fist and four hundred refusals, and she has never once been able to use it for anything.

She has used it for something.

Whatever is on that panel is exactly what she thinks happened. Not approximately. Not the version a person would leave out. It is what she thinks, in a hand that cannot go anywhere else, on paper she made out of a blanket, and there is no second column anywhere in the world to read it against and no reader and no office and no man who could tell her she has got it wrong.

She wrote what the bound takes and what a man does, and it is four sentences long, and every one of them is true.

## The Difference Between The Two Records

Here is what she got on the nineteenth at the fourth hour, lying on the boards, and it is the difference between his book and hers and it is four hundred words long and it has one sentence in it.

His is a record of a thing that was done to people.

Hers is a record of the fact that it was done, on the day it was being done, by somebody who was there.

That is all of it. Not better. Not truer. He has nine hundred years of the same four four sentences worth of material and he has it in a better hand and on better paper and he is at the end of it, and she has got one leaf and eight panels and four sentences and she wrote them on the second day after a man told her the day and the hour, on four fingers and a thumb, in nine feet by eleven.

And he would have to write nine hundred years to get one sentence further than she has.

She said that last part out loud once, at the ninth hour of the nineteenth, in the ordinary voice, and then she was angry with herself for four minutes, because it is not a competition and she has never once in eight years treated anything as a competition, and she stood there and got it back down.

It is not a competition. It is a record. And it is a record because it is true and it was made by the person it is about, and those are the two things it has and neither of them is an argument.

## She Cannot Fix It Later

The hand cannot write a false line. That is the only thing in her body that has ever been of use to anybody else, and it took her four years to find a use for it.

It cannot be taken back and it cannot be rubbed out.

She tried that at the second hour of the nineteenth, with her thumbnail, on the first of the four, for four seconds, and it did not work and she was glad and then she was not glad and then she was glad again.

## The Ninth Hour Of The Nineteenth

The book has eight leaves. Four sentences are on the second leaf. The first leaf has a date on it and a place under the date and nothing else, and there are six leaves with nothing on them at all, and she is going to leave it that way, and the empties are hers and nobody else is going to fill them.

She read the four sentences to herself out loud once, in the ordinary voice, standing at the table at the ninth hour of the nineteenth.

Then she stood there with the book in her hand and worked out, in nine seconds, that she had missed the one part of it.

She has been treating a record as something a person makes.

The one she made at the sixth of a month and the one she made in the fifth of a year she could name and the four of them in four volumes of offices are all records, and every single one of them was made by somebody else and read to her or read over her or written down without her being there.

There is one thing that has ever made this hers and she has never once done it.

She has to read it back to him.

That is not a request. It is the mechanism, and it is the only mechanism, and it worked once in a room with the shutters up in the fifth month of a year she could name when she made a man of sixty-one read a page of hers aloud to four people before it went into the best book on nine days of road.

She is going to do it at an opening in a wall, in daylight, with a book on a table and a man on the other side of a jamb, and she is going to read four sentences out loud that she wrote with her own hand on paper she made out of a blanket, and then she is going to say the word.

Two days. The ninth hour on the twenty-second.
