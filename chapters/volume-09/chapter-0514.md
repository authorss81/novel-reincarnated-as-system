# A Boy Of Fifteen At The Gate

She got to the end of the long road at the eighth hour of the twenty-first of a month, having started it out of the lane at the second hour of that day, and it is eleven miles, and she knows that because she counted it with a bar.

## Eleven Miles

The bar is nine feet nine from the butt to the point of iron and she has measured it against a door frame in a room in a month she could name, and it has not changed since she was fifteen.

She put the point of it down on the made road every nine feet nine and walked, twenty-three hundred and something times, on two days, on a body at eighteen days without bread and an arm open four days in five, and she got it to eleven miles and four hundred yards and stopped counting.

It is the same eleven miles from the crossing to the gate. She had walked four of them on the fourteenth of a month in a month that has gone and stood in the middle of it at the ninth hour and turned round, and she had thought that was the whole of what she knew.

## Two Days On It

She walked it out of the lane at the second hour of the twenty-first and slept one night in a ditch at the sixth mile with her clothes on and got up at the fourth hour and walked the other five.

There was not one person on eleven miles of made road in two days. There were four carts going east and they did not stop and one of them had a driver looking at her from four hundred yards for a mile and a half and then looking at the road.

That is a road with nothing on it. She has walked nine days of road for two years and every part of it has somebody on it going to or coming from somewhere, and this is eleven miles of it with four carts on it in two days, and that is the fact of the whole of it in one measurement.

She ate nothing on the two days. She took water at a butt at the fourth mile and at the eighth and her right arm was open for most of the second half and her fourth count on a plain flat mile took about four seconds to come back instead of about two.

And at the sixth mile, in daylight, on the second day, she said out loud once, in the ordinary voice, to a made road with nothing on it.

“There is nothing here,” she said. “That is what eleven miles is.”

## The Gate And The Cup

The gate is a chain across a made road between two posts, and there is a tin cup hanging off the chain on a wire, and the wire is green and the cup has been off that chain so long that the green has come off onto the chain as well.

There is a boy of fifteen at it.

That is the whole of what is at the end of the long road and she stood nine hundred yards off for nine minutes working out whether that was a thing that could be a thing or whether she had got it wrong, and it was a boy of fifteen at a chain with a cup on it.

He was not the man who was there before.

She knows that without having been told, in the way she knows things: the boy stands the way a boy stands who has been doing a thing for a year and is not long enough out of it to have opinions, and the chain is up, and there is a cart on the near side with a load on it that wants to go through.

He had his hand on the chain and he was waiting for the cart to stop rolling before he took it up.

He works it from the fourth hour to the seventh and again from the eleventh to the third, and in between he sits on a stone at the near side of the road with his legs out. The cup is for the hot part of the day. It has hung there long enough that the green of the wire has come off onto the chain.

He is fifteen. He has been doing it since he was fourteen and he has no idea that any of this is unusual, and he did not look at her bar with any expression at all, which took her a full minute to understand.

## What She Was Going To Do

She had worked all of this out on the flat a mile short of the gate at the seventh hour.

She wanted to get past without being asked anything at all. Not her name and not where she is going and not what she is and not why she has been two and a half days on a made road with a bar at the light end.

She had bought four months of that and she had given it away on a bench on the third of a month, and she was going to walk up to the last thing on nine days of road that had not noticed her and ask her to go on not noticing her.

And the gate had a boy on it.

She stood there for a while and then she went up to it with the bar on her shoulder, because she had walked eleven miles to find out what was at the end of a road and the road had a chain on it.

## What He Said

He put the chain up when the cart had gone through and he came back and put it down and looked at her, and then he looked at the bar, and then he looked at her again.

He did not ask her anything.

He did not ask her name. He did not ask her where she was going. He did not ask her why she was walking east with a bar in the rain and had been walking for two days.

He said five things across four minutes, in between working the chain for two carts that came up, and none of them was a question.

“The man that had this chain is not here.”

“He went in the spring.”

“Nobody put anybody else on it.”

“The chain goes up and the chain goes down.”

“There is not an office here. There is a chain and a cup and me.”

Then he took a drink out of the cup that hung on the chain, which is what the cup is for, and wiped his mouth on his hand, and looked at her bar again.

## The First Thing That Was Not Careful

She stood on the near side of that chain for half a minute and let it be a thing, because it is not a small thing.

The chain does not know her. The chain does not know that a girl came out of a lane in the fourth month and has walked out of every room in two years. The chain does not know about a book, or a bench, or a man with a brush, or a place somebody named on a bench in daylight.

The chain goes up and the chain goes down. A cup hangs off it because a boy works a gate for nine hours in the hot part of the day.

That is the whole of what is at the end of the long road and it is the first thing on nine days of road that has not been careful with her, and she stood there with a bar on her shoulder in the rain and discovered that she did not know what to do with a person who was not careful with her, and that she had never had to work that out before.

## What She Lifted

A cart came up from the west at the ninth hour loaded with boards, and the boy had it up and down nine times an hour and he was fifteen and he was doing it alone.

She put the bar down against the post and took the bottom of the chain in her left hand and took the weight of it and lifted it up over the cart and held it up over nine feet of made road and put it down behind.

Four seconds. The cup swung on the wire and did not hit the post.

He did not ask her to and he did not say thank you and he did not make a face.

“Two more,” he said.

There were two more. She lifted the chain for both of them and she took nothing for it and he said nothing about that either, and after the second one he went to the cup and had a drink and held it across, and she drank out of it, because her hand could take that and it is not a gift.

## What Is Past The Gate

Past the chain the road keeps going for about four hundred yards and then there is a bar on two posts.

That is all there is to see from the gate. It is a single bar across a way between two stone posts, the height of her chest, and it is not a gate and it has no chain on it and there is no cup and no boy.

A bar on two posts, four hundred yards off, standing on a made road in the flat middle of eleven miles of made road.

She stood at the gate with the cup back on its wire and looked at it for a quarter of an hour and did not go and stand in front of it.

What she worked out on the last mile west, at the third hour of the twenty-second, out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, sitting on a stone off the road with her bag beside her, is this.

She bought four months of being nobody by never once going east of that bar.

And there is exactly one thing in the world that cannot be refused by a person being careful, and it is a book with a man behind it, and she has been eleven miles east for two days looking for it, and a girl who is careful with her own safety has never once gone and stood in front of a bar on two posts and found out what is on the far side of it.

She walked a quarter of a mile east on the twenty-second and turned round at a stone that somebody has set in the grass on purpose, and came back to the chain, and asked the boy one question about water.

Then she went west two hundred yards on the made road and put her bag down and lay down on the flat with her clothes on and did not sleep.
