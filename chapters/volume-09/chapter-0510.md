# What They Think Is Coming

She walked nine miles east at the fourth hour of the ninth of a month to a frame four hundred years old standing in a cut, and she stood in the room with the six day books from the fourth hour to the seventh, and she asked for nothing, and nothing was asked of her, and that is the whole of the ninth.

## The Room With Six Day Books

It is off the cut on the low side, a long low room with a bench along one wall and six books on a shelf, one for each working party on the frame, and the days go up the inside of them in a hand that is not any of the nine men’s.

A woman of forty-one keeps them. She has a rate at the head of the second book in ink that has not been wet in a month, and a stool, and a pot of something for the men who come in wet, and she has been on that stool for six years.

That is the arrangement. You go in and you say your name and you say what you did that day, and she writes it in the book for your party, and on the last day of the week the floor takes a rate out of what it takes in and the balance is what the men get.

There is a piece of paper for none of it, because there is nothing on this ground to put a piece of paper. There is a woman with a book and nine men who have worked the same frame since before she came here, and it has worked that way for four hundred years.

Ning Yan had stood in that room eleven times in eight years and had been written in one of those books twice.

## Three Hours

She stood in it from the fourth hour of the ninth to the seventh and she had a bar leaning against the wall by her and she did not sit down.

At the fifth hour the first man came in off the frame with sawdust on him and put his hand flat on the second book and said his name and said thirty-one feet of sill and went out.

At the sixth hour another one came in and said his name and said a stone out of the cut and a day and a half on the leg-man’s bench, which is not a joke and everybody in the room heard it, and went out.

At the sixth hour and a half a third man came in and said his name and said nothing else at all, and she wrote nothing down, and he went out.

Eleven men came through that door between the fourth hour and the seventh. Eleven names, all of them said out loud, all of them in a book in nine seconds, and not one of them looked at the woman standing by the wall with a bar leaning against it.

That is the finding and she has it exactly and she said it out loud once, at the seventh hour, in the room, in the ordinary voice, to a woman of forty-one who had not asked her a single thing for three hours.

“They think somebody has got me,” she said.

The woman of forty wrote a figure in the second book and did not look up, and after a while she said, “That is not a thing I can put anywhere.”

“No,” Ning Yan said.

“All right,” the woman said, and went on writing, and that was the end of it and it was the ordinary courtesy of a counter and it was correct.

## What A Line In One Of Those Books Is Worth

It is a figure at the end of the week and it is never much, and the women who keep them have been keeping them for six years and there are four of them on this ground and not one of them has ever been cruel to anybody.

It is also the only thing she has ever been able to do that has ever been any use to a stranger, and her hand will not take it.

That is not fastidiousness and it has never once been a choice. She stood in a room in a month she could name and put money into her own hand from a woman behind a counter and her fingers shut on it and something in the arm went the other way, and the money was in her hand for about four seconds and then it was on the floor, and the woman picked it up and did not ask and has not asked since.

She can do the work. She cannot be paid for it. That is the eighth cost in eight years and it is the one that has kept her off every floor that could have used her, and she has never told anybody about it in a room because there is no room.

## Why

She stood in the road outside for a quarter of an hour after the seventh hour and worked out the mechanism, and it took nine minutes, and it is the same mechanism as everything else that has happened to her in a month, and it is a page.

If they thought she was free, one of them would ask her.

Anybody on this ground with a hole in a job for half a day will look at the strongest person within nine hundred yards, because that is what the hole is for. She has had forty-one lines a month read off a board twice and taken none. She has stood at a rail for eleven counted minutes and got nothing. She has been offered work by a man with a paste pot and a rag and a ladder, twice, and both times because she had nothing.

None of that is in the room any more.

It is not that they have stopped noticing her. She was in that room for three hours and eleven men came through a door nine feet from her and every single one of them knew exactly where she was standing, because a man knows where a strong body is in a room the way a dog knows where another dog is.

It is that asking her has stopped being a thing anybody does. Nine men, one of whom has a bar on his own leg that she fitted with her own two hands in a month that has gone, and asking her is the same as asking a person who has got somebody.

## Zheng

He came down off the frame at the ninth hour and she was still on the low side of the cut with the bar.

He got his weight over onto his good leg the way he gets off anything, and he stood four feet off, and he looked at the cut and not at her.

“You have been east,” he said.

That is all he said.

She waited for the rest of it, because on the seventh of a month he gave her nine sentences and one of them was that he was not going to ask her where she got to.

He said the rest of it with his jaw instead. She watched him do it, all of it, for nine seconds: he looked at the frame, and at the wall, and at the four men still on the sill end, and he did not say the words for the bar on his leg, and he did not say the words for the rate, and then he went back up the frame and did not look back.

She stood there and let that be a thing for a while.

## The Anger

It came up two hundred yards west of the cut on flat ground at the ninth hour, and it was clean and it was cold, and it was the first anger in five months that was not for wanting a thing.

She was not angry at the nine men. She would have done exactly what they did, and she has said so on a bench before, and she has meant it.

She was angry at the arrangement.

Four months. She stood at a rail eleven counted minutes. She stood in a store two hundred times and got good. She read a full board twice and took none. A woman of fifty said to her face that a day with nothing on the bench was a day she had nothing for her to do, and meant it, and was right.

And then on the third of a month she said forty words out loud on a bench in daylight to a man with a brush, and all of nine days of road changed its mind about her in eleven days, and now there is not one thing in the world she can do that a person will ask her to do.

That is what she has for it. That is the price of a sentence she chose and wanted and would say again.

She got to the flat at the west end of the lane at the tenth hour and she said it out loud once, in the ordinary voice, standing up, with a bar in her hand.

“I am not going to explain myself to anybody,” she said. “Not one word, not to one of them, not in a room, not on a road. Eleven people think somebody has got me and the only way to put it right is to stand in a room and say what came for me and where it went, and there is not one room on nine days of road I can put that in.”

## Four Miles East

The leg-man’s bench is four miles east of the cut on a made road and it is a bench and a hut and a leg-man who has been on that road thirty years and has never once asked her a question.

The arrangement is the arrangement and it has held for two years: he puts a loaf out on the bench and she eats a third of it standing up and he takes nothing for it and he has never said that he is giving it to her and she has never said thank you.

It is one day in four. It was the third of this month and it was the seventh and it should have been the eleventh, and it is the ninth, and she was owed one.

She walked the four miles at the tenth hour with the bar at the light end and got there in an hour and a quarter on a body at half.

There was a loaf on the bench under a cloth.

The leg-man was on the bench with his leg out in front of him in the last of the light and a shoe off and his sock turned down, and the loaf was under the cloth beside him, and he had put it out and he had not put it out.

He did not turn round. He did not look up. He sat on that bench with his sock turned down and a loaf eleven inches off his hand and he did not put it out.

She stood on the road in front of that bench for the length of time it takes to count to a hundred and it is not a long time, and then she took the bar and went west into the dark.
