# Two Miles That Go Nowhere

She went west out of the far ground on the fourteenth of a month at the fourth hour with the ring in one pocket and the dish in the bag and the bar on her shoulder, and she did not go through the bar on two posts, and she has not been through it since the second hour of the eighth of the month beginning 1,514.

## What She Had

Twelve days of standing in an opening in a wall at the fourth hour, and a light in a window behind it that had not gone out once, and a man in a chair some mornings.

Eleven miles of made road between that wall and the turn where the lane meets the store road.

Four fingers and a thumb. Right arm open four days in five. Eight of eleven holds. Four counts of breath everywhere including inside a loaded reach, and five seconds of it at the fourth hour on flat ground.

Nineteen years old. No wages. She ate a third of a loaf off a stone wall at the cut on the sixteenth and there has been nothing since, and she is thinner than she was on the eighth of that one.

A dish with a lump of clay in it and a ring a finger’s width, both put in a bag with her own two hands.

## The Seventeenth

She got to the gate at the eleventh hour on the fifteenth and the boy of fifteen was on the chain and he asked her the question he asks everybody and she said no and he said all right, and that is the last conversation on nine days of road that has anything to do with her and it is not about her.

She went past the gate and down the long road.

Eleven miles, and she had counted it twice with a bar and she did not count it again. There were four carts on it going east and one going west and none of them stopped and one of them had a man in it who had said good to her once at ten feet and drove on.

She slept the night in the ditch at the sixth mile with the bag under her head.

## The Lane

She came into the lane at the crossing on the sixteenth at the seventh hour and went past the shed at the end of it without looking at it, and she went past the woman of fifty, who was on the step and did not say anything at all, which is the third time that has happened.

Then the cut, and the frame, and the board outside the room with the six day books, and she read the whole board down in nine seconds and there was still nothing on it with her name on it, and it has been eleven weeks.

There was a loaf on the low stone wall at the mouth of the track, squared up with a hand, put out and not put out by a man she has never spoken to and has never once been introduced to, and it had been there since the sixth hour. She ate a third of it standing up with her back to a frame four hundred years old and she put the other two thirds in the bag, and she did not say thank you.

Then the store road.

And at the store road, a quarter of a mile short of the turn, there is a bench.

## The Bench

It is a bench outside a store five miles west of a mill and it is two feet off the road and it has been two feet off that road for two years, and she has stood at it a hundred times and eaten at it and gone away.

She stopped at it at the ninth hour on the sixteenth and she stood where she has stood a hundred times, and the store was shut, and the woman was behind the counter, and there was a loaf on the shelf that a man who stopped coming has not paid for and has never been given away.

Ning Yan looked at the bench for about nine seconds.

She knew where it was. She could have said how it was built. She knew which board in it had split in the winter and which side of it she had always stood on and where the man used to put the heel of his hand.

None of that was anywhere in her.

There was no past in it at all. It was a bench, four feet long, two feet off a made road, and she had been in the same place as it a hundred times and there was nothing in her anywhere that had ever been to it.

## Two Miles

The lane is two miles between that bench and the turn.

She walked it on the seventeenth, in daylight, with the bar on her shoulder, on four fingers and a thumb, and it took her exactly as long as it has always taken.

That is the whole of it and it is why she went over it four times.

It took her as long. Her boots came down on the same stones at the same rate and her arm went open at the same place at the ninth hundred yard, which is the low wall at the mouth of the track down into the cut, and her fourth count on the flat at the four-mile mark took four seconds, because that is what it has taken since she was seventeen.

And there was nothing in it.

Two miles of lane between a bench and a turn, walked four times in one day by a woman who has walked it two hundred times in two years, and not one yard of it went anywhere she had been.

She is not slow. She is not tired, though she ate a third of a loaf off a stone wall on the sixteenth and has had nothing since. She is not confused and there is nothing wrong with her and she has four fingers and a thumb and eight of eleven holds.

The ground is there. The stones are there. The distance is exact.

It is a road she cannot go back down.

## The Cost

She said it out loud once, on the seventeenth at the ninth hour, at the turn, in the ordinary voice, with nothing within nine hundred yards and no wall and no door in sight.

“I cannot go back down this road,” Ning Yan said.

Then she stood at the turn for a quarter of an hour and let that be a thing, because it is the third one and it is the largest of the three.

Not Lowwater. Not the mill. Not the store, and not the shed four miles east of a mill whose wheel has not turned since before she came.

Two miles of lane. A bench. A cut. A frame. A woman of fifty on a step. A board with forty-one lines on it.

She can lie down in any of them and nothing holds. She can walk all of them and they go forward.

## The Stage

It came up at the fourth hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544, out of the flat at the turn, a quarter of a mile from the bench and eleven miles from a wall, with no wall and no door within nine hundred yards and no person within nine hundred yards.

The clay came up out of the dish in her bag and it came up out of the place low behind her fourth rib at the same time, and it came up four inches and stood there, the way it has stood four times in four hundred days, and she said the word out loud on flat ground in the ordinary voice and it did not go anywhere.

Day one thousand four hundred and eighty-eight is the last one of these, and day one thousand five hundred and sixty-one is this one, and five hundred and sixty-one less four hundred and eighty-eight is seventy-three.

Seventy-three days.

It is the shortest gap between any two of them since she stood at the second stage of the realm, and it is short because she stopped waiting to be paid four months ago and stopped waiting to be asked three weeks ago and there has not been an office left to wait for since the fourth month, and she has not said any of that out loud to anybody and is not going to.

It took nine seconds and it said the one sentence into the ground, and it did not say anything after it.

[Ning Yan, past the line, servant-born, under no ceiling any county holds. Severance, stage three, is held.]

Then nothing.

## She Did Not Weep

She sat on the flat at the turn with the dish beside her for four minutes and then she put her hand down and it came down and stood on the clay and was steady, and she listened for nine minutes for a second sentence.

There was no second sentence.

She is nineteen years old and she is past the ceiling by three stages and she has no wages and no shed and no place and a road behind her that she walked four times in one day and could not once go back down, and there is one sheet of paper against her ribs that she opened with her own two hands in the fourth month of a year she could name.

She did not weep about it.

That is not courage and she is not going to say that it is. She has had four of these in four hundred days and she has cried at none of them, and the reason is that there is nothing in the lane to cry at, because the lane is not hers any more and it is not anybody’s, and a person cannot weep for a place that has stopped being the place they were.

## Turning Back

She turned round at the turn at the seventh hour of the eighteenth and went east.

She did not go back down the lane. She went in at the other end of it, up past the cut and up past the frame and the board outside the room with the six day books, and she did not stop at any of them.

East up the lane past the shed at the end of it without looking at it. Past the crossing with its painted board and its chain and the boy of seventeen on it.

Eleven miles of long road in the last light of the eighteenth, with the bar at the light end and the dish in the bag.

And at the gate she did not go through and she did not stop, because there is a bar on two posts four hundred yards east of it and she went past it on the outside and stood in the room with the boards on the floor for the night.

The light in the hut window was burning.

## What Is Left To Decide

She lay on the boards with her clothes on and did not sleep, and she worked out the last of it, and it took four minutes and it is the whole of the eighteenth of a month.

She cannot keep a place by staying in it. That has been true since a shed with boards on the floor stopped being hers on the nineteenth of a month in a year she could name.

She cannot be met where she is expected. That has been true since the fifth of a month that has gone, and it is why a man of nine hundred years walked four miles up a lane and found nobody in it.

And now the road behind her is gone, in the way that a thing is gone when it is still there.

There is one thing left on nine days of road that she has not got, and it is the only thing in the world she has ever been entered for, and there is a man of nine hundred years behind a wall at the end of eleven miles of made road with a light on in his window who keeps a book.

She is going to have to go and be written down, in daylight, in a room, on the ordinary condition.

And the room is nine feet by eleven and it has a chair in it and a table and a book, and she has slept on its boards for thirteen nights and has not sat in the chair since the thirteenth of a month that has gone.
