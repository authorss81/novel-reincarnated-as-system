# A Light Behind The Wall

She was in the room on the ninth, the tenth and the eleventh of a month and she did not go through the opening once in three days, and that is a thing she has thought about more than anything else since.

## Three Days

The room is nine feet by eleven with boards on the floor, a chimney that draws, a chair, a table, a book on the table, and a bar of a wall across the back of it with an opening in it four feet wide.

There is a bed frame against the west wall with nothing on it and a shelf and a jug and a blanket on the shelf, folded, in a manner that is four hundred years old.

That is all of what is in the room. She has been through it nine times in three days and counted the objects, because counting the objects is what she does.

## What Three Days Came To

She had not eaten since the second of this month and there is nothing in the room and there is nothing within four hundred yards of it, and she has a skin at the gate and she went and filled it twice at the sixth hour and the tenth hour and carried it back through the bar on two posts and through the opening in the wall, and it cost her a number of seconds she did not count.

She is eighteen years old and she has been in that room three days with a door that shuts and a man on the other side of a wall.

She went through the opening eleven times in three days. She went through it to look at the hut window and she went through it to look at the flat and she went through it twice in the night for no reason at all except that her body wanted to be standing in it.

There is not one thing on this ground that asks her anything. There is one opening in a wall in it and she has walked through that eleven times and it has not asked her for one thing either.

## The Chair By The Light

She asked for an answer out of the wall at the second hour of the twelfth of a month and she got nothing for nine hours.

She did it properly. She stood in the opening and she said his name out loud into a hundred yards of flat ground in the ordinary voice, once, and waited, and the light in the hut window did not change.

Then she said his name again, which is the one time in eight years she has said a name twice, and she waited another nine minutes.

Then she went and shut the door of the room and stood with her hand on the inside of it and waited for the latch to go, and it went, and that is the sound he makes, and she stood there in a nine-foot room with her hand on a shut door and listened to the smallest thing anybody has ever heard her make.

## The Light

The light in the hut window is the whole of what she has.

It has been on continuously since the ninth hour of the eighth of a month and she has watched it for three days from a hundred yards and it has not gone out once, not for a minute, not in daylight and not in the dark.

She went out to the window on the tenth and she put her eye against the glass and there is a small room in there and there is a light on a stand in it and there is a chair by the light.

And on the eleventh she went out again and stood at the window for an hour and there is a chair by the light.

That is all that is in the hut. She has not seen him and he has not come to the window and she has not seen him move.

## What She Said To The Wall

She said one thing out loud to the wall on the twelfth of a month at the ninth hour, in the ordinary voice, standing in the opening with her face to a hundred yards of flat ground and a lit window at the end of it.

She said it to a wall and she does not know whether it carries nine feet or a hundred yards and she did not raise her voice.

“I know it is you in there,” she said.

That is all of it. Seven words, once, in the ordinary voice, and she has said everything out loud exactly once all her life and this is the first thing she has said that she was not saying to the room she was standing in for the sake of the count.

Then she stood in the opening for about a minute and a half and nothing came back out of the dark, and she went in and shut the door and waited for the latch.

## The Chair In The Room

At about the fourth hour of the thirteenth she sat down in it.

There was nothing else to do with her body. That is the whole of the reason and she is aware that it is the worst of the reasons she has ever had for anything.

She had been up for two days. She was thinner than she had been on the eighth. Her right arm had been open since the ninth hour and had not closed, and her fourth count on a plain flat floor with nothing in it had taken nine seconds at the fourth hour on the tenth and she had measured it twice.

She pulled the chair out from the wall and sat down in it and put her back against the straight back of it and her hands in her lap.

It is not a good chair. It is straight-backed and it has no cloth on it and it is nine hundred years old and it is the chair of a man who keeps a book in a room nine feet by eleven against the near face of a wall.

## What An Empty Chair Is

She sat in it for about twenty minutes and then she got up and pushed it back against the wall and squared it, the way a person squares a thing.

And that is the whole of the thirteenth and it is the thing she will not be able to put down for the rest of her life.

She has seen an empty chair before. She has been in rooms with them. A chair by a wall with nobody in it is the ordinary furniture of a thing that has happened to a person, and the whole world has a way of getting round it, which is to say that the person is not in the chair.

This chair is empty because a man of nine hundred years is alive on the far side of a wall he opened for her, and there is a light on in his window, and he is not coming back.

That is worse than one, and it is worse because there is nothing to be done and nobody to be angry at and nothing anywhere on nine days of road that can be sent for.

## What She Did Not Do

She got up out of the chair and she did not go through the opening in the wall.

She has been through it eleven times. It costs her nothing. There is no bar on it and no threshold and no door and the boards of the room are the same boards on both sides of it.

She stood in it for a long time on the night of the thirteenth and looked east at a window with a light in it a hundred yards off and she did not go.

She wants that written down plainly, because everything else in this is a machine and this is the only part of it that is a person.

A wall she has walked through eleven times in three days, at no cost, with no bar on it and nothing broken anywhere on it.

And a light on the far side of it that has not gone out for six days.

She stood there and she could have gone through it and she did not, and she did not go through it because there is nothing in that hut she is entitled to, and that is the whole of the reason and it took her twenty minutes at a wall at the fourth hour of the fourteenth to be sure of it.

## The Whole Of The Night

The bill arrived at about the fourth hour of the fourteenth and it did not arrive as a figure and it did not arrive as a sentence.

It arrived as a nine-foot room with a man in it who is not answering.

She lay down on the boards with her clothes on at the eleventh hour of the thirteenth and she lay on them until the light came off the flat, and she got up twice, and at the second hour she went to the door and shut it and waited for the latch, and at the ninth hour she went and stood at the opening in the wall again.

The light in the hut window was burning. It was burning at the fourth hour and it was burning at the ninth hour and it was burning at the second hour of the fourteenth and she watched all three of those from about eleven feet off, and there has not been one minute since the ninth hour of the eighth of this month when it was not.
