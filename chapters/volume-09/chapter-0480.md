# Three Coppers And Bread

She went west on the fourteenth because there was nothing else to do with a morning, and she got to the store at the third hour of the fifteenth, and she slept under a lean-to of cut timber at the side of the made road at the eighth hour of the fourteenth with her boots on.

Five miles past the mill and then some. The mill itself is four miles east of where she sleeps and the wheel has not turned since before she came, and the road between the two is flat and made and about as interesting as the inside of a board, and she has walked it enough times that her feet know the one loose stone in it.

She had three coppers when she started out. She had bread for a day when she started out, and she ate what was left of it at the mile stone and then she was hungry, which is a thing she has not been in four months, and she noticed the difference and did not enjoy it.

## The Store Counter

The woman who keeps the store has been keeping it for two years and has never once asked Ning Yan anything that was not about bread or about the price of bread.

She has the same slow way of moving round the counter to get behind it that she has always had. There are two shelves of nothing in particular behind her and a bench for people who are not buying and a lamp in the daytime that is lit for the look of it.

“Good,” the woman said.

That was the whole of the greeting and it was two years old, and there was nothing in it and nothing taken out of it.

Ning Yan stood with a bar against the counter that she had to hold at the light end because the heavy end has been closed to her for good, and she put two coppers down on the wood, and the woman picked them up and put them in a box and brought out a loaf and set it on the counter.

Two coppers. It has been two coppers every time for as long as she has been coming here.

## Two Coppers A Loaf

She took the loaf and stood in the middle of the floor of the store holding it in the crook of the arm that is open half the time, and she had not wanted bread.

That was the thing she could not get past for a minute, standing there, with the lamp lit in the daytime.

She does not want food. That is a settled thing and it has been settled for four months and she went into it on purpose and came out of it exactly where she wanted to be. A body at the top of a realm does not ask for anything, and she worked that out early and did not fight it and has not fought it since.

And then she stood in a store and put down two coppers for bread because she wanted it.

Not needed it. She could have walked another four miles and been perfectly fine. Wanted it, in the plain way a person wants a thing, with a mouth and not a reason, and she had a body that had stopped asking for everything and had then, on a made road at five miles past a mill that has not turned, asked for one specific thing.

She said it out loud there, which she should not have done, because it is a store and there is a woman in it.

“I have not wanted anything in four months,” she said, “and I have just paid two coppers for bread because I wanted it.”

The woman looked at her.

“All right,” she said, and went behind the shelves.

## What She Did Not Buy

There is salt at that counter and there is oil and there is a thing made of bean that costs a copper and keeps for nine days and that she has bought twice in two years because she was walking.

She looked at all three of them on the fourteenth and she did not buy one of them, and she wants that on the page because it is the shape of the whole of the last four months in a single counter.

The rate is the rate. Nobody in the history of that store has ever been talked into a thing, and she is eighteen years old with a bar she cannot hold at the heavy end and eight of eleven holds, and there is not one argument she is able to make to a woman behind a counter about the price of anything.

She has got no wages. She has got no season. There is nobody nine days of road who would hire her for anything at all.

And she is the only person on nine days of road who can read a hand that four people cannot read, and she has not read one for money in eight months, because the price of reading one is a room with somebody in it and she said no in one of those rooms.

Three coppers. Two of them on the wood. One left.

She picked the loaf up in the crook of her arm and she went out and she did not say thank you either, because she had not said anything else all the way in and she was not going to start on the way out.

## The Board In The Shed

She came up to the shed at the seventh hour of the sixteenth with the loaf and one copper and the bar, and she put the loaf down on the boards and got the point of the bar out and wrote the figures on the board where nobody checks them.

Three. Two. One.

And then, because she is a person who does not let a sum stand on a board without the rest of it, she wrote underneath it, in the same hand, on the same board:

“eight coppers on a book nine days of road west, and my hand will not take them, and she has said she will never chase them.”

And under that:

“four coppers put on a counter for a reading, and a man of forty swept them back before my hand closed on them.”

She stood and looked at the two lines for a while.

Neither of them is money. One of them is a debt somebody else is holding and the other one is a price somebody decided not to charge her, and she has carried both of them for months without once putting them next to each other, and putting them next to each other did not make them into anything.

They were not money on the fifteenth and they are not money on the sixteenth and no arrangement of them ever is going to be.

## The Anger

It came up on the made road at about the second mile coming home and it was not a big one and she had it out and was finished with it inside a quarter of an hour.

It was this. That a woman is allowed to want bread.

That is all. Nothing else. She has been a resource for eight years, and a resource that is not being used at all is a resource with a great deal of time on its hands, and she had spent four months being proud of that and being fed by nobody and reading nothing for anybody, and the first thing her body did with all of it was ask for a loaf of bread.

She was angry with herself and she was angry with the road and she was, for four minutes, angry with a woman behind a counter who had put two coppers in a box and brought out a loaf and said “good” and gone behind her shelves, and who had done nothing wrong at all.

She worked it out on a stone by the mile mark and she let it go, because it does not get better with carrying.

Then she ate a third of the loaf sitting on a stone at the side of the road, and it was the best thing she has put in her mouth since the fourteenth of a month four months back, and she was not seen eating it, and that was not why she liked it.

## One Day Of It

She was up before the light on the sixteenth and she made the ring and put a new lump in it and did not go down to the rail, because the rail is a thing for a morning and she wanted to spend a morning on it properly and she had not had a morning she wanted to spend in four months.

She sat on the boards with the loaf beside her and worked out what she had left.

Three coppers, two of them on bread on the fifteenth, and one left.

One and a half a day, at two a loaf, is three.

She had three and she had one, and the difference was two days of walking, and she could stand in a store at eight o’clock in the morning and put two coppers down for a thing she wanted, or she could walk, and she had one.

The board in the shed was a board and the board four miles west of her was full of work she could do and could not take, and there is not one thing on nine days of road that wants anything from her at all, and she has said that out loud twice and both times it came out where arithmetic always comes out.

Then she sat with the loaf on her knees and thought about a thing that had nothing to do with money.

She had bought bread because she wanted it. That meant something was still in her that wanted things, and she had assumed for four months that there was not, because there is nothing in her that wants to be needed.

The two of those are not the same thing, and she has been living in the gap between them for four months without ever once looking down into it.

She looked down into it for a quarter of an hour on the sixteenth and what was down there was not a person who wanted bread.

It was a person who wanted to be asked.
