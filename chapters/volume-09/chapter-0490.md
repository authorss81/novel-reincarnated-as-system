# The Count After Opening

On the second of the month she went down to the lane at the fifth hour and stood at the rail, and the first thing she did was read the board.

Forty-one lines.

She read all forty-one down and got the same forty-one she has got for forty-nine days now, and the board is still full, and there is not a line on it in any hand that refers to anything she has done in the last six days.

There is no fifth entry. There is no entry of any kind. She has been inside her own coat with the only document in the world open against her ribs for six days and no floor on nine days of road has put a single mark anywhere.

That is the answer to the question she came down there to ask, and it took four minutes, and then she stood there with it.

## The Second Question

She has never once asked the man behind the counter a question.

That is two years. Two hundred times she has stood at that counter and the man has asked her about a rate four times and she has answered every one, and she has never asked him one word about anything, because there was never a reason and because a person who reads forty-one lines is not a person who asks a counter about a rate.

So she asked him one.

“What does a dressed stone come to now.”

She asked it in the ordinary voice at the sixth hour, standing four feet off, the way the boy on the stair asks him things.

He told her. He gave her the rate and then he gave her the date it went up and then he said the stone was going to be short this month and gave her the reason in nine seconds, and it was all correct, and it was the same voice he uses for everybody who has ever asked him anything.

She stood there and worked out that she had just done the only experiment available to her and it had failed, and it failed because of her.

She cannot tell whether he treats her differently.

That is the whole of the finding and it is worth more than it sounds. She has spent eight years building an instrument out of being the person a place can put its hand on, and she has just found out that the instrument does not run backwards. A floor cannot be asked whether it is afraid of you. A man behind a counter cannot be asked whether he thinks about you.

The only way to measure what your absence is worth is to be absent somewhere that keeps a figure on you.

And there is not one place on nine days of road that keeps a figure on her. That is what she bought. She bought it in a room with the shutters up.

## What Has Not Changed

She walked the whole of it in four days, because she is not a person who trusts one floor.

The rail: no change. Forty-one lines, full board, nothing that wants anything from her.

The store: good. Four hundred times now. A woman who has been good to her for two years and who has never asked her a name and who said “good” to her on the third of this month and has not said one word since.

The trough road: forty goats and a stone trough and a full skin.

The lane, the cut, the frame, and the room with the six day books, which is thirteen miles east and which she got to at the eighth hour on the fifth with no bread and a bar on her shoulder and her back in a state she did not enjoy.

She stood in that room and she read all six books.

She did it in a quarter of an hour and she is quick. She went to the one that would have her in it — the fourth, the one they used to write her column into and then her date into — and she read the last eleven pages of it, all of them, from the top.

There is nothing about her in any of them.

There has been nothing about her in any of them since the seventh of a month, when a strip came back down the mill road on a cart that does not go east, and what is in the book now is a line that is not a name, and the date against that line is the third of a month, and a man of sixty-one wrote it in the ordinary hand.

Not one word has gone in since. She is not on any floor of that building. She has not been asked about a rate in a fortnight. Her name is not against a date anywhere and never is going to be.

## The Fourth Book

She read the fourth one twice anyway, because it is the one that matters and because she is the only person alive who can read it, and here is what is in it.

Two hundred and eleven loads. One frame. A rate that has not moved since a month that has gone. Nine men with a date against them and a figure and a hand she can read.

Eleven pages, in four months, and not one of them has her on it, and she has put more work into that room in two years than any other person who has ever stood in it, and the reason is a sentence about a column being a request that she said to herself sixty-three days ago on a made road.

There is a thing she wanted from that room for four years and it was never wages. It was being in the book.

That is what nobody on nine days of road would have guessed about her, and she worked it out on the fifth of the month standing in a room with six day books in it, and then she walked out, and the walking was thirteen miles and she had nothing in her.

## Thirteen Miles On Nothing

She had not eaten since the twenty-first of the month before. She is not hungry, and that is wearing off, and she has been getting one day out of seven out of the trough road and a body at the top of a realm is not a body that can do that for long.

She came in at the ninth hour on the fifth with her right arm open and her back in a state she did not enjoy and she lay down on the boards and lay there for a while and did not get up.

That was the sixth of a month by then. The door does not shut and she barred it with the light end of the bar and lay in a shed with no bread in her for the second night running, and the small open place behind her fourth rib took the fourth count forty times before the light, the same as any other night.

She slept nine hours, which is the most she has slept in two years, and she did not dream and she woke up able to stand up.

## The Win

She got back to the shed at the ninth hour of the fifth and lay down on the boards and let it happen, because it took two days to come and she had earned two days.

It was this, and she said it out loud once on the boards, because she says everything out loud exactly once:

She is free.

Not the way she was free in a room with the shutters up. Actually free, in the way that the word means, for the first time since she was fourteen years old.

Nothing on nine days of road can enter her. Nothing can read her. There is no hand in any building on any road that can put a column against her and follow it, and she did that with one act in a shed with a bar under the door, and it took nine minutes.

Whatever comes for her now has to come with its own hands.

She lay there and let forty minutes of that go by.

## The Cost

And then she worked out what it costs, and it cost her nine seconds to work it out and it took the rest of the night.

Nobody noticed.

That is the cost and there is no way round it. She has done the single hardest thing anybody has ever done on that ground and the ground has not moved. The rail is full and does not have her on it. The counter has its rate. The day books have six of them and not one word in any of them.

If she had walked into a floor on the third of a month and put a hand on the wood and asked every person in the room to look at what she had done, she would have been entered by the ninth person before she got to the door, and there would have been a column with her on it going east on the first, and a man of sixty-one tying a folder.

Instead there is a shed with boards on the floor and a woman at a store who says good.

She is the only person in the world who knows what is written on that sheet, and there is nobody she could tell, and she is not going to try, and that is not a small thing to have decided on a sixth day of a month with no bread in her.

## Keeping Still

And then she did the thing the chapter is named for.

She went to the end of the six day books and she read the fourth one backwards from the last page to the first, which takes nine minutes, and there was nothing, and she came home.

And she made a decision about the shed.

She is going to keep sleeping in it. Not because it is comfortable — the door does not shut and she has to bar it with a piece of her own bar to shut it at all — but because she wants to know whether a place stays.

That is what she is testing, and she has been in this shed two years and has never once thought of it as hers, and she has now decided to find out whether it can be. She is going to lie on those boards every night and do nothing and watch whether the boards go somewhere.

And on the sixth of the month, at the seventh hour, on the cart track a mile short of the mill, with the light going and no bread and thirteen miles in her legs, a man was standing nine feet off to the side of the track.

He had a notebook shut under his arm.

He had been standing there for four minutes before she saw him, and he had not moved and he had not said anything, and he did not move and he did not say anything now.
