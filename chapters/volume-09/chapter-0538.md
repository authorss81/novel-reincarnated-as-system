# Where Keeping Is What He Does

She put a book of her own on a flat stone under a ladder at a post on a strip road at the ninth hour of the twenty-fifth of a month, in daylight, in front of three people, and she did not ask anybody to keep it.

## Four In The Morning

She came onto the strip road in the dark at the fourth hour and there was nothing on the board at the post.

That means it is between days. The board is filled off a cart every nine or ten days and it will be twenty years in the spring coming.

It had been two days since the last one and she knew that before she had walked the last mile, because she had read the board on the first of a month and it had eleven lines on it.

There was a trough at the foot of the post with a dipper on a chain and a rail and a ladder that went up to the board and not past it.

She filled the skin and drank a quarter of an hour standing up and sat down on the rail with the bar at her feet, and she waited five hours, and there was nothing on nine days of road that wanted anything from her, as usual.

## The Stone

It is under the bottom of the ladder where nobody walks, and it is the size of two hands together, and there have been three names in the book on it in nineteen years and every one of them the man with the paste pot put there himself.

She got her own book out of her coat.

It came out the way a thing comes out that has been lying against a body for two days. Eight leaves the size of a closed hand, folded in half three times. A date and a place on the first leaf. Four sentences on the second. Six empty ones.

She squared it on the stone the way you square a thing, and she put it down beside the other book with an inch of stone between them, and she did not put it on top of the other one.

## She Did Not Ask Him To Keep It

There were three reasons and she had all three of them by the fourth hour and she wants them on the page because it is the single act the whole of this is for.

She cannot put it in his hands. Her hand will not take and it will not put a thing into anybody else’s hands either, and that is one rule and it has been one rule since she was fourteen, and it does not have an exception in it for a man she has read four sentences to.

She cannot ask him for it. She has stood at that ladder twice in two years and had it in her hand to ask for the stone and did not ask, and on the first of a month she asked him for a sheet of paper and he said no, and a man who keeps three names under a ladder for nineteen years is not going to be asked to keep a fourth by a girl with a bar who has been there twice.

And there is nothing owed.

A thing that is kept is not a thing that is owed. She worked that out at a post on the first of a month and it has been the truest thing anybody has said to her on nine days of road in four months.

So she did not ask. She put it where keeping is what he does.

## The Man With The Rag

He came at the seventh hour and he had a cart and a driver and a boy of eleven with him and he started on the board with the paste pot.

He saw her on the rail. He saw the book on the stone.

He did not say one word about either of them.

He did not say what it was, or how long she had had it, or why she had put it there. He worked down the board with a rag and a pot for a quarter of an hour, and then he washed the rag in the trough and hung it on the rail and put the pot down and stood there four minutes doing nothing at all, looking up the strip road.

That is a man who has kept three names under a ladder for nineteen years without an office asking him for any of them and he is not going to be a man who asks a fourth one what it is for. It is not his business. Keeping it is the whole of the business and asking is not in it.

He has never asked her anything. On the first of a month he told her she had not been eating and he did not ask. On the eighteenth of a month that has gone he told her where there is a loaf on a bench four miles east and he did not say how he knew.

## In Front Of People

Three of them and all three of them saw it.

The man of fifty-five with the paste pot. A carter of thirty-five who had brought the cart up the strip road from the west and who came over to the trough to water a horse while the board was being filled. And the boy of eleven, who was holding the near end of a board and looking at her the way a boy looks at a thing he does not understand and is not going to be told.

There is nobody on nine days of road who will carry anything for her and not one who wants anything out of her.

And a thing that is put down in daylight in front of three people is not put down where nobody will find it.

That is the entire method and it took her thirty-seven days and twenty-two miles and a refusal at that very rail and a blanket she took off a shelf and never put back.

## What Is In It

Eight leaves. Nine hundred and six sheets of blanket went into it and nine of them came out square and two of those nine are a book.

Four sentences, on the second leaf, in her own hand, in soot off a chimney breast, on paper she made with her own two hands in nine days on a table in a nine-foot room, and the hand that wrote them cannot write a false line.

It is the only record in nine hundred years of a man’s keeping that says why anything is kept, and it is not in his book, and it is nine miles west of the only room in the world in which his book is ever opened, and no office has a copy of it, and there is no reader on nine days of road who can be sent for.

She put her hand flat on it for a second and then she took it off, because a thing she reaches cannot be put back and she was not reaching for it. She was only going to feel that it was still there.

## Two Hundred Yards East

She turned round at the ninth hour because the horse had moved.

There was a man two hundred yards east on the strip road with a bag on his shoulder, walking west.

She did not hear him on the flat. There is not one thing within a mile of that post that makes a sound, and she has the ear for it, and he was not there a second before and he was there.

He was walking with his weight on one leg. He had a bag with a book in it and the strap over his right shoulder and he was taking nine steps to her ten.

He came west out of the far ground at the eleventh hour on the twenty-second of a month, which is the day after he wrote one line in a book nine inches thick and shut it, and it is twenty-two miles from that room to that post, and it took him three days.

Three days. She checked it at the ninth hour of the twenty-fifth with her hand flat on a book of her own: twenty-two miles, three days, and one thousand five hundred and ninety-eight less one thousand five hundred and ninety-five is three.

There is a wall in the ground four hundred yards east of a door eleven feet wide and it is still open. There is a table standing in it with nothing on it.

There is a chair against a wall in a nine-foot room that she has not sat in since the thirteenth of a month that has gone, and a window a hundred yards off with no light in it, and a man who is nine hundred years old standing two hundred yards away from her with a bag on his shoulder.

He is nine miles west of the only place in the world where that book is ever opened, and he is never going to open it again there.

That is the price of it and it is on the page and she is not going to describe it twice.

## She Says His Name

She did not go to him and she did not call out and she did not ask him anything.

There are three people at that post and all three of them saw a man come up a strip road from the east, and there are three people at that post and all three of them heard what she said next, in the open, in daylight, at the ninth hour of the twenty-fifth of a month, standing four feet off a ladder with a book of her own on a flat stone under it.

“Shuyin,” Ning Yan said.

She said his name. Once, in the open, in daylight, with three people standing in it, and she said it at a person instead of into a hundred yards of flat ground, which she has done every other time in eight years.

She has said a name out loud four times in her life and this is not one of the times she was ever afraid of doing it.

She did not thank him. He did not ask her for anything, and there is nothing in a name that anybody can be thanked for.

He stopped nine feet off and put his weight on the other leg.

She looked at him.

“I am here,” he said. “Ning Yan.”
