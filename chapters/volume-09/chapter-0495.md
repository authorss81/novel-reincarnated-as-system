# Thirteen Miles East

There were nine men on the frame at the second hour of the sixth of a month and it was not the ninth hour the way the work runs at the lifting floor, because they had started before she could see the ground.

Four hundred years of oak, nine feet wide, and it goes down at the wrong end and has gone down the wrong end for as long as anybody on that ground can remember. A man on each end of it and two on the bar, and one of the nine had a leg wrapped to the ankle and went the length of it thirty times a day.

She stood at the head of the cut with the bar on her shoulder and the wind coming up the cut after her, and nobody on that frame looked up, and she had been the strongest person in every room she had stood in for two years and there was not one person within ninety feet of her who had any way of knowing that.

The board outside the lifting floor nine hundred yards west of a shed at a mill was full, and it had been full for forty days, and one line of the forty-one was that a man of thirty-four wanted a leg dressed who had lost his on the fifteenth of a month two months back.

Four months. The number was hers and she checked it on the walk and she did not like it, because a sheet that sits four months on a leg-man’s bench is a sheet that is not going to be dressed.

## The Man Who Had It

She went along the low side of the cut to where he was sitting on the wall with his leg out in front of him and his bar across his knees, and she had the whole of it ready and did not use any of it, which is what she has done every time since she was seventeen.

“Zheng.”

That was all she said first, and he looked up, and it was only him on the wall.

“You have a sheet,” she said.

He put his hand inside his coat and brought out a fold of paper gone soft at the creases and held it out to her, and he did not ask her how she knew, which told her what she needed to know in about a second and a half.

He said, “A leg man wrote it in the fourth month and I have had it four months and there is not one person on this ground who can read it.”

“You cannot read it.”

“No,” he said. “I have had four months of looking at it.”

## What Was On It

It took her forty seconds, in daylight, sitting on the wall with her boots in the dirt, with a bar laid across her knees and nine men working a frame four hundred years old thirty feet off her.

Three lines of figures. Two of them were ordinary and the third one was a length taken at an angle across a curve, and it had a figure under it that did not agree with the other two by about a ninth of an inch, and the ninth of an inch was the whole of the leg.

She read it three times.

Nobody has asked her to do anything since. That is the sentence she has been carrying since the seventh of a month and she is not going to let a sentence do a job.

“You have asked me to do this leg, so I am going to do it.”

She said it in the ordinary voice, out loud, once, and then she went and got the leg-mould out of a box under the wall, because he had carried it four miles and had it standing against the wall by his own leg, and nobody on that ground had used it since the last man who lost one.

## The Question

She set the mould on the wall and centred it and put her left hand flat on it to hold it, the way she had held a dish of clay four miles from a mill seven days before.

Then she asked it the question, and it was the same question, and it took the mould the way it takes everything.

She did not ask it about the leg. She asked it, “What holds this.”

It went out and took the weight of the mould out of her hand and she felt it go, and her left hand came up off it about an inch and stayed in the air, and three of the nine men on the frame stopped what they were doing and looked over.

That is the thing about it and it has always been the thing about it. The reach does not do the work quietly and it has never once done it quietly, and there is no version of it that a man standing thirty feet off cannot see, and she has never once worked out how to make it smaller.

It took the fitting off the mould in about a minute and it did it in one movement and it did it the way it did everything, which is to say that it was finished.

She put her hand back down on the mould on purpose and it was holding nothing at all.

## Under Her Feet

And nine seconds after the fitting was finished, something came up out of the ground under her feet.

Not under the mould. Under her feet, in the middle of the working part of a cut, where a hundred men have put bar and boot into that ground since before the frame was four hundred years old.

It came the way it came at a shed at a mill two months ago out of a place behind her fourth rib that is open the size of a coin and is not going to close, and it was not a sound and it was not a voice and it did not come out of her hands or her arm or a hold or a count of breath.

It went into the ground and it did not come back.

She stood in the cut with her hand flat on a mould and let it go through her and she did not move and she did not look down, and nine men on a frame thirty feet off her had something else to be doing.

What she knows about it is three things and she put them in that order on the wall at the fourth hour.

It came when a line on a board she did not take got cleared.

It went somewhere she cannot follow.

And the place it comes out of has been open since the eighteenth of a month two months back and he has been in there and has had nothing to spend for a hundred and twenty-nine days and there is nothing in the world she can put in front of that.

She has been carrying an instrument for eight years that is the only one of its kind on nine days of road and it has never once done a thing for her that was not stone, or a chain, or a leg dressed out of a book with a hand in it that four people cannot take.

Whatever that was on the seventeenth of a month on a rail with a full board, she has been refusing it for four months without knowing she was refusing anything.

## The Money

Zheng did the thing at the fifth hour and she had been waiting for it since she sat down on the wall.

He took nine coppers out of the inside of his coat and put them on the wall between them and did not move his hand.

That is a leg. Four months of it and a leg-man’s sheet and a mould carried four miles and a man on a frame four hundred years old nine times a day since the fifteenth of a month, and nine coppers is what a man has.

Her hand cannot take anything that is given to her. That is not a choice and it is not modesty and it is not something she is going to be talked out of in the ordinary voice, and she has never once in eight years got a hand to close on a coin somebody put down in front of her.

So her right hand hung an inch over the wall and did not close, and her left hand was in the strap of the bar because it had been since the seventh mile.

She watched it not close for four seconds and then she said the thing out loud, once, in the ordinary voice, on a wall at the edge of a cut.

“I cannot take it,” she said, “and I could not take it if you put it in my hand, and I am not going to stand here and be looked at while I fail at it.”

“All right,” he said.

And he took the nine coppers off the wall and put them back inside his coat, and he did not argue, and he did not say anything about it being for the sheet, and there was nothing wrong with his face at all, and that was the whole of it and it took eleven seconds.

## The Thing She Said Instead

And then she said a sentence she had worked out forty feet back while he was not looking at her, and she said it once.

“You do not owe me for it,” she said. “You owe me for the four months. There is a leg man in this county who writes a sheet four people cannot take and hands it to a man to carry, and that is the price, and it is not yours and it is not mine.”

He looked at her.

“You can read it,” he said.

“There are about four people in the world who can read it,” she said, “and there is not one of them within nine days of a leg-man’s bench.”

That was true and she had worked it out on the walk, and she left it there.

She stood up off the wall and put her hand flat on the frame for a second because the frame was in front of her and it was four hundred years old and it was the strongest thing within nine hundred yards.

Then she stopped, because she had put her hand on it without asking anything, and the reason she had put her hand on it was that it was there.

She took her hand off.

And the leg was in the mould and the sheet was in her hand and there were four hundred yards of cart track she had walked once already in eight years to learn how to catch a man doing one thing in fifty-three, and the whole of what she had learned had just come out of the ground under her feet and gone somewhere she cannot follow.

She did not stay on the ground. She went up to the top of the cut and lay down on flat ground with the bar across her knees, because the wind comes up a cut at the fourth hour and a cut is the worst place on nine days of road to sleep in.

She was not there in the morning. She was up before the light and she went back down to the cut and Zheng was on the frame already and his leg was wrapped and there was a strip of new wood on a bar that he had not had the day before, and it was on the low side, and it was the right length, and nobody on nine days of road had put it there except her.
