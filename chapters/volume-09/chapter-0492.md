# The First Step Past

On the twelfth of the month she worked the ring for six hours and it came up a finger’s width and stopped, and she had expected that, and she worked it anyway.

Clay out of her own skin, water out of her own skin, pressure from the heel of a hand that is the only hand in the world that has ever been on this ring. She centred it. She worked the inside wall with a thumb that has four fingers’ worth of company and no more. She set the dish on the boards where it goes.

It came up a finger’s width off the boards and stopped.

That is what it did four months back and it is what it did at the sixth hour of the twelfth of this month, and between those two things she has slept about a hundred and forty nights on boards four miles east of a mill whose wheel has not turned since before she came.

She did not try it a second time. That is new as well.

## A Finger’s Width

She sat on the boards afterwards with her hands on her knees and her breath at four counts and nine hours of daylight gone, and she did not feel the thing she has felt every time she has done that work in two years, which is the settling of her into the argument.

There is no argument left. She has known for two months exactly what a stage is and what she does not have, and the knowing stopped costing her anything four months ago, and what replaced it on the twelfth of this month was a thing she had not expected.

She did not want a second pair of hands.

That is the whole of it and she said it out loud once on the boards at the seventh hour, because she says everything out loud exactly once.

She has wanted a second pair of hands since she was fourteen. She wanted one in a shed at a mill with a ring coming up and a woman of sixty-one holding a rim with two hands, and she wanted one on a floor where four people cannot take a sheet, and she wanted one so badly for two years that she put a refusal into the best book on nine days of road rather than take a room with a man qualified in it.

She did not want one on the twelfth of this month. She had eaten nothing since the seventh and she had worked a ring for six hours with one arm at half and she was tired in a way she had not been since she was fifteen.

What she wanted was somebody to have asked.

## What A Floor Measures By

She went down to the rail at the sixth hour on the thirteenth because she wanted to see forty-one lines again and she did not need to, and she stood there and read them and took none.

That is the whole of what a floor has for her, and she looked at it properly for the first time.

Every line on that board is a weight or a length. Loads by the load. Stone by the yard. A chain by the oiling. Nine men and four days. A leg dressed on a man of thirty-four.

There is no line on it, and there has not been for two years, and there is not going to be after the nineteenth either, and the reason is not that nobody wants her.

The reason is that there is nothing about her to put on a line.

She cannot be paid and she cannot be given anything and she cannot write a false line, and a thing she reaches cannot be put back, and there is nothing above her in the world and the door is a piece of paper she has had against her ribs for four years.

Past the ceiling there is no work any floor on nine days of road can put on a board for a person. Every floor in the world measures a person by what comes out of the ground or by the length of it, and she is not either of those any more. She is a person who can read forty-one lines twice and take none, and not one floor in the world has a column shaped like that.

She said that out loud at a rail on the thirteenth of a month, once, in the ordinary voice, with four people on a floor and none of them within thirty feet.

## The Second Pair Of Hands

She worked out the rest of it on the fourteenth, at the shed, on the boards, in about an hour.

A stage is three things. That is the shape of it and it has been the shape of it since a shed in a winter a long way from here: a room, a second pair of hands, and a number of hours.

She can give it the room. She has been in one for two years and has never once been asked to leave it and nobody within eleven miles has any claim on it.

She can give it the hours. She has two years of them and they are not going to be taken off her, because there is nothing on this ground that wants any of them, which she has been treating as a wound for four months and which is, for this, the exact thing she needs.

She cannot give it a second pair of hands.

And then she stopped, because she had got to the end of the sentence she had started, and she sat there and looked at the end of it for nine minutes.

There is no second pair of hands in this shed.

## The One Question

What there is, is one person, who is her, who has four fingers and a thumb on the right hand and an arm that is open half the time and eight of eleven holds and breath at four counts everywhere, including inside a loaded reach, and who has been the second pair of hands in every single thing she has ever built for eight years.

She has been standing next to her own hands for eight years and calling them one pair.

That is the finding on the fourteenth of a month at the fourth hour of the afternoon, and it is the first one she has had in eight years that she could not have had in any other place, and it is the reason she is not going to ask anybody for anything on the nineteenth.

And it is where the instrument comes in, and she came to it nine seconds later, and it came out of her flat.

Her reach answers a question. That is the whole rule and it has been the whole rule since she was fourteen in a room with a man of thirty in it, and she has used it four hundred times, and every single one of the four hundred was somebody else’s question about stone or a chain or a leg.

She has never once asked it a question about herself.

Not once in eight years. Nine thousand days. Four hundred answers, all of them about work, and not one of them about her own body, and she never had a reason until the fourteenth of a month with a piece of paper against her ribs that says a door opens for the person it is about and for nobody else.

## The Nineteenth, Fourth Hour

So here is what she is going to do on the nineteenth of the month, and she put it at the bottom of the housekeeping on the board and not anywhere else.

She is going to get up at the fourth hour with nothing in her, because there has been no bread on that ground for a month and she is going to take a day of not eating in the ten days before it on purpose, because she is not going to do this with something in her hand.

She is going to bar the door with the bar.

She is going to work the clay and put the dish down and put her own two hands on it, which is not a second pair of hands, and she is going to do that in a room with nobody qualified in it.

And then she is going to ask it one question.

She does not know what it is yet. She has been turning that over for two days and there are nine things it could be, and she is not going to choose one of them out loud, because the last thing anybody on nine days of road has ever done for her is choose a thing for her in a room.

What she does know, and wrote on the board with the bar’s point on the fourteenth of a month, is this:

It has never once been asked about her.

Then she scraped that line off, because it was the same mistake as the days, and she wrote it again underneath in the housekeeping where it is a number and not a wish.

Bread: none. Water: trough road, once in two days. Ring: a finger’s width. Holds: eight of eleven. Breath: four.

And under all of it, in the same hand, the last line of the fourteenth of a month:

Five days.

## The Fifteenth

She went down the trough road on the fifteenth at the fifth hour and back at the ninth with a full skin and forty goats that did not look at her, and on the way back she stopped a mile short of the shed because her right arm went open and it did not close when she asked it to.

That is four seconds in the middle of a made road.

She stood there until it came and then she walked the last mile, and she worked out on the way what the four seconds meant and what it had always meant, and it is that the arm has been open half the time for two years and she has never once gone looking for the half of the time it is shut.

There is a thing in eight years that will only do what she asks, and she has used it four hundred times for other people, and she has never once asked it about the arm or the four fingers or the eight holds or the fourth count she has to go and get at night.

She was not hungry and it had stopped being done to her and she was thinner than she was in a month.

Four days.

She lay on the boards on the fifteenth at the tenth hour and put her left hand flat over the paper against her ribs and worked it out for the last time, and then she went to sleep, and she did not say his name out loud once in five days, and nothing came back, and she had stopped waiting on the fourteenth.
