# Two Places A Name Can Go

She spent the thirteenth, fourteenth and fifteenth of a month at an opening in a wall with two feet of plank table in it, and on the fifteenth she asked him what happens if she says neither, and he told her in four sentences, and it is the answer to every question she had come out of the far ground with.

## The Thirteen Days

The table was in the opening. The book was on it from the ninth hour to the eleventh hour and he came out at the ninth and went back at the eleventh, three days running, and he did not repeat himself and he did not sit down.

She stood on her side of the jamb with her left hand on the stone and a bar of a wall across the back of her room and nine days left on a thing she had started on the twenty-second of a month that has gone.

Three days of the most ordinary thing anybody has ever done to her.

## What Happens If She Says Neither

She asked it at the ninth hour of the fifteenth, in the ordinary voice, and she had it ready and it was eleven words.

“What happens if I do not choose either word.”

He put his hand flat on the open page, which she noticed and which meant nothing at all, and he looked up at her.

“You are written down,” he said.

That is the first one.

“The rule has two words in it and it has not had a third one in longer than you have been alive,” he said.

That is the second one.

“I have outlived having a preference,” he said.

That is the third one, and it is the only sentence of the six he has said since the seventh that she has had to sit down for.

“Either one. I will take either one and I will not come back to you about it,” he said.

And that is all he said and he shut the book.

## The Only Thing She Has A Say In

She worked it all out on the sixteenth at the fourth hour, lying on the boards, and it took her forty minutes, and it is the plainest possible arithmetic and it is the whole of what she has.

She is entered either way.

That is not a threat and it is not a way of putting pressure on her. It is a rule older than she is, and it does not have a third place in it, and it does not care in the least what she says about it.

The one thing she has is the word.

Not whether, and not when, and not whether she stands there while it happens. The word. Nine hundred years of a man writing people down and the only part of it that has ever belonged to a person is which of two nouns gets put at the end of their line.

She said that out loud once, at the ninth hour of the sixteenth, to the boards of a nine-foot room, and then she waited nine minutes for it to be worse and it did not get worse.

## The Page She Gave Him

That is the second thing and she got it out on the sixteenth at the second hour, and it is the only thing in three days that came as a surprise.

“The page I gave you on the third,” she said. “The one you copied out into your own hand. Is that the line.”

“No,” he said.

She had not thought that it was and she was not sure afterwards whether she had or not.

“It is a page about you,” he said. “It is not the line. The line is a line, and it has a date on it and one word on it, and it is not what you wrote.”

“And it stays.”

“It stays,” he said. “Whatever word I use, that page is in the book, and it was in the book before you gave it to me and it will be in the book after.”

That is all he said about it and it took under thirty seconds and it was the plainest he has ever been with her.

## The Question About The Door

She asked it on the sixteenth at the ninth hour and it is the only question she asked him in three days that was not about the book, and she asked it because on the fourth hour of the ninth she had come out of that opening and looked east at a lit window and the question had been standing in the room with her for nine days.

She put it in the ordinary voice and she did not soften it.

“What happens to the one on the wrong side of a wall you opened from the inside.”

He was quiet for four seconds.

“The one who opened it stays on the wrong side of it,” he said.

One sentence. That is all he gave her and he gave it to her the way he writes a date, and she did not get a reason and she did not get an exception and she did not get nine hundred years of it.

She stood there and looked at him for nine seconds, and that is everything she got for the only question she asked him in three days that was not about the book, and she had carried it eleven miles of made road in her head and she left it there, and she is not going to ask it a second time.

## What She Did Not Like About It

She did not like it for two reasons and they are both about him and neither of them is about the wall.

The first is that he said it as a rule.

Not as a thing that happened to them on the eighth of a month in a nine-foot room with the latch down. As a rule, in the present tense, about whoever, the way he would say it about a person he had never met in the ninth hundred year of it.

The second is worse and she only got it on the morning of the seventeenth and it is what she carries out of here.

It is correct.

## What She Believes And What She Is Not Going To Say

She believes him about the sixth sentence and she has believed him since about the eleventh, and she is not going to be arguing with him about it and she is not going to tell him she is.

It is a small thing to believe a man is right about. It is not a small thing to say so out loud to a man who has said nothing at all.

He has not asked her whether she agrees. He asked her nothing in three days. He put five sentences about a record and one sentence about being kept on a table and four sentences a third place that does not exist, and he has not once put the question back to her.

So she is going to carry two things at once for the rest of this and she has said them both out loud, once, at the fourth hour of the seventeenth, standing in the opening with the jamb cold under her hand, and she is not going to put them down.

She believes a person on his book is better kept than a person off it.

And she is not going to say so to him.

Those are two separate sentences and they are not the same one and the distance between them is nine hundred years and four fingers and a thumb.

## The Two Words And What She Does Not Know

She went over both of them for two days and she did not get anywhere, and that is the finding.

Two nouns. One of them is what he said to her in the ordinary voice on the tenth of a month, about a person on his book and a person off it.

The other one she has never had explained to her by anybody, and she asked him for it at the ninth hour of the seventeenth, and he said it has two words in it and that is the end of the words, and he was right about that too and she hated it.

So here is everything she knows about the two places a name can go, on the seventeenth of a month, at the fourth hour, sitting on the boards with the wind coming off the flat.

She does not know what either one of them does to a person.

Not one. In eight years not one person has ever sat her down and said what being entered is, and she has spent four months finding out what a single thing she cannot take does, and she found that out from a woman behind a counter in nine words.

She is going to have to say one of the two words out loud in daylight in front of a man who will write it down, and she does not know what either one does, and there is nobody on nine days of road she can ask.

And she cannot ask him, because asking him is the only move she has left and she would have to spend it on the wrong question.

## The End Of The Three Days

He came out at the ninth hour on the seventeenth with the book under his arm and did not put it on the table.

He stood on his own side of the jamb for a minute with his weight on one leg and he looked at a wall set in the ground that runs north and south further than the flat goes, and then he looked at her.

“I am going to say the rest of it out loud tomorrow,” he said. “I have not said it out loud to anybody in a long time and I am not going to say it to you today.”

“All right,” Ning Yan said.

He went back through the opening with the book.

She stood there in the wind and worked out that she had been given a day, and that she had not asked for one, and that a man of nine hundred years who keeps no staff and holds no court and has asked her nothing about himself for eight years had just told her that there was going to be a tomorrow with the light on it.

## The Bag On The Table

At the fourth hour on the eighteenth she went out and the table was in the opening and the book was not on it.

The bag was on it.

He had left it, and it was on her side, and the strap and the flap of it were undone and there was a brush lying on the open pages of a book that was not the one he writes names in — it was a thin one, the shape of a pouch, with a brass ferrule and a brush as long as her hand.

He had left it there and gone back.

A brush in a nine-hundred-year-old hand was going to be on a table in a hole in a wall at the ninth hour of the day after tomorrow, in daylight, with the book open, and there was nothing in nine days of road that would move him and nothing in nine hundred years that would make him wait.
