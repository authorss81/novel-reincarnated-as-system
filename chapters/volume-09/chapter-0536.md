# One Line And A Date

He wrote it on the twenty-second of a month at the ninth hour in daylight, with a brush nine hundred years old, on a page of a book nine inches thick, in front of her, and it took him forty seconds and there was no speech at all.

## What Came Out Of The Hut

He came through the opening at the ninth hour with the book under his arm and the bag on his shoulder and he set the book down on two feet of table and did not open it at first.

Her book was where she had squared it up on the twentieth. He looked at it for a second and a half and then he moved it, the way a person moves a thing off a space to work on it, and put it on the dirt on her side, and she did not pick it up.

That is everything that happened to the book of her own and it is nine seconds and she has thought of it more since than of the other thing.

Then he opened the book and found the page and put the brush on the right of it.

## One Line And A Date

He did not look up while he wrote it and he did not read it out and he did not say the word first.

He put the date at the top, the way he has been putting the date at the top since before she was born, and then he stopped for a second, and then he wrote one word underneath it in a hand that four men in six weeks could not get a stone over a painted mark for.

Four letters. It took him four seconds.

Lost.

That is the entry and there is one line of it and there is a date, and she was standing four feet away from it with her hands at her sides and she saw every letter of it go onto the page.

## He Shuts The Book

He dried the brush on the thin book in the bag, nine seconds, and he closed the big one and put the strap over it and did the flap, and that is the sound he makes and she has known that sound since the ninth hour of the eighth of a month.

He put it under his arm.

“You are on it,” he said. “The line is in and it stays in and it does not come out. That is not a thing I have to want.”

“All right,” Ning Yan said.

That is everything that was said of the entry itself and it is twenty-seven words and two words and neither of them touches the word on the page.

## What It Is To Be Written Down As Lost

Here is the thing she could not get past for two days afterwards, and it is the only part of this she is going to be angry at, and she is not going to be angry for long.

She is alive.

She is standing in daylight four feet from an opening in a wall with a bar of her own on the ground behind her and a book in her hand and her heart going and the fourth count available to her in four seconds, and she is nineteen years old.

And the word on the page is the word for a person the bound took.

He is not saying she is dead. He is not saying she has gone anywhere. He is writing down, in a book that has nine hundred years in it, the plain fact that a person got to the top of a realm with nothing to take them past it, and that the thing at the top of it took her, and that this is what it wrote.

Nobody was harmed. Nobody threatened her. Nothing in a nine-foot room or on a hundred yards of flat ground could be called an attack by anybody who was standing where she was standing.

A name went in. She is here.

Those two things were in the same room at the same time and nothing in nine hundred years says they cannot both be true, and she is going to have to carry them both, and she is not going to be able to put them down on either side.

## What It Cost Him

Nothing that he has not paid before.

That is the finding and she got it on the evening of the twenty-second standing at the rail outside, and she checked it, and it held.

He does not prefer. He said so on the fifteenth and he was not lying then and he was not lying today. He took the one she gave him. It cost him a line and forty seconds and it cost him nothing else at all, and the whole of what he did to her across fifteen days cost him a table, a bag, fifteen days of carrying a book across a hundred yards of flat, and four words out of the dark on the sixteenth of another month.

She had gone at it from every side for two days on the nineteenth and the twenty-second, looking for the bill, and the bill was on her side of the opening and not on his, and that is not a way of writing that a man has won.

## What She Thinks Lost Means

She went at that on the twenty-third as well, lying on the boards, for an hour.

If lost means gone, then a name written as lost is a name the book has stopped waiting for. If it means taken, it says what the rule says it says and no more. If it means kept out of the way, then she has refused the better one and taken this one on a word she invented at four hours in the morning.

She got to the end of the hour and she had three readings and no way of telling them apart, and there is not one person on nine days of road she can ask, and the one man who could answer has just written the word down and gone.

So it goes with her. Whatever it is, it goes nine miles west with a book in her coat, and she will find out in four months by watching whether anything comes for her.

## He Walks Her To The Door

That is the last of it and it is not a speech and there was no one else there.

He picked the brush up at the ninth hour and he would not leave it on the table, and he put it in the bag and put the strap over the bag, and then he said one ordinary thing.

“Twenty-two days,” he said.

She did not ask him what he meant and she has not gone over it since, and it is the only thing he said on the twenty-second that she has not been able to place anywhere in nine hundred years.

Then she went west.

“The chimney draws,” he said.

She said nothing back. There is no arrangement in the world in which a woman answers a man who has told her a thing about a chimney, and she is not going to be the one who makes it, and she has thought of that sentence more than anything else he said in fifteen days.

The opening is four feet wide with a jamb on each side and the room is nine feet by eleven with the wall along its back and its door on the west side, which is eleven feet from the jamb.

He came with her as far as the jamb.

That is the whole of it. He came with her as far as a man can come who opened a wall out of a room and cannot go back through it, and he stood on his own side of the stone with his bag on his shoulder and the brush in it and his weight on one leg, and he stopped there.

She went the last eleven feet on her own.

She has been through that opening eleven times in three days, at no cost, with no bar on it. She has never once been stopped by it, and she has never once stopped herself at it.

She stopped at it four feet from the jamb on the twenty-second of a month at the ninth hour in daylight and she had to stop, and a man who was walking beside her four seconds earlier was not able to take the last eleven feet with her.

She shut the door and waited for the latch.

It is the only piece of furniture in the world she has any feeling for and she shut it on a man standing in daylight eleven feet away who cannot come in and who is not going to be let in, because there is nobody to let him in.

## How The Two Of Them Ended It

He did not say anything that wanted thanking and she did not say anything back.

That is how the last fifteen days of the most consequential fifteen days of her life ended, at a door, in daylight, between a woman and a man of nine hundred years who had said thirty-two words of it.

She does not think of it as a thing either of them did wrong. She put him on the wrong side of a wall. She keeps a door shut. Neither of those is a mistake and both of them are permanent, and that is everything that happened between them and it took fifteen days.

## The Two Days After

Nothing came to the opening on the twenty-third or the twenty-fourth.

She was on a made road for both of them and she did not come back. The table stood in it with nothing on it from the fourth hour to the eleventh hour and then past the eleventh hour and then past the dark, and the dirt on both sides of it was flat where his boots had been for fifteen days.

On the twenty-third at the ninth hour there was no light in the window of the hut a hundred yards east.

On the twenty-fourth at the fourth hour there was not either. She was at a crossing eleven miles off it with her right arm open and she did not know that and she has not asked and she is not going to.

## What She Is Left Holding

She picked her book up off the dirt at the fourth hour of the twenty-third.

She had to open the door to get to it, because it was two feet from the jamb on her side and she had put it down to give him the space to write.

Eight leaves. A date and a place on the first one. Four sentences on the second one. Six empty leaves, which are hers and which nobody else is ever going to fill.

It went inside her coat, flat, against the open place low behind her fourth rib, on the other side of a sheet of paper that has been there four years and that she opened with her own two hands and has never once called anything.

And there is nine days of road between her and a post on a strip road with a flat stone under a ladder on it, and a man with a paste pot has kept three names there for nineteen years without being asked, and she has three days to walk it.

She does not know what *lost* does. Nobody on nine days of road knows what either word does and the only person who has ever known is a hundred yards east with a brush in a bag and a table in a hole in a wall.

She is going to find out by walking.
