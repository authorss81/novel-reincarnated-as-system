# The Ground Finds Out

It took eleven days and it happened the way weather happens, and she was a long way inside it before she worked out that it had finished.

## Coming West

She came west on the seventeenth and the eighteenth, on her own two feet, with a bar at the light end and a skin and no bread.

She slept one night in a ditch and she slept one night under the cutting floor at the shed at the end of the lane, four feet high where a man can stand, on a stone floor, and she was not careful about either of them.

That is the first thing that changed and she did not notice it for nine days.

The carrier came through at the usual time on the nineteenth with four boards on a bar.

She was at the kerb filling the skin.

He did not stop and he did not pull up and he did not go past her at thirty feet either.

He came down off the lane to ten feet, the way a man comes past a horse on a narrow road, and he said good to her at ten feet and went on.

That is the whole of the nineteenth and it is the first time in forty days that a person on nine days of road has done an ordinary thing to her.

## The Counter

The woman at the store five miles west of a mill put a loaf on the counter and did not take it off again.

She put it on the wood at the ninth hour on the twentieth and she said good and she said, “You are thinner,” and she pushed it four inches towards her with two fingers and left it there.

“You are not going to take it,” the woman said. “I know that. I have known it since the twenty-third of a month that has gone and I did not know it until I said so, and I have thought about it.”

“All right,” Ning Yan said.

“I am not going to do it again and I am not going to stop doing it,” the woman said. “That is not a favour and it is not a question. I am going to put it there and you are going to walk out of my store, and I have put that on my own counter and I have been putting bread on this counter since I was twenty-three.”

And there is a loaf on a shelf behind her that a man who stopped coming has not paid for and has not come for and that she has never given away to anybody and does not give away now, and she is the only woman within nine days of road who has ever been careful with Ning Yan for no reason at all.

## The Frame Room

She went to the cut at the fourth hour of the twenty-first because there was work there and because she had been going there for two months and because it was the only thing on nine days of road that had ever fed her.

The board outside the room with the six day books had the space on it filled in. A man’s name was under it in a hand she could take, in a day with a figure against it, and the space was no longer a space.

She stood in the road and read it and then she read the whole board down, forty-one lines, and it took her a quarter of an hour.

Nobody was on it. Not one line out of forty-one had her name on it in any of them, and she had carried a stone up past a painted mark on the twelfth of a month with four men watching, and there is no line on that board for a woman of eighteen who is the strongest person within nine hundred yards.

And the woman of forty-one inside wrote nine names in six books that day and not one of them asked anybody anything.

## The West Door

The woman of fifty was on the step of the shed at the end of the lane at the seventh hour on the twenty-second and she had not sent for Ning Yan and she did not come down off the step.

“You have been west twice and east twice in a month,” the woman said. “The east is not mine and I have never once pretended it was.”

“All right,” Ning Yan said.

“I am going to say the other thing once and then I am going to stop,” the woman said. “Whatever it is that came up the lane to you in the fifth month, it has finished. Whatever came for you has got what it came for or it has not, and either way it is not in my lane any more, and there is a frame room with a bed in it and a door on the west side with a bolt on it.”

“You have offered it twice,” Ning Yan said.

“I have offered it twice,” the woman said, “and I am offering it a third time, and I am not asking, and I am not going to ask, and I would like you to understand that a woman of fifty on a lane like this does not make a habit of saying a thing three times to one person.”

And she went inside and shut the door, which she has never done before.

## The Twenty-Third

The cart that comes down that lane six times a day went past at the fourth hour on the twenty-third with the man on it who is twenty-two, and he did not pull up.

He did not pull up, and he did not go past at the ordinary speed either. He slowed to a walk for about nine seconds alongside her and then took it again and went by, and he said good to her as he went by, the way he has said good to her four hundred times in two years.

She stood in the road and watched the back of a cart go four hundred yards and she was not disappointed and she was not relieved and she found that she had nothing to feel about it at all, which is the second strangest thing that has happened to her in a month.

Nine days earlier he pulled his horse up two hundred yards short of her and then went on, and the whole of what she had done for forty days was decided by that, and she had been carrying it as a thing that had been taken off her.

Now there is nothing on him either.

He is a man on a cart. Whatever had come for her has finished, or has not, and it makes no difference to a man on a cart at the fourth hour on the twenty-third, and he is not going to ask and she is not going to tell.

## Being Believed

Here is the finding, and she worked it out on flat ground half a mile west of the store at the second hour of the twenty-fourth and it took eleven minutes and she said it out loud once, in the ordinary voice, sitting on a stone with nothing within nine hundred yards.

The misreading has ended.

Not because anybody was corrected. She has not corrected one word of it to one living person in two months and she was not going to start on the twenty-fourth, and the page is a shed at the end of a lane in a book nine hundred years old and there is nowhere on nine days of road she can put it.

It has ended because the truth turned out to look exactly like the mistake.

Something came for a girl with a bar. Something came up the lane to her and it has been seen by eleven people in nine days of road over two months, and it is standing in the ground at the end of eleven miles of made road past a bar on two posts, and a man is behind it with a light on in his window and he is not coming back.

They were not careful about a rumour. They were careful about a fact.

## The Strangest Thing

And here is the part she has not been able to put down since the twenty-fourth, and it is the strangest thing that has happened to her in a year.

She has been entered four times in eight years by other people’s hands. She has been read to. She has been wanted, kept, rated, refused and read back. She has stood in front of a man behind a counter and said no to him in a room with the shutters up, and it went into the best book on nine days of road and the word good went with it.

In eight years nobody has ever simply believed a thing about her.

Eleven people on nine days of road believed a thing about her for two months and it was not true, and not one of them asked her for anything, and not one of them wanted anything out of her, and not one of them wrote her down.

They were only careful. And being careful with a person costs that person nothing at all, which is the one thing in this world she has never had and has never once been able to pay for.

And it was about a thing that had not happened.

## The Only Thing Left

So it is finished, and she is nineteen, and she is past the ceiling by two stages, and she has no wages and has had none for four months, and there is no bread in her since the second of a month.

And on the twenty-eighth of the month she walked the lane at the fourth hour from the shed at the end of it down past the cut and the frame and on to the store road, in daylight, with nothing behind her.

It took an hour and a quarter and it was the same as it has always been.

Every yard of it was the same yard.

She stopped at the turn where the lane meets the store road and she stood there and she counted it, and it is the last thing on nine days of road that is hers, and she has known that since the twenty-fourth of a month and she had been walking on it every day since without looking at it.
