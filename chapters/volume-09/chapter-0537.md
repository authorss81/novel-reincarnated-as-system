# The Road That Does Not Go Back

She went west out of the far ground on the twenty-third of a month at the fourth hour with a bar on her shoulder and a book inside her coat, and she did not look at a window with no light in it, and she walked twenty-two miles in two days and none of them went anywhere she had been.

## Out Of The Far Ground

The table was still standing in the opening with nothing on it.

She went through the opening at the fourth hour and she did not go back through it, and she did not put her hand on the jamb, and she wants that written down because the jamb was cool and she has been putting her hand on it at that hour for twenty-six days.

Four hundred yards east of the wall there is a bar on two posts with nothing on it. She went past it on the outside.

She has not been through that bar since the second hour of the eighth of the month beginning 1,514, and she has gone past it in one direction or the other eleven times since, and she has never once wanted to go through it, and the reason she has never once wanted to is not in her anywhere.

## The Gate

The boy of fifteen was on the chain at the sixth hour and he put it down and asked the question.

“No,” Ning Yan said.

“All right,” the boy said, and put the chain up for a cart behind her.

She has been through that gate nine times in two months and it is the same every time and it will be the same next time.

That is everything at the end of eleven miles of made road for a man of fifteen and a cup on a wire, and it took nine seconds.

## Eleven Miles

She did eleven miles of made road between the sixth hour of the twenty-third and the fourth hour of the twenty-fourth, and she did it on four fingers and a thumb with a bar on her shoulder, and she slept one night in the ditch at the sixth mile with the bag under her head.

She has not eaten since the sixteenth of this month and she is not hungry and that is finished, and she is thinner than she was on the eighth of one that has gone, and she has been thinner every day since.

At the ninth hour on the twenty-third her right arm went open at the eight-mile mark and it stayed open. It shut at the second hour of the twenty-fourth, which is four days in five, and she has not kept a count of it in a month and she did not start one on the road.

Her fourth count on flat made ground at the sixth hour went for four seconds, and she stopped, and it came back in four, and she walked on.

There were four carts going east on that road in two days and one going west and none of them stopped and one of them had a man in it who has said good to her four hundred times in two years.

## The Crossing And The Lane

She was at the crossing at the fourth hour of the twenty-fourth and she went into the lane and up it without stopping anywhere.

The woman of fifty was on the step of the shed at the end of the lane at the seventh hour and did not come down and did not say anything, and Ning Yan went past her at nine feet.

The offer stands. It has stood since the second month of a year she could name and it does not need an answer and it will not get one, and the whole of what she has to say is that she is sorry, and she did not say so and she did not slow down.

At the cut there was a stone on the frame.

It was at the eighth foot, above the painted mark, and it had been put down square by a person who is not here and it was the size of a loaf, and nobody was there and there is no line on any board within nine hundred yards that says somebody was asked for it.

She stood in front of the frame for four seconds and then she went on.

The board outside the room with the six day books had thirty-nine lines on it and she read the whole thing down standing up in nine seconds.

There is nothing on it with her name on it and there has been nothing on it with her name on it for three months, and she carried a stone up past a painted mark on the twelfth of a month with four men watching.

## Two Miles

The bench is two feet off the store road and it is four feet long and there is a store behind it with a shuttered window.

She came to it at the tenth hour on the twenty-fourth and she did not stop.

That is all of it and she is not going to make anything of it and she is not going to be a person who looks at a bench on the way to something. She knows which board in it split in the winter. She knows which side of it she has always stood on. She knows all of that and none of it was going anywhere and she walked past it at the rate a person walks.

She did not go into the store. The loaf on the shelf behind the counter that a man who stopped coming has not paid for is not hers and has never been hers and is not going to be hers, and the woman at the counter shut her own door once in the ninth month of a year she could name and has not asked her for anything since.

The lane is two miles from that bench to the turn.

She walked it at the tenth hour and the eleventh hour of the twenty-fourth and it took her exactly as long as it has always taken her, and there was nothing in it.

## The Turn

The turn is where the lane meets the store road and it is a quarter of a mile from the bench.

She stood at it at the eleventh hour of the twenty-fourth with the bar at the light end and the bag against her ribs and eight days of nothing in her since the sixteenth of this month.

It is a quarter of a mile of flat on a made road where the clay came up out of a dish on the eighteenth of a month that has gone and a thing came out of the place low behind her fourth rib and said one sentence into the ground.

There is a road going west off it here. It is nine miles to a post with a board on it and a ladder against the side of it and a trough at the foot, and there is a flat stone under the bottom of that ladder with a book on it.

She has been there twice in two years. She asked a man with a paste pot for a sheet of paper there on the first of a month and he said no, and she walked back twenty-two miles with nothing in her hands, and she has a book in her coat that she made out of a blanket.

## The Night On The Strip Road

She was four miles short of the post at the fourth hour of the twenty-fifth and she sat down on the low side of the road because the fourth count had gone at about the eighth mile and she was not going to spend it twice in a mile.

It is not hunger. It is a body at nine days without bread and four fingers and a thumb carrying a bar nine feet nine for two days, and it does not feel like anything and it stops being able to do a thing, and that is the whole of it.

Her right arm went open again at the seventh mile and she put it into the strap of the bar for an hour, the way she has done on a cart track for two years, and the strap is bound out of her own coat lining and there is not four inches left in the lining to give it.

She counted the miles by the bar. That is the method and it is a bad one and it is the only one she has got in the dark on a road with nothing on it, and by it the post is four miles and a bit west of where she was sitting, and a board gets filled off a cart every nine or ten days and it has been two, and there is not going to be one on it at the ninth hour.

## What She Is Carrying

Nine days is what she has been without anything to eat and four days in five is the rate of the arm and four seconds is the fourth count on flat ground and nine is the fourth count inside a loaded reach, and she is nineteen, and the figures are the method and the figures are the whole of what she has to work with.

A bar. A skin. A bag with a dish and a lump of clay in it and a ring a finger’s width.

A sheet of paper against her ribs that she opened with her own two hands in the fourth month of a year she could name and has never once called anything.

And a book the size of a closed hand, eight leaves, a date and a place on the first, four sentences on the second, six empty ones that are hers.

## Nineteen And Not Weeping

She did not weep about a road and she is not going to.

That is not courage and she has said so out loud twice in four months and she is not going to say it a third time for a lane.

She is nineteen years old and she is past the end of a realm by three stages and she has no wages and no shed and no room that is hers and a name written in a book nine hundred years old with one word at the end of it that she does not know the meaning of.

And she has not wept over any of it since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 at the fifth hour on a flat at a turn, sitting with a dish beside her, listening for a second sentence that did not come.

A person who has not wept over a stage and is not going to weep over a road has made a decision on what she is going to spend the next four months on, and it is not going to be this.

## Nine Miles In Front Of Her

She stood at the turn for a quarter of an hour and then she went west.

Nine miles of strip road on the twenty-fourth night and the twenty-fifth morning, on a bar, at nine days without bread, with a book of her own inside her coat against the open place low behind her fourth rib.

Behind her, four hundred yards east, there is a table standing in a hole in a wall with nothing on it and a door shut on eleven feet of boards and a window a hundred yards off with no light in it, dark now for the second night.

She did not look at it.

There is a man who left it, and he is not in it, and there are three names in a flat book under a ladder nine miles west that no office has ever sent him a thing to do with, and there are four sentences in a book of her own that a man of nine hundred years heard her read out loud at an opening in a wall four days ago.

Nine miles. She went into the dark for it at the eleventh hour of the twenty-fourth with four fingers and a thumb and nothing else in the world.
