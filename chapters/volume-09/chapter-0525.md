# The Chair She Will Not Sit In

She started on the twenty-second of a month with a bar of a wall across the back of a nine-foot room and thirty-three days left in it, and she was going to work out how to be worth the price of one, and it took her eleven days.

## The Two Times

She has said his name twice in forty days.

On the twelfth of a month at the second hour she stood in an opening in the wall and said it into a hundred yards of flat ground and waited nine hours and then said it again, which is the once in eight years she has said a name twice, and there was a light on in a window a hundred yards off and it did not change.

On the sixteenth, at the ninth hour, she said a different sentence out loud at a gate in daylight in front of three people, and four words came back out of the dark from seven hundred yards east nine hours later.

That is the whole of forty days of it.

She is not going to say it a third time. She worked that out at the third hour of the twenty-second and it did not take long, because there is no arrangement in the world that carries a sentence the last hundred yards and she went over all of them on her own two feet in nine minutes and there was not one.

## What She Wants Is Not An Answer

Here is the finding, and she got to it on the twenty-third at the seventh hour after a day of trying and it took her four sentences.

She does not want an answer out of him.

She has wanted one for eight months and she has wanted it in a way that has nothing to do with curiosity, and she gave it up somewhere between the twelfth and the twenty-second without noticing the day.

What she wants is to be asked for something. Once. By somebody who needs her to do it.

That is a different thing and it is smaller and it is the only one of the two that has ever been in her life at all. She has been entered four times and been rated and kept and refused and read back and entered again, and every one of those was a person doing something to her that she had not asked for, and she has never once in nineteen years been told what was wanted.

## The Strongest Person In A Nine-Foot Room

She put it out loud once, standing up, at the ninth hour of the twenty-third, in the ordinary voice, and it is worth putting down because it is the shape of the whole of the thirty-seven days.

She is the strongest person in this room. She is very likely the strongest person within nine hundred yards of it and there is a bar on her shoulder that is nine feet nine and she walked eleven miles of made road on it in five days in the fourth month.

And there is nothing in this room that needs any of it.

That is not a complaint and she is not going to make it one. There is no plank to lift and no rail to carry and no board with forty-one lines on it anywhere within four hundred yards and nobody in the world is standing in front of her needing a stone taken up a stair. She has been the answer to a question four hundred times and she has not been the answer to one since the twelfth of a month that has gone.

So she can do anything at all in this room and none of it is any use, and she is the only person in it, and the room is nine feet by eleven, and there is no arrangement anywhere on this ground that will put a second person in it.

That is the specific shape of it and she is not going to put it more plainly than that, and the reason she cannot is that a plain sentence would be about wanting company, and she is nineteen and she has been on her own since she was eleven and she is not going to be a person who says that out loud to a wall.

## The Chair

She had sat in the chair once, at the fourth hour of the thirteenth, for twenty minutes, with nothing else to do with her body, and she had got up afterwards and pushed it back against the wall and squared it the way a person squares a thing.

She did not sit in it again.

Not out of feeling. She has never had anything to feel for a chair and she is not going to start on the twenty-third of a month.

The reason is that sitting in it is the first thing she would do in a room she was in, and there is no second thing after it. A person sits down, and then the chair is a chair somebody sat in, and the room is a room somebody was in, and there is a difference between a room you are at and a room you are in and it has to start somewhere and that is the only place it can start.

So the chair stands against the wall nine feet from a bed frame with nothing on it, squared, against the wall where it was.

## What Is On The Table

There is a book on the table.

She had gone past it sixteen nights without touching it and she went near it on the twenty-first out of nothing more than having been in the room too long, and she opened it once and shut it and put it down square where it had been, and she has not touched it since.

It is a book with nothing written in it. Not one leaf has ink on it. Not one leaf has been written on and rubbed out. The leaves are clean to their edges and the stitching is old and the cover is plain boards with no name on the spine and no number and nothing whatever written on the front of it.

It is the exact size of the thing she needs and not one inch of it is any good to her.

## Why She Did Not Take It

She wants this written down carefully because it is everything she has for thirty-seven days and it is going to look, to anybody reading, like a sentimental hesitation and it was not one.

It is not that she cannot pick it up.

She could pick it up with two hands. She could put it inside her coat against the place low behind her fourth rib where a sheet of paper has been sitting for four years, and she could carry it nine miles and put it down on a stone. She has carried a frame of ash nine feet nine up eleven steps of stair past a painted mark. A book weighs what a loaf weighs.

It is that she will not, and there is no getting round it, and there has not been any getting round it since she was fourteen years old in a room with the shutters up.

So a book has been lying on a table in the room she is being let have for eighteen days, with nothing in it, exactly the right size, and it is hers if she takes it.

She has not taken it and she is not going to and she is not going to be hungry for it either, because she has decided what to do with it and that is the twenty-fifth.

## The Six Things

The six places were not far away and none of them was closed to her, and she went over them in one sitting at the seventh hour of the twenty-eighth, standing up, and it took forty minutes.

The room with the six day books. A woman of forty-one writes nine names a day in six books and has written in them every day for nineteen years, and there is a board outside it with forty-one lines a month on it, and a space means a thing is wanted and has not been taken.

The frame. A painted mark on an upright at the eighth foot, and four men had gone above it in six weeks and two of them had brought a stone to the mark and could not get it over.

The store with the loaf on the shelf. The woman at the counter, who puts a loaf down and does not take it off again.

The rail with the line on it that she can read twice and take none of.

The trough road and the carrier and the four boards.

And the post nine miles west with a board a foot square on it, thirty-one lines, all of them a thing going down a road.

Six places. Nine days of road. Not one of them shut to her and not one of them able to use her, and she sat with that for twenty minutes on the floor of a nine-foot room and then she got up and made her hands do something else because there was nothing else to do with them.

## What Being Worth It Came To Mean

She got the other half of it on the first of the next month and it was the simpler half and it took her nine minutes.

Being worth the price of one is not being strong. She is strong. Being strong has got her a refusal in the best book on nine days of road and a stone carried up a stair with four men watching and not one line on a board since the twelfth of a month that has gone.

Being worth the price of one is turning up with the thing.

Every arrangement in eight years that ever wanted something out of her wanted a hand. A man with a brush, a man behind a counter, a man with a bar, a man with a book. They wanted her hands and they wanted them at a place, on a day, and she has said no to every one of them and been right on all of them.

What she has never had is the other shape, which is somebody who wants a thing that has to be written or made or done, and who cannot do it themselves, and who is going to be told no and is going to take the answer.

## The Work Itself

Here are the eleven days, put down as it went, because there is nothing else to look at.

The twenty-second and twenty-third: finding out what she wants, which is not an answer.

The twenty-fourth and twenty-fifth: finding out what she has, which is four fingers and a thumb, a right arm open four days in five, eight of eleven holds, four counts of breath everywhere including inside a loaded reach, and a bar.

A bag with a dish and a lump of clay in it, a ring a finger’s width, a skin at the gate, and a piece of paper against her ribs that she opened with her own two hands in the fourth month of a year she could name and has never once called anything.

The twenty-sixth: finding out what a person has to have in order to be asked for a thing, and the answer is that she has to be holding something and looking at it, and there is no board and no counter and no line.

The twenty-seventh to the thirtieth: four days in which she went over the whole of nine days of road in her head, place by place, and found six things she could have gone and done and worked out why she could not go and do any of them.

The first and second of the next month: the last two days of it, in which she came to the end of the list and the list had one thing on it.

The one thing on the list was a piece of paper she made with her own two hands.
