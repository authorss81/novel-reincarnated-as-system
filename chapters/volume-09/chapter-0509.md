# What The Page Is Worth

She counted it out on flat ground in the lee of the low bank at the second hour of the sixth of a month, with the bar across her knees and the ring and the dish in a bag at her side, and it took forty minutes, and it is the last thing she is going to get for nothing.

## The Inventory

She starts at the top and goes down, which is how she has counted everything since she was eleven years old.

Money: none. The last of it went on bread at a counter five miles west of a mill on the twenty-third of a month two months back, and there has been nothing since, and the fold in the inside of her coat where it was stitched shut is flat.

Wages: none. Four months of them. Forty-one lines a month she could read off a full board twice and take none of, and the last floor that would have put her on one shut its shutter in the fourth month without saying a word to her about it on the day, and has not said one since.

Bread: none since the third of a month, standing up, a third of a loaf off a bench four miles east of here, which is the ninth day of that.

Shed: none. There is a bench at the end of this lane with a plank door and a post to put a bar against, and it is a line in a book nine hundred years old, and she is not going to sleep in it again.

She got that far and sat with it for a while, and then she went on to the body, because the body is the part of the count that has been going the wrong way for five months.

## The Body

Four fingers and a thumb. The index on the right hand has been gone since she was fifteen and what is left of that hand is four fingers and a thumb and a grip that will hold a thing but not turn it.

The right arm open four days in five. It went open twice on the fifth and once in the night and it is a rate now and not an event, and she has stopped writing down which days because writing them down would be a way of hoping.

Eight of eleven holds, and she went through all eleven standing up on the flat at the fourth hour of the sixth, and eight, and then she tried the ninth and put it down.

Four counts of breath everywhere. Four counts walking. Four counts standing in the wind. Four counts inside a loaded reach, which is the one that matters, and the fourth count is gone and got every single time and she gets it, and it takes her a count and a half longer to get it than it did in the first month.

A place behind the fourth rib, open the size of a coin, open since the eighteenth of a month in a year she could name if anybody asked her, and it is what the reach comes out of and it does not close and there is nothing above her to close it with.

Eighteen years old, and thinner than she was a month ago, and the thinness has stopped being a thing she notices and started being a thing she measures.

## The Heavy End

She picked the bar up at the heavy end on purpose at the third hour, because a bar she cannot hold at the heavy end is a fact she has been carrying for two years and has never once tested on flat ground with nothing at the end of it.

She took it up ninety feet.

At ninety feet her right arm came open, not halfway and not at the end, but in the middle of the lift, the way it does, and she felt the weight go over onto her left and she put it down before it went, and her boots were in the ground on both sides and nothing happened and nothing came down.

Then she stood up off flat ground without a hand on anything, which she can still do, and picked it up again at the light end and carried it four hundred yards with no trouble at all.

Two hundred yards of carrying at the light end. Ninety feet at the heavy end. That is the whole of the instrument and she worked it out in eleven minutes on open ground where there was nothing to fall on and she wants it in the page that she tested it there.

## Forty Seconds

Then she did the other count, and it is the one that took the forty minutes.

She gave a page away on the third of a month, at a crossing, in daylight, sitting on a bench, and she said forty words out loud in the ordinary voice and a man of nine hundred years wrote them down in forty seconds with a brush in his own hand.

Forty words said. Forty seconds written. One date. One place.

And then she walked away from a bench with nothing in her hands that she had not walked away with from every counter on nine days of road in two years, and the count she could do at the second hour of the sixth of a month on flat ground was this.

The page cannot buy her a roof. The page cannot buy her bread. The page cannot put a name in a day book on this ground, and her hand would not take the money if one of the six day books had a line in it with her on it.

What the page buys is one thing and the thing is that a man can find her, and that is all of it, and there is no other use for it.

## What Forty Seconds Bought

She got the shape of it out at the fourth hour and it took nine minutes and it is the finding and she said it out loud once, on the flat, in the ordinary voice, with nobody within nine hundred yards.

The page carries a date and it does not carry a place.

She gave him both. She gave him a date and she gave him a shed at the end of this lane, and he used the date and walked the lane four times and told her so on the seventh in the ordinary voice with a leg-mould in a box beside her.

So there is one thing in the world that can find her. She wrote it herself, on a bench, with her own two hands, out loud, on the third of a month.

And it finds her where she was.

That is the whole of it and it is the whole of what she has. The only thread in the world that can be pulled to bring somebody to her ends in a shed she is a quarter of a mile from and has not slept in for four nights, and she put it there herself with her own mouth in daylight.

She was found by accident on the seventeenth of a month in a month that has gone. She is findable on purpose from the third of this month, and she does not enjoy the difference, and she has not enjoyed it for six days.

## Five Days

It started on the ninth of a month and it is five days old and she counted it on the flat at the fifth hour because she counts everything and because the arithmetic is the only thing she has ever had.

A man of fifty with four boards stopped saying a word. A man of twenty-two pulled a horse up two hundred yards short of her and then went on and said the word as he went by. A woman behind a counter put a hand on a loaf and took it off again.

Eleven of them, and every one of them is right about the shape and wrong about the person.

She has not corrected it once. Correcting it means the page — it means standing in a room and saying out loud what has come for her and where it went, and there is not one room on nine days of road she can put that in, and the only room there is belongs to a man with a book and a rule older than she is.

So she is going to let eleven people be wrong about her for as long as it takes, and it goes on for as long as she does not say it, and that is the same mechanism as a page and she worked that out a month ago on a bench and wrote it on a wall.

## The Decision

She got up at the seventh hour and packed the ring and the dish and the bar and she did not go up the lane to the shed at the end of it.

She said it out loud once, on flat ground, in the ordinary voice, standing up, with the bag in her right hand and the light going off the flat.

“I am not going back to that shed,” she said. “It is on a page and I put it on the page, and there is a man nine hundred years old who can walk up that lane any day he likes, and if he is there one night while I am in it then the whole of what I have bought in four months is worth a shed and a plank door and a post.”

And then she did the last of the arithmetic on her feet, standing, and it took four minutes.

There is a bench four miles east of here where a leg man works, and there is a frame at the cut nine miles east where nine men are putting a thing up that has been four hundred years in the ground, and there is a counter five miles west where a woman has never asked her one question in two years.

Not one of those three is hers. Not one of them has ever been hers. Every one of them is a person being decent about a shape they have got wrong, and being decent is the cheapest thing anybody on nine days of road does, and it holds for exactly as long as it costs nothing.

Careful runs out of food before it runs out of anything else, and she said that out loud too, once, because she says everything out loud exactly once, and then she picked the bar up at the light end and went east up the lane with nothing behind her and nothing in front of her that she was owed.
