# Counting Toward Nothing

On the thirteenth of the month she put three figures on the board on purpose, in a row, with the bar’s point, and then she sat back on the boards and looked at them and waited for them to do what figures have done for her since she was fourteen.

Eighty-five days.

Three coppers.

Forty-one lines.

She had put eighty-five there because it was the true figure on the eighth of a month and it was the number she had carried the longest. She had put three there because it was what she had on the eighth of a month. She had put forty-one there because it was on the board outside the lifting floor on the seventeenth and she had not gone back to look since.

That is three numbers, and she gave them four days.

## Where They Point

The first two days were nothing. That is worth saying, because four days ago she expected four days to do something, and on the first day she was sure they would, and on the second she was still sure.

On the third she went over it line by line, which is a thing she does with a hand and not with a number.

Eighty-five days is a distance. It is the distance between a day a man put something down and a day she is standing in. It has a shape. It points at a man.

Three coppers is a quantity. It has no shape and it points at a counter, and a counter five miles west of a mill does not have anything at the end of it.

Forty-one lines is a quantity and it points at a board, and a full board points at a floor, and the floor is the only one of the three that is nine hundred yards from where she sleeps.

So two of the three numbers point at places, and the places do not point back, and one of them points at a man who is in a room behind a room with nothing moving in it.

## The True Figure Of The Days

On the sixteenth she measured it, because she had promised herself on the seventh that she would measure it and she has never once failed to do a thing she promised herself.

Day one thousand two hundred and fifty-six is the day he put a thing down and did not pick it up again. She did not have that written anywhere, and she had not written it anywhere since a month ago, and she got it in nine seconds because there is one date in eight years she has never had to look up.

Day one thousand three hundred and seventy-nine is the day it is.

The difference is one hundred and twenty-three days.

She checked it twice, once with her fingers on the bar blade and once the long way, and it came to one hundred and twenty-three both times, and that is the figure by subtraction at the sixth hour of the sixteenth of a month.

One hundred and twenty-three.

And the eighty-five she had been carrying was true on the eighth of a month, and it stopped being true four days after that, and she had gone on writing it forward for thirty-nine days.

Thirty-nine days of writing down a number she had not measured.

She sat with that for a while.

Thirty-nine days is not a large figure and it has nothing to do with him at all. It is a figure about a woman with a point and a board who could not be bothered to do a subtraction that takes four seconds.

That is what she had been doing with the days. Not waiting. Doing the next to the next day, because the next to the next day is easier than the day.

## Two Hours Of Six Days

And the other thing she did that afternoon, on the sixteenth, with the board in front of her and the light going off it, was to work out what it takes.

Two hours out of six days. That is what the ninth stage came in, and it came in on a morning in a month two months back without her asking and without her being able to stop it, and before that it came in as two hours of six days out of the hands of a woman of sixty-one who walked eleven miles to bring them and who was asked.

Six days of somebody else’s hands. Two hours of somebody else’s hands.

She has had none of it for sixty-nine days and she is going to have none of it for as long as the arrangement lasts, and there is no version of the next one in which somebody walks eleven miles.

She waited for that to feel like something. It had been on the board as a want since the seventh of a month and she looked at it on the sixteenth and it was a want with no body attached to it.

Nobody is going to come with two hours in them.

That is not sad. That is a fact about a floor and a shed and eleven miles of made road, and it is the same fact that is on the board at the rail, and it is why the board is full and why she is not on it.

## What Is In The Shed

She did that inventory on the sixteenth too, because she had nothing else to do and because there are nine lines of housekeeping on a board and a room is not a number.

Three boards off the floor and two more that are not boards. The roof cord she tied herself, four years back, with the loop doubled because she knew what she was doing and has never said so out loud. A ring in a low dish with clay in it and a lump going down an inch every month. The bar, which came with a strap that went at the buckle, and a strap she cut off her own coat lining and bound herself, and the cutting is still visible on the lining in a place where there is no lining any more.

The door. The post the bar goes round. A board with arithmetic on it, half of it scraped.

One dish. One cup she has not used in four months. A coat with a lining four inches narrower than it was.

That is all of it. She has carried all of it eleven times in two years between two places nine days of road apart, and it has gone in a bag and on her back and once on the end of the bar, and on one occasion she has slept with it in the open because there was nowhere to put it.

She has never once thought of the shed as hers.

That is not a feeling and it is not a complaint. She has never once called it hers, and there is a reason, and the reason is that the last thing she kept for two years she had to give back, and the year after that she was somewhere that was not hers and has been on four floors since that belonged to somebody else and on one rail and one counter that were not hers either.

What she does with a place is use it until it stops being useful and then go.

## What She Counts Now

So she stopped counting toward anything, and she did it on the sixteenth of a month at the seventh hour, and it took four seconds, and she has put one thing down in her life before and it took four seconds and the whole of the next month.

She counted what was.

That is all. It is an enormous downgrade and she has never done a bigger one.

Bread. Four days of it on the seventh and one day of it now, because she has been careful and careful runs out. Water, which is free and is six miles to the trough road and not eleven, and a full skin carries one day. The ring, which asks for water and pressure and a day, and gives back nothing and has never disappointed her. Eight of eleven holds, which she goes through standing up four times a month. Four counts of breath, and where the fourth count goes at night. Four fingers and a thumb.

And the ground. How far it is to the trough road and back, and to the store and back, and to the mill and back, and to the rail and back, and how long each of those takes at four counts with a bar.

That is housekeeping. It is what a person does with a day that is not going anywhere else.

She wrote nine lines of it on the board on the sixteenth and it took a quarter of an hour and not one of the nine lines pointed at a man, and the board went from being a thing she came back to in the morning to being a thing she uses in the afternoon.

## One Day Of Bread

She went down to the lane at the eighth hour on the sixteenth and got back up to the shed at the ninth with a full skin and no bread, because there was no bread to buy and no money to buy it with, and that was the first morning in four months she had gone at a problem with a bag.

She had not been hungry since the eighteenth of a month two months back.

That was put there. It was taken out of her on purpose, by something that did not ask, and it had held for one hundred and twenty-three days, and it appears to be finished.

She ate a third of what was left of the loaf and she put the rest on the board where she could see it and she had one day of bread and no money and no wages and no season and nothing on nine days of road that wants anything from her.

She had spent nothing in thirty days to stop counting, and that had cost her everything in days.

She sat on the boards in the dark with the wind coming in under the door at a foot a second and put her left hand flat against her coat, over the place against her ribs.

There was a piece of paper in there. Folded in four, soft at the folds, that she put there herself with her own hands four years ago and has never once opened.

She had said his name into this shed on the eighth of a month and got nothing. She had stood at a rail on the seventh and counted eleven minutes and got nothing. She had scraped a hundred and fourteen days off a board and put down nine lines of housekeeping and got nothing.

There was nothing left on this ground that she could do, and there was one thing she had never once done, and it was against her own ribs, eleven inches above the place that will not close.
