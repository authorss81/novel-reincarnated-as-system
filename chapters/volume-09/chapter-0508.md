# The Shed Without Her In It

She worked the lump for forty minutes on flat ground a quarter of a mile west of the shed at the end of the lane, and there was no wind off the low ground that night, and she could hear the frame coming down the wrong end about half a mile off at intervals, and that was the only thing on nine days of road that could be heard from where she was lying.

She centred the clay in the dish and put her left hand flat on the rim to hold it, and it did the thing it has done four hundred times and it moved a very small amount, and she did not ask anything for a while.

She had not eaten since the second of a month. There had been a loaf on the leg-man’s bench on the third and she had had a third of it standing up, and there had been water at the post and in her own skin and in the trough, and that was all of it for three days.

Then she asked the question, and she said it out loud, in the ordinary voice, on the ground, at the fourth hour of the fifth of a month, with nobody within nine hundred yards and the door of no shed in sight.

“What am I at.”

## What Came Back

That is the whole of it. Four words in the ordinary voice, once.

The reach went out.

She felt it go the way it has gone four hundred and something times, out of the place behind her fourth rib that is open the size of a coin, and it did not come back in nine seconds and it did not come back in a minute, and the dish under her left hand got lighter by exactly the weight of it, the same as the nineteenth of a month, and her left hand came up off the rim about an inch and stayed in the air.

What came back was not a weight and it was not a length.

That is the thing she could not have told anybody and the thing that has been in her since the fourth hour. Four hundred answers have come out of that reach in eight years and every single one of them has been something a person could put on a board — a load, a yard, a chain, a leg, a second pair of hands — and it has never once come back with anything else, because she has never asked it anything else.

It came back with a shed.

Not a shed she was in. A shed she was at, in the way a person is at a thing, the way a page names a place, the way a man with a book names a place when he goes and looks.

The shed at the end of the lane. The bench against the far wall. The post by the door with the bar’s strap round it. Nine days of it, and then three nights of not being in it.

That is what she is at, and it came out of the ground under her on flat ground a quarter of a mile away from it, and it is a shed with boards on the floor and a door that shuts and a man with his weight on one leg who shuts every door he walks through and waits for the latch before he does anything else at all.

## The Stage

She put her left hand back down on the rim on purpose and it was holding nothing at all.

Then the clay came up, and it came up about four inches off the flat ground, and it came the way it came on the nineteenth of a month, which is to say that it did not ask her and she was not consulted and she could not have stopped it if she had been able to stand there and say the word.

She said the word. Out loud, on the ground, at the fifth hour of the fifth of a month, four fingers and a thumb and no food in her since the second.

It did not go anywhere. Nothing in the world failed. No door opened and no book shut and no person in a room looked up, because there was not one person within nine hundred yards and there had not been for four days.

It took nine seconds and it came up out of the ground under her the way everything has ever come up out of the ground under her.

And it said the one sentence, into the ground, the way it has said eleven things in eight years, and it did not say anything after it.

[Ning Yan, past the line, servant-born, under no ceiling any county holds. Severance, stage two, is held.]

Then nothing.

She sat on flat ground with a dish four inches up in the air and did not move for four minutes, and then she put her hand down and it came down and stood on the clay and was steady, and she listened for nine minutes for a second sentence, and there was no second sentence, and she has never once been surprised by that and this is the second time she has not been frightened of it.

## The Cost

She did not find out what it had cost until the seventh hour and it was not dramatic and it was not in her body.

It was about a shed.

She lay back on the low bank with the dish beside her and worked it out the way she works everything out, which is by subtraction, and it took nine minutes and it went like this.

On the nineteenth of a month, in a shed four miles east of a mill, a piece of clay came up three inches off the boards and she learned that a place stops being hers for having slept in it, and she said that sentence out loud once on the boards and has not said it since.

On the fifth of a month, on flat ground a quarter of a mile from a shed at the end of a lane, with no walls and no roof and no door within nine hundred yards, a piece of clay came up four inches out of open ground and the cost was not a shed.

It was the other way round.

She cannot be met where she is expected. That is the whole of what came out of it. A person who comes for her comes to the place she was, and the place she was is a line on a page in a nine-hundred-year-old book, and the page was written by her out loud on a bench at a crossing on the third of a month in daylight with a boy forty feet off, and she chose every word of it.

She lay on the bank and let that be a thing for four minutes.

She has not been in that shed in three nights. She is four hundred yards from it and she is not going to be in it and she was not going to be in it when she asked the question, and the reach still gave it to her.

It does not take what is true. It takes what has been written down.

## The Man With His Weight On One Leg

She saw him at the seventh hour and he was about half a mile off coming up the lane from the west.

She was in the lee of the bank and he was on the high side and he could not have seen her, and she watched the whole of it, and she wants it in the page that she did not move and she did not stand up and she did not go to him.

He came up the lane at the speed of a man with his weight on one leg. He got to the shed at the end of the lane at the fourth hour past seven. He put his hand on the plank door and opened it and went in.

And then he shut the door behind him and waited for the latch, which she heard from four hundred yards on a still night on flat ground, and it is the smallest sound anybody has ever heard her make and it is the sound a man makes when he has done a thing nine hundred years.

He was in there a quarter of an hour.

She lay on the bank and watched the light of the window go on and off as he moved about in it, twice, and she could see from where she was that he went to the bench and stood at it, and then he stood at the door and then he went to the bench again.

He came out at the half hour, and he shut the door behind him, and he waited for the latch, and then he stood on the flat ground eleven paces off the door and did not go down the lane for four minutes.

Then he went down the lane at the speed of a man with his weight on one leg.

She did not get up. She had a dish four inches up in the air beside her and no bread in her since the second and an arm that had gone open twice that afternoon, and she lay in the lee of the bank a quarter of a mile west of a shed and she watched a man of nine hundred years walk away from a door, and that is the whole of the fifth of a month.

## What It Is

She worked out the rest of it on the bank, afterwards, in the dark, and it is the thing that is going to be the rest of this and she has said none of it out loud.

For eight years the only thing that made her findable was being the strongest person in every room, and she spent four months buying the opposite of that and got away with it, and nothing came.

And there is one book in the world that does not need anybody to read her in order to write her down, and she put herself in it on purpose, on a bench, in daylight, with her own two hands, on the third of a month.

So there is nothing now between a man and her except a page, and she gave him the page, and at the seventh hour of the fifth of a month he came to the door on the page and shut it and waited for the latch and stood there for four minutes and went down the lane.

It was not that she hid.

She did not hide and she has never hidden from anything in eight years and she is not going to start at a low bank a quarter of a mile off with a dish in her hand.

He found the place. He did not find the person, and there is no place on nine days of road he can write on a page that will be where she is, and she did that by not going back to a shed for three nights, and she had no idea she was doing it until the seventh hour.

Her own progress was the misdirection.

She lay there and said that out loud, once, into the dark, on flat ground, in the ordinary voice, because she says everything out loud exactly once, and then she got up and packed the bar and the dish and went east along the lane with the frame coming down the wrong end about half a mile off, and there was nothing behind her and nothing in front of her and no shed with boards on the floor within four miles that anybody could point at.

She was eighteen years old and she was past the ceiling by two and she had said a sentence on a bench that she cannot take back, and the whole of what she had left to her was the going.
