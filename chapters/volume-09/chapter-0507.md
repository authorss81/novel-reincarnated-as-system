# She Told Nobody Where

She worked out the question on the twenty-eighth of a month, at the shed at the end of the lane, on the bench, in the afternoon, and it took two hours and it is the only two hours of the last month she has been proud of.

There were nine questions. There have been nine since the fourteenth of a month two months ago, when she wrote the date down on a wall and said she was not going to choose one of them out loud, because the last thing anybody on nine days of road has ever done for her is choose a thing for her in a room.

She went through the nine on the twenty-eighth and threw eight of them away, and the going was quick, and the eight were all about strength and all of them were about becoming able to do a thing to something.

That is what the question is for. She has asked it four hundred times about a load and a length and a chain and a leg, and every one of those was a thing somebody else needed done and she was the only person within nine hundred yards who could do it.

The nine were all that shape and all eight of them were rubbish.

The ninth one came up at the third hour of the afternoon and she stopped, because she had been asking it since she was seventeen.

## What She Had Been Asking

It was not a question she had thought up. That is the whole of what she got out of two hours and she has not said it out loud to anybody and she is not going to for some time.

She has been asking it about forty-one lines a month for two years. She has been asking it about a rate behind a counter four times in eight years and every one of them came back with a figure. She has been asking it about a bench outside a store and a trough road and a shed and a yard at the turn and a strip and a name.

What am I at.

Four hundred times, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it has answered with work every single time, because that is the shape a question has to be in for a floor to put it on a board.

And the answer it gave every time was a place somebody else kept, held open for her, with a rate on it.

## What She Was Not Going To Do

She was not going to choose one out loud and she was not going to write it on a wall and she was not going to say it to the woman of fifty at the end of the lane or to the man on the post nine miles west or to a boy at a crossing who does not want to know.

She said that out loud, once, on the bench, at the fourth hour of the twenty-eighth, and it is the arrangement for the four days after it.

“I am not going to choose it in front of anybody,” she said. “I am going to ask it at the fourth hour of the fifth with a ring and a dish and nobody within nine hundred yards, and I am not going to have told one living person where that is.”

And then she did not sleep in the shed.

## Three Nights

That is the part she would not have believed in the fourth month and it is the whole of the third day on the page.

She put the bar against the wall at the eighth hour of the twenty-eighth, and she did not sleep on the bench, and she went down the lane in the dark a quarter of a mile and slept in the lee of the low bank with her clothes on.

On the twenty-ninth she slept under the cutting floor at the shed at the end of the lane, which is a stone floor and not a bed and about four feet high where a man can stand, and she lay on it for two hours and got up.

On the thirtieth she went back to the trough road and slept in the same place she had slept at the second month of this thing, on the bank, with forty goats and a stone.

Three nights in three places and a page in a nine-hundred-year-old book with the name of a shed on it.

She had not run. She wants that said in the plainest words available, because everything else in this month has been about a person being careful and a carter pulling up his horse.

She had not run, and she was not hiding, and she had not told a soul where she was going to be, and she had chosen three different places in three nights without deciding to, in the way that a person does not decide things any more when they have stopped wanting a room.

## The Woman Of Fifty

The woman of fifty came down the lane at the seventh hour on the second of a month and stood in the doorway of the shed at the end of it with her hand on the frame, and she said it in the ordinary voice before she came in.

“You have not been on the bench.”

“No.”

“I am not going to ask about it,” the woman said. “I told you in a month that has gone that if there is ever a day when there is nothing on that bench then that is a day I have nothing for you to do, and that I would tell you so on the day and not before. There has been nothing on it since the third. This is me telling you so on the day.”

“All right.”

“You look thin,” the woman said.

“I am thin.”

The woman did not come in.

“Whatever it is that has come up the lane to you,” she said, “you can have the frame room and there is a door on the west side with a bolt on it, and I am not asking, and I will not ask in front of my own hands.”

And she went back up the lane, at the speed of a woman of fifty who has been walking this lane for nine years.

Ning Yan stood in the doorway with a bar on her shoulder and watched her go, and that is the fourth person this month and it is the fourth one to be wrong about her, and it is the first one who has been kind about it in a way she could not refuse.

## The Fifth

She got the arithmetic done on the third at the shed, in the afternoon, on the bench, and she wrote it out in the dirt because the wall at the end of the lane has been getting full.

Day one thousand three hundred and ninety-four is the first of a month. Day one thousand four hundred and twelve is the nineteenth of the next one, and that is the day a piece of clay came up three inches off a floor in a shed with a bar under the door.

Day one thousand four hundred and eighty-eight is the fifth of a month after that one.

That is seventy-six days by subtraction, and she checked it twice, once with the bar’s point and once the long way.

And the last line, which she wrote underneath, because she is a person who writes the rest of it down and not only the wish:

Nineteen days ago somebody wrote down where I sleep. I have not slept in it since.

She looked at that one for a while and then she went and got the ring and the dish and put them in a bag with her own two hands, because she is not leaving a dish in a shed that a page says is hers.

She did not sleep on the bench on the fourth.

She went down the lane at the tenth hour in the dark and lay down in the lee of the low bank a quarter of a mile west of the shed at the end of the lane, with the ring and the dish and the bar and a bar of wood off the frame for a fire she was not going to light, and she was up at the second hour and she was not cold.

At the fourth hour of the fifth of a month she had about eleven hours of dark left in front of her and a dish with a lump of clay in it and four fingers and a thumb and no bread in her since the second.

## The Fourth Hour

The arithmetic came out where arithmetic comes out, sitting on the ground with a bar of wood she was not going to light under her arm.

Bread: none since the third, and a third of a loaf standing up at the leg-man’s bench. Water: her own skin, and the trough four hundred yards up the lane, filled at the tenth hour and good for a day.

Ring: a finger’s width. Dish: in a bag, with a lump of clay in it, because she is not leaving a dish in a shed that a page says is hers.

Body: four fingers and a thumb. Right arm open twice that day and once in the night, and it is four days in five now. Eight of eleven holds, gone through standing up at the eleventh hour. Breath at four counts and the fourth one gone and got. A place behind the fourth rib, open the size of a coin, and that is what the thing comes out of.

And the last line, which is the one she put her finger on:

Nothing on this ground can find me except a page. I wrote the page.

Then she got up and walked two hundred yards and came back and did it again, because she was not going to lie on a bank at the fourth hour of the night before the fifth of a month and wait for anything, and there was nothing to wait for, and she had proved that on the seventh of a month in a shed a mile short of a mill with her own two hands.

She sat down at the third hour with her hands round her knees and did not say his name once, which she has not done since the eighth of a month, and there was nothing in her head at all.

That is the fourth time in five months and she stopped finding it frightening somewhere around the twentieth of a month without being able to say when.
