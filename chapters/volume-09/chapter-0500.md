# The Book He Carries

“Show me it,” Ning Yan said, on the nineteenth of a month at the ninth hour, at the end of the lane where the ground is flat and you can see a long way.

He took it out of a bag that is not a bag, it is a piece of oiled cloth with a strap on it, and he did not hand it to her.

That is the first thing and it is not a small thing, and it went on being the first thing for the rest of the day. He held it.

“All of it,” she said.

“You can look at it,” he said. “You cannot have it, and I am not going to pretend to you that those are the same request, because they are not.”

She said, “That is fair,” and it came out of her before she had decided on it, and he nodded as though it were a thing she was entitled to say.

## What It Is

It is not large. That is the first fact about it and it went against every one of the four books she has been entered in, all of which were larger than a door.

It is a foot and a half by ten inches and it is the thickness of a week of bread, and it is bound in something that has gone the colour of a dried thing and has been rebound twice because she could see the two places where it had been, and the thread in it is not thread, it is a cord the thickness of a pencil.

The boards are not boards. They are something pressed.

The outside of it has no writing on it at all, which she had not expected and which she looked at for a long time.

She did not touch it. He held it with his left hand flat on the cover and his right hand at the edge, and he stood with his weight on one leg and he did not turn round to the wind, and she noticed that and did not know what it meant and worked out two hours later that it meant he was keeping the wind off the edge of it with his body.

## What She Could Not Read

She is the only person within nine days of road who can read a hand four people cannot take, and she has been that since she was seventeen, and she stood on flat ground at a crossing and looked at a page of a nine-hundred-year-old book for four seconds and could not read one word of it.

That is not a small thing either and it took her a while to be honest on it.

The hand was small and upright and it did not go the way a hand goes. It sat on the line the way a person sits on a chair without moving. It had been sitting there for longer than she has been alive and it had never once been in a hurry.

“You are not going to be able to read it,” he said.

“No.”

“That is correct,” he said, “and I want you to know that I have watched four people try, and one of them was me, eight hundred years ago.”

That is the whole of what he said on it and he said it without any weight on it at all, and it is the first time in eight years that anybody has been in a position to tell her the limits of the only instrument she owns and got it right.

## How Many

She asked him how many names were in it and he took eleven seconds, which was longer than any answer he had given her.

“I do not know,” he said.

“You have written it for nine hundred years.”

“Yes.”

“You do not know how many are in it.”

“No,” he said, “and I have never once counted, and four people have asked me and I have given the same answer to all four, and it is the only true thing I know about the book.”

“Would the count be a number.”

“It would be a number,” he said, “and it would be wrong, because I do not write every day, and I do not write in order, and I do not write the ones I have not got to yet. It is not a register. It is a thing that I keep.”

## How He Finds Out

“Then how do you know when somebody gets there.”

He shut the cloth over the book and put the strap through and did that carefully, and she watched him do it.

“You do not fall off the top,” he said. “That is the thing that people cannot get hold of, coming up from the bottom. It is not a cliff and it is not a fall and nobody slips. You get to the top of a realm and then there is a person standing at the top of it who has a document, or there is not, and that is all that happens.”

“And if there is not.”

“Then there is a man nine hundred years old who finds out,” he said, “and that is me, and I do not find out quickly, and I have got the wrong shed sitting against for two hours to show for it.”

## The Question She Wanted

She stood on flat ground and worked her way to the thing she had come out here for, and it took her four minutes and she did not hurry it.

She said, “What do you have on me.”

“Nothing.”

That is the whole of his answer and it did not have a clause on it.

“You have been up this lane nine days for a person you have got nothing on.”

“Yes,” he said.

“You could have written me down in a book with a name in it nine days ago if you had anybody’s word for anything.”

“Yes,” he said. “If I had a page about you I would have had it written down before I came up here, and I would not have had to ask a boy at a crossing for a shed, and I would not have sat against a shut door for two hours. What I have to write on is what other people have written. That is the whole of what a book is.”

## What That Meant

And then she understood it, standing there, and she has said out loud once in her life that she understands things and she did not say it out loud this time and there is a reason, which is that she has eight days left of not having said anything out loud, and she was not going to start.

There are four pages in the world with her name on them.

Three of them were done to her by offices that had a use for her. One of them she went at herself on purpose, on the third of a month two months back, at the fourth hour, in a room with the shutters up, and it is in the best book on nine days of road and it cannot be taken out of there by anybody including her, and it is the only thing in eight years she chose.

He has nothing about her because nothing has ever been put in front of him about her, and he is not an office and he cannot send for anything and there is nobody nine days of road who would carry a word about her to a man on a lane for a copper.

So the thing she was going to have to hand him was the only one she had.

She worked it out on the twentieth of a month at the ninth hour, at the crossing, with a boy putting a chain up and down, and she said nothing to him at all of it for two more days, and in between she went east to the cut and worked a leg for nothing, and west to the store and stood at a counter with no money, and she lay down on boards at the end of a lane and did not keep the place.

That is what two days is when there is nothing to be done to a thing and the thing is the only thing there is.

On the twenty-first of a month he came up the lane and stopped nine feet off and did not come any nearer, and she said, “I have read out what is in my coat.”

And he said, “Then say it to me, and I will write it down, and you will hear it, and I will not stop you.”

## The Bag And The Road

The cloth it is in is the third one. He showed her that without being asked, because she had looked at the strap.

“There have been three,” he said. “The first went at a corner in a year I am not going to tell you of. The second one I put in a river, because there was a week in it that was not mine to carry.”

“Why.”

“Because it had three names in it that I had got wrong,” he said, “and a book with a name in it wrong is a worse thing than no book, and I did not know that until I had done it once.”

That is what a man of nine hundred years does with a mistake and it is the first thing he has told her she did not already know, and she has thought about it every day since.

## Whether He Is In It

She asked him that at the ninth hour, standing in the road, and it was the question she had been carrying since the seventeenth.

“Is your name in it.”

“Yes,” he said.

“Why.”

“Because I got to the top of a realm nine hundred years ago with no document to take me past it,” he said, “and I have been in it since I was near the age you are, and it is the first name in the book and it is the only entry I have ever been able to check.”

“Kept or lost.”

“Kept,” he said. “Nine hundred years and it has not saved one of them and it is still the correct line, and I have never once wanted it to be the other one.”

She walked up the lane after that with the whole of it turned over four times before she got to the shed, and the thing she could not get round was that he had answered a question she had not asked, which is that he is written down, and he is still standing here, and he has never once in nine hundred years been relieved of a thing he did not want to be relieved of.

## The One She Did Not Ask

There was one more thing in her at the ninth hour and she did not ask it and she has not asked it since and she is not going to for a long time.

She wanted to ask him what he does with the ones he gets wrong on the ground, in the years, between the days he comes up a lane. She wanted to ask him whether there is anybody past the turnstile who can read the book and who is not him. She wanted to ask him what would happen to a name in it if he stopped writing.

All three of those are the same question, and it is the one question on nine days of road that nobody has ever thought to put to a man with a brush.

She stood in a road at the ninth hour with all three of them ready and she asked none of them, and the reason is that she has been the only person on nine days of road who reads things for nothing for eight years, and she knows exactly what happens to a man who is asked to account for a book by the person who is next in it.
## What The Road Calls Him

She asked him two more things at about the tenth hour on flat ground at a crossing and neither of them was about the rule.

“Is that a list.”

“Yes,” he said. “It is a list and it is not a register and there is no way to find a name in it, and I have never once wanted to.”

“Then what do people on this road call you.”

“Warden,” he said. “That is the only word I have got that is not a name, and it is the only word I have, and I have never once said it to a person who was not going to have to repeat it.”

“Then I will be saying it.”

“You will have to,” he said. “There is nothing else, and you are going to be here a long time.”
