# They Started Being Careful

It started on the ninth of a month and it was not a thing anybody decided.

The carrier came through at the usual time with four boards on a bar and she was at the kerb filling the skin, and he came down off the lane at thirty feet and went past her without stopping and without the thing he says to everybody, which is morning, which he has said to her twice.

That is all that happened on the ninth. A man of fifty carried four boards past a woman and did not say a word.

She noticed it and stood there with a skin in her hand and worked out, in nine seconds, that she was not disappointed. She was watching. That is the difference and it took her two years to be able to tell them apart.

## The Fourth Person

The fourth person was on the eleventh and it is the one she has kept.

There is a cart that comes down the lane six times a day and goes back up it six times, and the man on it is twenty-two and he has been on it since he was nineteen, and he has said good to her four hundred times in two years in the way that a man says good to a thing on his road that he has got used to.

He came through at the fourth hour on the eleventh with a load and he saw her a hundred and forty yards off, coming out of the lane with a bar, and he pulled the horse up.

He did not stop for her. He pulled up two hundred yards short of her and then took it again and went past at the ordinary speed and said good to her when he went by.

But he had pulled up.

That is the whole of what she has got and she went over it on the twelfth for an hour with a leg-mould in her lap and could not make it anything else.

A man of twenty-two who has said good to her four hundred times pulled his horse up two hundred yards short of her and then went on, and if he had not pulled up she would not have known there was anything in it.

## What She Worked Out

She worked it out at the ninth hour on the twelfth, at the shed at the end of the lane, sitting on the bench, and it took her forty minutes and it is the most useful forty minutes she has had in two years.

Here is the ground as it stands.

For four months there has not been one thing on nine days of road that wanted anything from her. She stood at a rail for eleven counted minutes and got nothing. She stood in a store two hundred times and got good. She read forty-one lines off a full board twice and took none. A woman of fifty stood in a doorway and said this is the day I have nothing for you to do and meant it.

Four months of that, and every single person on it is used to it. They know what she is. She is a person on a road who is very strong and is not working and has not asked for anything, and every one of them has made their own arrangements about that in four months and none of the arrangements has cost them anything.

And then, on the third of a month, something came for her.

That is what the ground has. It has no idea what came for her. It has a woman with a bar who has been left alone for four months and who has, in four days, started walking out of her lane at the sixth hour and coming back at the ninth, and who has a man in her handwriting beside her, and who says good to a shopkeeper and buys nothing and stands at a bench and does not eat.

A man of fifty stops saying good. A man of twenty-two pulls up his horse two hundred yards short.

They are not being cruel and they are not being frightened, exactly. They are doing the ordinary thing that people do to a thing they cannot see the size of, and every one of them is correct to do it, and she would do it herself.

## What She Cannot Do About It

And then she worked out the part that does not go away, which is that she cannot correct it.

She cannot tell the carrier that nothing has come for her, because something has.

She cannot tell the man of twenty-two that she is all right, because she does not entirely know that she is, and she has not been able to establish it since the seventeenth of a month, and a woman who has not established whether she is all right is in no position to tell a carter she is.

She cannot tell the woman at the store, who has been good to her for two years and has never once asked her a question, and who would take that sentence in the ordinary way and put it down somewhere, because that is what a counter is.

And the reason she cannot correct any of it is the real reason, and it is the seventh of a month at the fourth hour.

The reason is that the only thing there is to be careful of is a book nine hundred years old with her sentence in it, and there is nowhere on nine days of road she can put that.

## Two Months

She started counting it on the ninth of a month and she counted it the way she has counted everything for five months, which is badly, on the bench, in the evening.

It is the ninth of a month and it is the fifth day since she said the sentence out loud on a bench at a crossing, and the carter pulled up at two hundred yards on the eleventh, and the carrier stopped saying good on the ninth.

So the thing began a week before the sentence, or a week after it, and she cannot work out which, and she has been trying for two days, and the answer is that she does not know, because a thing that has been true for four months does not begin on a day anybody can point at.

What she can say is that it is the first time in a year that a thing about her has been got wrong on this ground, and that it has been got wrong by eleven people who have all been correct, and that the wrongness is going to grow for as long as she carries it.

That is what she wrote on the wall at the ninth hour of the thirteenth of a month, four lines, and it is the last line of them that matters.

It goes on for as long as I do not say it. That is the whole mechanism and it is the same mechanism as a page.

## The Long Road

She walked out to the end of the lane on the fourteenth and went past the crossing and the painted board and the chain and the hut, and she had never been on the long road and the long road is made and flat and goes east for eleven miles to a gate with a chain on it.

She went two miles down it and stood in the middle of it at the ninth hour in the morning with nothing behind her and nothing in front of her and came back.

That is the whole of what she got out of it and she wants it in the plainest possible words because everything else in the last six days has been a book.

She walked two miles east on a made road with a bar on her shoulder and her right arm open and a leg-mould in a box she was taking back to the cut, and there was not one person on it, and nothing came, and nothing was written, and no door waited for a latch, and she stood in the middle of it for four minutes and then walked back.

And on the way back she worked out that she had gone two miles east to be somewhere nothing could reach her, and that four months ago she would have found that restful, and that on the fourteenth of a month it took nine seconds to stop being restful and turned into its opposite.

## The Decision

She got back to the shed at the end of the lane at the eleventh hour on the fourteenth and she did not sleep, and she sat on the bench in the dark and did the arithmetic of it, and it came out to nine days of walking one way.

There is a post on the strip road nine miles west of here with a board on it and a man on the board with a paste pot and a rag, and he has been on that board since a month she could name if she wanted to, and he fills it off a cart every nine or ten days, and she has walked past it eleven times in two years and stopped at it twice.

Twice, both times at a time when she had nothing, and both times she had gone on to somewhere else.

She wanted to know what a post does with the people who come down a road. That is not all of why she was going and she knew it was not all of it and she did not argue with herself over it at four hours of the morning, because she says everything out loud exactly once and she was not going to say that sentence.

She got up at the fourth hour and she did the ring and she packed the bar and she went west.

## What She Did Not Do About It

She put herself in front of the carrier on purpose on the way, at the second hour, with the leg-mould in a box on the road where he had to come within thirty feet of her, and she stood still.

He came within thirty feet and he said good.

It took her nine seconds to work out that he had decided to say it, and another nine to work out that he had decided it for her — that a man of fifty carrying four boards had made a decision in a lane somewhere in the last four days over whether to put one word into the air near a woman on a road, and had gone about his morning having made it.

She did not thank him. She has not said thank you to anybody in eight years except once, to a man nine feet off a cart track, for four coppers of bread.
## The Word They Have Not Met

Nobody on nine days of road knows his name, and one word for it, and the word is Warden, and eleven people have spent a month deciding what it means without ever once coming within thirty feet of him.

That is what she thought about on the sixteenth of a month at about the fourth hour, sitting on the bench with the light going off the flat ground: the whole of what has changed on this lane in a month is a word that eleven people have made up a meaning for, out of nothing, out of a girl who went east for four days and came back with a leg fitted.

A word gets around faster than a person. A word gets around by people who are careful.
