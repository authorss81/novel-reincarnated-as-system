# Where The Face Lets Go

On the tenth she got four feet along the face and on the eleventh she got the rest of it, and what it cost her was the arm and the ramp and about nine seconds of knowing exactly how long she had.

The work was not stone work. Stone work was the bar going under and the stone coming up and the stone going on a sledge. This was going under, which is the one thing nobody on a working reach is ever taught and every person who has done it knows by the time they have done it once.

She went in on her knees with the bar and worked the clay out from underneath with the bar’s own weight, four inches at a time, and backed out, and banked it with a board and a wedge so the face could not sit down into the hole she had made in it, and went in again.

It is slower than anything she had done in five months. Four inches at a time, four inches at a time, and every time she backed out she had to get a knee up out of a cut she was in to the thigh with a bar in her hand and four fingers on one of them.

By the third hour of the second day she had the face undercut for eleven feet and the bank above it standing on nothing but its own weight, and she had nineteen days left before the far date, and she was in the cut again.

## The Going

It went the way they go, which is to say that it did not fall and then stop.

The whole line of it let go at once, from the near end to the far end, in one movement so even that it took her a second to understand it was a movement and not a sound. Two feet of bank coming down on the face she had spent two days cutting, at the height of her chest, and the sound of it arriving after the movement, which is how a bank goes.

The bar was under.

She had it in her right hand and the bar’s own length was under the falling lip and her right hand was on it and the ground came down on the back of that hand and pushed it, and the four fingers closed on it because four fingers close when the mind has gone somewhere else and the body does the next thing it knows.

That was the whole of it. That was the entire mechanism of how she got out of a bank at the end of a reach with four fingers on one hand and a bar in the other and nobody within a day’s walk in either direction.

The bar took the weight. That was what the bar was for and she had been putting it under things for four months without ever once needing it to be the only thing between her and the ground. She felt it take, and she put her left hand on the flat of it above her head, and she shoved, and she got a knee up.

Eleven counts, and she counted them afterwards the way she had counted every one of them since a stage in the fourth month of a year she has never had to name.

Then she was out, on the flat of the bank, on her hands and her knees, and the bank was where it had fallen, and the bar was half in under it, and the wind was moving the dust off it in a long grey sheet.

## What Came Down With It

She lay on the flat for a while and breathed in four counts until the four counts were four counts again.

The right arm would not come in. Not pain, exactly. It came as far as it came and stopped where it had always stopped, and it stopped further. She lay there and put it down and picked it up and put it down again, and it was open half the time now instead of a third, and she could feel the difference the way a person can feel the difference between two weights.

She had been at half before, once. It had gone back.

That was the sentence. It did not go back. That was the whole of what she learned in the ten minutes she lay on the flat of a bank at the end of a reach with dust going off it in the wind, and she learned it by testing, which she had not wanted to do and did anyway, and the testing took about four minutes, and afterwards she sat up and did not do it again that day.

The ramp.

She got up and walked to the sledge with the bar in her left hand and picked up the first stone of the day and tried to lift it over her shoulder onto the bed, the way she had lifted four hundred stones a day for five months, and her right arm came up to about the height of the stone and stopped and stood there.

It is a short arm now for anything above the shoulder. She is as strong in it as she was in it yesterday. It will simply not go over.

## Telling Da

She walked back up the reach in the last of the light with the bar in her left hand, and Da was at the trestle because he was at the trestle most evenings in the season, and she had promised him one thing and she went and kept it.

“The face let go,” she said. “At about the fifth hour. It came down on the bar.”

Da did not say anything for a moment. He looked at her right arm, and he looked at the far end of the reach, and he did the arithmetic that foremen do, which is what is left of the day and what is left of the bank and who is standing in the reach.

“It is down,” he said. “Off the face. On the flat.”

“It is down. I am not giving it up.”

“You are going to have to clear it before anybody can get a bar to it, and you cannot lift over your shoulder, and it is a two-foot bank.” He said it flatly, the way he said everything, and he was not being cruel, and he was not being kind, and both of those would have been easier.

“Yes,” Ning Yan said.

He looked down the reach for a while.

“I will put two men on the clearing tomorrow. Half a day each, and it comes out of the season and not out of anybody’s wages, and here is why, and it is not kindness and I want that understood: a face that has let go once will let go again, and it is behind a cut four feet deep, and I am not having a man under it at the end of a season for a bank nobody hired on.” He picked the tally up off the plank.

“You do not say thank you and I do not want you to. If it comes down again you are the one who is under it and you are the only one who can decide to be.”

She stood at the trestle with dust in her coat and a bar in her left hand and an arm that would not go over her shoulder, and she did not say thank him, because she did not want to say it and because she had told him she would not and had meant it.

What she felt was not the arm. The arm was a fact and she had been keeping facts since she was seven.

What she felt was something she had not felt since the fourth of a month four months back and could not name then and could not name now, and it was this: the reach had been empty the whole time it was happening, and it would go on being empty, and the thing that had put her in a cut at the end of it was not the ground.

It was a decision made the evening before by a man with a bar in his hands, out of the only thing he owned, which was the truth, and he had handed it to her and she had taken it.

The eleventh closed on a woman with nineteen days of a season, a bank down on the flat in a grey sheet, an arm open half the time, no ramp, no heavy end, and two men coming at the fourth hour in the morning to clear ground she had put on the flat by herself.

She slept with the bar by the door again, which she had done twice in five months, and she did not dream of lines, and she was up before the light with four fingers and a left arm and a bank to clear.
