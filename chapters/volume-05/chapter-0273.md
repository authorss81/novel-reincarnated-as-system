# The Walk Back Out

On the twelfth she walked east before first light with the bag lighter by one sheet and the oilcloth against her ribs, paper and stubs and marks, all of it hers and none of it checkable.

The floor was behind her. The wash house smoked. The tower light burned. The counting room held its date. She had stood one attendance she had already lost, put a paper for her brother’s name where somebody had to look at it, daylight, her name said out loud, asked for and entered, and walked back out, because there was nothing left in there that was hers to carry.

The road east was the same road west walked backward, ruts and hedges and banks, and it asked the same price, days. She paid them walking, breath in four counts, right arm open a third of the time, four fingers’ worth of grip on the bag strap.

What she felt at first light was a hollow sort of clearness, the kind that comes after a thing is done and cannot be undone. She wanted, before the day was an hour old, to see the yard and the post and the low building with smoke out of it, and then to stand at the plank and put her own hand on the column and see what nine days of nothing had written in it. That was the want, and it was hers, and it had a furnace in it she could not yet reach.

## The Report

On the fourteenth a carrier going east with stone passed her and slowed, and the driver, a woman of about forty with dust in her hair, looked at her bag and her coat and her hands.

“Floor still on?” the woman said.

“Floor still on,” Ning Yan said. “Nine men. Same nine.”

“Wash house smoking?”

“Smoking.”

“Tower lit?”

“Lit.”

The woman nodded the way carriers nod at news that is not news, and flicked the team on. That was the whole of what went inside the bound by consequence: nine men still on the floor, the wash house smoking, the tower lit, the counting room holding its date, people doing the only thing they were built to do, nine days west and out of her reach.

She did not ask a second question, because a second question would say the first one had not been news to her, and that would be the end of the only road that runs between here and there.

She walked on with the report given and the road under her, and the paper was where somebody had to look at it, and Ning Bo’s account was opened and worked and not settled. It would be settled by his own doing, at a price to her she could not name and had not yet agreed to, and it would be settled when the years that were not hers to fold had folded themselves. That was not this road’s work.

## The High Ground

On the fifteenth the road crossed high ground with no shelter and no water, and the wind worked at her coat and found every gap in it. She walked with the bag pulled tight and the oilcloth doubled inside, and the paper gone west stayed dry because it was gone, and the stubs stayed dry because she kept them so.

At midday she rested in the lee of a standing stone where carriers had left marks, tallies cut shallow, names worn short by weather. She ran the flat of the second finger over one mark without reading it, feeling the cut under the skin, and thought of a strip of cloth going up on a post every nine or ten days whether or not there was anything in it, and of paste gone pale and stiff as it dried, and of a hand that would not read what it had put there.

A team passed below on the lower track, bells faint, dust rising, and she watched it the way she watched everything that moved without being driven. The driver did not look up. The road held them both without holding either.

She walked on with the wind behind her in the afternoon, and the miles came easier, and the furnace ticked under her foundation from four days away, nearer with every step, steady or out, and the not-knowing sat in her the way unworked days sat in a column, present as gaps and nothing else.

## The Valley

On the sixteenth the road dropped into a farmed valley with hedges and lit windows, the first settled country since the floor, and a dog barked from a yard and was hushed. She bought bread at a carrier’s shelter at the valley head, dearer than five, and ate it outside with her back to the wind, and the seller said the mill reach was two days east, the way sellers say distances that are not promises.

She slept that night under a roof with five other walkers, oilcloth against her ribs, bag strap round her wrist, and the rain came in the night and drummed on the shingles and stopped before dawn. At first light the valley smoked with mist and the hedges dripped, and she walked out of it with wet boots and dry paper.

In the morning at a trough she washed the dust off the flat of the second finger and looked at it afterwards, the way she looked at it every morning now, and it was only a finger. She put it flat on the page at the sixth hour wherever there was a page, and it took eleven minutes, and the eleven minutes were still hers in a way that almost nothing else was.

Under her, in the shelter, where the others were asleep:

“You are east,” he said. “And you are held.”

## The Seventeenth

On the seventeenth the pay-day arrived whether or not she had worked it, and she had not worked it, nor the days round it.

She was five days east of the floor, four days west of the yard, with no plank to stand at and no book to write in, and the column four hundred yards past the post held the gap the way unworked days stand, present as gaps and nothing else. A day not worked was a day not paid and no day could be moved in either direction by anybody, and the pay-day answered in the column and not in the asking.

She ate bread bought off a carrier at his price, dearer than five coppers, eaten with her back to the wind, and counted the gap on her fingers. One pay-day missed. The twenty-seventh would be missed too if the road held her, and it would not, because she would be standing at the plank on the twenty-second at the latest, and she had done the arithmetic every night since the floor.

The carrier she bought off was going east for stone with an empty cart and did not ask where she had come from, which she would have answered if he had, because the answer was one sentence and the sentence was hers to spend. He put four coppers of bread into her hand and took eight for it, in the open, in front of nobody, and put the eight into a fold of his coat and said the going was good past the ridge and hard after it. That was the whole trade.

What she felt was not hunger. Hunger had left her at the first Foundation and never came back. It was the heaviness of a thing carried straight, a season with weight in it, gaps clean, dates true, and a column that would go on being true whether she stood at it or not, which was the part she had not expected to be able to bear.

The seventeenth closed on a woman walking out with a paper left behind her, a figure on a far-side book against hours she had not yet worked, a pay-day missed, and a road under her that charged days and asked nothing else.

She had asked for one hour and got it. She had been entered. She walked east.
