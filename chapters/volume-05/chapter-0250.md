# Eight Feet From The Fire

On the eighth of the month she finished the heavy end by about the sixth hour and she had not asked for the heavy end and had not been refused it and had been paid the same as the four men at the easy end, and at about the sixth hour and a half she went and sat at the trestle.

There is a fire under that trestle and it belongs to the eleven men and there is no fire on this reach that she has to pay five coppers a day to stand near.

That was the whole of the difference and it is the only one there is, and she had two months inside a line of work to find out what the same fire costs when it is not the same fire.

## What It Is

The cold is not weather.

It has not got a source and it did not have one two months ago on the other side of the rise, and there is nothing in this country, on either side of it, that is doing it to her.

It is the stone of a body running at four counts of breath with a base under it that finished heavier and colder than the one before it, and it is the thing her body still does to her, and it is the only thing it does any more, because she has not been hungry since the first of the Foundation stages.

Inside the bound she was five coppers a day closer to a fire and outside that ring she was not allowed in.

Past the post the ring costs nothing. She can stand with her boot in the ash and her knee against the trestle leg and the wind at her back, and there is not one man on this reach who has any right to move her and not one of them who is going to try.

And she was colder at it than she is standing out in the open.

That is the whole of the discovery and it is not a mystery and she did not work for it. The remedy is the same remedy on both sides and the price of it is different, and the difference is that one side of a line of work charges a woman five coppers a day for the cure and the other side gives her the cure and lets the thing stand.

A woman who cannot buy warmth can still not get it. She had a man behind a counter offer her the answer to that once, two months ago, and put a pan back under a shelf, and she had crossed three-quarters of a mile of the worst ground on nine days since.

## What The Fire Is Doing

The fire is a fire in a ring of stones somebody built about nine feet across and it burns about forty minutes on a good arm of wood and about an hour and a half on a bad one, and it is fed by whichever of the eleven men has the wood, and it is fed because the men at the trestle are there at the sixth and seventh hours and are cold.

That is all it is.

It has no arrangement behind it. There is no pot under a shelf in it and no woman behind a counter who has thought about it and no rule that says anybody is owed warmth at it. It is eleven men and a ring of stones and a wind off the rise, and she is inside the eight feet because she is standing there and they have not moved her.

On the other side of the rise, the eight feet were the inside of a ring that cost five coppers a day, and she had been outside it every one of eleven days and had eaten bread at the counter from a woman who told her to stop standing in the middle of her floor.

Both of those rings are eight feet wide. One of them is a circle and one of them is a rule.

## What The Hut Is Like

It is a hut with three boards in it and it is not hers.

There are three of them in a row at the bottom of the yard behind the end house and they belong to the yard and they are slept in by whoever is working, in that order, and hers is the third one and she is in it because she is the last one hired and there is not one arrangement about it that anybody made.

Three boards and a roof and a nail with a coat on it and a cold that comes up through the floor, and she is colder in it than she is at the fire, and she has been colder in it every night since the second of a month.

There is no store on that floor, because the store is four hundred yards up the track and there is a counter in it and a loaf on it at five coppers, and she eats that outside.

That is the whole of the domestic life she has on the far side of a line of work, and four months ago she had a board under a shelf in a room where a woman told her to stop standing in the middle of the floor, and she has not thought about that room once since she crossed.

She thought about it for about four minutes on the ninth of a month with her hands not working.

## An Hour And A Half

She stayed an hour and a half.

She was as close as she could get without standing in it, with bread in her hand that she had not finished, and the four men at that fire were talking about a cart that had come off the track at the mill, and they did not move for her and they did not make room and they did not say one word about her being there.

The fire was not enough.

She had known it would not be. It had not been enough on the other side of the rise either and she had paid five coppers a day there for eleven days for not being enough, and there is no arrangement anybody can make with a body of nine stages that changes what the air does when it arrives.

What it does is the same. That is all it does. It goes through the coat and off the hands and out between the ribs and there is a fire going a foot from her knee and she is the coldest thing at that fire.

## The Eight Feet

The eight feet exist while eleven men are sitting in them.

That is the arrangement and there is nothing else in it. The fire is theirs, it burns on their hours, it is fed by a man who goes and gets wood in the dark, and it is let down when they have finished with it and not one minute before.

At about the eighth hour four of them put their coats on and went up the track and one of them kicked the fire out into the stones on the way past, the way a man does it at the end of a day, without stopping and without looking at anybody.

She was still at it.

That is the part of it that is not about her and is the part she thought about for about an hour afterwards. The fire did not go out because the wind got up or because the night came or because anybody had decided anything.

It went out because four men had finished, and a woman with a season booked to the end of it and a bar and four fingers' worth of grip on one hand had not been asked, and had not thought to ask, because there is nothing on this reach to ask.

She stood at the trestle until it had gone down to the stones and then she walked back to the third hut and it was not warmer, and it was the same one she has slept in since the second of a month.

## What Four Months Of It Cost

She did the sum standing up at about the fourth hour on the ninth of a month, without doing it on paper, because there is nothing within nine days of road to check a sum against.

Inside the bound she paid five coppers a day for the right to be inside a ring of eight feet, for eleven days, and she was still colder at it than she is in a hut with three boards in it.

Past the post the ring is free and she is still colder at it than she is in a hut with three boards in it.

That is the sum and it comes out the same at both ends, and the five coppers bought her a place at a fire that was not warm, and she paid it for eleven days and did not know at the time that the fire was not warm.

Somebody had told her at a bread counter that she could stop standing in the middle of a floor, and it had been the largest single kindness anybody has done her in four years, and it cost that woman a pot and a pan, and she is on the other side of the flat from both of them.

She is colder now than she was there and the remedy costs nothing, and the difference is that she has gone from paying for the wrong thing to standing in it for nothing.

## The Wanting

She wanted a pot and a shelf and a fire that was hers.

That is all. Not a person. Not a document. A pot under a shelf and a fire behind it that she did not have to pay five coppers a day to stand near, and a counter she could walk up to at the seventh hour and be told to stop standing in the middle of the floor.

She wanted it for about an hour on the ninth of the month in the third hut with her hands not working, and the wanting was so ordinary and so stupid that it embarrassed her, the way wanting something for a reason embarrasses you and wanting it for no reason does not.

She has four years of reasons. She has a claim and a course that closed its ground and a man in a plain coat and a brother whose register reads reconciled and a floor nine days west with nine men on it and a finger she has not got any more.

She did not want any of that at about the fourth hour of that night.

She wanted a pot.

She did not say it out loud to anybody, because there is nobody within nine days of road to say it to, and because the only person who could hear her has no body and cannot do anything about a pot.

## Where It Leaves Her

The ninth of a month was a day like every day since the second. She is in a term with a date at both ends of it, in a column that is hers, at five coppers, on a bank, with a stub coming on the seventeenth.

And the only thing that changed about the cold between the two sides of a line of work is which of them charges her for it.

Four hundred yards up that track there is a low room with a counter in it and a stove and six men, and the man at the counter reads papers out loud, and on the seventh of that month she is going to go into it.
