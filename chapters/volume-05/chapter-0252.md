# The Price Of Acting

She asked it at the counter on the twelfth of the month at about the seventh hour, and she asked it in the ordinary voice, and it was one question.

“What would it take.”

He did not have to look anything up. He put his hand on the sheet the way he had put it there two days before and he said it out loud at the speed a man reads so that the people behind him could hear it, and there were six of them behind him and none of them moved.

“A claim is a figure,” he said. “A figure is only paid against something. The only things on this side of that line a claim can be paid against are hours worked past the last working point. And there is one run of those with dates in it on this reach and it is in a book four hundred yards down that track with a woman's name at the top of it.”

He put his hand off the paper.

“That is her column.”

## The Six Men Behind Him

Two of them had been in the room the whole time she was at the counter and neither of them had moved and neither of them had looked at her, and that is what a room full of men looks like when a woman is standing in it and is not doing anything they are waiting for.

One of them worked in the yard at the mill and one of them kept the ledger at the mill and the other four are the same four every time she has been in here, which is twice.

They have all heard papers read out loud. That is a room where papers get read out loud and it is not a room where anything happens while a paper is being read, and it went on behind her the way a fire goes on behind a conversation.

She got the whole of the price in about forty words from a man who was reading off a sheet, in front of six people who were not listening, at about the seventh hour on the twelfth of a month.

## What He Did Not Say

He did not say one word about a person.

That is the thing to hold on to in that room. In about forty words he set out the price of the only thing he can do and he did not say her name, and he did not say that the woman with the column is in the room, and he did not look at her when he said it, and he did not stop reading to find out whether she was still standing at the counter.

He has not got a warrant and he has got a sheet and the sheet does not have her name on the top of it, or anywhere she has seen. A claim is a figure and a figure is paid against hours and the hours are in a book four hundred yards down the track and the woman who owns them is somewhere on this reach and is not this person, as far as anything in that room knows.

If she had walked in there and said her name, the six men at the back would have had to work out what it meant and the man at the counter would have had to stop reading his sheet, and one of those two things would have cost them an afternoon.

She did not give it to them.

## In The Same Breath

“Nobody in this room is going to take a person's hours off a piece of paper that somebody else read out loud in a room,” he said. “If she ever says yes they will take them. And until she says it the claim stands and it costs us nothing to stand.”

Then he went back to his book.

He did not say it twice and he did not say it in a different voice and he did not sell it. He had said the whole of what that room knew about the thing and about the price of it in about forty words, and then he had gone back to the work the room was open for, and one of the six men at the back asked him about a cart and he answered that.

That is the resistance and it is not a wall.

There is no version of this in which the man at that counter is the enemy of anything. He is not refusing her. He is not protecting her. He has told her the price of the only thing he is able to do and he has told her that he is not permitted to do it without her, and both of those are the ordinary facts of a room that can act on a figure.

It is an offer with a price on it, made by people who are right to make it.

## What It Is

The only thing in this country that can be charged for is a day somebody worked.

That is the whole of it and she worked out how long ago she had stopped being able to be surprised by a fact of that shape.

She came across three-quarters of a mile of the worst ground on nine days in the dark on the second of a month because a claim of eight hundred jin was travelling on a licence she had never opened, and because the county that had the answer was nine days east and the line's own people did not know she existed, and because being outside was the only place where nobody could put her hours into anything.

And the ground past the last working point is not a place where nothing is charged. It is the only place within nine days of road where the charge can be met at all.

The instrument she ran from is the instrument that pays it.

And she is the only person in the world who can hand that instrument over, and there is not one other person on this reach who can, and the four men who wrote the marks on the stubs in her oilcloth cannot and the six men behind that counter cannot and the man who keeps the board on the post cannot and a man in a plain coat standing nine feet off a trestle in a wind cannot.

It is hers. It is four hundred yards away in a book with a date at each end of it and her name at the top.

## What She Said

She said no.

Once, in her own head, in about nine words, with no speech of any kind, standing at a counter in a room with a stove in it at about the seventh hour on the twelfth of a month.

She did not say it out loud and she did not say it to him and she did not say it to the six men and she did not write it anywhere. She has said no to a thing like this three times in four years and this is the fourth, and it took nine seconds and there was nothing in it.

It closed nothing she can see. Hou Sanliang is still unpaid and still patient and has still never met her, and a date is still entered against that figure in a counting room in Lowwater by a hand that is not the counting room's, and it has not been torn off and it is not going to be torn off by anything she does or does not do on this reach.

The bill is still hers. She has just found out who the creditor's clerk is.

## The Walk Back

Four hundred yards down a cart track, past the post with the board on it, in the dark, with the bar over her shoulder.

She did not go west. She did not stand at the post. She did not think about going back, and the road is shut for the season and has been shut since the carts stopped and there is not a man on this reach with a date for it.

He said one thing and it was in the ordinary voice and it was not advice and it bought nothing.

“You have said it once now,” he said, “and a thing you have said once can be said again in a different room with a different voice in it.”

She did not answer him.

She went down to the trestle at about the ninth hour and the man at the plank was still on the book and the book was open on the plank and the column on the near side of it had a name at the top of it and dates going down it in order and a far date written in a hand that was not hers at the far end of the run.

## The Last Page

She put her hand flat on the book for about as long as it takes to read a line.

The book is a wage-book kept by people who are not a county and are not obliged to keep it, and the column on the near side of the plank is a column of hours, and it is hers, and there is a name at the top of it in a hand with the heel gone that has been there since the second of a month.

It goes up a stair on the seventh of a month to a room in the low building four hundred yards away, and the room puts it on a spike with other sheets, and one of the sheets has a figure on it that belongs to the woman whose name is at the top of that column.

That is the whole of what she knows and every part of it came out of an ordinary room in about forty words.

She did not open the licence. She has not opened it in four years and she did not open it on the twelfth of a month with the price of the claim in her head.

She did not write anything anywhere. There were nine stubs in the oilcloth against her ribs in four marks and a plate under all of them and a hut with three boards in it four hundred yards east.

She was Foundation nine, and there is nothing above nine anywhere in the world, and the only place on this reach where anybody could ever act on her is four hundred yards behind her at a counter, and the only thing they would act on is a column of hours that is hers.

She went to the hut.

The season runs to the end of the carts, the road that goes back inside is shut, and there is a thing that has to be written and has not been written. The only thing that has changed on this reach since the second of a month is that an office four hundred yards away now knows exactly which book it would have to open.
