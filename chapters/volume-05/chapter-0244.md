# A Light In The Tower

There was a fire under the trestle on the twenty-fifth because the men were cold and there was a hauler at it who she had not seen before.

He was about sixty and he had a coat with a stone-dust track up the back of it that went as far as one shoulder and not the other, and he had been at the pot from about the sixth hour and he had been talking about a job the way men talk about a job they have finished with.

Not to her. He had not looked at her once that she caught, and she had been sitting in the gap at the side of the trestle for about a quarter of an hour before she understood that everything he said was being said at the fire.

She listened to it going past her the way you listen to weather.

## The Whole Of The Evening

There were five of them and it went on for about two hours and it was the first conversation she has sat inside since the bad flat.

She had forgotten what it is like to be in a room where nobody is talking to her.

It is not restful. It is the opposite of restful. Every one of them was talking across her and over her and past her, and the two hours were about a cart at the mill and a debt between two of them and a brother-in-law and a price for threshing, and she understood about half of it and had no part in any of it, and it went on exactly as it would have gone on if she had got up and gone.

Inside the bound every room she has stood in has turned. A registrar’s hall. A hall with no roof. A foreman’s gate. A counter. A county office. A room with nine rows in it and a man with a door.

The first thing she had done here was to work out how to be in a room without turning it, and she had got it right, and the cost of getting it right is that she spent two hours in a room on the far side of a line of work hearing nine days of road go past her.

## Why He Said It There

Nobody at that pot has ever met Feng Lushi.

The man of sixty has hauled to that rise about nine years and has never been past the first landing of that stair and does not know what is at the top of it and could not tell you the name of the man who works there.

He was talking about a job he had finished with, the way men talk about a job they have finished with, which is that it turns into a thing that belongs to everybody and to nobody about two weeks after you have stopped doing it.

That is why it came to her. That is the whole of the mechanism. There was no act and no favour and no man deciding to bring her a fact about nine days of road. There were five men at a fire and one of them had been somewhere and was tired of it.

## What He Said About The Light

“There is a tower up on your side of that rise,” he said, to the pot, “and there is a light in it.”

The pot went on saying it.

There were four men at that fire and not one of them was waiting for him to finish.

“It does not stop,” he said. “I hauled up there four times last season and it is on at the fourth hour and it is still on at the ninth and it is on when I came down. A man in there copies. He has done it since before I came and he has not stopped and none of us ever went up and asked him what for, because he is not a man you go up and ask.”

He put his hand round the pot.

“You could stand at that fire four nights in a row,” he said, “and it would be the same light, and it would be on, and that is the whole of what I know about it.”

## What He Said About The Stair

“And the stair it goes down is the only stair on that floor,” he said. “He comes down it with a bundle in his arms about the seventh hour and he goes up it about the eighth, and there is a clerk at the top of that building whose arms go up four flights with the bundle and come down empty.”

“Comes down empty,” the man beside him said.

“Comes down empty,” the hauler said. “Nine years I have been putting my back to that ground and I have seen those arms go up with a bundle and come down without one about a hundred times, and not one of us ever asked where the bundle went, because where it went is upstairs and upstairs is not a thing you ask a man about who has not asked you a thing.”

He said it as a fact about a job he had finished with. He said it the way the keeper says a thing about the ground, in the ordinary voice, because it was interesting for about a minute and then it was not.

## What He Said About The Floor

“Your side is a floor of nine,” he said. “It is the same nine it was last season. I have been up and down it on loads and there has not been a man off it and there has not been a man on it I did not see there last year.”

She stopped breathing for about half of what he said next, which was about a second and a half, and then she started again at the same count.

They were all still on it.

Nine men on a floor nine days west and every one of them still on it. The tower with a light in it that goes on at the fourth hour. The arms going up four flights and coming down empty.

Every one of those things had been true a month ago and had not been checked by her since the second of a month, and was now being checked, without her, by a man of sixty who had never been inside the building and did not know one single thing about the man who copies in it.

## What She Did Not Ask

There were four men at that pot and there was a fifth and she was at the side of it.

She could have asked one question. A woman who has worked nine days of ground can ask a hauler about a tower without it being anything, because everybody who works this reach has hauled up that rise at some point and a question about a light in a window is the most ordinary question there is.

And the second question would have been the one that mattered, and there was no second question that could be asked by a woman who has never been on that floor in her life.

She sat in the gap at the side of the trestle for about another hour and a half and she listened to a man of sixty talk about a job he had finished with, and the wind came off the rise, and at about the ninth hour four of them went and the fire went down to the stones in it, and she was the last one there and then she was not the last one there.

## The Rest Of The Evening

She walked the two miles back with the bar over her shoulder and she worked the three things over on the way, in the ordinary order, the way she works everything over.

A light that does not stop means a man is copying at the fourth hour and has not stopped, and a man who has not stopped in nine years is a man who has been asked to keep a hand steady by somebody he works for and is not being paid for it and is not going to be told.

Arms up four flights with a bundle and down without one means there is a room at the top of that building and something is going into the room and not coming out of it in the ordinary way, and everybody on that floor has seen it and not one of them has ever asked.

Nine men and the same nine means nobody has left, which means the ground is working, which means a floor of nine is on a floor and not off one, and she has not thought about that in three months because there had been no way to think about it.

Then she got to the hut with three boards in it and it was the same and she was the same and there was not one piece of paper anywhere within nine days of road that any of it was on.

## What It Is To Be Nine Days Off

She has been nine days from that floor since the second of a month and she has not been able to do one single thing about it in any direction, and she has not asked for that to change, because asking for it to change costs the arrangement, and the arrangement is a wage-book and a bar and a bank at the fourth hour.

And on the twenty-fifth evening a man she had met once, in the last hour of a day, told her three facts about it in the time it takes to eat bread, and none of the three was addressed to her, and she is going to be carrying all three for about a day and a half.

She wanted to be told one more thing about that floor by somebody who had seen it this week.

There is not one person within nine days of road who has seen it this week and is standing at a fire she can get to, and the only two ways of finding out are to ask a question she cannot ask or to go nine days east on a road that is shut for the season.

## What She Took Away

A light that does not stop. Arms going up four flights and coming down empty. Nine men and the same nine.

That is the whole of the news from inside the bound since she crossed and it is not good news and it is not bad news and it is the only news there is, and it came to her at the seventh hour in the mouth of a man who does not know her name and does not know where she was on any of the days he is talking about.

She did not write any of it down.

She had a back of a dead weight ticket to write it on and a flat of a second finger and about eleven minutes a line and she did not write one word of it anywhere, because the three facts are about nine men and she has got them in her head and she is not going to put them on paper where somebody at a plank can come across them.

The twenty-seventh is a pay-day. The man at the plank can tell her what happens to a book when the carts stop.
