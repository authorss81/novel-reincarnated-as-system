# The Room Can Act

She went up the cart track on the tenth of the month at about the seventh hour, past the post with the board on it, and into the low room four hundred yards beyond it.

A month ago she stood nine feet off the outside of that door for about four minutes and did not go in, and she worked out on a stone, in about nine seconds, why.

There is no version of a woman with four fingers’ worth of grip on one hand, and eleven holds in the other, and no pot, and no household, and a licence she has never opened, becoming somebody that room has to act for.

That has changed.

It has changed in the worst direction, and she knew it before she was through the door, and she went through the door anyway. She is a woman with a season in a wage-book on this reach and a run of days in one hand with a second date at the far end of it, and she is the most findable person within nine days of road and the least protected.

## The Walk Up

Four hundred yards of cart track and then the post, and she did not stop at the post.

There is a board on that post and a keeper who comes up it with a cart every nine or ten days, and on the tenth of the month she walked past it without slowing and did not look at it, and that was a thing she had decided about two hundred yards back.

She is not going to stand in front of it again. She stood in front of it for two hours on the twenty-sixth of a month a month and a half back and got ninety parts of an answer out of a man who works nine feet away from it every day of his life, and there is a tenth part and it is not coming off that board, and there is no instrument within nine days of road that would get it off.

The walk is about eleven minutes and she was at the door of the low room at about the seventh hour of a clear evening.

She had nine minutes to decide what she was going to say in there, and she used about four of them on a question about a book, and she put the other five where she has put everything for four years, which is nowhere.

## The Room

Same room. Same counter. Same stove with a plate on it and a pot that had been going all day. Same six men standing about nine feet back, two of them on a bench and the rest on their feet, and the same fire in the same place in the room.

The man at the counter was the man who had read it out loud a month ago.

He had a sheet of paper in front of him and a book and he did not look up when she came in, and he finished the line he was on, and then he looked up, and he waited.

The room did not stop. That was the thing she was not ready for and it took her about ten seconds to get used to. Six men in a room do not go quiet because a woman walks into it, because a woman has walked into it before at about the seventh hour and read a counter and gone out again, and a room is a room.

## The One Question

She had come with a question and it was the wrong one and she left it where it was.

She asked the question a worker asks at a counter, in the ordinary voice, with her hands down.

“Is the work out past the post written down anywhere else on this side.”

He looked at her for about the length of a line.

“The trestle book,” he said. “There is not another one on this reach. The yard keeps a day book for the stone and the trestle keeps the wages and they do not talk to each other and they do not need to.”

That was the answer. It took four words and it was complete, and he had given it to her because she asked, and he had given her nothing else.

She did not say her name. She did not say the figure on the spike is hers. She asked a question about a book and she got an answer about a book.

## What She Wanted To Say And Did Not

There were three sentences in her when she was at the counter and not one of them was about books.

The first one was her name. The second one was that the figure on the spike has been hers since she was sixteen and that it was entered in a counting room in Lowwater by a hand that is not the counting room’s. The third one was that she had crossed three-quarters of a mile of the worst ground on nine days on the second of a month with her hands cut to get to a room where nobody had ever heard of her.

She gave none of the three.

She asked a woman-at-a-counter’s question about a wage-book and got a woman’s answer about a wage-book and stood there for four more minutes, and then she asked the only other one there was.

## Then He Went Back To The Sheet

And that is where the thing happened, and it happened because she had asked him a question and because he is a man who works at a counter and because there was a woman standing at it at the seventh hour on the tenth of a month.

He put his hand flat on the sheet.

“It is entered in good order,” he said, “and it travels with the licence, and it is not affected by the relicensing, and it stands.”

He said it the way he said the conditions a month ago, which is out loud, in the ordinary voice, at the speed a man reads so that the people behind him can hear which part he is on.

“It is actionable on this side,” he said.

He did not look at her when he said that and he did not look at the six men behind him either. He had a sheet of paper in front of him and he read a line off it and he read it out loud because that is what happens at that counter.

“I will tell you what it has been since the twenty-ninth of a month back,” he said. “It has been on this spike with the other sheets and it has not gone anywhere and I am not the office that answers it and I have not sent it anywhere because I am not the office that answers it.

There is no office in nine days of road behind me that answers it and there is one in nine days of road in front of me that can, and this is the first of them in the whole life of that figure.”

Then he put his hand off the paper and looked at her again.

“You are asking about books,” he said.

“I am asking about books.”

“Well,” he said, “there you are.”

## Why He Said It To A Woman About Books

She worked it out on the track on the way down and it came out in about nine minutes.

A counter reads papers out loud. That is not a thing he did for a woman and it is not a thing he did because of anything about her. There were six men in that room and one of them had asked him about a cart about a week ago, and the figure has been on that spike for a month and has been read out loud in that room to whoever was standing there, and on the tenth of a month a woman was standing there.

If she had come in on the seventh instead of the tenth, or on the seventeenth, it would have been said to a person who was not her.

That is what an ordinary voice in an ordinary room is. It is a room doing what it does on a day.

## What It Is

The claim stands.

It is on a spike in a low room with a stove in it, in good order, travelling on a paper she has never opened, and it is eight hundred jin, and it has been standing since she was sixteen, and it has been sitting in a room that can do something about it for a month and has done nothing about it and is not going to be stopped by her.

And she did not say her name, and the man at the counter did not ask her for it, and no man in that room has ever been told whose figure it is, and they are six men and a book and a fire and the reason that claim has not moved is not anybody in that room.

A claim entered in good order, standing, actionable, and read out loud in the ordinary voice of a man checking a thing, in front of six people who have heard papers read out loud before and were not interested in it.

She has carried that figure for two years. She is eighteen years old. She walked nine days of road and three-quarters of a mile of the worst ground on it to get away from a thing that had gone past every office she had ever stood in front of and landed in a room with a stove in it.

And she had wanted, for two years, an office that could answer it. There is one four hundred yards behind her.

The only thing it has instead of an answer is an ability.

## What She Felt

She was not frightened and she was not proud.

She was angry, in the plain and complete way, standing at a counter about four hundred yards from a plank that had her name at the top of a column in it.

And she was angry at herself, which was new, and it took the whole of the walk back down the track.

For four years she had wanted the office and the answer confused. She had wanted a room that could act and a thing she could not be got for to act on, and she had crossed a flat in the dark to get to one, and she had got to it, and the room can act.

The difference between what she wanted and what she has is about nine feet of wind and a door.

He had said one price in this room and it was not a price of money, and he had not said it yet, and he had not said it because she had asked about books.

She had one question left and it was the only one that anybody in that room is able to answer, and she was still standing at the counter.
