# The Man the Board Keeps

The row comes down past him once a day.

It comes the way it has come since before he had a Chosen One, since before he had a name he could keep, four or five breaths, never missed, asking nothing and taking nothing. Something walks it and looks at him. He cannot see who writes it back. He is read by the layer, not reading it.

This time the row stops.

Yuan stands in the layer’s own reading-space, which is not a room the way rooms are rooms, and it is a room the way only this place is a room. White lines going out in all directions, entries hung on them the way washing is hung on lines, dates at the left, figures that are not measurements of ground. A board at the far end keeps what a keeper past the line pastes onto it, strip by strip, every nine or ten days whether or not there is anything to put in it. He has watched that board for years without ever being on it.

Today there is a hand at the board.

The hand belongs to a keeper of about fifty-five who has kept a board nine years and never read a word of it. The hand holds a strip wet with paste. The hand does not know what it carries. On the strip, among marks nobody on the reach can read, is a line in her column, dates in order, hours against them, season at each end, name at the top in two hands. Her hours. Her season. Her name.

The hand waits.

What Yuan feels is fear, plain and cold, the way he felt it on the terrace when she told him to stop describing things in images. He has no hands, no body, no qi of his own, no way to speak to anyone but her, and now the layer is offering him a way to be spoken by someone else, and the price is on the table before the line goes in.

The price is this. His unentered state. The date of the morning he died.

He keeps the apology. He loses the day it happened on.

He understands the rule perfectly. If the keeper’s hand enters him as a readable line, her hours hold past the line without her, and an office past the line can read him without her from that hour. Ledger as a standing held stage ends with this volume. What replaces it is his readability. That is the sentence the layer puts in front of him, and he reads it the way he reads everything, as a man who treats people as entries and calls it love.

He also understands that it makes him findable. That is what the ending needs, when he has been doing the readable thing on purpose since long after now, when the Court finds him, when the fall needs a man the reader has seen in a room. He does not know any of that yet. He knows only that being read without her means being somewhere without her, and he has never been anywhere without her since the first night.

That is the whole of what he has to decide, and it sits in him the way stone sits in a sledge.

He wants, concretely, for her hours to hold. That is the want, small and his, and it holds through the fear.

## The Hand

The keeper’s hand moves to the board. The paste is wet. The strip is ready. The layer waits the way layers wait, without patience and without impatience, because waiting is what a layer is for.

Yuan steps forward in the only way he can step, which is to turn his attention toward the line and let it take him.

“I am here,” he says. “I am willing. I am entered.”

Every sentence he speaks is present tense. No other sentence in this room is his.

The hand presses the strip to the board. The paste takes. The line goes in among the lines, dates at the left, hours against them, and with it goes a second line, finer, in no hand anyone living uses, a readable line that is him, entered so that her hours hold.

The cost comes after, the way costs come after stages, in the order a body must be moved in, empty first and then take.

The unentered state goes first. He feels it go the way she felt the turn go out of her stroke, not weakened, gone. He has been unentered since he woke as a voice, lodged in the announcement layer, read every day and never written, and now he is written, and the unwritten place in him closes.

The date goes second. The morning he died. Nineteen years old, badly, on the wrong side of a sect that was about to take his core, last coherent thought an apology. He keeps the apology. He loses the day. He reaches for the morning and finds the apology without the date under it, the way a book holds a line with the date torn off, and he knows he will never again know which morning it was.

He stands in the reading-space with the row stopped over him, four or five breaths stretched long, and lets it happen without stopping it, because stopping was never offered.

## The Reading

Something walks the row once more and looks at him, and this time it sees more than it saw before.

Before this hour it saw a voice lodged in the layer, read every day, never written. From this hour it sees a line that can be read from outside the bound without her, by an office past the line, in the layer’s own reading. He can be read without her. That is the consequence and it is his.

He stands in the room that is not a room and feels the new state settle the way days settle at fires, stone by stone, into something carried. He is entered. Ledger as a standing held stage is ended. What replaces it is his readability, and it will hold to the end of the book, through Hand and Door and Court and Announcement, until the fall.

What he feels then is not relief. It is grief, plain and unadorned, for a date he will never again know, and under the grief a steadiness he did not expect, the kind that comes when a thing is done and cannot be undone.

He speaks once more, to no one, to the row, to the board, to the hand that has already gone back up the track with an empty paste pot.

“I hold,” he says. “I am held. I am here.”

The row moves on. Four or five breaths end. The reading-space empties the way it always empties, without sound and without witness.

Far east past the line, a woman walks out of a bound with a paper left behind her, asking nothing, entered, and her hours hold behind her because a man let a hand enter him as a line so that they would.

He did it on purpose, in daylight, and nobody persuaded him.
