# The Man the Board Keeps

The row comes down past him once a day.

It comes the way it has come since before he had a Chosen One, since before he had a name he could keep, four or five breaths, never missed, asking nothing and taking nothing. Something walks it and looks at him. He cannot see who writes it back. He is read by the layer, not reading it.

This time the row stops.

Yuan stands in the layer’s own reading-space, which is not a room the way rooms are rooms, and it is a room the way only this place is a room. White lines going out in all directions, entries hung on them the way washing is hung on lines, dates at the left, figures that are not measurements of ground.

A board at the far end keeps what a keeper past the line pastes onto it, strip by strip, every nine or ten days whether or not there is anything to put in it. He has watched that board for years without ever being on it.

Today there is a hand at the board.

The hand belongs to a keeper of about fifty-five who has kept a board nine years and never read a word of it. The hand holds a strip wet with paste. The hand does not know what it carries. On the strip, among marks nobody on the reach can read, is a line in her column, dates in order, hours against them, season at each end, name at the top in two hands. Her hours. Her season. Her name.

The hand waits.

## What He Is For

He has been a standing thing in the Ledger since the first night, held in place by her attention, the way a man is held in place in a room by whoever is in the room with him. He has never been written. A voice lodged in a layer and read every day is not the same as a line on a board, and every day for years he has been the difference between those two things in a place where the difference is the whole of what a life is.

The keeper’s hand would make him the second thing. A line on a board, in ink, with a date at the left of it, where anybody with eyes can find it.

What Yuan feels is fear, plain and cold, the way he felt it on the terrace when she told him to stop describing things in images. He has no hands, no body, no qi of his own, no way to speak to anyone but her, and now the layer is offering him a way to be spoken by someone else, and the price is on the table before the line goes in.

The price is this. His unentered state. The date of the morning he died.

He keeps the apology. He loses the day it happened on.

He works the rest of it out the way he works everything, standing still, letting the terms come to him instead of going to them. If the hand enters him, her hours hold past the line without her. An office past the line can read him without her, from that hour, and reading him does not require her to be standing anywhere at all.

The Ledger stops holding him. He has been held there as a standing in a book for four years by one woman’s attention, and the attention is a hand on his collar, and hands get tired, and a line on a board does not get tired.

He also works out the other half of it, and this is the half the layer does not put on the table.

Being read means being findable. A line on a board can be looked at by an office, and an office that looks at a line sends for the line, and being sent for is a thing that happens to men in rooms with doors. He has never been anywhere without her. He has been a voice with her and a voice in her head and a voice in the place between, and everywhere he has ever been, she has been the reason he could be there at all.

That is what he is being asked to give up, and the layer does not ask it in those words because the layer does not use words for the things it takes.

## The Want

What he wants is her hours. Not her safety, which he has never been able to move by wanting it. Not the claim, which is a piece of paper moving between offices and has never once moved because she was frightened. Her hours, specifically, the run of them in the first book with dates in it, because that run is the only thing standing between eight hundred jin and a man she left standing in a county that could not pay it.

If she goes back nine days west and finishes that floor, and then walks off and does not come back, and does not stand the hours, the hours stop. The figure dies on a board in a room with a stove in it, and nothing in the world ever hears of it again, and no book anywhere records that eight hundred jin was ever owed.

So he wants the hours to hold past her. He wants them to hold in a place where she is not required to be present. He wants, for the first time in four years, to be the instrument instead of the thing instrumented.

That want is small and it is his and it holds straight through the fear, which is not a small thing and does not.

## The Hand

The keeper’s hand moves to the board. The paste is wet. The strip is ready. The layer waits the way layers wait, without patience and without impatience, because waiting is what a layer is for.

Yuan steps forward in the only way he can step, which is to turn his attention toward the line and let it take him.

“I am here,” he says. “I am willing. I am entered.”

The hand presses the strip to the board. The paste takes. The line goes in among the lines, dates at the left, hours against them, and with it goes a second line, finer, in no hand anyone living uses, a readable line that is him, entered so that her hours hold.

The cost comes after, the way costs come after stages, in the order a body must be moved in, empty first and then take.

The unentered state goes first. He feels it go the way she felt the turn go out of her stroke, not weakened, gone. He has been unentered since he woke as a voice, lodged in the layer, read every day and never written, and now he is written, and the unwritten place in him closes over like water closing over a stone.

The date goes second. The morning he died. Nineteen years old, badly, on the wrong side of a sect that was about to take his core, last coherent thought an apology.

That was the price taken exactly as it had been named. He keeps the apology. He loses the day.

He reaches for the morning the way a hand reaches for a door frame in the dark, and the frame is there and it is not. The apology is there, whole, exactly as he left it, the last thing he had managed to be.

Underneath it there is no morning. There is no light in the room and no open door and no hour. He had a day once, and it was the day of a thing that happened, and now he has the thing and not the day, and he will not have the day again for as long as there is anything left of him to have it in.

## The Reading

Something walks the row once more and looks at him, and this time it sees more than it saw before.

Before this hour it saw a voice lodged in the layer, read every day, never written. From this hour it sees a line that can be read from outside the bound without her, by an office past the line, in the layer’s own reading. He can be read without her. That is the consequence and it is his.

He stands in the room that is not a room and feels the new state settle into him, into something carried, and he is entered, and the Ledger is no longer holding him as a standing in a book, because the strip on the board holds him now and the strip does not need her to look at it.

What he feels then is not relief. It is grief, plain and unadorned, for a morning he will never again know, and under the grief a steadiness he did not expect, the kind that comes when a thing is done and cannot be undone.

He speaks once more, to no one, to the row, to the board, to the hand that has already gone back up the track with an empty paste pot.

“I hold,” he says. “I am held. I am here.”

The row moves on. Four or five breaths end. The reading-space empties the way it always empties, without sound and without witness.

A keeper of about fifty-five walks down off a ladder with a paste pot in one hand and a rag in the other and does not know what he has done, and goes home along a track with the cart empty, and the board keeps one more line than it kept yesterday.

Nine days east, a woman is walking. She does not know it has happened yet. That is the arrangement, and he chose it, and it was the only thing on the table that was for her.
