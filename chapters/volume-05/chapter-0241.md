# The Number In Her Hand

She worked the fifteenth and the sixteenth and the seventeenth, and on the seventeenth she stood at the plank at about the seventh hour and the man at the plank put three lines of the day in front of her and did not look up, and then he went to the back of the cart and got what was in the box.

The reaches are written down at the top of the book. That is the first thing she ever looked at properly on the day she got here, because it was the only thing on the page that was not a day or an hour.

Pays fall on the seventh, the seventeenth and the twenty-seventh. There is no other line under it and there is no note against it.

She had the seventh in the book in her own hand with the six days before it in her own hand, and the twentieth, and the seventh again, and the seventeenth was the third time the same thing had happened and she had not seen the pattern until a man at a plank used two more days on it.

It is a ten-day instalment and there are three of them in a month, and every man at that yard has been paid on those three since before she came, and the day a man can move is not a day a man can move.

## The One Question

She asked one question in three days and she asked it at the plank at about the sixth hour on the sixteenth with the pen not in her hand.

“Can a day be moved.”

He looked up. It is the first time he had looked up at her in eleven days and he did it for about the length of a line and then went back to the page.

“There is no such thing,” he said.

He said it in the ordinary voice and he said it as a fact about the book and not as a thing put to her, and he did not explain it, and she did not ask him again, because he had answered a question and the answer was four words and she had got what she came for.

There is no carrying forward. There is no carrying back. The hours in the column are the hours in the column and they are the hours the wage is for and there is nobody at that plank who has the power to put an hour in on a day the day was not worked on.

And that was the whole of it.

She cannot borrow against the seventeenth and there is nothing to borrow against. There is no purse behind it and no tin and no second wage and no man on that reach who has a day he could do without, and the one hundred and forty odd coppers in a wash house nine days west have been gone since the evening of a day in a month that is four months back.

## The Column, Going Down

She had been in a room once with nine rows of figures in it and a man with a door.

That is what the far-side book is and it is the same object as nine counties and a district and a course, and the reason nobody on nine days of road has ever heard of her is that in all of them she is a row, and a row is a thing that arrives and goes and the office does not need to know a name to move it.

Here she is a row too. The difference is that a row on this side of a line of work is a woman’s whole reason for being in a country.

The pay is the wage for the days in the column and the column is the only evidence there is. If a day is not in there then the day did not happen and there is no second copy of it anywhere on this reach and no man alive can put it back.

## What She Took

Ten days at five is fifty coppers of work and ten days of bread at five is fifty coppers of food, and the number in her hand at the start of a day was the same as the number in her hand at the end of it.

She did not stand at that plank and do it out loud and she did not do it twice.

She had stood at another counter two months back with shelves behind it and a woman behind the counter who told her to stop standing in the middle of her floor, and she had done this exact sum there with these exact two numbers and come out in this exact place, and the whole of what had changed between that counter and this one was the ground she had crossed in the dark on her hands and cut.

She had been certain the first time. She was certain the second time and being certain twice was worse than being certain once, because the first time had cost her something to learn and the second time had cost her nothing and had still worked.

She spent it on the days it was earned for and she put nothing by.

## The Ten Days

There are ten days in an instalment and she knows which ten because the book knows.

From the fourth to the thirteenth. The seventh is in the middle of it. Then the fourteenth to the twenty-third, then the twenty-fourth to the third of the next month. That is the shape of three instalments in a month of thirty and it is the shape of every country she has ever had wages in except this one, where the two books each ran for part of a month and neither of them told her what the other one was doing.

She got used to the ten inside four days and then stopped noticing it, in about the way she stopped noticing the five coppers, which is to say she noticed it every single day and never once found it interesting.

The interesting part is not the interval. The interesting part is that the interval is a line of somebody else’s making, and that the wage on a given morning is decided by a set of days that were fixed before she came here, and that there is no version of any of this in which she can change it.

That is the whole of a pay-day and it took a book and a man at it to teach her.

## What A Man At A Plank Does

He tore a strip off the book at the rule when he was done and put it on the plank face down and went and got a pot.

That is a counterfoil. It is about nine inches by two, off the same paper as the book, and it has the date on it and the stretch and the hours and the rate and his mark in the corner, and it goes face down on the plank and into a coat.

There are seven of them in an oilcloth against her ribs now. The six she crossed the flat with are in three marks and this one is a fourth, and none of them can be checked against anything by anybody, because the man who wrote it is the man who made it and there is no second copy of it inside nine days of road.

A mark nobody checks is worth exactly what the man who made it is worth, and that was worked out on a stone two months ago at the other end of nine days and it has not changed and it is not going to.

## What She Wants

She wanted, on the sixteenth evening in a hut with three boards in it, to have one day in hand that she had not worked.

She had never wanted that in her life. She had been paid by the day for four years and had eaten every day of it and had never once thought about the day after tomorrow, because there had always been something between her and the day after tomorrow.

On the far side of a line of work there is nothing between her and it.

The day after tomorrow is a line in a book with a date at the left of it and the space for a wage in it, and the wage goes in when the day is worked and does not go in otherwise.

And the line is there in the morning, on the seventeenth and on the eighteenth and on the nineteenth, and it will be there on the morning after the pay, and there is no version of this in which she stands at a plank and says that a day is spoken for.

She wanted it and she could not have it and there was no way to buy it.

On the seventeenth she had about five coppers in her hand at about the fourth hour and about five coppers in her hand at about the eighth hour, and she did not find that remarkable any more, and she had stopped finding it remarkable somewhere in the third week of doing it, which was on the other side of the rise and not here.

## What It Is Now

She is not hungry. She has not been hungry since the first of the Foundation stages, which is the better part of five months, and that has stopped being strange.

What arrived instead of the fear of not eating is the cold of depending on a number coming back on a day that cannot be moved, in a country where not one person has ever moved a day for anybody, for anybody at all, in nine days of road.

There is a difference between a wage you are owed and a wage that arrives, and the difference is not the money. The difference is that a wage that arrives is a thing other people are doing, and she has never in her life had one.

The seventeenth came at the seventh hour whether or not she had worked it, and she had worked it, and a stub came off the book at the rule and went face down on the plank, and the day after the seventeenth is the eighteenth and it wants the same thing out of her that the fifteenth did.

There is a bank of stone past the post with a heavy end on it and eleven men at it and no one at that yard is going to walk over and give a woman the heavy end.
