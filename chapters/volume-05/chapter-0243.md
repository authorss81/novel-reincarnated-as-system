# The Rate For A Road

The team went past the yard on the twenty-second at about the second hour with a load on it and a woman walking at the near end of the cart with her hand on the upright.

Wei Ran came through without permission and has never said when, and she said so in four words in a field two months back and has not elaborated on it since, and she was not going to elaborate on it now.

She slowed down for about the length of a cart being walked past a yard and she did not stop.

“You are still on that bank.”

“Yes.”

“You are on the heavy end.”

“Yes.”

“Nobody gave you the heavy end.”

“No.”

“That is correct,” Wei Ran said, and walked four more paces.

## What She Bought

There was a stone on the cart she had to get over the sill and she got a hand under it without asking anybody, and it went up, and she put her weight back on the upright and looked at her.

That is how a person gets something bought on this reach, and it had been how she got everything bought for four years, and she had it in her hand before the second length of cart had finished going past.

“I want to know what a cart costs on this ground,” she said.

Wei Ran came back four paces.

“Six a load to the mill, and the team is eight a day, and I run it every third day and there are four of these loads on a cart and there are two of us.” She said it at the same speed she was walking. “That is for a man with a bar and his own back. You would be cheaper than him and I would not come to you about it because I have a team.”

“Who is your team.”

“He is nineteen and he is my brother’s boy and he is worth the eight.”

That was the rate. She gave it in three sentences about a fact and not about her and she did not look at her while she said it.

## The Road

“There is a second thing you want,” Wei Ran said, “and I am going to say it once and then this cart is at the ramp.”

“The road that goes back inside.”

“The road in is shut,” Wei Ran said. “Shut for the season, the same as it is everywhere on this side, and I have no date for it and there is not a man on this reach with a date for it. Everybody goes the other way or does not go.”

“And when it opens.”

“Then a cart takes a person and one bag for ninety coppers off the far end of the reach and goes in about four days, and that is what it costs, and it is the same in whatever month it opens in.” She got her shoulder under the upright again.

“It is ninety and not twenty because it is four days and it is a bad road and it is not a favour. It has never been a favour. I have been up and down this ground in four years and I have said this to four women and two of them had worked the same stretch as you are working now.”

“You have said it to four women.”

“I have said it to four women and one man and I do it in about ten words and I say it at the same time as something else, and that is the whole of my trade on this reach,” Wei Ran said.

The cart went on up the track.

## What It Means

Ninety coppers.

At five a day that is eighteen days of the only thing she can sell, and she is nine days into a term of work on a bank on the far side of a line of work with eighteen days of it already behind her and the twenty-seventh of a month four days off.

It is not a price that stops her. It is a price she can put her hand on and count, and that is the part she did not expect.

Nobody else within nine days of road has one. The men at the trestle are all going the other way or are not going. The mill is four miles east and nobody four miles east has any reason to know what the road inside the line costs, and the line’s own people have not had a road to price since the season closed.

She has been on the far side of a line of work for nineteen days and the price of the going has been in her head the whole time as a thing that does not exist, because it does not exist, and a woman came past with a cart this morning and put a figure on it and walked on.

## What A Yard Costs To Run

That is the whole of his trade and he said it while walking and she has been turning it over since.

Six a load to the mill is not about stone. It is about a team of two and a cart and about a third of a day each way, and the eight a day is what a nineteen-year-old with his own back is worth and he is her brother’s boy, which means the eight is what a man is worth to a woman who owns him.

There is no part of that number that has anything to do with what a person can lift.

She has carried the other kind of arithmetic across nine days of road for two months and she knows exactly what it looks like when a body is priced against what it can produce.

On the far side of that line there is a bar and a ramp and a woman at five coppers and a man of forty with a bad shoulder. The man of forty is paid more than she is. He is right to be, and she is right to be, and neither of those two facts touches the other one at any point.

## The Number She Had Never Had

She stood on the step of that store at about the seventh hour with the ticket in her hand and worked out what ninety coppers is against five a day, and the answer came back in eighteen and it did not take her any time at all, and that was the part that frightened her.

Four years she has been in a world where every number she has ever been given about herself had a person behind it who wanted something. There is a figure of eight hundred jin that a man entered in a counting room. There is a ceiling on a licence. There is a count of days in a reason column a foot and a half wide.

Ninety coppers has nobody behind it. It is the price of a wheel on wet ground and four days of a bad road and a bag, and the woman who quoted it does not know where she sleeps and is not going to know and will not think about it again.

A number with nobody behind it is the only kind she has ever been able to trust, and she has had one for about two minutes.

## The Second Time She Saw The Cart

The team came back down the track at about the sixth hour on the twenty-fourth and went past the yard again with an empty bed on it, and Wei Ran was on the near side again this time and not on the other.

She did not stop.

She lifted a hand about four inches off the upright and put it back, which is what a carter does at a yard where she has worked before, and that is the whole of what passed between them, and it took about two seconds.

That is the second time in four years that a woman who goes up and down this ground has acknowledged that she was on it. The first was in a field at about the sixth hour three months back and it went like this and it has not been a thing since.

She went down the track to the mill with the empty cart and the wind behind her and she did not look back, and there was nothing in that either.

## The Ticket

She wrote it on the back of the third dead weight ticket at about the seventh hour, sitting on the step of the store with the bar across her knees.

The ration’s marks are on that ticket already and she wrote clear of every one of them, in the space above, with the flat of a second finger on the left hand at about eleven minutes a line.

Six a load. Eight a day for a man and his own back. Four of these loads on a cart. Ninety, west, for a person and one bag, and it is not a favour.

Four lines and about forty-five minutes, and the wind was coming off the rise and it was the kind of cold that makes the flat of a finger go first and it did.

Then she folded it and put it in the oilcloth above the plate with the rest, and she did not do a sum of it, and she did not compare it to anything.

There is nothing within nine days of road to compare a rate to.

## The Thing Under It

The second time a carter has offered her a number for a road and not a favour.

The first was at the cart turn, two months back, twenty coppers, and she did not take it up and she has not thought about it since except that she has just thought about it.

And this one was ten words said while walking, to four women and a man, at the same time as something else.

She does not know what to do with that and she is not going to find out what to do with that on a reach where not one person in a month has put a want of their own to her, and she got up off the step and put the bar on her shoulder and went back to the trestle.

The only price anybody on this side of the line can quote her for going back where she came from is the price of the going.

There is a pot under the trestle at the seventh hour and there is a man on this reach who has hauled to the line more times than she has been standing on this ground.
