# The Wash House Door

On the thirteenth a rain came in the night and the yard spent the morning on wedges and rails, because sound in the ground loosened fixings the way weather loosened them, and Da moved two men to the ramp boards to check them.

She held the bar for the lifting while a man half her age drove the wedges, and the furnace ran through the middle of the hold, steady and banked, and the rails came true. The work was ordinary and she worked it the way ordinary days were worked, stone after stone, breath in four counts, arm open a third of the time.

At midday the woman with the scarred forearm said her sister washed at a house nine days west and the door stood open for carriers’ women, the way women say things that are not invitations, and Ning Yan heard the wash house in it, the girl of twenty-four at a wash house, leg mended, copying lists, holding a bunk secret years back.

She said nothing with it. The wash house was nine days west, out of her reach, doing the only thing it was built to do. That was the whole of what it said this batch, once, by mouth, on a bank, and never as a second scene.

What she felt was tenderness, sudden and unguarded, for a girl with a lath and cord for a splint who had copied carriers’ lists fair for an estate that was not hers so nobody answered for it twice. She wanted, concretely, to send word that the door held. That was the want, and it had no instrument and no date, and she held it anyway.

## The Slab

In the afternoon a slab came off the bank wrong, edge under, weight sideways, and it went toward her foot the way slabs go when the bank is wet, without warning and without malice.

She moved the way she had moved since she was sixteen, bar over, weight turned, foot clear, and the slab went past her boot and settled against the sledge with a sound like a door shutting. Nobody saw it happen. There was no version of this where a man came, because the bank was hers and the hands were hers, four fingers and nine holds and a slowed finger and a furnace under them.

She stood with the bar in her hands breathing in four counts until the breath settled, and felt the risk settle with it, plain and physical. A furnace was not a mending and never offered to be one. A slowed finger was not a mending either. The body was what it was, and it held.

After the slab she spent an hour with the younger man checking the sledge runners, because a slab gone sideways strained fixings, and the runners had shifted a finger’s width on the near side. They righted it with the bar for the lifting and wedges for the seating, her hands on the bar while he drove the wedges, furnace running through the middle of the hold. Da walked past and looked at the seating and nodded once, and that was the whole of what the yard said about a slab missed and made good.

What she felt then was fear, clean and late, arriving after the danger the way fear arrives on banks, and under it relief that sat in the legs like water.

## The Evening

At the plank she wrote the date in, longer, permanently, hour gone into the writing, page held flat without being asked, column going down in order under the far date in his hand.

She put her hand flat on the book and felt the paper under the four fingers, and the column was hers, and that was the whole of what she knew.

He said one thing that evening and it was present tense and it bought nothing.

“You are whole,” he said. “And you are holding.”

The thirteenth closed on a woman with a furnace, a slowed finger, thirteen stubs, a slab missed, a wash house door open nine days west, father’s paper west, brother’s decade reachable, figure on a far-side book, season holding.

Physical risk with no rescue, and a real deadline in every gap no book could move. The batch had both, and more than one, and the road each way was the largest of them.
