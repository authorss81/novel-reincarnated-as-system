# Nobody Inside Can Read It

The boy came up the track on the nineteenth with a slate and said the six words and waited, and she sent him back the way she had sent him back before, and then she went up the track herself the following morning and stood at the counter.

“The office has entered her,” the boy had said, which was six words and was true, and she had wanted to know what she had been entered as for about thirty hours.

The man behind the counter had the book open flat in front of him and a sheet on the spike beside it. The stove was going. Six men at their cups in a room that smelled of wet coats and hot iron.

“Yesterday,” Ning Yan said. “You entered me.”

He turned the book round about four inches and put his thumb on a line near the bottom and said it in the ordinary voice, the way a man says a thing that is on his page and not a thing he has decided.

“Ning Yan. Past the last working point. Entered at the seventeenth. Good. Hours against her figure in order with dates, season at each end, no correction since.”

Then he did not turn the book any further, and he did not read it out.

## The Difference

“The seventh was read out in this room once,” Ning Yan said.

“The seventh was read out because it answered something. An entry that answers nothing gets written down.” He took his thumb off the page. “That is the whole of the difference and it is not a small one, and I will not pretend to you that it is a small one, because you will find it out in about a year and it is better if you have it now.”

Nobody inside nine days of road could read that line. The bound had no column for a thing done past its own boundary and never had had one, and there were four county books nine days west that held a name they had written themselves and could not check, and every office between here and there had spent four years finding reasons not to open a book.

There was now a line, in ink, in a hand, on a page, in a book four hundred yards behind her, that said a woman past the last working point was good.

She felt that go through her in a way that had nothing to do with her foundation and everything to do with the floor she was standing on.

## The Feeling

It was relief.

That was the thing and it arrived before she could get a hand on it and it arrived the way water arrives in a dry place, all at once and into a place that had not been dry for a long time without her noticing that it had.

She had been illegible for five months. That was what she had bought with a crossing and it had been the best trade she had ever made, and she had gone five months without a single person in the world being able to say one true thing about her to another person. She had stubs nobody could check and a furnace nobody could hear and an account nobody had settled and a claim nobody had answered, and she had slept on all of it.

And now there was a word about her, in ink, on a page, in a book.

And the horror came up under the relief about four seconds later and it came up the way the anger came up, low and behind the shoulders, and this is what it was:

She was glad of it. She was standing in a room with a stove in it at the seventh hour on the twentieth of a month and she was glad that a clerk had written a word about her in a book, and she had spent four years learning what that gladness was worth in a girl of seventeen who had wanted a number in a column for herself and had got it.

The word was good. The word had a price. In four years she had heard that word in four rooms and it had cost a person something every single time.

“Who reads it,” Ning Yan said.

“Us. Whoever comes to this counter with something to ask and we go and get the book.”

“An office.”

“An office,” he said. “There is one of these within nine days of road. Past that there is nothing in nine days of road there is at all, and the county nine days west cannot read this page and has never been able to and is not going to be able to in the next thousand years.”

## What Good Is

“Then what it is for.”

He looked at her for the first time in five months, properly, the way a man looks at a person he has been reading a line about.

“It is for the figure,” he said. “You have eight hundred jin on my book against your hours, and you are the hours. When I answer it, I am answering it about a person.”

“A person is a thing an office can vouch for and a number is not. I can vouch for you because there is a word in my book saying you are good. Yesterday I could not have vouched for you, and there was not one thing I could have put in that book yesterday that would have meant anything to a man in a room nine days west.”

“So the figure is easier.”

“The figure is possible.” He closed the book on his palm. “Not easier. Possible. There is a difference and it is the only difference in this room that has ever mattered.”

And then he said the other half of it, which he said in exactly the same voice, and which she had not been ready for, and which went into her the way a stone goes into water.

“It cannot be taken out.”

“No.”

“I am asking.”

“You are asking.” He did not look down and he did not soften it, and there was nothing in his face that was cruel and nothing in it that was kind.

“An entry is not a thing a person can ask out of a book. It goes in when we put it in and it stays. A book does not give a thing back and there is nothing a person can write that takes a line out of one. I am not being unkind to you and I would be telling you the same thing in a year.”

## The Notebook

She stood at the counter with that in her and looked at the far wall, where a low table stood with a slate on it and a chair, and there was a wet mark on the stone floor under the table in the shape of a man sitting there a great many times.

“Who sits there.”

“A man with a notebook. He comes on the fourth hour and writes four lines and he has been doing it since before you crossed and he will be doing it after the season turns.” He put the book flat again. “He has never once put a name in anything that has come out of this room. Not yours and not anybody else’s. If he did, the room would be a different room.”

That was the entire protection she had against every office in nine days of road, and it was one man and a notebook and a habit, and it was not hers and she could not thank him for it and had no way of ever finding out that she had been thanking it.

She wanted, on the twentieth of a month at about the eighth hour, to know whether the word in that book was true.

She was eighteen years old and she had four fingers on one hand and an arm at half and a furnace in the ground behind a hut and seventeen stubs in a fold of cloth and no way in the world to check a single one of those things, and a clerk four hundred yards behind her had just written down in ink that she was good.

There was no instrument. She had looked for one all the way from the door to the counter and there was nothing on the counter that answered to it.

She went back down the track and worked the ordinary end all afternoon and lifted four stones to the younger man’s four and did not lose once, and her breath was four counts, and her arm was half, and none of it was worse than it had been at the seventh hour that morning, and it was not better either, and the only thing that had changed in the whole of it was a word in a book that no county could reach.

## The Count

Ten days to the thirtieth. One figure. One run with twelve holes in it. One office that needed the run to be finished and true before it could write the answer out and send it west to a counting room in a town she had stood in the street of nine days of road and had not knocked on.

She wanted, concretely, to have that office’s book closed on her inside of ten days, with a word in it she had not chosen, in a country that could not be argued with, nine days of road from the only family she had left in the world.

Under her, at the boards that night, with the ring ticking through a course of brick she had laid herself:

“You are readable,” he said. “And that is not a kindness.”

She had not told him she was glad. There was no reason on that reach for a word spoken at a low table on the fourth hour to reach him at all, and she had watched the ceiling for a while wondering about that and had come to no conclusion she was willing to act on.

The twenty-first closed on a woman who had been entered as good in a book four hundred yards behind her, by a clerk who had never asked her name, and who could not be un-entered, and who was one day closer to a thirtieth than she had been the day before.
