# The Only Book With Her In It

The twenty-ninth of a month came round and she stood at the plank at about the seventh hour with the day written in and the stub torn off and put face down and went and did not eat.

It was a month.

That was the thing she was not expecting, and it came up out of the arithmetic while she was standing there looking at a column of her own hours going down in order with dates against them.

Thirty days ago, a man in a low building four hundred yards up that track read a figure out loud off a sheet of paper in the ordinary voice, so that the six men behind him could hear which part he was on. Six men and a stove. Nobody in that room knew she was standing outside the door.

A month.

## The Two Pieces Of Paper

There are two pieces of paper within nine days of road that have Ning Yan on them, and she stood at a plank on the far side of a line of work and worked out what that means, in the ordinary way she works everything out.

The first is the book on the plank. It is the only book in the world that holds her as a person with days in it. Her name is at the top of the column and there are dates going down it in order and hours against them and a rate, and it has been continuous since the fourth of the month that is a month back, and there is a stub for every day in it.

The second is the sheet on a spike in a low room with a stove in it. That is the only other piece of paper within nine days of road with her name on it and she has never seen it and does not need to.

Both of them are in the same country.

Not one county within nine days of road has ever heard of either one. There is a county book nine days east that closed its ground against her on the seventeenth of a month three months back, and there is not one line in it, or in any of the other eight, that would take a woman standing on the other side of a post.

Nothing anywhere in the world joins the two pieces of paper. Not a rule and not a man and not a wedge of paper going up a stair.

And the figure on the second one is eight hundred jin.

She stood there with that for about as long as a man reads a line.

## What She Did Not Do About The First One

She has had a run in one hand with dates in it twice before in four years, and both times somebody else ended it.

The first time it ended because a course lawfully closed its own working ground against her and the dates stopped at the seventeenth of a month, and the man whose book it was said out loud that the run he held her to ended there, and he was not unkind about it, and he did not have to be.

The second time it ended because she took two books in two hands and split it herself, on purpose, in daylight, at a trestle, in the time it takes a man to turn a bar over, so that neither of them could carry a continuous run and a man in a plain coat could not get a document that said where she had been.

She did that to herself. She has never once regretted it and she has thought about it every day since.

And she stood at a plank on the thirtieth of a month and looked at a column that has been going since the fourth without a break and worked out that the only reason it has not been broken is that nobody within nine days of road has any instrument to break it with.

## What A Figure Is

She did not build a theory on it. She has been reading both kinds of paper since she was sixteen.

A claim is a figure. A figure is only paid against something.

That is not a thing about this room. That is what a wage-book is for and what an office is for and it is the only thing either of them has ever done. Everything on nine days of road that is worth anything is worth something against a day somebody worked, and a licence and a standing claim against a body travel on paper and get paid out against hours, and the hours have to be somewhere and the somewhere has to have a book with a date in it.

And the only hours on this side of the line there are, are the ones in her column four hundred yards behind her.

The book on the plank is the only book in the world that holds her as a person with days in it. The sheet in the room past the post is the only other piece of paper in the world with her name on it. Both of them are in the same country and no county within nine days of road has ever heard of either.

And the only hours anybody on this side of the line can put against a figure are the hours in the first one.

Nobody in that room knows she exists.

## What It Costs One Person

She stood at the side of a plank on the far side of a line of work and understood that the only instrument in the world that can close a bill of eight hundred jin is a column of hours belonging to a woman who has four fingers’ worth of grip on one hand.

The instrument is hers. She is the only person who can decide to give it away or to keep it.

And she is three-quarters of a mile from the flat she crossed on her hands and cut in the dark on the second of a month and has not seen since.

She was angry for about eleven minutes and it was not the anger from the stone outside the building a month ago.

There was nothing to be angry at. A man at a counter read a document out loud because that is what a counter does. A man at a plank writes down what he is given and does not volunteer facts about his own book. A man with a cart tears a strip off a rule every nine or ten days and has done it since before he came and the one before him did it too, and he cannot read a word of what he is keeping.

Every one of them is doing the only thing they are built to do.

And every one of them is right.

She wanted, for about the fourth of those eleven minutes, for one of them to be a villain, and there is not going to be one on this reach, and the reason there is not going to be one is that there is no one here who wants anything from her.

## The Track To The Post And Back

She went up the cart track at about the seventh hour and stood at the post for about nine minutes.

There was a strip on the board dated four days back that was four inches wide and had a figure at the right of it in a hand she cannot read, and there were eight others above it going back past the point where she can count any more.

She did not put her hand on the plank. She did not go and ask anything of anybody. She stood in front of a thing that has kept a record for nine years and could not read one word of it and had a figure of her own hours in it four hundred yards behind her, and she worked out on the spot, standing up, in about nine minutes, that there is not one instrument within nine days of road that can join the two.

There is not one. Not a word of it and not a man and not a wedge of paper going up a stair.

She walked back down four hundred yards of track in about eleven minutes and she got to the yard and it was dark and the tools were in the end house and the fire under the trestle was let down, and there was nothing on this reach to tell her that she had just stood at the only post anybody within nine days of road has ever stood at where the whole of the shape of her life was visible at once.

## What She Did About It

She went back down the track to the yard and picked the bar up and went at the heavy end for about an hour and a half, and then it was dark and she was at four counts of breath on a ramp with a load on it and she went down and slept in a hut with three boards in it.

The next morning she was at the bank at about the fourth hour with the bar in her left hand.

That was the decision and she did not say it out loud and it did not take a sentence.

Stopping does not narrow a figure. The figure was read out loud in a room with a stove in it on the twenty-ninth of a month a month ago whether she was working or not, and it is there tomorrow, and it will be there when the carts stop and start again. The one thing that work changes is that it hands the people who are able to act on it a longer column.

So she went out at the fourth hour.

He said one sentence and it was in the ordinary voice and it was about her and not about the ground, and it bought nothing and she spent nothing on it.

“The ground you crossed to get here is the only ground on nine days where anything at all can happen to you,” he said, “and you worked that out a day and a half ago and you have been at the heavy end anyway.”

She did not answer him.

At the seventh hour she wrote the date in and it took about eleven minutes and the man at the plank held the page flat with his palm without being asked.

The man at the plank has a second date to write at the far end of a column and a rule to write it at, and he has not written it, and she has not asked him for it again.
