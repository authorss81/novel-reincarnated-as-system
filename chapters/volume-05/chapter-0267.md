# The Worker’s Reading

On the twenty-eighth the sound came out of the west at about the fourth hour, and the whole yard heard it, and nobody on the yard knew what it was but her.

She was at the trestle with the book open, writing the date in with the flat of the second finger, eleven minutes, the page held flat, when it went along the ground under the plank and up through the soles of her shoes.

It came low, under the arm, the way thick drift rang under a bar, and kept coming after the source stopped, for about as long as it took to count eleven, and she knew the shape of it the way she knew her own breath.

An announcement. Made nine days west, inside the bound, in the open, the way stages go inside the bound unless they are ordered otherwise. She knew the shape of it the way she knew her own breath, four counts and then the sound, and then the drift taking it.

The man at the plank stopped his pen for about two seconds and went on. The eleven men at the fire heard nothing they had a word for. The keeper up at the post set down his paste and stood still with his head turned west, listening to a thing he had heard for nine years without ever being told it.

## The Board

At about the sixth hour the keeper came down the post with a strip wet with paste and put it up, and on it, among the marks nobody on the reach could read, was a fresh reading in his hand.

She saw it from the bank, a dark line on pale cloth, still damp, and she knew what it was before she had finished the stone, because she had watched him write a thing he could not read once before, on the twenty-ninth of a month three months back, when the sound had been hers.

## The Walk Past

At midday she carried stone to the ramp by way of the track, the long way round that passed the post, because the short way was water, and she walked past the board without slowing and without looking, the way she had walked past it on the tenth.

The strip stood damp on the post among the strips going up every nine or ten days whether or not there was anything to put in them, and the fresh reading stood dark on it, and she did not stop, because she was not going to stand in front of it again.

A stage told nine days west had come east past the line as sound the drift carried. It had gone onto the far-side board as a reading. That was what the board was for, and she held the knowing the way she held the bar, with both hands, until evening.

A stage told nine days west had come east past the line as sound the drift carried. It had gone onto the far-side board as a reading. That was what the board was for.

She worked the morning with the furnace under her and the knowledge under that, setting down early, moving stone by hand, and she waited for the evening fire the way she waited for everything on that reach, by working until it came.

## The Day Between

The day between the sound and the fire was ordinary, and she worked it the way ordinary days were worked, stone after stone, breath in four counts, arm open a third of the time.

Da moved two men to the ramp boards to check them, because sound in the ground loosened fixings the way weather loosened them, and the yard spent an hour on wedges and rails.

She held the bar for the lifting while a man half her age drove the wedges, and the furnace ran through the middle of the hold, steady and banked, and the rails came true.

No person on the yard said one word about the morning’s sound. It had gone along the ground under all of them and up through all of their shoes, and none of them had a word for it but the keeper, and the keeper was four hundred yards up the track with his paste and his strips.

## The Fire

They sat at the eight feet after the day, eleven men and her, the fire let up into the stones, rain gone, cold steady, and an old worker from the mill end with ash on his coat said it to nobody in particular, looking at the fire.

He was a man of about sixty who had worked that reach eleven years and never heard a stage announced in his life, and what he said came out in short sentences, plain and complete.

“Being told is one thing,” he said. “Being charged is another.”

The fire went on behind the words the way fires go on behind conversation.

“Sound comes east,” he said. “Drift carries it. Board takes it. That is a reading.”

He poked the fire with a stick. Nobody answered him, because he had not asked anything.

“What goes in the ground stays there,” he said. “No county book takes it. Told is told. Charged is charged. They were never the same thing.”

Then he was done, and the fire went on, and one of the eleven men laughed at something Da said about a wheel, and the night stood the way nights stand on a working reach.

## What It Paid

She walked back to the hut in the dark with the furnace banked and the bar by the door, and held the worker’s sentences beside the nineteenth and the twenty-sixth, the way she held everything, plainly and without machinery.

A stage she had taken on the far side had gone into the ground and reached no county book at all. A stage told inside the bound nine days west had arrived past the line as drift-carried sound and gone onto the far-side board as a reading.

Being told was what happened inside. Being charged was what the ground carried. The bound had never noticed the difference.

That was the whole of it, said once, in a worker’s mouth, on the page, in short sentences. The ninety-parts account stood. The tenth part was not given. Nothing on the reach was proved wrong.

She put her hands flat on the earth behind the hut where the ground was warm, and felt what was charged there, steady and without witness, hers and in no book.

## The Two Holdings

She held the two holdings beside each other in the dark, the way she held everything beside the book, plainly and without machinery.

In the ground behind the hut, a furnace taken at her own order on the nineteenth, where nothing was announced, gone into the earth and staying there, reaching no county book at all.

On the post four hundred yards up the track, a reading taken off drift-carried sound on the twenty-eighth, told inside the bound nine days west, pasted onto a strip by hands that did not know it, going onto the far-side board because that was what the board was for.

One she had paid for with twenty-nine coppers and two holds and the turn out of her stroke. One had ridden the drift past the line and gone onto a board, costing her nothing, because the board took readings and the ground took stages.

She wanted, concretely, to keep the furnace banked through the coming days. That was the want, small and hers, and it held.

He said one thing that night and it was present tense and it bought nothing.

“It is said,” he said. “And it is staying said.”

The twenty-eighth closed on a woman with a furnace in the ground no county could hear, a reading on a far-side board no county could answer, eleven stubs in four marks, three refusals standing, a draft nine days west with no date, and a season with weight in it.

Something on the far side now held her stages in two places, in the ground and on a board. No office nine days west knew either one.
