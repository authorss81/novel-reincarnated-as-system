# Nine Feet Off

He was at the trestle on the third of the month at about the seventh hour, about nine feet off, in a plain coat, with the notebook shut against his side.

He had been on the far side since the fourth of a month three months back. He came down the track from the east again, which is not the direction he came from when he first got here, and he had been about nine hundred yards west of that trestle for two days and had not come inside nine feet of it in either of them.

She was at the plank writing the date when he arrived and it took her about eleven minutes and he stood the whole of it at nine feet without saying anything, which is a thing she has now had twice.

He has not asked her a question since the fourth of a month three months back.

## What He Did For Two Days

He was there on the third and on the fourth.

On the third he came down the track from the east at about the fifth hour and stopped about nine feet off the ramp and wrote for about an hour, standing up, in the wind, the way a man writes who is not going to be given a table.

On the fourth he did it again at the same hour and at the same nine feet and did not speak to her at all for about three quarters of the first hour, and then at about the sixth hour he said the thing in one breath, and she had not asked for it.

He does that. He gives a woman the whole of the position in one breath so that she does not have to spend a morning working out which part of it she is going to have to fight, and he has done it four times now in a hall with no roof and on a step in a lane and on high ground and on the fourth of a month three months back, and it has never once been anything else.

She has never once asked him for any of it and he has never once waited to be asked.

## The One Breath

He said it at about the seventh hour in the ordinary voice, and he said it the way he has said it four other times in this book, which is the whole of the reason she listens to it at all.

“I have not bought anything, I have not threatened you, I have not touched you, I have not entered anything and I have not asked you a question,” he said, “and I have been on this side of that post for sixty days and I have not put one word in anybody's book on this side of it, and I want that in the same breath as everything else so that you do not have to spend a morning on it.”

Then he took the notebook out from under his arm and stood holding it.

He did not open it. She has never once seen the page and she has never once asked to.

## What He Said About The Claim

“You have been to that room,” he said.

“I have been to that room.”

“Then you know what is on the spike,” he said. “It has been on the spike for a month and the man at that counter has not taken one step with it because he is not the office that has to answer it, and he is correct about that and so is everybody in that room, and it is going to sit on that spike until somebody above him is the office it is for.”

He put his thumb flat on the notebook against his side.

“I do not want it answered.”

She stood at the plank and did not move.

“I have not wanted that since the twentieth of a month four months back,” he said, “and the thing I wanted then was that my own order should not be carried out, and I said so on high ground in front of you and I would say it again in that room.

This is the same sentence with a different figure in it. An answered claim is an answer somebody above me has to reckon with. I have never once believed the reckoning was going to be the right one and I have never once believed it was going to be the gentle one either, and I have been in this for nine years.”

Nine feet off. A notebook against his side. A wind off the rise.

“I am not telling you what to do with it,” he said. “I do not know what you should do with it. I am telling you what I want, because I have never once given you anything that was not true and that is the only thing I have got.”

## What She Did Not Ask

She did not ask him what was in the space. She had been told at the fourth of a month three months back and it was a countersignature in an office's hand dated from this side of the post and it was not an answer to what he asked for, and it is not an answer today.

She did not ask him whether the people on the line knew she existed, because he told her they do not, and he was right, and she has not forgotten either half of it in three months.

She did not ask him whether the man at that counter is the office the claim is for.

She stood her nine feet and she listened to the whole of it and she did not say one word back to him at any point in about four minutes, and at the end of it she took her hands off the plank and picked the bar up.

## The Rest Of The Two Days

He left after about four minutes and went up the rise, and she stood at the plank and did not write the date for about the length of a man reading a line.

She did not write anything and she did not ask him to come back and she did not say his name, and there was not one question in her she was going to put to him on any day this month.

On the fourth he came down at the fifth hour and wrote for an hour at nine feet and then went up the rise again, and on the way out he stopped about where the track turns and turned round.

"I am going to be on this side of that post for the rest of this run," he said, "and I will not come inside nine feet again unless you put me there. That is not a thing you have to answer."

Then he went.

## The Cost Of It

She has spent four years wanting one man in this world to be the one she could be angry at.

Four years and eleven documents and two offices that wanted something out of her and a course that closed its own ground against her lawfully and was right to, and not one single time in any of it had anybody stood in front of her and been wrong about a thing in a way she could put in front of a man with a plank.

And this is the one man within nine days of road who has never taken a single thing from her, who buys nothing and touches nothing and asks nothing, and he stands nine feet off in a wind and tells her, in the ordinary voice, that he does not want the one thing in the world that she has wanted for two years.

What he wants and what she wants were the same thing from the fourth of a month three months back until about ninety seconds ago.

He has not lied. He has not escalated. He has not said one word that is not true and he has not threatened her with anything at all and he has never taken anything and he is a professional disagreeing with his own office for the second time in this book, out loud, in front of one person, at no cost to himself and at the whole of the cost to her.

You have spent four years wanting him to be the one man in this you could be angry at. He is standing nine feet off your own plank and he has never once given you a reason.

She wanted him to lie. She has wanted him to lie once for about three months and she did not know she had the want until about the second hour of the third evening of that week.

## What He Wants

She got it out of him on the walk back, in about nine minutes, standing at the end house in the dark with the tools in it.

He wants the claim to keep standing.

That is what he said at nine feet and that is what he has wanted since the fourth of a month three months back and it is the same position he took about his own order, and the reason is the one he gives every time and it is not a small one: a thing that is still pending is a question somebody above him has to answer, and he has nine years of that and has never once seen the answer come back right.

A dismantling carried out is a closed file. An answered claim is a closed file. What he is sitting on is a sheet on a spike in a room that cannot answer it.

He does not want that to change and he has not the power to stop it changing and he is the only person within nine days of road who has told her so in advance.

She has been on the far side of a line of work for a month and a bit and not one person on it has told her anything about what they want. He told her at nine feet in a wind and it cost him nothing and it was the most expensive thing anybody has said to her in four years.

## Two Of The Eight

She had two sentences of him and she spent them on nothing that was useful.

“You have spent four years wanting him to be the one man in this you could be angry at,” he said, and she did not move, “and he is standing nine feet off your own plank and he has never once given you a reason.”

She did not answer him and she did not spend a mark on it.

What they bought her was a thing that is going to sit on her for about a week: that the man who has spent nine years telling her the truth is going to go on doing it, and that he is going to go on doing it about something she wants, and that there is not one thing she can do with that.

The seventh of the month is a pay-day and there is a rule at that plank and a stub for every pay and she is going to be owed one and it is going to have four marks on it and none of them is going to be worth anything to anybody.
