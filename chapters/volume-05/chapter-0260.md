# A Draft Goes West

On the twenty-first the office wrote west, and the claim moved without being answered.

She knew it the way news travels on a working reach, not told and not hidden, carried on the track the way dust is carried. The carrier’s round went west at first light with papers and bags and persons, nine days hard going, no date on the round, and among the papers was a draft in the office’s hand.

She stood at the bank at about the sixth hour and watched the cart go up the track toward the post with a cover rolled at the back and a rail for bags, the same shape as the cart on the fourteenth, and the driver forward in the road seat.

She counted the lading the way she counted everything, bags and papers and persons, and among the papers a draft in the office’s hand, figure business, office to office, the second movement of a claim that had stood since she was sixteen.

She wanted to know what was on the paper. That was the want, plain and unadorned, and it stayed unmet, because a woman booked to a stone bank does not stop a carrier’s round to ask about an office’s draft.

## The Morning’s Stones

She worked the morning on the heavy end the new way, lift with the furnace, set early, move by hand, and the stones went where she put them, placed and solid, and Da watched two land and nodded once and walked away.

The furnace ran steady through the middle of every stroke, stronger than she had ever been, and the ends were dead the way ends were now, and the middle carried the day.

By midday the hand with four fingers ached in the new grip and the heel-gone palm held, and she ate bread on the sledge with her back to the wind, five coppers at the seventh hour, and watched the track stay empty the way tracks stay empty after a round has gone.

Nine days west. No date on the round. Comes when it comes. That was the carrier’s time, and it was not book time and not office time, and it carried both.

## The Track

At midday she carried stone to the ramp and Da said it the way he said everything, with his eyes on the lading.

“Office paper west,” he said. “Figure business. Goes on the round.”

“With a date,” she said.

“With no date,” Da said. “Round carries what it carries. Comes when it comes.”

Nine days west. A paper with a figure on it riding under a cover with bags and persons, on a round that went when it was full and came when it came, toward a counting room in Lowwater with a date in it entered by a hand that was not the counting room’s.

She set the stone down and went back for the next, and she worked out what had changed and what had not, the way she worked out everything, with the bar in her hands.

The claim stood. It was entered in good order and it travelled and it stood, and this was the first office within nine days of road that had ever been in a position to act on it, and it had now written west on it.

Not answered. Not paid. Not assessed. Not torn off. A draft west with no date on it was a movement and not an answer, and the difference was the whole of the debt pressure, and she held it the way she held the bar, with both hands.

## The Room Again

At about the seventh hour she went up the track past the post without stopping at the board, four hundred yards to the low room, and stood at the counter the way she had stood on the tenth and the twelfth.

The same room. The same stove. Four men this time instead of six, two on a bench and two standing, and the man at the counter with a book open and the spike beside it with sheets on it.

She asked the question a worker asks, in the ordinary voice, with her hands down.

“Did paper go west on the round.”

“It went,” he said. “Office to office. Figure business. It goes on the carrier’s round and it comes when it comes.”

Two of the four men looked up at the word figure the way men look up at words that might be work, and looked down again when it was not.

“With whose hand on it,” she said.

“Office hand,” he said. “Not a person’s. The round carries office paper the way the trestle carries hours. That is what the round is for.”

“Mine,” she said. It was not a question and he did not take it for one.

“The sheet stands,” he said. “The spike holds it. Nothing is taken off it. Nothing is paid against it. Until she says it the figure stands and it costs the room nothing to stand.”

He did not know the figure was hers and he had never asked. He read the standing off the sheet in front of him at the speed a man reads, and the four men heard papers read out loud the way they always heard them, and were not interested.

She felt relief go through her first, that nothing had been taken, and then anger, that the paper had gone without her word on it, and then the cold settling underneath both, which was the knowledge that an office that can act will act in the way offices act, by writing, whether or not the woman is standing at the counter.

## What Moved

She walked back down the track in the dusk past the post with the board on it, and worked out the movement the way she had worked out the second asking on the fifteenth.

First movement: read aloud in a room that could act, on the twenty-ninth of a month three months back. Standing, actionable, not answered.

Second movement: a draft west on a carrier’s round with no date on it, office to office, nine days hard road. Standing, actionable, not answered.

The claim had moved and moved, and the date in Lowwater was untouched, and Hou was unpaid and patient and had never met her, and the coming was a different number nobody had quoted.

She wanted the draft to carry nothing of her column. That was the concrete want, and the room had given her the half of it that rooms give, which was that nothing was taken until she said it.

At the plank she wrote the date in, eleven minutes, and put her hand flat on the book for a line’s length, and felt the column under her palm, dates in order, hours against them, continuous since the second of a month.

## The Two Papers

She sat against the boards in the hut with the oilcloth on her knee and held the two papers of the reach beside each other in her head, the way she held everything beside the book.

One paper west with no date on it, office to office, nine days hard road, a draft with a figure on it riding under a cover with bags and persons.

One column east in a book with her name at the top, dates in order, hours against them, four hundred yards down the track, with a far date at the far end in a different hand.

The draft kept carrier’s time. The column kept book time. Neither waited for her, and both were hers to answer, one by saying yes and one by working the days.

She wanted the draft to travel light. That was the want, plain and unspoken, that it carry paper and not hours, office and not woman, figure and not column.

The draft was nine days west with no date on it. The column was four hundred yards down the track with her name at the top. The two kept different time.

She went to the hut and banked the furnace and sat against the boards, and the night closed on a debt that had learned to travel.
