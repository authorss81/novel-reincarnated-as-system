# What The Furnace Keeps

On the twenty-seventh the pay-day arrived and she worked it, and the column took the day the way columns take days, in order, hours against it.

She went to the plank at about the seventh hour with the bag put away and the bar over the shoulder, and stood in the short queue the way pay-day queues stand, three men before her with stone dust on their coats, none of them talking. The man at the plank paid each in turn, strips torn at the rule, face down, into coats, and read the run for each from the book.

Her own run read from the seventeenth to the twenty-seventh, gaps present as gaps, days worked in order, hours against them. He tore one more strip off at the rule, face down on the plank, into her coat. Twelve stubs now in the oilcloth in four marks, none checkable by anybody within nine days of road.

She held the new stub a moment before it went into the coat, feeling the tear-edge where it had come off the book, straight and clean, the contractor’s mark on the back, and put it with the rest, the twelfth in order, no overlap and no gap.

Five coppers of the day into her hand and five out for bread at the store. The number the same both times, the way it always was. The gaps cost what unworked days cost, days not paid, and nothing could move them, and she did not ask.

What she felt at the store step was the heaviness of a thing carried straight, debt paid where debt could be paid, road paid where road had to be paid, column clean, season holding. She wanted, concretely, to take the second stage of the third realm at her own order, in the open, where nothing was announced. That was the want, and it had a furnace and forty-four days in it.

## The Days Between

The next five days were ordinary and she worked every one of them, and they were the last five she would work the way she had worked up to that point, and she had not told anybody that and was not going to.

The heavy end had come back to her on the tenth and stayed. Nine holds, four fingers bound against the dust, no turn at the end of the stroke, lift with the furnace, set early, move by hand. The man with the bad shoulder counted her loads with his eyes and did not count them out loud. The younger man who drove wedges was better at the ramp boards than she was and knew it and let her lift anyway.

She did not tell the yard. A stage taken past the line was not yard business until the ground said otherwise, and the ground said nothing until it was asked. She had told no person in five days, not the man at the plank and not the woman with the scarred forearm, and the word furnace had not been out loud to a living person since the ninth, and that had been said to the ground.

Each evening she banked the furnace with three handfuls of ash and sat against the boards and felt it tick under her foundation, and each evening she counted the same thing without saying it. By the last of them it was forty-three days since the nineteenth, and one more day to the third of the next month, and then a thing that could not be undone and would not be announced to anybody.

The woman with the scarred forearm moved one place along the stones at the fire on the thirtieth without asking and without comment and put her bag down in the gap, and Ning Yan moved one place along beside her, and neither of them said a word about it, and the eleven men at the fire made room the way fires make room.

## The Order

On the third of the month she worked the heavy end through the morning, nine holds and four fingers, and turned stone the way she had turned it since the nineteenth, lift with the furnace, set early, move by hand, and at about the second hour Da paid her the day’s understanding without words, by pointing at the ramp and walking away.

## The Stones

The morning’s stones were ordinary and she worked them the way she would remember working them, which was carefully, because they were the last stones she would ever turn with one stage under her.

She chose each lift the way she had chosen lifts since the fourth of a month four months back, bar under, nine holds, weight up, no turn at the end, set down true. The missing turn was there in its absence, felt every time and never spoken of.

A man at the easy end watched one stone land true and did not say a word about it, and she took the next one off the bank and set it down in the same place, and the whole of the farewell the yard had for her was that a stone had landed true and nobody had made anything of it.

She was done at the fourth hour. A day worked is a day paid and the pay would come on the seventh with the rest, unmovable, and today’s hours would go into the column in the morning the way every day’s hours went in.

Behind the hut the furnace stood banked, brick and wire and clay, forty-four days since the nineteenth, eighty-five days of far-side wages behind the second stage and forty-four behind the first, materials bought at far-side prices, body with four fingers on one hand.

She stood with her hands down, in daylight, the way she had stood for every order since the fourth of a month four months back, and gave it out loud, because she had given the other two out loud and was not going to give the third one under a board.

“Now,” she said. “At my order. Here.”

He said one sentence, and it bought nothing and advised nothing.

“You are ordering,” he said. “And I am here.”

She emptied herself the way the four-clause order required, empty first and then take, and the furnace came up under her the way a floor comes up under feet, heavier than the first time, the ground not easier.

The ground took it. No county in nine counties heard anything at all. There was nobody within nine days of road to be told. There was no book in any of the nine with a column for a thing taken past its own boundary. What she had taken went into the earth under the hut and stayed there.

## The Cost

The heat came up through the foundation the way warmth comes up through banked ash, steady and without flame. It ran the four counts of her breath and settled them without changing them. It ran the cracked aperture and held it without mending it. It ran the right arm, open a third of the time, and left it open.

The cost came after, in the writing she did to test it, lining the date into the column at about the sixth hour with the flat of the second finger.

Eleven minutes had been the measure since the fourth of a month four months back. The line took longer now. She felt it on every stroke, the flat moving slower, the number of strokes the same and the time wider, and she did not hurry past it, and she did not test it twice the way she had tested holds after the heel went.

The flat of the second finger went from eleven minutes a line to longer, permanently. That was the price on the page, and it would not come back, and no practice would rebuild the speed because the speed had gone into the furnace the way order goes into everything she pays for.

And every hour she wrote after it cost her the hour it was written in. She felt that too, the way she felt everything the ground took, plainly and without machinery. An hour in the column and an hour out of the day, gone into the writing, staying there.

She put the pen down and sat with her hands on her knees breathing in four counts.

She would write slower for the rest of her life.

Every date would cost her the hour it took.

[Ning Yan, past the line, servant-born, under no ceiling any county holds. Golden Core, stage two, is held.]

The layer said it once, in the far-side house form, announcing and nothing more. It explained nothing and advised nothing.

She stood past the last working point with a furnace of her own under her and four fingers on one hand and nine holds on the other and a turn gone and a finger slowed, and the ground held what no county could hear.

What she wanted, with the ink still wet, was a full day with the furnace new in her. She wanted to find out whether a stage that had cost an hour a line was worth a day that could turn stone. That was tomorrow’s want, and it was enough.
