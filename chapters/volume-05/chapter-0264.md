# News From Nine Days Off

On the twenty-fifth a hauler came east on the coming round and sat at the yard fire after the day, and said what the inside was doing, and she could not ask a second question.

The hauler was a woman of about forty with a road face and a pot she shared the way haulers share, and she had come off the nine-day road that morning with bags and papers and a cart that needed a wheel looked at.

Ning Yan sat at the edge of the eight feet the way she sat every night, inside the warmth while eleven men held it, colder than she had been in a hut with three boards, and listened the way she had listened at a pot two months back.

The fire burned on the eleven men’s hours, free to her and costing her nothing, and at about the eighth hour four of them would let it down into the stones without asking anybody, the way they always did.

Until then it stood up into the dark, and the hauler shared the pot the way haulers share, and the talk went the way talk goes at fires, ruts and wheels and loads, and Ning Yan sat with stone dust on her coat and bread gone since morning and said nothing.

## Three Facts

The hauler talked about the road the way haulers talk, ruts and water and where the wheels caught, and then she said it the way strangers say things that are not news to them.

“Tower’s still lit,” she said. “Fourth hour and it does not stop. Clerk’s arms up four flights with a bundle and down empty. Floor’s nine men and the same nine.”

Three facts from nine days west, in a hauler’s mouth at a fire, by accident and without weight. A light that did not stop. A bundle going down a stair and arms going up with it and coming down empty. Nine men on a floor and all of them still on it.

Ning Yan felt love go through her like a hand again, the way it had at the cart turn, and under it grief, and she put both where she put everything.

She could not ask a second question. To ask would be to say the tower was news to her that was not news, that she knew whose stair the bundle went down and whose arms came down empty, that nine men on a floor nine days west were the cost of every decision she made.

So she asked the question a worker asks at a fire, about the road.

“How is the road west.”

“Hard,” the hauler said. “Nine days hard going. Wet at the bottom. Passable.”

A man across the fire asked about the wheel the hauler’s cart needed, and the hauler answered it at length, wood and iron and where the wobble sat, and the talk went to wheels the way talk goes at fires, and Ning Yan sat inside the eight feet with the three facts held quietly, the way she held everything that could not be asked after.

## The Departure

The hauler went east in the morning with the wheel mended and the pot washed, the way haulers go, without farewell, and the yard worked on, and that night the fire was let down at about the eighth hour by four men without asking anybody, the way it always was.

She banked the furnace with the three facts standing beside the fair copy in her head, two sightings of one road, one from the track at the post and one from a fire at the yard.

The bundle going down the stair. The clerk’s arms going up. The fair sheets tied with cord. The keeper’s paste. It joined the way true things join, without forcing, and she did not write it down, because some joinings were papers and some were only standings, and this one was a standing.

That was the whole of what she got, and it was enough, and it was nothing, the way news from inside had been nothing and everything since the crossing.

## What It Meant

She walked back to the hut in the dark with the furnace banked and the bar by the door, and worked out what the three facts meant laid beside the twenty-third.

The bundle going down the stair and the clerk’s arms going up four flights was the road the fair copy had travelled, seen from the other end, nine days west, in a hauler’s mouth that had never met the writer and did not know his name.

The tower lit at the fourth hour was the stair still carrying. The nine men still on the floor were the cost, held steady, doing the only thing they were built to do.

## The Pot

She had shared the hauler’s pot the way workers share, two mouthfuls of something thick and plain, and the hauler had asked her nothing, and she had given nothing but the pot back wiped clean.

That was the dealing at fires on that reach. Food shared without names. News given without weight. She did the work and let the arrangement stand.

She sat against the boards in the hut and held the two mouthfuls the way she held everything given freely, carefully, because freely given things on that reach were rarer than paid ones, and the pot had been freely given.

She wanted to write it down. That was the concrete want, to put three facts on the back of a dead weight ticket in wash-code the way she used to put everything down. She did not, because a ticket with inside news in a far-side hut was a paper no reach could hold, and she folded the want away with the rest.

He said one thing that night and it was present tense and it bought nothing.

“They are standing,” he said. “And you are standing.”

She lay on the boards listening to the furnace tick, and felt the nine days of road between the two standings the way she felt the four counts of breath, as a thing that did not stop whether or not she measured it.

## The Wheel

In the morning she checked the hauler’s mended wheel the way workers check mended things, one hand on the rim, eye along the wobble, and the hauler nodded once from the road seat, the way haulers nod when a thing is sound.

“True,” Ning Yan said.

“True enough for nine days,” the hauler said, and led the team out east with the wheel running straight, and the yard took the morning the way yards take mornings, stone and sledge and carts.

She watched the cart go with the three facts held quietly, tower and bundle and nine men, and turned back to the bank with the bar over her shoulder, and worked the day the new way, lift with the furnace, set early, move by hand.

## The Counting

At the plank that evening she wrote the date in, eleven minutes, the page held flat, and stood with her hand on the book, and counted what the twenty-fifth had given her against what it had cost.

Given: three facts from nine days west, a mended wheel watched true, a day worked and paid on the twenty-seventh to come. Cost: nothing the book could hold, only standings, a tower lit and nine men standing and a bundle on a stair.

The man at the plank went down the column in the ordinary way, and the date went in under the far date in his hand, and the column held days the way it always held them, without comment on what rode beside them.

The column took the day. The standings stayed outside it, the way love and grief stayed outside it, and she put her hand flat on the book for a line’s length, and went to the hut.

The twenty-fifth closed on a woman with news from inside she could not ask after, a fair copy in a far-side book she could not claim, and a furnace of her own ticking under the boards.

Both standings held. Neither answered the other.
