# Standing Where Nothing Came

She was outside in the dark on the first of a month because she had been outside on the first of a month since she was ten years old, and there is no version of a woman who does that which lets her sleep through it.

The village was off a lane about four hundred paces back from the made road and it had a dog in it, and the dog did not care about her at all, which she took as a good sign and was not.

She stood in the lane with the lamp in her left hand and the book of eight leaves against her fourth rib and the book had twenty-two names on one leaf and two leaves still blank, and she waited for something to come up nine hundred and four steps.

Nothing came up nine hundred and four steps.

Nothing came up anywhere. There was a field and a hedge and a woman doing something at a trough about two hundred paces off, and it was the fourth hour, and the whole of the thing she had been standing for since she was ten years old had a lane in it and a dog in it and nothing else.

## A Woman With A Lamp Of Her Own

The woman came out at about the fifth hour with a lamp that had a flame in it, and she was about sixty, and she said, “You are the one who walks.”

“I am.”

“You have been standing there since before the dog.”

“Since the fourth hour.”

The woman put her lamp on the trough and looked at her properly, and then said, “It is the first.”

“It is.”

“What do you do on the first.”

That was the question and it had been standing in her for eight days and she had not known it was coming, and she answered it standing, in the ordinary voice, in the lane, in front of a woman of about sixty holding nothing.

“I stood and waited for a bundle of boards to come up nine hundred and four steps,” she said. “On the first of every month for nineteen years, at about the fourth hour, wherever I was. It came on time. That was the whole of it.”

The woman looked at the dark lane for a while.

“Nine hundred and four steps,” she said. “Where.”

“In a mountain about four days east of here, and it is not there now, and the steps are not there now, and that is the only true thing I have got about it.”

“Nineteen years.”

“Nineteen years and every one of those firsts.”

“And nothing came this morning.”

“Nothing came this morning,” Ning Yan said, “and nothing came on the first of the month before it, and nothing is going to come on any of them, and there is not one person in nine counties who knows that there has been a last of them, and there is not going to be one, because there is nothing left to announce anything.”

## The Number That Is Only A Name

She said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the lane, at about the fifth hour on the first of a month, because it was a true thing and because she said true things out loud standing.

“Nine hundred and four steps was a number of steps in a stair that went somewhere. It is a name now. That is all it is.

There is no stair and there is nothing to come up it and there has not been anything to come up it since the thirtieth of a month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four, and that is thirty-one days, and I am not going to improve on the nine words I said on that day and I have not got better ones and I am not going to look.”

The woman of about sixty picked her lamp up off the trough.

“Nine hundred years,” she said. “That is what my mother said when the terrace came down.”

“That is what I said at the weir,” Ning Yan said. “Nine hundred years, gone, in nine days. I have not got anything past that and I have not had anybody to hand it to.”

“You have got me standing in a lane at the fifth hour,” the woman said, “and that is not nothing, and it is not much.”

“It is not much,” Ning Yan said.

## The Arithmetic Of A First Of A Month

Then she did it, because the ninth hour had not come and she had nothing else and because it was the first of a month and it was the only day in her life she had ever been able to put anything on.

She did it out loud, standing, in the lane, in front of the woman, in the ordinary voice.

“One hundred and ten days out of a room,” she said. “Eight hundred and ninety-three days in that room before it.

“Right arm open at the elbow nine hundred days, and it is not going to close, and it is not going to close in nineteen years either, and I have worked that out and I have said it out loud and there is not one person in this lane to check me.

“Two hundred and thirty-four days since the seventh of a month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four. Thirty-one days since the thirtieth of a month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four. Nine of those days were the fall and twenty-two of them were a lane and a made road and a girl at a mill.”

The woman said, “You say numbers out loud to people.”

“Yes.”

“Why.”

“Because it is the only way I have ever been able to say a true thing to a person,” Ning Yan said, “and I have looked for another way for nineteen years and there is not one and I have stopped looking.”

## What She Was Going To Do About It

The dog had gone off round the back of the cottages. The woman went to let a beam of water into a trough and came back and stood beside her in the lane, which Ning Yan noticed, because in nine days of walking she had got used to standing alone in front of people and had got used to the whole of a place not knowing what to do with her.

“You are going to stand here on the first again,” the woman said. It was not a question.

“Yes.”

“On the first of the month, in the dark, on your own, waiting for something that is not coming.”

“Yes.”

“For how many firsts.”

That stopped her. She stood in a lane with a woman of about sixty and worked it out standing, in the ordinary voice, with her hands where they could be seen, and what came out was that she did not know, and that she had not got an instrument that would tell her, and that the number of firsts left to her in the rest of her life was a thing she could not measure and had never once tried to.

She had tried for nineteen years to measure things that could not be measured, and it had been a job, and it had ended on the thirtieth of a month, and what was left of it was a woman standing in a lane because there was a day.

“All of them,” Ning Yan said.

The woman let about a count of two hundred go by and then said, “Then I will want to be asleep when you do it.”

And she went in, and shut her door, and did not stand with her, and had told her where she would be.

## The Empty Part Of The Morning

It got light at about the sixth hour and she stood in the lane and watched the ordinary morning of a village where the first of a month was a first of a month and nothing else.

A boy went past with two goats. A woman put a blanket over something on a line. Somewhere behind the cottages a door went and a voice said something about a bucket. Nine hundred years of a first of a month, and the whole of what was left of it was that the lane was cold and that she had said the true part of it out loud to one woman and that the woman had not asked her for anything.

She said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, to a lane with not one person in it, that it was the last first of a month that anything had ever been due, and that there would be others, and that they were ordinary days now, and that she was going to stand in one of them every month for the rest of her life with nothing to check.

Then she walked back down to the made road with the lamp in her left hand and went west, because there were eight counties left and about three hundred and forty-nine people in them she could not put a face to, and she had said on the twenty-third that there was no figure for how long that would take and she was not going to make one now to make the morning feel better.
