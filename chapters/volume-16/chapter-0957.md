# Easier And Worse

Guo Sheng told her on the tenth of the month in the middle of the queue, in front of about thirty people, and he told her because he had been working the queue for ten days and he could stand it no longer.

“You say it the same way every time.”

She looked at him.

“You say it exactly the same way. Word for word, the same speed, the same place you put your hand down after. I have heard it about forty times and I stopped hearing it somewhere around the eleventh, and yesterday there was a man in front of me who had waited for you since the first light and you did not know he was there until you had already said it.”

She did not say anything.

“I am not saying it to be cruel,” he said. “I am saying it because you told me nobody sent you and nothing is done to you and I have believed that for ten days, and I have worked out something else since. You have got easier at this and it has got worse, and the two things have happened together and I do not think you can see it from inside.”

## What She Did About It

She stopped.

She made herself stop for a whole day, which was the ninth and part of the tenth, and she stood at the end of the queue with her arms down and she did not say the three sentences once.

It was the worst decision she had made since the eleventh of a month at about the sixth hour and she made it standing, in the ordinary voice, out loud, in front of everybody who was waiting for her, because the rule does not have an exception for a reason.

Forty-one people came and went on that day and she did not say the sentences once to any of them.

Eleven of them went away. Two of them came back.

## What It Sounds Like From Outside

The thing she could not get past, for about two days, was that he was right.

She stood and listened to herself.

She had put the same three sentences into the open air about two hundred and seventy times in ten days, standing, in the ordinary voice, and by about the sixth day they were coming out of her before she had decided to send them. Not before she had decided whether to say them. Before she had decided to say them at all.

She would see somebody walking past who was forty paces off and not coming to the queue and never had, and she would find that the first sentence had already left her mouth.

She caught it four times in two days and on the fifth time it was all the way out, standing, in the ordinary voice, to a man going past who had stopped, and he turned round and said, “Was you talking to me?”

“I was not.”

“You were,” he said. “I heard my name in it.”

She said sorry and he went on and that was the fifth one.

## A Floor And A Roof

She went and took a floor on the ninth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and four.

That was not a thing she had done since the morning of the eleventh of the month beginning three thousand and seventy-four at about the sixth hour, and she found out how long it was when she sat down on it at about the fourth hour of the afternoon and did the subtraction standing up, which is not a thing that can be done sitting down.

Twenty-eight days.

She had been in a room for seven hundred and eighty-three days before that and she had been out of one for twenty-eight, and both numbers were correct and they were about a quarter of what the keeping had been doing to her, and she had got so far from thinking of the arithmetic as belonging to the mountain that she had put those two facts side by side on a floor in a village and had to stop for a count of a hundred before she could go on.

The woman of the house came in at about the fifth hour with a bowl of something hot and put it down on the floor beside her, and did not stay.

Ning Yan looked at the bowl for about a count of forty.

Then she got up and went and found the woman in the yard and said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, that she was not able to be given anything, and that she was sorry, and that the bowl was going to go cold and she was very glad of it.

The woman said that was a strange thing to be and went on with what she was doing.

## Said To Herself

She tried them on herself on the tenth, out loud, standing, in a yard, to nothing.

Not the binding line. The three sentences that come before it, and the fourth one that asked the question. She said them to the inside of her own head, standing, in the ordinary voice, the way she had said six figures a morning at the ninth hour for nineteen years, and she listened to them from the inside the way you listen to your own hand.

The price of it is on the record. I do not know what it costs me. No one has sent you, and nothing is done to you, and no one but me binds you. My name is Ning Yan and I am asking whether you will let me put you into an arithmetic of mine.

That is what it sounds like from in here, she said out loud, standing in a yard, on the tenth of the month. It sounds like four words said to somebody who has already stopped listening.

It did not sound like a sentence that had taken a woman of forty off a stall and put her into a record for the rest of her life. It did not sound like anything. It sounded like weather coming on.

That was what she had made it. Not a spell and not a machine and not a sentence nine hundred years old. Three sentences said in the ordinary voice, over and over, until they were nothing at all, which is how you wear a groove in a road.

## The Part She Was Not Ready For

The part she was not ready for was not that it had gone flat.

It was that she could feel it going flat at the time.

She could stand on that road with a dead lamp in her left hand and a number in her head and say a thing out loud that she knew made a person afraid and less solid and unable to come back out, and she could feel, while she was saying it, that it was going to stop meaning anything to her, and she could keep doing it.

That was new. She had spent nineteen years inside an apparatus that would not let anything go. It went back over the same ground and brought the same figure and would not let an item out of her mouth until it had been said a fourth time and had a date on it, and every sentence she had ever made had been made inside something that would not stop it going.

There was nothing in her now that would stop it.

She went and worked the queue for about four hours after that and she could feel the whole of it going flat in her mouth while she was using it, the way you feel a step that is not there any more, and she did it to about sixty people in those four hours and she did it well.

The day it goes flat and the day it stops working out are going to be the same day. That was the finding. She was not going to be able to put her hand on the moment it happened, because there was not going to be a moment. It was going to be a road she had worn out.

She stood at the end of the queue on the tenth of the month and worked out, standing, that she had spent nineteen years being unable to get away from a single thought and she had got to the other side of it by walking down a mountain, and that the getting to the other side had not made the thought better. It had made her faster.

## The Tenth Day

Two hundred and eighty-eight by the ninth hour on the tenth.

She said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and it came out.

And then she stopped with her mouth still open, because she had not done the sum.

She had sixty-eight on the first of the month and a hundred and forty-nine by the fifth hour of the fourth, and two hundred and twenty-one by the seventh, and after that she had been adding in her head between sentences the way you add while you walk, and it had gone up every time.

The number came out of her mouth in about a count of two, the way the first two hundred days had come out of her mouth, and it was bigger than the last one she had said out loud, and she had not got a row of anything behind it.

She stood on that road with a lamp in her hand that had not carried a flame since the fourteenth of a month and worked out, standing, that she had said out loud a number she could not derive.

That was the rule. She had said it out loud, in her own mouth, since she was ten years old, every morning for nineteen years, that she does not say a number she cannot derive.

And she had just said one out loud, to everybody standing at that end of the road, and the number in it was the number of people in her arithmetic, and there was not one line of work behind it.

She put her hand flat on the wall at the gap in the road and did not move for about a count of a hundred and twenty.

Then she went back to the end of the queue and put the next number out loud to find out whether it came out of her the same way, because she had just told forty people she did not say a number she could not derive.
