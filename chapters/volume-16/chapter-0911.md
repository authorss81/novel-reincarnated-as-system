# The Seventh


“Two hundred and fifty-five days since the ninth stage. Six hundred and eighty-six days in that room. The cage out four hundred and fifty-three. This arm open six hundred and ninety-three days. One thousand four hundred and twenty-eight gone at this morning.”

She said it sitting on the stone at the near end of the table in the making-place, on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and eighty-four, with the lamp burning steadily on the stone beside her on a boy’s oil.

The bar stood six hundred and forty-three days against its wall, dated nowhere.

He had said nothing since the ninth hour of the twenty-ninth.

## Going Looking

That was two days. She had spent both of them at the near end of this table with her hands flat on the register, reading by thumb, finding the seventh cut and the eighth and the ninth and getting nothing out of any of them, and the whole time his half had been in her head the way a room is in a house, present, taking up space, making no sound at all.

She said his name into the room, which was the way she went looking for him and the only way there had ever been.

He was there.

“You have been quiet for two days,” she said.

“I have been quiet for two days,” he said. “I am here. I have not gone anywhere and I have not stopped hearing you and I have not put a figure on a thing once in two days, which for me is the longest anything has ever taken.”

“Why two days.”

## The Same Every Time

“I was at the edge on the twenty-ninth and I have been there since and I did not want to say anything and then say the wrong thing.”

“You said you have been turning it.”

“I have,” he said. “I have turned five mercies and four lines and the sixth cut and the third term, which came up nine hundred and four steps on the first and proved itself, and I have turned them over the way you turn figures, once, twice, forty times, and they come out the same every time and have come out the same every time since the eighth.”

“And.”

“And I cannot do it with this one,” he said.

He said it the way he said dates.

“That is the first thing in six hundred years I have had to turn over that I cannot turn over at all, because there is nothing on either side of it. Five mercies have a keeping on one side and the walls on the other and you can turn them against each other for as long as you have breath.”

She had been sitting with it long enough to hear what he had not put at either end.

“This has nothing on either side of it. There is me on one side and there is a woman on the other and there is no third thing to bring round and set them against, and so it will not turn.”

“You have been sitting in it for two days with nothing to set against it.”

“I have been sitting in it for two days with a date in it,” he said. “That is not nothing. That is the only thing in six hundred years that has ever turned a man who does not go quiet.”

## The Seventh

She sat on the stone with her hands flat on the floor and let the word land where he had put it.

“I am going to say yes,” he said.

The lamp stood on the stone. The register lay on the table with everything in it. The room at the top of a tower had no wear in it and there was no light in it in the world except a boy’s oil in a lamp on a stone floor.

“Not in the morning,” he said. “Not in a count of ninety. I have been at the edge since the twenty-ninth and I have three more days of being at it in me and then I am not going to have it any more.”

“Which day.”
“The seventh of this month,” he said. “That is three days. I have not said it before because a date said out loud in this building is a date that cannot be taken back, and I have had two hundred and fifty-five days since the ninth stage and one hundred and sixty-two days since you left your own room and six hundred years of being a voice.”

She heard where the sentence turned.

“I have never once had a date of my own that I had picked, and I am not going to spend it by telling you at the wrong hour.”


He was quiet for a count of forty.

“It goes in the channel,” he said. “In the ordinary voice, the one you use. There is one thing I have learned from the cut you found on the fourteenth and I have been holding it for nine days and I am telling you what I am going to do with it.”

“You are going to read it before it is a line anywhere.”


“I am going to say yes standing in the only place I can stand, which is here, and in the voice the offer is made in, and the thing that made me is listening in that channel and has been listening in it for six hundred years, and when I say it, it is said. That is what the second of the four says. There is no writing. There is no sign. It is in the mouth it goes out of and it does not come back into it.”

## What Happens to Her


She put her hands flat on the stone and asked the thing she had not asked in two days because she had been afraid of the answer and was no longer.

“What happens to me.”

“The first term stands,” he said. “You go up whole. You are carried and it is not a taking. There is a Ward of your own and you keep it, and none of it costs you anything you have not already spent.”

“And the walls.”

“And the walls,” he said. “And the layer. And the mercy of a bounded world, which is real, and which came up nine hundred and four steps this morning with a boy’s hands on the cord and not one person in the room.”

“So on the seventh of this month,” she said, “the world does not change for anybody, and it does not change for me, and you get what is on the table.”

“No,” he said. “That is not what I am offering and you know it is not, because you have read the second of the four lines and the third of the four lines and the second one is about where it has to be answered and the third one is about not taking it back.”

“Then tell me what it is you are offering, because in two days you have said the word *offer* about nine times and you have not once told me what is on the other side of it for me.”

He was quiet for a count of forty.

## Four Minutes

“I am offering myself against something,” he said. “I have not worked out what. And I am telling you that on the fourth of this month, three days out, in the dark, with no figure on it, because you have spent six hundred years in a machinery and you are owed one true thing before this and it is all I have got.”

She sat very still on the stone floor at the top of a tower.

“I am asking you for four minutes,” he said.

“For what.”

“For not saying anything in it.”

She put her hands flat on the stone.

“I have heard that before,” she said. “I said it to myself on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and twenty-four about a cost and then it came out of me anyway, in her own mouth, into the keeping. I am not being careful. I am not going to hold four minutes the way I have held things.”


“I am not asking you to hold it,” he said. “That is the whole of the difference and I have had two days to get it right and I have not got it right. I am asking you to be in the room.”

She turned round on the stone so that she was facing the middle of the room rather than the table.

“Whatever you are in the middle of, whatever it is that is in your mouth when I get to the edge, it is going to be in your mouth, and I would rather it were in your mouth than in a hand I have asked to be quiet.”


“You have not asked to be quiet.”

“I have asked for four minutes,” he said, “and that is what I am asking for, and I am asking you now on the fourth of the month so that you have three days to refuse me, because you said at the ninth hour on the twenty-ninth that it is not yours to make and I am not going to take that back from you by doing it in a hurry.”

She sat on the stone with a lamp burning on the floor beside her and a man inside her head asking her for the first thing he has ever asked her for.

“Yes,” she said.

He did not say anything for a while, and then he said, “Say the whole of it.”

“I will not stop you and I will not speak and I will be in the room for all four minutes of it,” she said, “and on the seventh of this month at whatever hour you say it, in the ordinary voice, in front of the thing that made you.”

She got up off the stone and stood, because standing was for what mattered, and she put her hands flat on the table edge above the register.

“Is there anything else you want in the four minutes besides me not speaking.”

“Yes,” he said. “I want you to count them.”

“No.”

“I have never in six hundred years been in a room where a woman was counting. That is the whole of the want and it is small.”

“No,” she said. “I am not going to count them. You asked me not to put anything in the way of it and a count is a thing in the way, and I have said a number out loud every morning since the first of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and sixty-four and I am not going to say one inside the only four minutes you have ever asked me for.”

He was quiet for a count of forty.

“That is the first thing you have refused me that I actually asked for in nine years,” he said.


He had said the date out loud in a room at the top of a tower. He had asked her for four minutes and she had given him them standing, in the ordinary voice, with no figure on any of it, and he had three days and she had three days, and the seventh of this month was going to arrive like every other day in this keeping, on time, whether there was anybody standing under it or not.
