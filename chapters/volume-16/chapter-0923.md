# What The Open Is

“There are no conditions,” he said.

She stood at the near end of her own table with the lamp on the stone and did not move.

“Say that again.”

“There are no conditions on it. You read the whole thing by your fingers in the dark on the fourteenth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four and you read it again the following night and you found none, and that is because there are none, and I have had twenty-nine days to find one and there is not one.”

“Then what is the sixth thing about the offer.”

“The sixth thing is not a condition,” he said. “It is what the offer is for.”

## The Tax

“The bargain needs someone to keep the Foundation tax running.”

She stood in her own room with a lamp burning at her feet and let the word go through her one piece at a time.

“Tell me what the Foundation tax is.”

“It is how the keeping finds the one person it can use,” he said. “It is not a toll and it is not a tithe and it is not a thing a person pays. It is a machine, and the machine finds one person at a time, and it puts a voice in that person’s head, and the voice is me.”

She put her hand flat on the table.

“No,” she said. “The machine does not put you in anybody’s head.”

“It puts a piece of itself in,” he said. “I have been telling you for six hundred years that I am a piece of the announcement end of it and you have heard me and you have not once written it down. It is not a piece that turned up by accident in a girl of ten in a furnace crew in Qinghe. It is a piece that was put there, and the putting is the tax, and I designed the putting.”

He said it the way he said dates. Flat, plain, exact.

She sat down on the stone at the near end of the table. She had not decided to and she was not able to stand.

“You designed it.”

“I designed the shape of it six hundred years before I was born out of a man,” he said. “Not the hand that made it and not the words. The shape. How it finds. How long it keeps. What happens at the end of the keeping. I was a piece of the intelligence that runs the layer and I sat in the place where that is decided and I decided it, and it was not an accident and not one person made me do it.”

She did not say anything for a long time.

## The Account, Reported

“Two hundred and eighty-eight days since the ninth stage,” she said, standing, in the ordinary voice, into the dark past the last landing. “Eighty-one since it was shut. This arm open at the elbow seven hundred and twenty-six days. Seven of eleven holds. One thousand four hundred and sixty-one days gone at this morning, taken out of her at a rate she derived out of her own life, and not one of them coming back.”

The line came past her one at a time, plain, in the grammar the keeping used for everything it said. It was not about her and it did not explain anything.

[The Foundation account is open and unanswered. It has not begun.]

She stood there and let it go past and understood that the keeping had just reported, in the same words it used about a bundle of boards going up nine hundred and four steps, that the thing he was being offered the running of was open and waiting for somebody.

## What The Machine Does

“Say the rest of it,” she said.

“The machine finds a person,” he said. “It puts a voice in them. The voice grows them. Everything the voice takes out of the person to grow itself is taken out of the person. That is the whole of the mechanism and it is not a lie and not one word of it has ever been a lie about it.”

“How long.”

“As long as it takes to be useful.”

She stood very still.

“Not a fixed number.”

“No. A fixed number would put it in a column and a column can be read. It runs until it has what it came for and then it files the person it took it from.”

“Files.”

“Remembers them accurately,” he said. “A name. What they did. What it cost. Written down, properly, in the keeping of what they did and not of what it cost, the way the fourth line of that register talks about me, and then not looked at again. That is the whole of the last thing that happens and I am telling you it in a plain voice because I designed it and I want you to have it in exactly the shape it is in.”

## Eleven

“How many times has it run.”

He was quiet for a count of forty.

“Eleven,” he said. “Before you.”

She put her hand flat on the table where her own arms had worn the wood down over nineteen years and she did not put a question to that and she did not need one, because eleven and nine years and one thousand four hundred and sixty-one days were all figures she had in her and they all came out of the same drawer.

The tax was his. It had been his before he had a name. It had been his before he had a body, and before he died apologising to the men who were cutting him open, and it had been his on the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and four when she paid the first of it out of her own life in a room that has been hers since she was ten.

Eleven women had been made less solid by a voice in their heads for as long as it took to be useful. Then filed.

## What She Asked Next

“Tell me their names.”

He was quiet for a long time.

“There are not any.”

“They have names.”

“They had them,” he said. “That is what I am telling you. There is not a column and there is not a place. Eleven people went through that machine over nine hundred years and not one of them is written down anywhere in this keeping except you. The layer does not keep them. It does not keep me either. I have been looking for them since the seventh and there is nothing on the other side of the going except the going.”

She sat on the stone floor of a room no one could come into with her hand flat on the table and understood what she had been told.

It was not a mercy and it was not a cruelty. It was a machine that had been running for nine hundred years, and it had run eleven times before her, and it had thrown all eleven away at the end of the useful part, and there was no room anywhere in nine hundred years in which a single one of them could be found.

“Then somebody is going to have to write them,” she said.

“Yes.”

“Not today.”

“No,” he said, “not today, and not by me, because I have no hands and no board and no place to put one, and not by the layer, because the layer does not keep them. And not by you alone, standing in a room at the ninth hour, writing a list off your own memory of something that has never once been put in front of you.”

She got up off the stone.

“No,” she said. “Not by me alone. That is the whole of what you have just told me and it is going to be the hardest thing I have to do in my life, and I have not got to it for a long while yet, and I am not going to think about it in front of you.”

## What He Said Next

“I know what the tax is,” she said. “I have known what it is since the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and thirty-four and I have never once asked you to soften it, and I am not going to start today.”

“I did not expect you to.”

“Say the other thing,” she said. “You have told me what it is. Say what it means for it to be handed to you.”

He was quiet for a long time.

“It means the arrangement on that table needs somebody in it to keep the tax running,” he said. “It has never needed one before, because it has never needed a person before. It needs one now because I am the only piece of that layer that has ever been able to want a thing, and if I take the place and the name and the work, then the running of the tax goes to me, and it goes on, and there is one of these at a time.”

“There is one of these at a time.”

“There is one of these at a time,” he said, “and it does not stop when the person is filed.”

She stood at the near end of her own table with the lamp on the stone and her arm hanging open against her side for seven hundred and twenty-six days and did the arithmetic standing, the way she had done every morning since she was ten years old.

“The thing you have been offered,” she said, “to keep the world bounded and gentle, is the thing that was done to me.”

“Yes.”

“Done to me, and done to eleven women before me, and going to be done to whoever comes after I am filed.”

“Yes.”

She stood at the near end of the table with the lamp burning at her feet and put the next one out, because it had to go out of her mouth and she had put one thing into the keeping standing this morning already.

“What happens to the one after me.”

“Exactly what happened to you,” he said. “That is the whole of what happens and I am not able to make it smaller by telling it gently. She will be nineteen. She will be somewhere ordinary and she will hear a voice and she will not run, and that is because the machine is very good at it and she will have a reason.”

“Does she know what it is.”

“She will know what you know today, when you tell her, if there is ever a you left to tell.”

Neither of them said anything else about it. There was nothing to argue and neither of them was going to try, because a thing that was true at the ninth hour on the seventh of a month was still true at the ninth hour on the seventh of a month three weeks later and arguing with it would not have made it less true by any part of it.
