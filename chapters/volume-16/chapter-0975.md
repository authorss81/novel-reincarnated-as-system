# Never Chosen

The thirtieth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four was a market day and about forty people came down off the hills before the light with nothing to sell, to stand against a wall and wait for a market that had not started yet, and that was not a thing Ning Yan had arranged and it was the reason she had chosen the day.

She was in the gap before the light. She put the lamp down on the wall outside it, because she was not going to be holding anything, and it had not carried a flame since the fourteenth of the month beginning three thousand and seventy-four and it was not going to start now.

She had not slept on the twenty-seventh or the twenty-eighth or the twenty-ninth, and her breath had gone to nine before she had done anything at all that morning, and nine is what her body does standing still, and it had been four on the flat in a room for nineteen years.

## About The Ninth Hour

She was not brave. She had worked that out on the twenty-eighth of the month and she had not argued with it since.

A container with nothing in it is not a container. She had said that standing, in the ordinary voice, at the top of a stair, before she had bound a single person, and she had built four hundred people on it and there had not been one figure in it that she could not derive.

Standing in a gap in a wall on the thirtieth of a month, she found that the only thing she could think about was that her hands were cold and that the fifth word kept arriving early.

It did not arrive early on the twenty-seventh. She had said that out loud to a wall on the twenty-seventh and had known at the time that it was not true.

## Eight

They came down the road in ones and twos after the light and they stood along it in a line going back towards the pass, because that is what people do on a road, and she had told them the day and the hour and the place and had not told them to stand in anything.

Kan Shou came up off the flat with her shutter across her arm. He Yu came with the daughter-in-law, who had never been told a single thing and who had come anyway. The carter with the broken shaft came about the eighth hour and stood four paces to her left. Guo Sheng came and stood at the back where he always stood. The man with the yoke came and stood on the wall.

The woman of about sixty came and sat down on the wall, which was where she sat.

The boy of about seventeen, who had asked on the seventh of the month whether it came off the ceiling for them too, came, and stood about ten paces off, and did not come any closer.

That was eight.

She counted them standing in the gap, out loud, in the ordinary voice, because she counts, and she got eight, and she could not check it, because she cannot be met where she is expected and she cannot follow a name she is given, and four of the eight of them are in the arithmetic and four are not, and there are three hundred and ninety-six people in nine counties who were never told there was a place to stand.

She said that last part out loud to the eight of them before she said anything else at all.

## The Arithmetic Spoken First

“I am glad you came.”

“Do not say it like that,” Kan Shou said. “You have been getting things wrong in front of people for a month and saying so out loud is the whole of the correction there is. Do not take it back with a pleasant word on top.”

“All right,” Ning Yan said. “Then I will not say it at all, and I will start with the arithmetic.”

Then she said the arithmetic, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the rain, with the eight of them along a road behind her.

“The price of it is on the record. I do not know what it costs me. Not one person was sent and nothing is done to you and I am the only one who binds you. Four hundred of you said yes on a road. Not one of you has been asked to do anything and not one of you is going to be.”

“The record is for a sentence. I am going to say nine words at the ninth hour at this gap, in the ordinary voice. It is going to take the ceiling off. It may not work. I do not know what it does to you. I have had twenty-nine days to find that out and I have not found it out and I am telling you that standing up before I do anything at all.”

“Not one person can say no to it now, and I am not going to pretend otherwise. Not one person could say no to it when they said yes, and not one person can say no to it now. That is the price and I put it on the record at the start of this and I am putting it on the record again this morning.”

Somebody at the back said, “Say the last part again.”

She said the last part again, standing, in the ordinary voice, and a man in the second county who had not come was going to hear it from somebody by the end of the week.

## The Sentence

And then she said the nine words.

She said them standing, in the gap, in the ordinary voice, at about the ninth hour on the thirtieth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four, first in her own voice, before it was a line anywhere, with eight people standing along a road behind her and about forty other people against a wall two hundred yards off with nothing to sell.

“Never chosen. I was told. There is a difference.”

She got to *Never chosen* before she got to the fifth word, which was on the twenty-seventh the thing she could not do at all.

## What Took It

The ground under her boots went quiet.

It is a quiet she has known since she was ten years old. It is the quiet that comes up through the earth when the keeping takes a thing, and the only other time she had ever felt it under her feet in the open was on the first of a month with a bundle of boards going up nine hundred and four steps.

Nothing came up.

That was the whole of what happened in the road. The rain went on falling at the same rate it had been falling since before the light, and eight people were standing along a road, and a carter was four paces off her with a broken shaft, and nothing came up and nothing came back and nothing ever does.

She waited about a count of four hundred, because she has waited for everything in her life for exactly as long as it took, and then she stopped waiting, because there was no more of it.

And then the quiet under her feet did not stop.

That was new, and it was the only new thing.

A bundle going up nine hundred and four steps takes about a count of six hundred and then the ground is ordinary ground again, and she has known that since she was ten years old standing at the foot of a stair. The ground under her on the thirtieth of a month went on being quiet for the rest of that hour, and she could not stand still for it and she had to keep shifting her weight.

## What She Found

And then, with not one of the eight looking at her, because every one of them was looking up the road instead, she put two fingers flat on her own ribs on the left side, high, behind the fourth one, and pressed.

There was skin.

Not a place in the skin. Skin, the ordinary kind, with nothing at all under it, the same as the two inches below it and the two inches above it.

She had been a child when there was a place there the size of her thumbnail and it had never once been any different size on any single day she had put her hand on it, and it had never once hurt, and there was nothing there now.

She kept her fingers where they were for about a count of a hundred.

## The Rule She Kept

She did not say it out loud.

That was the discipline and it was not about the layer and it was not fear. A thing is said in the voice of the person it is about, standing, before it is a line anywhere, and she did not know what that was.

She had no figure for it. She had no idea whether a place closing meant that a keeping had stopped, or whether it meant that something else had happened to her body on a wet morning while she was standing in a gap in a wall. She was not going to put a thing into nine hundred years of a record that she could not derive.

So she took her hand off her ribs and said nothing at all, and that was the first time in nineteen years she had found anything out about herself and had not said it out loud.

## The Tenth Hour

The rain stopped somewhere in the middle of the tenth hour, and the eight people behind her went quiet all at once, all together, for no reason at all that she could see.

A dog stopped in the middle of the road. A carter with an empty cart came up from the flat at about the tenth hour and turned round and went back down it without stopping, and Ning Yan watched him do it and did not understand it.

Nothing came down.

She had no instrument for anything that was not her own ribs. There was no report and there is no office and there is no line anywhere in nine hundred years that is going to tell her, and the road was a road and the wall was a wall and the market started about the eleventh hour the way it does.

He Yu came forward about ten paces and stopped about four short.

“What do we do now.”

That was the same question Guo Sheng had asked her at this gap on the nineteenth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and four, and a woman of about fifty had asked it then and she had gone and found out how far the queue had got, because there had been a queue to find out about.

There is no queue.

There is no office and there is no list and there is no road to three hundred and ninety-two people and there is nothing behind her but a mountain with a bundle that came up on time, and there is not one other person in the world who has any idea that nine words were said at a gap in a wall this morning, and she is the only person in the world who can be asked what happens next.

She put her left hand flat on the stone of the gap, because it was the only thing there she could put a hand on.

“I do not know,” Ning Yan said.

And that was the answer, and it was true, and it was the first true thing she had said in nineteen years that had no figure behind it. Not one of the eight of them was in a position to hear the difference. She was not going to stop and point at it.
