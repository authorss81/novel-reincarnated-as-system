# The Arithmetic For It

Four hundred and twenty-six days since the ninth stage. Two hundred and nineteen since it was shut. Eight hundred and fifty-seven days in that room, and seventy-four days out of one. Eight hundred and sixty-four days with this arm open at the elbow.

One thousand five hundred and ninety-nine days gone, taken out of her at a rate she derived out of her own life, not one of them coming back.

She said all of it standing at the ninth hour on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four, in the ordinary voice, at the gap in the low wall, first in her own voice before it is a line anywhere, with rain coming in off the flat and about six people on the road.

## What She Had To Say

She had to say the arithmetic for it.

That was not a preference of hers and it was not an instrument. She had told four hundred people that the price was on the record and that she did not know what it cost her, and she was going to say a sentence about the walls on the thirtieth of the month in front of whoever was standing at a gap in a wall, and the only thing she had ever been able to say to a person out loud for nineteen years was a figure.

So on the twenty-fifth of a month she sat down on the wall in the rain and did the whole of it, and she did it the only way she has ever done anything, which is from the top, down, each part against another part.

## The Column She Started With

Four hundred.

She said it out loud and she derived it standing up in about a count of six hundred, all the way from the twentieth of a month on a hillside, and it came to four hundred exactly, and it was the only figure in her life that had ever got away from her.

Then she put the next one under it.

Six thousand mornings. Nineteen years of a figure at the ninth hour, every day, standing, in the ordinary voice, in a room and then on a road and then out in the open air.

Then nine hundred years of a ceiling.

Then one thousand five hundred and ninety-nine days gone, and a rate she can derive out of her own life with a stub of charcoal on the flat of a board.

Then eight hundred and fifty-seven days in a room that ended on the eleventh of a month beginning three thousand and seventy-four.

Then three hundred and ninety-nine names said out loud in twenty-nine days, of which she can put a face to about thirty and a name to about fifty.

## The One She Did Not Explain

And then she put a fifth one under the others and said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the rain, at a gap in a wall, on the twenty-fifth of a month.

“A hundred and eighteen days since the twenty-seventh of the month beginning three thousand and fourteen.”

Then she left it where it was.

She did not say what happened on that day and she did not say what it weighed and she did not say why it was in the column at all, and she went straight on to the sixth figure, which is the day she is going to say the sentence on, and which is five days from that wall.

## Where It Would Not Close

And then the whole of it stopped working, and she got that out loud as well, standing, in the rain, to about six people who had no idea at all what she was doing.

She put nine words in the column.

That is where the instrument ran out. She had known it was going to happen since the fourth of the month and she had gone on anyway.

She had been taking figures out of the air and checking them twice every morning since she was ten years old, and it was the only thing in the world that had ever found out a fact for her, and it had one scale in it, and the scale was made of days.

There was nothing on that scale for nine words.

She put the hand with three fingers and a thumb on it flat on the wet stone and worked at it for about a count of three hundred.

“Not one of my own figures will tell me what nine words weighs. That is what I have found out this morning, and it is the first time in nineteen years that my own arithmetic has come back with nothing, and I am going to say nine words in five days and I am going to have to do it with no instrument at all.”

## Building It From The Column Instead

She sat in the rain and looked at the column for about a count of a hundred and twenty, and then she did the thing she does, which is to say it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, whether anybody hears it or not.

“Right. Then it is not a column. Then it is a sentence, and a sentence is made out of figures I have already said out loud, and it has to be true, and it has to be in the plainest words I have, and it does not get to have anything in it that I have not already put into a keeping standing up in the ordinary voice.”

And then she built it, out loud, in the rain, at a gap in a low wall, on the twenty-fifth of a month, in about a count of two thousand.

“The price of it is on the record. I do not know what it costs me. Not one person has sent you, and nothing is done to you, and I am the only one who binds you.”

“Four hundred of you said yes on a road. There is no office and there is no list and not one of you has been sent anywhere and there is no name for what you are.”

“It is for a sentence. I am going to say nine words on the thirtieth of this month at the ninth hour at the gap in this wall, in the ordinary voice, and it is going to take the ceiling off.”

“It may not work. I do not know what it does to you. I have had twenty-nine days to find that out and I have not found it out, and I am telling you that standing up before I do anything at all.”

She said the whole of it once, and then stopped, and sat down again.

## The Man Who Stopped

The carter with the broken shaft had been coming up that road for three weeks with the same shaft, or a new one that was going the same way, and he stopped on the twenty-fifth about two thirds of the way through it.

“You have been standing in the rain for an hour talking to a wall.”

“Yes.”

“You said ‘it may not work’ out loud.”

“I said it in the ordinary voice.”

“You said it in the ordinary voice to about six people who were not asking you anything.”

“That is the rule and I cannot make an exception for a reason.”

The carter put a hand on his cart.

“Say it again slower and I will listen to the slow one,” he said, “because I have got two hundred people in the second county and you have not told one of them anything and neither am I, and if you are saying this on a road then it is going to get out of a road, and it got out of this one about three weeks ago without either of us.”

So she said it again, slower, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the rain, to a man of forty-one with a broken shaft, and she got to the fourth part of it and the fourth part was the one about the ceiling coming off, and he put his hand flat on the side of his cart the way he had put it there three weeks ago.

“What is it going to do to the ones who said yes.”

“I do not know.”

“Not to you.”

“To any of you,” Ning Yan said. “I have not found it out and I have told you that and I am going to keep telling you that until I find out or until I stop, and I may stop.”

He stood there in the rain for a while with his hand on the cart.

“Say it on the thirtieth, then,” he said, “and say that part loud, because there are two hundred people in the second county who are going to find out about it from somebody else and I would rather it was the two of us.”

## What She Did Not Say Out Loud

There was one more thing she did on the twenty-fifth of a month and she did not say it to anybody.

She worked out standing, in the rain, at a gap in a wall, what it would be to be wrong, and she found that she could not put a figure on that either, and that the whole of nineteen years of arithmetic had been built on the assumption that being wrong was a thing that happens to figures and not to people.

She has not been wrong in front of a person since she was ten years old and she has never once been wrong about a sentence.

There is a difference between those two things and it is the same difference, and she did not say that out loud to the rain either, because she has made her own decision about what goes into a keeping and she was not going to break it on a morning when she was about to ask nine hundred years of a record to take something from it.

## Where The Instrument Ran Out

That is where the day went and that is the end of it and it is not enough.

She has had an instrument since she was ten years old and it has never once come back with nothing, and she has worked it out loud to the sky on six thousand mornings and it has never once been wrong in front of her, and on the twenty-fifth of a month it sat in the rain and could not weigh nine words.

And there is nothing else in the world to put in their place.

No register, no board, no book, no office, no list, no other instrument anywhere in nine hundred years. Four hundred people in an arithmetic that does not close, a fifth figure on a wet wall that she put there and did not explain, and five days.
