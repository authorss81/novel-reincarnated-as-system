# An Empty Place

The fog came up off the flat before the light on the thirteenth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four and it stayed on the ground all day, and she went out into it because she could not do this in front of anybody, and the only way to stand in front of an empty place is to walk a long way in one direction and then stop.

She walked until there was nothing behind her that was a road with people on it.

## The Fourth Term

She said the rule out loud standing in the fog at about the seventh hour, which she does, and it came out the way it has come out four times in nineteen years.

A thing is said in the voice of the person it is about, standing, before it is a line anywhere.

Four times. A boy at a table in a mountain who had been told nothing. A number said at a gap in a wall on the last day of a month that went to nine hundred years of a keeping on its own. Three hundred and ninety-nine people out of her mouth one at a time over twenty-nine days. And a refusal, said standing in the ordinary voice, with somebody else in the room, on a night she has not gone back to in her own head since.

Four, and she had used every one of them on something she had already decided was true.

This was the fifth, and she did not know yet whether it was true, and she was going to say it anyway, and that was the difference between the fifth one and the other four and it was the reason she had walked out into the fog.

## What She Said

She stood in the fog with her back to nothing at all and her hands where anybody could see them, which was the shape of it, and she said the nine words.

“Never chosen. I was told. There is a difference.”

She said it standing, in the ordinary voice, first in her own voice, before it was a line anywhere.

Then she shut her mouth and waited.

## What Came Back

Nothing came.

She stood in the fog for about a count of six hundred, which is longer than it sounds, and she felt the ground under her boots the whole time because she had felt a bundle come up under her on a first of a month and she knew exactly what it was like when the keeping takes something.

The ground did not do anything.

She said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to a fog with not one living thing in it: now say it back.

And nothing said it back, and the fog went on being fog, and her own voice came back off about forty paces of wet air and that was all there was.

## The Third Reason

She had three reasons and she put them in order standing, in the ordinary voice, in the fog, from the top, down, each part against another part, the way she does.

It is not true. She had checked it for about a count of eight hundred on the tenth of a month, standing, in bad weather, and it was true, and she could not show it to anybody, and true was the only part of it she had any confidence in.

It is in the wrong shape. That was possible, and it was the reason she had spent a day on the length and a day on the grammar and a night on the order of the words, and she had not spent them because she had a feeling.

Or it needs a person, and there is not one here.

That was the third one and it was the one she could not argue with, because the rule says a thing is said in the voice of the person it is about, and she had put her own voice to it and had said it standing and had said it first, and there had been no part of the rule that had anything to say about somebody being there to hear it, and she had never once tested it.

## The Longest Part Of It

The waiting was the part she had not planned and the part she was worst at.

She is good at one thing. She can stand in the open in bad weather with an empty road in front of her and say a number out loud at the ninth hour, and she has done it six thousand mornings, and it has never cost her anything, because a number is the only kind of sentence she has ever had that she can put down on the ground in front of herself and pick up again.

A number can be checked. That is the whole of why she has kept every one of them for nineteen years. It is not because she is strong. It is because a number stands still after you have said it.

She said the nine words and then there was nothing to check them against. There was no second reading and no second figure and no pair to put the pair against, and the whole of the apparatus she has used on every single morning of her life was sitting in her head with nothing at all to do in it.

So she stood in the fog with two fingers on her own ribs and pressed, and the place behind the fourth one was there, the size of a thumbnail, and it was the same size it had been on the tenth of a month and the same size it had been on the nineteenth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and four.

## What It Costs To Have Said It

She said the whole of it out loud at about the third hour in the fog, standing, in the ordinary voice, to nothing.

“I have just said the truest thing anybody has ever said in this county, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and it went out of my mouth and there was nothing at the other end of it.

“I have said three hundred and ninety-nine names out loud in twenty-nine days and a count that went into a keeping on its own, and I have said a sentence about myself for the first time in nineteen years and the fog is the same fog it was a count of six hundred ago.”

And then she did the thing she had been not doing for about a count of four hundred.

She tried to take it back.

“I withdraw what I have just said. It was said to an empty place and there was no one in it and it is not on the record and I am not bound by it and it is not a sentence.”

That was the withdrawal she had made in front of forty people on the thirteenth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and four with a carter’s promise in it, and it did not take that one either, and she had known it would not before she opened her mouth, and she had opened it.

It runs one way.

She had spent nineteen years finding that out and she had spent it again in a fog with nothing in it, and the second time was worse, because the first time had been about a carter and this one was about her.

## The Thing That Is Left

She stood in the fog and worked out what she had, and it was worse than nothing and it was not nothing.

She had said it. It could not be un-said. Whether it was in a keeping in a mountain nine hundred years old was a thing she could not find out and could not ask about and could not wait for an answer to, because that keeping had never once answered her a question in nineteen years.

So the whole of what she had learned from walking into a fog was that she could not check it, and that not being able to check it was the ordinary condition of everything she had ever done, and she had spent nineteen years pretending that her arithmetic made her different from other people.

It did not make her different. It made her faster.

## The Road Back

She came back in at about the seventh hour with the fog gone off the flat and the road exactly where it had been.

Kan Shou was at her stall with a jar in each hand. Two boys were arguing about a pig. The carter with the broken shaft had come back to the same broken shaft with a different one. The woman of about sixty who had wanted to know about her son was sitting on the wall at the gap in the road in the place she always sat, and she got up and made room for Ning Yan on the wall and did not ask her a single question.

That was the ordinary evening of a road in one county on the thirteenth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four and it was happening exactly as it had happened the evening before and the evening before that, and the nine words were in the air somewhere behind her and not one living person had heard them.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, on the wall, with five people within hearing, that she had said a thing out in the flat and that she was not going to say what it was yet, and that she was going to wait a whole day to find out whether it had gone in, and that if it had not she was going to say it again.

She did not tell them what she was waiting for. She had four hundred people in her arithmetic and about nine of them standing within calling and not one of them had asked her a question about herself in twenty-nine days, and she was not going to hand it to them before she knew.

Then she lay down on the wet stones at the gap with the lamp beside her and did not sleep again, and the day she was waiting for began.
