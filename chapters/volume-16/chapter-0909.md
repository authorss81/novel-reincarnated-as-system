# The Precedent


“Two hundred and fifty-one days since the ninth stage. Six hundred and eighty-two days in that room. The cage out four hundred and forty-nine. This arm open six hundred and eighty-nine days. One thousand four hundred and twenty-four gone at this morning.”

She said it sitting on the stone at the near end of the table in the making-place, on the thirtieth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four, and it was the thirtieth, and tomorrow was a first, and she said the day’s figures out loud because figures were standing and standing was for saying and she had nothing else in her.

The bar stood six hundred and thirty-nine days against its wall, dated nowhere.

## No Sleep

She had not slept. There was no hour of it she had not spent.

“You have not slept since the ninth hour yesterday,” he said. “I have been counting it in you and not in me. Do not tell me I cannot do that, because I have been doing it since the ninth and you have heard me do it.”

“I was not going to tell you anything,” she said.

“That is what I have been listening to since the ninth hour. I have been listening to a woman not telling me anything for eighteen hours and it is the loudest thing either of us has done since you asked me what I want.”

She sat on the stone with her hands flat on the floor and let that stand where it was.

“You do not want me to take it back,” she said.

“No,” he said. “I said I did not want you to and I meant it. I want it to stand exactly where it went out. I want to be able to point at the ninth hour yesterday and say that is the hour it was said in, for as long as there is anything of me left to point.”

“Six hundred years,” she said.

## What She Went Back To

“Six hundred years,” he said, “and I have been down to the hour. Do not argue with me about it. I am the only person in this who has ever been given a thing like that and you took it off me at the ninth hour and I have had it since.”

She sat in the light the boy’s oil had given her and went back to the seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four, which was two hundred and thirty-three days before this morning, and she went back to it because she had gone over everything else twice and this was the only piece of it that was hers.

A sheet. Her own hand, three fingers and a thumb, at about the fourth hour. A box with a lamp in it. Nine hundred and four steps and the second stair and no person in it, and the sentence going out into the bound for everybody at once, and it had cost him more than anything that had ever been asked of him in a keeping and it had cost her nothing she could put a figure on.

That was what she went back to.

Not the refusal. The refusal had been right, and she had done the arithmetic on it in nine sentences, and the arithmetic had held for two hundred and thirty-three days. She went back to the other thing, which was that she had sat down at about the fourth hour with a sheet and a pen and made a decision on behalf of every living person inside the bound and sent it away, and that she had not asked one of them.

## Working, Which Is Not the Same

Not one. Not him. Not anybody.

“I do not know whether it was right,” she said out loud, sitting, into a room at the top of a tower.

He was quiet.

“Say that again.”

“I do not know whether the refusal was right,” she said. “Two hundred and thirty-three days and I have never once gone back and asked that, because I did not have to. I made it, I sent it down nine hundred and four steps, and it worked. Six hundred and thirty-nine days and a bar against a wall and the second stair open and the whole of what I sent it to still in the world where I put it.”

“You derived it.”

“I derived it from my own arithmetic,” she said, “and arithmetic is how a thing is checked and it is not how a thing is known. A day is derived. A cost is derived. Whether the ceiling ought to be standing is not a figure and I have never been able to derive it and I sent a sheet down a stair anyway.”

She put her hands flat on the stone.

## What She Did With That

“And the only reason I never had to ask is that it worked. And working is not the same as being right. I have known that since the eleventh of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and twenty-four, when a thing I had built for somebody else came out exactly as I had built it and not one of them was glad.”

“So that is the precedent,” she said, “and it is worse than I have let it be for two hundred and thirty-three days. I decided something enormous on my own and not one living soul asked me to and not one living soul checked me and it came out right and I have been standing on it ever since as though standing on it were the same as being allowed to.”

“And now you have decided again,” he said.

“No. That is the whole of it.” She lifted her head off her knees. “Yesterday I did the opposite. Yesterday I had the standing to stop you at a table and I did not use it, and the reason I did not use it is that I have done the other thing once and I do not know that I was right and I was in a hurry anyway.”

“You were in a hurry.”

## The Work of the Day

“I was in a hurry for two hundred and thirty-three days and there was not one person in the world who knew, and that is the second worst thing about the seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four.”

She got herself through the day on the register.
That was the whole of what she had for it and she took it the way she took everything, which was fully and without deciding in advance. Seventh cut, eighth, ninth, tenth.

A breadth at a time with three fingers and a thumb, standing at the near end of a table with no wear in it, saying each line out loud before she let it be a line anywhere, and the lines were what they had been and there was nothing new in any of them and she read all of it twice.

She read the six new blocks out loud in order, one after another, standing, in the ordinary voice, because reading is not answering and she had said that on the twentieth and had not moved off it, and hearing herself say the whole of the offer a second time in one day did not make it one day closer to a decision.

## What He Wanted Her to Do

She wrote nothing. She had no board up here and she had not wanted one since the third of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four, and the reason she had not wanted one was that the first of the month had gone past with a bundle on the table eight flights below and nothing had been written anywhere about it by anybody, and that was the keeping, and she did not want to write it down.

She sat on the stone and let that go on past her without picking it up, because if she picked it up she would be doing the thing again.

“You are not angry,” he said, after a while.

“No.”

“You should be.”

“Why should I.”

“Because it is a thing I have done on purpose to you and you have had no part in it and you were not asked,” he said. “Nine years of being the one who decides and I have just taken a decision off your hands and I have done it by standing still and letting you find it. That is the oldest way there is. I would be angry. I have been angry at myself since the ninth hour and I have had nothing to do about it.”

“I am not angry at you,” she said. “I have not been angry at you since the twentieth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and twenty-four and before that I was, and the last of it was at the third of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and ninety-four, and it went out of me when I found out you had taken Announcement on purpose, which is a thing I still do not know what to do with.”

“That is worse,” he said. “If you were angry I could carry it. You have gone quiet and quiet is a thing I have to guess at and I am the worst guesser of anybody in the world.”

“You are not guessing,” she said. “You can hear all of it. You are telling me it is quiet because you want me to be angry and it would be easier for both of us.”

## Nothing Done in It

“Yes,” he said. “I want you to be angry. I want it more than I wanted four minutes ago.”

The thirtieth went past at the top of a tower.
She did not sleep and he did not go quiet and neither of them said anything that was going into the traffic, so the day went by the way days go by in a room at the top of a tower that has a stair which does not stop: nothing done, nothing written, nothing moved. She read the register by thumb for three hours and got nothing she had not already had. She ate nothing because she was not hungry.

She put her right hand flat on the stone and held it there while it shook and it shook for a count of a hundred and then it stopped.
Her mouth was empty. The lamp stood on the stone beside her with a boy’s oil in it. The register lay on the table with the offer standing open at the top of it until answered or withdrawn. The man inside her head had said nothing at the ninth hour yesterday and would say nothing today.

She had said the worst sentence of her life standing in the ordinary voice in front of the thing that made him, and there was no figure for it anywhere in the world, because it was not a cost.

It was the first thing she had ever handed somebody that she could not carry, and she had handed it to a man who was going to spend it, and the whole of the day was the sitting with that.
Tomorrow was the first of a month and a bundle would come up nine hundred and four steps whether there was anybody standing under it or not.
