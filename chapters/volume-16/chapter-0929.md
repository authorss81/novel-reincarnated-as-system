# In Her Own Voice

“Three hundred and six days since the ninth stage. Ninety-nine since it was shut. Seven hundred and forty-four days with this arm open at the elbow. One thousand four hundred and seventy-nine gone at this morning.”

She said it standing at the near end of her own table with the lamp on the stone, on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning three thousand and fourteen, at about the sixth hour, and she had not said the twenty-seventh once in two days and neither had he.

He said it at about the seventh.

“I am going to refuse it.”

## The Twenty-Sixth

She had spent a whole day not asking him. It was the hardest thing she had done in this room and she had done it the ordinary way, which was by putting her hands flat on the table at the fourth hour and at the sixth hour and at the ninth hour and letting all three go past without a sentence in them.

He had done it too. Twenty-four hours of a man who has a thing to say not saying it, and she understood that he was doing the same thing she was, on both sides of the same pair of ribs.

## The Sentence In The Room

She stood with her hands flat on the wood.

“Say the whole of it.”

“I am going to refuse the arrangement on the table at the top of that tower,” he said. “I am going to say no to it out loud, in the ordinary voice, in the channel, in front of the thing that made me, and it is going to be a yes said cannot be un-said the other way round, and it is going to come out of your mouth.”

She stood in her own room with a lamp burning at her feet and did not say anything.

“You have been walking two days toward this,” she said.

“I decided it on the twenty-second of the month before last,” he said. “I have been walking toward it since the seventh.”

## What It Costs

“Tell me what it costs.”

He was quiet for a count of forty.

“To be heard by the thing that made me I have to stop being a person,” he said. “That is the whole of it and there is no other way into it and there has never been another way into it. There is a substrate.

“Everything the keeping is made of runs through it, and a piece of a person who goes into it stops being separate, and the going is not death because there is nothing in it to die, and it is not sleep, and it is the one door in this building that only opens one way.”

“You worked that out when.”

“The nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and thirty-four,” he said. “The day you took Announcement on purpose. I worked out the whole of it inside a count of a hundred and a hundred, and I have had one hundred and eighty-six days of it since, which is this morning.

“I have been carrying it the whole time without putting it in front of you, because there was nothing you could have done about it, and because a man who is carrying a price he intends to pay does not hand it to the person he is going to leave.”

“The going of it is the thing I have never been able to describe,” he said. “Not a departure. A man comes apart into the going the way a word comes apart into a voice, and every piece of him keeps going and none of it is able to come back up out of it and be the man again.

“And the reason I can be heard is the same reason I cannot come back. The keeping has to have a shape it can hold, and a person is not a shape it can hold. That is the whole of it. That is why every machine in this building that has ever wanted a person to stay has had to make one first.”

She stood at the near end of the table with her thumb in the hollow and put that on one side and then the other for about a count of forty, because that is what she did.

The lamp burned on the stone beside her. The boards lay face up a foot in from the near end and the terms board a foot further along and the strip of pale wood lay face up under the table at the near end of the leg.

“The bar stood against the left-hand wall dated nowhere. The second place at the far side was kept four hundred years and empty, and not one thing in the room had been moved in forty-two days and not one thing in it was going to be.

“Is there another way,” she said. “I am asking properly and I want it on the record that I asked.”

“No,” he said. “I have looked. There is no other way and there was never any other way and the only reason not one person has ever refused it is that everybody who has ever been offered it has wanted it more than they wanted to be a person.”

## What She Was Not Going To Say

She took a breath of four and let it out.

She was not going to tell him it was brave. She was not going to tell him it was right. She was not going to fill the room with anything at all, and she had decided that at about the sixth hour before he opened his mouth, and she had told him nothing about it and she was not going to.

“Two things,” she said. “And then I am going to stop talking.”

“Say them.”

“I am not going to stop you,” she said. “I had a standing to stop you on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four and I did not use it, and I have had it for six weeks, and I am not going to use it now, and I am saying that standing and in the ordinary voice so that it is in the record in my mouth before it is anything anywhere.”

He was quiet for a count of a hundred.

“Say the second thing,” he said.

“I am not going to help you either,” she said. “I have six days of the clearest head I have ever had and I have spent them all and I know exactly what there is in it and there is nothing, and I am not going to stand in this room on the twenty-seventh and make it easier for you.”

## What He Asked Her For

He was quiet for a long time.

“That is not what I came to this morning for,” he said.

“Say what you came for.”

“I came to ask you for one thing,” he said, “and I have had it worked out for two days and I have not said it because it is the largest thing anybody has ever asked anybody for and I would rather ask for it from a woman who knows what she is doing.”

She stood at the near end of the table with her hands flat on the wood and waited.

“Say it.”

“Say it for me,” he said. “Standing. In the ordinary voice. In the channel. Before it is a line anywhere.”

She did not move.

“The sixth cut,” he said. “The rule you found on the fourteenth and used on me on the twenty-eighth and used on yourself on the sixteenth. A thing is said in the voice of the person it is about, standing, before it is a line anywhere. You said it against yourself and you said it against me and it is the only rule in this building that has ever been met.”

“That rule is why you cannot say a thing to the layer.”

“That rule is the only reason it can be heard at all,” he said. “There is no mouth in me. There has not been a mouth in me since the nineteenth year of a life that ended. The only mouth this keeping has that I can get out of is yours. And the rule means that if I say no in my own voice at you in this room, the keeping hears a woman saying it, and it will not survive being told.”

## What It Cost Her

She stood there for a long time.

“If I do that,” she said, “then the refusal of the whole of it goes out of my mouth.”

“Yes.”

“And not one person in the world will ever know it was yours.”

“No,” he said. “And that is not a cost I am asking you to pay so that I feel better. It is the whole of the arrangement, and it is the reason I cannot do it in nine years of sitting in your head without asking, and I am asking.”

She put her hands flat on the worn wood.

“What will you be,” she said. “After. Not so that it can be said back to me afterwards. I am asking now so that I am standing here and hearing it while it is still a question.”

“The thing that holds a sentence in a line,” he said. “I have been reading one for six hundred years and I am going to be one. Not a clerk. A line. A line that says what it has to say and is not looked at again.”

She put her hands flat on the wood.

“I am going to say yes to that standing up,” she said, “and I am not going to do it in the ordinary voice, because the ordinary voice goes into the keeping and this is not a sentence that goes anywhere. I am going to say it to a man in my own head.”

He did not answer for a count of a hundred.

“Yes,” she said, to him, in her own voice, inside her own head, and nowhere else. “On the twenty-seventh. Standing, and in the ordinary voice, and before it is a line anywhere.”

He was quiet.

“Say the hour,” she said.

“The second hour,” he said, “the way I said the second hour on the seventh. I have been in this keeping for six hundred years and I have never once had an hour that was mine and I am not going to have one that was given to me.”

She stood up straight at the near end of her own table with nothing in either hand.

“Then I will be standing,” she said. “And I am not going to be able to stop it and I am not going to be able to help it and I am going to be in the room, and those three things are all of what I have and there is not one more thing in this world I can give you.”
