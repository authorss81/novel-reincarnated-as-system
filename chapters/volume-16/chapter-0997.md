# The Broken Kiln

She went back to the Broken Kiln on the seventh of a month beginning seven thousand nine hundred and thirty-four because she had been asked in the yard in the fourth county whether anybody had ever gone and looked at it, and she had said no, and then she had walked four days east to find out why she had said no so easily.

The yard was a ruin of fired brick and a collapsed roof with a chimney broken at half its height and a yard wall of fallen stone, and there was no lock on anything and no sign that anybody came, and she had been fourteen years old the last time she stood in it.

There was a hole in the ground at the back of it about the size of a door.

## What Was Holding It

She stood over the hole for about a count of six hundred before she understood what she was looking at.

There was nothing on it. There was no stone over it, no mark cut in a stone, no bar, no wedge, no weight, and there had been one the last time she had been there, because a seal is a thing that is put on and it is put on by something that is still running.

The ground under the hole was ordinary ground. She put her hand flat into it up to the second knuckle and it was cold in the ordinary way and it was earth and there was a stone in it about the size of an egg.

That was the whole finding and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the back of the yard of the Broken Kiln, to about four people who had walked four days with her because there had not been anything else for them to do and she had not been able to send them anywhere.

“The seal is not open,” she said. “There is no seal. There is a hole in the ground and a woman standing next to it.

“For nineteen years I had understood that there was a thing in the world that could hold a place shut, and I never once found out what it was made of, and I have just found out that it took something that is not here any more, and that is all it ever was. There is not one place in this world that is shut now.”

A man of about thirty who had come with her said, “Is that a good thing.”

“I do not know,” Ning Yan said. “I am not going to tell you it is a good thing. It took nine hundred years and a keeping and about four hundred people I cannot undo, and the thing that did the holding has gone, and what it was holding has let go, and that is three facts and none of them is a judgement.”

## What Was Under It

They got it out at about the fourth hour with a bar and about six of them, because she could not do it with one arm and had never been able to.

It was under about two feet of ground and it was in three pieces and it was wrapped in oiled cloth that had gone hard and black, and the cloth came off whole, and under the cloth there was a single fired plate the size of her hand.

She had held it before. She had carried it against her skin inside a coat for about a week when she was fourteen, with a copy of what was on it written out in her own hand in the lining, and she had read it by window light and traced the small brushed lines on its face, and it had a name on it that was not her brother’s and a date and a disposition in three words.

She sat down on a fallen stone in the yard of the Broken Kiln at about the fifth hour and put it in her left hand, because that was the hand that carried the lamp, and she had never once put two things in one hand.

## The Part That Did Not Happen

She put her hand flat on it and waited.

That is the whole of what she can say about it and she has said it out loud standing in front of everybody who has asked her about it since. She waited for about a count of four hundred for anything at all to happen.

The first time, she had laid her hand flat on it through a coat and it had gone warm under her palm and stayed warm until she took her hand off. And then she had put it back in that hole with her own two hands and covered it over, because a week of it was up and it was not hers.

Nothing came up through her hand this time. There was no warmth anywhere else in her, and there was no second feeling in her palm, and there was not one thing in the world that answered her from anywhere, and the ground under the yard of the Broken Kiln was ordinary ground.

That was the cost and it was the whole of the cost and she had not known when she set out on the four days walk that it was going to be that and she had not known when she sat down that it was going to be that and she had not known anything at all until the count of four hundred was finished.

She was thirty-two years old and she had put her hand on the only object in the world that had ever let a person hold something and it had not let one single thing happen, and it had never been the plate.

## The Four Who Walked With Her

The four who walked with her had not wanted to be there by the second day.

One of them was a woman of about twenty-two who had come on the second of the month because there had not been anything else in the yard that week, and one was a man of about thirty who had heard about the hole from a man who had heard it from the woman who had been in the eighth county, and one was a boy of about seventeen who wanted to see whether anything came up nine hundred and four steps and had been disappointed in nine counties.

They dug for four hours and they said very little for most of it, and then the cloth came off, and the woman of about twenty-two said, “That is a piece of a person.”

“That is a piece of a person,” said Ning Yan.

“Is it?”

“It is a fired plate with a name and a date and three words on it, and the name is not my brother’s, and I have not got a way of telling anybody whose it is, and it has been in the ground here since before I was born and I do not think anybody was ever told about it either.”

The man of about thirty said, “You knew about this.”

“I knew there was a hole in this yard when I was fourteen years old, and I had had a plate in my hands in it for a week and I had put it back in that hole with my own hands and covered it over,” said Ning Yan. “And I had not thought about the hole again for about eighteen years, and finding it again this afternoon is worth more than the plate is.”

And they carried on, and the woman of about twenty-two did not ask her anything else about it, and Ning Yan noticed that and did not know what to do with it, because she had a rule about saying true things out loud standing in front of people and had been telling these four things about a hole for three days.

## What It Was Like

And then she looked at it for a long time, because that was all that was left to do.

It was warm. It had been under two feet of ordinary ground for about fifteen years and it was warm, and it was warm in the way a forearm is warm, and it gave about the eighth of an inch under her thumb when she pressed and it came back up when she let go.

Skin-like. That was the word she had used in the kiln at fourteen and she had been right then and she was right now, and there was not one person in the world she had ever been able to tell that to, and there was not one person in nine counties who would have understood the shape of what she was holding if she had told them.

She turned it over. The brushing on it was still clear. The three words of the disposition were still there in a small even hand and she did not read them out loud, and the reason she did not was not a rule, it was that not one person in that yard had asked her to, and the day had been long enough.

And that was what the layer ever let out of a sealed place, once, in nine hundred years: a piece of a person that was warm and that gave under your thumb.

She had been carrying a book of eight leaves against her fourth rib for nineteen years with twenty-two names on one of them and two leaves still blank, and there is not one thing in the world that let a place out except this, and the place is a hole in the ground at the back of a ruin with a chimney broken at half its height.

## What She Said At The Gate

She said the figures out loud at about the seventh hour, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the gate of the yard, to about four people who were going to walk her back.

“Four thousand nine hundred and eighty days since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four. That is thirteen years and three hundred days and I am thirty-two.

“Four thousand seven hundred and seventy-seven days since the thirtieth of a month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four.

“Four thousand eight hundred and fifty-six days out of a room. Five thousand six hundred and thirty-nine days in that room before it. Five thousand six hundred and forty-six days with this arm open at the elbow.”

Then she said the last of it, standing, in the ordinary voice, because it was true and because she was holding a warm thing in her hand for the first time in nineteen years and had to put something out loud with her mouth while her hands were full.

“There is not one sealed place left in nine counties,” she said. “Not one, and there was not one this morning that was not somewhere under a person’s feet, and not one person in nine counties knows where they were, because the only list of them was in a building in a mountain and that building is four days west of here and I have not been in it since I was nineteen years old and I am not going back.

“That is not a thing I am going to fix. I am saying it out loud standing up, in the ordinary voice, in front of four people, because that is the rule and it is the only rule I have left.”

She put the plate in the front of her coat, against her chest and not against her skin, and picked up the lamp in her left hand, and walked out of the gate of the Broken Kiln at about the eighth hour with about four people behind her who were talking about nothing in particular, and the ground under her boots was ordinary ground and it did not hold anything.
