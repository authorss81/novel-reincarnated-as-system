# Not The Mercy

The road was filling again by the sixth hour on the thirtieth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and four, because it was the last day of a month and the last day of a month had been a market day on that road for as long as anybody in that flat could remember.

People were coming down off the hills with nothing to sell and putting their backs to a wall to wait for the morning, the way you wait when you have nowhere to be and a market to walk into.

Ning Yan sat on the wall at the gap with the lamp beside her and did the thing she had come out here to do, which was to take every one of the five meracies in turn and put it on the ground beside her and look at it.

## The Five

She did it the way she does everything, which is from the top, down, each part against another part.

No unchecked ascension. She had held nine for nineteen years and could have gone past it and had not, and that was a mercy, and she had always been able to say why.

No harvested souls. She could not say where that one came from and had not asked in nineteen years, and it was a mercy, and it was the first word of it.

No immortal war over the inside of a small place. A mercy. The whole of a county of people who had never heard of the immortals and had got old and died at a rate that was on the inside of ordinary.

A layer that keeps. A mercy, and the only one of the five she had ever been able to see the size of, because she had been inside one for two years and had read one every morning for nineteen.

And the last one, which was about her and which she had spent nine years being unable to look at, which was that she would go up properly and be given a room of her own and be left alone in it.

## The Marking

She marked each one as she put it down, and the marks were the marks she had been putting on things since she was ten years old.

The first three she marked without stopping.

The fourth took her about a count of two hundred, because a keeping that keeps is a keeping of what, and she had read the incoming end of one every morning since she was seven and had never once seen it hold a door shut or a stair barred or a road lifted.

And the fifth she could not mark at all, and she left it off, and she left it off on purpose.

## The Fifth

She got up off the wall and walked twenty paces back down the road and stood in the middle of a market that had not started yet and tried to say the fifth one out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice.

She got about six words of it out.

The words were about a room of her own and about being left in it and about never being opened again by anything that used her. They came out of her in the ordinary voice at a market road on the last day of a month, and they were so good, and they were the only one of the five she had ever actually wanted, and she had wanted it since she was seven years old and had wanted it every single day since.

She stopped after six words and stood in the road.

That was a mercy. She said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, to an empty road, because the rule does not have an exception for a reason. That was a mercy and it was the best thing anybody had ever offered her and she had spent nine years not being able to put it down.

And she was going to say a sentence that took the ceiling off the thing that had offered it.

## The One That Did Not Move

Then she got to the part she had actually come out here for, and it came out of her standing, in the ordinary voice, on the last day of a month, with about thirty people on a road behind her and a market coming.

“The walls are made of announcements.”

She said it and then she waited, because she wanted to hear whether she had said it correctly, and there was no one on that road who was going to help her check.

“Every bound place in the world is held at nine because something has said out loud, at the moment it happened, that it happened, and the saying is what does the holding. Not the stone. The saying. Nine hundred years and every wall in it is a sentence that has been kept.”

## The Reversal

She sat down on the wall because her legs had gone, and she went on out loud, which she does.

“So the sentence I have to say takes the thing that holds the walls. And everything on that wall in front of me that I have just called a mercy is a mercy because the holding is there.”

That was the whole of it and it took her about a count of forty and it had been true for about nine hundred years and she had never once had to look at it.

She put both hands flat on the stone of the gap.

“It is not the mercy I am refusing. I have never in nineteen years refused anything that was good. That is the whole of what I have got and it is the only thing about me I have ever been able to say out loud in front of somebody. And the sentence takes the walls, and the mercy is whatever is left of the walls afterwards, if anything is, and that is what is coming tomorrow.”

## Where She Was Going To Be Bad At It

Guo Sheng had come up the road at about the seventh hour and had been standing about ten paces off for some time, working the queue out of habit in a place where there was no queue, and when she stopped he came the rest of the way.

“You are going to take something off people,” he said, “who have not been told it was on the table.”

“Yes.”

He stood there and worked at that for a while.

“Then say that first.”

“No.”

“You cannot say a sentence that takes a thing down in front of four hundred people who never knew there was a thing to take down.”

“No,” Ning Yan said. “I cannot. And I have not started.”

He turned the yoke over in his hands for a while.

“You have been in a room eight hundred and thirty-two days,” he said, “and you have not asked one of them one single question about what they want done to them.”

“That is not true.”

“Ask them one.”

He said it the way he said everything, without any weight on it at all, and then he went down the road to help a carter get a wheel off a rut, and left her standing at a gap in a wall with the whole of what she had to do in front of her.

She had told four hundred people the price was on the record and she had told them she did not know what it cost her. She had not once told any of them what the record was for.

## What She Was Bad At

She was angry with herself for about a count of a hundred on the wall after he had gone, and it was a clean anger and she had not had one on that road for some days.

Nineteen years of refusing things. Two of them. Both of them good, both of them right, both of them a thing she had done to herself with her eyes open.

And this was going to be the third, and it was going to be the first one where the good and the thing she was refusing had come apart, and she was not going to get to be right about it, because being right about it was the whole of what she had ever had.

## Tomorrow

She stayed on the wall until the light had gone, because she wanted to be standing there when the day turned, on the last day of a month, with about thirty people sleeping against a wall a hundred yards off who had come to a market that had not started yet.

A bundle comes up nine hundred and four steps on the first of a month. It has done it every thirty days for as long as that keeping has existed and it will do it at the fifth hour tomorrow.

She had never once known how many firsts of a month she had left.

She said that out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, to the wall, because it was the third true thing she had said that day and she wanted all three of them in the keeping before the morning.

Then she lay down on the wet stones with the lamp beside her and did not sleep, because she had decided that if she was going to be awake on the morning a bundle came up, she was going to be awake on it.
