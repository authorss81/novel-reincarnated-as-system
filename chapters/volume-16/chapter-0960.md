# Four Hundred

Her name was He Yu and she had been standing at the far end of that road for four days because she had a farm, a daughter-in-law, and a house she could not leave without two days’ work being left undone in it, and she had decided twice to go home and finish it and had not gone.

She came forward when Ning Yan came down the road towards her, and she said the first thing.

“What do I have to bring.”

“Nothing.”

“I have a bundle.”

“Leave it there.”

“All right,” He Yu said, and she did not put it down.

## What She Was Told First

She stood in the rain at the edge of a road and said it standing, in the ordinary voice, with her hands, where anybody could see them, empty.

“The price of it is on the record. I do not know what it costs me. No name taken into the keeping is sent anywhere. Nothing is done to you. I am the only one who binds you.

“That is what it is and that is all of it, and I have said it before you have answered, standing, in the ordinary voice, before it is a line anywhere, and you can say no and I will not ask you again.”

He Yu looked at her for a long time.

“You said that to about three hundred people.”

“Yes.”

“And they said yes.”

“Three hundred and ninety-nine of them have said yes.”

He Yu did the arithmetic her way, which is to say slowly and without moving her mouth, and got to the same figure, and Ning Yan watched her do it and felt something go cold all the way down through her, because that was the one thing nobody else on that road had ever done.

“You have not got one of them written down,” He Yu said.

“No.”

“You have got the number.”

“I have got the number, and about fifty names, and I could put a face to about thirty of them this morning.”

## The Question She Asked Back

“Then what happens when one of them dies.”

“The keeping keeps what they did and not what it cost. I have not got anything else to tell you about that.”

“So there will be four hundred people in a record and one of you at the bottom of it.”

“Yes.”

She said that the same way she had said everything else, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the rain, and she watched a woman of about fifty take it in.

Nine hundred years of it, and not one person who went through it has ever been found, and there is not a column and not a place and not a name.

She did not say that to He Yu. She had said it once in nineteen years and she was not going to say it on a road to somebody who was standing there deciding.

He Yu said, “Then my name goes in and not just the number.”

“No,” Ning Yan said. “Your name goes in first.”

## Hers To Say

That was the whole of what was left and it took her about a count of forty to work out why she had said it.

A thing is said in the voice of the person it is about, standing, before it is a line anywhere. She had bound a boy at a table with one clause and then a woman at a stall and three hundred and ninety-seven more out of her own mouth in the ordinary voice, and not one name of them had ever gone into a record in nine hundred years because not one of them had asked to be in a record.

He Yu had asked. She was the person it was about. It was hers to say.

So she said it.

“He Yu. I am saying your name out loud first, standing, in the ordinary voice, before it is a line anywhere. He Yu, and you are the four hundredth, and the price of it is on the record, and no one sent you, and nothing has been done to you, and I am the only one who binds you.”

He Yu’s mouth went. She put her hand up over it and got it under control in about a count of twenty.

“My mother,” she said. “Say my mother.”

“No.”

“She has been dead nine years and there is not one line anywhere about her and I have been thinking about that on that road for two days.”

Ning Yan stood still.

Then she said, “No. I am not going to say a person who has not asked to be said. That is the whole of the rule and you are the one who taught me the shape of it on this road, because you said you would not do a thing on the edge of a road on your own. It does not run backwards. It is not mine to open.”

He Yu stood in the rain for about a count of a hundred.

“All right,” she said. “He Yu. Say it.”

“He Yu.”

“I am saying yes.”

## Where There Was No Light

Ning Yan said it standing, in the ordinary voice, at the end of a road, in front of Guo Sheng and about nine other people, and there was no light on it and nothing came back and nothing ever does.

“He Yu, I bind you into the Sisterhood. You are the four hundredth. The price of it is on the record. Nothing is done to you. I am the only one who binds you. I told you what was on the record before you answered, and you answered, and I said your name out loud first and you said it back.”

Four hundred.

## The Figure She Does Not Have

And then, because there was nowhere else to put it and she had been carrying it for a day on a road in the rain, she said the other thing, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the plainest words she had.

“I do not know what it is costing me.”

He Yu looked at her.

“I have said that on this road to every single one of you and not one of you has ever once been told what it actually is.

“I have carried a figure out of my own life since I was ten years old and it is the only figure I have ever had, and it is out of me and not of this.

“I have an arm that will not close and a hand with three fingers and a thumb on it and seven holds out of eleven and a place behind my fourth rib the size of my thumbnail, and one thousand five hundred and sixty-three days gone out of me, and that is what it cost before I ever said a word to any of you.

“I do not know what four hundred of them has cost. I have not found it since the seventh and I have been looking every morning at the ninth hour. I have not got a figure and I am not going to make one up for you at the end of a road.”

## The Part She Was Going To Say Anyway

She said it out loud, in the ordinary voice, and she put her hand flat on the stone of the gap in the wall while she said it.

“Eleven women went through that machine before me.”

She had said that once in nineteen years and she had said it standing in a room eight flights below a table with no wear in it, and this time there were about ten people standing on a road in the rain, and she said it to them anyway, because she had come down here to say sentences out loud and there was no reason the hardest one should stay in a building.

“Eleven women went through it, one at a time, and not one of them is written down anywhere in nine hundred years of the keeping. There is not a column. There is not a place. There is not a name, and there has not been one name put in a record in this world for the whole nine hundred years that says what that machine does to a person and what it costs them.

“And there are four hundred of them now, and every single one of them is a whole person who said yes out loud while they were standing up, and I have said the number in my own mouth before it went anywhere, and there is a figure in a keeping on the other side of it now that was not there this morning.

“That is the answer to the only question I have got in the world and I did not go looking for it. I did not go down a mountain to find it. It came out of the side of a queue.”

## What It Cannot Do

Nobody unbound a person out of this by wanting them less.

She said that out loud too, standing, in the ordinary voice, on the nineteenth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and four, and she said it as a finding and not as a warning, because she was not warning anybody of anything. She was putting the last of it in.

“This is on the record now and it is the four hundredth line of it and there is no sentence in the world that takes one of these off a person who said yes. Not in a year. Not when I am gone. Not by me and not by anybody.

“I said that standing, in the ordinary voice, before it was a line anywhere, and I said it because there was a woman at a stall at the bottom of a pass who told me on the twentieth of the month that she was not going to know what she had agreed to for about a week, and I wanted it in from me and not from a keeping.”

## The End Of It

She put two fingers under her own ribs on the left side, high, behind the fourth one, and pressed, and there was nothing at all under her fingers.

It was the size of her thumbnail and it had been the size of her thumbnail since she was a child, and it had not been any different on any of the days she had put her hand on it since she came down out of that mountain.

She stood there in the rain at a gap in a low wall with a lamp in her left hand that had not carried a flame since the fourteenth of the month beginning three thousand and seventy-four, and four hundred people in an arithmetic she had carried off a mountain on her own, and there was no report, and there was no number, and the place behind her fourth rib was the size of her thumbnail and had been since she was a child.

Guo Sheng said, from about twenty paces off, “What do you do now.”

She looked at the road. It ran on past the gap, down across the flat and away into the next valley, and it had gone out of that road four days ago, and there was no queue on it anywhere and no office and nobody sent.

She had nine hundred years of a ceiling and a keeping behind her and four hundred people in front of her and a sentence she had not got.

She said that out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the rain, on the nineteenth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and four, and about nine people heard it.

“I have got four hundred people and I have not got one sentence, and the sentence has to be said by me, standing, in the ordinary voice, before it is a line anywhere, and I do not have it yet.”

Then she picked up the lamp and went down the road to find out how far the queue had got, because four hundred people could not carry a sentence she had not found, and she had not started looking for it.
