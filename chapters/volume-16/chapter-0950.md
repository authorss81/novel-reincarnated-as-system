# The Plainest Words She Had

She tried it twice more on the twenty-second and it got worse both times, and the second time was the worst of anything she had done in nineteen years, and she did it standing in the open in front of two men who had come in out of the rain to look at her.

They were not angry. That was the part she had not expected. One of them said he did not understand a word of it and would not mind if she asked again another day, and the other one said no, and said that he was glad she had come and asked him at all, which she had not been ready for.

She went and sat under the eaves at the end of the row with her back against a cold wall and worked out what had gone wrong, and it took her about a count of three hundred.

On the twentieth she had had no sentence and had made one.

Now she had one. And having one was worse, because she had it in the shape it had come out of her mouth the first time, and it was not a good shape, and every time she went back into it she went into the version and not the sentence.

## Four Blank Leaves

She took the book out from against her fourth rib where it had been since she was ten years old.

Eight leaves. Four of them written on, and one of those had gone down nine hundred and four steps in a cloth and had never been opened by anybody. Four of them blank, and blank since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four.

She had said out loud, more than once, standing, in the ordinary voice, that she was not going to write on them.

She sat under a wet eaves with the book open on her knees and understood that she had said that about a room, on a mountain, in the ninth year, and she was not in the room.

She had decided it in a place. She had carried the decision out of the place with her, the way you carry a plate out of a house, and it had not survived the door.

## What She Wrote

She found a flat board off a wall and a stub of charcoal out of the bottom of a basket and she wrote on the flat of it with her left hand, because her left hand had three fingers and a thumb and her right hand could not do it.

She wrote for about a count of six hundred.

She wrote what she had said to Kan Shou, and then she cut it down, and then she cut it down again, and then she cut out the part about the ninth stage and the arm and the days, because a person standing in front of a stranger does not need a stranger to know what a stranger has been through.

What was left was short enough to say without thinking.

She read it twice on the flat of the board.

Then she went through it standing up, out loud, in the ordinary voice, at the end of the row in the rain, to the stones, the way she had said six figures at the ninth hour for nineteen years.

It did not work.

## Why It Did Not Work

She stood in the rain and got to the bottom of it in about a count of two hundred, and what she got to was this.

A thing is said in the voice of the person it is about, standing, before it is a line anywhere.

She had known that for two years and she had used it twice, both of them on herself, and both times it had held because she had said it out loud with her own mouth on a day she had chosen.

A board could not do that. A board could not stand. A board could not say a word out loud in a particular voice on a particular day to a particular person, and a person reading words off a board three days after she wrote them was not a person saying a thing in their own voice, and the keeping was not interested in a person reading.

So there was no way to save it.

She had one instrument and it was her own mouth and it only worked while it was moving, and it was going to have to do a great many more of them in a county where she knew not one person, standing up, in the open, out loud, and every single one of them was going to be a different day for her and she was not going to be able to rehearse.

That was what she had come down here to find out and she had found it in the rain outside a row of roofs in about a count of three hundred.

## What She Kept

She cut it down once more.

She got it to about what you would say to somebody standing in front of you about a thing that had just happened, and it kept three parts and lost the rest.

The price of it is on the record.

I do not know what it costs me.

No one has sent you, and nothing is done to you, and I am the one who binds you.

That was all of it and she could say it standing in the rain to a stranger without stopping to think about any part of it, and she said it four times to the stones to be sure, and it came out of her mouth the same way four times.

Then she took the charcoal and put the whole of it on the blank leaf, and she put the leaf back against her fourth rib with the other seven, and she did not take it out again.

She was not going to read it. She knew that standing at the end of the row with the rain going off the eaves, and she knew it in the ordinary way a person knows what they are not going to do, and she carried it anyway because she had carried four blank leaves against her own ribs since she was a child and she was not going to start throwing things away.

## What She Could Not Learn

She tried to learn it on the twenty-third and she could not.

She got a man on the road at about the sixth hour, a man of about fifty going somewhere with a bundle on his back, and she stood in front of him and opened her mouth to say the three sentences and the three sentences were not there.

She had said them four times to stones the day before. She had written them on a flat board in charcoal and had cut them down twice and had got them to three lines. She could have said them backwards.

She stood in the road with her mouth open and nothing coming out of it for about a count of thirty, and the man said, “Was you going to say something to me.”

“Yes.”

“Then say it.”

“I have got it,” she said. “I have got it exactly and it is not coming out.”

He looked at her, and then he said, “All right,” and shifted his bundle, and waited.

And then she said it. All three sentences, standing, in the ordinary voice, to a man in a road, and it took her about a count of two to get going and then it went out the way it had gone out four times to stones.

The whole of what had gone wrong was the count of thirty. She could hold three sentences in her head for four repetitions to a rock and she could not hold them for a stranger who was standing there waiting for them.

That was the thing about the instrument. It did not take a rehearsal and it did not take a road and it did not take a face. It took a mouth that was moving, and a mouth that is moving is the only kind there is, and she would have to have three hundred of them in three hundred places.

## The Two That Had Said No

At about the seventh hour on the twenty-third she went back to the two men who had said no and asked one of them why.

He was sitting on an upturned crate with his boots off and he looked at her for a while before he answered.

“You told me you did not know what it would cost you,” he said. “You told me that plain, in front of me, and you did not dress it up, and that is the only reason I believed anything you said at all. Everybody who has ever wanted something off me has told me what it would cost me.”

“And the other man.”

“The other man said no because he has four of his own and he was not going to put a fifth one under a thing he could not see,” the man said. “That is not him being a coward. That is him having four.”

She stood in the wet and listened to a man give her a reason, and it was the first reason anybody had given her for anything since she was ten years old and had not run.

## What She Went Looking For

She went out at about the eighth hour, before the light had gone altogether, and walked down the road until there was a person on it.

The first person she found was a woman carrying a pole over one shoulder who had come off the same pass she had come off and was going the other way, and who was about her own age and had stopped to put her load down and drink out of a bamboo tube.

She listened to the whole of the short version standing in the road with the pole on the ground at her feet.

And then she asked a question about it.

She had the short version ready. She had said it four times to stones the day before. She said it to him, and it went out of her mouth standing and in the ordinary voice the way it had gone out four times the day before.

And then the woman asked her what the price of it was going to be, and she told her the truth, which was that she did not know.

And the woman did not shout at her, and the woman did not go away.

She said, “Then ask me again where there are other people. I will not do a thing like that on the edge of a road on my own.”
