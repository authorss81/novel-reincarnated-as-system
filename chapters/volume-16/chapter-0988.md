# Nine Counties On Foot

There is rubble where the Nine Steps Terrace was, and she went and stood on it on the twenty-third day because there was nothing else in four counties she was able to think of going and look at.

Nine hundred years, gone, in nine days.

The lowest course is still dressed stone and the six above it are not there at all, and the whole of the middle of the terrace has come down into the cellars, and about four hundred people took everything they could carry out of it in a day and a half and left the rest. There is a marker in the middle of it, broken in three, and the pieces are about four feet apart from each other.

Lowwater is underneath it and Lowwater is still there.

## Saying It On The Rubble

She stood in it at about the ninth hour and said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of about forty people who had come down off the stone to look at it, because it is the only way she has ever been able to say a true thing and she had not found another in twenty-three days.

She said it short, because she had said it long on the ninth day and there was not one word of it she had any better now.

“Nine days. Ten places in four counties where that stone stood, and eight of them came apart in order and I watched every one of them. Two of them were not there when I went back to look on the ninth day. This is the tenth.

“Nine hundred years of a ceiling at nine in every realm there was, and there was not one mark on any of it anywhere that said where it was kept.

“It came off in nine days because of nine words I said at a gap in a wall on the thirtieth of a month, standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of eight people. I was the only person alive who knew that and I had said it out loud, standing up, before anything else.”

And then she said the other half of it, which she had thought about for six days and had not managed to make smaller.

“I had not got one word for what happened now and there was not one person in four counties who had asked me for any of it back. I was not going to tell them that it was the right thing to do. I had not found out.

“I had told four hundred people that I would not know what it cost and I did not know, and it had not killed anybody. That was the whole of what I had got.

“It was not a victory and it was not a reward and there was not one thing on this earth that had been given to me for it.”

## What She Was Going To Do

She had stopped looking on the seventeenth day and she had spent the six days since doing the only thing there was, which was being reachable, and on the twenty-first day the thing she had been doing stopped being enough.

Not because it ran out. About forty people a day came to the weir at about the ninth hour and she put the same true things out loud in front of them, and on the twenty-second day a man of about thirty with a yoke came up to her and asked her whether she was going to be at the weir next month as well, and she had to think about it for about a count of forty before she answered, and that was the whole of the change.

So she did it on the twenty-third day, in the rubble, standing up, in the ordinary voice, in front of about forty people, and she said it before anybody had asked her for it.

“I was going to go and find them.”

## The Figure That Would Not Close

Three hundred and ninety-nine people in nine counties.

She put it down standing, in the ordinary voice, out loud, in order, in the rubble, the way she has put down every figure she has ever said in nineteen years.

Four hundred said yes on roads. One is standing here. Three hundred and ninety-nine are not.

About fifty of them she can put a face to. About three hundred and forty-nine she cannot, and there is no way of finding them except by standing in front of them.

She cannot be given anything. She cannot be paid. She cannot follow a name she is given. She cannot be met where she is expected and she cannot be sent, and there is no office and no list and no message.

So she has to walk into nine counties she has been in for eight days and find about three hundred and forty-nine people one at a time.

And she got that far standing in the rubble and then she stopped, because the figure would not close.

There is no column for that. She had about a third of one county in eight days and she had been walking in a straight line for none of it, and there was not one rate in her arithmetic that would tell her how long it takes one woman with one working arm to find three hundred and forty-nine people who do not know she is coming.

So she said that out loud as well, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the rubble, to about forty people.

“The figure did not come out. I had got a number at both ends and there was nothing in the middle that would do it, and I said that standing up before I did anything else, because I was not going to stand in a pile of stone and put a number on it that I could not get at twice.”

## What She Said About The Work

A woman of about forty who had come down off the stone with a barrow said, “Then you have not got a plan.”

“No.”

“You have got a thing you are going to do.”

“Yes.”

“Then that was what it was,” the woman said, “and you had been at this long enough to know that the two were not different.”

Ning Yan stood in the rubble of a terrace and let that go past her, because it was true and because she had no answer to it and because she had said out loud four days ago that she was not going to invent one.

Then she said the last of it, standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of about forty people, and she put the arithmetic first and the arithmetic was short.

“Three hundred and forty-nine people. Nine counties. On foot. There was not one person in nine counties who could go and do it for me and I was not going to go and ask for one, because I had said out loud for a month that I could not be given anything, and I was not going to start asking for a favour on the last morning of what I had been doing.

“I was going to walk into a county and stand in it and say the three sentences out loud, in the ordinary voice, to whoever was standing there. And then I was going to do it again the next day in the next place.

“I was not going to have a figure for how long that was, because I could not get one, and I had said that out loud here in front of forty people before I started, so that not one person in nine counties could stand in a road in a year and say they were never told.”

She put the lamp down on the stone, because she was not going to be holding anything.

## Nine Counties

She went west out of Lowwater at about the eleventh hour of the twenty-third day of a month, on foot, with a book of eight leaves against her fourth rib and a lamp she could not refill in her left hand, because there was no longer any reason in the world to set a lamp down.

A hundred and two days out of a room. Eight hundred and eighty-five days in that room before it.

Twenty-three days since the thirtieth of a month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four.

That was the whole of what she could derive, and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, on the road west out of Lowwater at the eleventh hour, to about nine people who had walked with her as far as the first ford.

## The First One On The Road

She had been walking about half a day when a boy of about thirteen came up the road from the south and stopped about four paces off her and asked her what she was doing.

That was the whole of it. He had a bundle on his back and a bootlace in his hand and he was about to ask somebody else.

She stood in the road and put out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, the three sentences, in the order she had been giving them in since the twentieth of a month beginning three thousand and one hundred and four.

That the price of it was on the record, and that she did not know what it cost her and did not know what it cost anybody else, and that not one person had sent him anywhere and that nothing was done to him and that she was the only one who bound anybody.

That a sentence had been said on the thirtieth of a month at a gap in a wall and that it had taken nine days and that she did not know what it was for or whether it was right.

And then the last part, which is the one she always said last and which she had never once found a way to soften.

That he could say no and it would stay on the record, and that she had to say that out loud before he answered anything, because it was the rule, and the rule was the whole of what she had ever offered anybody.

It took her about sixteen minutes.

The boy of about thirteen listened to the whole of it standing in a road with his bootlace in his hand and then said, “That was about four sentences.”

“It was three and a half.”

“You counted them.”

“I count all of them,” Ning Yan said. “There is not one other thing in the world I know how to do and you have just had all of it.”

He thought about it and then he said, “I am going to have to go to my uncle’s,” and went off down the road, and did not say no, and did not say yes, and she wrote nothing down because she had no way of knowing which of the two he had done in his head on the way.

That was the first of the three hundred and forty-nine, and it took her sixteen minutes, and she derived a figure out of it standing in a road in the last of the light.

Three hundred and forty-nine, at about sixteen minutes each, came to just under four days of standing still in one place and talking to one person at a time.

Just under four days of talking. And nine counties of walking to get to them, and not one figure anywhere in the world for that.

Then she stopped saying them, because the run of them had ended on the fifth day of a month on a rim with a girl of about nine and a piece of chalk, and she was not going to start it up again on a road because it would have been a comfort and she was not going to have one.

And she went on walking, and the ceiling stayed off, and nothing came back, and there was not one person in the world who had come to ask her for it, and she was nineteen years old and she was going to spend the rest of her life walking into counties that did not know yet, with a book against her fourth rib with two blank leaves left in it and a lamp that would never be lit again, and nothing else on her at all.

That is not a victory and it is not a reward and not one person on that road put a hand on her shoulder.

It is what there was.
