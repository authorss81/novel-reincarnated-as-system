# Held Harder Than The Record

Bao Ren had been at the back of the queue for two days with a broken cart shaft and a tarred bed on his own cart, and he came to her on the thirteenth of the month and he came to her on purpose.

He was about forty and he had hands like a man who had been getting up before the light for thirty years and he had listened to her say the three sentences twice before he came forward, which she knew because she had said them on the tenth to somebody else in front of him.

She said them to him standing, in the ordinary voice, at the edge of the road with the rain going past them both.

He said yes.

She waited, because she had been waiting since the first of the month, because a man had once said yes and then asked about his brother, and she was not going to be wrong twice.

He did not add anything.

She said the line.

“Bao Ren. I bind you into the Sisterhood, and the price of it is on the record. Nobody sent you. Nothing has been done to you. Nobody binds you but me, and I told you what is on the record before you answered and you have answered.”

He said, “Now say the other thing.”

## The Thing She Had Not Said

“There is no other thing.”

“There is,” he said. “You said a yes to me about three sentences ago and you have not asked me a single question since you said them. Every one that came to me since the twentieth asked me about the price. What I have got out of it. What happens at the end. What it does to you.”

He put his hand on the flat side of his cart.

“I am going to go home tonight and I am going to lie in the dark and I am going to be afraid, because that is what I do, and I want to know one thing and I want it in the ordinary voice where other people are standing and can hear it.

“When I am afraid, does it hold.”

She said, “Yes.”

## What She Said And What It Was

She said it standing, in the ordinary voice, at the edge of a road, in the rain, with about twenty people within hearing, and it went out of her mouth the way everything she said went out of her mouth, and there was no pulling it back afterwards and there was no way of putting it on a different side.

Then she stood there and listened to what she had just done.

That was the shape of it. Not a sentence about a person. A promise, about a person, to a person, made out loud, in front of witnesses, that she had no instrument for at all.

She could derive every number she owned. She had one number that came out of her own life and she had derived it since she was a child and it was out of her to this morning. She did not have one thing in her that knew what a promise about the holding of a binding costs, because it had never been asked for before and because she had never in nineteen years had to keep a promise to a person rather than a figure.

She had spent nine years saying figures out loud into a wall. She had kept every one of them for six thousand mornings and had not been able to break a single one, and she had thought until the eleventh of a month that was the same as being trustworthy.

It was not the same. Figures hold because she holds them.

## What He Wanted It For

Bao Ren waited, and then he told her, because she could see he had been carrying it since the ninth.

“I have had a good life,” he said. “I want that on the record because nobody is going to be surprised by it. Forty-one years, one cart, a bed, a woman in the second county and a boy in this one who does not speak to me. Nothing in it has ever gone wrong the way you would expect.

“I have never once in forty-one years not been able to get out of a thing.”

He looked at the road.

“Every trade I have been in, every debt, every bad season, every man who has owed me and not paid, I have got out of all of it by wanting out of it hard enough. That is the whole of what I am. I am a man who gets out.

“And you are telling me, out loud, in front of this road, that when I want out of this one, it will not let me. I want that. I want a thing that does not let me.”

She said yes again before she had worked out whether she was going to.

She said it standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of twenty people, and she said it a second time on purpose, slower, because a thing said out loud can be made plainer by being said again and she had nine years of a rule that said the first time is the one that counts and she was not going to spend the second one being clever.

## What She Bound

Then she bound him, and she found that she could do the whole of it without taking a breath, which was not a thing she was proud of and did not have time to look at.

“Bao Ren. I bind you into the Sisterhood. No one has sent you. Nothing has been done to you. I am the only one who binds you. And I have said to you in the ordinary voice where this road could hear it, that it holds, and I have said it twice.”

Bao Ren took his hand off the cart.

“Good,” he said. “Then I have got one.”

He went and picked up the spare shaft and went to work on the wheel with his back to her, and she watched him for about a count of two hundred, and she thought, standing in the rain, that he was the only person in thirteen days who had not asked her a single question about what it cost.

He had asked her one question, about himself, and he had wanted the answer to be no.

## Taking It Back

She tried to take it back at about the seventh hour and it was the first time in nineteen years she had tried.

She stood at the gap in the low wall with the count in her head and the rain still going and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of a queue of about forty people, in the plainest words she could find.

“I said to Bao Ren that it holds when he is afraid, and I am withdrawing that. It is withdrawn, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and it was said first, and it is not on the record, and I am not bound by it and neither is he.”

Nothing moved.

That was the whole of what happened, and she had known it was going to be the whole of what happened before she opened her mouth, and she had said it anyway, standing, in the rain, in front of forty people, because a rule that only runs one way is not a rule anybody would keep for nineteen years unless they had kept it anyway.

It runs one way.

A thing is said in the voice of the person it is about, standing, before it is a line anywhere, and it goes in, and there is no sentence in the world that undoes one of those out of a keeping. She had known that for two years, and in two years she had never once put her own mouth to it, and then a carter with a broken shaft asked her for one thing.

Forty people watched her try. That was the price of finding out. She had made a promise out loud in front of twenty of them and withdrawn it out loud in front of forty, and the only thing that had been gained by the second was that everybody now knew she had tried.

## The Fourth Of The Day

She asked eleven more people after him.

That was not in her plan and she did not do it because of the plan. She did it because she was standing at the end of a queue with a thing stuck in her chest like a swallowed stone and the only way she had ever known to get a thing out of her was to put it into the keeping in her own mouth, and there was one instrument and it was her own mouth and it moved.

She asked a woman with a hoe. She asked a boy who was about twelve and whose mother stood behind him saying nothing at all. She asked a man of about sixty who listened and then asked whether his wife could be told about it afterwards, and she said yes, and she meant it, and she had no idea at all whether it would still be true in a month.

She got it out into the keeping once for every one of them, and it was no lighter at the end of the day than it had been at the start, and it was the first time in nineteen years that putting a thing in had not made it smaller.

Then, at about the eighth hour, she found that she was saying the three sentences to a woman who was not coming forward, who was about forty paces off, and who had never stopped.

She shut her mouth on it about two thirds of the way through the second sentence and stood there with the rest of it in her face.

The woman looked over. Ning Yan put her hand up flat, palm out, the way you stop a cart, and the woman stopped, and turned, and went on up the road.

“I am sorry,” Ning Yan said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, to somebody who was a long way off by then and could not have heard a word of it.

## The Count

Three hundred and forty-four by the ninth hour on the thirteenth.

She said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and she derived it, because she had worked out on the tenth that she was going to have to do it every single night whether she wanted to or not, and it was the one figure in her life that had ever got away from her.

Three hundred and forty-four. One of them was a boy of sixteen in a mountain with oil he made and could not buy. One of them was a man of forty-one in the second county who had spent four days getting a thing that could not be got out of.

There was no place in nine hundred years of a keeping where a promise like that was written down except the record of the binding, and the record of the binding said nothing about promises, and she had made it out loud, in front of twenty people, on a road, in the rain.

The only copy of it was in a carter’s head, forty counties away, by morning.
