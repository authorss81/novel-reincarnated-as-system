# Firsts

Three hundred and forty-two days since the ninth stage. One hundred and thirty-five since it was shut. Seven hundred and eighty days with this arm open at the elbow. Seven hundred and seventy-three days in this room. One thousand five hundred and fifteen days gone at this morning, taken out of her at a rate she derived out of her own life, not one of them coming back.

She said it standing at the near end of her own table at about the sixth hour on the first of the month beginning three thousand and seventy-four, and then she took the near board up off the face of the table and read it by and put it back face up a foot in from the near end, because it was the first of a month and that was what a first of a month was for.

She felt it come up.

It came up through the stone under her feet the way it had come up on the last first of a month, a bundle of boards corded twice going up nine hundred and four steps of a stair that did not stop at nine, and she stood at the near end of her own table with a board in her hand and let it come the whole way up.

She did not go to the foot of the stair.

## The One She Did Not Go Under

She stood where she was and listened to it go, and she worked out, standing there with the board in her hand, that this was the twenty-sixth first of a month she had been in this room for and that she had gone to the bottom of the stair and stood under twenty-five of them and had not gone under this one, and that she had not gone under it on purpose and that she had not decided to.

At about the fifth hour a bundle came up nine hundred and four steps to a room with no wear in it and a table with a register on it and an offer withdrawn on it, and a woman at the near end of a table eight flights and a stair below it heard it go and did not move.

Nothing came back.

That was the other half of it and she had not been expecting it. There was no line about a bundle and there was no line about anything else, and the keeping had given her nothing back at the ninth hour since the twenty-fifth and it gave her nothing at the fifth hour of a first of a month, and a thing that had said a sentence about a bundle to the woman in this room every first for four hundred years had said nothing at all on this one.

## What A First Of A Month Is

She put the board down face up and put her hands flat on the wood and did the arithmetic, because she was going to do it anyway and it was the only instrument she had.

A bundle of boards comes up nine hundred and four steps on the first of every month. It goes to a room with no wear in it. It is cut, and the boards go somewhere she could not name, and a piece of what I am went out of this building on the twenty-seventh of the month before last and did not come back.

She stood there with the whole of it in front of her and did not like the shape of it.

“A first of a month does not need anybody,” she said.

“No,” he said.

“It has not needed anybody for four hundred years.”

“No.”

“It came up nine hundred and four steps on the first and it is going to come up nine hundred and four steps on the next one, and on every one of them after that, and it is going to do that whether there is a woman at the near end of a table in the room below or not.”

“Yes,” he said. “That is what I have been trying to tell you since the twenty-seventh and you have not asked me once about it.”

She took her hand off the wood and put it back.

“Nineteen years,” she said. “Nineteen years I have marked every one of them. I have said the ninth hour of every first of a month in this room like it was a day that had something on it, and every day in between I have counted as the day after a first, and the whole of my arithmetic is built on a day that goes up a stair by itself.”

“It is not nothing,” he said. “It is the only day that tells you the keeping is still here. That is what a first is. Nobody has to be in the room for the keeping to be up.”

## The Whole Of A Day With Nothing On It

She did the day the way she had been doing days since the seventh of the month before last, which was badly and slowly and to the end.

She took the terms board up about the seventh hour and read it by and put it back a foot further along the table. She went up the room and stood four feet off the place at the far side and looked at it, because she had looked at it once on the twenty-second and she was not going to stop looking at it now, and there was still nothing worn in a place that had been kept four hundred years.

She put her hand flat on the bar against the left-hand wall and took none of her weight on it.

She sat down on the stone at the near end of the table with her back against the leg of it and put her hand up under her own ribs on the left side, high, behind the fourth one, and found the small open place that was there, the size of her thumbnail, and it did not feel like anything, and she left her hand there for about a count of two hundred.

And then she sat there and worked out what she had with her for the next thirty days.

A first of a month, and thirty days between this one and the next. One of them, on time, going up nine hundred and four steps whether she was standing under it or not. And then the ordinary days in between, thirty of them, of which there was not one that would come up a stair or go down one or turn anything over, and every one of which she would get to by standing at the near end of her own table and saying six figures out loud into a room.

She had thirty days of that in front of her, and she had never once in nineteen years had a day that was not the day after something or the day before something.

“All right,” she said, out loud, to her own room, in the ordinary voice. “Then that is thirty of them and they are all mine.”

## The Thirtieth And The First

“Say your figures at the ninth hour,” he said.

She put her hands flat on the wood.

“You said that yesterday at about the ninth hour and I did not say them.”

“I know you did not say them. You let the hour go past with me talking and you have not said one figure out loud in this room since and you have told me why.”

“I did not tell you why.”

“You told me the reason and the reason was that you were not going to be the thing that stopped me,” he said, “and you have not said one since, and I have been sitting in a wall for a whole day waiting to hear six numbers out of a woman.”

She stood at the near end of her own table and felt the ninth hour coming up on the room the way she had felt it every day of her life.

“No,” she said. “I am not going to say them on a first of a month.”

“All right.”

“I said no to you and I am not going to take it back because you waited a day for it. I let the hour go yesterday and I am not going to say them because it is the first of a month and a first of a month is a day that comes up nine hundred and four steps whether I am in the room or not, and I have decided this morning that I am not going to give it to it.”

“That is not what a first of a month is for,” he said.

“That is what it is for now.”

He said nothing about that for the rest of the day.

She stood at the near end of her own table at about the ninth hour of the first of the month beginning three thousand and seventy-four and did the whole of the day again, near board and terms board and the length of the table with her hand along the wood.

She did not say a figure. The keeping gave her no line. The bundle went up nine hundred and four steps to a room with no wear in it and came to the top and was cut and went somewhere, and nothing in the world was different by a single thing.

The next first of a month was thirty days off. She stood there at the ninth hour and worked that out and had nothing to do with it, because a day had used to come up a stair every thirty days and it had been on her calendar every morning of nineteen years, and it was going to keep coming up that stair whether she was standing under it or not, and there was not one figure in the world that put her at the bottom of it.
