# What The Keeping Wrote Down

She waited a fortnight for an answer and then it came on the fourth of the month at about the fifth hour, and it came out of the ground while she was standing in a queue.

That was how it had always come. In a building it had come out of the dark past the last landing, one piece at a time, plain, in the grammar the keeping used for everything it said. Out here it came up through the road under her feet, the way the going of a bundle came up under her on a first of a month, and it did not choose a quiet moment, and there were about twenty people within sight of her at the time.

## The Waiting

She had no instrument for it.

That was the thing she got to first, standing in the road with her left hand shaking, on the fourth of the month. She had put a fortnight of questions into the open air and she had had no way of asking whether any of it had landed anywhere, and she had known that on the twentieth and she had done it anyway, because the alternative was not doing it.

She had been checking the only way she knew how, which was to go on saying things and see whether the world started behaving differently. It had not. The market had gone on. A man had hit his thumb with a mallet about the second hour and said a word she was not going to repeat. Kan Shou had sold her jars and had not said one word about anything.

A fortnight of nothing.

And then, in the middle of a queue, at about the fifth hour, it came.

[One hundred and forty-nine persons stand in the arithmetic of the one this layer keeps. The price of each is on the record. It was not asked for them. The walls are kept.]

## What Was In It

She stood in the road and let it go past her the way she had let everything from the keeping go past her for nineteen years, which was all of it, without hurrying it, and it was plain, and it did not stop anywhere.

One hundred and forty-nine.

She had sixty-eight on the first of the month and she had been working two days since and she had taken the count at the ninth hour both nights and it was one hundred and forty-nine and she had not put that number into the keeping in her own mouth.

It had been written down after her.

That was the whole of what had happened on the road and she stood there and worked it out in pieces while a queue waited behind her.

The rule was hers and she had not broken it once in nineteen years, and what had come up out of the ground was not a judgement on anything she had done.

She had been saying it in the open air, on hillsides, in the rain, to people who had not agreed yet, and she had believed, somewhere underneath the believing, that it could not work out here. That the room was the instrument. That the mountain was in it.

It had worked out here.

And the keeping had written it down after she had said it, which meant there were two of every one of those one hundred and forty-nine, a line in her own voice and a line in the record, and hers was the one that was first, and it was the only thing she had that the keeping did not also have.

## What It Said About Her

It did not say one word about her.

That was not a mark of respect and it was not coldness and it was not mercy. It was a report. A bundle is not due and it goes up nine hundred and four steps and none of them has been late, and the Foundation account is open and unanswered and has not begun.

One hundred and forty-nine persons stand in the arithmetic of the one this layer keeps. The price of each is on the record. It was not asked for them. The walls are kept.

Four sentences, plain, and not one of them had her name in it, and not one of them had said anything about what it was doing.

She had been hearing the keeping since she was seven years old and she had never once heard it put a thing down that it had not been asked to put down.

## What She Did About Not Being Believed

The trouble with that was that she was the only person on that road who knew it had happened.

She worked it out at about the sixth hour while a man who wanted to know about his thumb waited in front of her.

She had been saying things out loud in the open air for about a fortnight, and the only evidence in the world that any of it had been taken was a line that not one other person had heard. She was not going to be able to walk into a market tomorrow and say so, because the second she said it out loud it would be a claim and not a fact, and there was no instrument on this road that weighed claims.

She tried three ways of getting it out into the open and all three of them were a lie.

She could not say a voice had spoken, because she had been hearing that keeping since she was seven years old and not one person in that flat could hear it, and if she said it out loud she would be telling forty strangers that she was the one the layer had put a voice in, which is true, and which would put her in a place they would not come to again.

She could not ask somebody else whether they had heard anything, because they had not, and because the asking would be a way of making a thing true that was not true.

And she could not put it in her own arithmetic, because her arithmetic was hers and it had never once held a thing she could not check, and this was the first thing in nineteen years that she could not check and could not get out of.

## What She Did With It

She said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the middle of the road, where twenty people could hear it or not.

“It has written down a hundred and forty-nine of them and it did not ask to. It has not said what they are for. It has not said what it costs. It has said the walls are kept.

“I said every one of those sentences out loud first. That is the rule and it has not been broken once in nineteen years and it is not being broken now.

“And it has gone on keeping the walls. That is the last sentence of the four and I am going to have to carry it for a long time.”

Nobody on that road asked her what the four sentences were.

## The Two Things In One Breath

She got through the rest of the day and the night after it and she did the whole of it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, to everybody who came.

Somebody asked her at about the eighth hour, a young man with a bad hand, whether she had just heard something.

“No,” she said. “There was nothing.”

There had been something. She was not going to say so to a stranger on a road and she was not going to pretend to a man that she had heard nothing, and she split the difference because that is what she had, and she went back to the end of the queue.

Here was the thing she had got out of the four sentences, and she said it out loud on the fifth of the month at the sixth hour, standing, on the edge of the flat with the light coming up, and she said it twice.

“The thing that took me out at a rate I derived out of my own life has been running for nine hundred years and it has not stopped and nothing I have said in the last fortnight has stopped it, and now there are a hundred and forty-nine people in the same record as it and the keeping wrote that down and went on keeping the walls.

“I said those two things in one breath because for the first time in nineteen years they were in the same record at the same time, and I could not see a way to say the first without the second standing next to it.”

It did not answer her.

It had never once answered her a question, in nineteen years, and she had known that, and she had said it out loud anyway because the rule does not have an exception for questions and she was not going to be the first one to make one.

So she had a hundred and forty-nine people in a record and not one line on that road that anybody else had seen, and she had to go on standing in front of people all day and being looked at, and the only thing she could put her hand on was the fact that she had said it first.

That was the rule and the rule had never once been for her comfort.

## The Queue On The Fifth

Guo Sheng found her at the edge of the flat at about the ninth hour on the fifth and said that there were more coming off the hills than there had been on the first, and that they were coming off the hills for no reason, and that he had asked two of them where they thought they were going and one of them had said they did not know and the other had said she had heard there was a queue where a woman took things.

“That is you,” he said. “That is what you are now. You are the thing people say at the bottom of a hill.”

She stood with that for a count of forty.

“Not one of them said I was here,” she said.

“No,” he said. “But they all know, and I asked them, and that is how a thing works when there is no office to be sent to.”

## What She Could Not Take Back

At about the seventh hour on the fourth she had stood in the road with a hundred and forty-nine in a line in the keeping and sixty-eight of them the road had watched her put there.

The other eighty-one she had done in her own voice with no one looking on, on a hillside, on a road, in front of a woman at a stall and a carter at a wall and thirty-eight others who had come to her one at a time in the dark.

She had said all of it standing, in the ordinary voice, before it was a line anywhere.

And it had got in after her.

She stood on the edge of the flat on the fifth of the month and said the last thing of the night out loud, and it was not to anybody.

“It is on the record,” she said, “and there is no putting a hand on the side of a page and stopping it going down, and there has not been one time in nineteen years that I have said a thing out loud and got it back.”
