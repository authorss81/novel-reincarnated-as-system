# Nine Words

Four hundred and five days since the ninth stage. One hundred and ninety-eight since it was shut. Eight hundred and thirty-six days in that room, and fifty-three days out of one. Eight hundred and forty-three days with this arm open at the elbow.

One thousand five hundred and seventy-eight days gone, taken out of her at a rate she derived out of her own life, not one of them coming back.

She said all of it standing at the ninth hour on the fourth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four, in the ordinary voice, at the gap in the low wall, first in her own voice before it was a line anywhere, with the road empty.

## The Length First

She worked out how long it had to be before she worked out what it was.

That was not the way she had been taught anything and she did it anyway, because on the twenty-third of a month in the rain she had found out that the only instrument in the world is a mouth that is moving, and a mouth that is moving has got one breath in it, and a hand with three fingers and a thumb on it can hold about four of anything.

So she stood at the gap in the wall and built words out of nothing, one at a time, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and counted them on the fingers she had, and when she got to nine she tried to go on to ten and could not get the first four back.

She tried it eleven times over the course of that day.

The first two times she used real words and they ran out at six and at seven. The third time she used the words out of a keeping report and got eleven and lost the first three. After that she just said whatever came, standing, in the ordinary voice, to a wall, and counted on her fingers.

A stone. A carter. The third of the month. Her mother’s floor. The left hand. A stair that does not stop at nine. Nine hundred years. The fourth of this month. A boy with pigs.

That is what her mouth did when it was left to itself and it was not nothing, because it got to nine about half the time and it never once got past nine without her losing the front of it, and a girl of nineteen standing on a wet road building nine words out of the air in the ordinary voice is the least dignified thing she has done since she came off that mountain.

Nine was the most her mouth and her hand could do at once without the front of it going out of her. Anything over nine and she lost the beginning. Anything under nine and there was room left in the breath for something else to come in.

Nine.

She said that out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at a gap in a wall on the fourth of a month, and it went into the keeping, and it was the first thing in nineteen years she had worked out about a sentence rather than about a figure.

## Three Of Them

Then she built three, and she said each one out loud before she built the next, because a thing is said in the voice of the person it is about, standing, before it is a line anywhere, and that does not stop applying when there is only her.

The first one was ten words and it was about the four hundred.

“Four hundred persons stand in a keeping that never asked them.”

She said it and then stood there for about a count of two hundred and nothing came, and by the time she had finished she had lost the first four words on the way out, which is what she had told herself in the morning would happen at ten, and that was the first thing she had proved with her own mouth in four days.

The second one was a demand.

“Take the walls down. I am standing here saying it out loud.”

She got about four words into it and stopped, because she had worked out on the twenty-eighth of the month that a machine which puts things down does not put down a thing that has not happened yet, and a demand is a thing that has not happened yet, and she had built nine words around a thing that could not go anywhere.

## The Third One

The third one she built with the grammar in her head. In the keeping’s shape, plain, about herself, in the past, in the way a keeping reports.

“I was told at seven that I was chosen.”

She said it standing, in the ordinary voice, at a gap in a low wall on a road where there was not one person on it, and she heard it go out of her and hang there in the wet air and come back to her own ears, and it was nine words and every one of them was true.

Then she waited, because she was going to be honest with herself about what she expected, which was that the ground would go out from under her the way it does when a bundle comes up.

The road stayed a road.

She waited about a count of four hundred. Nothing came. The lamp did not light, and it had not carried a flame since the fourteenth of the month beginning three thousand and seventy-four and it was not going to start now.

## What It Was

She said it a second time, straight away, to see whether it came out the same.

It did not come out the same. About one word out of nine, and the word that moved was the fifth, and it moved because by then it had stopped being something she was building and had become something she was delivering, which is the whole of what a groove in a road is.

She had done three sentences to about two hundred and seventy people in ten days and worn them flat. This was worse, because a flat sentence is the same sentence and this one changed under her while she was saying it.

So she could not rehearse it. She had known that since the twenty-second of a month in the rain, when a board under an eave had not stood up, and here was the proof of it in nine words on the fourth of a month.

## What The Body Did Without Asking

Her body made most of the decisions that day and she did not like it and she could not overrule it.

Her breath went to nine at about the seventh hour and stayed there, which is what it does standing still, and it had been four on the flat for nineteen years, and she had been out of a room for fifty-three days and had not slept two nights running and had had nothing to eat since the first of the month of the market before last.

The hand with three fingers and a thumb on it went at about the third hour and was worse in the afternoon than in the morning, and she found out again after fifty-three days out of a room that she was better with something in it than without, so she held the lamp for a while and put it down and picked it up twice for no reason at all.

And at about the eighth hour she sat down on the wall because her left leg had gone at the knee, which it did now, and she had stopped noticing that on the eighteenth day of the road and had started noticing it again the moment she had something to be frightened of.

Not one person in nine hundred years was going to come up that road and see a woman of nineteen sitting on a wall in the dark saying a sentence to an empty stretch of it.

## The Leaf Against Her Rib

At about the fourth hour she took the book out from against her fourth rib where it had been since she was ten years old and opened it on her knee.

Eight leaves. Four written on. Four blank, and blank since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and forty-four, and one of the four written on in charcoal about four days ago and put back without being read.

She had a stub of charcoal in the fold of the eighth leaf and she looked at it for a while and then she put the book away.

She had proved on the twenty-second of a month in the rain that a board cannot do this. What she had written then was for a stranger to read standing up, and that is not what this is.

This has to be nine words in her own mouth on one particular day in a particular voice in front of whoever turns up, and there is no version of that on a leaf that gets her there, and if she put it on paper it would be a copy of a sentence, and what she needs is the sentence.

She had made her own decision about that and she did not make it out loud, which is the one thing she has never once done in nineteen years and which she noticed about a count of forty later.

## Turning The Instrument The Wrong Way Round

She said it a third time and got it out clean, and then she stood there feeling sick about the whole of it, because a sentence that can be got out clean is a sentence that does not mean anything yet.

Then she sat down on the wall and worked out what she actually had.

She had a length and no content. She had nine words in the right grammar in the right tense about the right person and not one of them was the thing, and the reason none of them was the thing was that she had not gone and found out what the thing was.

She had been building it from the outside in.

She had the only instrument in the world that had ever found out anything, and it was hers, and it had been hers since she was ten years old, and in nineteen years she had never once pointed it at a sentence.

She said that out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, on the fourth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four, to a road with about four people walking up it who had no business being there.

“I have taken figures out of the air and checked them twice every morning since I was ten years old, and I have never once used it on a thing that was not a figure. It is the only instrument I have got that has ever found out anything, and I have been using it on the wrong thing for nineteen years.”
