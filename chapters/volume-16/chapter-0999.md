# The Account She Carried

“What did it cost you.”

The question had been standing in nine counties for nineteen years and it came into a yard above Lowwater in the mouth of a woman of about thirty-four who had been in the yard about a month, on the third of a month, in the afternoon, with a yoke over her arm.

Not what it cost the four hundred. Not what the ceiling was for. What it cost you.

Ning Yan stood in the yard with her hand on the bucket beside her and did not answer for about a count of eight hundred, and then she said, standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of about twenty people.

“I have got the account,” she said. “I have had it since I was fourteen years old and I have never once read it out loud, and I am going to read it out loud this afternoon at the ninth hour, and I am saying now, standing up, that I am not going to put a nice shape on it and I am not going to leave anything out of it, and that is the whole of the warning anybody gets.”

The woman said, “You do not have to read it to me.”

“No,” said Ning Yan. “That is why I am going to do it in front of twenty people instead of in front of you.”

## The Entries With Figures

She did the figures at the ninth hour, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the yard, in front of about twenty people, and then she read the rest, which had no figures in it at all.

“Six thousand four hundred and seventy-six days since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four. Seventeen years and three hundred and fifty-six days. I am thirty-six.

“Six thousand two hundred and seventy-three days since the thirtieth of a month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four.

“Six thousand three hundred and fifty-two days out of a room. Seven thousand one hundred and thirty-five days in that room before it.

“Seven thousand one hundred and forty-two days with this arm open at the elbow. Three fingers and a thumb on the right hand. Seven of the holds gone.”

She stopped for about a count of forty, which is what she does between figures, and then went on to the part with no figures in it at all.

## The Entries Without Figures

“These are the ones I cannot weigh and I am going to read them out loud and I am not going to invent a unit for any of them, and if it sounds like that is me dodging then it is me dodging.

“One. Four hundred people on a record that I cannot take them off, and I do not know what that costs them, and I told them so out loud before any of them answered, and it is the only thing I have ever offered anybody.

“Two. Four hundred and one. That is what nine counties are saying at the moment and it is what a woman with a basket said in a lane in the seventh county ten years ago, and I cannot get at the number and cannot correct it and have not been able to since the seventh county.

“Three. A night in the third year of my keeping. It is on the list. There is no unit in it and there has never been one and it did not get any lighter by being put down.

“Four. A list of decisions. Every one of them was made by a person about a person who was not in the room, and none of them was a cost, they were decisions, and I have never been able to get at the size of that in anything I know how to count.

“Five. Nine words, said standing, in the ordinary voice, at a gap in a wall, by a person of about nineteen who did not know what they would do. That is why there is a yard above Lowwater and there is no unit for that either.”

She stopped.

There were about twenty people in a yard above Lowwater at about the ninth hour on the third of a month, and the sun had gone off the top of the far wall, and a dog that lived under the east side got up, moved about nine paces, and lay down again somewhere else.

## The Part She Was Doing Without Knowing

She found out about half way through that she was counting the pauses between the entries.

That was the whole of it and it took her about a count of four hundred to notice and about another four hundred to decide not to do anything about it.

She had gone five entries into the account without a gap in her mouth longer than about a count of forty between any two of them, and she had not planned it, and when she noticed she went back over the last two and could not find where the keeping of it had happened.

Most of the yard had not noticed anything about it. A woman of about thirty had her arms folded. A boy of about fourteen was sitting on the wall with his boots off. A man of about fifty was standing about four paces off with his head slightly down, the way a man stands when he is listening to somebody rather than waiting for them to finish.

She read the rest of it with the pauses exactly as short as they had been and she explained them to not one person, and afterwards, for a long time, she could not have told anybody what the pauses had been for.

## What The Yard Did With It

And then about twenty people did something she had not expected and had not braced for, and it took her about a quarter of an hour to work out what was happening.

They started saying the figures after her.

Not the entries without figures. The numbers. A woman of about forty said six thousand three hundred and fifty-two days out of a room back at her, in a flat voice, halfway through, and got it right. A man of about twenty said seven thousand one hundred and forty-two and got that wrong and was corrected by a boy of about nine who had it perfect.

They had been in a yard for about ten years watching a woman say numbers out loud at the ninth hour every morning and they had taken to doing it the way children take to a thing, and there were not one instruction anywhere in nineteen years that had ever told them to.

“That is the second time somebody has taken a figure off me,” Ning Yan said, standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of about twenty people. “The first was in a lane in the seventh county and it was the wrong number by one and it went round nine counties and I could not get it back.

“I am not going to try to stop this. I want to say that standing up before somebody decides to stop it for me. Take the figures. Take them into the county, and into the third county and the eighth county, and get them right if you can get them right and get them wrong if you cannot.

“And where you have got one wrong, do not wait for me. Say it out loud yourself, in the ordinary voice, standing up, to the next person. I will not be able to get at it. I have never been able to get at anything I did not say in front of the person it was in front of.”

## The Reason It Closes

She finished late, about an hour after the yard had emptied enough to talk in. There were about nine people left and not one of them had said anything, and she said the last of it standing, in the ordinary voice, with her hands where they could be seen.

“This closes tonight. I am saying that out loud standing up so that it is in front of them first.

“It closes because there is not one person left in the world that I can hand it to. There is no office. There has not been an office above anything in nineteen years. There is no list, no message, no way to send anybody and no way to be sent, and there has not been since the ground came off.

“And not one person in the world has told me that it was settled, and it was not settled. I want that put down in the ordinary voice and I want it heard by everybody standing in this yard. It was not settled. It is not going to be settled. There is not one person left to settle it with, and that is the whole of the reason it closes, and it is not a reason.”

The woman of about thirty said, “Then what are you closing.”

“The reading of it,” said Ning Yan. “That is all of it. I am closing the reading of it.”

## The Second Time Through

And then she did the thing that mattered, and it took her about a quarter of an hour, and about twenty people watched it.

She went over the whole of it again, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in the yard, and she added nothing to it.

She did not say that any of it had been let go, because it had not been, and there was not one word available to her that would make it so, and a word said out loud standing goes into the air and cannot be taken back out of it, and she was not going to put one into a yard in the dark and then go on living in it.

She did not say that any of it was settled, because it was not, and she had said out loud within the hour that it was not, and she could not say the other thing anywhere near the place where she had said the first.

And she did not say one word about the fourth entry beyond the nine or ten she had already read out loud standing.

That was the hardest and she had decided on it at about the seventh hour without telling anybody.

## The East Trough

What she did instead was put her hand flat against the front of her coat, where the plate from the Broken Kiln was, and leave it there for about a count of four hundred, and take it off, and go and wash about nine buckets in the trough at the east side, because the east drain had been dug out in the fourteenth year and the trough had not been since.

The people went. The dogs stayed.

The light went off the top of the far wall and went up the sky from the other side of it, which it did every night and had done every night for about nineteen years, and there was nothing written on it at all.

She did the trough and the buckets and she came back into the yard and stood at the west end, where the light was better after the fourth hour, on the third of a month beginning nine thousand four hundred and thirty-four, in the eighteenth year of a thing she had started at twenty and had been counting since she was ten.

Then she went in, and she got up at about the fifth hour, and she did what she did every morning at the ninth hour, and there were about twenty people in the yard for it, and none of them knew that the account had been read out loud the night before and that it had cost nothing and settled nothing and was not going to be settled by anybody ever.
