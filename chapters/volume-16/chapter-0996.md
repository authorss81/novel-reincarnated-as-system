# She Said What She Was For

The girl was about fourteen and she asked it in the yard at about the fourth hour in the afternoon with about nine other people standing about four paces off listening, and she asked it the way a child asks a thing it has been told about.

“What are you for.”

Ning Yan put her hand on the bucket beside her and stood there for about a count of four hundred.

She had been asked what she wanted by a girl of about nine on a rim in the second month of a life she was not having any more of, and the answer she had given had been that she did not want anything, and she had said it out loud standing and it had been true and it had been the first true thing about herself she had said in nineteen years.

She could not give that answer here.

There were about nine people in a yard above Lowwater who had walked or been carried there, and two of them could stand in front of another person and say a true thing out loud for four or five minutes, and a woman was in the eighth county trying to make a third, and a boy of about ten was copying a hand that had been dead longer than he had been alive, and there was a rule about the fourth hour because of the light.

She was the only one in nine counties who was answerable for any of it and the yard knew it and had known it for about four years and had stopped asking.

## What She Found Out Standing There

She found it out by doing the search, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, because that is the only way she has ever been able to establish that a thing is true.

There is no office in nine counties above a yard with one working woman in it.

There is no list and no message and no way to be sent and no way to be met where she is expected. She cannot be given anything and cannot be paid and cannot follow a name she is handed, and she has said all of that out loud to about nine people a week for years and none of it has improved by being said.

And there is not one person alive she is able to ask what she is for, and there is not one person alive who is able to tell her, and there never was, and the search she had done once at a gate with a man of about sixty had cost him the only thing he had ever been able to answer yes to.

She came to the end of it standing in a yard in the afternoon with a timber lying across the beaten earth where it had come out of the top of the far wall an hour before, and about nine people standing four paces off her, and she said the last of it.

“I have been putting a question into the air at the ninth hour every morning for close on twelve years,” she said, “and there is not one person in the world who has heard it, and there never was, and I did not find that out this afternoon. I found out that I had already found out.

“There is not one person to tell me what to do and there has not been one since I was ten years old. And I have gone on standing in the middle of a yard waiting to be told, and I have been inside that for about four hundred days, and it looks exactly like teaching.”

## The Timber In The Yard

She had got the timber down before the girl asked, or rather she had got the timber down at about the second hour and then the girl had asked at the fourth, and about nine people had watched her do it with one arm and about four of them had not believed she would manage it and all of them had been wrong.

It had been up in the top of the far wall for longer than the yard had existed and the wall had been a wall before that and the timber had been in it before that, and it had come down at about the second hour of the afternoon on the twenty-seventh of a month in a wind that had not been doing anything in particular.

She got two women out from under the east end of it and a boy of about six out of the way of it, and she took the weight of about nine feet of it on her left forearm and her shoulder and put it down flat, and her arm went for about a count of two hundred afterwards and she had to sit down on the bucket for a bit.

That was the whole of the physical cost and it was not new to her and she did not make anything of it.

What she noticed, sitting on the bucket, was that the four people who had watched her do it had not said one word about it, and that in a yard of about nine people at the fourth hour there was a girl asking her what she was for, and that she had answered the timber by doing it and had not answered the girl.

## She Said What She Was For

She stood up off the bucket and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people in a yard above Lowwater, and she said it long enough to get all the way to the end of it in one go, because a thing said out loud standing goes into the air and cannot be taken back out of it and she had known that since she was about ten years old and had been using it as a tool ever since.

“I am the one who teaches here,” she said.

That was the first four words and she did not stop there.

“Not the only one. About forty of you can stand in front of somebody and say a true thing out loud for a few minutes and I cannot teach you the rest of it and have not been able to for ten years.

“But the answerable one is me. From this morning, in this yard, when a person asks what this place is for, the answer is mine, and if the answer is wrong then it is mine, and there is no office above this yard and no document and no name for it and there never was, so there is nothing in the world that can take it off me or hand it to somebody better.

“I have spent eleven years waiting to be told. I am stopping that this morning. I am going to say, standing up, in the ordinary voice, at the ninth hour, in front of whoever is here, what we are doing and what it is for, and I am going to do it every morning, and the first one of those is this afternoon’s and I am doing it now.”

The girl said, “What are you for.”

“Right now I am for telling you what we do this afternoon,” said Ning Yan, “and then I am going to mend the top of that wall, because it has been in the wall for longer than any of us and it is not going to stand there much longer and there are about four of you who have been under it since the second hour.”

## The Fifth Hour Of The Afternoon

And then she did the thing she had just said she was going to do, standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, at about the fifth hour, and it turned out to be a completely different thing from anything she had said out loud in nineteen years.

She told them what they were doing that afternoon. Two women were going to dig out the east drain. The boy on the bucket was going to finish the line and then start another one and she did not tell him whose it was.

A man of about forty was to go and find out whether the lane to the third county was passable, and if it was not passable he was to come back and not try it, which was the only instruction in the whole of it that she cared about.

That took about four minutes and about nine people did not leave.

Then she got to the second half of what she had promised, which was what it was for, and she stood there for about a count of eight hundred with her mouth open.

“In the old version I would have told them what it cost me,” she said. “What is on the record. That I do not know what it cost anybody, that not one person was sent anywhere, that no person could be un-bound. I have said that out loud in front of every one of you and it is the truest thing I have got and it is the whole of what I have ever offered anybody.

“And it is not what this yard is for. This yard is for the drain and the line and the lane. I have been standing in the middle of it telling you what it costs me for about four years and you have been doing the work and you have not needed one word of it.

“So the ninth hour from tomorrow is going to be this. What we are doing and what it is for. And every time the old thing is true I will say the old thing, because it is true, and I am not going to lose it just because I have found something better.”

## What It Cost

She did it at about the fifth hour and it took about ten minutes and about nine people stayed, and then she went and got the timber back up on her own with a wedge and about four people holding the far end, and it took two hours and a half.

Then she sat down and did the arithmetic out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, to about four people who were still there, because the rule was the rule.

“Four thousand two hundred and twenty days since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four. That is eleven years and two hundred and sixty days and I am thirty.

“Four thousand and seventeen days since the thirtieth of a month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four.

“Four thousand and ninety-six days out of a room. Four thousand eight hundred and seventy-nine days in that room before it. Four thousand eight hundred and eighty-six days with this arm open at the elbow, and not going to close.”

Then she said the last part of it, standing, in the ordinary voice, because it was the true part and because she had not said it out loud standing anywhere at any time and the rule was the rule.

“I have been the one who teaches here since the second morning I came up this lane and I have not known it out loud until the twenty-seventh of a month beginning seven thousand one hundred and fifty-four, and I am thirty years old, and I have not been at the gate my brother comes to in about eleven years and neither of us has broken that and I am not going to go out there today.

“That is what it costs. There is no relief in it. I have got about nine people in a yard and a rule about the fourth hour and a thing I said this afternoon that cannot be unsaid, and there is nothing in the world above my head.”

She stopped, and about four people were standing in the yard in the dark with the wall mended and the timber lying flat where it belonged, and not one of them told her she had got it right.
