# The Arithmetic Was Hers

Three hundred and fifty-eight days since the ninth stage. One hundred and fifty-one since it was shut. Seven hundred and eighty-nine days in that room, and there was no room.

Five hundred and fifty-six days since a cage lamp went out on a shelf in a building she had walked out of three days ago. Seven hundred and ninety-six days with this arm open at the elbow. One thousand five hundred and thirty-one days gone at the sixth of this month and four days since, taken out of her at a rate she derived out of her own life, not one of them coming back.

She said all of it standing in the open air at about the ninth hour on the seventeenth of the month beginning three thousand and seventy-four, in the ordinary voice, into nothing, first in her own voice before it was a line anywhere, because that was the rule and because she was not going to stop keeping it because there was no room to keep it in.

Six of them. There had been six of them every morning for nineteen years and there were six of them now, and she did not add a seventh, and she went on standing where she was.

Nothing came back.

She gave it a count of two hundred. She had not given a silence a count of two hundred since the second hour of the twenty-seventh of the month before last, and she gave it that count out of nineteen years of habit, and there was nothing in her that was going to answer it.

## What She Had Believed

She stood on a slope of open ground above a line of worn grass with a dead lamp in her left hand and worked out, standing, what had actually happened to her in nineteen years.

She had believed the room did the arithmetic.

That was it. That was the whole of the thing she had got wrong, and she had got it wrong for nineteen years while being the most precise person inside nine hundred years of a keeping, and it had taken a hillside and six days of not being able to get down it to find.

Every morning for nineteen years she had stood in that room and put six numbers into the dark and the dark had taken them, and she had taken that to mean that the numbers came from the mountain. She had taken it to mean that a keeping nine hundred years old had an arithmetic in it and she was the one reading out of it, the way a voice reads a figure off a wall.

She put the six of them apart on a hillside in the wind.

The ninth stage was taken on a day. She subtracted the day. The arm went open on a day. She subtracted the day. The days came out of her at a rate she had derived out of her own life and the rate was the only arithmetic in any of it and it was hers, and she had made it herself, out of her own life, when she was a child, and she had gone on believing since then that a thing she had made herself must have come from somewhere else.

Nothing on this mountain had ever given her a number.

She said that out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and then she said it again because she wanted to hear it be true the second time.

Nineteen years. She had been ten years old and not running, and she had been given a pen, and she had been given a count, and she had built a whole life out of the difference between what she was given and what was taken, and not one number in nineteen years of it had come from the mountain.

She had made them. She had checked them. She had said them out loud at the ninth hour into a room that took them and said nothing back, and she had gone on doing it six thousand times.

The taking had been the room’s silence and not its arithmetic.

## The Second Thing That Was Not There

The other thing she had believed was that the keeping was what took it.

She stood on the slope with the wind going through her wet clothes and worked at that one for a long time before she could get it into a shape she could look at.

A sentence said standing in the ordinary voice, before it is a line anywhere, and then the keeping takes it. That was a thing she had found on the fourteenth of a month at the fourth hour by running her fingers past the fifth mercy, and it had carried every binding since and one person in her arithmetic and it was the rule that made her who she was.

She still believed the rule.

What she had not believed, and had never once tested, was that the taking needed a room.

If the keeping was nine hundred years old and went through a mountain and every wall in the world at once, then the taking did not need a building. A building is a thing people put round a machine so that they can be near it and not touch it. She had spent seven hundred and eighty-nine days inside the thing people put round it.

## What That Meant

It meant the instrument had never been the room.

She stood on the slope and put her hand flat on the grass, which was a thing she had not done in nineteen years, and worked out what was actually in her hands.

One arithmetic, made by her, kept by her, carried by her, correct wherever she stood, in a mountain or on a road or in a county where she knew not one soul. Six figures every morning of her life. One breath of four counts. Seven on a rise. Nine standing still.

That was all she had brought down nine hundred and four steps’ worth of mountain with her, and it was enough to keep doing what she had always done, and it was not enough to do the thing she had come down here for.

She had no instrument for the thing she had come down here for.

## The Part She Could Not Test

She stood on the slope and looked at what she had to do.

Somebody has to be told a thing out loud, standing, before it is a line anywhere. Then the keeping takes it. She had done that once, at a table at the top of that mountain, in a room with no wear in it, and the keeping had taken it and had not answered and had not objected.

That had been in a building.

The keeping was nine hundred years old and it went through the whole of that mountain and every wall in the world at the same time, and she had worked that out standing at the foot of a stair on a morning she had had the going of a bundle of boards under her feet, and she had worked it out and not taken it anywhere.

Whether it would take a thing said on a hillside, in the open air, in front of a person who had not asked for it, she could not test.

She had no way to test it that did not involve doing it in front of somebody.

She worked that out twice, because it seemed like the kind of thing that might come out differently the second time, and it did not.

That was the wall in front of her and it was the first one in three days that she had not been able to get round by putting her left hand on a root, and she stood on the slope with her dead lamp in her hand and worked out that she had to go and find a person.

## What Was Below Her

There were roofs.

She had not seen them the day before and she had not looked, and there were nine of them and a bigger one with a slope of dark wood on it and the smoke was coming off that one and going sideways in the same wind that had been pushing at her for three days.

There were animals in the ground above them and the ground was worked, in lines, with a stone wall round one part of it, and she had seen ground worked in lines exactly once before in her life and had been ten years old at the time.

She stood on the slope above it and did the only arithmetic that was available to her about it.

She had been in a room for seven hundred and eighty-nine days. She had never once in that time been in a place where she did not know every object in it. She had been under a roof with nothing given to her and nothing taken from her that she had not counted, and the last thing she had done before she left was put her hand into a hollow that was the shape of her hand and let it be the shape of her hand.

Down there were nine roofs and a queue of smoke and people who would look up.

She had to find out whether the keeping would take a thing said in the open air before she said one, and the only place in the world she could find that out was standing in front of somebody, and she had no sentence.

She had never once in nineteen years had to have a sentence for a person. She had said six figures a morning into a room and once, at a table with no wear in it, a clause in the ordinary voice about a boy of sixteen carrying oil, and she had built all of nineteen years out of the difference between those two things.

There was a path from her feet down to that smoke, and she had three fingers and a thumb on the hand that could hold a lamp and a dead lamp in it, and she had not got a sentence for anybody in the world.

She had to go down there and find out whether the keeping would take one when she found it, and there was not one thing in her that could tell her.
