# It Did Not Object

She spent the seventh of the month finding out whether the world had changed, and she did it the only way she knew how, which was by asking people.

She had been out of the high country for eleven days and had a queue on a road for seven of them, and she had not once asked a single ordinary question about the shape of the place she was standing in, because she had never once in nineteen years needed to know what a ceiling was doing, since she had been the one who had gone past it.

She asked a man at about the seventh hour, on the second day, while she was waiting for a woman with a bad hand to make up her mind.

“How high can a person get.”

“Depends on the realm.”

“What is the top of one.”

He looked at her. “Nine,” he said. “Nine is the top of every realm there is. That is not an opinion, that is the shape of it. Why, what have you got.”

“Nothing. I have got nothing. I have never asked.”

“No,” he said, in the ordinary voice people use with somebody who has said something odd, “you have not.”

## Nine Is The Top Of Every Realm

She stood by the edge of the road for about a count of a hundred after he had gone.

She knew what that was. She had gone past it. She had shut the ninth of the seventh realm at the peak of it in a room at the top of a mountain with a bar against the wall dated nowhere, and she had never once, in the whole nineteen years, put the two things side by side.

Nine is the top of every realm there is.

That was a thing people said about the world, and it had been true for every person who ever said it, and it was still true, and she was standing on a road in a county where she knew three people and she had just put a hundred and forty-nine of them into a record with a keeping that made that true, and the keeping had not stopped, and the walls had not moved.

She found three more that day who said some version of it, and two of them were at nine themselves.

One of them was a man of about thirty with a broken collarbone set badly who had been at nine for a long time.

“I am not complaining about it,” he said, when she asked him why he did not go past. “There is nothing to complain about. Nine is the top. People in the cities say there is another one after and there is a thing that comes for you when you break through into it, and it is not true, and it is not true because there is nothing there. I have had a long time to find out and there is nothing there.”

“Have you tried.”

“I have tried every day for eleven years,” he said, and then he laughed at her face, and she understood that she had asked it in the voice of somebody who knew there was something there.

She said nothing else to him. She went and stood by the wall for a count of two hundred and felt the whole of it close over.

## It Had Not Objected

The layer had recorded a Sisterhood it was not asked for, at the fifth hour, on the fourth, in plain report grammar, four sentences.

It had not objected to the hundred and forty-nine.

It had not said that there was a condition on them. It had not put a price on them of its own. It had not asked her what she was doing, which is the one thing that every keeping she had ever read had done, at some point, with a turn in it and a narrowing.

It had said the walls are kept, and it had said that in the same four sentences in the same voice it used about a bundle of boards going up nine hundred and four steps on the first of a month.

She stood on the road and worked out what that meant and it was not a small thing and she said it out loud on the evening of the seventh, standing, in the ordinary voice, out loud, with the whole road going past, because she said it now whether or not there was a person in earshot.

“A machine nine hundred years old has been taking people since it started and it is doing it this morning, and I have just found one hundred and forty-nine more people standing in it, and it did not stop, and it did not change, and it did not have anything to say about me.”

She was not sure what she had wanted it to do. She had not known that she wanted anything.

Then she found out what she had wanted, at about the seventh hour, and it was not a small thing, and she said it out loud standing on the road with the words in her own mouth before she had decided to say them.

“I wanted it to stop. I have been on this road eleven days with a queue on it and a number in my head and I have been waiting every morning at the ninth hour for the keeping to come up out of the ground and tell me there was something wrong with what I have been doing.

“It did not tell me. It wrote a number down and carried on. And I have been standing here eleven days waiting for a thing that has not happened because in nine hundred years it has not happened to anybody, and it was never going to, and I want that said out loud because I am going to be here tomorrow and I do not want to keep waiting for it.”

## The Mistake She Made

She got a man’s name wrong on the seventh of the month and she caught it herself before she said the line.

He was about thirty and he had told her at about the eighth hour that he was called Fen and she took it in, and she stood waiting for him to finish, and when he had finished she opened her mouth and put out the name of a man she had bound on the third day in the dark near the pass.

She shut her mouth on it with the name half out.

She said sorry.

She had said sorry in the open on the thirtieth of last month and it had gone into a keeping, and she said it again here, and it went into the same one.

“All right,” the man said. “Fen.”

“Fen.”

“You have said a lot of other people’s names to me over the last three days,” he said, “and I want you to know that I noticed.”

She did not say anything to that. She could not say anything to that. There was no answer to it that was not either a lie or a thing she had to say standing in the open in front of everybody, and she was not going to stand in a queue and confess to a queue that she had got two hundred and some names in her head and about forty faces.

She had not got a way of carrying them and it was going to get worse and not better.

## The Question At The Back Of It

Two hundred and twenty-one by the ninth hour on the seventh.

She said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and a woman at the back of the queue, who was about fifty and who had been coming for two days and who had said yes on the afternoon of the sixth, said:

“What happens to us when you die.”

Ning Yan did not answer for about a count of a hundred.

It was not because she had nothing to say. She had three things to say and all three of them were true and none of them was an answer, and she had worked out in about a count of forty that she was not going to be able to say any of the three in front of two hundred people standing in a queue because they would all take it as a promise.

So she said the only one of them that was not a promise.

“I do not know what happens to you at the end of it,” she said, “and I am not going to stand here and tell you it will not matter, because it might, and because I have never in nineteen years been able to put a figure on what a person is owed by a thing they have gone into and I cannot do it for two hundred and twenty-one other people either.”

## What That Put Her In

She went and sat on the wall at the gap and put her hand flat on the stone and let it sit there.

Nine hundred years of it, and not one person in the world can be found from that machine, and not one of the names that went through it could be said by anybody. What the keeping keeps about a person is what they did and not what it cost, and then it is never looked at again.

That was the shape of the thing she had just started.

Not the binding. Not one of the two hundred and twenty-one was being sent anywhere and nothing was going to be done to them and every one of them had been told what was on the record in the plainest words she had before they answered.

The other half of it. That she was the one keeping the count. That when they were gone she would be the one with the figure, and she did not know what the figure would be, and the keeping would remember what each of them had done accurately and to the last detail and it would not have one line in nine hundred years about what any of it cost.

She had been made less solid for nineteen years by that machine and she had understood every part of it at the time and had asked nothing about what it was for.

She was asking now, at the ninth hour, on a wall, at the gap, and there was nobody in the world to ask.

That was the whole of the coldness of the four sentences that had come up out of the ground on the fourth. It had not objected to her. It had put a hundred and forty-nine of them in a record and gone on keeping the walls, correctly, in the same voice, and it was going to do exactly that for every one of them until there was nothing left of them to do it with.
