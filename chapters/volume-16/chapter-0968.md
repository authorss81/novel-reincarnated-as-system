# The Difference

The sleet came in off the flat about the fourth hour on the tenth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four and it came sideways, the kind that gets into a coat and stays there, and she was a long way out on the flat with nowhere to stand under and nothing to get to.

She stood up in the middle of it.

That was the whole of what she had been doing since she came off the mountain and it was going to be what she did for the rest of it: stand up, in the open, in bad weather, and work something out, and have nothing to put it on.

She did the working in the order she does everything, from the top, down, each part against another part, standing, out loud, in the sleet, with the words going sideways off her as she said them.

## What A Report Is

A keeping of nine hundred years puts down what has happened.

Not what will happen. Not what ought to happen. What has happened, one piece at a time, plain, in the order it happened in, and it does not put down two things in one line.

She had been reading one of those every morning since she was seven years old and she had never once had to think about the rules of it because you do not think about the rules of a thing that has never failed you.

She said all of it out loud in the sleet, and her voice went off sideways and she did not care.

“A report cannot be a complaint. It cannot be a demand. It cannot be a thing that has not happened yet, because the machine does not put down a thing that has not happened yet. I worked all of that out on the twenty-eighth of a month sitting on a wall, and it is right, and it is not enough.”

## What Is Not In One

And then she went through nineteen years of the incoming end, standing, in the sleet, going down the days, the way she goes down a row of figures.

And she found the thing that had been in front of her the whole time.

In nine hundred years of that keeping there is not one line that says it chose anybody.

Not one. Every line in the whole of it, and she has read a slice of it every morning of her life, is about a person and what happened to that person. The thing that made the announcement is never in the announcement. The announcement arrives after, and it reports, and whatever did the choosing was somewhere else, and it was never the keeping.

She stood in the sleet and felt the whole of nine hundred years go past her.

“All of it is after,” she said. “Every single word anybody in the world has ever heard from that thing was a word about something that had already been done.”

## What That Is About

Then the size of it arrived and she had to stand in the sleet and let it.

If not one line in nine hundred years says that keeping chose anybody, then in nine hundred years it has not chosen anybody. Not one person. Not once. Every announcement that has ever been made anywhere in the world has been a report about a thing that somebody else had already done.

There are thousands of them. There have been thousands for nine hundred years. And not one of them was a sentence in which the keeping said it had decided anything, because it does not decide anything, it arrives afterwards and it says what has occurred.

She had spent twelve years believing that a voice in her head at seven years old had said something about her that was not the same as the nine hundred years of everybody else.

It was the same as the nine hundred years of everybody else. It had arrived afterwards. It had been about something that had already been done.

And that was what made the sentence she needed. It was not an accusation. She had been wrong for twelve years about the whole of it and an accusation would have been a complaint and a complaint is not in the grammar.

It was going to be a report about the keeping, in the keeping’s own grammar, spoken by a person, and the person was going to be the only thing in nine hundred years that had ever stood inside one and been able to say so out loud.

## What Happened To Her

“So what happened to me.”

She said it and the sleet took it.

She was seven years old. A voice said she had been chosen, and it said it in her own head, and it used the same grammar as everything else the keeping had ever said to her, which is the only reason she had believed it for twelve years.

She had gone looking for the line.

Nineteen years, every morning at the ninth hour, a book of eight leaves against her fourth rib, and she had gone looking for the line that said she had been chosen, and there has never been one, and she had never once noticed there was not one, because she had been the one looking for it and you do not look at your own arithmetic.

There was not a line.

There had never been a line. There was a voice in a child’s head at seven years old using a true voice to say an untrue thing, and she had spent twelve years checking the figures underneath it because the figures were the part she could check.

## The Difference Is Not In Her

Being told and being chosen are not the same and the difference is not in her.

The difference is that one of them leaves a line in nine hundred years of a keeping and the other one does not.

That was it. That was the whole of it, and it had been true for twelve years and it had cost her an arm and a hand and seven holds out of eleven and a place behind her fourth rib the size of her thumbnail and one thousand five hundred and eighty-four days out of her own life, and she had found it on a flat in sleet on the tenth of a month with nothing to write it on.

She said the two halves out loud, standing, to the wind.

“Not one person chose me. There is no line. There was a voice at seven and it used a true voice and I believed it for twelve years and I went and looked for the line every morning of my life.”

And then, because it had to be said out loud before it could be anything at all, she said the other half of it, which was the half that mattered.

“There is a difference.”

And she put her hand flat against her own coat over the fourth rib and pressed, and there was a place under her fingers the size of her thumbnail that had been there since the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and four, and it was not any bigger and it was not any smaller, and it had been the same size on every single day she had put her hand on it, and it had never once hurt.

That was what it had bought. Twelve years of believing a voice, and a place under her ribs the size of a thumbnail, and not one thing that hurts.

## Nine Words

Then she built it, and it took her about a count of eight hundred, and she did not say it.

She said the words one at a time, standing, in the sleet, and put them in order against each other the way she puts figures against each other, and it came to nine, and she counted them on three fingers and a thumb and there was one over and she left it.

Never chosen. I was told. There is a difference.

Nine words. Past tense. About her. In the plainest words in the world. One thing at a time and then the thing that the one thing means.

A report, in the grammar of a keeping of nine hundred years, saying something that has already happened, spoken by the person it is about, standing, in the ordinary voice.

And the whole of the nine hundred years of that machine had never once had one of those, and she was not going to survive it either if she did it in the wrong shape.

She had about a count of three hundred afterwards standing in the sleet doing the worst part of it, which was checking.

A machine that has never been refused by anybody it recognised. A machine that has kept the walls for nine hundred years and has put down four hundred persons and the price of each without being asked and has gone on keeping the walls. A machine that has never once written down a line saying it chose anybody, because it never has, because it is a thing that reports.

And this sentence says a true thing about it, in its own grammar, standing up, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and there is not one example of the sort anywhere in nine hundred years of it.

She worked out that this was the only thing she had that it could not survive being told.

## What She Did Not Do

She did not say them.

That was the discipline and it was not about the layer and it was not about fear. She had nine words in her head for the first time in her life and the rule is that a thing is said in the voice of the person it is about, standing, before it is a line anywhere, and there was not a person standing there.

She had four hundred people in her arithmetic and about nine of them within calling on a road in one county, and she had not decided about a single one of them, and she was not going to spend the only true thing she had ever found out on a flat in the sleet with the wind on it and an empty horizon on every side.

So she stood in the sleet with nine words in her head that she could not say and had to work out who they were for.
