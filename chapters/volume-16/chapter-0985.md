# What He Came To Say

She got to the gate at about the fourth hour of the tenth day and there was a man standing on the near side of it with his hands at his sides, and she knew him in about a count of forty and she did not go any faster for it.

He had a coat on that she had not seen since she was at the turn, and his hair had gone further back than it was, and there was a burn on the outside of his left hand that was older than the last time and newer than the time before that.

He did not come through the gate. He was on her side of it, which she noticed, and she stood on her side of it too, and for about a count of four hundred not one of them said anything at all.

Then he said, “You used to say *I am going to see you before the week is out.*”

“That was not what I said on the thirtieth of the month.”

“No,” Ning Wu said. “You just said it in the past tense about a thing you are doing today. You have never done that in your life.”

She stood at a gate with no wall on either side of it and looked at her brother and worked out, standing, in the ordinary voice, on her own, that he was right and that she had no idea when she had started.

“I had not said a thing out loud before it was true since the thirtieth of the month,” she said. “Before that I did it for nineteen years. It was not a good instrument and I found that out the hard way.”

“Right,” he said. “That is not nothing.”

## Nine Days Of Ground On Foot

He had come over nine days of ground that was not where it had been, on foot, with a cart for the first three days and then without it.

“A first of a month went past and nothing came up,” he said. “I have known about a first of a month since I was in the ground and I have never once known one not come. That is all I came for. I did not come for anything else and I have not thought about anything else in nine days.”

“You were the only person inside that knew what I was.”

“You were the only person in the world who knew what you were, from the inside,” Ning Wu said. “And I was the only person in there who had been told, and I was told by a man with a wet thumb who wrote it down because it was his day to write it down, and that was before you were ever in the room.”

That was not what she expected him to say and she felt it go past her in about a count of two hundred.

## What He Came To Say

He had come to say one thing and she made him stand there and get to it, because she has never once in her life let a person off a thing they had walked nine days to say.

He said, “I wanted to know whether I was going to have to do anything.”

That was the whole of it. It took him a long time to get to and he got to it in four pieces, and when he had said it he put his hands in his coat and stood there.

“Nine days of ground that was not where it was. Four hundred people off a terrace and three hundred out of a valley, and my name had come up twice in nine days, once from a woman in a street and once from a man with a broken shaft who said it in the voice people use when they want you to know they have said it.

“And not one person had come to see me. Not one person had said anything to me. And I did not come to ask you to make it not happen.

“I came because there was a first of a month with nothing on it, and I was the only one of the people inside that wall who would have gone to look for you, and I went to look for you, and I was here.”

She stood at the gate and put it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of her brother, before she answered any part of it.

“I did not do that. There was no way on this earth for me to have done that. I bound four hundred people by standing in front of them on a road and saying three sentences out loud, and not one person was sent anywhere and not one person was entered anywhere and I was not able to send anybody or give anybody anything or follow a name I was handed.”

“I knew you could not.”

“Then you knew what the answer was.”

“No,” Ning Wu said. “I did not. I was asking you because you were the only one who would know and because you had said out loud that you would say things before anything else happened.”

## What He Had Of His Own

She said it, and it was the true part and it was the only part she had.

“I said nine words at a gap in a wall on the thirtieth of a month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four. I was standing and I said them in the ordinary voice in front of eight people and the layer wrote them and there was not one thing on this earth that could put them back.

“I did not know that it did what people were saying it did. I could tell you ten places in four counties where that stone stood, and eight of them came apart in nine days, and two more places where there was nothing at all. That was the whole of what I had, and I had not got one word for what it was.”

He listened to the whole of it.

Then he said, “They moved me up out of the furnace crew before I was in the ground.”

She had not known that. She had known the kiln record and the reassignment and that it was above the authority of the furnace, and she had not known it was the other way round, that it was done to him, and done early.

“Why.”

“Because of you,” Ning Wu said. “That is the only reason. There was a boy in the intake and his sister was in the furnace crew and it was the day for it, and I was moved up to a place where they do not touch people, and I have been in places like that ever since, and that is the whole of what anybody ever did for me in my life.”

He said it flat, standing on the near side of a gate.

“I was not telling you that so that you would do something about it. I was telling you because you were going to spend the next year working out whether you did it, and you did, and there was nothing on either side of that for either of us.”

## What She Could Not Do

She could not put a hand on it.

That was not a decision and it was not a refusal and it was not a wall she had built. She had one arm that worked and a brother standing about four paces off it and she could have put it on his shoulder and she did not.

What she did instead was say the true thing out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, before she said anything else, which is the rule and has been the rule since she was a child.

“I was not going to be able to say the thing you wanted me to say. Not that day and not in a week. I was not able to put it anywhere and I had not got it and I had told four hundred people that I would not have it before they said yes, and I was not going to go back on that in front of my brother in front of a gate.”

“All right,” Ning Wu said.

“I was not going to stop standing here either.”

“I did not think you were going to.”

## The Bundle He Brought

He had a bundle on the bank with him the whole time and she did not notice it for about a count of three hundred, and when she did notice it she did not know what to do with it for about a count of six hundred after that.

There was bread in it and there was a piece of bacon and there were about nine pounds of something she could not identify and it was wrapped in a cloth that had been on his cart when he was thirteen.

He put it down on the bank between them at about the seventh hour without saying anything at all.

“I am not able to take that,” Ning Yan said.

“It is not for you,” Ning Wu said. “It is for the walk.”

“I am not able to be given anything.”

“You are standing at a gate on a hill in the cold for nine hours a day and there is nothing on you from this morning. I have walked nine days and I have eaten every day of it. That is the whole of the arithmetic and I worked it out on the fourth day.”

He did not look at her.

“I am not giving it to you. I am putting it on the bank and you can do what you want with it and it does not cost you anything to do it.”

She stood there for about a count of four hundred with her hand out and not touching it.

And then she took about half of it and left him the other half, and that was the whole of what either of them was able to do on that bank, and neither of them mentioned it again for the rest of the day.

## What They Did Instead

They worked out an arrangement on the bank at about the eighth hour, sitting down, like two people doing sums, and neither of them called it anything.

She cannot be met where she is expected. She cannot follow a name she is given. She cannot be given anything and cannot be paid and there is no office and no list and no message and there are three hundred and ninety-nine people in nine counties who have not been told that there was a place to stand.

He can walk. He has been walking since he was a child in a barrow ground and he has not given it up, and he has not given it up for her and she has not asked him to.

So he will walk. She will stand in one place at the ninth hour. He will come to the gate and stand on the near side of it, which is hers, and she will put out loud, in the ordinary voice, in front of about nine people, what she has been putting out loud in front of nine people for a month.

Not a promise and not a joining and not one word said out loud about what happened between them.

Every third day, unless the ground will not let him cross.

And that was the whole of what she said, standing, in the ordinary voice, at a gate, on the tenth day of a month, with her hands where he could see them.

“Eighty-nine days since I walked out of the room I was in. That was what I had to give him and I had said it before anything else so that it was in front of them first.”

Ning Wu stood up off the bank and looked at her for about a count of two hundred.

“Eighty-nine,” he said. “I would have come on the first.”
