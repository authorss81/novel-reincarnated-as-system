# A Child Counting

Eighty-four days out of a room. Eight hundred and sixty-seven days in that room before it, and a door that closed in the month beginning three thousand and seventy-four.

She said both of them standing at the west rim at the ninth hour on the fifth of a month, in the ordinary voice, in the cold, to about a hundred people who had slept on the ground there, and then she went to say the next one and there was not one.

## Reaching Down The Side Of The Bed

It was like putting your hand down the side of a bed for a cup that has been there every morning for nineteen years.

She had said six figures at the ninth hour every day since she was ten years old. She had said them in an order. She had got to the end of the two about her own life and she reached for the third and there was nothing in her hand.

She stood there for about a count of two hundred with her mouth open, in front of about a hundred people, and nothing came, and then she closed it.

There was no line about the ceiling coming off. There was no line about nine hundred years. There was no line about the number of mornings or the rate at which she had been losing them, and there was not one figure of any of them that she had put into that column for nineteen years.

What she had said about her own life, twice a day, every day since she was a child, had been a way of standing still.

She said that out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, to a hundred people on a rim.

“I did not have the rest of the numbers. There was a run of them and they were not there this morning and I was not able to put a substitute in the place of one, and if anybody here wants to tell me what they were, I was not going to take it from you.”

Somebody at the back said, “That is the first thing you have ever said that I did not understand.”

“Yes,” Ning Yan said. “It is also the first true one.”

## Holding What She Had

About a hundred and forty people were on that rim and they could not stay on it.

That was not sentiment. It was arithmetic. There was stone on the west rim for about the length of two carts and then there was grass with a drop off it, and there was about four days of water in the village and two days of anything to eat with it, and every number in that was a number she could derive standing up in the ordinary voice.

So she said the whole of it out loud, in order, on the rim, in the cold, and she gave them the arithmetic first and the arithmetic was not good news.

“There was stone here for about two carts. There was water in the village for four days. There was food in the village for two days with it.

“And there was a road on the far side that climbed, and it climbed out of the bowl into country I had not walked, and I walked nine posts on the way in and I could find the way back out with my own eyes.”

A man of about sixty at the front said, “So we go up the road.”

“Some of you will. I was not going to tell you to go and I was not going to tell you to stay, and that was not me being careful. I was telling you what the numbers were and I was not able to give you anything that morning, and that had been true for a month.”

Then she did the other thing, which was the thing she had actually done at the flat with the terrace and had not understood until it was done.

She said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and it came out plainer than anything she had said in nineteen years.

“You had a number now and I was not one of you and I was not in charge of this. That was new and I had not had it before. So use it.”

## About Thirty Stayed

About thirty stayed on the rim.

About a hundred and ten went out on the road that climbed, on their own feet, over about four hours, in ones and twos and in families, and she stood on the west rim the whole of it and did not say one word to any of them except the road name when somebody asked.

There was no procession. There was no gathering. Not one person looked for somebody to put at the front of it, because there was no such person and there was no way of making one, and they had not needed one for four days already.

One woman of about thirty came back over the rim at about the eleventh hour and said, “The road goes up. There is a wood. There is a stream coming out of the wood going east and it is not the stream in the village.”

She said that last part as though it were the important one, and it was. Ning Yan wrote nothing down. She could not keep what she finds by holding it and she has not been able to for nineteen years.

She put the stream out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, so that about thirty people on a rim would have it, and she did not write it, and she has said for a month that she cannot keep what she finds by holding it and the whole of the last four days has been the finding out.

## What She Was Going To Do About That

She sat down on the west rim at about the eighth hour and worked out, sitting, in the ordinary voice, to about four people lying down near her, the thing she had been putting off since the sixth hour of the fourth day.

She could do it and she could not do it at the same time.

Four counties. A wall line with a day missing out of the middle of it. A bowl with about three hundred people in it. A gate with nothing on either side of it. And behind all of it three hundred and ninety-nine people in nine counties who had said yes to three sentences about oil and had not been told one word since.

She could stand in one place and be right about that place. She could not be in two, and she could not send anybody, and she could not be given anything, and she could not be paid, and there was no office in nine counties that would take a thing off her hands and carry it.

That is what she said out loud, and then she said the part that she had only worked out once she had said the first part.

“So the only thing that worked was that people came to where I was. That was not a choice I had made. It was the only shape it could be in and it had been the shape of it since the twentieth of a month on a hillside, and I had been calling it a road.”

She said that and then she said the harder half of it, standing, in the ordinary voice, because the harder half was the one that had to go into the record first.

“And it meant that about four hundred people were going to have to walk to a rim to find out anything, and there were about three hundred of them who did not know that I was on it. I had no way of going to tell them. I had said that out loud every day for a month and it had not improved by saying it.”

## The Only Instrument In The County

She measured the eastern rim at about the seventh hour because the eastern rim was the part that had gone and she wanted to know whether it was going to go again.

She did it in her own paces against her own arm, which she has done since she was fourteen and has never once been wrong about in front of her, and she got a figure, and then she timed how long the ground had taken to come down by the length of her own breath standing still, which is nine, and which is the same nine every time and has been since she was ten years old.

And then she stopped, because she understood standing on a wet rim in the cold what she actually was.

There was not one other person in four counties who could do either of those two things. Her own arm and her own breath were the only instruments left in nine hundred years of a bound, and she had been carrying them about since she was a child, and not one person had ever asked her for them.

Her right arm came up about a foot while she was doing it and stopped, the way it has stopped for eight hundred and seventy-four days, and she finished the measurement with the left, and she noticed that it had become the ordinary way rather than the hard way.

## A Child Of About Nine

There was a girl on the rim of about nine who came and stood about four paces off and watched her do it twice.

The girl had a piece of chalk out. She had found it, and she had worked out by herself that you can count on the flat of a slate, and she was doing it.

Ning Yan let her get it wrong for about a count of a hundred.

Then the girl said, “That is the wrong one first.”

“It is,” Ning Yan said.

“Why.”

“Because it was the biggest one and I had always said the biggest one first and not one person had ever asked me why. It was not because it was more important. It was because it had been first since I was ten, and my mouth knew it and my mouth did not know anything else about that column.”

The girl thought about that for a long time with her chin on her hand.

“Can I do it,” she said.

“You can do it. It is not mine. It was never mine.”

And she said the two figures out loud again, standing, in the ordinary voice, and the girl wrote them on the slate in her own hand, upside down, and got one of the two the wrong way round and did not notice for about a count of four hundred.

## The One At The End Of It

She said the two figures at the ninth hour and the girl said them after her from the slate, and they were the right way round by then, and she had got all of them, and then she looked up.

“What is the next one.”

That was the end of it.

Ning Yan stood on the west rim of a bowl in the cold on the fifth of a month with a girl of about nine looking at her and about thirty people lying down behind her, and she worked out the answer standing up, in the ordinary voice, in front of both of them.

“There was not one more,” she said. “That was the whole of the run. And you were the first person who had ever asked me for the next one, and I had been standing here nine days without knowing that was a thing anybody could ask.”

The girl put the chalk down.

“Can you not make one up.”

“No.”

“That is sad.”

“Yes,” Ning Yan said. “It was. Come and look at the eastern rim with me and I would show you how to count something that was not gone.”
