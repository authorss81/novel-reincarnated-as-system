# The Valley Mouth

She went through the gate at about the fifth hour of the fourth day, on her own, with a basket, and it took her about a count of forty to walk through a thing that had been shut for nine hundred years and did not once touch her.

She had expected something. She had stood at that gate at the last of the light on the third day and worked out what she expected, standing up, in the ordinary voice, to a shut gate, and what she had expected was that it would be a door.

It was a gap in two piers with a black leaf of wood standing open in it, and she walked through, and the ground on the far side was ordinary ground and the air was ordinary air, and there was not one thing about it that she could have put a figure on.

## The Bowl

About two hours inside, in country she did not know, the road went over a rise and there was a bowl of hills underneath her with about three hundred people in it.

The bowl was about as wide across as the whole of the flat she had been working, and it had a village in the bottom of it with a stream through it and about nine fields that had been in cultivation for as long as anybody there could remember.

And it had a ring of posts round the rim of it.

She counted twenty-two of them going round, and they were the same stone as the nine on the wall and the one on the quarry terrace, and they were the height of a man’s chest and nothing at all was cut on any of them.

That was not the part she stopped for either.

The part she stopped for was that the three hundred people were in the bottom of it.

## Not One Word Of It Said Anywhere

She came down off the rise into the village at about the sixth hour and about forty people were in the street and not one of them had been told a single thing.

That was the part she had not braced for, and she braced for it wrong, and she had braced for the opposite.

She had braced for a keeping. Nineteen years of a line coming up under her feet in the ordinary grammar at the ninth hour, four plain sentences, a bundle, and the walls are kept.

She had expected the whole shape of it to be here in the bottom of this bowl with a set of figures in it, and she had been ready to stand up in the ordinary voice and put a figure against the next one.

There was nothing in nine hundred years of that habit that would have taught her what to do on a street where not one person had said a word.

Not one person had said a word.

A woman of about sixty was outside her own door with a child on her hip telling a neighbour that the eastern bank had moved in the night, which was true, and that it had moved again since, which was also true, and that there was a woman on the rim going along it calling numbers out loud, which was the strangest of the three and got the most argument.

There was no line. There was no set of figures. There was a street and a woman of sixty and a child and a thing that had happened.

So Ning Yan stood in the street and did the only thing there was, which was to say what she had said on a road at a gap in a wall a month ago, in the ordinary voice, standing, with her hands where people could see them.

“What I had for it was this. I walked a line of wall yesterday and there was a day of it missing out of the middle, and a gate standing with nothing on either side of it, and I came through that gate this morning.

I had no idea what did it. I was not able to tell them whether it would do it again tonight and I was not going to tell them it would not. That was all of it and it was a very little and I said it here before I said anything else.”

The woman of about sixty looked at her for a long moment.

“You have come a long way to say that.”

“Yes.”

## Inside A Thing That Was Holding Them

She worked that out standing at the rim with the wind off the west, in about a count of three hundred, and it came out the way it came out in nine hundred years of nine counties and she had never once in her life been on the wrong side of it.

A person inside a bound is not inside a fence. A person inside a bound is inside a shape, and the shape is the lowest place there is, because everything else in the world slopes off it.

That village was in the lowest place in four counties, and three hundred people had been born in it and had died in it and had married in it for longer than the posts round it had been standing, and every single road that came into that bowl came down, and not one road had ever gone out of it upward, and not one person in it had ever asked why, because there was nothing in it to ask.

She had been in the lowest place in the world for nineteen years. She had simply been in one that had a wall on it.

## A Line That Stayed

The first move came at about the seventh hour and it came down the eastern side of the bowl in a long slow shoulder, the way a shoulder of ground comes down when the ground under it lets go of something it had been holding.

About forty people went down with it. They went into the stream and they did not drown and none of them broke anything, and what happened was that the eastern side came in about a body’s width and the stream came up the other side and there was a lot of shouting and a great deal of water in about nine houses.

She found the line that stayed at about the eighth hour.

It was the rim itself. Every part of the rim on the far side held, and the whole of the west side held, and about a third of the eastern rim had gone. She stood on the west rim and she could see the whole of the bowl from it, and there was one road out of it on the far side that climbed.

So she did the only thing her arithmetic had ever been good for, which was to put a line down and then count along it.

She counted the people out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, along the rim, from the point where the eastern side had gone round to the road that climbed. Three hundred and nine, when she had done the whole of it twice from both ends and got the same figure, which is the only arithmetic she trusts.

And then she worked out where every one of them was, which is not arithmetic at all and took her the rest of the day, and is the first thing she has ever done that no part of her training covers.

## Going Back For A Chest

A man of about fifty went back down into it at about the eleventh hour for a chest.

She saw him go and she went after him, and she caught him at the foot of the eastern slope with his arms round a chest he could not carry up by himself, and while the two of them were standing on the flat brown place with the stream coming past, the ground on the east side went again.

It did not come down onto them. It came down about nine paces away on the other side of the stream, into the field that was already gone, and the sound of it was the sound of a great deal of earth going into water, and it went on for a while.

She stood there with a man and a chest and nine paces of air between them and it, and she waited it out, and she waited it out standing up, and her breath was at nine the whole time and she could not have brought it down.

They got the chest up the slope in the ordinary way, one end each, and she got him to the rim, and then she sat down on the rim for about a count of four hundred before she said anything to anybody.

## A Hundred People On The Rim

They came up out of the bowl all through the afternoon and into the light, three hundred and nine of them, and there was no part of that that she organised and no part of that anybody else organised either.

They came up because there was a woman on the rim going along it saying a number out loud in the ordinary voice.

By the last of the light there were about a hundred of them standing on the west rim in the cold, and a hundred more had gone out on the road that climbed, and the rest were in the village with nothing to stand on.

They asked her the same question a great many times. She gave the same answer every time, standing, in the ordinary voice, with her hands where people could see them.

She said: she did not know. She had walked nine posts and one gate and one day of missing ground and she had counted three hundred and nine people out of a bowl and she could not tell them what had done it or whether it would do it again tonight.

And about the fortieth time she said it, a woman of about fifty at the back of the crowd put her hand up and asked it a different way.

“Were you going to be here tomorrow.”

Ning Yan stood on the rim of a bowl in the wind and worked out, standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of a hundred people, what the answer was.

“Yes,” she said. “I would be here in the morning. And I would be here the day after, and I would be here until there was nothing left on the rim for me to stand on.

“And I was telling them that then, standing up, so that they could hold me to it and so that I could not get up in the morning and find a reason not to be.”

The woman at the back of the crowd did not answer that, and neither did the crowd, and about forty of them sat down on the rim, which is what people do when they have decided to stay.

Ning Yan stayed there until about the ninth hour and said one figure out loud into the cold, which was that there were eighty-three days between that morning and the day she had walked out of the room she had been in for eight hundred and sixty-six days, and that she was going to be standing on that rim in the morning.
