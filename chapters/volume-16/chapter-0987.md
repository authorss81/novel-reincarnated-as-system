# Four Days At The Crossing

She had been at the crossing since the fourteenth day of the month and the seventeenth was the fourth day of it.

She went there in the first place because she had run out of the other kind of looking, and because there was one thing in the world she had never once in nineteen years failed to do, and she wanted to find out whether it had stopped.

The crossing is where two made roads go across a flat and a low field wall comes down to the edge of the northern one with about a foot of gap in it where somebody took a stone out before she was born and did not put it back.

She had said nine words in that gap on the thirtieth of a month at the ninth hour.

## What The Ground Did Not Do

She stood in the gap at about the fifth hour and waited for the ground to go quiet under her boots.

That is the thing. Since she was ten years old she had known the quiet that comes up through the earth when a keeping takes a thing, and she had felt it under her feet in the open on a first of a month with a bundle of boards going up nine hundred and four steps, and she had felt it once on the thirtieth of a month at about the ninth hour in a rain and it had not stopped.

She stood in the gap for about a count of six hundred.

Nothing came up through the ground. There was ordinary earth under her boots and a cart a good way off on the northern road and a woman selling cheese about forty paces away who was arguing with a customer, and under all of it the flat ordinary quiet of a flat in the middle of a day.

She stood there for about two hours on the first of the four, and three hours on the second, and on the third she stood in it from the fifth hour to the dark with about a hundred people within calling of it, and on the fourth, which was the seventeenth, she stood in it from the light and did not sit down at all, and she looked at every one of them.

## What There Was To Find

She made a list of the four things she had, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the gap, on the first of those days, to about nine people who had come to hear what she had.

The bundle. There was no stair and she was not going back to it and she was not going to pretend she was going to look.

The quiet under the ground. She had just spent four hours in it and it was not there.

A line in the ordinary grammar at the ninth hour. She had not had one since a bundle and the walls in the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four, and she was not going to ask for one and she had never been able to.

And anybody in nine counties who wanted any of it back.

That was the fourth one and she put it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at a gap in a wall, because it is a thing you say out loud before you go looking for it, and she had been looking for it in her own head for about a week and it had taken her four days to get to the gap and say it.

Then she went and looked.

## What People Were Doing

She asked, in the flat, at the stalls, on the roads, at the terrace at Lowwater, at the weir, and at the two places in the second county she could get to. Not the question. That was not the point.

The point was what a person did on a morning in the seventeenth day of a month.

A man of about sixty was moving a boundary stone. Not a wall stone. A boundary stone, a field one, about knee high, and he had a barrow and two men and he had taken it up and put it down about four times and was standing looking at where it had been.

Two women were arguing about a boundary and were enjoying it.

A carter was carrying a beam to the far bank of the gate and had been doing it for three days and had got quite good at it.

The kiln-man’s wife had two hundred and nine pots and a kiln that would not take another firing for a season, and she had put them out on the flat to dry and had made a roof over them out of a cart.

Nine counties of people were doing the ordinary things that people do when there is a great deal to do and the reason is not going to be settled this month.

She stood at a gate for about a count of four hundred and worked out, standing, in the ordinary voice, on her own, what that was.

Not one of them was waiting.

That was the finding and it took four days and she had braced against every other version of it. Not one person in four counties was going to be standing there on the seventeenth day of a month waiting to be told what had happened to their world, and not one of them had come to her to be told, and the two men moving a boundary stone on the flat would have been astonished to be told they had come to her.

She looked for it for four days and there was not one sign of it anywhere.

## Stopping On The Fourth Day

She stopped on the fourth day at about the eighth hour, standing in the gap in the low field wall, in the ordinary voice, to herself, out loud, because she has said every true thing out loud standing since she was a child and she was not going to make an exception for the one that was about herself.

She said the arithmetic of it, which is short and which she got right first time.

Seventeen days. One stone. Four days of looking in one place and about two counties walked before that. Nine counties she has never been in. No bundle, no stair, no line in the ordinary grammar, no report, no office, no list, no message, and not one person in the world she is able to ask whether they want it back.

Nothing came up. And no one came. And she had looked properly, and she had looked the way she looks, which is with her own two feet and her own two eyes and her own arithmetic, and there was not one further thing she is able to do.

So she stopped.

That was the whole of the seventeenth day and it was the first decision she had made since the thirtieth of a month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four that was not about keeping somebody alive, and she made it standing up, with no instrument.

Not because she is brave. She had said out loud on the twenty-eighth of a month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four that she was not brave and she had not argued with it since and she was not going to start now.

Because she had four days, and she had looked, and looking was the whole of what she had, and she had got the answer and it was that there was not one coming back, and a person can look for four days and then has to do something else.

## The Question She Had Never Been Asked

The girl of about nine came up the road from the south on the fourth evening with the slate on her knee and the chalk in her hand, and she had walked it herself, and not one adult had come with her.

She came in on this, and she came in on it the way a child comes in on a thing that has been standing in a room.

“What did you want.”

Ning Yan was sitting on the west rim with her back against a stone that had been a wall of something once, and the question went into her and went all the way down, and it took her about a count of two hundred to find out that there was nothing at the bottom of it.

“Out of all of it,” the girl said. “Out of the nine days and the stones and the four counties. Out of it all. What did you want.”

She has been asked that question in four hundred different ways by strangers on roads for a month and not one of them had asked it that way. Every one of them had wanted to know what was about to happen to them. Not one of them had wanted to know what she wanted, because everybody had worked out in about the first count of a hundred that she was not a person who wanted things.

“I did not want anything,” she said.

The girl waited, because she was about nine and she was not going to be satisfied with a thing that short.

“No,” Ning Yan said. “I found that out about nine days ago and I had not told anybody yet and I would rather you had it than that I had kept it.”

“The shape of it was: there was a thing being done to nine hundred years of a world by a machine that had never once been asked anything, and the sentence was the only thing in the world that could go into it, and I was the only one who could say it.

“And I have been asked nine thousand times since whether it was going to be all right, and not one person has asked me whether I wanted to, and I have not wanted anything. That is not a virtue and it is not a wound. It is a woman who spent nineteen years in a room being told what she was for and then went out and found out that there was nothing in her at all that wanted a thing.”

The girl thought about that with her chin on her hand.

“That is sad,” she said.

“Yes,” Ning Yan said. “It is. Come and look at the eastern rim with me and I will show you how to count something that is not gone.”

## The Lamp In Her Left Hand

She picked the lamp up off the wall at about the eighth hour.

It had not carried a flame since the fourteenth of a month beginning three thousand and seventy-four and she had brought it down a mountain and across four counties and set it down outside the gap on the thirtieth because she was not going to be holding anything while she said nine words.

She stood there and looked at it for about a count of two hundred.

She could not refill it. There is no oil in nine hundred years of any keeping in this world and there has not been for seventeen days and there was not one drop of anything she could put into it.

She could leave it on the wall outside the gap, which is where it had been for seventeen days and where it would keep perfectly well.

She picked it up and put it in her left hand and carried it down the road, and there was no reason on this earth that she could put in a column, and she was not going to try.

And then she stood in the gap for about a count of a hundred and worked out what she actually had, standing, in the ordinary voice, on her own, which is the whole of what the next of it is going to be made out of.

Three hundred and ninety-nine people in nine counties, who said yes to three sentences about oil on a road, and who have not been told one word since.

Ninety-six days out of a room. Eight hundred and seventy-nine days in that room before it.

One hand that works and one arm that does not. A lamp with nothing in it. A book of eight leaves against her fourth rib with twenty-two names on one of them that are still blank the other way, and two leaves left in it.

And no instrument in the world that weighs any of that, and not one word anywhere in nine hundred years that tells a person what to do at the end of a thing they did on purpose.

She was nineteen years old and it was the seventeenth day of a month and she had run out of things she knew how to do, and she stood there and let that arrive, and it did not frighten her, and she did not know what to put in its place.
