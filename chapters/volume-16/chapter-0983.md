# What Two Men Said

The eighth day was the first one she lost.

She had gone into it with a list of nine places in her head, out of two counties she had walked, and she came out of it at the light with three of them answered and six of them not, and the six of them were not gone bad. They were simply not known.

## Asking Two Men To Walk

She cannot be sent. She cannot be given anything. She cannot follow a name she is given, and she cannot be met where she is expected, and there is no office in nine counties that will carry a question to a village.

What she can do is stand in front of a person on a road and ask, and she has been doing that for a month and it is the only thing she has ever had.

So she asked, on the morning of the eighth day, at the second hour, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the flat part of a road outside a town with a weir on it, in front of about nine people who had come down to hear what she had.

She asked two of them to walk.

Not one to the east village and one to the west, to do a job she had put in their hands. Two men she had not chosen because of anything, who were standing there, and she told them both the whole of what she wanted and both of them said yes in front of the others.

She gave them the same four questions and she wrote the four questions down on a slate so that they would carry the same words, because she could not keep what she finds by holding it and had never been able to.

Then she stood in the road and watched one of them go east and one of them go west, and she worked out, standing there, in the ordinary voice, that she had just put the only two pairs of eyes she had for two counties into two counties that could not see each other, and that if one of them was lying about it she would have no way of finding out for about three days.

She said that part out loud too, standing in the road, before they were out of sight.

## Two Reports

They came back at about the sixth hour and they did not agree, and both of them were honest.

The boy who had gone east said that the ground at Ardwick had gone on the second day of the month and had gone again on the fourth, and that there were about ninety people off it and a stream running through where the smithy had been, and that they were all right and were living in the mill.

The carter who had gone west said that the ground at Ardwick had not moved at all, and that on the fourth day of the month about ninety people had walked out of Ardwick over the wall with their own carts and gone down the road, and that the smithy was still standing and the stream was where it always was.

Both of them had the same four questions and both of them had the same slate.

She stood in the road between them and worked out, standing, in the ordinary voice, that the two things cannot both be true and that she had no instrument on either side of that which would tell her which one to keep.

And then she worked out the second half of it, standing, which was the half that cost her the day.

There was a person in a mill nine counties away who either had a smithy or had not, and she was the only person alive who had sent two men to find out, and the answer she had was two answers, and she was going to have to live on top of that.

## What She Did With Two Answers

She said both of them out loud, in the road, in the ordinary voice, standing, to about nineteen people, one after the other, with the men standing there while she did it.

That was the rule and the rule is the whole of what she has ever had. A thing is said standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of the people it is about, before it is a line anywhere, and she was not going to keep the version she liked better in her own head where not one person could hear it and use it a week afterwards.

Then she said what was going to happen about it, standing, in the ordinary voice, before anybody asked her.

“The four questions were the same and the two answers were not the same and I was not able to find out which of you was telling me the truth this week. I was going to go on working on what I could reach. I was not going to choose one of you because I liked the shape of it better.

“I was not going to send anybody to check, either. It was two more days of walking for a person with my arm and I would be using it.”

The carter said, “So what do I tell the people at Ardwick.”

“The true part. That a woman asked and got two answers and does not know. They will not like it and it is the only thing you have.”

## The Book

She used a leaf in the evening.

That had not been in the plan for the eighth day and it had been in the plan for the ninth for a month, and the reason she did it on the eighth was that about a hundred and forty people had come down off the terrace at Lowwater and the far half of them had gone on to a place with a wood and a stream in it and she did not have one of their names.

It is a book of eight leaves against her fourth rib. It has been against her ribs since she was a child and she has carried it across four counties in nine days.

There was nothing else in the world to write a name in.

And a name cannot go on a leaf before it has been said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of the person it belongs to, before it is a line anywhere, and she has done that four hundred times on roads and it has never once taken her longer than the conversation.

## Twenty-Two Names

It took six hours and she got twenty-two.

That is not a figure she would have believed at the fourth hour of the previous day, and she put the arithmetic down on it standing, in the ordinary voice, in the dark, by the light of a lamp that had not carried a flame since the fourteenth of a month in a year she had stopped counting.

Twenty-two names in six hours. One every sixteen minutes, and each one of them standing, in the ordinary voice, with the person, and their right to say no said out loud before their answer, and the price said out loud before their answer, because that is what they said yes to on a road a month ago and she was not going to hand it back to them short.

A woman of about twenty-six who had come down off the terrace with a baby on her hip said no, and said it clearly, and Ning Yan wrote her name and a line under it and moved on.

That one took four minutes and it was the hardest of the twenty-two.

## The Mother Who Came To Do It For Him

The twenty-second name belonged to a boy of about fourteen and his mother came and gave it to me instead.

That is not allowed and I told her so, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the dark, by the lamp, and she told me the reason and the reason was that she had been on the road for two days and her arm had gone on the way and she was not going to be able to stand up in front of me.

I made her stand up.

It took me about a count of two hundred and it was the worst thing I did that day and I have not been able to put it anywhere. I said that a name is said out loud standing by the person it belongs to, before it is a line anywhere, and that she had come here to do a thing that could not be done for him by anybody.

Then she stood up and it took her a long time, and she said it.

And when I asked him whether he wanted it, he said no.

Not because he did not want it. Because he had not been asked, and he was about fourteen, and he had been told by his mother on a road that there was a thing he had said yes to, and he wanted to know what it was before anybody wrote it down anywhere.

So there is a line under his name and there is a word against the word, and the word was no, and I have written both.

At about the eleventh hour she put the book back against her fourth rib with twenty-two names on a leaf that had been blank since she was a child, and there were two leaves left in it that were still blank, and she did not write on them, because there was not time and because a blank one is worth more than a page.

## Somebody Asking Her For A Direction

A boy of about thirteen came up out of the dark at about the twelfth hour and said that his mother was in a place called Torrenden and he could not find it, and he wanted to know which way.

She stood in the road with a woman of about thirty beside her who had been crying about something else entirely.

She could not find it either.

That is the whole of what happened to her on the eighth day and it is worth saying plainly because it took the whole day to arrive at and she arrived at it in front of a boy of about thirteen.

“I did not know where Torrenden was. I walked about two counties in the last three days and every road in them was somewhere it was not a week ago, so the place your mother was in was either where it was or it was somewhere I had not been, and I had got no list and I could not give anything and I could not follow a name I was handed.”

The boy said, “Then what have you got.”

“Two things,” Ning Yan said, “and they were the only two and I was telling you both of them standing up.”

She held up one hand with three fingers and a thumb on it.

“One. If you go back the way you came and keep going past where you started, you will hit a road with a made surface and a weir on it, and there are people standing at it at this hour.

“I would be on it until the light. I was there every night and I was not able to be anywhere else.”

“And the other.”

“The other was that I was going to be there in the morning as well,” Ning Yan said, “and you had got a night to decide whether you wanted to ask me again in the morning, and I was not going to tell you not to.”

The boy of about thirteen thought about that for a while.

“That was not much,” he said.

“It was what I had got,” she said. “I had not got any more than that and I was not going to invent some.”
