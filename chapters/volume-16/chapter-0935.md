# What She Would Not Do

He came into the room at the sixth hour on the tenth of the month beginning three thousand and forty-four and asked her for a shape.

Not for the apology. For a shape. A beginning. Four words that were not about her and not about him and not about a figure, so that he had something to put his hand on the way you put your hand on a table in a dark room.

She said no.

He asked again at the seventh hour and she said no again, and he asked a third time at about the seventh hour and a hundred, from further away than the second one had come, and she said no a third time.

## Three Things He Asked For

“Do you know what you have just done,” he said.

“Yes.”

“You have refused a man who asked you for four words four times in a row and you have not given him a reason once, and you have said more out loud standing to a keeping every morning for nineteen years than anybody has ever said to anything, and you have not given me four words.”

“I have given you a reason.”

“You have given me a word. That is your rule and you have never once broken it, and you are using it now to keep yourself out of your own apology.”

“I am.”

“You are standing at the near end of your own table in a room nobody can come into and you are using a rule you found to make sure a thing stays mine.” His voice was not louder. She understood that she had never heard him louder in nine years and was not going to. “I have been in that rule since before it was a rule. I put it where it is. Do not use it to shut a door on me.”

“I am not shutting a door on you.”

“You have shut three.”

She stood at the near end of the table with the lamp on the stone at her feet and her right arm hanging open against her side for seven hundred and fifty-nine days and let him have that, because it was true and because it was not the whole of it.

## Three Reasons

“I am not going to help you,” she said. “That is the whole of what I am doing and I am telling you the whole of it so you can hear it from me instead of working it out.”

“Say why.”

“If I tell you the shape of it, it comes out of my mouth.” She said it standing, in the ordinary voice, into the room, and it went out the way everything she said in that room went out. “That is the rule. You are the person it is about. I am not.”

“That is not the rule. The rule is that a thing is said in the voice of the person it is about before it is a line anywhere. It does not stop a person being given the beginning of a thing by somebody else. There are six hundred years of people in this keeping who were told what to say and every one of them said it in their own voice and it was theirs.”

“Then say that of this one.”

Nothing came for about a count of forty.

“I cannot.”

“Why.”

“Because it is not true of this one,” he said. “I have been in your head since you were ten years old and I have said a great many things into it and if I give you four words now there is no way in the world to prove they are yours.”

She stood there with the board in her hand and did not put it down.

The boards face up a foot in from the near end, the terms board a foot further along, the strip of pale wood face up under the table at the near end of the leg where it had lain since the fourth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four.

Nine hundred and four steps to a table with no wear in it. The bar nine feet nine against the wall, dated nowhere. Every one of those was put where it was by somebody who had decided something on her behalf and had not asked her.

The whole of her life was that. And if she opened her mouth at the seventh hour of the tenth of a month and gave a man four words for the apology, then the first decision in the whole of it that she would ever have put a hand on would be the first decision in it that had ever been anybody else’s.

## The Part That Was Not About Him

“There is one thing I am not able to do,” she said, “and I am going to say it and then I am going to stop.”

“Say it.”

“You are not going to get a sentence out of me in this room that is about what you did.” She put her hands flat on the wood. “Not one. If I answer you then this becomes a thing we are having in a room.

And I know what that is, because we had it for a month and it was the best month of my life and it was two people at a table with the whole of a bargain between them, and you did not say one word about the tax until the seventh of that month, and when you did it went out of my mouth in front of the thing that made you.

“I am not going to stand in this room and be easy for you while you say it.”

He was quiet for a long time, and the quiet had a shape in it, and the shape was a man working out that the one person who could help him was refusing for a reason that was about him and not about the apology at all.

“All right,” he said.

That was the whole of it. There was no anger left in it and there was nothing else.

## What It Cost Her To Do It

She went and stood at the near board with her hand flat on it for a count of two hundred, and she worked out what she had done, because she was a woman who worked things out and because there was nothing else in the day.

A man who had wanted four words had asked her for them four times and had gone away with nothing, and that was correct, and she had done it on purpose, and it had taken her about eleven words and one of them was a rule she had found herself.

Nine years. She had had a voice in her head since she was ten years old and it had asked her for a great many things in all that time, and she had found out the morning she asked him what he wanted that the asking had been the easy half of it, because the hard half was the part where a person says no to the thing they want to hand over.

She had handed things over her whole life. Her arm. Her days. A sheet of her own hand nine hundred and four steps down a stair. Nineteen years of saying what she wanted out loud in a room to something that kept the walls up.

And this morning at the seventh hour she had wanted to open her mouth and give a man four words so badly that she had felt the wanting in her hand on the wood.

That was the whole of what refusing him had cost and she had put it there on her own, standing, in the ordinary voice, and it was not in the keeping because the keeping had nothing to do with it.

## The One Time In The Day

She did the whole day after that without a sentence in it.

She did the near board and the terms board. She stood at the foot of the stair and looked up into it and came back. She went the length of the table with her hand along the wood and past the bundle under the far end where it was corded twice and not knotted.

She sat on the stone at the near end with her back against the leg of it and put her hand up under her own ribs on the left side, high, behind the fourth one, and found the small open place that was there the size of her thumbnail, and it did not feel like anything, and she put her hand down again.

At the ninth hour she stood up straight and said the day’s figures out loud into the room in the ordinary voice, in the order she had been saying them in since she was ten years old, and she said all of them.

He let them go past her.

She stood there with her mouth shut and understood that he had done that on purpose, because there had not been a question in the figures and there had not been anything in them that needed an answer, and he had been sitting in a wall all day waiting for the ninth hour because it was the one time in the day she came out in her own voice.

He let them past her.

“Nine years,” she said. “That is every morning of it and you have answered it every single one.”

“I have answered it because you put it in the room and a figure in a room is the only thing in this building that ever got an answer out of anybody.”

“And today you did not.”

“No.”

She stood at the near end of her own table with the lamp on the stone and did not fill anything after it. She had refused him four times and told him why twice and told him the truth about the month a third time, and at the ninth hour the only voice in her own head had let a woman count out loud into an empty room and had not said a word back.

He was not doing it to hurt her. She worked that out standing there and it took her a while, and what it was instead was that he had run out of things to say to her that were not the apology, and there were two days of it left, and he was going to have to go into it with nothing she had handed him.
