# What It Is For

Kan Shou came up the road from the flat at about the seventh hour with her stall shuttered and her apron off, which Ning Yan had not seen her do once since the twentieth of the month beginning three thousand and seventy-four, and she came to the gap in the wall and stood in front of it rather than beside it.

“Do not say anything yet,” she said. “I have been asked four times this week by people who are not you and I have said the three sentences about oil and have not said anything else, and I have not slept twice.”

“All right.”

“I am going to ask you one thing and you are going to answer it in the ordinary voice with those people standing there, because if you answer it to me privately I will not believe it and I will not be able to hold it.”

“All right.”

## The Question She Came Up With

“What is it for.”

She said it the way she said everything, which was exactly the way Kan Shou said things, and the road had about nine people in it within hearing and two of them stopped.

The price of it is on the record. I do not know what it costs me. No one has sent you, and nothing is done to you, and I am the only one who binds you.

That is what was on it. That was what four hundred people had said yes to, and Kan Shou had come up the road to ask her what it was for, and she was right to ask, and there had been nothing in any of it.

She said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at a gap in a wall, on the seventh of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four, with the people within hearing.

“It is for a sentence. I am going to say a sentence. It is nine words long and it is mine and I have not got the words yet, and when I have got them I am going to say it standing in the ordinary voice, and what it is for is taking the ceiling off.”

She let that sit for about a count of a hundred.

“I am not telling you it will work. I am telling you what it is for.”

## What Was Not Told

“Right,” Kan Shou said. “Then tell me the rest of it, because you have just told me what it is for and you have not told me what it does.”

“It is on the record.”

“The price of what is on the record. You have said that sentence to me and to about three hundred and ninety-nine other people, and every one of us heard the same three words and thought they meant something about us.”

That was the piece Ning Yan had been carrying since the nineteenth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and four and had not put anywhere.

“It does not,” she said. “I have told you what it costs me and I have not told you what it costs you, because I do not know, and because I did not have it when you said yes and I have not gone and found out since.”

“You have had a month.”

“I have had twenty-nine days of asking,” Ning Yan said, “and eighteen days of four hundred being done and not one word of it anywhere, and you have just told me what is wrong with that and I had not worked it out.”

“You had not,” Kan Shou said, “and you have been standing in the middle of this road saying sentences to strangers out loud every hour of all of it. What did you do with them.”

“I have been looking for a copy of it,” Ning Yan said, “and there is not one.”

## What A Person Asks

A carter’s boy of about seventeen had been standing at the far end of the road since before either of them started, and he came forward about six paces and said, “Does it come off the ceiling for us too.”

“I do not know.”

“Does it come off for us.”

“I do not know that either.”

“Then how do you say yes to it.”

“You already did,” Ning Yan said. “On a road. Standing up. And you can say no now and it will not come off the record and I have to say that out loud before you answer to anything, which is the rule and which is the whole of what I have got to give you.”

The boy looked at her for a while.

“We do not get a vote,” he said.

“No.”

“We do not get told.”

“No.”

“Then what do we get.”

“You get told before anything, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and you get it from me and not out of a keeping, and you got the same thing from me at the start and you are getting it now. That is the whole of what there is.”

The boy stood there with that for a while and then said, “Then I am in it anyway.”

“Yes.”

“Then that is fine,” he said. “I did not come up here to be asked twice. I came up here to know what it was for so that I could decide whether I minded.”

And he went off down the road, and he had not been bound at any point in that conversation, and there were four hundred people in her arithmetic and he was not one of them, and Ning Yan stood in the road and let that arrive.

That is the second time this morning that somebody had asked her a fair question and been told a true thing and had gone on without being in anything.

## The Nine She Can Reach

She worked out what she had while she was standing there answering the boy, and it took about a count of two hundred and it was the same working she had done on the sixteenth of the month and it had come out the same.

Four hundred people in the arithmetic.

About nine of them within calling of a road in one county in the whole of the world, and the other three hundred and ninety-one are out there somewhere in nine counties and she has got no way of finding a single one of them.

She cannot be met where she is expected. She cannot follow a name she is given. She cannot be given anything. She is not sent anywhere and nothing is done to her and there is no office and no list anybody has given her and no message.

The word had gone to the next valley on the fifth of the month and had stopped there, and she had put four hundred people into a keeping nine hundred years old by talking to them on a road one at a time, and there was not one single way in the world she had ever had of getting to two hundred of them again.

“Listen to what I am not saying,” she said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, to the road. “I am not saying it is hopeless. I am saying that whatever is going to happen to those three hundred and ninety-one people is going to happen to them without me in it, and the only thing I have ever had is my mouth in front of a person, and there are nine of them here.”

## Where The Two Of Them Meet

She said the rest of it out loud after Kan Shou had gone, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the gap in the wall, and it took her about a count of six hundred and it was the longest thing she had said since she came off the mountain.

“I cannot work out what it costs the four hundred of them. I have tried every day since the nineteenth of the month and I have got nothing, and it is not because there is no working. It is because there are two things in it and I cannot do the second one, and the second one is what it takes out of four hundred people when the sentence is said, and I have not got the sentence.”

She put her hand flat on the wall.

“So I have told four hundred people the price is on the record, and I have told them I do not know what it costs me, and I have never once said the third thing, which is that I do not know what it costs them either, and I have been telling them the second one in a voice that sounded like the third one.”

She stood there with that for a while.

“Twenty days on a road and I have found out that the sentence I cannot find is the smaller of the two things I have been working on.”

## The Question She Owes

Guo Sheng had told her on the last day of the month that she had not asked one of them a single question about what they wanted done to them, and she had told him it was not true, and it had been true.

So she asked.

She stood at the gap in the low wall at about the ninth hour on the seventh of the month and asked everybody on that road what they wanted done to them before the walls came off, standing, in the ordinary voice, out loud, one at a time, and she wrote nothing down because she could not be given anything and she could not keep what she found by holding it.

Nine people answered and not one of the answers was the same.

A man who wanted it to be quick. A woman who wanted to be told first and never told at all. A boy who wanted it not to be on his village. A woman of about sixty who said she wanted to know afterwards whether her son, who is in a sect somewhere she cannot name, is still standing at nine.

A carter who said he wanted it to happen on a market day so that there would be people about.

The last one was Kan Shou, who had come back up the road about half an hour after she left, and who said, “I want it to happen where I can see it from my stall.”

Ning Yan stood at the gap in a low wall and had nine answers and could not use one of them, and had told four hundred people the price was on the record.
