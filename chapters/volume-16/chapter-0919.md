# Two Days of It

She had one question in her mouth for six days and on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and eighty-four she got it out.

It was about the seventh hour, at the near end of her own table, with the lamp on the stone and her hand in the hollow the wood had worn under her own forearms.

“What is the work that matters,” she said.

He was quiet.

Not quiet the way he goes quiet when he is deciding. Quiet the way a room is quiet.

## Nothing Back

She had gone at him with the arithmetic for nine years and the arithmetic had never once failed her, and on the twenty-eighth of a month she used all of it in a row and got nothing.

She asked it at the fourth hour and she asked it at the sixth and she asked it standing with her hands on the table and she asked it sitting on the stone with her back against the leg of it.

She put it to him the way she puts a figure to a day, from the top, working down: is it a thing that is done, is it a thing that is kept, is it a thing that is carried, is it a thing that is taken. Four framings in about a count of three hundred, and four answers, and three of them were I do not know and the fourth was yes.

She waited for a count of a hundred.

“What is the work that matters,” she said again.

“I heard you.”

“You did not answer me.”

“No.”

She went at it the way she went at everything, which was with the arithmetic.

“Is it something with hands in it.”

“I have no hands.”

“Is it something that goes out into the bound.”

“I do not know.”

“Does it come up nine hundred and four steps.”

“I do not know.”

“Does anybody see it happen.”

“I do not know.”

“Is it on the board that was face up under the table when I was ten years old.”

“No,” he said. “It is not on that board and I would have felt it if it were.”

“Does it cost anybody.”

He was quiet for a count of forty on that one.

“Yes,” he said. “Everything that has ever cost anybody in this keeping cost somebody in another place and the other place is usually a person who has not been met.”

She stood there with her hand flat on the wood and worked it out standing, the way she worked everything out, and the working took her a count of forty and she got it in one piece.

“You have had four words on that register for thirty-five days,” she said. “Archived. Addressed. Remembered. A name that is not the one you died under. Work that matters. You read them to my face on the twenty-third and you said you could not guess at it in front of me, because a man who guesses at the one thing he has been offered for six hundred years says the guess out loud and believes it by morning.”

“Yes.”

“It is not the twenty-eighth.”

“No,” he said. “And I still do not want to say it to you out of arithmetic.”

“Why.”

He was quiet for a count of a hundred and a hundred again.

“Because I can,” he said.

She stood there with the lamp burning at her feet and looked at the boards lying face up a foot in from the near end, and there was nothing on any of them to write the question down on.

“There is no board in this room.”

“No.”

“There has not been a board in this room since I was ten years old.”

“No,” he said, “and you have not wanted one in six years, and I have not asked you for one in six years, and I am not going to start this morning by asking you to put a piece of your arithmetic down on paper where it would have to be looked at.”

She put her hand flat on the table where the wear was.

“I am not going to look at anything,” she said. “I am going to stand at the near end of this table and ask you until one of us gives.”

## The Two Days He Asked For

She put her thumb down hard on the wood.

“Say the rest of it.”

She did not wait for him. She went and stood at the boards with the near one face up a foot in from the near end and she put her hand flat on it and did not lift it, and she stayed there a count of two hundred, and the lamp stood on the stone throwing its light along the floor and not up the wood.

Then she came back to the near end of the table.

“Work that matters is the only phrase in the whole arrangement that has never once been defined for anybody,” he said. “It is a promise of a kind of work and not of a task. And there is a kind of work that can be run by something that has never touched the world and never will, and a kind that cannot, and I have not looked at which one this is in thirty-five days, and I am going to look at it.”

“Then look at it today.”

“I am going to look at it on the thirtieth.”

“Why the thirtieth.”

He was quiet again.

“Because on the twenty-second you asked me what happens now and I told you I said yes because I was tired,” he said. “And a tired man has about two days of it in him. After that he is not tired. After that he is something else, and I do not know what, and I have known since the seventh that I do not know, and I have been using the not knowing to get through the days.

“I want two more days of carrying it rather than putting it down, and I want them to be the twenty-eighth and the twenty-ninth, and on the thirtieth I will tell you what the work is whatever it turns out to be, and if I have to do that standing in front of a thing that made me then I would rather not have done it tired.”

She stood very still.

“You want two days.”

“I want two days.”

“You have been asking me for things since the fourth and every one of them has been for me,” she said. “Four minutes of silence. The right to speak. The right not to be named. And this is the first one you have asked for that is for you, and you have asked me for it in the fourth hour after I have spent six days waiting for a definition.”

“Yes.”

“I am not going to give you two days.”

She took a breath of four and let it out.

“I am going to ask you at the fourth hour and at the sixth hour and at the ninth hour and at whatever hour it is when the day turns over, and I am going to keep on doing it,” she said, “and there is not one rule in this room that says a woman cannot ask a man the same question four times.

“That is not an argument. I am telling you what I am going to do so that you can decide what you are going to do about it.”

He did not answer that at all.

## The Rule, Used On Him

She stood at the near end of the table and put both hands flat on the wood, three fingers and a thumb on each, and the right arm hanging open against her side for seven hundred and seventeen days, and she stood because standing was for saying.

“On the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and eighty-four,” she said, “at about the seventh hour, in this room, in the ordinary voice, I asked the man inside my head what the work that matters is, and he did not answer.”

The place behind her fourth rib pulled and she let it pull.

“A thing is said in the voice of the person it is about, standing, before it is a line anywhere.”

He was quiet.

“I said that on the fourteenth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four, at the fourth hour, at a table with no wear in it, and I found it by running my fingers past the fifth mercy and I have carried it for thirty-five days,” she said.

“And I have not once used it. Not on a board, not on a figure, not on a person. And I am using it on you now, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the channel, before it is a line anywhere, and you cannot get it back and neither can I.”

She put her hand flat on the table where the wear was.

“That is the first time anybody has done that,” he said.

“Yes.”

“It is in the record now.”

“It is in the record now in my voice about you, and not one person alive can come and take it out, and that includes you, and it includes me.”

He was quiet for a long time, long enough that the light on the edge of the table had moved a hand’s width along the wood.

“You have just made it impossible for me to go on not answering,” he said. “I want you to know that I know that is what you have done and I am not going to pretend I did not see it.”

“I did it on purpose.”

“I know you did it on purpose,” he said. “You have never once done a thing to me off purpose in nineteen years and I would have found it.”

“Will you tell me on the thirtieth if I do not ask you again.”

“Yes.”

“Then I am not going to ask you again.”

“That is the correct decision and you have made it in about a count of ten, which is faster than you made any of the others.”

She put her hand flat on the table where the wear was, and the wear took it the way it always did.

“I have taken two days off you.”

“No,” he said. “You have not taken anything off me. You have made a day out of two days of me going quiet, and a day is a smaller thing than two days of nothing happening, and that is all you did, and you did it standing, and you did it before it was a line anywhere, and I am not going to stand here and be a man who complains to the only person alive who has ever used that rule on anybody.”

She stood in her own room with the lamp burning and the second place at the far side kept four hundred years and empty.

The not-answering had been his. It was in the channel in her voice now, said standing, said first, said before it was anything anywhere, and it could not be called back, and in two days he was going to tell her what the work that matters was, and she had spent the only rule this room had to make him do it.
