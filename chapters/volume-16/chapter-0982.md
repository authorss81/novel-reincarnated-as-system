# The Road That Bent

The road out of Lowwater goes east along the water and turns north at the second ford and goes on for about as long as it takes a cart to get warm, and Ning Yan had walked it on the morning of the sixth day on her way in with forty people behind her.

It did not turn north at the second ford on the seventh day.

It went straight on, as far as a long cart past the place where it turned, and then it turned north further on, and then it went back west in a loop and came out on the far side of a house that had been on the north bank for as long as the house had been there.

She stood in the middle of the road at about the ninth hour with the light coming white off the water and she looked at it for about a count of six hundred.

## A Bend That Was Not There

There was no bend. There was one road going where one road had always gone and the turn had simply not happened, and where it had not happened there was a piece of country that had not been on any road, with a man’s furrow across it about four days old.

She was the only person she knew of who could say that it had not been there. And she knew it because she had walked it on the way in on the sixth day, with about forty people behind her and her own feet on it.

So she had two things, and they did not fit.

She had a road she had walked the day before. She had the road in front of her. And the whole of her training is that when two of those disagree, the one in front of her is the one that is true, and she had learned that on a wet mountain when she was nine years old and it had never once been wrong.

She got her own pace out and she put it down, because it is the only instrument she has ever had, and it agreed with itself, perfectly, the way it always does, and it did not have one single thing to say about the difference.

## Two Counties Of It

She walked it.

That is what she did for the whole of the seventh day and part of the morning of the eighth, and it is the longest single piece of work she has done in nineteen years that had no figures in it at all.

She walked about two counties. The road bent where it should not. A stream that had come in under a bridge came in over the bridge and went off east where it had gone south. A field boundary that had run down the slope from east to west ran down it from north to south, and the two of them had been put there by people who were dead, and the boundary was right and the map in her head was wrong.

And it was not one valley and not one district.

That was the part she stopped being able to carry at about the fourth hour of the afternoon.

She had braced for a district. She had a word for a district and she had a figure for how far she could get in a day in the arm she has, and she had worked out at the third hour that if it was a district then she could have it finished inside four days and could stand somewhere in the middle of it afterwards and say she had done it.

It was not a district.

The ground had gone from under about two counties, everywhere, not in a piece, and there was no edge to it that she could stand at and look back along. A woman at a door in the second village told her that the man across the road had been on the other side of the wall nine days ago and had walked through on the fourth day of the month, and that he had brought a chair.

He had brought a chair.

## The Woman With A Paper

She was in a street in a town with a low weir on it at about the seventh hour of the seventh day with a folded paper in her hand and about a week of not sleeping in her face.

“My son went into a sect at fifteen,” she said. “It is a county I cannot name and it is not one of yours. He was at nine in his twentieth year and they wrote and said so, and that was two years ago, and that was the last of it.”

“I was sorry.”

“A man came through here on the fifth day of this month and said they had put every one of their people out on the road and had not given any of them anything to eat and had not kept a list. That is what he said and I have asked him four times what he meant and he will not say more than that.”

She put the paper flat on the weir and opened it and it was a letter with a seal on it and about four lines of writing in it.

“That came on the first. That is the last I have had from them and it says he is well and it says he is to be patient.”

She looked up.

“He was at nine two years ago. He is not at nine now. I know he is not, because my sister’s boy is at nine and he came through here on the sixth day looking for work and he could not look at me for the whole of the conversation.”

She was not asking Ning Yan anything. She had put the paper on the weir because the weir was flat and because she had been holding it for a week.

“I was not able to tell you what he was,” Ning Yan said.

“No.”

“I have been in four counties in seven days and I have not been in that one and I could not get there this month and I am saying that to you because you have been standing here with a piece of paper for a week and somebody has to say the true thing out loud.”

“No,” the woman said. “Somebody had to.”

## What She Was Going To Put In The Record

She stood at the weir for about a count of four hundred after the woman had gone and worked out, standing, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people who were standing about, what she was going to say and whether she could derive any of it.

She could not.

Not one part. She had two counties of ground that had moved and she had eighty-six days out of a room and a road that had lost a bend, and she had no instrument on any of the three that would put one against another, and there is no column in nine hundred years of anything that takes a county.

So she said the true thing, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the weir, out loud, which is a thing she has done about four times in nineteen years.

“I came in this county eight days ago. I came out of it on the sixth and this is the seventh and it is not the county I came out of. Every road in it is somewhere it was not. I have walked two counties of it and there is not one edge to it anywhere that I could stand at.

“And I do not have one word for it. I have said a sentence on the thirtieth of a month at a gap in a wall and I am the only person alive who knows I said it, and I have no way to show you that it was me and I would not want to prove it if I could.

“You were going to have to do the rest of it yourselves and I was going to be standing in the middle of wherever you were. I was not able to do anything else. I did not have an office and I did not have a list and I was not able to be given anything or to be sent or to follow a name I was handed.”

A man of about thirty at the back said, “That is the first thing anybody has said to me in seven days that was not somebody telling me it would be all right.”

“It was not all right,” Ning Yan said. “It was knowable. Those were two different things and I had only got one of them.”

## Not One Word For It

She went back along the weir at the ninth hour and said her figures out loud to the water, standing, in the ordinary voice.

Eighty-six days out of a room. Eight hundred and sixty-nine days in that room before it.

Then she reached down the side of the bed for the third one out of habit, the way a hand goes down for a cup that has been there every morning of a life, and there was nothing in her hand.

She had not been in the habit for two days. She was going to have to give that up as well.

And then she sat down on the low weir above a town where a woman of about forty was going to spend the rest of the night not knowing what her son was, and she worked out the only thing she had left that was any use to anybody on this earth.

Nine hundred years. There was a figure for that, and it sat in her the way the old six figures had sat, and it was the only one of the whole of her arithmetic that was still standing.

Nine hundred years of a ceiling at nine in every realm there was, and about four hundred people in nine counties who had said yes to three sentences about oil, and a mark on a terrace at the top of a town that said nothing on it anywhere.

She could not weigh a county and she could not weigh nine words and she could not weigh a woman with a piece of paper in her hand.

What she had left, she worked out at about the ninth hour on a weir with the water going under it, was the number of days. Days, and weather, and bodies, and hands. That was all of the instrument there was, and she had been carrying it about since she was a child, and there was not one other person in two counties who was carrying any.
