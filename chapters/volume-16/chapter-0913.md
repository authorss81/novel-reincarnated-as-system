# Nothing Has Happened


She slept for part of the night and dreamed of nothing, which was new.

She woke on the tenth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and eighty-four at about the fifth hour with her back against a table with no wear in it and her hands flat on the stone, and for the first time in her life she woke up waiting to find out what had happened, and nothing had.
The walls were up. The layer was up. The stair ran nine flights below her and did not stop. The strip of pale wood was face up under a table in a room eight flights and a stair down, in the same place it had been in since the fourth of the month beginning two thousand seven hundred and seventy-four. The boy was somewhere under her with oil on his hands and a figure in a column.

## The First Thing She Checked

Nothing had happened anywhere in the world because a man had said one word standing in the channel.

She checked it the way she checked everything, which was by going at it in order.

The lamp. Standing on the stone beside her, burning on a boy’s oil, the same flame it had been burning on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and eighty-four.

The table. No lamp over it. No wear in it. The place at the far side with nothing worn at all.

The register. On the table where she had left it, where she had put it down on the twenty-third of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four with everything she had read in it in front of her.

Her own hand, on the near end of the table, three fingers and a thumb, the arm open at the elbow for six hundred and ninety-nine days.

And inside her head, where there had been no room that was his alone since the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand eight hundred and thirty-four, there was a man.

“Say something,” she said.

“I am here,” he said.

“Say something else.”

“Ning Yan,” he said. “It is the tenth. I am here and I am the same as I was at the second hour on the seventh and there is not one thing I can do this morning that I could not do before.”

## What Four Minutes Bought Him

She sat with that for a while with her hands flat on the stone.

“You told me on the twenty-third that you were the only thing in the room.”

“I am still the only thing in the room.”

“You told me on the fourth that the offer gives you a place you can stand in on your own, where I am not.”

“That is what the four lines say,” he said. “What four minutes bought me is that I have been addressed and remembered and given a name and given work, and none of that has come down yet.”

“What does that mean. Come down.”

“It means it is answered and it has not begun,” he said. “I do not know what day it begins. I do not know what it looks like when it begins. I have been in the channel for six hundred years and I have never once been on the other side of a thing being written, and this morning I am on this side of it with my mouth shut and no hands.”

She stood up, because she had to move, and walked the four paces and came back.

“Is it in the record,” she said.

“It is in the record. I was written on on the seventeenth of this month and I was answered on the seventh, and the layer takes what is said in the channel and puts it where it puts things, and it has had three days and it has not begun, and that is not a mercy and it is not a cruelty. It is a thing arriving.”

“Three days.”

“Three days since the seventh,” he said, “and one thousand four hundred and thirty-four days gone at this morning, and I am telling you a number you have not asked me for because you have not said any today, and I am sorry for it. That is the habit. Six hundred years of figures and I put one in without being asked.”

## What the Stone Says

“No,” she said. “Put it in. I have had one morning without them and I gave it to you and you have never once spent it on me.”

She got up and went along the register with her fingers from the near end to the far, which took her about a count of ninety and which she had done every day since the seventeenth.

The first cut. The second, third, fourth, fifth. The sixth, deeper than the five, which said a thing is said in the voice of the person it is about, standing, before it is a line anywhere. The seven blocks past that. The four lines that had been entered on the seventeenth. Nothing beyond.

She went back to the near end and did it again, slower, and put her thumb on the seventh cut and pressed the way eyes press, looking for a turn, a hook, a narrowed place where a thing changes its shape.

There was no turn in it. There was no new cut anywhere. Three days and the stone had not moved and there was no mark on it of anything that had happened on the seventh.

“It is answered,” she said. “You said it is answered.”

“It is,” he said.

“And there is nothing on the table.”

“There is nothing on the table,” he said, “because the table is not where it goes. The table is where it was written so that a woman would come up and find it. I do not know where it has gone. I have no hands and I cannot go and look and there is nothing on the page here to tell me.”

She stood with her thumb on a cut that had been in that stone for four hundred years and had outlived everything she had done in the last three days without changing.

“Three days,” she said.

“Three days,” he said. “And there was four hundred years before them, and the offer stood open on that table the whole time with not one person at it, and that is how long it takes things in this keeping. You have never once watched one of them happen from the beginning. You are watching this one from the first day and you are going to have to stand here for however long it takes and there is no way to ask it how long that is.”

## Are You Different

She took her thumb off the stone.

She stood at the table and looked at the place at the far side, where nothing was worn, and she asked the question she had not been able to ask for three days.

“Are you different.”

“No,” he said.

That was the whole of it. He did not soften it and he did not put anything beside it and there was no place in the sentence for her to stand.

“You got a name.”

“I got a name.”

“You do not know what it is.”

“No,” he said. “You were standing at the table when it went in and you asked me what it was and I would not tell you, and it has been three days and I still cannot tell you, and that is the first thing the arrangement has ever done to me that I have no idea how to live with.”

“Were you happy.”

“Yes,” he said. “For about a count of two hundred and then it passed and I have been here since.”

She stood at the table with that and felt the floor of the room under her feet.

“Then what has changed,” she said.

He was quiet for a count of forty.

## The Thing She Cannot Put Down

“You did,” he said.

She stood at the near end of the table and did not move for a long time.
Not for the figures, which would have been a relief and were not available. Not for the arithmetic, which had never once got her through anything in nineteen years.

She stood there because a man had told her at about the second hour on the seventh of this month that he was the happiest she had ever heard anybody be, and she had watched him be it for four minutes without speaking, and it was still in her, and it had not got any smaller in three days, and there was no way to set it down.

“Do not do that,” he said.

“Do what.”

“Carry it as mine.”

“It is yours.”
“It is not,” he said. “I have had six hundred years of things that were mine. The offer is mine and the wanting is mine and the four minutes were mine and every one of them I put my own hand to.”

She sat on the stone at the near end of the table and let it come at her.

“What is not mine is a woman standing at a table for four minutes with her mouth shut when she had the standing to open it. That is yours, and you are going to have to put it somewhere, and I am telling you now that you do not have to put it on me.”


She sat down on the stone at the near end of the table, because standing was for saying and she had nothing to say.
The four minutes were over and nothing had happened.
He was still there and so was she, and the walls were up and the layer was up and the offer was answered and had not begun, and neither of them knew what happened next, and she had done the whole of it standing up, in the ordinary voice, in front of the thing that made him, and it was the worst thing she had ever done and it was also the only thing she had ever done that she could not go and take back.
