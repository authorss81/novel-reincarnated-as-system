# A Figure, Not An Answer

She asked it at about the sixth hour on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning three thousand and one hundred and four, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the middle of the road at the crossing, with eleven people inside hearing.

## The Question

She said it plainly and she said it slowly and she gave it three parts, because she had learned on the twenty-third of a month on a road that a thing said once to a stranger goes out of her mouth before she has decided to send it.

“What is a refusal called in you. How does one of them get put down. What shape has one got.”

There was a woman with a yoke on the road who had stopped and was looking at her, and about eight other people who had come off a hill for no reason anybody could give, and a carter who had waited since the light.

She said the three parts again, a little slower, so that not one person in that road could say afterwards that she had put it badly or left a part of it out on the first time.

She then stood there from about the sixth hour until about the eighth, in the ordinary voice, with her hands down at her sides, and she waited.

## Nothing

Nothing came.

She had spent the whole of the twenty-fifth waiting for the other end of it and she had got nothing, and she had told herself that the reason was that she had not asked properly, and on the twenty-eighth she asked properly and she got nothing again.

It does not answer. It has never once answered her a question in nineteen years, not in a building and not in the open, and the reason had never once been that she had not said it in her own voice standing up first.

She found that out on the twenty-eighth and it took her about a count of five hundred to find it, and she found it standing at the crossing with the mud going cold under her boots.

She went back through nineteen years of it standing up, which she can do at about the speed of a person reading, and she put the ones in order.

She had asked it things in a room eight hundred and thirty days and got figures at the ninth hour every morning, and she had put her questions into the open air on a road for twenty-nine days and got four plain sentences up under her feet at the fifth hour of the fourth, and neither of those is an answer to a question, because a figure comes back when you put a thing in and the thing going in has already happened.

That was the whole of the mechanism and it had been in front of her the whole time.

## The Figure

Then she stopped asking and started doing what she had done every morning since she was ten years old, which was to derive.

A refusal is a thing a person does to a record.

In nine hundred years of the keeping, not one person had ever refused anything the layer had its hand on. She could derive that figure standing up and she did, and it took her about a count of forty, and it came out at nothing at all.

Zero.

Not an unknown figure. A zero, with the working behind it, derived on a wet road in the ordinary way she derives everything, and she said it out loud standing, in the ordinary voice, because she had told four hundred people she does not say a number she cannot derive and she was not going to start.

“Zero refusals in nine hundred years. That is a figure and it is a true one, and it is not the answer to what I asked, because a figure tells you how many of a thing there have been and not what one of them is made of.”

## What She Could Not Use

And then she put her own nineteen years up against it and got the thing that made her put her hand flat on the wall.

She had read that keeping every morning since she was seven years old.

Not once in nineteen years had she seen herself in it.

Not her name, and not her count, and not one refusal, and not one binding, and not one thing she had ever said out loud standing in the ordinary voice in a room for eight hundred and thirty days. Four hundred persons had gone into it at the fifth hour of the fourth of the month and it had put four plain sentences up under her feet, and she had been in it nineteen years and she had never once been in a line of it.

So the two refusals she had made in her life were not in there either, and she had made them, and she could not read either one back, and the only sample of a refusal in the whole of nine hundred years was in her own head where nothing could check it.

## The Two

She went through them standing, because she does not trust a thing she has not been through.

The first was a place kept for somebody who was never going to sit in it, and she had said no to it twice and been right twice and it had been good for her both times.

The second was a thing she had been asked for by somebody who needed her, and she had said no to it standing, in the ordinary voice, and she had been right about it, and it had not been a good thing at any point in the four days she spent deciding it.

Two. In nineteen years. She could say it in her own voice in the ordinary way, and she derived it standing up, and it was a figure and it was the only figure in the world that meant anything on the question she had asked.

She stood in the road and let that finish arriving.

“I am the only one. That is the whole of the finding and it is worse than the zero, because I have got two of them and I cannot see the shape of either one, and there is nothing on this road or in this county or on that mountain that is going to show me.”

## The Carter

The carter who had been waiting since the light said, “Are you asking it about the walls.”

She looked at him.

“Everybody on that hill has been saying something is going to happen and you have been stood in the middle of a road asking a thing out loud about what it is like,” he said. “You are asking it about the walls.”

“Yes,” Ning Yan said.

He nodded for a while.

“Then you will not get an answer to that in the ordinary way,” he said, “because a thing that has kept a place gentle for nine hundred years is not going to hand you the wording.”

## The Whole Of The Twenty-Eighth

She asked the whole of the day.

She went down the flat to about the ninth hour in the rain and she asked about forty people between the crossing and the edge of the next valley, standing, in the ordinary voice, out loud, and she put every single one of them into the keeping whether or not they answered, because the rule does not have an exception for a question.

Eleven of them said no out loud. Four of them were wrong about what she was asking and answered it anyway. One of them was a boy of about eleven who said, “I asked my mother and she said not to tell you,” and then told her.

Not one of them had ever heard of a refusal.

Two of them had heard of the walls and one of those was a man of about fifty who said the word “bound” in a way that made her stop and ask him about it for about a count of a hundred, and what he turned out to mean was that he owed money, and that was the whole of it, and there are two different words and they have nothing to do with each other and one of them is nine hundred years old.

She was angry for about a count of two hundred after he had gone, and it was a clean anger and it was the first one she had had since she came off the mountain.

Nineteen years. The only instrument in the world and it is a pair of hands and a mouth that will not stop moving, and no copy, and no example, and not one line in nine hundred years with her own name on it, and now a carter with a yoke telling her what she already knew for free.

## The Part Where The Instrument Failed Her

She said it out loud to the crossing.

“I have been reading that thing every morning for nineteen years and I could not tell you one single thing about how to stop it, and I cannot be given anything, and I cannot ask anybody where to look, and I have to build the whole of it out of a grammar that only goes one way.”

She got up off the wall and did the rest of the day in the ordinary way, standing, in the rain, asking people what they knew, and what they knew was nothing, and she put it into the keeping anyway about two hundred times because the rule does not have an exception for a question and she was not going to be the first one to make one.

At about the fourth hour a girl of about sixteen came and stood about four feet off and said, “Do you want me to hold the lamp.”

“Yes,” Ning Yan said, and then found that she had nothing at all to give her for it, because she had not said one pleasant thing out loud to another living person in so long that her mouth did not have the shape for it.

So she said, “Hold it about a count of sixty and then put it back down and tell me whether your arm gets tired.” And the girl did it, and it did, and that was the whole of what she had.

Then she sat down on the wall and put the lamp beside her and worked out which way was the one way, and it took her the rest of that day and most of the next night, and what she got was one line, and the line was that a machine that puts things down does not put down a thing that has not happened yet.

Which meant the sentence she had to say could not be a complaint and could not be a demand.

It had to be a report.

And she had never in her life had to write one.
