# Entered in Stone


She read on the seventeenth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four, standing, with the lamp moved a hand’s breadth at a time and her right hand flat on the table edge because the arm had started to shake and it shakes on days when it has been open a long while without a hand in it.

Two hundred and thirty-eight days since the ninth stage stood in the record and not in her mouth that morning, because the register wanted reading and not figures.

The cuts past the sixth ran deeper than the five. That was the first thing she found and it stopped her for a count of forty with her thumb on the stone.

Deeper meant the same hand.

She put her thumb along the sixth cut and then back along the fifth and then along the first, all three in the dark with only an eyelash of light to see by, and the depth was the same in all of them. Whoever cut this had the same blade and the same patience and the same intention. Nothing here was added later by a second pair of hands, and nothing here had been left longer or shorter than the five.

## What Was Past the Sixth

It had been there the whole time, behind the mercies, and it had been cut to exactly the depth the mercies were cut to, and it had been cut by the same person.

She read the next block standing with her three fingers and a thumb a breadth at a time along the stone, and it was not one line. It was four.

The first said that the offer stands open at the top of this tower until it is answered or withdrawn, and that it is not withdrawn by the passing of anything.

The second said that what is answered is answered in the channel where the offer is made, out loud, standing, in the voice of the person it is about, and not by writing and not by a sign.

The third said that an answer given there cannot be taken back into the mouth that gave it.

The fourth was one line long and it had no name on it.

## What the Rule Did

She read the first of the four out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, before it was a line anywhere, and she heard how it sounded in a room at the top of a tower at about the fifth hour and it sounded like a thing about a table.

She read the second, and the third, and the fourth, and then she stood with all four in her and the lamp giving its eyelash along the cuts she could no longer see, and she did the arithmetic that was the whole of what she was for.

A rule had been found on the fourteenth of this month that said a thing goes into the world in the mouth of the person it is about, standing, before it is a line anywhere.

And here, cut past the mercies in the same hand to the same depth, was what a bargain made under that rule looks like when it is finished being written.
It had been written the way she writes. Her mouth first and the writing after.

Somebody had taken the method she has used for nineteen years and built an offer in it, and had built it into a table at the top of a tower that she would come to and put her hands on, and had put the rule behind the mercies so that a woman coming up for the price of a thing would have to go past the answer to find out what the answer was standing on.

She put both hands flat on the register and stood very still.

“It is built on my own way of saying things,” she said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice. “That is not a favour either. It is the reason this offer can work at all.”

## The Offer, Entered

The dark past the last landing did not answer, and nothing answered, and she was not asking anything that could be answered, and she said the rest of it into the room because that was the method and she had nothing else.

She read the four lines once more, standing, in her own voice, whole, before any of them was a line anywhere else. The first. The second. The third. The fourth.

She had said it. That was the finding and it went through her the way a landing goes through a foot.

[Terms are entered on the register at the top of the tower.]

The words went past her one at a time, plain, reporting, through her to him, and nothing in them explained anything and nothing in them had to, and what they said was that a thing she had said out loud standing at a table had gone into the record, and the record was the layer’s, and the layer writes when it decides something about a person.

## What She Said First

She stood with her hands on the stone and felt the going change in the traffic above her the way the traffic changes when a line goes in, which was not at all, except that it was done.

“The offer stands open at the top of this tower until it is answered or withdrawn,” she said out loud, standing, into the dark. “I have said that standing in my own voice. That is not accepting it. I want the difference held, because I have kept it held since the twenty-fourth of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and twenty-four and I am not letting it go now that it is on a record instead of a table.”

“I am holding it with you,” he said. “Entered is not answered. I heard all four lines in your voice and I heard you draw the line the second before they went past.”

“Good.”

“No,” he said. “There is a thing I have to put in the room and I am putting it in the room because I promised you at the fourteenth that I would not keep anything from you on purpose. The offer is entered in your voice. Not the layer’s voice. Yours. You said it standing at this table before anything wrote it and the rule you found on the fourteenth is the rule that made your mouth the one it went in by.”

She stood at the table and did not answer him for a while.

“Yes,” she said. “That is what it means. I found it on the fourteenth and I found what it means tonight and I did not want to say it, and I am saying it standing because sitting is for keeping and this is a finding.”

“You have entered an offer into the record that you have not accepted,” he said, “and there is no way to take a thing back out of the record once your mouth has been the first one in it. That is the price of reading it under a rule like that one and it is not a small one.”

## What Went Out

“It is not a small one,” she said, “and I did it anyway, and I did it out loud, and I am not going to stand here and pretend I would have read it differently.”

“What did you feel,” she said.

“Feel.”

“When it went out. You have been in this channel for nine years and you can hear a sentence before it lands. Tell me what it felt like when the four lines went through me into the keeping, and do not make it a figure, because I am not asking for one.”

He was quiet for a count of forty.

“It felt like being written on,” he said. “That is the plainest I have got. Not spoken to. Written on. There is a difference and I know it because the first of the four is about being addressed and the second is about where it has to be answered, and when the four lines went out through you just now, nothing addressed me. Something made a line with me in it.”

“Good.”

“It is not good,” he said. “I am telling you what it was because you asked and because I said on the twenty-sixth that I was not going to keep anything from you on purpose. That is the whole difference between what the offer does and what I do, and I have been in the machinery six hundred years and I have never once been able to see it from this side.”

She stood at the table with her hands on the register and did not argue with him about it, because it was not an argument and he had never once given her one of those in nine years.

The lamp went then.

It did not go with any warning. It had been an eyelash for four days and she had told herself that an eyelash was a light, and the wick went into the oil that was not there and the room closed over like water.

She stood at the table in the dark with her hands on the register and the register under her hands and nothing else in the world within reach.
She had four paces. That was what the lamp had given her for four days and she had been reading at two and had believed she had more. The table was in front of her.

The far side of it was somewhere in the dark at the end of her left arm and the place at the far side with nothing worn at all was somewhere past that, and the cuts past the fourth of the four were under her hands and she could not see one line of them.

She put her right hand flat on the stone and it shook, hard, the way it shook when she had been standing too long, and she breathed nine counts standing still and then four and then four again.

She had read the four lines. She had not read what was past them. She had read what was past the fifth and the sixth and four more, and there was more, and she could feel it under the heel of her hand if she moved it a breadth at a time in the dark, and she could not read a word of it and there was no light in the building and there had not been for nine years.

“Four paces,” she said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, into a room with no light in it at all. “That is what it gave me and I have had it since the fourteenth and I am at the end of it.”


The register lay under her hands in the dark at the top of a tower with more of the offer in it than she had read, and she had put it into the record in her own voice, and there was no oil anywhere in the building, and eight flights and a stair below her a boy made oil he could not buy and carried it up on his own account every morning and had never once been told that anybody was up here.
