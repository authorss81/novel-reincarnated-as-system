# Not Due

He asked her for one thing at the sixth hour on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning three thousand and forty-four and it was the smallest thing anybody had ever asked her for.

“Do not get up.”

She was on the stone at the near end of the table with her back against the leg of it and her right arm hanging open against her side.

“I am going to get up.”

“I know you are going to get up,” he said. “You have got up and sat down and gone the length of that table so many times since you were ten years old that the count of it is not a number anybody keeps, and every time you do it now I lose about a count of forty of it.”

“Then sit still yourself.”

“I am not able to sit still,” he said. “That is the whole of what I have been telling you since the seventh. A thing in a wall is not a body. I cannot hold one place for longer than it takes to say something, and if you are not in one place then the thing I am saying has nothing to sit on.”

She put her hands flat on the stone on either side of her and stayed where she was.

## The Whole Of The Day

It went on from the sixth hour until near the ninth.

She did not get up. That was the whole of what she did that day and it turned out to be harder than any of the arithmetic she had ever put into a keeping, because she was a woman who worked and the room was the work.

There is a board a foot in from the near end and a terms board a foot further along and a strip of pale wood face up under the table at the near end of the leg, and she could not touch any of it.

At about the seventh hour he went back over something and she let him. At about the eighth hour he got to a thing she had not heard of before and she found out that there were three of those and that all three were about the same morning, and that morning had not been the one she would have chosen.

He was not very good at it.

He repeated himself nine times. He said one thing and then said it again a different way about a count of two hundred later, worse, and she understood that he did not know he had said it the first time. Twice he came to the edge of a reason and turned off it on his own. Once he said her name four times in a count of one hundred, which was four times more than it was worth, and did not appear to know he had done that either.

She sat on the stone floor of a room no one could come into with her hands flat on it and let a man in a wall practise at being a person in a room, and her back went against the leg of the table and she did not move.

## Nine Times She Nearly Got Up

A woman who has been at the same piece of work for nine years did not stop moving because a voice in a wall had asked her to. The body went on doing the counting.

She nearly got up at about the sixth hour and a hundred, because the near board was face up a foot in from the near end and she could see the grain of it from where she was sitting.

She nearly got up at about the seventh hour, because the lamp had gone down a little in the wick and she could see the flame lean. She nearly got up at the seventh hour and a hundred, because there was a draught off the stair and the skin between her shoulder blades had gone up on it.

She nearly got up about nine times in every hour of that day, and there were nine of them that she counted, and the reason there were nine was that she had counted the walking about her own room thousands of times in nineteen years without ever once working out what the number was for.

By the eighth hour her left hand had gone from the stone. She took it off and shook it out and put it back and it went again inside a count of two hundred, and it was three fingers and a thumb, and it had been three fingers and a thumb since she was sixteen years old and it had never once been a reason not to do a thing.

By the ninth hour the lamp had burned down far enough that the far end of the room was not dark any more but a different colour, and she understood that she was watching oil go, and she did not get up to do anything about it, and she was not going to be able to say afterward whether that had been obedience or something else.

There is a thing that happens to a room when the light in it goes all day.

The things in it do not move and nothing happens to them and they get smaller, and after a count of two hundred or three hundred you cannot see the boards at the far end of the table from the near end of it, and the only thing in the room that has not changed is the bar against the left-hand wall, which is a bar nine feet nine long and is dated nowhere and which she could find in that room at any hour of any day with her eyes shut.

## What Three Hours Of It Was

She listened to it the way she had listened to the keeping every morning since she was ten, which is to say completely, without any part of her doing anything else.

Three hours of a man talking.

In nine years he had talked to her about everything there was to talk about and every one of it had been about her. What she was made of. What it had cost. What a stage was. What she could be got to spend. What the walls were. What he was. Nine years of a voice in her head that had never once gone a whole morning without putting her at the end of it.

On the twenty-fifth of the month beginning three thousand and forty-four, sitting on the stone floor of her own room with a lamp going down at her feet, she sat through three hours of a man talking about what he had decided, and he did not say her name once after the first of them.

She understood that she was in a room with a man apologising to her, and that an apology is the only kind of talking there is that does not go on talking to the person it is for.

She had no figures for that. There was nothing in this room to count about it and she had never in her life found that to be a relief.

## The Last Ordinary Thing

It came in the middle of the eighth hour, out of the dark past the last landing, one piece at a time, plain, in the grammar the keeping used for everything it said, and there had been no line of any kind in this room since the second hour of the twenty-seventh.

[The bundle is not due. It goes up nine hundred and four steps on the first of a month, and none of them has been late.]

She let it go past her.

It was not about her. It did not say anything about a woman, or a room, or a table, or an arm that had been open at the elbow for seven hundred and seventy-four days, or about a thing that had gone into the substrate of the keeping on the twenty-seventh of last month. It said that a bundle was not due, and it said how a bundle goes up, and it said it in the same words it had used about four hundred years of firsts.

She stood there and let it go, and she thought, for no reason she could have named, that she was going to remember the exact shape of that line for the rest of her life.

## What Came Back At Nine

At the ninth hour she stood up off the stone and went to her own table and said the day’s figures out loud in the ordinary voice, in the order she had been saying them in since she was ten years old.

She said them all.

Nothing came back.

She stood there with her mouth shut and waited, because in nineteen years there had not been one morning on which the keeping had heard six figures out of her mouth at the ninth hour and given back plain nothing.

She waited a count of two hundred and two hundred. She said a second thing, which was not a figure, and she said it standing, in the ordinary voice, into the dark past the last landing.

Nothing came back.

The building was nine hundred years old and it had kept the walls of the world up without being asked once in all that time, and it had gone on doing it through a refusal and through four minutes of a man being happy and through a thing going into the substrate of it on the second hour of the twenty-seventh, and at about the ninth hour of the twenty-fifth of a month it had said that a bundle was not due and had not said a word since.

Nothing was broken. She went and put her hand on the wall on either side of the table and it was a wall and it was stone and it was cold. The bars in the stair were where the bars had always been. The layers under the mountain were doing what layers do.

What had closed was not the keeping. It was the line to her, and it had closed somewhere between the second hour of the twenty-seventh and this morning, and she had been standing in the room on both sides of it.

She stood at the near end of her own table with a lamp burning at her feet and understood, standing there, that in nineteen years she had never once said a figure at the ninth hour into a room that did not answer, and that she was going to have to go on saying them.
