# The Same Failure For A Decade

She had been in the yard above Lowwater for about two years and she had failed at the same thing for ten, and the ten years had not been spent failing in one place.

She put it out loud on the morning of the seventeenth of a month beginning six thousand four hundred and four, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the west end where the light was better after the fourth hour, in front of about twenty people who had come to be told a true thing.

“Three thousand three hundred and thirty-six days out of a room. Four thousand one hundred and nineteen days in that room before it. Four thousand one hundred and twenty-six days with this arm open at the elbow, and it is not going to close, and I have said that out loud to you and I am going to keep saying it out loud to you.

“Three thousand four hundred and sixty days since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four. That is nine years and two hundred and twenty days and I am twenty-eight.

“Three thousand two hundred and fifty-seven days since the thirtieth of a month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four.”

Then she said the other half, which was the half she had said out loud at the weir and at a rim and in a lane, and which had not improved in ten years.

“I have failed at one thing since I was nineteen years old and I have not got better at it at all. I can stand in front of a person and say a true thing out loud in the ordinary voice for as long as the other person will stand there, and I have never once been able to teach anybody to do it.

“Not one. Ten years. There are people in this yard who can say a short version and there are people who cannot, and every single one of the ones who can got there by watching me for seven days or staying near me for nine, and I have never once been able to put it in somebody faster than that, and I have tried.”

## The Woman With Four Days

A woman of about thirty had been in the yard for about a month and on the fourth of that month she came and told Ning Yan that she was going to the eighth county on the eleventh day of the month to start it there, because her mother was from there and there was a boy in her mother’s village who had a slate and could count.

“I have been trying for a month,” she said, “and I cannot do it.”

“Tell me what happens.”

“I stand up and I look at you.”

That was the whole of what came out and it took about a count of six hundred, and Ning Yan stood in the west end of a yard and worked out standing, in the ordinary voice, that this woman had spent a month going to the front of the yard and standing in front of her and getting nothing at all, and that she had four days left and there was not one thing she could do with four days.

“Look at me instead of her,” she said.

The woman tried it for about two hours and could not do it, because Ning Yan was standing four paces off with her hands at her sides and she could not get her eyes off her.

## What She Did With Four Days

She did nine things in four days and none of them worked and she said sorry out loud in the open for each one, in the ordinary voice, standing, in front of the woman and in front of anybody who had come to watch.

She said the three sentences at length, from the front, with the pauses. The woman wrote them all down and got them perfect and could not stand there having written them.

She said the reason first and then the sentences. That was closer and it went wrong because the woman began apologising out loud to the person in front of her before she started, which is not one of the three sentences and is not allowed to be one of them.

She got a boy of about sixteen to do it and had the woman watch him. That was the best one and it did not work at all, because the woman watched the boy’s hands.

She stood behind her. That failed so completely that the woman turned round twice.

She put the woman in the yard at the ninth hour with a real crowd and it failed because the woman could not find the sixteenth minute.

She let the woman watch a woman of about nineteen do it and asked her afterwards what the difference was, and the woman said, “She is better than me.”

“Try it again without that.”

It failed again.

She said it as a thing about herself rather than a thing about the other person and the woman got nine sentences out before she stopped.

And she tried to teach her the arithmetic instead, the seven counts and the pauses and the sixteenth minute, because that was the only language she had ever spoken, and the woman got all of it exactly and could not use any of it at all.

## What The Woman Said On The Fourth Day

On the fourth day the woman came and said she was going that afternoon, and Ning Yan said sorry out loud for the ninth time in front of about nine people, and the woman said, “Do not.”

“It is the same thing I have said for nineteen years.”

“It is not,” said the woman. “You have said it nine times in four days. You said it four hundred times in a county once. That is not the same thing at all and you know it is not the same thing.”

And then she said the other thing, which Ning Yan carried out of the yard with her and put down in front of about nine people at the ninth hour two days later.

“You do not know how to teach and you are not going to learn how to teach by watching yourself teach. You have been doing the one thing in the world that you can do, standing up, and you have been doing it at people for nineteen years, and there is not one of them who can do it.

“I am not blaming you for that. I am telling you that you have spent ten years trying to make a thing out of a person by standing in front of her, and a person is not made that way, and you would know that if anybody had ever stood in front of you and done something to you.”

She went that afternoon with nine days of road in front of her and no instrument and nothing she had been able to learn, and Ning Yan walked her to the made road, which is about a quarter of an hour, and did not go past it.

## The Part Of It That Was Her Own Fault

She got angry about it that night, which she had not done about this in about nine years, and she let it happen, which she had not done about anything in about four.

She stood in a yard at about the ninth hour with about nine people still in it and the anger came up out of the place where she kept the thing she could not do, and it was not a good anger and she did not pretend it was.

The woman had been right and she knew exactly how she was right, and the knowing was the whole of what she had. There was not one thing about it that she could blame on a machine or on a wall or on a person who was gone.

A person had watched her for seven days and gone and did it. A person had stayed nine days near her and did it. Those two were not taught at all.

“You have four hundred days of standing in the middle of this yard,” she said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, to about nine people at the ninth hour, “and four days of one of the three sentences, and you have told two people in ten years and it took nine days each, and you worked that out at the fourth hour this morning in about a count of forty.

“I did not know any of it until the fourth hour today. That is not a figure about teaching. That is a figure about standing in the middle of a yard for ten years.”

Not one person said anything, and she did not ask them to, and she stood there with the anger in her for about a count of four hundred and then it went down the way anger goes down when there is not one thing at the end of it to be aimed at.

## The Arithmetic Of A Failure

On the sixth day she did it properly, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the yard, because it was the only thing she could do with it.

It came to about one person every nine days for nine years, and about thirty people in the county who could stand in front of somebody for a short while, and about four hundred days of somebody standing and watching her while she said the true thing at the ninth hour.

Which came to about four days of her, spread over ten years.

And she could not do the next part of it, and she said so, standing up.

“I cannot work out what that becomes in a year or in five, because it is not arithmetic any more. It is people and it is about nine days and it is whether a person is ready, and I have not got an instrument that weighs whether a person is ready, and I have spent nine years with a mouth full of instruments and none of them weighs that.”

She put her arm up and it came about a foot and stopped, the way it had stopped every day for four thousand one hundred and twenty-six days, and she let it hang.

“There is one woman in this county who can do what I can do,” she said, “and I made her, and it took me nine days that I did not know I was spending, and there is one in the eighth county who is going to try to make one before the month is out, and I have not got any part in that at all.

“And I am twenty-eight, and the yard has about nine people in it who can stand in front of somebody for a few minutes, and that is what ten years looks like, and I am the one it looks like it is for.”

She stopped there, because that was a thing she had worked out in ten years and it did not have a way of being said any better, and because about nine people were in the yard at the ninth hour and had heard it, and because a thing said out loud standing goes into the air and cannot be taken back out of it, and she did not want to say it any better.
