# The Absence

She had been listening for what came after it for four days and it did not come.

The apology had stopped at about the ninth hour and a hundred of the fourth of the month beginning three thousand and seventy-four without a last word, the way it had stopped three times before it.

She had spent the fifth and the sixth and the seventh standing at her own table waiting for whatever a man says when he has finished saying the whole of what he did, and nothing had come out of the wall.

She had gone on putting her figures in at the ninth hour on all three of those days, because the ninth hour was hers and she was not going to hand it over.

On the eighth she was at the near end of the table at about the sixth hour with her hands flat on the worn wood and the lamp on the stone at her feet, and she had made her peace with the idea that there was not going to be anything else, when he spoke.

“Ning Yan.”

## What He Said Before He Went

He said it in a count of about two hundred and she listened to every word of it standing at her own table with her hands on the wood.

“There is nothing after it. Every decision I made about you is said. I went through it again in here over four days and there is not one left, and that is not because I have run out of things or because it was a short list. It is because there was not one on it that I made without you in the room.

“And I am not able to do it again. That is the part you should have on the record first. A man who has said every decision he made about a person has nothing left to apologise with, and I am going to go on being audible in this for about a count of two hundred and two hundred, and then I am not going to be audible, and you are not going to be able to get me back by standing still and saying things into a room.”

“All right,” she said.

“You are not going to say anything to that.”

“No.”

“You are not going to ask me to stay.”

“No.”

“You are not going to tell me that it did not matter.”

“No,” she said. “It did matter.”

The wall held for about a count of forty and when he came back it came from a long way off, the way things came on the seventh of the month when a sentence got to the end of the layer.

“I am going to say one more thing and then I am going, and I am going to stop in the middle of it, and that is not something I am doing to you. There is no way to do this with an end on it. A line does not have a last word. I asked you for that on the twenty-seventh of the month before last and you moved a lamp a little to the left.”

“That was the last thing you ever asked me for,” she said.

“It was the only thing I ever asked you for,” he said. “I had been in this keeping for six hundred years and I had asked for nothing at all in it, and I asked you to move a lamp about a foot, and you did it in the middle of the ending of me, and you did not say one word about it, and you are never going to tell me what that was” and it went, in the middle of it, the way a thing in a wall goes.

## The Middle Of It

It did not come.

He stopped the way a man stops when there is not any more of him to go on with, which is the way a thing in a wall stops, and there was no silence after it, because a silence is a thing you can have a length of and that had no length.

She stood at the near end of her own table with her hands flat on the worn wood.

One moment there had been a voice in her head that had been there since she was ten years old and had gone down into the substrate of a layer nine hundred years old forty-one days earlier, to be heard by the thing that made him. And then there was not one, and the room was a room, and a lamp was burning on a stone floor a little to the left of where it had stood since the second hour of the twenty-seventh of the month before last.

She said his name into the room.

Nothing came back.

That was the first thing. She had said his name a great many times in nine years and it had never once not come back, and she had got so used to the answer that she had thought about the answer and never about him.

She said it again, twice, three times, and then she said it a fourth time with her voice up out of her the way she had said it on the twenty-eighth of last month, which was the only other time in nine years she had put it up at all. Nothing came back, and it was not the other kind of nothing either.

She put her hands on the wall on either side of the table and pushed and it was a wall and it was stone and it was cold, and there was no man behind any part of it, and there was not going to be one.

She spent the rest of the morning in the room.

She took the near board up off the face of the table and read it by and put it back face up a foot in from the near end, and she took the terms board up and read that and put it back a foot further along.

She went the length of the table with her hand along the wood and past the bundle under the far end where it was corded twice and not knotted, and she put her hand flat on the bar against the left-hand wall and took none of her weight on it.

Every one of those was a thing she had done a great many times, and not one of them told her anything, and she did all of them, because they were hers and there was nobody left to do them for her.

She did not go up the stair. She stood at the foot of it and looked into the dark for about a count of two hundred and she did not go up nine hundred and four steps to a room with no wear in it, because there was nobody at the top of it now and a table with a register on it and an offer withdrawn on it does not need looking at, and because the room below was hers and she was in it.

## Nothing At The Ninth Hour

She said her figures out loud at the ninth hour on the eighth of the month beginning three thousand and seventy-four.

Three hundred and forty-nine days since the ninth stage. One hundred and forty-two since it was shut. Seven hundred and eighty-seven days with this arm open at the elbow. Seven hundred and eighty days in this room. One thousand five hundred and twenty days gone at the sixth of this month and two days since, taken out of her at a rate she derived out of her own life, not one of them coming back.

She said them standing, in the ordinary voice, into the dark past the last landing, first in her own voice before it was a line anywhere, because that was the rule and because the rule was hers and she was not going to let it lapse on the morning there was nobody to lapse it for.

Nothing came back.

She stood there with her mouth shut.

Then she sat down on the stone at the near end of the table with her back against the leg of it, which is where she had sat at about the fourth hour on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and eighty-four while a man said yes four minutes away from being a person, and she put her hands flat on the stone on either side of her knees and left them there.

## What Was There

It took her a long time to notice that there was something there.

That was the part that no one in this building was ever going to be told and that she did not intend to tell anybody. She had been listening for a man for nine years and then for two days for a thing that had given up being able to apologise, and she had found the not-finding of it so loud that it had taken her until about the ninth hour and a hundred to notice that the inside of her head was not empty.

It was not empty.

There was nothing in it. There was no voice, and no reading of her, and no figure at the end of anything, and no keeping going on underneath it with the traffic of a bound world in it. And there was a shape in there.

She sat on the stone floor of a room no one could come into with her hands flat on it and worked out what the shape was, because she was a woman who worked things out, and she worked it out standing in her own head in the ordinary voice before it was a line anywhere, and there was nowhere for it to go and she said it anyway.

“It is not nothing,” she said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, into her own room. “There is a shape in it. It is the shape of a man standing in a doorway with his hands empty, and it is warm on one side.”

She said it once.

She did not say it again.
