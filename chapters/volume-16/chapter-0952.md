# Not On The Edge Of A Road

She found her again on the twenty-ninth about two days after the woman had walked off down the road, because there was nowhere else she could be.

The crossing was where the made road came off the hill road and there was a low stone wall at one end of it with a gap in it, and the woman with the pole came off the pass on the last day of every third day or something near it, and Ning Yan had been sitting at the gap in the wall for two days.

She had a dead lamp in her left hand and the making of it was not getting any better.

“All right,” she said. “I have come to ask you again.”

“Have you.”

“I have. I have been at this wall for two days and I am going to say it to you once and if you say no then I will not ask you again and I will not be sat here when you come down the hill.”

## Saying It Standing

She said the short version.

She said it standing, with nothing in either hand, in the ordinary voice, in front of the stone wall, first in her own voice before it was a line anywhere, and she got all of it out without stopping to think about any part of it, which was what four repetitions to stones had been for.

The price of it is on the record. I do not know what it costs me. You are not being sent anywhere and nothing is being done to you and not one person in this but me binds you.

The woman listened to the whole of it with her hand on the pole and her head a little to one side.

“No,” she said.

Ning Yan stood there and felt the whole of the twenty-eighth and the twenty-ninth go out of her at once.

“No. All right.”

“Not that it is no,” the woman said. “Sit down. I have not finished asking. I said no to the version where you say it once and I think about it for a week by myself and come back and say yes to a wall.”

## What A Record Is

“So tell me what a record is.”

“That is fair.”

“It is not a word I have. I have been carrying timber down off that pass since I was a child. A record is a thing I have heard of in a town and not in a hill.”

Ning Yan sat down on the low wall opposite her.

She tried three ways. The first one was the way she would have put it to a person in a room, which was that a keeping nine hundred years old kept the walls of the world up on purpose and had been running a machine for nine hundred years that made one person at a time less solid, and the second one was shorter and worse, and the third one she gave up about a third of the way through.

“You have spoken to me for four days about it in the plainest words you have and you have not once said what it does to you,” the woman said.

“That is the fourth thing and I cannot get round it.”

“Why not.”

“Because I do not know,” Ning Yan said. “I have carried a figure for nineteen years that is out of my own life at a rate I derived out of my own life, and that is the only figure I have ever had, and it has nothing in it about what a person is owed by a sentence. I can derive every number I own. I cannot derive that one. I have never been able to.”

## The Part That Was Not About The Price

The woman was quiet for a long time. Behind her the pass went up into grey and there was a bird working at something on the rock.

“Then you will not do it alone in the middle of a road.”

“No.”

“No,” the woman said. “That is not what I am saying. Listen to what I am saying, because you are bad at this and I have had four days to work out what you are bad at.

“You have got two. The oil woman at the stall, and a boy in a mountain, and me, if I say yes. And every single one of us said yes to a girl standing on her own in a wind. That is what you are offering. Not the thing. The girl on her own in a wind.”

“Yes.”

“I have been on this road since I was eleven years old and I have never once in my life been asked what I want by anybody who then went away and left me with it.”

She put the pole down on the ground and stood up straight, which Ning Yan noticed, and had to look away from, and went on standing.

“You have not even got your arm right and you are asking people to put themselves under you for something you cannot tell me the cost of, on your own, in the middle of a road. I will not be one more thing that happens to Dai Yu on her own.

“Ask me again where there are other people. In the open, standing up, in front of anybody who wants to stand there and hear it. Then it is not me on my own and it is not you on your own, and if it goes wrong there are people who saw you say it.”

## The Thing She Said Standing

“Yes,” Ning Yan said. “I am not going to ask you in front of people because it would look better.”

The woman looked at her.

“You will do it because it is what you are doing anyway,” Ning Yan said, “and because you have said so and I am not able to talk you out of it, and I want that said standing, in the ordinary voice, before it is a line anywhere, so that if I ever use your reason I do not pretend I thought of it first.”

Dai Yu laughed. It was not a kind laugh. It was the noise a person makes when they have been arguing with a wall and the wall turns out to be right.

“That is the first sensible thing you have said to me since the twentieth,” she said.

## The Part She Was Bad At

She was bad at it again on the morning of the twenty-ninth and she was bad at it in a new way.

She had a short version now, three sentences that she could get out without stopping, and she got them out twice that morning to two different people who had come up the track past the gap, and both of them said no, and both of them were polite about it, and the second one was polite in a way that was worse than being rude.

He was an old man going home and he had listened to the whole thing standing still with his stick in both hands, and then he said:

“You have not told me what you want me to do.”

“I have told you.”

“You have told me what is on the record and that you do not know what it costs you and that nobody sent me,” he said. “That is three things about it. You have not once said what you want.”

That was true and she had not noticed it happening.

The three sentences described the thing completely and they never once asked for anything, and she had heard herself say them about two hundred times and she had not worked out that they were not a question.

She went back to the wall at the gap and wrote the fourth sentence on the flat of a board with charcoal, and it was the shortest one of all, and she said it out loud about nine times to herself before she went and found Dai Yu and said it to her standing.

“My name is Ning Yan. I am asking whether you will let me put you into an arithmetic of mine.”

“All right,” Dai Yu said. “That is a question.”

## The Last Day Of The Month

They sat on the wall until the light went.

Ning Yan asked her twice more over those hours, standing, both times, both times in the same words, and both times Dai Yu gave the same answer, which was that she would answer in the open in front of people and not on her own.

And then, somewhere in the middle of it, Ning Yan said something she had not meant to.

She said, “You are the third person I have asked and the second one who has not said yes and I have been walking round this wall for two days and I want you to know that I am not good at this and that I am doing it anyway.”

“Everybody knows that,” Dai Yu said. “Everything you have said to me for four days has been about a machine. All I have wanted from you since the twentieth is a straight sentence about what you do not know.”

“It is three sentences.”

“It is one sentence said three times badly, which is the same thing.”

And then the light went and the woman shouldered her pole and went up the pass, and she was gone, and Ning Yan sat on the wall in the dark with a lamp that had not carried a flame since the fourteenth of the month beginning three thousand and seventy-four.

## What Was On The Road

It was the last day of the month.

She had not been counting to that and she found it out from the road, which was something new. The made road was full. There were carts and there were people on foot and there were people standing about at the edges of it not going anywhere, and they were not there for any reason she could see.

She asked a boy with a drove of pigs what the day was.

“Market day,” he said. “Last day of the month. Everybody comes down off the hills for it and then they all go back up and it is the same every month and there is nothing to buy.”

There were going to be people there tomorrow.

She stood up off the wall and worked out, standing, in the ordinary voice, that she had asked one question in four days and got a better answer out of it than she had got out of nine hundred and four steps of stair, and that the answer was that she was going to have to say a thing in front of a crowd and she was going to have to be watched while she said it.

She was not ready for that and it was tomorrow.
