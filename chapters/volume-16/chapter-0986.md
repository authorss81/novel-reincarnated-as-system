# The Man Who Kept The Gate

She went back to the gate on the thirteenth day because there was one thing about it she had not been able to leave, and it was not curiosity.

There was a difference and she had known it since she was about nine years old.

Curiosity is a thing you do when you have nothing else. She had something else, every hour of every day, in four counties, and she had not gone back to the gate for three days.

## A Road Going Through A Gap In The Stones

The gate had a market on it.

That was the first thing and it was the whole of what had changed, and she stood at about the fourth hour about ten paces back from it and let it come to her in about a count of two hundred.

There were about sixty stalls on the near bank. There were carts on the far bank standing where no cart had ever stood before, because there had been a wall. A woman was selling salt out of a barrow on the far side and four men were carrying a beam through and one of them was telling the others, quite patiently, that it was much easier than it had been.

There was a gate.

There were two piers and a beam and a black leaf of wood on a hinge, standing open against the near pier, and a man of about sixty was sitting on a stool about two paces inside it with his hands on his knees.

That is all a gate is. She stood there and worked it out standing, in the ordinary voice, on her own, in about a count of four hundred.

A gate is a thing that people walk through.

A gate is not a boundary. A boundary is the other thing and it has gone, and it is possible to stand at a gate for nine hundred years and believe that the gate is the boundary, and it is not, and when the gate is a gate there is nothing at all in the world that anybody has to do about it.

## The Room Behind The Piers

There was a room behind the near pier with a door in it, and she had not known it was there, and she went and looked at it.

There was a table in it. There was a chair. There was a window with the shutter open facing the far side.

There was no wall behind the house.

That was the entire finding and she stood in the room in the cold for about a count of six hundred, and the man of about sixty came and stood in the doorway and did not come in, and waited, which she noticed.

Behind the house there was a field, and the field went down to a stream, and the stream went east, and there was a hedge about nine hundred paces off with a wood behind it, and then there was ordinary country.

She had been twenty-nine years old on the morning she walked into that room and the whole of the last three days had been her trying to find the edge of what had come off.

There was not one thing on the field that had a mark on it.

## What He Did Every Day

His name was Du Wen and he had been at that gate for longer than he would say, and she asked him standing, in the ordinary voice, what he had done every day, because she wanted to know what a thing goes to pieces into and she had no other way of asking.

He thought about it for a while.

“Two things,” he said. “I looked at the far side every hour and I wrote down what came through.”

“On what.”

“On the day’s sheet. They came on the day’s sheet and they went on the sheet behind the door and they went on the third sheet when there was a third sheet.”

She looked at the table and at the chair and at the space behind the house.

They were not there.

That was not a figure. She did not put a number on it and she did not ask him for one and she could not have put one on it if she had asked. There was a table and there was a window and there was a field, and that was all of it, and whatever had been going on that table for however long had gone the way the wall went.

Du Wen said, “I am going to ask you something and you can say no.”

“Yes.”

“Am I to keep the gate.”

That was the cost of the thirteenth day and it was standing in a doorway about two paces off her.

She could not answer it.

## What She Could Not Answer

She stood in a room behind a pier and worked out, standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of a man of about sixty, what she was entitled to say.

She is not able to be given anything. She is not able to be paid. There is no office in nine counties above anything and no list and no message, and she cannot send anybody and cannot be sent. She has been telling people this for a month and it has not improved.

And she was standing in a room with a man who had asked her a plain question and she had no way to tell him to keep his post and no way to tell him to go home, and either of those would have taken a piece of paper off the table in front of him.

So she said the true thing out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of him, before she said anything else.

“I was not able to keep a gate and I was not able to let one go. I had no office and there was no office above this one and there was not one person in nine counties I could give that to. I was not able to be given anything and I was not able to pay you and I was not able to send a message anywhere.

“What I could tell you was what I had seen with my own eyes in thirteen days, and it was a table with a window in it and a field behind the house and not one thing on the field with a mark on it, and I had not got a figure for that because there was no instrument in the world that weighed a field.”

Du Wen said, “That is not an answer.”

“No,” Ning Yan said. “It was the true one. I would rather give you the true one and let you decide than give you one of the other kind and have you find out later which it was.”

## A Man Who Came Through On The Fifth Day

A man of about forty was sitting on the far bank eating something out of a cloth, with a barrow and three tools, and he had come through on the fifth day and had not gone back since.

She asked him the two questions she asked everybody. What he had, and what he was going to do.

“I have a brother on this side,” he said, “and I had a house on the other one, and the house is still there and the brother is over there.”

“So you were living in a barrow.”

“I was living in a barrow by the road and I had got a job carrying beams for the woman who was doing the far bank, and I had got her to say that I would be at it in the morning, and I had not asked her a single question about any of it and neither had she.”

She stood there about a count of forty.

“You have not asked what came off.”

“No. She had not told me and I had not asked and we were both getting on with the thing in front of us. That was not a virtue and I was not going to claim it was one.” He wiped his hands. “If you have come to tell me what it was, I am going to have to tell you that I have not got any room for it today. I have got a barrow and a job at the fourth hour.”

“I had not come to tell you anything,” Ning Yan said. “I came to look at the gate.”

“Then you had looked at it,” the man said, “and it was a gate, and I was going to be carrying that beam in about an hour.”

## Nothing To Announce

He sat back down on his stool.

There was a boy of about nine at the gate trying to lift the black leaf of wood, and a carter had come up and was waiting to see whether anybody would stop him, and a woman had set her stall down on the far side and was calling out a price in a completely ordinary voice.

Nothing was announced. Nothing had been for thirteen days, and there was no office in which anything would have been announced, and there were not one person in four counties who had any idea what was supposed to be standing there in the first place.

She went and got the black leaf of wood up off its hinge by herself, with one arm, in about a count of four hundred, and put it flat on the ground against the near pier, because a thing on the ground is not a shut gate.

Du Wen watched her do it and did not stop her.

“Do you want me to put it back.”

“No,” Ning Yan said. “I was not able to tell you what to do. I had told you the whole of what I had got and it was a field with no marks on it.

“I was going to walk through it once and come back on the other side, and then I was going to go and do the only thing I was any use for, which was to be standing somewhere that people could reach. That was the whole of the thirteenth day and I had not got anything to add to it.”

She went through. It took her about a count of two hundred and nothing happened on either side of her at all, and the market went on being a market, and the beam went on across, and the man on the stool at about two paces inside the gate did not get up and did not stop her and did not ask her anything else.

And that was the finding of the thirteenth day, and it cost a man the only thing he had ever been able to answer yes to, and she could not give it back and she could not take it away, and she did not think it was hers to hold in the first place.
