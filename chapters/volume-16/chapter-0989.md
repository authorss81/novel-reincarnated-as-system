# A Length And Not A Count

A carter with a broken axle asked her how far it was to the next water, and she had the answer before he had finished the question, because it was the one kind she had always had.

“Two of an arm past the elm, and then take the ditch. The ditch turns before you get to it. Do not go by the field.”

He said, “I have never seen your arm.”

“It will not change by the time you have seen it,” Ning Yan said. “Go by the ditch.”

He went by the ditch. She watched him get to the turn before he had any further trouble, and that was the last thing her arithmetic did for anybody that day.

## What The Second County Asked Her

The second county began at a mill with a broken wheel and she had no way of telling by looking at it where one county stopped and the next began, and neither did the carter, and neither did the woman who came out of the mill yard with her sleeves rolled.

The woman asked her how many people were in the village up past the ridge.

She opened her mouth.

There was nothing in it.

That was the whole of it, and it took her about a count of four hundred to stand in a yard with her mouth open while a woman waited for an answer to a question about a village on a ridge. There was no figure and there was no way of making one, and she had not had one in her hand since a morning on a rim, and she had known that, and knowing it had not stopped the mouth.

“Have you been up past the ridge,” she said.

“No,” said the woman. “Not this year.”

“Then neither have I,” said Ning Yan, “and I am not going to tell you a number I have made up.”

The woman looked at her for a while and then said, “You are the one from the west road.”

“I am.”

“My sister said you said something about what it cost.”

“She heard part of it,” Ning Yan said, “and part of it was said by people who had heard part of it, and by this morning there is not one version of it in this county that is right, and I am the only one who knows the right one and I am standing in your yard, and you are the first one I have found this month.”

## The One Instrument She Has Left

She said the true part of it out loud then, standing, in the ordinary voice, in the yard, to the woman with her sleeves rolled, because it was a true thing and because the rule was the rule and had been the rule since she was a child.

She had thought about it for nine days without saying it. That was the honest way round, because a thing she says out loud standing is in the air afterward and cannot be taken back out of it, and she had wanted to be sure of the wording, and the wording had been four words long.

“The figures are gone,” she said. “I have got a length and I have got days and I have got hands and I have not got anything else, and I have had that for nine days and I did not know I had it until a woman in a yard asked me for a number and my mouth opened and there was nothing behind it.”

She put it down and she felt it go past her into the air and stay there, which is what happens to everything she says, and which had never once been the point.

She had said it once, and she was not going to say it a second time, and she knew on the way out of the yard that she would think about it every day for a year and say it out loud only once.

## What She Did With A Ridge

So she did the only thing she could do, which was the thing she had been doing for nine days, and she did it on the ridge.

She went up past the ridge with the lamp in her left hand, because there is still no reason in the world to set a lamp down, and she walked into the village at about the ninth hour and stood in the middle of it with about nine people in front of her and she gave them the three sentences at about sixteen minutes each.

That the price of it was on the record, and that she did not know what it cost her and did not know what it cost anybody else, and that not one person had sent him anywhere and nothing was done to him and she was the only one who bound anybody.

That a sentence had been said on the thirtieth of a month at a gap in a wall and it had taken nine days and she did not know what it was for or whether it was right.

And that he could say no and it would stay on the record, and she had to say that out loud before he answered anything, because it was the rule, and the rule was the whole of what she had ever offered anybody.

It took her about two and a half hours for nine people and she got to the end of it with the light going and her right arm hanging the way it has hung for eight hundred and ninety-six days and not going to close.

The ninth one was a woman of about forty who had a list of what she had and a list of what she had lost, and who said, before Ning Yan had finished, “I have heard it. My mother has heard it and her mother has heard it and I came to see whether the woman saying it was the same shape.”

“That is the whole of what I have to offer,” Ning Yan said. “That somebody said it and then somebody else said it and now you have come and looked at my face.”

“And are you.”

“I am.”

The woman said, “Then that is worth the walk and I will go and tell my mother,” and she did not say yes and she did not say no, and Ning Yan wrote nothing down, because a name cannot go on a leaf before it has been said standing in the ordinary voice in front of the person it belongs to, and she had had two hundred words of it and not the sixteen minutes.

## The Arithmetic She Could Still Do

She got back down off the ridge at about the eleventh hour and she stood on the made road in the dark and did the arithmetic that she still had, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, to herself, which is the only audience there has ever been for it.

One hundred and six days out of a room. Eight hundred and eighty-nine days in that room before it.

Twenty-seven days since the thirtieth of a month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four, and nine of them were the fall, and eighteen of them were walking.

Two hundred and thirty days since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four, which is the last day there has been with anything on it, and which is the only fixed day in the world she still had.

Four days of walking since she went west out of Lowwater in the dark, and about nine thousand paces of them by the length of her own leg, and nine counties of road between that place and where she was standing.

That was all of it.

That was the whole of what she could derive standing up in the ordinary voice at the eleventh hour on a made road in the second county of nine. She had had six figures every morning since she was ten years old and she now had five, and all five of them were about days, and one of the five was a day she could not get at with her hands.

## The Boy On The Road

A boy of about nine came up the road from the south with a slate on his knee and a piece of chalk in his hand and no adult of any kind with him, and he stopped about four paces off her and looked at the lamp in her left hand, which had been dark since the fourteenth of a month beginning three thousand and seventy-four and had no reason in it at all.

“You have not got a light in that,” he said.

“No,” Ning Yan said.

“Then why are you carrying it.”

“Because there is no reason to put it down.”

The boy thought about that for about a count of forty and then said, “My aunt says you are counting.”

“I was.”

“Have you stopped.”

“No,” Ning Yan said. “I have got a length and I have not got a count, and I told a woman in a yard this afternoon that the figures are gone, and I have said it once and I am not going to say it again.”

The boy put his chalk down on the slate and looked at it.

“Then you can count those,” he said. “I can do them. You say them and I will tell you if you have got it wrong.”

She stood in the dark on a made road and worked out, standing, in the ordinary voice, that she was going to walk eight more counties on foot with a boy of about nine who could count for her, and that she had not asked for one and was never going to ask for one, and that he had offered, and that she had not been able to say no to him.

“Say a number,” Ning Yan said, “and I will say it after you.”
