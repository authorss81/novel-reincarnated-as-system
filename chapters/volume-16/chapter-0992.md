# A Second Mouth

The girl with the slate came up the road out of the fourth county with nine children behind her and she was about twelve years old and she had been saying the three sentences to them for about a month.

Ning Yan heard her from about two hundred paces, standing in a hedge gap, and she listened for about a count of six hundred without going out, because she wanted to know what the girl did with it before she found out what the girl had been taught.

The girl did it badly.

She did it in the right order and she got the middle one nearly right and she said the part about being able to say no in a bright voice with no pause in it at all, and she said the whole of it in about four minutes, and Ning Yan had always known that four minutes was the number and had never once watched somebody get it wrong.

## The Arithmetic Of Seven Hundred And Fifty Days

She had been walking for six hundred and fifty days and she put the whole of it out loud at the ninth hour on the second morning, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the side of the road, in front of about nine people who had come to find out what she was.

“One thousand one hundred and ninety days since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand nine hundred and fifty-four. That is three years and a hundred and ten days and I said it out loud because there were six hundred and fifty of them and not one of them was a next day.

“Nine hundred and eighty-seven days since the thirtieth of a month beginning three thousand and one hundred and thirty-four, and I was twenty-two in the middle of that and I am twenty-two now.

“One thousand and sixty-six days out of a room. One thousand eight hundred and forty-nine days in that room before it. One thousand eight hundred and fifty-six days with this arm open at the elbow, and it is not going to close in any of the time I have left either, and I have said that out loud three times now to three sets of people and not one person has ever argued with me about it.”

“Where were you in all of that,” said a man of about thirty with a barrow.

“Here,” said Ning Yan. “That is the whole of what I have got about it. There was no there to be anywhere else.”

## What Was Wrong With It

She came out of the hedge gap and the girl saw her and stopped, and the nine children stopped, and there was about a count of two hundred on the road with not one of them knowing what was about to happen.

“That was fast,” Ning Yan said.

“It is three sentences,” said the girl.

“It takes about sixteen minutes.”

“Why.”

And that was the whole of the problem and it had been waiting for her since she was about nine years old, and she stood in a road in the fourth county and answered it honestly.

“Because you have to say it standing,” she said, “and in the ordinary voice, and you have to say out loud that they can say no before they answer anything, and if you say it fast it is a thing being repeated, and if it is a thing being repeated then a person can agree with it and it will not have cost them anything and they will not have heard the part where it was mine.”

The girl said, “Then I have been doing it wrong for a month.”

“For a month,” Ning Yan said. “And four children behind you have been getting it wrong for a month, and they will have it wrong for the rest of their lives, because that is what happens to a thing that is said fast, and there is not one person in the world who can put it right except the three of us standing here.”

## The Four Ways She Tried

Then she tried to teach her, and she did it badly, and she did it four times, and she said sorry out loud in the open for each of them, which is what she has done since she was about ten years old and has never once found a way round.

The first way was to say it properly, all of it, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the speed she used, with the pauses she used. She got four sentences in and the girl interrupted her to ask whether the thing was going to hurt, which was a question the girl had not been asked and could not have answered.

The second way was to shorten it. She cut it to about nine sentences and the nine sentences left out the part about not knowing what it cost, and she could hear it leave and she stopped in the middle of the eighth one.

The third way was to make it into arithmetic, which was the only instrument she had ever owned, and she got as far as naming the three days and the sixteen minutes and the order, and the girl said, “That is not the same thing,” and it was not.

The fourth way she tried was to say the reason first, out loud, before the sentences, in front of the nine children, about what the price of it was and what she did not know about it. That one was closer.

“Because of the first one,” the girl said. “Because the reason is the thing that makes a person stand there for sixteen minutes.”

“Yes,” said Ning Yan.

“I would not have stood for sixteen minutes for the three sentences.”

“No,” said Ning Yan. “You would not.”

## She Was Never Taught By Anybody

And then she sat down on the verge of the road with her right arm across her knees, because it hangs, and she worked out standing in the ordinary voice where she had got the four failures from, and it was not from anywhere.

There was not one person in the world who had ever taught her a thing.

She had been taught the four-count breath by a woman in a furnace yard who had taught about forty children and had not time for any of them, and the breath had stuck. She had been taught the run of figures by a wall that wrote them down at the ninth hour every morning and had never once asked her whether she had understood them, and the run had stuck.

Everything she had ever learned had come to her as a thing that was happening to her while somebody else was busy.

And she had spent nineteen years being the one who stood in front of people and saying a true thing out loud, and she had never once been on the other side of it, and she had no idea what the other side was like, and she had just failed at it four times in front of nine children.

“I do not know how to do this,” she said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, on a road in the fourth county, to a girl of about twelve. “I have never been taught and I am not able to fake having been.”

The girl said, “Then I will do it and you will tell me what I have got wrong.”

So the girl did it.

She stood in the road with the slate in her hand and she took a long time at the start, and she said the part about the price being on the record first and she said it four times in four different ways and then she asked the girl in front of her whether it was true that the price was on the record, and when the girl said yes she moved on.

And she said the part about saying no before anything else at the front of it, not the end, and she said it as a thing that was being done to them rather than a thing that was being offered.

And she left out two whole sentences of the middle one and kept every word of the last.

That took about thirteen minutes and it was better than anything Ning Yan had done in four attempts.

## What She Decided

She stood in a road in the fourth county at about the eleventh hour with nine children and a girl of about twelve and she made a decision, and she made it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, because a thing she says out loud standing goes into the air and cannot be taken back out of it, and because she had been in the air for nineteen years and knew exactly what that cost.

“I am going to teach you to do that properly,” she said. “Not the three sentences. The way to stand there for them.

“And I am going to say the other half out loud as well, because you are twelve and I am not going to take advantage of a child who has come up a road on her own.

“You will be able to say the three sentences after me. And then you will be able to say them to people who are not me. And from the day that happens, there is not one thing I do in this world that I am the only person who can do, and I am not going to be able to find out who has said it wrong, and there is no instrument in the world that would tell me.”

The girl said, “Is that a bad thing to say out loud in front of nine children.”

“It is a true thing,” said Ning Yan, “and I would rather say it to you than have you find it out standing in front of somebody on your own.”

## The Girl Did It Again

She did it again at the fourth hour of the next day, in a yard, in front of about fourteen people, and Ning Yan watched her from about six paces off and did not say one word.

The girl was still not right. She went back to saying it fast in the middle of it, and she said the no part at the end instead of the front the second time, and two of the fourteen looked at Ning Yan when she got it wrong, which was the right thing for them to do and the wrong thing for the girl to cause.

But she stood there for twelve minutes and she let two people say no, and she wrote both of the noes down on the slate in her own hand with a line under the names, upside down, the way the girl of about nine had once done at a rim with a piece of chalk.

And when it was finished the girl turned round and said, “How was that.”

“You went fast in the middle,” Ning Yan said.

“I know.”

“You put the no at the end.”

“I know that too. I have not got it yet.”

“Then you have got the rest of it,” Ning Yan said, “and the rest of it took me nineteen years to learn and you have had a day, and you are going to be saying it to people for longer than I am going to be standing here.”

The girl put the slate down on a bench and looked at her.

“Where are you going to be standing,” she said.
