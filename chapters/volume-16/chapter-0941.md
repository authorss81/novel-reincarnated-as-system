# Her Own Arithmetic

He did not ask her for anything on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning three thousand and forty-four, and it was on that morning that she answered him.

She had thought about it for three days. She had gone over it on the twenty-fifth while she sat on a stone floor and did not get up, and on the twenty-sixth and the twenty-seventh while a man in a wall went on saying the whole of what he had done to her without a figure in it, and she had put it the way she puts everything, which is as an order of days.

She stood up off the stone at about the seventh hour and put both hands flat on the worn wood.

“I am going to say something and I am going to say it standing, in the ordinary voice, in this room, and I am saying it before it is a line anywhere, because that is the rule and because it is the whole of what is left of the rule that I have got.”

“Say it.”

## Four Days Of It

She had gone over it three times before this morning and each time the answer had come out different and each time the different one had been worse.

On the twenty-fifth, sitting still on the stone, she had had an answer that was a list, and it had had the shape of a settlement in it, and she had thrown it out before she got to the sixth hour because a settlement is a thing two people make and she was not making one.

On the twenty-sixth she had had an answer that was nothing at all, four words long, and she had gone back up the room and looked at the place at the far side of the table to see whether she could get it out of her mouth, and she could not, and she had understood then that the whole of it was that she had nothing to give him that was not arithmetic and she would not give him arithmetic as a gift.

On the twenty-seventh she had had an answer that was the truth and it had been one sentence and it had been about her, not about him, and she had taken it apart and found a decision in it, and a decision of hers made about him was exactly the thing she had been refusing to do for eighteen days.

And on the twenty-eighth she had got up off the stone and come to the near end of her own table and had found the thing that was left, which was that she had an account and he had never been told what it was, and that telling him was not a gift and was not a punishment and was not a settlement.

It was a woman saying out loud what had been done to her, in front of the person who had done it, so that there would be one record of it in the world with her name on it.

## The Account

“Three hundred and thirty-nine days since the ninth stage,” she said. “One hundred and thirty-two since it was shut. Seven hundred and seventy-seven days with this arm open at the elbow. Seven hundred and seventy days in this room. One thousand five hundred and twelve days gone out of me at a rate I derived out of my own life, and not one of them coming back.”

She stood there and let it be in the room.

“Nine years since a voice came into my head and I was ten years old and I did not run. Nineteen years of a girl being told what she was going to be.

Three fingers and a thumb on my left hand since I was sixteen. Seven of eleven holds. A place behind my fourth rib the size of my thumbnail that has not closed since the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and four and that does not feel like anything when I touch it.

“That is the account. I have carried it since I was ten and I have put every part of it into this keeping at the ninth hour in my own mouth, and there is not one item on that list that came from anybody else.”

## The Thing She Would Not Give Him

He was quiet for about a count of a hundred and two hundred, and she went on before he got there, because she knew what a silence that length is for.

“I am not going to say that it is all right.”

“All right,” he said.

“No.”

“Say it again.”

“I am not going to say it is all right, and I am not going to say it is not all right, and I am not going to say anything about it at all, because I do not know, and I have not known since the twenty-seventh, and I have had thirty-one days of not knowing and that is not the same as the nineteen years before it.”

She put her hands flat on the wood.

“I have been getting up at the ninth hour for nineteen years and putting six figures into a keeping that took them and gave nothing back, and I did it because it was the one thing in my life I was certain of. You were in my head and the numbers were mine and they went in every morning and they were there in the morning. And now there is nothing in my head at all and the numbers still go in and nothing takes them.

“I am not settling this with you. That is what I am saying and I want it understood that it is not a punishment. The account is on this side of the table and it stays on this side, and I am not going to work out what it comes to, and that is not because I cannot derive it.”

“You cannot derive it.”

“I can derive every item on it standing,” she said. “I have just done it. What I cannot do is put a figure on what a person is owed by another person and I have never been able to and I have had nineteen years to find out.”

## What He Did With That

He was quiet for about a count of forty.

“That is an answer,” he said.

“It is a set of figures.”

“It is the first thing anybody has said in this building that is about what I owe and not about what the keeping took, and I have had six hundred years of one and not one of the other, and I am telling you that I am going to take it, and I am taking it now, and you cannot stop me taking it.”

“No,” she said. “You cannot have this one.”

“Say it again.”

“You do not get to do that,” she said, and she put her voice up out of her for the first time in nineteen years. “You do not get to take my account and turn it round into your peace. That is what a keeping does. That is the whole of what this building has ever done to anybody, including me, and you have been inside it for six hundred years and you know exactly what it looks like when a person’s own figure is put back at them as a settlement.

“I said the days out loud. That is all I said. It is not a verdict and it is not a finding and it is not a thing to put down and pick up again on a better morning. It is the days, and they are mine, and you cannot have them.”

She put both hands flat on the wood and stood there with her chest going and waited for him to do the thing he was going to do, because she knew it and he knew it and neither of them had said it out loud.

## The Thing He Did Not Do

He said, “I am not going to say that it is enough.”

“No.”

“I want to.”

“No.”

“All right,” he said. “All right. It is not enough and you have not said that either and I am not going to get it out of you by working at it, and I am going to stand here and let that stand, because you have been a person for nineteen years who says things out loud and I have just watched you not say one, and it is the hardest thing I have ever seen anybody do in this room.”

She took a breath of four and let it out.

“Go on with it,” she said.

“Why.”

“Because you have two of them left and you are at the part where it is going to get worse, and I am not going to be the thing that stops it.”

He went on with it.

She stood at the near end of her own table for the rest of the seventh hour and the whole of the eighth hour and at the ninth hour she said the day’s figures out loud into a room where the keeping had not given her anything back since the twenty-fifth, and the days went in and there was nothing in her head to hear them.

That was the last answer she gave him.

She knew it standing there, at the ninth hour, with her hands on the wood, and she did not mark it and she did not say one word about it. Nothing after that morning was an answer.

What came after it was a man going on with the whole of what he had decided about her, day after day of it, and her standing in a room while it came. Not one more figure of hers set against one of his. She had been carrying that account since she was ten years old and she had set it down once, on the twenty-eighth, and she was not going to pick it up again for a man with four more mornings of it left to say.

She had wanted, for about three days, to be the kind of woman who said it was all right. That was the whole of what she had wanted and it was not a noble want and she did not pretend to herself that it was.

She had wanted to put one sentence in a room on the twenty-eighth of a month and have it be the end of it, because she was nineteen years old and she had been carrying an account since she was ten and she was so tired of carrying it that she would have given a man four walls and a name and a ward of her own to put it down.

She did not say it. Not because she was holding it over him. Because there was nothing in her that said it, and she had known that for three days, and she was not a woman who said things she had not found.
