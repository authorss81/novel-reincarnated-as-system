# Eight Hundred And Eighty Steps

On the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, which is the day two thousand five hundred and forty, a boy of sixteen came up eight hundred and eighty steps in a tower four miles up a mountain and stood in the doorway of a room with a lamp in a cage at the far end of it that had gone out six days before, and asked her why it was lit, and she did not have an answer that was small.

## The Fifth Of That Month

She stood in that room in the dark for three days and she did not go down either stair once.

She said the six terms out loud every day at the ninth hour, standing up, in the ordinary voice, one at a time, and on each of the three days she said the first one and then she stopped where the second one was and said the other four and then came back and said the second one anyway, because the fourth of the six is that she says it out loud as she goes before it is a line anywhere else, and she is not going to let a term she has kept for nineteen years be the one she breaks.

And on the fifth of that month she said a name out loud in the middle of them.

She said it once. She said it because a record has to say what it is between, and that has been in the six since she was nine years old and she has never once been able to work out how to keep the record and leave the name out of it.

Nothing in that room made a sound.

There is nothing in that room that makes a sound. There has not been a sound in it in two hundred and thirty-seven days, and there is not one thing in it that she can tell has heard anything, and she said his name out loud on purpose knowing what a hand does with what it reads, and she is not going down two days of road to find out what was written.

And then she went and worked out what she was going to say to a boy of sixteen when he came up that stair, and she got as far as the near end of the table and then she stopped and stood there for a count of ninety.

She said out loud, in the dark, at the near end of that table:

“I have got an arithmetic.”

She said, “And it is true and it is all of it and it is not one sentence, and I have never in nineteen years had anybody ask me a question and get an arithmetic, and I have never once in nineteen years had a person who wanted one sentence.”

## Boots On Four Hundred And Forty

On the sixth of that month she went down the low door and took the first four hundred and forty with her left hand on the rail and stood on the fourth landing in the dark with her hand in the cage of the lamp that has been out since the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four.

And at about the fifth hour she heard the tower’s own stair.

There are eight flights of it and they are four hundred and forty and four hundred and forty and the first four have a rail on one side and the second four have not got one, because the wall is the wall and there was never room for a second one.

Somebody was coming up it and it took a long time and it stopped four times, and on the fourth stop she heard a person sit down on a step on purpose, the way a person sits who is going to get up again.

She said out loud in the dark on that landing, and he could not have heard it because she was a hundred and forty steps under him:

“That is him.”

And she got up off that landing and went back up the four hundred and forty with her left hand on the rail, and she did not run, and she went up faster than she has gone up that stair in two hundred and thirty-eight days.

And she stood four feet off the near end of that long table in the dark and she listened to him come the whole way, and there were twenty-two paces of table between her and the far end of that room and there was not one thing in that room to see him by.

## The Doorway

He came into that room at the seventh hour of the seventh of that month and she was standing four feet off the near end of that table.

He had a lamp in his hand. It is an oil lamp with a wick and about a cup and a half of oil in it and there is no oil in this building and there has not been for nine years, and it had been in his hand since the second hour of the morning and it was one of the three he has, and the flame leaned over when he came through the door and then stood up straight when he stopped.

He stopped.

The flame did not lean in a draught. There is no draught. The light came up off the stone floor and put a hand’s width of itself along the near end of that long table and went no further, and the twenty-two paces from there to the far end stayed exactly as they had been for six days.

And he looked along that table the way a person looks at a room.

He looked at her as well. He looked at her for a count of nine and she could do nothing about her face, and nothing in that room decided anything when he did it, and it was the first time since the ninth hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four that anything had looked at her.

And he said, “It is out.”

And she said, “It is.”

And he said, “Why was it lit.”

## What A Row Is

And she gave him the whole of it, because he had come up eight hundred and eighty steps to find out why and she does not have anything else, and she said it standing, in the dark, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table with a boy of sixteen four feet off her and an oil lamp in his hand.

She said, “There is a lamp in a cage at the far end of that table and there has been one burning in it for four hundred years and there is no oil in it and there is no wick and there is nothing behind it and the wall behind the light is the wall.”

She said, “There is fifty-eight of my own hand from the top of that cage down to a cut in the stone of that wall, and the light comes down two of my own hand a day, and fifty-eight divided by two is twenty-nine, and so the light comes down to that cut twenty-nine days after a hand goes into that cage.”

She said, “A hand has gone into that cage at the second hour of the second of every month since before this building was a building and it costs whoever puts it there, and I have watched it eight times, and it is not oil and it is not money and there is nothing anybody in this county has ever been able to put in a lamp in a cage.”

She said, “It came down to that cut at the fourth hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and it did not stop on it, and that is because there was no hand in it, and there was no hand in it because I did not put one in.”

And she said the last of it standing there in the dark, and it is the plainest sentence she has said in this building in two hundred and thirty-nine days.

She said, “And I did that on purpose and I am the only person in this building who could have done it and I have not got a reason you would want.”

And he said, “That is not why.”

And she said, “I know that is not why.”

## The Place At The Far Side

And then he went along the table.

She did not see him go along it. He had the lamp in his right hand and he put his left hand out on the wood the way a person does in a room they do not know, and he went a good way along it and stopped and stood still for a count of ninety.

When he came back his face was in the light and hers was not, and he looked at her and she could not see his eyes.

He said, “There is a place worn in it.”

She said, “I know.”

He said, “That is worn by something being put in the same place a great many times over a very long time and it is not a hand.”

She said, “I know that as well.”

He said, “And it is not for anything in this room.”

And she said out loud, standing in the dark, at the near end of that table, and she said it to him and not to the room and she had known for two hundred and thirty-nine days that it was the plainest thing in this building:

“It is not a chair. There is no chair in this room and there has never been one. It is not a seat and it is addressed and it is the right size for somebody who is not here, and not one instruction in this room put anybody in it, and I found it on the ninth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and I have not touched it and I have not said one word about it out loud until this afternoon.”

And she said, “And I cannot tell you what it is for, and you are sixteen and you are standing in a doorway and you looked at that room the way a person looks at a room, and out of every one of those things I do not know the answer to a single one of them.”

## What Is In That Room

And then she said the whole of what is in it, out loud, standing, in the dark, at the near end of that table, because she cannot let a day go past without putting its figures somewhere other than in her head and because a boy is standing in a doorway.

She said, “There is a bar nine feet nine flat against the wall on the left of this room, broken end towards the door, with no strap on it, and I left it there on purpose when I was nineteen years old in this building and it is there now and I have not moved it.”

She said, “There is a stub of a nib and a cake of ink against my fourth rib that has not been let dry since the seventh of the month beginning one thousand eight hundred and seventy-four.”

She said, “There is a bundle of papers under the far end of that table corded round twice and not knotted and I have had my arm into it three times and never pulled it.”

She said, “There is a strip of pale wood on the floor under that table, a foot long and two inches across, with ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes at the head of it and the same figure in the first pair of cuts under the head and five in the second pair, in my own hand, and twelve pairs of cuts under that are empty.”

She said, “And there is no light in this room and there is not one instrument in the world that could read one word of it.”

And then she said the figures, and they were the last figures she said out loud in that room.

She said, “Two thousand five hundred and forty less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is nine hundred and eighty-one days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and not one of them going back and one of them gone into that lamp and one of them that light.”

She said, “And two thousand five hundred and forty less two thousand two hundred and ninety-four is two hundred and forty-six days that this arm has been open, and three fingers and a thumb on the hand that writes with, and nine hundred and four steps at the foot of that room and eight hundred and eighty at the top of it, and a boy of sixteen in a doorway and a brother two days of road and six hundred feet below me and a voice in my head that is a person and that I am not speaking to.”

She said, “And a lamp that has been out for six days that I put out.”

## The Question He Came Up The Stair For

And he stood in the doorway of that room with the lamp in his hand and he said the thing that a person of sixteen says when they have come up eight hundred and eighty steps and been given an arithmetic.

He said, “That is a lot.”

And she said, “It is all of it.”

And he said, “I did not come up here for that.”

And she said, “I know you did not.”

And then she waited, standing in the dark at the near end of that table, for a boy of sixteen four feet off her to say one more thing, and it took a count of a hundred and forty and she did not fill it.

And when he did say it, what he said was, “Is there anybody in here.”

And she said, “There is one person at the far end of that table and she has been standing at it for four hundred years and she has never once sat down and she has never once wanted to.”

And he said, “Does she know I am here.”

And she said, “I do not know. She has not looked up once in two hundred and thirty-nine days and she did not look up on the second of this month when she spoke to me and she did not look up when the light went out. I do not know whether there is anything in there that can tell the difference between a room with a person in it and a room with a lamp burning in it.”

And she said the last of it, standing, in the dark, at the near end of that table, and it is the last thing she says in this building and there is nobody in the room to hear it but a boy who came up eight hundred and eighty steps to ask why:

She said, “I had a sentence for you and I have not got it. I have got an arithmetic and it is true and it is all of it and it is not one sentence, and I have been in this room two hundred and thirty-nine days and I have not had one sentence that was any use to anybody until this afternoon, and you asked me for one, and I do not have an answer that is small.”
