# A Row She Could Say Out Loud

She worked out on the twenty-fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four that she was never once in her life unable to say a row out loud, and that the reason nothing had ever come of it was that she had never done it in a room like this one.

## What She Has Been Able To Do Since She Was Nine

She said it out loud to an empty room on the twenty-fourth, standing at the near end of that table with her left hand on the edge of it, at the seventh hour, and it took her a count of ninety.

She said, “I have been able to say a thing out loud, standing up, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, since I was nine years old. I have done it in a yard with a bar in my hands and on a road with a cart going past and at a frame with two hundred people behind me and in a room with the shutters up and against a rail on a copying floor, and in all of those it went somewhere.”

She said, “And there has never once been a hand in the room that copies figures exactly.”

He said, from the door, “You have got one now.”

She said, “I have got one now. That is the whole of what has changed in seventy-four days and it has taken me all of them to be able to say the sentence.”

## A Head And A Column And One Of The Two She Has

On the twenty-fifth she laid the four strips of wood out on the stone again in the order she finds them in and worked out what a row wants, out loud, one item at a time.

She said, “A row has a head at one end and the head is a figure. I have got that from mine and I have got it from all four of them because there is a mark at the head of every one and the mark on mine is a figure.”

She said, “A row has a column and the column holds figures and not things. I have got that too, from the width of the cuts and the shape of them.”

She said, “And a row is a foot long and two inches across and a foot and two inches across is the size of one person saying one thing, and I have said about nine hundred figures in this room in seventy-four days and not one of them has gone into a column.”

She said, “So the head is a figure and the column is figures, and I have got a figure and I have got nothing for the column, and those are two different problems and one of them is finished.”

## The Figure She Has Never Written Down

She worked on the twenty-sixth for the whole of a day and she got it out loud at the ninth hour and it came out in a form she did not expect.

She said, “There is a figure I have been carrying since the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning two thousand and fifty-four and I have written it on three things in my life, and none of the three of them went up a stair to a room.”

She said, “There is a slate at two posts with my name on it and a figure under my name. There is a board at the north end of a ground two days of road east with four sentences on it and a figure under them. And there is a strip of hand-made paper nine inches long with eighty-one lines on it in a bag at the bottom of eight hundred and eighty steps, and the same sum is under that in words.”

She said, “And the figure under all three of them is the same figure and it is the figure at the head of the column on the strip under that table, and the figure is what I am worth and not what I am.”

He said, “Write it again.”

She said, “If I write it again it is a line on a piece of wood in a room with a person in it and not a row, and I have got four of those and none of them has anything in it about me.”

## Why She Could Not Do It On The Twenty-Seventh

She stood at the near end of that table at the seventh hour on the twenty-seventh and worked out, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, why she was not going to do it in the next four days.

She said, “A row wants a head and a column. I can produce the head and I have produced it in a hundred rooms and it has never once gone anywhere, and I have known for four days that in this room it would go somewhere, and that is not what is stopping me.”

She said, “What is stopping me is the column. Because a column is not a price. A column is what a thing has done, and I have got one figure and it is what I am worth, and if I put that in a column in a room where things are decided by a hand then I will have a row with my price at the top of it and nothing else in it, and I already have that strip under that table and it has been wrong for eighty-six days and not one person in this building knows.”

He said, “Then what would go in the column.”

She said, “I do not know.”

She said, “That is not false. I have been in this room seventy-nine days and I have measured everything in it and there is not one thing in it that will tell me, and the three old rows under that table have columns in them and I cannot read a figure on any of them with the flat of my own hand.”

He said, “You said you counted that room nine times through a door before you ever came up it.”

She said, “I counted it nine times through a grille and there was no person in it, and I have not got an instrument that goes down a hole in the floor and there is not a hole in the floor.”

He said, “What is on your own strip.”

She said, “A figure at the head of it and one thing in the column and the one thing is the figure. So the first figure I ever got into a row, I got in by writing it on a slate at two posts on a road, and the room put it in for me and I did not have to say it to anybody at all.”

She said, “And that is the thing I have been getting wrong for ten years. I have been waiting for a hand to enter me and I wrote a figure on a piece of stone out of doors and it went up a road by itself.”

## What She Was At Nine

And on the twenty-seventh, at the eighth hour, she said the part of it that was about herself and not about the row, and she said it sitting down on the floor with her back against the near leg of that table, which is the only way she has ever said anything that frightened her.

She said, “I was nine. I had a bar and I had nothing else anybody had ever handed me and I wrote a thing on a leaf out of a blanket with three words on it in four capital strokes because a thing in capitals is a thing that cannot be got at.”

She said, “And I have had that leaf against my ribs for ten years and it is the only thing I have ever written that was not a figure and not a name and not a date.”

She said, “And I have never been in a room where anything I said out loud went anywhere. I have said things to voices. I have said things to a bar and to a post and to a stretch of road. I said six things out loud to a man on a ground with stones in it on the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand one hundred and fourteen and they went into a book, and that was nine years ago and that is the only time.”

She sat there and did not say anything for a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “And the next time it is going to be in this room, out loud, in front of a hand, with a boy of sixteen in it, and I have not got the sentence.”

## The Boy Standing In The Room Where One Look Decides

And then she said the part of it that was about the room and not about a row, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the ninth hour of the twenty-seventh, with a boy of sixteen four feet off the end of a table in a room where one look decides everything and there is no second look and no appeal.

She said, “On the fourth of this month I put a day of my own into that lamp with my own hand and I did it in this room and there is a person in this room and not one thing moved, and I have been seventy-nine days in a building where a thing can happen to a person and nothing gets said about it.”

She said, “And the first thing that is ever going to be said in here that is about me is going to be said with a boy of sixteen standing in the room, and he did not ask for that and I am not going to be able to choose it, because I am not going to be able to get a column out of myself without saying what I am and the only other person in here is him.”

He said, “I can go down the stair.”

She said, “You can go down the stair and you will be four miles from this room at the fourth hour and I will be here saying a thing to a person who is not listening to it.”

She said, “And I have not worked out what to say yet, and I am not going to put it to you before I have got it, because the last time I said something out loud to a person before I had finished it I had to stand and hear what it was.”

## The Sums Before She Slept

She did them standing at the near end of that table, as she has done every night since the eighth of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and the boy was asleep at the foot of it by the end of the second one.

She said, “Two thousand three hundred and eighty less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and twenty-one days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day.”

She said, “The light at the far end of this room is fifty of my own hand below the top of that cage and it goes down two a day, and fifty-eight less fifty is eight, and eight divided by two is four, and there are four days from this one to the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, which is the day the next forty boards come up that stair.”

She said, “And the figure on the strip under that table is four days, seven hours and twelve minutes out of date and it will be out of date when the forty boards get here, because those forty boards do not carry it and it has not been carried anywhere since the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four.”

She said, “And I have four days to work out what a column is for and I have not got the faintest thing yet.”
