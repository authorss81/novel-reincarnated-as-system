# Nib And Ink Against The Fourth Rib

She went down eight hundred and eighty steps in the dark on the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen and came back up them with a stub of a nib and a cake of ink against the fourth rib, and on the fifteenth she went down nine hundred steps of the second stair with nothing in her hands and came back up, and that one she cannot do.

## Down Four Hundred And Forty With A Rail

She went down at the second hour of the twelfth with the low door standing open behind her and the stair going down in front of her into the kind of dark she has been sitting at the far end of for a hundred and twenty-five days.

Four hundred and forty with a rail on one side and her left hand on it. Then four hundred and forty with a wall four inches off her hand on one side and nothing at all on the other.

Her right arm has been open since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and it is not going to close, and it was open that day, and she counted all eight hundred and eighty out loud one number at a time because she worked out a long time ago that a count in her head on a stair is a count that can be lost.

At the turn she stopped for a count of thirty and put her back against the wall and listened, and there was nothing to listen to, and she said out loud in the dark:

“I have gone down this without a rail four times in nine years and I have never once let myself know that I am not frightened of it.”

## The Cold Iron Of It

The lamp in a cage on the fourth landing went out on the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and there has been no oil in this building for nine years, and she went past it on the way down and put her hand into the cage.

The iron was cold.

She said, out loud, standing on that landing with a boy’s hand on that cage and not her own, because a boy of sixteen keeps that lamp and has said he would keep it going after she stopped asking, and he was right to say it:

“This is the lamp he keeps going and there is not one thing in it to keep going on.”

She said, “He came up that stair three times and went down twice and he asked me twice why the lamp at the top of this tower is still lit when the lamp he was told to keep going is not, and I gave him a number the first time and nothing the second, and he is four miles down that mountain keeping a cold cage and I cannot get one answer down to him that is both true and small.”

And then she went on down the rest of the second four with her hand off the wall for the last two hundred of them, on purpose, to see what it was like, and it was the same as the other times.

## Nine Things On A Floor

At the bottom of it there is a bag and she has walked past it on the eleventh of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four with it at her feet and said out loud that she was not going down for any of them.

She got it open with her left hand in the dark and put everything out on the stone of that copying floor, one thing at a time, and she named each of them out loud because she has named them before and she wanted them to sound the same the second time.

A dish. A lump of clay. A ring the width of a finger. A stub of a nib. A cake of ink opened on the seventh of the month beginning one thousand eight hundred and seventy-four and never let dry since. A key to a locked drawer thirty feet from where she was sitting. And a strip of hand-made paper nine inches long with eighty-one lines on it and the same sum written under it in words.

She said, “That is nine things and not one of them is a day, and I have said that out loud before and I am not going to improve on it.”

And then she picked two of them up off the floor and put them inside her shirt against the fourth rib, where the book of eight leaves is, and she did not touch any of the other seven.

She said, “I decided on the third of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four that I was not going down for any of these, and I said it again on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four with the bag at my feet, and I have not taken either of those decisions back. I have done one of them.”

She said, “And the decision was never about the nib or the ink. It was about the drawer. A drawer is a decision and a nib is a thing, and I said that on the fourth of that month out loud in front of a boy of sixteen and I am saying it again tonight on my own so that there are two of me who know it.”

## Up The Lower Four Without A Rail

She went back up at the fifth hour of the twelfth, in the dark, with two hard flat things against the fourth rib and nothing in either hand, and there is no rail on the lower four hundred and forty and there is no light on any part of it.

At four hundred and forty she sat down on a step for a count of twenty and put her forehead on her knees.

At six hundred and forty her breath went off nine and she stood still on the step in the dark and did not go on until it was nine again, and it took a count of seventy, and there is not one person in this building who would have known that had she not said it out loud.

And at the top of it, on the floor of that room, with the bar nine feet nine against the wall on the left where she put it on purpose and the person at the far end of that table with both hands on the near edge, she said at the seventh hour of that day, standing, in daylight:

“Four hundred and forty and four hundred and forty, and the lower four hundred and forty is the one I cannot put a hand on, and I did it with a nib and a cake of ink against my ribs and nothing in my hands at all, and I did it twice today.”

## What She Could Not Do In One Push

She rested on the thirteenth and the fourteenth. She took a reading of her own forearm on both of those days and it was the same to the width of a hair, and the ink she took up on the twelfth had not dried at all in two days, which she put her thumb in to be sure of, and which she had expected.

And on the fifteenth she went down the second stair.

She put the nib and the ink down on the stone at the near end of that table first, so that both her hands were empty, and she went down into it at the second hour, and there is no light on any part of that stair and there never has been and there is not one thing in this building she could carry up nine hundred steps to make one.

She counted the turns instead of the steps, because the turns are the only thing that goes wrong and she worked that out on the sixteenth of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four.

She came up out loud on each one. Sixty-four is a number she can hold and sixty-four is the number she counted that first time.

And at the sixty-fourth turn she stopped and sat down on a step in the dark with her back to the wall and her arm open in front of her and said the whole of it out loud, to a stair that has not got one thing to say back.

She said, “I have got sixty-four turns and nine runs that are long enough to count and the longest of them is a hundred and forty steps, and the other eight are between thirty and ninety, and that is everything I have and it is everything I had five months ago.”

She said, “And I cannot get to the bottom of that and come back in what I have left. I went down to the sixty-fourth turn tonight with nothing in my hands and no light on any of it and at the sixty-fourth turn I did not have the breath for the rest of it and I did not have it coming back up either, and I spent a count of twenty of the twenty I can do standing still on the way down.”

She said, “So I am not going down that stair for anything until I can do it in one push, and I am not going to try it in one push until I know how long it is, and those two things have to come in that order and there is not one instrument in this world that can do them at the same time.”

## The Sums On The Fifteenth

She did the sums at the ninth hour of the fifteenth, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table with the lamp at the far end of it twenty-six of her own hand below the top of that cage.

She said, “Two thousand four hundred and twenty-eight less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and sixty-nine days, and two thousand four hundred and twenty-eight less two thousand three hundred and one is a hundred and twenty-seven days in this room, and both of those are one more than they were yesterday and not one of them came off anything I did.”

She said, “And fifty-eight less twenty-six is thirty-two and thirty-two divided by two is sixteen and the sixteenth day from this one is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, and the figure at the head of my row is six days, sixteen hours and forty-eight minutes out of date and there is not one instrument above this line that would say so.”

She said, “And I have got a stub of a nib and a cake of ink against the fourth rib that have been in a bag at the bottom of that tower since before I could walk, and there is a bar nine feet nine against that wall that I am not carrying up or down anything, and there is a second stair under me with sixty-four turns of it that I cannot get to the bottom of, and there are sixteen days between this one and the first of a month.”

And then she said the last of it out loud and it is not a plan and it is not a promise and it is the first thing she has picked up off a floor in this building in a hundred and twenty-seven days.

She said, “There is a piece of hand-made paper in that bag nine inches long with eighty-one lines on it and the same sum under it in words, and there is a drawer thirty feet from where I was standing with a key in a bag I have left there on purpose, and I have not touched either of them and I am not going to this month.”

She said, “And the reason is not the nine hundred steps. The reason is that the four sheets of rice paper in that drawer have been blank for ten years and if I open it and they are not blank any more then that is gone, and I would rather find that out with a reason than on a day when I have already spent myself on a stair, because a page with a true line on it is the last thing in the world I have got that has not been written on in nineteen years.”

And then she stopped, and the lamp went on coming down that wall two of her own hand a day, and the hands at the near edge of that table went on doing the one thing they do.
