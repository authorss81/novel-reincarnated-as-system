# Nothing In Her Hands

She went down the tower’s own stair in the dark on the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen with nothing in her hands, and she came back up it with a stub of a nib and a cake of ink that has never let dry since the seventh of the month beginning one thousand eight hundred and seventy-four, and then she went down nine hundred steps of the second stair with no light at all and came back up, and she said out loud that she could not do it and that the decision is not changed.

## Four Hundred And Forty And Then Four Hundred And Forty

She went down at the second hour of the twelfth of that month with the low door standing open behind her and the stair in front of her going down into a kind of dark she knows, because she has been in a room with the light at one end of it for a hundred and twenty-four days.

Four hundred and forty with a rail on one side and her left hand on it. Then four hundred and forty with a wall four inches off her hand on one side and nothing at all on the other.

Her right arm has been open since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and it does not close and it was open that day, and she counted the whole of it out loud, one number at a time, because she has found out in a hundred and twenty-four days that the count is the only thing she has ever had that does not get lost on a stair.

At the turn on the second four she stopped for a count of thirty and put her back against the wall and listened, and there was nothing to listen to, and she said out loud, in the ordinary voice, in the dark:

“I have gone down this without a rail four times in nine years and I am not frightened of it and I have never once let myself know that I am not frightened of it.”

## The Fourth Landing

The lamp in a cage on that landing went out on the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and there has been no oil in this building for nine years, and she went past it on the way down and put her hand into the cage.

The iron was cold.

She said, out loud, standing on that landing with a boy’s hand on that cage and not her own, because a boy of sixteen keeps that lamp and has said he would keep it going after she stopped asking and was right to say it:

“This is the lamp he keeps going and there is nothing in it to keep going on.”

She said, “He has asked me twice why the lamp at the top of this tower is lit and I have not got an answer for him and he is a hundred and twenty-four days older than the last time he asked and he has not stopped.”

## The Bag And What Is In It

And at the bottom of that stair there is a bag and she has walked past it on the eleventh and gone down the second stair on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four with it at her feet, and she said out loud on that day that she was not going down for any of them, and she is not going to change that today.

She said it standing on that floor with the bag in front of her, in the ordinary voice, in the dark, to nobody, because the sixth hour of that day the hands at that table went out of the room and she was under it and she heard them come back up.

She said, “I decided on the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four that I was not going down for any of them and I said it again on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four with the bag at my feet and I have not changed my mind and I am not going to pretend today that I have.”

She said, “And that decision has never been about the nib or the ink. It has been about the drawer. A drawer is a decision and a nib is a thing. And I said that on the fourth of that month out loud in front of a boy of sixteen and I am saying it again here on my own so that there are two of us who know it.”

## What Was In It

She got the bag open with her left hand in the dark and put everything out on the floor of that copying floor, one thing at a time, and she named each of them out loud because she has named them before and she wants them to sound the same.

A dish. A lump of clay. A ring the width of a finger. A stub of a nib. A cake of ink opened on the seventh of the month beginning one thousand eight hundred and seventy-four and never let dry. A key to a drawer thirty feet from where she was sitting. And a strip of hand-made paper nine inches long with eighty-one lines on it and the same sum written under it in words.

She said, “That is nine things and not one of them is a day and I have said that out loud before and I am not going to improve on it.”

And then she picked two of them up off the floor and put them inside her shirt against the fourth rib, where the book of eight leaves is, and she did not pick up any of the other seven.

She said, “I am not re-deciding. I decided it in this month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and I decided it again in the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and what I am doing now is picking up the two that are not the drawer.”

## Up Eight Hundred And Eighty With No Rail

And she went back up eight hundred and eighty steps at the fifth hour of the twelfth of that month, in the dark, with a stub of a nib and a cake of ink against her ribs and nothing in her hands, and there is no rail on the second four hundred and forty and there is no light anywhere on that stair.

At four hundred and forty she sat down on a step for a count of twenty and put her forehead on her knees.

At six hundred and forty her breath went off nine and she stood still and did not go on until it was nine again.

And at the top of it, on the floor of that room, with the bar nine feet nine against the wall on the left of it and the person standing at the far end of that table with both hands on the near edge, she said out loud at the seventh hour of that day, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice:

“Four hundred and forty and four hundred and forty, and the top four hundred and forty is the one I cannot put a hand on and I did it anyway, and there is not one person in this building who would have known that I had.”

## Down The Second Stair With No Light On It

And on the fifteenth of that month she went down the second stair.

She put the nib and the ink on the stone at the near end of that table first, so that her hands were empty, and she went down into it at the second hour and there is no light on any part of that stair and there never has been and there is not one thing she could carry to make one.

She counted out loud the whole way. She counted the steps and the turns together the way she worked out on the seventeenth of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, one, two, three, and turn, four, five, six, and turn, and she got past nine hundred and at nine hundred she expected the number to go.

It did not go. She got to nine hundred and eleven and then she was going back up because she could not get her breath at all.

She sat down on a step in the dark at the ninth hour of the fifteenth of that month with her back to the wall and her arm open in front of her and said the whole of it out loud, in the ordinary voice, to a stair that has not got one thing to say back.

She said, “I cannot get to the bottom of that and come back in what I have left.”

She said, “I went down nine hundred and eleven steps of it tonight with nothing in my hands and no light on any of them and at nine hundred and eleven I did not have the breath for the rest of it and I did not have it on the way up either, and I have got a count of twenty out of eleven that I can do standing still and I spent that on the way down.”

She said, “So I am not going down it for anything until I can do it in one push, and I am not going to do it in one push until I know how long it is, and I do not know how long it is, and that is the whole of the order of those two things.”

## The Sums And What Is Left Of The Month

And she did the sums on the sixteenth of that month standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table with the lamp at the far end of it and the boards under it.

She said, “Two thousand four hundred and twenty-eight less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and sixty-nine days, and two thousand four hundred and twenty-eight less two thousand three hundred and one is a hundred and twenty-seven days, and both of those are one more than they were yesterday and not one of them came off anything I did.”

She said, “And fifty-eight less twenty-six is thirty-two and thirty-two divided by two is sixteen and the sixteenth day from this one is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, and the figure at the head of my row is six days, sixteen hours and forty-eight minutes out of date, and there is no instrument above this line that could tell me so.”

She said, “And I have got a stub of a nib and a cake of ink against the fourth rib that I picked up off a floor four hundred and forty steps down on the twelfth of this month, and there is a bar nine feet nine against the wall on the left of that room that I am not carrying up or down anything, and there is a second stair with nine hundred and eleven steps of it under me that I cannot get to the bottom of, and there are seven days between this one and the first of a month.”
