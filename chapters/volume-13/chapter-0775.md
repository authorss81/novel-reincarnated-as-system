# The Sixty-Third Day

She said the six terms out loud in that room on the eleventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four, standing up, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, one at a time, for the first time in two hundred and thirteen days, and on the eighteenth of that month she worked out that the fourth of them is the reason she is going to refuse, and she did not know that when she said it.

## Six Terms In The Room Again

She said the first one out loud at the ninth hour of the eleventh of that month, standing at the near end of that long table, and it came out the way it has always come out.

She said, “One thing at a time.”

And then she said the other five, one at a time, standing up, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, and it took a count of ninety and the hand at the far end of that table did not move and did not look up and did not stop working, because a hand writes what a person said when a person says a figure and there was not one figure in the six of them.

She said the fourth one out loud again on its own afterwards, standing in daylight, because it is the one that does the work:

“You say it out loud as you go. Every line. Before it is a line anywhere else.”

And she said the sixth one out loud again on its own after that, and her voice went a different way on it and she noticed that it did:

“It is a record. It is not a hand. You do not decide anything. I decide everything, and the only thing you have is that it is written down.”

And there was not a seventh one and she did not ask for one.

## The Two Hundred And Thirteen Days In Between

She said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table on the eleventh, with a person standing nine feet off along it:

“I have kept every one of those six in this room every single hour for two hundred and thirteen days and I have not said them out loud in here once.”

She said, “And I did not know that was a difference until this morning, and I have been working it out since the ninth hour and it took me most of the day, and what it is is this.”

She said, “The sixth term is that I decide everything. And I have not said the sixth term out loud in this room since the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, and on that morning I said a man’s name out loud in here after he asked me not to.”

She said, “And what I have been doing for two hundred and thirteen days without saying it is deciding.”

She said, “Not out loud. That is the whole of it. I have been deciding one thing a day in this room for two hundred and thirteen days and not one of them has been in the air.”

## The Fourth Term Against A Name

And on the eighteenth of that month she worked out what the fourth of the six is for, and it took her from the fifth hour to the ninth and she did not eat and she did not go down either stair.

A line is a figure. A figure goes into a column. A column is on a piece of wood or on a board or in a row.

The fourth term says that every line has to be said out loud as she goes, before it is a line anywhere else, and that if there is one she has not heard first then it is not a record, it is a thing somebody did on their own, and there is no version of this in which she finds out about it afterwards.

She said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table on the eighteenth:

“The amendment would put a figure in a column in a row on a piece of wood and that figure has not been said out loud in this room and I have not heard it first.”

She said, “And that is what the fourth term is for. That is the exact case it was made for, and it was made for in nineteen years ago about a man and it is being used today about a column, and it does not care what it is about.”

She said, “And I have been escaping being counted since I was nine years old and I put a five in that column myself on the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four because I wanted to be counted by something that could not count.”

She said, “And the room has offered me a way of taking it out again and the room is being kind to me about it and every part of it is true, and the fourth of the six terms says I cannot take a line I have not heard.”

And she stood there for a count of ninety with her hand on the edge of that table and then she said the thing that is going to cost the light.

She said, “And the room said it has never amended a row. And there is no instrument in the world that amends a row twice. So if I do it there is one name in that column forever and I do not get to check it once.”

## Holding Still On The Wall

On the nineteenth she went out into that room and stood in the middle of the floor of it, which is the middle of nothing, and she put her hands down flat at her sides and she held still the way the person at that table holds still.

And she got a count of eleven out of it.

Her breath did not go off nine on the fourth count. Her weight stayed on both feet. She did not put a hand on anything. And the light came down that wall two of her own hand a day while she did it and did not lean in a draught and did not stop.

She did it again on the twentieth and on the twenty-first and she got the same figure every time, and on the twenty-first she got it four times in a row with a count of forty in between each one and she was still standing there at the ninth hour when she stopped.

She said out loud, standing in the middle of the floor of that room, and it is the only time in this building that she has said anything about herself that she has not said out loud twice:

“I have not been able to do that since the fifth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four and I worked out on that day why I could not, and I was right about the reason and wrong about how long it would take.”

She said, “Being looked at is what holds a person still. And there is not one thing in this building looking at me, and it turns out that is not the only way.”

And then she said the rest of it and she said it into the middle of that floor and she said it slowly.

She said, “I have been standing still in front of boards for two hundred and eighteen days and boards do not do it. I have been standing still in front of a lamp for two hundred and eighteen days and a lamp does not do it. And I have just done it four times in a row on a stone floor with nothing in the room but a person who is not looking at me and a light going down a wall.”

She said, “And the only difference is that I have something to be still for.”

## Why Sixty-Three And Not Fifty-Eight

And on the twenty-first she worked out why the next one comes on the twenty-first of this month and not on some other day, and she said it out loud at the near end of that table at the ninth hour, as arithmetic and not as a lesson, because that is how she says every figure in this room.

She said, “The fifth stage was taken on the eighteenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, which is the day two thousand four hundred and sixty-one, at the tenth hour, off a board, with a nib, in my own hand. The fourth was taken on the twentieth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, which is the day two thousand four hundred and three.”

She said, “Two thousand four hundred and three to two thousand four hundred and sixty-one is fifty-eight. And two thousand four hundred and sixty-one to the twenty-first of this month is sixty-three.”

She said, “And I have not worked out a rule for fifty-eight and I did not work out a rule for a hundred and thirteen before it, and I am not going to work out a rule for sixty-three tonight.”

She said, “Two thousand five hundred and twenty-three less two thousand three hundred and one is two hundred and twenty-two, and the only thing I have counted every single one of them is days. And it turns out that counting a thing every day is not the same as knowing when it happens.”

She said, “And what I have instead of a rule is that I can hold still on a stone floor with nothing in the room, and I could not do that on the twentieth of that month and I could not do it on the eighteenth of this one, and on the twenty-first I could do it four times.”

She said, “And I am going to take the sixth stage off that table in the morning at the ninth hour, standing, in daylight, on a board, with a nib, out of my own hand, the way I have taken every one of them, because there is no one in this building who can give it to me and no board above the line that has my name on it and I have not got the use of a hand that is whole.”

And she said the last of that day out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table, with her right arm open in front of her and the light at the far end of that room thirty-six of her own hand below the top of that cage.

She said, “And I have not said one word to that room about what I am going to do about the name in the column, and that day is in this month, and I am not going to say one word about it before then.”
