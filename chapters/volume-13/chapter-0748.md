# Two Words In An Ordinary Voice

She said the two words out loud at the tenth hour of the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, standing on the floor at the near end of that long table in a room with a person in it, and there is no instrument in the world that takes a name out of a row that has been above the line for four hundred years.

## Four Times The Hands Were Not There

She was under that table five times in six days and on four of those the hands at that table were not there when she came out, and she did not understand what she was looking at until the third one.

On the twenty-third of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four she went under there at the third hour to read the strips and she came out at about the fourth and both hands were on the near edge of that table.

On the twenty-fifth she went under at the seventh hour and came out and a lamp in a cage on a shelf at the far end of that room has never once gone out and it was burning and both hands were on the near edge.

On the twenty-sixth she went under at the second hour and came out and the near edge of that table was bare.

And she did not think anything of it. She went along that table on her hands and knees with her breath at nine and she did the sums out loud against the wall behind that lamp and she did not look at the near edge again.

On the twenty-seventh she went under at the second hour and came out and the near edge was bare again, and she stood on the stone four feet off that table for a count of twenty before she could tell what it was.

It was that she could hear the room differently. It was quieter and it had a shape to it that it does not have with a person in it.

She said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the second hour of the twenty-seventh of that month, alone:

“That person has gone out of this room.”

She said, “And the low door has been standing open the whole time and there is not one light on any of that stair, and I have been twenty feet under a table the entire while it happened.”

And at the ninth hour the hands were on the near edge of that table again and the head was down over it and nothing had happened at all.

She went and looked at the floor and there was no mark on it and no dust disturbed on it, and there is not one mark in this room that says a thing was taken out of it or put into it.

And on the twenty-eighth she went under at the ninth hour and came out and they were not there, and on the twenty-ninth she went under at the seventh hour and came out and they were not there either.

Four times.

She said it out loud once, on the twenty-ninth of that month, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice:

“Four times in four days and I was under that table every one of them and I do not know where that person goes and I am not going to follow them down that stair and I am not going to stand in that doorway when they come back up it.”

She said, “A room that is expecting somebody cannot be met by me, and that is one of the fourteen, and I have not said it out loud in nineteen years and it has just cost me four times in four days.”

## The Tenth Hour Of The Twenty-Eighth

On the twenty-sixth of that month she did the sums, and she did them again at the ninth hour on the twenty-seventh, and on the twenty-eighth she stood up at the tenth hour and said the whole of it.

She said it standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, one item at a time, in front of a person at a table who had not looked up once in a hundred and ten days.

She said, “I am nineteen years old.”

She said, “I have three fingers and a thumb on my right hand and my right arm is open and has been open since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and it is the arm I am writing with and I am writing open.”

She said, “I breathe four counts everywhere and seven on a rise and nine standing still at the top of eight flights, and I had seven of eleven holds this morning and I have the same now.”

She said, “There is a small open place low behind my fourth rib the size of a coin and it opened on the eighteenth of the month beginning one thousand five hundred and forty-four.”

She said, “I am four stages into the seventh realm and I took the fourth of them off that table myself, standing, on the twentieth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, because there was no row to hand it to.”

She said, “I am eight hundred and fifty-two days lighter than I was on the eighteenth of the month beginning one thousand five hundred and forty-four, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and one day of it went into that lamp on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and did not come back.”

She said, “And there is a voice in me that has been with me nine years and I have carried its name against my ribs for ten years in four capital strokes in my own hand, and it has one name and it is not mine.”

## One Question In The Ordinary Voice

And he asked her not to.

He said, in the ordinary voice, standing, in daylight, in the ten seconds before she said it, and it was the first time in nineteen years that anything in her head had spoken to her while there was a person in the room:

“Is there a way to do this that does not have me in it.”

And that was one question and it was the whole of what he had said for nineteen years and it was the only thing he said before she said it, and she did not answer it.

## Two Words

She said them.

She said, “Yuan Sheng.”

Two words, out loud, standing up, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, on the floor at the near end of that long table in a room four miles above a mountain where things are decided by a hand and the hand is at the other end of it and there is no second look.

And the hands came off the near edge of that table.

She had watched that happen twice before now and both of those times there had been forty boards or a bundle of papers in front of the person.

There was nothing in front of the person.

The hands went along under the shelf where the boards are stacked and one came out and it was not the top one, and she did not see which one it was, and it came up onto the wood.

And a stroke went onto it. And then something else went onto it.

And she stood four feet off the end of that table with her arm open at her side and her breath at nine and she could not see the board from where she was standing and she was not going to go and get it.

The hands went back onto the near edge of that table and the head came down over it and the standing went on exactly as it had been going on before.

And there is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain who asked her on the way down eight hundred and eighty steps what a row was for, and she did not answer him then, and she has not got an answer now either, and she is not going to be able to give him one at any point in this.

## What He Said And What It Costs

And he did not ask her a second time.

And he did not hide it.

He said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, standing, in daylight, in that room:

“That is the first time in nineteen years that you have put me in something that is not your own book.”

She said, “Yes.”

He said, “I asked you once and you did it anyway and I am not going to ask you twice. That is mine to decide and I have decided it and I am telling you what it is so that you have got it.”

She said, “Say what it is.”

He said, “There is a thing that reads records. It wanted a shape with a hand on it for four hundred years and your bill was settled on the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and it has not wanted a name until now.”

He said, “A name is easier to find than a shape. A shape is a shape. A name is a string that goes in a mouth and a mouth is the only thing in the world that carries a name anywhere at all, and you have just put one in a record four miles above a mountain in a building where a hand copies figures exactly and does not decide anything.”

He said, “And the thing that reads records does not speak when it finds something. That is the whole of what I am telling you and I am telling it to you standing up in daylight in a count of about forty because you said it out loud and I am not going to stand here and let you find out on your own.”

She said, “Then it will find it.”

He said, “Yes.”

She said, “How long.”

He said, “I do not know. I have never had one in a record above the line and I have had one in a book with you for nine years and nothing has ever come for either of us, and there is not one figure in this world I can give you for it.”

## The Six Terms Said Out Loud In That Room

And then she said the six terms, in that room, as she has said them in every room she has ever said them in, standing up, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, one at a time, and he did not interrupt her once.

She said, “One thing at a time.”

She said, “In daylight.”

She said, “Standing up.”

She said, “You say it out loud as you go. Every line. Before it is a line anywhere else.”

She said, “If there is one I have not heard first then it is not a record, it is a thing you did on your own, and there is no version of this where I find out about it later.”

She said, “And if you put down one word that is not true, I will know it inside a day, and there is no road back for either of us.”

She said, “And it is a record. It is not a hand. You do not decide anything. I decide everything, and the only thing you have is that it is written down.”

And then she stopped, in a room with a person in it, with two words of his in a board on that table that she could not see.

And there was not a seventh one and she did not ask for one.

## What It Is Now

She did the sums at the eleventh hour of that day, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, because she has never once in her life let a day go past without putting its figures somewhere other than in her head.

She said, “Two thousand four hundred and eleven less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and fifty-two days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and that is the same figure I said out loud at the tenth hour of this morning, and it has been one more every day since and it did not come off anything when I said his name.”

She said, “And the figure on the strip under that table is five days, twenty hours and twenty-four minutes out of date and there is not one person in this building who could ever know.”

She said, “And the light at the far end of this room is fifty-two of my own hand below the top of that cage, and fifty-eight less fifty-two is six, and six divided by two is three, and the third day from this one is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen, which is the day the next forty things come up that stair.”

She said, “And I have said his name out loud in a room where a hand copies figures exactly, on purpose, knowing what a room does with an entry, and there is no instrument in this building or in this county or in the whole of the world that takes a line out of a record that has been above the line for four hundred years.”

She said, “And I did not do it for him.”

She said, “And he did not ask me twice.”
