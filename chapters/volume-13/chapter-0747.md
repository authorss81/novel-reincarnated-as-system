# What A Row Has To Say

She worked out on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four what she is going to have to say out loud in that room, standing on the floor with a bar nine feet nine against the wall behind her, and she did not say it.

## What She Is, Said Out Loud, Standing Up

She said the whole of it out loud on the twenty-second of that month, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the seventh hour, in front of a person at a table who has not looked up once in a hundred and four days.

She said, “I am nineteen years old. There is no date on that and there is no place on it and I am saying it standing up in this room and it is the first time in nineteen years I have said my age out loud to a person who could write it down.”

She said, “I have three fingers and a thumb on my right hand. The index went in the first year of my life and the little finger went in the middle of a line on a slate on the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and it did not finish the line.”

She said, “My right arm is open and it has been open every day since that first and it is not going to close, and it is the arm I wrote a figure on yesterday, and I wrote it open.”

She said, “I breathe four counts everywhere and seven on a rise and nine standing still at the top of eight flights, and it has been that since before I could count and it is permanent.”

She said, “I had eight of eleven holds this morning before I got on that table and I have one less now.”

She said, “There is a small open place low behind my fourth rib the size of a coin and it opened on the eighteenth of the month beginning one thousand five hundred and forty-four and it has not closed in eight hundred and twenty-four days.”

She said, “I am lighter by a figure that is the day less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine and it has been one hour and twelve minutes a day the whole time and not one of them going back, and one of them went into a lamp on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and did not come back.”

She said, “I am not hungry and I have not been since before I could count.”

She said, “And I am four stages into the seventh realm, and that is a figure too, and I wrote it on a board in this room yesterday in my own hand.”

## A Row Has To Say What A Thing Is

And then she stopped, on the twenty-second, and did not say the rest of it for two days.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“A row has to say what a thing is. That is not an opinion. That is the only shape the thing has. Every one of the four strips under that table has a figure at the head of it, and on mine the figure in the column is the figure at the head, and I do not know what is in the columns of the other three and I am not going to guess.”

She said, “And a figure is not what a thing is. A figure is what a thing costs. I worked that out on the tenth of this month when I said one number out loud in this room and a hand wrote it on a board, and it is the whole of what that room has ever wanted off me.”

She said, “So the price is not a row. The price is the head of a row. And the column is something else and I have to find out what it is before I can say anything at all.”

## What A Figure Is And What A Name Is

On the twenty-third she worked out the two things that are available to her to put in a column, and she said both of them out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “There are two kinds of thing I could say in this room that are not a price.”

She said, “One of them is a name. A name goes in a column and the row knows what the thing is, and I have known that since I was nine years old, because a name has had a fifth of it taken every day since the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning two thousand and fifty-four and it is still in a column and still going.”

She said, “And a name is not free and it does not stop. One in twenty of every day comes off a name, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and I have been paying that on my own name for eight hundred and twenty-four days and it has not once been entered.”

She said, “And the other one is a sentence. And a sentence is what a column is not for, and a hand that copies figures exactly is not going to put a sentence in a column and hand it back to me.”

## The Voice Against Her Ribs

And on the twenty-fourth, at the sixth hour, she got to the third thing, and she said it out loud standing, in the ordinary voice, in front of a bar nine feet nine that she left against the wall on the left of that room on purpose, and she did not say it out loud again.

She said, “And there is a voice. It has been counted with me for nine years and it asked me for a pen on the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand one hundred and fourteen in a ground with two hundred people in it, and it says everything out loud before it is a line anywhere else, and there are six terms and there is no seventh and the sixth one says it is a record and it is not a hand and it does not decide anything and I decide everything.”

She said, “And it has exactly one name in it that belongs in a record, and it is not mine, and it is on the third leaf of a book of eight leaves made out of a blanket that has been against my ribs for ten years in four capital strokes in my own hand, and I wrote it when I was nine because a thing in capitals is a thing that cannot be got at.”

## What Happens To Six Terms In A Room Like This

And then she said the part that made her sit down on the floor, and she said it out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, and she said it once and she has not improved on it.

She said, “A hand at that table copies figures exactly and it is not allowed to decide anything and neither is a man with a pen saying six terms out loud before he writes a line.”

She said, “And that is not a resemblance. That is the same shape, and I have been standing in two rooms this month and been handed the same thing in both of them and been told in both of them that it does not decide.”

She said, “And the six terms do not survive this room.”

She said, “They survive two people who said them out loud standing up in daylight in a ground with stones in it. They do not survive a hand. There is nowhere in a column for one of them to go, and there is not one figure in the world that means in daylight and there is not one figure in the world that means standing up, and if I say them in this room they are four sentences about nothing in a place where a person writes figures.”

She said, “And the fifth one cannot be held by anybody who is not the two of us and there is no version of it that binds a hand, and I have known that since the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand one hundred and fourteen and I am not going to say it again here.”

## The Day She Had Not Got Yet

She stood there on the twenty-fifth with her back against that bar and she worked out, out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, what she is going to have to say in that room, and she said the shape of it and not the thing itself.

She said, “A row has to say what a thing is, and what I am is not a figure and it is not a name, and there are nineteen sentences of it standing in front of me and not one of them is the one.”

She said, “And there is one thing in me that has a name in it and a row can only say a voice by the name that is in it, and I have got two words, and I have had them against my ribs for ten years, and I have never once said them out loud in a room where a person could write them down.”

She said, “And I am not going to say them on the twenty-sixth, or the twenty-seventh, or the twenty-eighth, or the twenty-ninth of this month, and I do not know yet which day I am going to say them on.”

She said, “And the boy of sixteen came up eight hundred and eighty steps to ask me one question and I gave him a number, and a hand that copies figures exactly is not going to take his word for one word of it, and there is not one thing I could do with him afterwards that would make it true that I said it, and I am not going to spend one of those four days finding out whether he would.”

She said, “And I have got six days to the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen and forty things are going to come up that stair on it, and I am not going to know a day until I know a day, and that is the first time in nineteen years I have said out loud that I do not know a day and meant it.”

## The Sums She Did Anyway

She did them standing in front of the wall with the bar behind her, because she does the sums whether or not she has got anything else.

She said, “Two thousand four hundred and eight less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and forty-nine days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and not one of them going back.”

She said, “And the figure on the strip under that table is five days, sixteen hours and forty-eight minutes out of date.”

She said, “And the light at the far end of this room is forty-six of my own hand below the top of that cage, and fifty-eight less forty-six is twelve, and twelve divided by two is six, and the sixth day from this one is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen, which is the day the next forty things come up that stair and the day that lamp stands on that line with nothing above it and nothing below it for the second time in four hundred years.”

She said, “And I am not going to have said a word out loud in this room by then, and that is mine and not anybody’s, and there is not one person four miles down that mountain who is going to be able to tell me whether that was the right call, and there is no person in this room who would know what the right call was.”
