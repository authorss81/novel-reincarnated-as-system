# Twenty-Nine Days To The Mark

She did the sum on the twenty-second of the month beginning 2,324, standing on the floor of the room with her back to the door and her feet on the stone, and she did it four times, and it came out the same four times.

## The Sum She Could Not Do Any Other Way

Fifty-eight of her own hand from the top of that cage to the cut in the wall.

Two of her own hand a day.

Fifty-eight divided by two is twenty-nine.

She did it in the ordinary voice, out loud, standing, with a count of a hundred between each of the four, and she did it twice by putting fifty-eight into twenty-nines and twice by putting two-nines together, and she got twenty-nine all four times and she did not believe it until she had done it twice.

She said, out loud, at the fourth hour of the twenty-second of the month beginning 2,324:

“Twenty-nine days from the top of that cage to that line in the wall, and a month is thirty days, and a lamp in this building was at the top of that cage on the first of this month when the forty things came in.”

She said, “And so the light in that cage reaches the line cut in the wall on the thirtieth of this month, and today is the twenty-second, and that is eight days.”

She said it out loud standing up and then she stood there and let it be a thing in the room.

## The Reason She Could Not Count It Between Two Turnings

And then she said out loud the reason she had to do it that way round, because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

She said, “I have seen that lamp turned once. On the first of this month, in the ten minutes before the forty things came in, and it was at the top of that cage after and it had not been at the top of that cage before.”

She said, “And you cannot get an interval out of one turning.”

She said, “And there is no interval to get, either, because the room counts how often that lamp is turned in hands and I count in days, and a hand turns a lamp as often as it has to and no hand has ever written down how often that is, so there is no figure anywhere in this building that I could copy out of a book and use.”

## What A Day Of Slack Would Look Like

And then she spent the whole of the twenty-third of the month beginning 2,324 working out what a day of slack would actually be, standing on the floor of that room with her back to the open door, and she got it out loud and it took her most of the day.

She said, “A day of slack is the thirtieth of a month, and it is a day like any other day, and there is nothing about it on the inside of me and nothing about it on the outside of me and I would not know it had come.”

She said, “And on that day the light in that cage is standing on a line cut in a wall and it is not going any further, and if nothing turns it on the first of the next month then it stays on that line until it does.”

She said, “And that is a room with a lamp in it that has not got enough in it for one day, on one day in every month, for four hundred years, and the whole of the cost of that has been carried by the person at that table and by whoever came before them at it and not by anything in this room.”

She said, “And I am not going to be able to find out what happened on any of those four hundred days and I am not going to be able to find out whether anybody ever worked it out, and there is not one mark on that wall but the line and there is not one mark anywhere in this room that says a day went by unused.”

And then she stood there and let that be in it, and then she said the thing she actually had come to say about it, and it is the first thing in four days that was not arithmetic.

She said, “I have been lighter every day since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544, and there has never once been a day I could have lost and not lost, and I have never once in nineteen years been in a place where there was a day going by and I could not reach it.”

She said, “And I am not thinking about that.”

She said, “I am going to stand there and finish the sum I did yesterday and I am not going to think about that.”

## The Second Sum Standing Up

And on the twenty-fourth and the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 2,324 she did the sum standing up with her feet on the floor, four times, and she got twenty-nine every time, and on the fourth one she did it the other way round and got it out of twenty-nines and got twenty-nine.

Then she put the other half of it out loud, which is the part with a date in it.

She said, “A lamp that was at the top of that cage on the first of this month reaches that line on the twenty-ninth day after it. That is the twenty-ninth day of a month of thirty days, which is the thirtieth.”

She said, “Two thousand three hundred and twenty-four plus twenty-nine is two thousand three hundred and fifty-three, and two thousand three hundred and fifty-three is the thirtieth of the month beginning 2,324, and today is the twenty-fifth, and the difference is five days.”

She said, “And the next forty things come up that stair on the first of the month beginning 2,354, which is the day after, and I do not have to do anything about any of this at all, because the room will turn that lamp when the room turns it.”

She said, “And I have five days and there is nothing I am going to do with five days, and I am not going to be here on the twenty-eighth.”

## One Day In Every Month

And then she worked out what twenty-nine is, standing, in the ordinary voice, and it is the whole of what she found and it took her a count of a hundred and forty.

She said, “Twenty-nine days from the first of a month is the thirtieth. That is the last day of a month of thirty days.”

She said, “And a lamp that is at the top of its cage on the first is at that line on the thirtieth, and the next turning is on the first of the next month, because that is the day this room is given forty things on and a hand turns it as soon as they are read.”

She said, “Which means that for one day in every month, that lamp is sitting at the line cut in the wall with nothing above it and nothing below it, and there is not one thing in that day.”

She said, “And there has not been one of those days used for four hundred years, and there has not been one of those days missed either, and the whole of what that means is that there is a day in every month that has never once been spent.”

She stood there with it.

She said, “That is the first thing I have found in this room that is a thing I could do and not do.”

## Twenty-Nine Against Thirty, Out Loud Twice

And she did it again on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 2,324 standing, in the ordinary voice, at the ninth hour, with a bar against the wall on the left and a person at the far end of the table, because she has never in her life put a number in a room once.

She said, “Fifty-eight of my own hand from the top of that cage to that cut in the wall.”

She said, “Two of my own hand a day.”

She said, “Fifty-eight divided by two is twenty-nine, and a month is thirty, and one in thirty days in every month goes by with that lamp standing on that line and nothing turning it.”

She said, “And the whole of what I have found out in forty-eight days in this building is that there is one day in every month in it that has never once been spent, and I am the only person who knows it, and I have got no hand to spend it with that is not the hand I have got.”

And then she said the reason she was not going to do anything about it, and she said it as arithmetic and not as a feeling, because it is the only way she is able to say anything at all.

She said, “Because the last time I put a true number where a hand could reach it, it went up a stair in a bundle with about forty other boards and it is the only figure about me in this building and it has been out of date since the first of the month beginning 2,294 and there is not one person in this room who could tell me.”

She said, “And I am not putting another one anywhere until I know what the first one is for.”

## Why She Did Not Say It To The Room

And then she said why she was not going to say any of it to a person, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the sixth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,324, in a room with a person in it.

She said, “That lamp has been run on twenty-nine days in a month of thirty since before anybody alive was born and the room has never once been late.”

She said, “And it is not late. A hand turns it as often as it has to. And if a woman stood at that table and told a room of correct people that their lamp is a day short and there is a day going by unused, then the only thing that would have happened is that a room which has not looked at me since the first of this month would have had a reason to.”

She said, “And I am not going to buy a row with a sentence that is a guess about a lamp.”

## What She Cannot Add

And then she said the part that has been standing in her way since the seventeenth of the month beginning 2,324 and it is the part she has not got an answer to.

She said, “There are two counts in this room and I can do both of them and I cannot add them.”

She said, “Twenty-nine is how many days the light lasts from the top of the cage to that line, and that is a number of days and it is a number I can put my hand on. And a hand turns that lamp as often as it has to, and that is a number of hands, and it has not been written down anywhere in four hundred years and it is not going to be written down by me because there is nothing in this room to write with and a hand does not stop to be asked.”

She said, “And if a hand turns that lamp tomorrow instead of waiting eight days for the line, then twenty-nine is not the number, and it was never the number, and it is only the number of days of light in there and that is a different thing from how often.”

She sat down on the floor at the near end of the table with her back against the doorframe.

And the light at the far end of that room went on coming down two of her own hand a day, and she had the twenty-nine, and it is the first number she has worked out in this room that she cannot do anything with, and she put it nowhere, and it is not in five blank leaves against her ribs.
