# Nothing In It She Can Count

She counted the room for four days, on the twelfth, the thirteenth, the fourteenth and the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,294, and it took her all four of them, and here is the whole of what she got.

## Eight Things

One table. One lamp in a cage on a shelf. One stub of candle in a dish with a grey bloom of cold wax in the bottom of it. One door, standing open the width of a person against the inside wall on the right. One bar on the floor against the wall on the left. One stair going up out of the far side. One person standing at the far end of the table. And one floor.

That is eight things and she has counted eight things in a room in nineteen years that had two hundred people in it.

The floor she did in paces on the twelfth. Nine feet from the inside of the door to the first step of the second stair, and the room is a shade over six feet across at the near end, and the table is twenty-two paces long, and she could not get the far end of it because the far end of that room is dark and the dark does not have an edge in it.

The table she did on the thirteenth. It stands four feet off the floor. She knows that because she can see the underside of it from where she is standing and the underside is above her shoulder, and she has never in her life not been able to reach a table and she has not been able to get a hand on the edge of this one.

## A Person Does Not Go In A Column

On the fourteenth she tried to put the person at the table into a number and it would not go.

She has counted two hundred people in a ground of bare earth and she did it standing at the north end of it, and the two hundred were in a column and were not in front of her, and that was a different thing from this and she has known it since she was ten years old and had never had to do it in a room.

There is no way to count a person who is standing six feet from you with their hands on a table. You can count their breaths, and she did that on the fourteenth for a count of three hundred, and it came to four to a minute and did not change once and she does not know what that is a measure of.

She said it out loud on the fourteenth, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of the table:

“I have put two hundred people in a column and four men in a column and a column on the other side of a piece of wood that faces a wall, and I have never once had to put a person in a column who was standing in the room with me, and that is the first thing in this room my counting has not done.”

She said, “And it is not going to do it. There is not one arrangement of this that I can find, and I have had two days to find one and I am not going to spend four.”

## The Door Has Nothing On It

On the fifteenth she went and stood in the open door and looked at everything that was in it, because in nineteen years she has marked a day by putting a mark on something, and if there is a thing in this room that carries a date then she has a date.

The door is a slab of wood with a hinge on one side and no latch and no bar across the outside of it any more. There is no nail in it. There is no scrape on the floor in front of it. There is no light coming into this room from anywhere at all, because the lamp in the cage at the far end of the table is the only light that has ever been in here.

She stood in the doorway for a count of a hundred and looked at the whole of it.

There is no mark on this door of any kind, and there has never been one, and she has put her eye to that door nine times in thirty days and looked at the same nine feet of floor and the same table and the same lamp and has never once seen anything that told her anything.

## The Stub Of Candle In The Dish

She went to the stub of candle on the fourteenth, because it is the only object in that room that is not furniture and not a person and not a bar, and she has been putting an eye to it through a grille nine times in thirty days.

It is about two inches of candle in a dish of grey clay, and there is one grey bloom of cold wax in the bottom of the dish, and the wax bloom is not soft and has not been soft for a long time.

There is no wick. It has been out for a very long time and not by burning, because the dish has no scorch in it and no black, and the bloom of cold wax in the bottom of it is the shape a wax pool makes when a thing goes out of it and is not disturbed after.

She said it out loud on the fourteenth at the fifth hour, in the ordinary voice, standing over a dish on a table she cannot reach, from below:

“That has been out since before there was a first of a month I know about, and it is not in this room to be used and it is not in this room to be looked at, and there is no wick in it and no pair of snuffers and no wick-hook and no rag, and there is not one thing in this building that has been made to put a light out.”

She said, “And there is not one thing in this building that has been made to put a light in, either, and there is a lamp burning at the other end of this room.”

## How She Keeps The Day

And then she did the thing that took her the whole of the fifteenth, which is to work out how she has been keeping the day in a room where the day does not turn, and she found out that she has been keeping it by sleeping, and that sleeping is not a thing she does much.

She has not been hungry since a year she can remember and being lighter has taken the wanting out of food and out of sleep and out of four other things. She sleeps in pieces. She has a count of a hundred lying on a stone floor and a count of four hundred standing at a table and a count of two hundred sitting with her back against a doorframe, and one of those ends in nothing for a while and the other two do not.

So she worked out that if she goes a whole day without sleeping she has still got the day, because she has got the count. And if she goes a whole day without sleeping and without counting, then she has not got the day at all, and there is nothing in this room that will tell her that it went.

She said it out loud at the seventh hour of the fifteenth of the month beginning 2,294:

“I have been marking a day by putting a mark on something since I was nine years old. There is not one thing in this room to put a mark on and there is not one person in it to say the day to and I am going to have to keep the day myself with a count.”

She said, “And I have got three counts and I can do about four hundred of them in a day and I cannot sleep, and so I have got the day. And that is the only reason I have not lost track, and it is the same reason I have not lost track of anything since I was nine.”

## Nobody Above This Line Loses A Day

And then she worked out the rest of it, standing in the open door at the second hour of the fifteenth, and she worked it out out loud because that is the only way she has ever got a thing to stay still long enough to be looked at.

She said, “I have counted days since I was nine years old by counting nights, and I count them because every one of them has taken a piece off me and I need to know how many pieces there are.”

She said, “Two thousand three hundred and five less one thousand five hundred and sixty is seven hundred and forty-five, and every one of those days cost me one hour and twelve minutes, and that is not a figure in a column somewhere. That is me. It is the only number in this building that changes, and it changes on my side of the room.”

She said, “And the reason there is not one mark on that door is not that the people in this building do not care what day it is. It is that nobody above this line loses a day. No one above this line loses anything at all. There is not one person in this room who gets lighter, and that is why days are not counted above this line, because you do not count what is not being taken off you.”

She stood in the door with that.

She said, “I have been standing in a building full of people who do not count days for four days and I did not know that was what I had walked into, and I counted that room nine times through a grille and worked out that there was nothing in it to count, and there was a person at the far end of it and a lamp going down and a mark on a cage and a figure in a row, and none of the four is on my side of the room.”

## What She Is Missing

And then she said the thing she wants, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the second hour of the fifteenth of a month in a room with eight things in it.

She said, “I want a second thing in this room that changes while I am in it.”

She said, “I have got one. I have been standing in it for four days watching a light come down off a lamp and I have not got an instrument for it yet and I am going to have one, and that is the whole of what I came up a stair for and it is not even the half of it.”

She said, “And I cannot see the day turn. I have not seen it turn in four days and I am going to go on not seeing it turn, and I am going to keep losing it anyway at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and the day this lamp is dark is a day I will have lost and will not be able to tell anybody about.”

She went and sat down on the floor with her back against the doorframe and her right arm open across her knees, and the one at the table did not look up, and the lamp in the cage went on doing the thing she cannot yet measure.
