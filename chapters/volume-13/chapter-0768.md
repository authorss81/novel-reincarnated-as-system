# Thirty Feet From A Copying Floor

On the fourteenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four she went down the low door and stood in front of a drawer with a lock on it thirty feet from a copying floor with four hundred volumes on the shelves, and she measured it, and she did not open it, and she had already said out loud on the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four that she was not going to.

## Thirty Feet From A Copying Floor

The drawer is in the wall at the end of that floor and the lock on it has not been turned in ten years. There are four sheets of rice paper in it and five blank leaves, and the five blank leaves have been blank for ten years, and she has never found out what is on the four sheets.

She measured the drawer on the sixteenth because she had nothing else to do with two hours and a hand, and what she got was that it is two feet along the wall and about five inches deep and the wood in the front of it has gone grey where a hand goes.

She put her fingers on the grey and stopped for a while.

And then she said out loud, standing on that floor in the dark at the ninth hour, with four hundred shelves going away from a person into the dark behind her and a lamp in a cage on the landing beside her that has been out since the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four:

“There is a key to this drawer in a bag at the bottom of the first four flights and I have been down to that bag four times in a hundred and eighty-six days and I have not gone down for it.”

She said, “And I have not gone down for a strip of hand-made paper nine inches long with eighty-one lines on it and the same sum under it in words either, and the dish and the lump of clay and the ring the width of a finger are in the same bag and none of them is a day.”

She said, “And I said out loud on the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four that I was not going to open it, and I gave a reason that day, and the reason was that I would rather be wrong in a column than right on a piece of paper that has not been read in ten years.”

She said, “And I have not improved on that reason and I am not going to, and I am standing in front of it again because I have four days before the light comes down to that cut in the wall and there is nothing else in this building I can do.”

## The Definition She Has Never Said Out Loud

She went up the first four hundred and forty with her left hand on the rail and she did not go down the second four, and she got up onto the top of that long table at the near end and went along it on her hands and knees, twenty-two paces, grain under her palms, breath at nine the whole way.

And at the near end of it on the seventeenth she said out loud what a row is.

She has worked out what a row is four times in this building and she has never once said it in words, and she found out on the fifteenth that she had been using the word the whole time without knowing what she meant by it.

It took a count of ninety and it came out in four pieces and she said them in the order they came.

She said, “A row is a place a thing is decided about.”

She said, “There is a head at the top of it and the head is a figure, and a figure is what the thing costs.”

She said, “There is a hand that fills it in, and the hand belongs to whoever is standing at the table, and the hand writes what a person said and nothing else, and that has been the rule here since before this building was a building.”

She said, “And a thing that arrives with no row is not refused. It is administered, and administering it means it waits, and there is a procedure for a thing that has waited, and the procedure is run by people who have run it four hundred times and will run it four hundred more.”

And then she stopped and stood at the near end of that table and did not say the fifth piece, because she has never worked out the fifth piece and she has been using four for a hundred and eighty-seven days without knowing that there was a fifth one missing.

She said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the ninth hour of the fifteenth:

“There is a column.”

She said, “And a column is figures, and it is not the same as the head, and I have been saying row and meaning the head and I do not know what the word is for the rest of it.”

## Getting A Board Up Twenty-Two Paces

On the sixteenth she got a board out of the stack.

Forty boards lie face up in the dark under the middle of that table in a stack. She has put her hand on all forty and not one of them is different from any of the others and she has not been able to tell which two of them are hers.

She took one out backwards, on her back, with the cross-beam across her shoulders, holding it against her chest with her left forearm because her right arm has been open since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and is not going to close.

It is the size of a slate and about the thickness of two fingers and pale wood with the grain running up it.

And then she lay on the stone under that table with it beside her and worked out for most of a day how she is going to get twenty-two paces of a four-foot table to the shelf in front of that lamp with a board in her hand and no one in this building to hand it to her.

She said out loud at the ninth hour of that day, lying on the flat of her back under that table with her arm open on the stone beside her:

“I cannot be given a thing.”

She said, “And that is not the same as not being able to carry one, and I have been confusing the two since the twentieth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and I have got fifteen days to stop.”

She said, “I can carry it. I carried a nib and a cake of ink up eight hundred and eighty steps inside my shirt against the fourth rib and I have been down nine hundred and four of another stair with a bag in my hand. Twenty-two paces of table is not eight hundred and eighty steps and I have been making it into eight hundred and eighty steps because it is easier to be frightened of a bigger number.”

And she got herself out from under that table and put the board in the stack where it had been, face up, in the dark, and she could not tell which of the forty it was when she had put it back.

## What She Could Not Do With The Bag

Later on that same day she went down the first four hundred and forty with her left hand on the rail and stood on the fourth landing and put her hand into the cage of the lamp that has been out since the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and the iron was cold the way it was cold on the fourth of this month.

And she said out loud, standing on that landing with her arm inside the cage:

“There is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain who has been up this stair three times and has never seen the light at the top of it lit, and there is no oil left in this building, and there has not been for nine years, and he has been keeping a lamp on this landing going on his own.”

She said, “And I have got three days before that light comes down to the cut in that wall and I am going to put a piece of my own on that table on the morning of the fourth day and let a hand read it once.”

She said, “And I am not going to tell him, and I said out loud on the sixth of this month that I am not going to tell him what a row is, and I have had it in my mouth every day since and I have not said it.”

And she went back up the first four hundred and forty with her hand on the rail and got up onto the near end of that table and knelt on the shelf in front of the lamp with the light twenty-eight of her own hand below the top of that cage, and she said the sums out loud against the wall because she cannot let a day go past without putting its figures somewhere other than in her head.

“Fifty-eight less twenty-eight is thirty and thirty divided by two is fifteen and the fifteenth day from the sixteenth of this month is the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four.”

She said, “And the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four is twenty-four days from the seventh of this month and fifty-eight of her own hand less ten is forty-eight and forty-eight divided by two is twenty-four.”

And she got herself down off the near end and stood four feet off it and looked along twenty-two paces of wood at a lamp that has not gone out in four hundred years, and there was nothing on it.
