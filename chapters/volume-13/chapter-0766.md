# Sixteen Days Under That Table

She put five into a column on the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, and on the eleventh of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four she took the strip of pale wood back out from under that table and put it on the top of it, because sixteen days is long enough to know that a room is not going to come and ask.

## What Sixteen Days Is

The first of those days she went under the table four times. The second she went under it twice and read both figures with her fingers and came out and stood at the near end with her hand flat on the wood.

On the third she did not go under it. On the fourth she did not go under it. She had worked out by the fourth that going under it was a thing she did because she had nothing else to do with an hour, and there is a difference between that and looking for something, and she had been doing the first one for a hundred and seventy-six days.

She said out loud on the eighth, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table with her left hand on the edge of it:

“The five has been in that column sixteen days and the room has not made one sound about it.”

She said, “And I have got four fingers and a thumb and I have put them on that wood with my own hand in my own light, and it is mine, and there is no procedure in this building for a thing that a person has put in a column on purpose.”

She said, “And I have never once in nineteen years been in a room where I could find out what happens next.”

## The Only Way There Is Of Finding Out

What she wanted was not the five back. She had made her peace with the five on the twenty-fourth of that month and she has not gone back to it once.

What she wanted was to know what a room does with a column it cannot add up.

She stood on the floor with her hand on the near edge of that table and worked at it for two days, and what she worked out was the thing she had been working out since the eighteenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and had not been able to use.

A thing arrives in this room as a board. The hand at the far end of this table comes down over it, once, in about the same count every time, and whatever the board says is what the board is, and it goes under the table and it is never looked at again.

That is the deciding. One look. There is no second look and there has never been anything to appeal with, and she worked that out on the sixth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four and she has been carrying it for a hundred and fifty-three days.

She said it out loud on the ninth, standing, in daylight, with her hand on the edge of the wood and her right arm open in front of her:

“The only way a room finds out anything is by being given something to look at.”

She said, “And a thing that arrives with no row is not a thing that has arrived, and that is not a decision anybody made. It is arithmetic. It came up a stair in an armful or it did not.”

She said, “And a strip of pale wood a foot long and two inches across with two figures in a column on it, put on this table by the person it is about, is a thing that arrives.”

And she stopped there for a count of a hundred and forty with her hand on the edge and did not go any further, because the next part of it was the part she had not wanted to say.

## What It Would Take Out Of Her

She said that part out loud on the ninth at the ninth hour, standing on the floor at the near end of that table, and her voice was not the ordinary voice and she noticed that and did not change it.

“If I put that on this table, the hand at the far end of it is going to come down over it once.”

She said, “And there is one look and there is no second one, and I have never had one at anything of mine, and there is not one thing in this building that will tell me after the fact what a look saw.”

She said, “And if the look is what puts the five in a row, then the five stops being a thing I did. It becomes a thing that was entered, and an entered thing is not a done thing, and there is not one instrument in this county that can tell the difference afterwards.”

She said, “And I have been escaping being entered since I was nine years old.”

And then she said the whole of it in one sentence, which is the sentence the two days came to, and she said it standing up in daylight in a room with a person in it.

“I am going to put it on that table anyway.”

## Three Marks On A Rail

She went down the low door on the tenth at the fourth hour and took the first four hundred and forty with her left hand on the rail, and she did not go down the second four at all.

There is a lamp in a cage on the fourth landing and it has been out since the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and she put her hand into that cage on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four and the iron was cold and she went back up.

She was on that landing again on the tenth and she was not looking for the lamp.

The rail on that landing has a flat top to it about the width of her hand, and there are three sets of marks cut into the top of it with something hard and thin.

The first is a group of five short strokes and then a longer one across the top of them. The second is four strokes and a longer one across the top. The third is four strokes and a longer one across the top, and the third group is deeper than the other two and the wood has started to lift along one of them.

She put her fingers on them and worked out how far apart they are in time by the wear, because a thing that is used more wears faster and a rail on a landing is only used by one person.

She said out loud, standing on that landing in the dark with her hand on the rail:

“There is one mark for each time he came up this tower.”

She said, “And there is no mark for going down.”

And she stood there for a count of a hundred and forty with her hand on the flat of that rail, and the lamp in the cage beside her was cold iron and had been cold iron since before she was in this room.

She said, “He is sixteen years old and he has been up that stair three times in his life and he came up it because there was no light, and there has been no light in this building for nine years except one, and he has never seen the one.”

She said, “And I cannot go down and tell him what a row is, and I said so out loud on the sixth of this month and I have not taken it back.”

## What She Took Up The Stair

She went up the first four hundred and forty with her left hand on the rail and she took nothing up them with her, which is the only thing in this room she has ever done that she could have done the other way round.

And at the near end of that long table on the eleventh she put her hand on the edge of it, and it is four feet off the floor and she can reach it and she can get up on it, and she has not got up on it in one movement in her life.

She got up on it on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and she has been going along the top of it on her hands and knees ever since, twenty-two paces, with the grain of the wood under her palms and her breath at nine the whole way.

And on the eleventh of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four she went along the top of that table for the twenty-third time in this building and she knelt on the shelf in front of the lamp with her hand on the strip of pale wood, and the light was eighteen of her own hand below the top of that cage.

She said it out loud, kneeling on that shelf in the light, because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

“Fifty-eight less eighteen is forty and forty divided by two is twenty and the twentieth day from the eleventh of this month is the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four, which is the day the next forty things come up that stair.”

And she put the strip of pale wood flat on the top of that table at the near end of it, face up, grain running up, a foot long and two inches across, and she squared it up with two fingers of her left hand, and it was the first time in a hundred and eighty-three days that there had been anything of hers on the wood.

And she got herself down off the near end onto the floor and stood four feet off it and waited.
