# A Figure She Cannot Hold

She found the row on the twelfth of the month beginning 2,324, on her back, on stone, under a table, with her fingers, and she has been able to read her own hand in the dark since she was nine years old and that is the only reason it took her two days and not twenty years.

## Under The Table With The Bundle

The bundle of papers is under the far end of that table with the string round it and it is a third the size of the armful and she had her arm into it up to the elbow on the tenth of the month beginning 2,324 and could not get anything out of it, because it is tied and she has three fingers and a thumb and one arm open.

Under the near end of the table, flat on the stone, face up, with its long edge toward the light, is a strip of the same pale wood as the boards and it is a foot long and two inches across.

It was not on the table on the first of this month. It was not there on the second. She put her hand on it on the eleventh and she has not been able to leave it alone since.

## A Head And A Hand And A Column

She read it with her fingers on the twelfth of the month beginning 2,324 at the second hour, lying on her side with the cross-beam across her back and her breath at nine and her face against cold wood.

There is a head at the left-hand end of it and a column running away from it, and the column has one thing in it, and the one thing is a figure.

She read the figure with her fingers twice and then a third time and she came out of that table in a count of sixty and sat on the floor of the room against the doorframe and did not do anything at all for a count of two hundred.

It is in her hand.

Ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes, written in the way she writes figures, with the way she makes a two and the way she makes a seven and the way she comes down on a stroke when the line is longer than the others.

She has never seen her own writing in another hand anywhere in the world and there is none of it anywhere in the world except in a bag at the bottom of a stair and on a slate that went up with about forty other boards and in this strip of wood on the floor of a room four miles above a mountain.

## Reading It Three Times

She read it with her fingers on the twelfth of the month beginning 2,324 at the second hour and then she came out from under that table and she had to go and lie down.

So she went back in at the fourth hour and read it again, and it took a count of sixty each time, and she read it a third time at the sixth hour and got the same thing all three times.

The figure at the head of that strip is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes.

She said it out loud lying on her back on stone with the cross-beam across her back, at the seventh hour of the twelfth:

“Ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes, and I wrote that on a slate at the two posts at the first hour of the first of this month with a nib I did not lift and my right hand on an arm that was open, and my little finger stopped being part of my hand in the middle of it.”

She said, “And it is in her hand.”

She said it and then she lay there for a count of a hundred and she did not say anything else, because the human sentence came to her in that hundred and there is no version of it she is going to put on a page.

A thing she wrote about herself at a frame in the grit with not one person in a hundred yards, in her own hand, on an open arm, is on a strip of wood on the floor of a room four miles above a mountain, in her own hand, and the only person in the world who could ever recognise it as hers is her.

And it is out of date, and there is no mark on it saying so, and the figure at the head of it has never once been checked by a person who could correct it.

## Two Days Under The Table And One Under The Bar

And on the thirteenth of the month beginning 2,324 she went under that table twice more and put her hand on the bundle of papers, and she did not pull the string, and she lay there with her face against stone for a count of four hundred and worked out the only three things there are to work out about a strip of wood on a floor.

She said them out loud one at a time with her mouth against the stone.

She said, “It is not on the table. It is off the table and under it and it is put there where it cannot be looked at from standing up, and the person who put it there had arms long enough to put it there without going down on one knee.”

She said, “There is one thing in it. A head and a column and one figure in the column, and the column is a column and there is not a second thing in it, and whatever else has come up that stair in four hundred years is not on this strip.”

She said, “And it has been under that table since the first of this month and it is the first thing in this building that has ever been about me.”

And then she came out from under it and went and sat on the floor against the bar lying flat against the wall on the left, with her back against the wood and her arm open across her knees, and she did not touch it and she did not move it.


## Why It Is In Her Hand

And she worked that out the same day, lying on the floor against the doorframe with her arm open and her breath at seven, and she said it out loud in the ordinary voice to a doorframe.

She said, “This room has one instrument and it is a hand with a nib in it and it takes fifteen seconds of work at a time.”

She said, “And a figure that is copied any other way than it was written is not that figure, and this room has been putting figures in a column for four hundred years and every one of them has to come out exactly right or nothing works.”

She said, “And so the only thing this building has ever copied perfectly, in four hundred years, is the price of me.”

## What Is Not On It

And then she said what is not on it, which took her a count of ninety and which is the whole of what she has.

There is not one word on that strip about what she is.

There is no name, because a name is a person and she has never had one on a thing above the line in her life. There is no place and there is no date, because the room does not know either of those about her. And there is nothing on that strip at all about what she is, and the whole of what she is went up that stair on a piece of pale wood with nineteen words on it in the first hour of the first of this month and the room has not got it.

She said, “It has got what I cost.”

She said, “And it has got it exactly.”

## It Is Going

And that is the part she had to work out on her back under a table with her fingers, on the twelfth, and it is the part she has not been able to say out loud since.

The figure at the head of that row is true as of the first of the month beginning 2,294. She wrote it herself on a slate at the two posts at the first hour of that morning and she carried it up a stair at the second hour of it and it was true then and it has not been true since.

One in twenty of every day comes off a name that has been in a column, and it is an hour and twelve minutes a day, and it has been coming off her since the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054, and it does not stop because it has been written down.

Two thousand three hundred and thirty-five less two thousand two hundred and ninety-four is forty-one days.

Forty-one days of an hour and twelve minutes is two thousand nine hundred and fifty-two minutes, which is forty-nine hours and twelve minutes, which is two days and one hour and twelve minutes.

That is how wrong that figure is. It is wrong by two days and one hour and twelve minutes and it was wrong by eight hours and twenty-four minutes seven days after she wrote it and it is wrong by more every hour she lies in this room.

## Not One Person In That Building

She came out from under the table and lay on the floor of the room on her back with her right arm open across her and looked at a lamp in a cage at the far end of a table she cannot reach, and she said the last of it out loud at the sixth hour of the thirteenth of the month beginning 2,324.

She said, “There is not one person in that building who could ever know that figure is going.”

She said, “Not because it is a secret. Because there is not one person above this line who loses a day. Not one person in this room gets lighter and not one person in this room has ever had anything taken off them for keeping it, so there is not one instrument in this building and there is not one head in it that is looking for a number that is going.”

She said, “And the row has been sitting on the floor under this table with my figure at the head of it since the first of this month, and it is the only figure about me in the world that is in a hand that can be read, and it is getting more wrong every hour, and it does not need me to be alive to go on being wrong.”

She lay there for a count of a hundred.

She said, “And I cannot hold it.”

She said, “I can hold four numbers and I have held a bar nine feet nine on a shoulder since I was nine years old and I have held a figure in my head for seven hundred and seventy-five days.”

She said, “And that figure is in a column on a strip of wood on the floor of a room four miles above a mountain and it is not mine to hold, and I am going to have to go and look at it every day for as long as I am in this room, and there is not one thing I can do about it.”
