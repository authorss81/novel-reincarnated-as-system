# Counting Him Down Eight Hundred And Eighty

The hand went up into that cage at the second hour of the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and she counted the seconds out loud for the fourth time in her life and then she took the boy down eight hundred and eighty steps herself.

## The Hand Going Up Into The Cage

She had been awake since the second hour before it and she had her eye on the bottom edge of that light and her left hand flat against the wall behind it, edge on, three fingers and a thumb.

At the second hour the hands came off the near edge of that table.

She did not have to say anything. She had been in this room eighty-four days and she had seen it twice and it was the third time and it is the same count every time.

The light went down. It came down the way water goes into a cloth, steadily, and the bright place it makes on the wall came down with it and got smaller at the bottom edge, and it reached the line cut in the stone and stopped on it and did not go under it.

And then the hand opened and the light came up off the cut, up off the level it had been standing at, up past the top of the cage, and came back down and settled where it usually is.

She counted it. Eleven seconds. She said the number out loud in the ordinary voice to a room with one person in it.

And then she got her own hand up against the end of that forearm before it and held it there and counted the thing coming off it, and it was the width of the little finger on her left hand, which is the same as the last two times, and the face at that table was thinner at the cheek than it had been on the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four.

And then the hands went back onto the near edge of that table and the head came down over it and the whole of the standing went on exactly as it had been going on before.

## Why He Had To Go Down

She told him at the sixth hour of the second of that month, standing at the near end of that table, and she told him in the ordinary voice because she does not have another one for anything that matters.

She said, “You have to go down that stair and I am going to take you.”

He said, “All right.”

She said, “That is not a thing to say all right to. Not one person in nineteen years has ever said all right to that and you have been in this room one day.”

She said, “The man who keeps the road is going to ask you again in about ten days and you are going to have to say something, and there is not one thing in this room you can say that will be true and useful to you, and the longer you stand up here the more there is going to be in your mouth.”

She said, “And yesterday the lamp in this room stood on that line with nothing above it and nothing below it for the first time in four hundred years, and it is not going to happen again until the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen, and I am not having that in a boy’s mouth on a made road.”

## Four Hundred And Forty With The Rail

She took him down on the third of that month and she started at the second hour and she counted all the way and she did not stop once and she did not go fast.

The first four flights are four hundred and forty steps and there is a rail on one side of them and she put her left hand on that rail and she kept it there the whole way, and the boy put his right hand on it behind her.

She counted out loud, one number at a time, in the ordinary voice, going down.

Forty. Fifty. Sixty.

At two hundred she was at seven counts of breath and she said the number and the count out loud together, because she has always found that if you say both of them the second one does not get away from you.

At three hundred and eighty she sat down on a step for a count of twenty and did not tell him she was sitting down, and he sat down on the step above her without saying anything either, and they sat there together for a count of twenty in the dark of a stair with no light on it at all.

And then she got up and said, “Three hundred and eighty-one,” and went on down.

## Four Hundred And Forty Without One

And then the rail stopped.

That is the whole of what the second four flights are: four hundred and forty steps and a wall four inches off your hand on one side and nothing at all on the other, and she had told him that on the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and he had got there before her, on the first attempt, standing on a landing with one hand on the frame.

She went down it first again. Four counts of breath and seven on a rise and she counted every step out loud because that was the only thing she had brought him up here for.

He took a hundred and ninety steps in the first hour and sat down, and she sat down two steps above him and said nothing for a count of a hundred and forty, the same as the first time, and then she said:

“That is a hundred and ninety and there are two hundred and fifty left and there is not one light on this side of this building and there has not been one since before you were on that floor.”

He said, “I know. I have done this in the day.”

She said, “Not this. You have not done this. There is nothing on this side to put a hand on and you have just proved you know that and you have still got two hundred and fifty of them in front of you.”

And he got up and did them.

## The Number She Gave Him Instead

They came out onto the copying floor at the sixth hour of the fourth of that month and she stood on the stone in daylight with her breath at nine and said the whole of it out loud, because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

She said, “Four hundred and forty and four hundred and forty, and eight hundred and eighty from that door to this floor, and I have counted it out loud going down and you have got it in your head now and it is the only thing I have got to give you.”

He said, “That is not an answer.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “You know it is not an answer.”

She said, “I know it better than you do. I have been giving people figures instead of answers since I was about ten years old and it is the one thing I have never once been able to stop doing and it is not cowardice, it is that a figure is the only thing I have ever had and if I do not give it then I have given nothing at all.”

He said, “Why did you not give me an answer on the stair.”

She said, “Because you asked it again and I have got nothing new, and I would rather tell you that than say a thing twice.”

## The Bag At The Bottom Of The First Four

There is a bag at the bottom of the first four flights with the front of it open and her name nowhere on it, and she walked past it going down and she walked past it coming back up, and she did not touch it, and she said out loud why.

She said, standing on the stone at the bottom of that stair on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, in the ordinary voice, to a boy and not to the stair:

“There is a dish in there and a lump of clay and a ring the width of a finger and a stub of a nib and a cake of ink opened on the seventh of the month beginning one thousand eight hundred and seventy-four that has never been let dry, and a key to a drawer thirty feet from where you are standing, and nine inches of hand-made paper with eighty-one lines on it and the same sum under it in words.”

She said, “And on the third of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four I stood in this stair and said out loud that I was not going down for any of them, and I have not gone down for any of them in nine days and I am not going down for them today, and I have not changed my mind and I am not going to pretend to you that I have.”

He said, “What is in the drawer.”

She said, “Four sheets of rice paper and I have not looked at the margin of one of them since the twenty-third of the month beginning two thousand and twenty-four, and that is a different decision from this one and I am not going to run them together in front of you.”

He said, “All right.”

She said, “Do not say all right to that. It is the second thing today you have said all right to and neither of them was all right.”

## The Lamp On The Fourth Landing

They came out onto the copying floor with four hundred volumes on the shelves in their cages and a drawer with a lock on it thirty feet off, and she had not stood in that room for eighty-four days and had not missed one thing in it except the lamp on the wall.

And he went about eight paces along that floor and stopped at a lamp in a cage on a bracket in the wall, which is the lamp he had been told to keep going, and which has been out since the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and he put his hand out to it and took his hand back.

He said, “There is a lamp at the top of that stair and it has not gone out in nine years.”

She said, “It has not gone out in nine years and I cannot tell you why and I have been eighty-four days in a room with it and I have measured it.”

He said, “Is it fed out of that jar.”

She said, “There is no jar and there has not been one for nine years and the lamp in that cage at the top is not fed at all. There is no oil in it and no wick in it and nothing behind it, and the wall is the wall behind the light.”

He said, “Then somebody puts something into it.”

She said, “Somebody puts something into it, and I am not going to tell you what or who, and you are not going to be able to use it and that is not me keeping it from you, that is me not knowing.”

He said, “So it is a lamp.”

She said, “It is a lamp that has been burning for nine years with nothing to put in it and no oil in the building and one person who stands in a room at the top of that stair and does not look up.”

She stood there in daylight on the fourth landing and said the last of it to him, and she said it out loud, and she said it slowly.

She said, “You will not find that out. There is nothing up there to find it out with. I have looked at everything in that room there is to look at and I have not found it and I have been there eighty-four days and I am the only person who has ever been in it.”

She said, “And if you come up there again, you will be doing it because you cannot leave a thing alone, and not because I asked you to, and I am not going to ask you to and I am not going to tell you not to.”

He said, “I will probably come up.”

She said, “I know. I have not got a figure for it and I have got a figure for everything else.”
