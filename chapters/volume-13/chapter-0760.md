# Standing Still In Front Of It

She stood in that room on the tenth, eleventh, twelfth and thirteenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four and let the only instrument it has come off that table and put its hands on her open arm, and it wrote down a figure, and the figure was what she cost.

## Where She Has To Stand

She started on the tenth because it was the first day since the fourth of that month with nothing on it, and she put herself where the room puts things.

She stood at the near end of that table with her feet together and her arms down at her sides and her head up, and she held still the way the person at the far end of it holds still, and she managed a count of nine.

Then her weight went onto the outside of one foot and her breath came off nine and she got it back, and it went off again on the seventh count, and she stood there for most of a day with nothing at all happening at the far end of the table.

She said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the seventh hour of that day:

“A comparison needs two things standing still and this room has only ever had one of them, and the one it has is a person, and I am not going to be able to be the other one of those two things while I am standing on my own feet in the middle of this floor.”

She said, “And I have never in nineteen years been able to be counted where I stand. That has been true since I was nine years old and it is not going to have changed in a hundred and fifty-two days.”

She said, “And I am not going to be able to choose where I stand either. I worked that out on the ninth of this month. There is no ground above this line. A row is decided standing up by somebody in a room they are standing in, and the only room up here has one floor and one table in it, and there is nowhere in this building to stand that is not the middle of this floor.”

## The Day Before She Did It

On the tenth she found out the one thing she had not known, which is what the room does with somebody who stands in front of it, and it found that out in about a count of eleven, and she had already worked out on the fifth of that month that she cannot hold still for eleven counts.

Then she stood there for the rest of that day with nothing at all happening at the far end of that table, and a person standing nine feet off along it with both hands on the near edge, and the light coming up on her from the far end the whole time.

And in the evening of the tenth she got under that table with a cross-beam across her shoulders and lay on her back on the stone and put her hand flat on her own strip and read the head of the row and the one figure in the column with her fingers, the way she has done about forty times.

She said it out loud, on the flat of her back, with the boards over her and the strip under her hand:

“If I say a figure out loud in this room at the place where the arms go, then that is a row, and a row is the only instrument this building has, and the instrument is a person, and I have not got one of my own.”

She said, “And I am going to be one of my own on the twelfth of that month, and I do not know what that costs and there is not one mouth in this building that is going to tell me before I do it.”

She said, “And I have still not got a figure for myself. Whatever is up on that shelf is going to have to come out of me, and there are two things I have got, and one of them is what I cost and one of them is what I am, and it will have to be one of those two, and I am not going to be able to choose which.”

## Standing Still

On the eleventh she got up on the table.

It took a count of nine and it has not taken four tries since the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four. She went twenty-two paces along the top of it on her hands and knees with the grain of the wood under her palms, and she knelt on the shelf in front of that lamp, in the light, with the wall behind it and nothing behind the light.

And then she did the thing she had not done in that room in six months, which is to say a row out loud on purpose, standing, in daylight, at the place where the arms go.

She said, “I am nineteen years old.”

She said, “I have three fingers and a thumb on my right hand and my right arm is open and has been open since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and it is the arm I write with, and I am writing open.”

She said, “There is a small open place low behind my fourth rib the size of a coin and it opened on the eighteenth of the month beginning one thousand five hundred and forty-four, and it is not hungry and it has not been hungry in a hundred and fifty-three days.”

She said, “And the figure at the head of my row is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes and it is eight days, one hour and twelve minutes out of date and it is what I cost.”

And she stopped there with her hand open on the shelf in the light, and she did not say anything else.

## What The Hand Did

At the ninth hour of the twelfth of that month the hands came off the near edge of that table.

She had watched them come off that edge twice in a hundred and fifty days. Both times there had been something on the wood in front of them, and both times a board had come out from under the shelf and a stroke had gone onto it.

This time nothing came out from under the shelf at all.

The hands went along the far side of that table toward her, and they came down the near leg of it, and they came the rest of the way to the floor, and she was on top of a four-foot table on her knees and could not get down fast enough and did not try.

They closed on her right forearm at the wrist.

It was not rough and it was not fast. It was the way a hand goes along a board at four to a minute. It went the length of her forearm once, and it came back, and it stopped on the wrist bone and held there for about eleven seconds while she sat on the shelf in the light of a lamp that has no oil in it and not go in it.

And she felt it go.

The distance the light came down that wall is the same distance the arm goes down, and she has said that out loud in this room twice, and she has never once felt it happen to anything that was hers.

Her right forearm went down by the width of the little finger on her left hand.

## What It Wrote And What It Cost

The hands let go of her and went back up the leg of the table and along the wood and stopped at the near edge, and a board came out from under the shelf where the stack is, and a stroke went onto it, and then a figure went onto it, and she was on top of a table nine feet off from where the figure was and she could not see it and she was not going to climb down and go and look.

She came down off the table the long way, on her hands and knees, twenty-two paces, and got her feet on the stone and stood there with her right arm open in front of her and her left hand round the wrist of it.

And then she said the whole of it out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table, and it took a count of a hundred and forty and she said it once.

She said, “It measured me.”

She said, “In four hundred years that hand has measured one thing in this room and one thing only, and it was measuring itself when it did it, and it has never once had anything else in front of it to measure.”

She said, “And I put myself in front of it on purpose. I got up on that table and I said a row out loud in the place where the arms go, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, and I said what I am.”

She said, “And it wrote down a figure, and I know what the figure is, and the figure is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes.”

She said, “And that is what I cost. That is not a person. I said my age and my hand and the place behind my fourth rib and it wrote down what I cost, because that is the only figure it has got for me and it has had it for a hundred and fifty-four days.”

And she stopped there for a count of ninety, and then she said the part that was the whole of those four days.

She said, “It is wrong.”

She said, “I stood in front of the only instrument in this building and it took my arm and it wrote down a price, and I cannot correct it, because a hand that copies figures exactly cannot copy a different figure, and I did not say a different figure.”

She said, “And not one mouth in this building is going to correct it. There is not one mouth above this line that knows what I said out loud on the twelfth of this month, and that board goes up that stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four with a price on it, and there is not one instrument in the world that takes a line out of a record that has been above the line for four hundred years.”

## The Sums On The Thirteenth

She did the sums on the thirteenth at the ninth hour, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table with the light at the far end of it twenty-two of her own hand below the top of that cage.

She said, “Two thousand four hundred and fifty-six less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and ninety-seven days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and two thousand four hundred and fifty-six less two thousand three hundred and one is a hundred and fifty-five days in this room.”

She said, “And fifty-eight less twenty-two is thirty-six and thirty-six divided by two is eighteen and the eighteenth day from the thirteenth of this month is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four, and the figure at the head of my row is eight days, two hours and twenty-four minutes out of date.”

And then she said the last of it, and it is the thing she said while she was sitting on the floor with her back against the near leg of that table with her right forearm open across her knees.

She said, “A hundred and fifty-five days I have been a figure in a column that has not been read once, and yesterday that room found out I was standing in front of it, and the first thing it did with me was weigh me against the only person it has ever weighed anything against.”

She said, “And it cost me an arm and it gave me a price, and I asked it nothing and it told me nothing, and I said what I am and it wrote down what I cost, and that is the whole of what a measure does to a person in this building.”

She said, “And I did that on purpose, standing up, in daylight, at the place where the arms go, and I am not going to be able to put it back, and there is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain who cannot be weighed by anything at all and who would not have believed me if I had said any part of this to him.”
