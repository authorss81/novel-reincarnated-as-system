# One Look And No Second

She worked out what decides in that room on the sixth of the month beginning 2,324, by doing it herself, standing at the near end of the long table in the ordinary voice, and what she worked out is the whole of the room, and it is a stroke, and a figure, and one look.

## Doing It Herself

She put her right hand on the empty table at the near end of it, an arm that has been open since the first of this month, and she looked along an empty table for a count of a hundred and forty, and then she did the stroke and the figure in the air with three fingers and a thumb on nothing.

That is the whole of what a hand does at that table.

She did it nine times. She timed them by counting and they came out at four to a minute, and she got the last one of the nine wrong by a count and did that one again.

A stroke in one corner. One figure beside it. And in between the hand and the figure, one look, and the look is the same length every time.

She has watched that hand go along forty boards in ten minutes and has never once seen it stop, and has never once seen it go back, and has never once seen it hurry.

## What Decides

She said it out loud at the sixth hour of the sixth of the month beginning 2,324, standing at the near end of the table, in the ordinary voice:

“The deciding in this room takes one look.”

She said, “And then a stroke and a figure. And that is all of it. There is no second look. There has not been a second look in four hundred years, because a second look is a thing you ask for, and a thing you ask for is a thing somebody has been refused, and there has not been one of those here either.”

She said, “I have spent nine years of my life learning that being recognised is the thing that costs. I went up a stair to be recognised and what I have got is a figure in a column under a head.”

She said, “And there is no appeal, because there is nothing to appeal with. There is not one shape to this that has a second look in it. There is no second look and there is no third one and there is no place to stand and wait for one, because that is what the place would be for.”

## What She Does To Find Out What Decides

She did the whole of the seventh, the eighth and the ninth of the month beginning 2,324 at the near end of that table, standing, in the ordinary voice, doing the only thing there is to do with a thing that decides and will not say anything about itself.

She redid the forty minutes.

She stood at the near end of the table where the hand stands and she timed herself, and she got four to a minute nine times out of nine, and she did not know what the ten minutes were for until she looked at what had changed on the boards, and then she knew.

She put a stroke in the top corner of a board in the air with three fingers and a thumb.

She put a figure beside it in the air.

And between the two of those there was a look, and the look was the same length every time, and she timed the look on the second and the third and the fifth and the seventh and it did not change by a count.

Then she went and got onto the table and lay down along the near side of it with her cheek against the wood and her eyes level with the wood and looked along the surface of it the way the head looks down at it, and she did that for a count of three hundred at the seventh hour of the eighth, and it is what that room is for, and she could not tell one board from another for the look she gave them.

She said, out loud, lying on a table four feet above the floor with an arm open:

“I have put my eye on that surface at the height that head is at and I cannot tell one board from another, and I have read my own handwriting in the dark with my fingers under a table in two days.”

She said, “And the hand does that in ten minutes for forty boards. Which means it is not reading what is on them.”

## Two Things She Cannot Do

And on the eighth of the month beginning 2,324 she worked out two things about herself in that room and said them both out loud before she worked out the second half of what decides, and she has never been able to leave one standing.

She said, “I cannot find out who that is. There has not been one name in this building and there is not going to be one and I could stand here for four hundred years and not learn a single thing about a person.”

She said, “And I cannot find out whether they know I am in this room. There has not been one movement made at that table that was at me. I said the whole of what I am out loud on the eighth of this month at the second hour, standing, nine feet from them, and the hands did not come off the edge and the head did not come up.”

She said, “And I am not going to find out that either. I have got no instrument for it and there is no way to make one and the whole of what I can measure in this room is the furniture.”

She sat down on the floor at the near end and said the two of them again inside her own head, which is the only place she has ever put anything she is not going to say out loud, and both of them stayed where they were.


## The Look Did Not Include Her

And then she worked out the half of it that was worse, and it took her the rest of the sixth and the whole of the seventh and most of the eighth, standing, and at the sixth hour of the eighth of the month beginning 2,324 she put it out loud in a room with a lamp in it.

She said, “That hand went along forty things on the first of this month at four to a minute, and it did not stop at any of them, and it did not stop at mine.”

She said, “And I have been in this room since the eighth of this month. I have been in this room on the day the forty things came in. I was four feet off the end of that table with the light on the other side of me, and I did not get a hand on it once, and I stood there and I watched, and I was not one of the things that went along it.”

She said, “And that is not a refusal. That is the whole of what I have to say about it and I am going to say it now in the ordinary voice: nobody has refused me and nobody has spoken to me.”

## A Thing That Has Not Arrived

And then she said the thing that took her two days to get to, standing, at the sixth hour of the ninth of the month beginning 2,324, at the near end of a long table with a bar against the wall on the left and a person standing at the far end of it.

She said, “A thing that arrives with no row is not a thing that has arrived.”

She said, “That is not a rule anybody made. That is just what a room is. A thing comes up that stair with a row on it and it is a thing that has arrived, and a thing comes up that stair with no row on it and it is nothing that has happened in this room, and it goes on being a thing that has not arrived for as long as the light lasts.”

She said, “And there is no procedure for a thing that has not arrived. There is nowhere for it to wait and there is nothing to fill in and there is not one person in four hundred years who has had to do it, and the reason there is no procedure is that it has never happened.”

She said, “And that is the whole of what has happened to me since the first of this month, and it is not cruelty and it is not a decision and there is not one person in this room who has thought about me at all.”

## The Worst Sentence Of Her Life

And she said the last part out loud as well, because she cannot leave a thing standing that she has not put into words where it exists, and she said it at the seventh hour of the ninth of the month beginning 2,324, standing, in the ordinary voice, with her back to the light and the one at the table at the far end with both hands on the near edge of it.

She said, “The not writing was not a decision anybody made.”

And then she stood in the room and let that be in it.

She said, “There is not one person in this building who chose to leave me out of a row. There is a hand that looks at things at four to a minute, and a figure that is the same figure on every one of them, and a look that is one look, and none of those three things knows that I exist, and not one of them had to decide anything.”

She said it into a room with one person standing in it at the far end of a table, and that person did not turn round, and that was not rudeness and it was not the point.

The point is that a room can be full and be empty, and that there is not one person in it who could be handed what she has just said.

## What She Wants Out Loud

And then, at the ninth hour, standing, in the ordinary voice, she said what she wants, and it is the first time she has said it.

She said, “I want a row.”

She said, “Not to be looked at again and not to have a second look and not to have anything said to me. I want a place where a thing about me goes, and I want to be in it, and I have wanted that since the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054 and I have never once been able to say it out loud in nineteen years because there has not been a room.”

She said, “And I am not going to be able to have one by standing here. I have been in this room since the eighth of this month and nothing about me has happened in it, and the reason is that a thing that has not arrived cannot be made to arrive by a person waiting for it.”

She went and sat down on the floor at the near end with her back against the doorframe, and her breath was at nine, and the light at the far end of the table went on not leaning.
