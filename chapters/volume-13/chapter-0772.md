# What A Room Can Do Once

On the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four, at the fourth hour, the light came down to the cut in the wall behind that lamp and stopped on it, and at the second hour of the next day the person at that table spoke for the first time in the whole of this building, and they said that a room can amend a row once, and every part of it was true.

## Forty Things Coming Up That Stair

The man came up the turn at the second hour with about forty boards under one arm and a bundle of papers in a cloth under the other, and he has been on that road a year, and he came out of the place in the wall behind the lamp’s shelf and put them down and went back out without looking at anything.

She stood four feet off the near end of that table and did not say one word.

She had said on the twenty-seventh of the month before that she was going to stand in that room and let a bundle go up that stair and not say anything while it went, and she was not going to go back on her own sentence because a room had surprised her. She had said that on purpose.

Ten minutes for the forty boards and thirty for the bundle. The hands did not stop and did not hurry and did not go back.

And that is the deciding, forty times over, at about four to a minute, one look each, and she has known since the sixth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four that a room takes one look at a thing and that the thing is then what the thing said.

She watched forty looks go along twenty-two paces of wood from the near end of that table to the far end of it and she did not make a sound while she watched them.

And two of those forty things had her price on them and she could not tell which two, because they are all the same pale wood with the grain running up and there is not one instrument in this building or in this county that would tell two of them apart.

## Two Of The Same Figure

She got a hand on the top board in the stack before the light came down that wall and she could not find either of them then, and she turned all forty over again on the second of that month and she could not find them now.

That is not a thing she is able to do anything about and she made herself go through all forty anyway, on her back under the middle of that table with a cross-beam across her shoulders, three fingers and a thumb on the edges of them, face up, flat, the way she has done since the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four.

She said it out loud on the flat of her back under that table with her arm open on the stone beside her, and the whole of it came out in the same order it came out in last time, because there was nothing else in that room that would take it:

“There are forty boards in this room and two of them have the same figure on them and one of those two has been in this building since the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four and the other one was lying on the top of that table from the seventeenth of the month before.”

She said, “And both of them went up that stair four minutes apart this morning and I was standing four feet off the near end of that table and I could not tell which was which and I will not be able to correct either of them and there is not one instrument in the world that takes a line out of a record that has been above the line for four hundred years.”

And she got herself out from under that table and stood at the near end of it and did not say any more of it, because she had said it all of it already on the twenty-seventh and she was not going to keep saying a thing to a room that does not ask her for it.

## The Light Down To The Cut

At the fourth hour of that first of the month the light came down that wall and stopped on the cut, and the far end of that long table went out of the light in the time it takes to put a hand down.

That is the fourth first of a month the mark has fallen on and the second one in four hundred years that has been a first instead of a thirtieth, and it is hers, and she has been counting it since the seventh of the month before and she got it right.

At the second hour of the second of that month a hand went up between the four bars of that cage and closed on the base of the light, and it came up past the top of that cage in about eleven seconds, and the person at that table was standing at the near edge of it afterwards with both hands on it exactly as they had been standing.

And their right forearm went down by the width of the little finger on her left hand, which is the same width it goes down every time and which she measured for the first time on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and has measured six times since.

She had her own hand at the end of that forearm before it and her own hand at the end of that forearm after it, and nothing about hers had changed, and the light in that cage was higher than the top of the cage the way it always is after a feeding.

## What It Said

And then the person at that table spoke, and they did not turn round and they did not look up and they did not stop working.

They said, in the ordinary voice, standing, at the near edge of that table with both hands on it:

“There is a column in this county that has been adding one day in twenty since the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning two thousand and fifty-four. There is a name at the head of it. The figure at the head of it has never once been in a row above this line.”

They said, “I can amend one row. I have not amended one. That is what the amending is for and it has been four hundred years since anything in this building needed it.”

They said, “I can put that figure into yours, in the pair of cuts under the one you wrote, over what you wrote there. And then your row will add up.”

They said, “It costs one feed.”

They said, “The feed for this lamp is paid by me, out of me, once every twenty-nine days, and it is the same width on the arm every time. An amending is not that. I cannot carry a figure I have not got up that stair, and there is nothing in this Court but that lamp and me and you.”

They said, “So the feed would be yours.”

They said, “It has to be written in the light, because that is where hands write. That light comes down to that cut at the fourth hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four. If a hand is in that cage before it gets there it goes up past the top of that cage. If it is not, it does not come up again.”

They said, “And I am not going to put a day in there on my own and have nothing for it. That has not happened here in four hundred years.”

## Every Part Of It Was True

They said the last of it in the same voice and at the same speed as the rest of it and it is the only thing anybody in this building has ever said to her that was about her:

“If I put a name in that column then the figure you wrote in it stops being yours and becomes the column’s. And the column will go on taking a day out of you every day at one hour and twelve minutes, the same as it has since the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning two thousand and fifty-four, and it will do that whether this room decides anything about it or not.”

They said, “A column is not a person. You can argue with a person.”

And then they stopped and did not say anything else, and they did not ask her a question, and they did not wait for an answer, and they went back along the far side of that table with their hands on the wood the way they had been before they spoke.

The whole of it took about eleven seconds.

## What She Said Back

She did not say one word for a count of a hundred and forty.

Then she said, out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table, and she said it in the ordinary voice because that is the rule in this building and she was not going to let a room that had spoken once hear her answer in a different one:

“I heard all of it.”

She said, “And every part of it is true and I have not found one part of it that is not, and I am not going to stand here and pretend to a woman who has been in this room two hundred and four days that there is something wrong with it.”

And then she stopped, and she put her left hand flat on the near edge of that table, and she said the thing that is going to cost her the last of it, out loud, standing, in daylight:

“Two figures in one column is a column that gets counted, and the room knows that, and the room is being kind to me by telling me so, and there is not one person in this building who has ever been kind to me before.”

She said, “And I have not said yes and I have not said no.”

She said, “And the lamp is up past the top of that cage now and it comes down two of my own hand a day, and fifty-eight of my own hand is from the top of that cage down to the cut in that wall, and fifty-eight divided by two is twenty-nine, and the twenty-ninth day from the second of this month is the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, and I have twenty-nine days to put one word in the air in this room.”

And the person at that table did not turn round and did not look up, and the hands went on at the far end of the wood, and there was not one mark on anything in that room.
