# Two Figures In One Column

She wrote a five into her own row on the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four at the ninth hour, standing, in daylight, with a nib out of a bag she had carried up eight hundred and eighty steps, under the figure the room had put there, and it cost her an arm and it cannot be taken out.

## The Board She Chose

She went under that table at the second hour of the twenty-second with a cross-beam across her shoulders and lay on her back on the stone and got her hand round her own strip and pulled it out from under the middle of that table and brought it out with her.

It is a board of pale wood a foot long and two inches across with the grain running up it. It came up that stair in an armful at the second hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, with about forty other things, and it has been lying face up on that stone ever since, and this is the first time in one hundred and seventy-one days it has been off the floor.

She did not use one of the forty boards in the stack and she had thought about it for three days and she is not going to explain why out loud because there is not one instrument in this world that would tell the difference.

She said, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table with the strip in her left hand:

“This is mine. This is the only piece of this room that has ever been mine and it has been lying face up under a table where it is not looked at for one hundred and seventy-one days, and I am not going to put it in a bundle with forty other things and send it out of the building.”

She said, “And I am not going to be rescued out of it and I am not going to be let. There is not one mouth in this building that knows I am in this room. There is no voice in my head that is going to ask me not to, and if there were one I would say no to it where I could be heard and I would go on.”

## What A Price Is

She stood there for a count of a hundred and forty before she did it, and she said out loud what she was about to do, so that the room had it in the air before the nib went near the wood.

She said, “A row has a head and a head is a figure, and the figure at the head of this one is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes and it is what I cost.”

She said, “A figure that is a price can only be added up. I can add it to another price and I get a price and there is not one thing I can do with the answer except keep going on adding.”

She said, “A figure that is a person can be counted. You can count how many there are. You cannot add a person to a price and get anything you could name.”

She said, “And I have been escaping being counted since I was nine years old.”

She said, “And there is a column on this wood with fourteen places in it and one of them has a price in it, and in one hundred and seventy-one days not one person in this building has put anything else in any of them, and I am about to be the first thing that has ever put a second figure in a column in this room.”

And then she stopped, and she waited until her breath was at nine, and she said it one last time.

She said, “Five stages into the seventh realm, taken at the tenth hour of the eighteenth of this month at the near end of that table, on a board, with a nib that has been in a bag for thirteen years, in my own hand, and I am saying it out loud standing up in daylight in the ordinary voice because that is the rule in this building and I am not going to be the one who breaks it.”

She said, “Five.”

## The Figure Under The Figure

She put the strip flat on the top of the table at the near end of it, which she can reach and which she can get up on, and she held it down with the fingers of her left hand and she put the nib to the wood with her right hand, three fingers and a thumb, on an arm that has been open since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four.

The second pair of cuts down in that column was under the first one and she had the tip of the nib in it in a count of nine.

And she wrote five.

It is one stroke and a turn. It is the same five that is on the board that went under the shelf on the eighteenth of that month at the tenth hour, and she looked at it for a count of twenty after she had written it and it was the same figure, and she had not made it up, and she was not going to be able to take it out.

And then her right forearm went down by the width of the little finger on her left hand.

It did it in a count of about nine while the nib was still on the wood, and it was the same width as the twelfth of that month and the same width as the hand at the far end of that table goes down every time that lamp is turned, and she had worked that out on the fourteenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four that the writing is not free and that the cost of the writing comes off the hand that writes.

And the hand that wrote it was hers.

She said, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table, with her arm open in front of her and her left hand round the wrist of it:

“There it is again.”

She said, “And it is me that paid it and not that person at the far end of this table, and that is the first time in four hundred years that the cost of a figure in this room has come off anybody except the one standing at it.”

## What It Was Like Afterwards

She stood at the near end of that table with the strip flat on the wood and her right forearm open in front of her for a count of a hundred and forty and did not say anything at all, and that is the first time in a hundred and seventy-two days that she has been in this room with something of hers in it and nothing to say.

And what she was doing was not feeling sorry and it was not feeling brave. She was waiting to find out whether the room would do anything.

It did not do anything. The light came down that wall two of her own hand a day and the low door stood open on a stair that goes down eight hundred and eighty steps, and the person at the far end of that table did not look up, and there was no sound anywhere in that room, and nothing had changed in that room since the fifth hour of that morning except that a forearm was thinner and a piece of wood had a five in it.

She said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the seventh hour of the twenty-second of that month:

“That is the whole of what it feels like and I had expected more and I am not going to pretend I had expected more.”

And then she said the thing she is going to have to live with, and she said it standing up, and it is the plainest sentence she has said in this room in a hundred and seventy-one days.

She said, “Nothing about that has changed. And that is how I am going to know, for the rest of my life, that I am in a row.”

## What Cannot Be Undone

She read it back with her fingers before she put it down, on the flat of the table, in daylight, standing, with the nib still in her hand.

The head is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes.

The column has a figure in the first pair of cuts under the head, and it is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes, in her own hand, put there by a hand at that table on the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, out of words I said in the open at two posts a long way from this building.

And the column has a figure in the second pair of cuts under that, and it is five, and it is in her own hand, and she wrote it nine minutes ago with a nib that has never once touched a column in this building.

She said, “That is two figures in one column.”

She said, “And a column with two figures in it is a column that gets counted, because one of them is a price and one of them is not a price, and the only way a room can do anything with a column with two figures in it is add them up.”

She said, “And it cannot be added up. Ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes and five. That is not a figure. There is not one thing in this building that knows what to do with that answer and there is not one thing in the whole of the world that I know of.”

She said, “And so this room is going to be holding a thing it cannot use, on a piece of wood a foot long and two inches across, under a table where it is not looked at, and it has been above the line for one hundred and seventy-one days and there is no instrument in the world that takes a line out of a record that has been above the line for four hundred years.”

And then she said the part that is about herself and it is the plainest thing she has said in this room in a hundred and seventy-one days.

She said, “I have been escaping being counted since I was nine years old, and I got a bar at nine and I have carried it ten years and I left it against a wall on purpose on the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four because there was nothing in this room I could put it down for.”

She said, “And there was not one thing in this room I could put it down for until the eighth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen, and it took me four days at the near end of that table to find out what it was, and it was a column with thirteen empty places in it.”

And she said the last of it standing, in daylight, on the twenty-fourth of that month at the ninth hour, with the strip of pale wood flat on the top of that table and her right forearm open across her knees.

She said, “I am not going to be let out of this and I am not going to be pulled out of it and nobody has asked me and nobody is going to.”

She said, “And being entered is not the same thing as being rescued and I know the difference and I have not confused the two for one minute, and I have not put my own figure in a column so that something would come and take it out.”

She said, “I put it in there because a figure that is a price can only be added up and a figure that is a person can be counted, and there are not one person in this building who has ever counted me, and I am nineteen years old and there is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain who does not know what a row is.”

She said, “And I did that at the ninth hour of the twenty-second of that month, standing, in daylight, at the place where the arms go, and there is not one mouth above this line that knows it happened, and I am not going to tell the room.”
