# The Question He Carried Up

She was sitting on the floor of that room on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four with her back against the doorframe and her right arm open across her knees, and the boots came up the second stair at the sixth hour and took a very long time.

## Boots She Has Heard Twice

She knew that stair in the dark. She had gone up it four times and had counted nine hundred of it before the number stopped being a thing she could hold, and she knew the turn, and she knew the sound a boot makes on the ninth step down off the turn, which is the first stone of that run that is not flat.

These were slower than that, and there were more of them, and about two thirds of the way up they stopped for a count of thirty and then started again.

She said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, to a room with a person in it who did not move:

“That is not the one who comes up on the first. That one has been on that road a year and puts forty boards down without breaking his stride and does not stop once on the whole way.”

And then she said the other thing, and it took a count of twenty to get it out of her.

“That is somebody who has not come up that stair.”

## A Boy Standing In The Door

He came out of the place in the wall behind the lamp’s shelf at the far end of the room and he was wet through and he had his hand on the frame where the stair comes out, and he was not standing, he was holding himself up on the wall.

He was sixteen. She knows that now and she did not know it then and she worked it out of him in three goes: the height of him against the door, and the fact that his coat was a man’s coat with the sleeves turned back twice and still short, and the way he was breathing.

He had come up eight hundred and eighty steps. There are eight flights in the tower’s own stair, four hundred and forty in the first four with a rail on them and four hundred and forty in the second four with nothing on that side at all, and he had come up the whole of it in the dark, because there is not one light on any part of that stair and has not been for nine years.

He got to the doorframe and stopped there and put his back against it the way she puts her back against it.

He said, “I am sorry. I know there is not meant to be anybody up here.”

## The Question He Came Up For

He got his breath. It took him a while and he did not try to hide it.

He said, “The lamp on the fourth landing went out.”

She said, “I know when it went out.”

He said, “It went out on the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four. It was the last of the oil and I put it out myself at about the ninth hour and I have not been able to light it since, and there has not been any oil in this building since, and the carter brings a jar on the first and it has been coming up empty since before I was on the floor.”

He said, “And they have started asking me why the lamp at the top of this tower is still lit.”

He said, “That is the whole of it. I have come up eight hundred and eighty steps to ask one question and it is that one and I have not got anything else.”

## What She Could Not Say

She stood there at the near end of that table with her arm open and she had her answer ready in the ordinary voice and she did not say it.

The answer was that there is no oil in this building and there has not been for nine years, and the lamp in that cage at the far end of this room has nothing behind it and no wick in it and nothing at all to put in it, and it is burning because a person at that table puts a hand up between four bars of iron and closes it on the base of the light and the light comes down and then goes up, and a person at that table comes back from that cage with less arm than they went in with.

She could not say that. Not to a boy of sixteen who had just climbed that stair in the dark.

And the other answer, which was the true one, was that she had put a day of her own into that lamp four days ago with her own hand and the day had not come back, and there was no way on earth to explain that to a person who had been asked a fair question about a lamp on a landing.

So she said, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice:

“I cannot answer it.”

## Three Answers She Gave Instead

The boy waited. He did not go out and he did not say anything and he stood in a doorway with his back against it and waited, and she has been in a room for sixty days with a person in it who had never once waited for her for anything.

She said, “I will tell you what I know and then you will still not have an answer and you will not be able to use any of it.”

She said, “The lamp in this room is not an oil lamp. There is no dish on it and no reservoir and no wick and nothing behind it, and the wall is the wall, and the light does not lean when you put your hand past it with the door standing open.”

She said, “And there is a person at that table who has been standing at it for a long time and who does not look up, and I have been in this room sixty days and that person has not turned round once.”

She said, “And that is everything I have got, and it is not an answer to your question and I am not going to pretend that it is one.”

## What She Wanted To Put In His Hand

She wanted to put something in that boy’s hand.

That is the whole of what was in her at the sixth hour of the eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four, standing in a room four miles above a mountain, and it is the first time in nineteen years that a person has come to her with a question and she has had something she wanted to give.

She had two coppers she cannot count. She had a bar nine feet nine lying flat against the wall on the left of this room with its broken end towards the door, and she is not carrying it up nine hundred steps. She had a strip of pale wood under this table with a head and a column on it and one figure in the column, and she is not going to hand a person a record out of a room where things are decided by a hand.

She had nothing at all.

She said out loud, in the ordinary voice, to the boy and not to the table:

“There is not one thing in this room I am able to put in your hand.”

And the boy said, “That is all right. I did not come up here for that.”

## What He Said Before He Went Down

He said, “You were on the fourth landing with a bar on your shoulder at the first hour on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and you were on the copying floor for about four hours, and then you were not.”

She said, “I was.”

He said, “I did not come up because of that. I have thought about it and I did not come up because of that.”

He looked at the table. He looked at it the way a person looks at a thing and not the way she has ever had a thing looked at in nineteen years, and he did it for a count of about nine without knowing he was doing it.

He said, “I am going to have to go down and say that I do not know.”

She said, “Say that you do not know. It is true and it is the only true thing either of us has got this morning.”

He said, “I do not know. I have been not knowing about it since the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and I have said so twice and they have stopped asking me and started asking the carter.”

## What Was Left Of It

He went back up the second stair and out of the place in the wall behind the lamp’s shelf and she listened to him go, and going is a great deal slower than coming and he took a count of two hundred to get to the turn and then stood on the turn for a count of forty before he started again.

Then she was alone with the table and the person at it and the bar against the wall.

And she stood at the near end of that table and did the sum out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

She said, “The eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four. Twenty-nine days, because the seventh is the seventh and this is the eighth. Two thousand three hundred and sixty-one less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and two days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and it was eight hundred and one yesterday.”

She said, “The light at the far end of this room is twelve of my own hand below the top of that cage, and it goes down two a day, and there are fifty-eight of my own hand from the top of that cage to the line cut in the wall behind it.”

She said, “Fifty-eight less twelve is forty-six, and forty-six divided by two is twenty-three, and the twenty-third day from the eighth of this month is the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, which is the day the next forty boards come up that stair.”

## What She Had Not Known Until He Was Gone

And then she stood still for a while, and then she said the last of it out loud, and it was the thing she had not known until that boy’s boots had gone up the turn, and she said it standing, in the ordinary voice, with not one other thing in that room able to hear a word of it.

She said, “He came up eight hundred and eighty steps in the dark with no rail on the top four hundred and forty of them, and he was wet through, and he has not been up those steps in two years without stopping, and he did it because a lamp went out on a landing.”

She said, “And I have been going up and down those steps for sixty days in the dark and I have not once thought about what they are.”

She said, “There is not one lamp on that stair and there has not been one for nine years, and a boy of sixteen has just gone down all eight hundred and eighty of them because I could not answer him, and I am going to have to take him back down them myself and I am not going to have one thing to say to him on the way.”

The person at the table did not move.

The light at the far end of the room came down two of her own hand in that hour and she watched it do it and said the number out loud, and there were eight hundred and two days of her in this building and twenty-three days of light in that lamp, and she had put one of them in on the fourth of this month and it had not come back.
