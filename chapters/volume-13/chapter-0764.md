# Two Clocks On One Piece Of Wood

She read both of those figures with her fingers for five days, standing, in daylight, and worked out on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four that the one she wrote is out of date as well, and by a different amount, and that she cannot correct it, and that this is the second of two ways in four days that she has made herself uncorrectable.

## What She Did With It Afterwards

She put the strip back on the stone under that table at the near end of it, face up, flat, in the same place it has been since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and she squared it up with her fingers so that the grain was running the same way it had been running.

And then she got out from under the table and went and stood at the near end of it and did not touch it again for five days.

She had expected something to happen and nothing happened. The person at the far end of that table did not look up. No hand came off the near edge. No board came out from under the shelf. The light came down that wall two of her own hand a day and the low door stood open on a stair that goes down eight hundred and eighty steps, and there was not one thing in that room that knew a figure had gone into a column at the ninth hour of the twenty-second of that month.

She said that out loud, standing, in daylight, on the twenty-fifth of that month:

“I have done the one thing I have spent nineteen years not doing and the room has not made a sound about it, and I have been standing here for five days waiting for it to make one, and it is not going to, and that is what being counted is.”

## Five Days Of It

She did not do anything else with those five days and she wants that on the page, because everything she has done in a hundred and sixty-eight days in this room has been a thing she found out, and five days of doing nothing at all to a thing she has already done is new.

She got up at the fifth hour and took a reading of her own right forearm, because it had been down by the width of the little finger on her left hand since the twelfth of that month and it had not come back, and she wanted a second figure against the first.

It was the same. She took three more over five days at the seventh hour and they were all the same to the width of a hair, and she wrote nothing down about that, because there is nothing in this room to write on except the strip she has just put a figure in and she is not going to use that for arithmetic.

She ate. She has eaten at the second and the seventh hour on every day since the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and she has never once thought about doing it, and she thought about it on the twenty-fifth and found that the whole of the arrangement was that there was not one person here to notice whether she did.

She did not go down the low door once. She did not go down the second stair. She did not put a hand on that table except to move her own strip back onto the stone.

And on the twenty-sixth she caught herself doing the thing she has done every day for one hundred and sixty-eight days, which is to count out loud, standing at the near end of that table, what the room was keeping for her, and she got as far as the head of the row before she stopped, and she stood there for a count of forty with her mouth shut.

She said out loud afterwards, standing, in daylight:

“I have been keeping a figure of a figure for five months. And there are two figures down there now and I am going to be able to do that for the rest of my life, and there is not one person in this building who is going to ask me to stop.”

## Reading Both Of Them With Her Fingers

She read them every day from the twenty-fourth to the twenty-eighth, standing, on the flat of her back under that table with her arm open on the stone beside her, and she named them out loud so that she would know she had not made either of them up.

She said, “The head of this row is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes. It is what I cost. I chose that figure in the open at two posts at the first hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and it went up that stair in an armful at the second hour of the same morning, and a hand at that table wrote it in this column, and that hand has never once known whose words it was writing.”

She said, “The second pair of cuts down in that column has five in it. It is one stage and it is five stages into the seventh realm and I wrote it at the ninth hour of the twenty-second of this month with a nib out of a bag and it took a count of nine.”

And then on the twenty-sixth she did the other three, out loud, lying under that table with her hand flat on the wood.

She said, “The three strips beside this one have gone soft at every edge and they are older than mine by a great deal and I cannot say how much and I am not going to guess. And every one of them has a figure at the head and exactly one figure in the column, and there is not one pair of empty cuts in any of them.”

She said, “And there is not one second figure in any of them. Not in four hundred years. And I have put one in mine in a count of nine on the twenty-second of this month.”

## Out Of Date By Different Amounts

And on the twenty-seventh she worked out the part that she had not thought of when she did it, and it took her most of that day, and she found it because she went to check the first of the two figures against the day and could not get it to agree.

She said, out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table on the twenty-seventh:

“The figure at the head of this row goes out of date at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and it has been going out of date since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and it is eight days, nineteen hours and twelve minutes out of date this morning, and it gets more out of date every hour and there is not one instrument above this line that would tell me so.”

She said, “And five does not do that.”

She said, “Five does not go out of date by hours. It went out of date the moment I put it there, because five was true at the tenth hour of the eighteenth of this month and I am not going to stop being five stages into the seventh realm tonight, and it is not going to be true on the day after the sixth one, and there is not one person in this building who knows that and not one mouth that would tell me when.”

She said, “And I cannot correct it. The figure at the head I could correct if there was a person to correct it, because a figure is a number and a number can be written again. Five is not a number that gets corrected. It gets replaced, and the day it gets replaced on is a day a hand in the light of that lamp chooses, and I worked that out on the eighteenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and I am not going to work it out again.”

And then she stopped for a count of a hundred and said the sentence that is the plainest thing she has said in this room, and it is about one person.

She said, “I have spent nineteen years making sure that not one thing about me could be added up and not one thing about me could be counted.”

She said, “And in four days I have put two figures in one column, and one of them is wrong by a quarter of an hour every hour and the other one is wrong from the day after the next time I take something off that table, and there is not one instrument in the world that can put either of them right.”

## A Row That Gets Added Up

And on the twenty-eighth of that month at the ninth hour she stood at the near end of that long table and said the whole of it out loud, standing, in daylight, and she said it once and she did not improve on it.

She said, “Ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes, and five. That is what is in that column and it is in my own hand on both sides of it and the second one I wrote on the twenty-second of this month.”

She said, “And a row with two figures in it is a row that gets added up. That is the whole of what I did and it is why I did it and it is the thing I have been escaping since I was nine years old.”

She said, “A price can only be added to. A person can only be counted. And I have put one of each in the same column on the same piece of wood in the same hand, and the room has not made a sound, and it is not going to, and there is not one person in this building who will ever read it.”

And then she said the last of it, and she said it about the bar and about the bag and about a boy, and it took a count of ninety.

She said, “There is a bar nine feet nine against the wall on the left of this room that I left there on purpose and I have not picked it up in a hundred and seventy-one days.”

She said, “There is a strip of hand-made paper nine inches long in a bag at the bottom of that tower with eighty-one lines on it and the same sum under it in words, and I am not going down for it, and that is not the reason.”

She said, “And there is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain who would come up eight hundred and eighty steps if I asked him to, and I am not going to ask him, because I am not going to leave that strip of wood out on this table for a day while I am gone, and I am not going to put a day of my own life into eight hundred and eighty steps while I have got a thing in this room that is mine.”

She said, “And that is the first time in a hundred and seventy-eight days that I have had something in this room that is mine, and I have not got one word to give him, and he has asked me twice about a lamp.”
