# The Body At The Table

She worked out on the sixth, seventh, eighth and ninth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four what a comparison is when the thing being compared is a person and the other half of the comparison is four hundred years old, and the answer is that every figure in every row in this building for four hundred years is a figure about the person at that table and not about the thing it was sent up about.

## Four Hundred Years Of One Arm

She started it because on the second of that month she had stood at the near end of that table and watched a hand measure itself.

The rule she worked out on the ninth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four is that a row is entered by the person it is about and that the hand writes down what the person says and nothing else, and she did not go over that. She has got it and she is not going to explain it again to herself.

What she had not asked in a hundred and forty-five days was where a figure gets compared with anything.

She went and got under that table at the second hour of the sixth of that month with a cross-beam across her shoulders and lay on her back on the stone with her hand flat on her own strip and her arm open on the floor beside her.

Lying on the stone with her arm open on the floor beside her, she said it out loud:

“A figure is said out loud in another room and it comes up a stair in an armful and a hand puts it in a column, and the hand copies it exactly. A copy is the same figure. It is not a measure.”

She said, “And so the question is not where it came from. The question is what it is a measure of, and there is no number anywhere in this building for it to be a measure of, and I have got that.”

She said, “And the only thing in this room it can be a measure of is the person standing at that table.”

## A Figure About That Person

She came out from under that table and said the rest of it standing up, in daylight, at the near end of that table, on the sixth and then the seventh and then the eighth, one item at a time, and she did not say any item of it twice.

She said, “Every figure that has gone into a column in this room in four hundred years was said out loud by a person who was in some other building, on some other day, standing up, and it came up a stair, and a hand read it against the body of the one person at that table.”

She said, “And that person has been the same person for the whole of that time. Same arm, same reach, same eleven seconds, same place. And every figure this building has ever kept is a comparison and not a measurement, and a comparison is a statement about the thing it is compared against.”

She said, “And so the figure at the head of my row is a statement about that person’s arm. It says how much has come off it and how much has gone through it. It says nothing whatever about what I am.”

And then she stopped, and she stood there for a count of a hundred and forty, and she said the sentence that is the whole of what six days of standing at the near end of a table came to.

She said, “That person has been standing at that table for four hundred years and has been less every single time that lamp was turned, and I am the first thing that has ever come up that stair that could see it.”

She said, “And every figure in every row in this county for four hundred years has been a way of measuring how much of them there is.”

And then she said the plain one, which is the sentence she owes a person for four hundred years of arithmetic not one of them has ever been given.

She said, “There is a person at the far end of this table who has spent themselves keeping this county’s arithmetic in order and has not been told, once, in four hundred years, that every number they have written down is a number about them.”

## What She Cannot Do About It

And she said out loud on the seventh and the eighth of that month what she is not going to do about it, standing, in daylight, and it took a count of a hundred and forty each time and she did not say either of them twice.

She said, “A room cannot be asked. I worked that out on the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and I am not going to work it out again.”

She said, “And I cannot be given a thing, so there is no way for me to hand a person a piece of what I have just worked out, and there is no version of this building in which a person who has stood at a table for four hundred years is going to be handed a figure by a woman who cannot reach the table.”

She said, “And I cannot say it out loud in a room and have it heard by the only other thing in it, because that person has not looked up once in a hundred and fifty days and did not look up when I said a name on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, and because a person standing at that table in this building is an instrument and an instrument does not get told what its readings mean.”

And then she said the thing that is about her and not about that person, and it is the part of six days she had not been able to get round to until the eighth.

She said, “I have spent this month working out that every figure in this building is a figure about one person who has not been told, and the whole of what I want out of it is that I would like one person in this world to be told.”

She said, “And I cannot do it, and there is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain who keeps a lamp going in a cold cage and does not know why, and he is the only person in this county I have any way of speaking to at all.”

She said, “And what I have worked out in six days is a figure about somebody else’s arm. That is not a thing I can hand to a boy. I have found out that the ruler is tired and I have got nothing to give anybody except the finding out.”

## The Two Strips Under The Table

She went back under there on the eighth and read all four of them with her fingers, because she had only ever read the heads of the other three.

There are four strips under that table. Hers and three older ones gone soft at every edge. Every one has a head at one end that is a figure and every one has a column, and the column is a column of figures and not a list of things.

She put her fingers in the wood at the head of it and said it out loud, with her back on the stone and the boards over her:

“There are four rows on the floor of this room and every one of them is a figure about the arm of the person standing at it.”

She said, “And three of those figures are older than mine by a great deal and I cannot say how much and I am not going to guess, and not one of them has anything written on the head of it but a figure, because a head is a figure and it has always been a figure.”

She said, “And not one of them has a name on it, or a place, or a date, or anything at all about the thing it was sent up about, and I have worked out in six days that that is not an omission.”

She said, “That is the arrangement. A figure is a measure of the one arm and it is not a thing about anybody, and a column of those is a record of what this room has cost.”

And she came out and said the rest of it standing at the near end of that table, and it was about a boy and a thing she had worked out three days before and not improved on.

She said, “I worked out on the fifth of this month that there is not one thing in this room that has ever looked at me, and that is why I cannot be measured standing still.”

She said, “And the boy of sixteen on a floor four miles down that mountain is a thing that has never been looked at either, and he cannot be measured here and he cannot be put in a column here and there is no way of telling him that without first telling him what a row is, and he has asked me twice about a lamp.”

She said, “And there is a stretch of the far side of that table the length of my own forearm, paler than the rest of the wood, with a shape worn into it that no tool made, and nothing in front of it and nothing on it, and I found it on the ninth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four.”

She said, “I have not touched it and I have not said one word about it out loud in a hundred and forty-nine days, and I am saying one word about it now and not a second one, and I would like to know why I am doing it on this day.”

And then she stopped, because she had said it, and she wanted to hear how it sounded in a room after six days of saying everything else out loud in it, and it sounded exactly like the other two sentences, and she could not tell which of the three of them was the dangerous one.

## The Sums On The Ninth

She did the sums at the ninth hour of the ninth of that month, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table with the light at the far end of it fourteen of her own hand below the top of that cage.

She said, “Two thousand four hundred and fifty-two less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and ninety-three days, and two thousand four hundred and fifty-two less two thousand three hundred and one is a hundred and fifty-one days in this room.”

She said, “And fifty-eight less fourteen is forty-four and forty-four divided by two is twenty-two and the twenty-second day from the ninth of this month is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four, and the figure at the head of my row is seven days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes out of date.”

And then she said the last of it, and it is the thing the whole nine days came to and she said it standing in daylight with nothing in her hands.

She said, “I have been a figure at the head of a column for a hundred and fifty-one days and the figure is what I cost and it is a statement about somebody else’s arm, and the two of those are the same fact and I did not know they were the same fact until this morning.”

She said, “And so there is not one instrument in this building that has ever measured a thing in this building. There has been one person, standing still, for four hundred years, being used as a ruler by everybody who has ever sent anything up a stair.”

She said, “And I cannot be that person and I am not going to try, and I have not got another instrument, and the only thing left to find out is what this room does when the thing it is measuring against is not available to it at all.”

And then she got under that table one more time and lay on the stone with her hand flat on her own strip and her fingers in the second pair of cuts down in that column, which was empty, and had been empty for the whole of the hundred and fifty-one days.

And she left it empty.
