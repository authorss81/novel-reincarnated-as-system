# A Rate She Can Measure

She found the rate of that lamp on the seventeenth of the month beginning 2,324, standing on the top of a long table on her knees in the light with her own hand as the instrument, and it is two of her hands a day, and it is the first number she has been able to measure in this room and it is not a rate anything else in the world is going at.

## Getting A Measure Of It

You cannot measure how far a light has fallen by looking at a light.

What she did on the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,324 was put the bottom edge of the bright place the lamp makes on the wall behind it against her own hand, laid flat, edge on, three fingers and a thumb across.

On the fourteenth the bottom of the light was at the top of her hand when she held it against the wall.

On the fifteenth it was half of a hand lower.

On the sixteenth it was a whole hand lower again and her own hand was the same hand.

She did not write any of it down. She has four numbers and she can hold four numbers and she held them across four days and on the seventeenth she put the four numbers in order out loud at the second hour, standing on the top of a table four feet off the floor, and there is not one mark on the wall and there is not one word of it anywhere except in her own head.

## Fifty-Eight Of Her Own Hand

And then she measured the other half of it, which is how far the light has to fall before it is at that line cut in the stone.

The top of the cage is as high as that light has ever been and she has watched it every day since the eighth of this month and it has not been higher.

She laid her hand against the wall from the top of the cage down to the cut and she did it four times because the first three times she lost her place, and the fourth time came out at fifty-eight of her own hand from the top of the cage to the line.

Fifty-eight.

And it falls two of her hand a day.

She said it out loud standing on the table, in the ordinary voice, at the fifth hour of the seventeenth of the month beginning 2,324:

“Fifty-eight of my hand from the top of that cage to that cut in the wall, and two of my hand a day, and I am not going to do that sum standing on a table in the dark half of a room. I am going to do it standing on the floor in daylight with my feet on a thing I can count.”

She got off the far side of the table onto the floor and stood with her back to the right-hand wall where the door is.

## Getting On The Table And The Four Days Of It

She got onto the table on the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,324 at the second hour and went twenty-two paces along it on her knees and knelt in front of that lamp, and this is the second time she has done that and the first time she has taken an instrument up there.

The instrument is her own hand.

She laid it flat, edge on, three fingers and a thumb across, against the wall behind the lamp, and she put the bottom edge of the light against the top of it, and then she took her hand away and looked at the wall.

And then she came back down off the table and went and lay down at the near end with her eyes shut and held four numbers in the order they were in, which is the only place she has ever kept anything that mattered.

On the fifteenth she did the whole thing again and got half a hand.

On the sixteenth she did it again at the second hour and at the fifth hour and got a whole hand, and the same hand, and she wrote nothing on the wall because there is nothing in this room to write on and she has said out loud twice that nothing from this room is going into five blank leaves against her ribs.

On the seventeenth she did it at the second hour and got another half, and that made four days of it, and she got the numbers out loud standing on the floor with her back to the door.

She said, “Nothing. Half a hand. A hand. Half a hand. That is four days, and the first one was nothing because I did not know what I was measuring yet, and the three after it are the same three.”

She said, “Two a day. It is two of my own hand a day and I have got that by three readings in three days and I could not have got it any other way in this building, and I could not have got it at all out of a book because there is not one book in this building.”

## What She Cannot Do With It

And then she said the part that turned it from a thing she had into a thing she wanted, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the eighth hour of the seventeenth of the month beginning 2,324.

She said, “I have got fifty-eight and I have got two, and I have not done them to each other yet, and I am going to do them tomorrow with my feet on this floor and not on that table.”

She said, “And I am aware that the last figure I got in this room is two days and seven hours and twelve minutes out of date on a strip of wood on the floor of this room, and that figure is not in the same kind of thing as two of my own hand a day, and I do not know what kind of thing a day of a person is and I do not know what kind of thing a hand of a wall is.”

She said, “And that is the first time in nineteen years I have had two correct numbers that do not fit and both of them are mine.”

## The Other Rate

And while she was standing there she did the other one, because she has been doing the other one every day since the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054 and it takes nine counts.

Two thousand three hundred and forty less two thousand two hundred and ninety-four is forty-six days, and forty-six days of an hour and twelve minutes is three thousand three hundred and twelve minutes, which is fifty-five hours and twelve minutes, which is two days and seven hours and twelve minutes.

Two days and seven hours and twelve minutes is how far off that figure on the strip of wood under the table is, right now, at the fifth hour of the seventeenth of a month, and it was two days and one hour and twelve minutes five days ago and it is going at the same rate it has gone at since the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,054.

A light in a cage is going down two of her own hand a day.

A figure in a column under a head is going down an hour and twelve minutes a day.

And not one person above this line has an instrument for it, because nobody above this line has ever had anything taken away from them at a rate they could measure either.

## The Thing She Could Not Say

And then she stood against the wall with her feet on the floor of that room and worked out how to say it, and it took her a count of four hundred, and she could not find one sentence.

What she wanted to say was: they are the same.

And that is a lie, because she has got two things going down and no way at all of knowing that one of them is why the other one is going down. She has a lamp with nothing in it and a figure with her price at the head of it and a strip of wood on the floor and a row and a room, and there is not one thing in nineteen years that has connected any two of those.

If she says it is the same and it is not, she has said a false thing out loud in a room where a hand copies figures exactly.

And if she says it is not the same, that is a lie too, because she does not know that either, and she has never in her life said a thing she did not know to be true.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the sixth hour of the seventeenth of the month beginning 2,324:

“I have said every single thing I have found since the eighth of this month out loud, in the ordinary voice, standing up, one item at a time, and there is not one of them anybody could have used against me.”

She said, “And I have got one thing I cannot put into words because I have not got an instrument for it and I have been waiting four days for one and it has not come.”

She said, “And I am not going to say a guess in this room. That is the first thing in nineteen years I have had to hold back and I am not going to hold anything else back, and if that costs me the one thing I came up here for, then that is what it costs.”

## What She Has

And then she said what she has, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the seventh hour of the seventeenth of the month beginning 2,324, in a room with a person in it who has not turned round once since the eighth of this month.

She said, “I have got a lamp that is going down at a rate I can measure, and a cut in a wall that is the same cut every time I have looked at it, and fifty-eight of my own hand between the top of the cage and the line.”

She said, “And a figure under a table that is going down at a rate I can measure.”

She said, “And there is not one person above this line with an instrument for it.”

She sat down on the floor at the near end of the table with her back against the wall, and the four numbers went into her head in the order they were in, and she had them, and the light at the far end of that room was two of her hand lower than it had been four days ago and coming down at the same rate.
