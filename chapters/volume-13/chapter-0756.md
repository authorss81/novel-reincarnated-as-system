# The Fifth Bundle And The Cut

The fifth bundle came up that stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four and the light at the far end of that room came down onto the cut in the stone behind it at the fourth hour of the same day, for the second time in four hundred years.

She stood four feet off the end of that long table with a person in it and did not tell it.

A hand went up into that cage at the second hour of the second of that month and the arm that did it is smaller afterwards by the width of the little finger on her left hand.

## The Last Three Nights

She started waiting on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen, which is a word she has never used out loud about anything and used about that light.

On the twenty-eighth the bottom edge of it was fifty-two of her own hand below the top of that cage. On the twenty-ninth it was fifty-four. On the thirtieth it was fifty-six, and she stood in front of that wall with the lamp on her face at the ninth hour and did the sum for the last time.

She said, “Fifty-eight less fifty-six is two and two divided by two is one, and the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four is one day from this one.”

She said, “And that is the whole of what is left of the ordinary arrangement, because tomorrow the mark falls on that cut instead of on a thirtieth, and it is the second time in four hundred years that has happened and it is the second time it has been my doing.”

And then she did the other sum, standing at the near end of that table, because she wanted the two of them in the same breath before anything else happened.

She said, “Nine hundred and four steps on that stair, the same number going up and coming down, and it is a hundred and forty-two days since I came into this room, and one day to the first.”

She said, “I have never in nineteen years had a thing I counted and a thing that was going to happen on the same day.”

## The Fifth Bundle On The Table

The boots came up the turn at the second hour of the first of that month and the man with about forty boards under one arm and a bundle of papers in a cloth under the other came out of the place in the wall behind the lamp’s shelf and put them down and went back out without looking at anything.

She stood four feet off the near end of that table and watched it for the fifth time.

Ten minutes for the forty boards at four to a minute and thirty for the bundle, and the hands did not stop and did not hurry and did not go back, and the head came down over the near edge the whole time, and nothing in that room knew that a woman was standing four feet off the end of it watching a thing go that she had no part in.

And at the end of it she said out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table:

“Forty minutes, and it has been forty minutes every month since the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four, and not one of the forty boards went up that stair with anything in it that I can see.”

She said, “And a boy of sixteen counted forty of those in ten minutes on a floor four miles down that mountain without being told twice, and I have not counted them on my own once in five months and I cannot get to that floor and look.”

She said, “And there is one figure on one of those boards that I said out loud in this room on the tenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, and there is one name on one of those boards that I said out loud in this room on the twenty-eighth of that month, and there is not one way in the world I have of telling which two of the forty they were.”

## Where The Light Stopped On The Wall

At the fourth hour of that same day the light came down to the cut and stopped on it.

She was standing at the near end of that table. She had been standing there for a count of ninety with her arm open across her knees.

And then the wall behind that lamp changed and she saw it happen. Not a light coming closer. The bottom edge of it went down the last two of her own hand and it went over the cut in the stone, because the cut is not a line of height, it is a place, and the light stopped on the near side of it and did not go under.

She said, out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice:

“There it is again.”

She said, “And it is the second time. And the first time was on the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and I did that one on purpose and I spent a day of my own life to do it.”

She said, “And this one is on the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four and the reason is the same day, and there is not one row in this building with a word about either of them, and the man who brought forty things up that stair two hours ago did not look at the wall behind the lamp on his way out and he is not going to think about it on the road down.”

She said it to a room with a person standing in it and she did not raise her voice, and not one thing in that room heard it, and she had known that before she said it.

Then she stood there for the rest of that day with the light standing on the cut and she did not put a hand on the table once and she did not go near the shelf.

## What Came Out Of That Cage

At the second hour of the second of that month a hand went up between the four bars of that cage and closed on the base of the light, and it came up past the top of the cage in about eleven seconds, and the person at that table was standing at the near edge of it afterwards with both hands on it exactly as they had been standing.

She had her own hand up at the end of that forearm before it and her own hand up at the end of that forearm after it.

And it was down by the width of the little finger on her left hand, and she does not have a little finger on that hand and has not had one since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and the one she used is the same as the other times.

She said out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the seventh hour of the second of that month:

“There it is again.”

And then she did not say anything for a count of a hundred and forty.

## The Figure On That Arm

That was the fourth time she had measured that arm either side of a stroke going onto a board, and it came out the same both times on that day, and the part that was new was that she could not put a name on it.

She said, “I worked that out on the fourteenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four. One lamp and one copied figure cost that arm the same width, and the room’s ordinary forty minutes cost it nothing, and I said it four times on the fourteenth and the fifteenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and I have not improved on it.”

She said, “And there is not one way in this building to tell a lamp from a row, because they cost the same and they go in the same hand.”

She said, “And I do not know what I have just paid that arm for. It is the width of one figure. It could be the figure I said out loud on the tenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four. It could be the name I said out loud on the twenty-eighth of that month. And it could be neither, because forty things go up that stair on the first of a month and one hand copies every one of them exactly, and that hand has never once known whose words it was writing.”

She said, “And I was standing four feet off the end of that table with nothing to write with and no way to see which board a stroke went onto, and that is not a thing that has gone wrong. That is the arrangement.”

And then she said the part that was about her, and it is the first time she has said this in a hundred and forty-four days.

She said, “I have been a figure at the head of a column for a hundred and forty-four days and the figure is what I cost and not what I am, and the last two times I said something out loud in this room that cost that arm something, and both times I could not tell afterwards whether it was me.”

## The Sums On The Second Of That Month

She did the sums on the second of that month at the ninth hour, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table, with the light above the top of that cage for the first time since the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four.

She said, “Two thousand four hundred and forty-five less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and eighty-six days, and two thousand four hundred and forty-five less two thousand three hundred and one is a hundred and forty-four days in this room.”

She said, “And the figure at the head of my row is seven days, thirteen hours and twelve minutes out of date and it is a foot long and two inches across and it has been lying on that stone since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and not one person has ever looked at it.”

She said, “And the light is above the top of that cage and it is twenty-nine days from that turning to the mark, and twenty-nine days from the second of that month is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four, and not one of them is gone.”

And that was the last of it, and it is the thing she said at the end, and she said it standing in daylight in a room with a person in it.

She said, “I have had five bundles come up that stair and I have watched every one of them and the room has never once looked at me while I watched.”

She said, “And tonight the light went onto that cut and stayed there all night with nothing above it and nothing below it for the second time in four hundred years, and not one word of it went into a row, and nothing was lost by it anywhere, and the only person in the world who knows it happened is standing in this room.”

She said, “And I have still not worked out what a row with me in it is for, and I have two days to go and get measured by the only instrument this room has.”
