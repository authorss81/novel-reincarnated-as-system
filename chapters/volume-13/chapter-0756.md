# What Came Off That Arm

The fifth bundle came up that stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four and the light at the far end of that room came down onto the line cut in the stone behind it at the same hour, for the second time in four hundred years, and a hand went into that cage at the second hour of the second of that month, and she took a reading of the forearm afterwards that she cannot give back to anything.

## Three Days Of Watching A Light Come Down

She watched it come down for three days without doing anything else at all, which is the first three days she has had like that since she came up this stair.

On the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen the light at the far end of that room was fifty-two of her own hand below the top of that cage.

On the twenty-ninth it was fifty-four. On the thirtieth it was fifty-six.

And the line cut in the stone is fifty-eight, and she said each of those figures out loud as she measured it, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, with her arm open at her side and a person at the table nine feet off along it who has both hands on the near edge.

She said, out loud, on the twenty-ninth of that month at the ninth hour:

“Fifty-eight less fifty-four is four and four divided by two is two and the second day from this one is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four.”

And on the thirtieth she said the same sum out loud with fifty-six in it and got one day, and she said it twice, once in the ordinary voice and once more slower, because she wanted to hear it land.

## The Fifth Bundle

And the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four came.

She stood four feet off the near end of that table and the boots came up the turn at the second hour and a man of about forty came out of the place in the wall behind the lamp’s shelf with about forty boards under one arm and a bundle of papers in a cloth under the other and put them down and went back out without looking at anything.

Ten minutes for the forty boards at four to a minute and thirty for the bundle of papers and the hands did not stop and did not hurry and did not go back.

And that is the fifth time she has watched that, and the fifth time it has taken forty minutes, and the first four times it was forty minutes and the fifth time it is still forty minutes, and there is not one row in that building with a figure in it about any of the five.

She said, out loud, at the end of the forty minutes, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“Forty minutes. Ten and thirty. And forty things come up that stair on the first of a month and go down it again and there has not been one change in four hundred years and there is not one this month either, and on the four days before this one a light has been coming down towards a line in a wall and nobody in this building knows that.”

## The Light Standing On The Cut

And on that day, at the fourth hour, the light came down onto the line cut in the stone behind that lamp and stopped on the cut and did not go under it.

She said it out loud standing at the near end of that table, in the ordinary voice, with nobody else in that room:

“It is on the cut.”

And she said, “It is on the cut and there is nothing above it and nothing below it and it has been two of my own hand a day since the second hour of the second of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen, and it is going to stay on that cut until a hand comes.”

And she stood there and waited, and the hands at that table stayed on the near edge, and the head came down over it, and there was no sound in that room at all for a count of two hundred, and at the fifth hour nothing had happened.

She said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, standing, in daylight:

“That is twice. That is the second time in four hundred years that a light in this room has stood on that cut, and it is because I spent a day of my own on the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four, and there is not one line of writing anywhere in this building that says so.”

## The Second Hour Of The Second

And at the second hour of the second of that month a hand went into that cage.

She watched it from four feet off the end of that table with her breath at nine. It took eleven seconds and she counted them, and the hand came out and the light went up the wall past the top of that cage and stayed there.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, in a room where one look decides everything and there is no second one:

“Eleven seconds. And it is a row of mine that has paid for it, because there is a board on that table with my own figure on it in my own hand and it has been in this building since the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen, and one hand has written on it exactly once and every word of that word has come off this arm.”

## A Reading And Three Things

And she went along that table on her hands and knees, twenty-two paces, with the light coming up on her from the far end the whole way, and she took a reading of that forearm at the fourth hour of the second of that month with her own left hand held up at the end of it.

She said, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table:

“It is smaller by the width of the little finger on my left hand.”

She said, “And I have taken six readings of that forearm and I have never once been able to say which thing a width came off it for.”

And then she did the whole of that out loud, because there is not one person in that building who is going to do it for her and she does not leave a thing standing.

She said, “I took the sixth reading on the fourteenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, and since that day two things have come off that arm and I have taken no reading between them, because on the first of them I was standing at the near end of that table saying his name out loud and on the second of them I was under that table.”

She said, “The first of them is fourteen days after the fourteenth of that month and it is a row. The second of them is eighteen days after the fourteenth of that month and it is a lamp.”

She said, “And I have got one width and two things and there is not one instrument in this building that could tell me which of the two it came off for.”

## What She Cannot Do With It

And she said the sting out loud on the third of that month, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table with the boards under it and the bar against the wall on the left.

She said, “I have spent a hundred and forty-four days saying that a lamp and a row are the same substance, and I proved it on the fourteenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four with six readings of one forearm, and I have not been able to divide a width into a thing since.”

She said, “And the price of a lamp is a day and the price of a row is not a day at all, and both of them come off that same arm by the same width, and if it was a lamp then somebody in this room has paid me a day, and if it was a row then a person has put words of mine into a column and has not been asked for it.”

She said, “And it is worse than that. There is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain who came up eight hundred and eighty steps three times and is the only person alive who could say out loud that he heard me say two words in this room on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four.”

She said, “And a hand that copies figures exactly is not going to take a boy’s word for one word of it, and I would not take his word for it either, and I am not going to ask him to come up eight hundred and eighty steps a fourth time to stand next to an arm I cannot measure.”

## The Sums

And she did the sums on the fourth of that month at the ninth hour, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table with the light at the far end of it two of her own hand below the top of that cage and a hand going into it every twenty-nine days.

She said, “Two thousand four hundred and forty-four less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and eighty-five days, and two thousand four hundred and forty-four less two thousand three hundred and one is a hundred and forty-three days.”

She said, “And the figure at the head of my row is seven days and twelve hours out of date and the figure at the head of my row is the only figure in that building about me and it is getting more wrong every day and there is not one person above this line who could ever know.”

She said, “And fifty-eight less two is fifty-six and fifty-six divided by two is twenty-eight and the twenty-eighth day from this one is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four.”

She said, “And that is the first time in a hundred and forty-four days that a lamp in this room has been turned by a hand at the second hour of a second, and I was in the room for it, and I have a width on a forearm that I cannot give to anything, and it is going to come down onto that same cut again in twenty-eight days.”
