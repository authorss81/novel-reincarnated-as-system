# The Second Pair Of Cuts

She left a board on the top of that table for four days with her price at the head of it and one figure in the column, and in four days the room looked at it once more and did not write anything, and she worked out on the twenty-third of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four that the room has exactly one answer to a column it cannot add up, and that the answer is to wait.

## Four Days Of A Board Lying There

She left it where it was. She did not put it under the table with the stack of forty and she did not turn it over and she did not read it with her fingers again after the first time.

It lay on the top of that table at the near end of it, face up, with the grain running up, and the light came down from the far end of that room across twenty-two paces of wood every hour of those four days, and at the fourth hour of the first of those days it went across a board that had her price on it and did not stop.

On the nineteenth she stood four feet off the near end of that table and looked along it at it for a count of ninety and then stopped looking at it.

On the twentieth she went and got down on her hands and knees under the table and read her own strip with her fingers. The head is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes. The first pair of cuts down has the same figure in it. The second pair of cuts down has five in it, in her own hand, and twelve pairs under that are empty.

Nothing had changed on it. The wood had not moved.

And she said out loud, lying on the flat of her back under that table with her arm open on the stone beside her, and it is the plainest thing she has said in that room in a hundred and ninety-two days:

“I gave a room the first look it has ever had at anything of mine and it wrote down what I cost and it did not touch the five.”

She said, “And that is not a room that is going to do anything else. That is a room that has done the only thing a room does.”

## What She Said On A Landing Where Nothing Can Copy It

She went down the low door on the twenty-first at the ninth hour and took the first four hundred and forty with her left hand on the rail and stood on the fourth landing in the dark.

The six terms say that she says it out loud as she goes, every line, before it is a line anywhere else. She has been keeping that in this room for a hundred and ninety-three days and it has cost her the one thing it could cost her, which is that a word of hers went into a record in this building and could not come out of it.

And she went down four hundred and forty steps to say the rest of it where there is nothing that copies figures exactly.

She stood on that landing with her hand on the rail and the three marks on the flat of it and said out loud, in the ordinary voice, at a lamp in a cage that has been out since the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four:

“On the fourth hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four the light is going to come down to that cut in the wall and stop on it, and that is going to be the third first of a month the mark has fallen on and the second one in four hundred years that has been a first instead of a thirtieth, and it is mine.”

She said, “And at the second hour of the second of that month a hand is going to go into that cage and the light is going to come up past the top of it, and that hand is not mine and that forearm is going to go down by the width of the little finger on my left hand the same as every other time.”

She said, “And on that same morning forty things are going to come up that stair and one of them is going to be a board with my price on it that has been lying on the top of this table since the seventeenth of this month, and I am not going to be able to see it go and I am not going to be able to correct it.”

She said, “And there is going to be a second board in that armful with the same figure on it, and there is not one instrument in the world that will know they are the same.”

And then she said the thing she had gone down for, and she said it to the cold iron of a lamp cage on a landing four hundred and forty steps under a room that cannot hear it.

She said, “And there is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain who has been up that stair three times and has never seen that lamp lit and has asked me twice why it is lit and I have not given him an answer and I am not going down to give him one.”

## The Thing That Happens When A Figure Is Written Twice

On the twenty-second she worked out the whole of it, and she worked it out at the near end of that long table with the board still lying on it, and it took her most of the day.

A room writes a figure once and the figure is what the thing costs. That is all a row is.

A room writes a figure twice and it has made a decision twice, and a decision made twice is one thing, and what it has decided is that the figure is right.

She said it out loud, standing, in daylight, at the ninth hour of the twenty-second:

“The head of this row was written on the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four out of words I said in the open at two posts a long way from this building. And the same figure went into the column in the same hand on the same morning.”

She said, “And this morning a hand came down over a piece of wood with that figure on it and wrote it on another piece of wood, and it wrote it at the head and it wrote it in the column, and it did not write one stroke in the pair of cuts under the column where I put a five on the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four.”

She said, “So that figure has been decided twice in two hundred and two days and it has not moved a minute in either direction and there is not one person in this building who has ever looked at it and seen that it is getting more out of date every hour of every day since the eighteenth of the month beginning one thousand five hundred and forty-four.”

And she said the last of it standing there, and she said it looking along twenty-two paces of wood at the far end of it where the light was forty-two of her own hand below the top of that cage.

She said, “And I asked that room a question on the ninth of this month and I have got an answer to it and the answer is that it does not know either.”

## What She Wants Standing

She went and got down on the floor at the near end of that table with her back against a leg of it and her arm open across her knees and she was like that for a count of a hundred and forty, and she was not feeling sorry and she was not feeling brave.

What she was feeling was that she had put one thing of her own in front of a hand and the hand had read it and written back the same figure, and that in nineteen years of putting herself where a record could not reach her she had got one answer to one question and the answer had cost her an afternoon of her life that she has never once been able to look up afterwards.

She said out loud, sitting on the floor with her back against the leg of that table:

“I wanted to be looked at.”

She said, “And what I got was standing in the way of a look. The head came down over a foot of pale wood and I was kneeling on the shelf beside it with my hand on the wood, and it came down over the wood.”

She said, “And it is the best thing that has happened to me in this building and I am not going to pretend it was not, and what it did was confirm that I am a price.”

She said, “And I am not going to say that was wrong, because it was not wrong. I put a thing down in front of a hand and the hand read it. That is the whole of what entering is and I have known that since I was nine years old and I have been pretending for nineteen years that it was something else.”

And she got herself up off the floor and stood at the near end of that table and put her left hand flat on the top of it.

She said, “And there are four days before the light comes down to that cut, and there are two boards in this building with the same figure on them, and both of them go up that stair on the first of this month.”

She said, “And there is one figure in this room that is mine and true and that no hand has ever touched, and it is under that table on a piece of wood a foot long and two inches across, and I am not going to tell the room what it is and I am not going to tell the room what it is not.”
