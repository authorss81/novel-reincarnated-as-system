# The Third Forty Boards

The third bundle came up that stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and a boy of sixteen counted it, and he got the number right and the rate wrong, and she has not got over it.

## Three Days To The First

On the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four she told him what was coming and she told him the date and the number, standing at the near end of that table, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “In three days a man comes up that stair with about forty boards under one arm and a bundle of papers in a cloth under the other, and he puts them on that table in one movement and goes back out without looking at anything, and that is the whole of what he does.”

He said, “Every month.”

She said, “On the first of the month. Every month. I have seen it twice and it is thirty days apart both times, and I have counted it out loud every day for sixty-three days and it is the only number in this building that is the same every day.”

She said, “And the hand at that table goes along the forty boards at four to a minute and does not stop, and the bundle takes three times as long as the forty boards, and the whole of a month is forty minutes.”

He said, “What is on them.”

She said, “I have been in this room twenty-three days when they came the first time and I could not tell my own board from any of the thirty-nine others, and I have not found out since, and that is not a thing I am going to be able to find out by looking harder.”

## The Boots At The Second Hour

On the twenty-ninth the light was fifty-four of her own hand below the top of that cage and on the thirtieth it was fifty-six, and she said both figures out loud standing against the wall, and the boy wrote them on the flat of the shelf with a piece of charcoal out of the copying floor, which he had gone and got on the twenty-eighth.

On the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four at the second hour she heard boots on the turn.

She stood four feet off the near end of that table the way she has stood for two of these, and the boy stood at the door and did not move out of the way and did not have to, because the man walks the length of that table and puts an armful down on it and turns round, and the door is at the near end and he goes out of the far end.

He came out of the place in the wall behind the lamp’s shelf with an armful of boards and a bundle in a cloth and put them down and went back out.

The armful went down in one movement. It took him less time than it takes her to get up onto that table.

## The Boy Counting Boards

And then the boy counted them, out loud, from the doorway, and she has thought about that sound every day since.

He counted them going on. He got to nine and then he started counting the time.

She said, “No. Count them going off.”

He said, “They go on quicker than they come off.”

She said, “They do not. Four a minute, going on and coming off, and it is the same four, and if you count them going on you will get a different number every time you do it because the first one is on before the last one is off.”

He started again at the near end and counted as they came off, and he got to forty, and he put his hand flat on the frame of that door and worked it out with his mouth moving.

He said, “Ten minutes.”

She said, “Ten minutes.”

He said, “How did you know that before I started.”

She said, “Because I counted it twice. On the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four and on the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four, and both times it was ten minutes, and I counted every one of them out loud and I did not get one of them wrong.”

## What She Watched Him Not Understand

And in the thirty minutes the bundle of papers took, she watched a boy of sixteen look at a leaf of paper going along that table and be entirely unable to make anything of it, and she had to stand there and let him not understand it in front of her.

She knows what he was doing. He was doing what she did at a frame with a board two days of road east of a turn, on the fifteenth of the month beginning two thousand one hundred and fourteen, when a carter came past and read a name off a line she had written and did not know what it was.

He said, at the twentieth minute, “Is that writing?”

She said, “That is writing.”

He said, “It is not in a line. It is all down the side and there are marks in the middle.”

She said, “It is a hand and not mine and not yours and not anybody who has ever been up that stair, and I have had eighty-one days with it going past and I have got nothing out of it.”

He said, “Can you read it?”

She said, “I cannot read it with my eyes and I cannot read it with my hands because it is not my hand and there is nothing on the stone under this table to hold it flat.”

He said, “Then how does anybody read anything.”

She said, “Somebody has to stand in a room and say it first. That is what I worked out on the twenty-third of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and this is the third time I have watched the other half of it happen without me.”

## The Second Person In Nineteen Years

She stood there and let that happen to her for a count of about sixty and did not say anything at all while it did.

Nineteen years. A yard with a bar. A road with carts. A ground with two hundred people in it. A room with the shutters up. A frame at a turn in a road. A copying floor. A mill yard. A bench on a wet morning. A stone stair with no one on it.

Nineteen years of being the only person in the room who could do that.

And a boy of sixteen stood in a doorway at the top of eight flights and eight hundred and eighty steps and did it in one go without being told twice.

She said out loud, at the ninth hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, standing at the near end of that table, in the ordinary voice:

“There is a second one.”

He said, “A second what.”

She said, “A second person who can count. That is all. You counted forty boards going off a table and you got ten minutes out of them and you have never done it before and I have done it twice and watched a room for eighty-three days to work out that not one other person in this building does it.”

She said, “And it has taken me nineteen years to find the second one and he is sixteen and he came up a stair nobody has come up in nine years because a lamp went out on a landing.”

## What He Could Not Do With What He Counted

And then he asked the thing that she had been waiting nineteen years to be asked and it was not the right thing at all.

He said, “What do you do with the number.”

She said, “What?”

He said, “Forty boards. Ten minutes. You have got them. What do you do with them.”

She stood there and found out that she did not have an answer, and that she had known for eighty-three days that she did not have one, and that this was the first time in nineteen years anybody had stood in a room and asked her what she did with a figure.

She said, “Nothing. I have been putting the figures where they are and keeping them, and that is the whole of what I have ever done with any of them.”

She said, “And that is not an answer and I am not going to pretend that it is one, and you have asked me the first question in nineteen years that I have not got ready for, and it took you four minutes.”

He said, “That is all right.”

She said, “It is not all right. It is the best thing anybody has said to me in a very long time and you are sixteen and you do not know what you have done.”

## The Cut In The Stone Behind That Lamp

And at that hour the light at the far end of that room came down onto the line cut in the stone behind it, and it stopped on the cut, and it did not go under it.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“Look at that.”

He came along the floor and looked at it.

She said, “That is a short line cut in the stone of that wall and the light has come down to it and stopped on it and it is not going any further. There is nothing above it and nothing below it.”

She said, “And that line was reached on the thirtieth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and the light was two of my own hand above it on the twenty-ninth and it took a hand to the cage to bring it back up, and a hand went into that cage on the fourth of that month this time and came back up further than it had ever been.”

He said, “Whose hand.”

She said, “Mine.”

He said, “Then it is going to reach that line again in twenty-nine days.”

She said, “It will be down on that line again in twenty-nine days from the next time a hand comes to that cage, and the next forty things will come up that stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen, and those are two different clocks and I have been keeping both of them for eighty-three days and they have never once agreed.”

She said, “And this is the first day in four hundred years that a lamp in this room has stood on that line with nothing above it and nothing below it, and there is not one row in this building with a single thing in it about what I did to get here.”

She said, “And the boards went along that table for forty minutes in front of two people and not one of them was looked at, and one of them is sitting at that table and one of them is standing in a doorway, and the one in the doorway is sixteen and cannot count and the one at the near end of the table cannot say what she is, and that is what this room was holding this morning.”

She said it out loud and then she said the other thing, and it was the only sentence she said that day that she had not said to herself first.

She said, “And you counted the boards and I have got the number, and neither of us has got a column.”
