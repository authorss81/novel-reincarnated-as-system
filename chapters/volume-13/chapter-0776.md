# The Second Look

She took the sixth stage off that table on the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four at the ninth hour, standing on the wood in the light, with a nib and a cake of ink against her fourth rib, on a board out of the stack under the middle of that table, and the person at that table looked at her, and it was the second look anybody in this building has ever taken of her, and it took eleven seconds.

## The Board Out Of The Stack Again

Forty boards lie face up in the dark under the middle of that table in a stack and she took one out backwards, on her back, with a cross-beam across her shoulders, holding it against her chest with her left forearm because her right arm has been open since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and is not going to close.

She could not tell which of the forty it was when she put it back, and she did not try to find out, and that is the first time in this building she has taken a board out and not cared which one it was.

She said out loud, sitting on the floor of that room with the board across her knees and the lamp in a cage at the far end of that table thirty-eight of her own hand below the top of it:

“The last one was taken on the eighteenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, which is the day two thousand four hundred and sixty-one. Today is the day two thousand five hundred and twenty-four.”

She said, “That is sixty-three days.”

She said, “And it is sixty-three and not fifty-eight and not a hundred and thirteen, and I have not worked out a rule for any of the three, and I am not going to work out one tonight, and a stage above this line is taken off a table in the light by a hand on a day the person who is taking it chooses, and I have been choosing days for nineteen years and this is the first one I have chosen for a reason I can put into words.”

She said, “I can hold still on a stone floor with nothing in the room and I could not do it on the twentieth of this month and I could not do it when I took the last one.”

She said, “And that is the whole of the reason and it is not a good one and it is mine.”

## Along Twenty-Two Paces On Her Hands And Knees

She got up on the near end of that table with her left hand and her weight on the arm that was closed that day and it took a count of nine, and she went along the top of it, twenty-two paces, on her hands and knees, with the grain of the wood under her palms and her breath at nine the whole way, and the light coming up on her from the far end of that room.

She knelt on the shelf in front of that lamp with the board flat on the wood in front of her, and there is nothing behind that light and the wall behind the light is the wall and the flame does not lean when her hand moves past it.

She put the nib to the wood with her right hand, three fingers and a thumb, on an arm that has been open since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four.

And she said it out loud, kneeling on that shelf in the light, in the ordinary voice, standing up in her own head the way she has said every stage figure in this room:

“Six stages into the seventh realm, taken at the ninth hour of the twenty-first of this month at the far end of a long table in a tower four miles up a mountain, on a board out of a stack, in my own hand, with a nib that has not touched a column in this building in nine years.”

She said, “Six.”

And she wrote it.

It is one stroke and a turn and it is a count and not a price, and it took a count of nine, and her right forearm went down by the width of the little finger on her left hand while the nib was still on the wood, which is the cost of writing and has been the cost of writing since the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four.

## They Had Not Looked Up Once

And the person at that table looked up.

They had not looked up once since the sixth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four, and every day since then has been the same day over again with a figure on it.

Not once on the eighteenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four when a hand came off the near edge and closed on her forearm at the wrist. Not once when she wrote a five on the twenty-second of that month with a nib under a table on the floor. Not once on the seventeenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four when a head came down over a foot of pale wood on the top of that table.

They looked up at her while she was kneeling on a shelf four feet off the floor with a board in front of her and a nib still on it.

And it took eleven seconds, and there was nothing she could do with her face.

She has held a person still by doing nothing at all and she has never once been on the other end of it. Her face did what a face does. She could not hold it still and she could not make it do anything else, and she was kneeling on a stone shelf above a stone floor with her arm open in front of her and a person nine paces off looking at the whole of her, and there was not one thing in the world she could have done with any part of herself.

Then they went back to the near edge of that table and put their hands on the wood.

## Nothing Was Written

And nothing happened for a count of ninety.

No board came out from under the middle of that table. No stroke and no figure went onto anything. The light came down that wall two of her own hand a day and did not lean. The low door stood open on a stair that goes down eight hundred and eighty steps.

She got herself back along the top of that table on her hands and knees and got down off the near end onto the floor and stood four feet off it and picked the board up off the shelf of the lamp with her left hand, and there was a six on it in her own hand, and she looked at it for a count of twenty after she had written it and it was the same figure, and she had not made it up.

And then she said it out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table, and she said it slowly, and it is the thing the whole of the eleventh took her to:

They looked at me and they did not write anything down.

And she said, “And a room that looks at a thing once decides what it is. That is the rule and it has been the rule for four hundred years and I have been standing in front of it since the sixth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four.”

She said, “And they have already spent the one look on a foot of pale wood on the seventeenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four and there is no second look and there never has been anything to appeal with.”

She said, “And they looked at me anyway, and it did not write anything, and I do not know what that is, and there is not one instrument in the world that reports what a look saw.”

## What The Sixth One Cost

And at the ninth hour of that same day, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that long table, with the board in her left hand and her right arm open in front of her and a person standing at that table, she named the cost of the sixth stage, once, out loud, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “Immortal Vessel 6.”

She said, “Six stages into the seventh realm, at sixty-three days after the fifth and a hundred and twenty-one days after the fourth, taken off that table on a board in my own hand at the ninth hour of the twenty-first of this month.”

She said, “And it has cost me the ability to be looked at again.”

She said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table, and then she stopped and did not say anything else for a count of ninety, and this is the whole of what is on the page about what that is:

“I have wanted to be looked at since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four.”

She said, “And I have had two looks in this room in five days and one of them was at a foot of pale wood and one of them was at me, and it is the last one there is going to be.”

She said, “And I am nineteen years old and I have got a thing I wanted and I have not got a use for it and there is not one person in this building who is going to give me one.”

And then she said the last of that day out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table, and it was about a boy and not about a look.

She said, “There is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain who would stand in a doorway and look at that room the way a person looks at a room, and he is not the hand at that table and it would not decide anything, and I cannot be looked at again.”

She said, “And I worked that out in one sentence and it has not helped me at all, and I have not said one word of it out loud on a landing four hundred and forty steps down, and I am not going to.”

And she put the board with a six on it back under the shelf in that stack of forty boards, face up, in the dark, where the board with a five on it has been lying since the eighteenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, and she could not tell which of the two it was when she had put it down.
