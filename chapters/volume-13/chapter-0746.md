# Off The Table Herself

She took the fourth stage off that table herself at the tenth hour of the twentieth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, standing on the wood in the light, and she is four stages into the seventh realm and there is not one thing in this building that can be given to her.

## What A Stage Is Taken On In This Room

She worked it out on the eighteenth and the nineteenth of that month and she worked it out out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, and there is not one step of it that has been in a book.

She said, “There is no board above this line. There is no slate and there is no counter and there is no ground. I have been in this room one hundred days and everything in it is stone and wood and a light in a cage and there is nothing anywhere in it that a stage can be taken on except a strip of wood with a figure at the head of it.”

She said, “And the strip is under the table on the floor and the hand that fills it is at the table, and the light it gets filled in is a lamp that has to be fed to be lit, and a hand feeds it, and the day the lamp gets fed is the day the row gets written.”

She said, “And the feeder chooses the day. That is the whole of it. There is no calendar up here. I have been keeping one for one hundred and two days out of a thing not one person above this line loses, and I have watched two turnings and one figure copied and not one of the three was on a date that anything in this room could tell me about.”

She said, “So a stage is taken by being written in a row, by a hand, in the light of that lamp, on a day the feeder chooses. And I have not got a row that is about me. I have got a row with my price at the head of it and nothing in the column, and I worked that out on the thirteenth of this month and I have not improved on it since.”

## The Board Out Of The Stack

And on the twentieth of that month at the ninth hour she went under that table and got a board out of the stack, and that is the whole of it, and it took her a count of sixty and she had to do it twice.

Forty boards lie face up in the dark under the middle of that table in a stack, and she had put her hand on all forty on the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four and not one of them was different from any of the others.

She took the top one.

She got it out backwards, on her back, with the cross-beam across her shoulders, holding it against her chest with her left forearm because her right arm does not close, and she got it clear of the stack on the second try and lay it on the stone and got herself out from under the table on her hands and knees.

It is the size of a slate and about the thickness of two fingers and pale wood with the grain running up it.

She said out loud, sitting on the floor of that room with it across her knees:

“I have been in this room one hundred and two days and I have never once taken a thing off that table, and there is not one row in this building with a single thing in it about what I am about to do, and not one person in this building is going to see me do it.”

## Along The Top Of That Table

At the tenth hour she got up on the near end of that table with her left hand and her weight on the arm that was closed that day and it took a count of nine and she did not have to try four times any more.

And then she went along the top of it, twenty-two paces, on her hands and knees, with the grain of the wood under her palms and her breath at nine the whole way, and the light coming up on her from the far end the whole time, and the bar nine feet nine lying flat against the wall on the left of that room with its broken end towards the door where she put it on the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four.

She knelt on the shelf in front of the lamp with the board flat on the wood and she wrote on it with her right hand, three fingers and a thumb, on an arm that has been open since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and is not going to close.

There is nothing behind that light and the wall is the wall and the flame does not lean when her hand moves past it.

She wrote one figure.

She said, out loud, kneeling on that shelf in the light, in the ordinary voice:

“Four.”

And then she said what that was, standing up off the shelf on top of a four-foot table in daylight in a room with a person in it, and she said it slowly.

She said, “Immortal Vessel 4. Four stages into the seventh realm, and nine stages past a ceiling that has not existed since before I could hold a bar.”

And nothing happened.

The person at that table did not look up and did not turn round and did not move, and there was no sound, and the low door stood open on a stair that goes down eight hundred and eighty steps, and there was not one thing in that room that knew it had happened.

## A Hundred And Thirteen And Not Seventy-Eight

And she said the interval out loud on the floor at the near end of that table at the sixth hour of the twentieth of that month, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

She said, “The first of the seventh realm was at two thousand one hundred and twenty-eight and the second at two thousand two hundred and six and the third at two thousand two hundred and ninety and this one is at two thousand four hundred and three days.”

She said, “Two thousand two hundred and six less two thousand one hundred and twenty-eight is seventy-eight. Two thousand two hundred and ninety less two thousand two hundred and six is eighty-four. And two thousand four hundred and three less two thousand two hundred and ninety is a hundred and thirteen.”

She said, “And a stage past the third working point cannot be taken against a room. It can only be taken against a row. And a row is entered by the person it is about and I worked that out on the ninth of this month and I have been in this room one hundred and two days and there was not one row in this building about me until the tenth of it.”

She said, “So it is a hundred and thirteen and not seventy-eight, and it is not seventy-eight because there was no row to take it against, and there has not been one for a hundred and thirteen days, and the seventy-eight and the eighty-four were taken against rooms and a room is not a row.”

She said, “And I am not going to say that I waited a hundred and thirteen days for it. I did not know it was there.”

## What It Cost And What It Is Not

And then she said the price, out loud, standing on the floor at the near end of that table at the seventh hour of the twentieth, in the ordinary voice, and she said it once and she did not say it again in that room.

She said, “It has cost me the ability to be given a thing.”

She said, “That is the whole of it and there is not one other part of it.”

She said, “And it cost the same as the lamp. A lamp takes eleven seconds and the width of the little finger on my left hand off the forearm of a person at that table, and a stage takes the whole of me and it cost not one person in this room anything at all, and I do not know yet which of the two of those is going to cost more.”

And then she said the part that made it different, because fourteen costs have been true since before I could count and this one is not the same sentence as any of them, and she said it standing up, in daylight, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “I have not been able to be given anything since the day I was born. That is a thing that has been done to me and it is a thing I have carried and it is a thing I could not change and I have said out loud in nineteen years that it is the reason I count everything.”

She said, “That is not what this is. This is that the way through is now the only way there is. Above this line everything is handed to a person. The lamp is handed down to a hand. The row is handed down to a column. And I am not in a column and there is nothing to hand anything to, and so I got up on that table and took it off myself, standing, in front of a room that has never once watched anybody take anything off that table.”

She said, “And the fourteen are still true and I am not able to put one of them down. It has simply stopped being the reason.”

## What Went Out Of Her Hand

And she took her own account of herself out loud on the twenty-first of that month at the seventh hour, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, because there are two figures in this room that moved and she is not going to have one of them go by unsaid.

She said, “Seven of eleven holds. It was eight this morning and it is seven now and I did not lose it going up that table and I did not lose it coming down it.”

She said, “And two thousand four hundred and three less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and forty-four days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and that is one more than it was on the twentieth and it did not come off my arm or off my breath or off my hand, and I have not been able to work out where it came off and I am not going to guess in a room where a hand copies figures exactly.”

She said, “And the figure on the strip under that table is five days, ten hours and forty-eight minutes out of date, and there is not one person in this building who could ever know that, and now there is one stage more that not one person in this building knows about.”

## What She Said At The End Of It

And she said the last of it out loud on the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, sitting on the floor with her back against the near leg of that table, in the ordinary voice, and she said it to herself and not out loud and not to a person, and it is the first thing she has said to herself in ninety-nine days.

She said, “I am not going to be able to be handed anything again as long as I am alive, and there is nobody four miles down that mountain who could do it if I could be, and not one person in this room would try.”

She said, “And I got up on a table four feet off the floor and took a stage off it with a hand that has three fingers and a thumb on it, in a room with a person in it, and nothing happened, and nothing about that is going to be able to be put back the way it was.”

She said, “And there is a board on top of that table with a figure on it in my own hand and there is not one instrument in the world that takes a line out of a record, and I wrote it myself, and that is the first time in nineteen years I have written something and known exactly what it was for.”

And the light at the far end of that room was thirty-eight of her own hand below the top of that cage and she went and looked at it, standing, in daylight.

Fifty-eight less thirty-eight is twenty, and twenty divided by two is ten, and the tenth day from the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen.

And the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen is ten days from that, and forty things are going to come up that stair on it, and she is going to be standing here with four stages on her and a board in a record that nobody in this building knows is hers, and she has not worked out the next sentence she is going to have to say out loud in this room.
