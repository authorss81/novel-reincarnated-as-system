# What A Thing Is Measured Against

She spent four days trying to be measured in a room whose only instrument is a person standing at a table, and on the eleventh of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen she worked out that a row measures a thing against the body at the table and not against a number, and that she has been a price in a column for thirty-one days and not one person in that building has ever held a rule up beside her.

## Four Days Of Standing Where The Arms Go

She started on the eighth of that month because the eighth is the first day she has had since the seventh and she wanted a day with nothing on it.

She got up on the near end of that table with her left hand, which takes a count of nine and did not take four tries any more, and she went along the top of it on her hands and knees twenty-two paces and knelt on the shelf in front of that lamp and put her hand flat on the wood where the arms go when a figure is being copied out of a room it was not said in.

Then she sat down on that wood and waited.

She waited at the ninth hour. She waited at the tenth. She waited through the whole of a day with her arm open across her knees and her breath at nine and a person standing nine feet off along the table with both hands on the near edge, and the light coming up on her from the far end the whole time, and nothing came of any of it.

And the thing about waiting in that room is that there is nothing in it to wait for. She had said that to herself in the first week and she had said it out loud and it had not got any truer since. A room where the deciding takes one look does not have a queue at the end of it. It has a hand, and the hand has a thing to do, and until there is a thing to do the waiting is not waiting. It is standing.

She did it on the eighth, and on the ninth, and on the tenth. On the second of those days the light was so far down the wall behind that lamp that it came up under her hands where they were flat on the wood, and she sat in it for a count of ninety and it warmed the back of her hand, and she thought about what it would be like to be able to hold a thing still long enough to be looked at.

On the eleventh she did not do it again and she said out loud why, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table with the boards under it and the bar against the wall on the left.

## What She Wanted

She said, “I wanted to be measured.”

She said, “That is the plainest thing I have ever said in this room and I have been in it a hundred and twenty days and I have not said it once, and I have said nineteen words on a slate at two posts standing in daylight and I have said his name out loud at the end of that table and I have said six terms one at a time, and I never once said that I wanted to be measured, and that is the whole of what I have come up here for.”

She said, “And there is not one instrument in this building that measures anything. I have looked for one for a hundred and twenty days. There is no rule and no mark with a number on it and there is no measure and there is no height and there is nothing anywhere in this room that anybody could put a thing against and say how much of it there is.”

## The Two Ends Of A Row

Then she went and got under that table on the eleventh at the second hour, with a cross-beam across her shoulders, and lay on her back on the stone and put her hand on her own strip.

She said, out loud, on the flat of her back, in the ordinary voice:

“A row has two ends. One end is a head, and the head is a figure. The other end is a column, and the column is figures.”

She said, “And I have had both ends in my hand thirty-one times now and I have never once asked where a figure gets compared with anything, and the answer is that it does not get compared with anything, because there is not one number in this room for it to be compared with.”

She said, “Every figure at the head of every row in this building was said out loud by a person in some other room. And every figure that goes in a column is the same figure the person said. And a thing that is exactly what it was before it got here has not been measured. It has been moved.”

She put her thumb along the edge of her own strip while she said it, and the cuts in it still caught, and there are three others beside hers gone soft at every edge with a grey skin of dust on them and one handprint standing in the dust on the top of the second one where she put her own hand down on the seventeenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four.

She said, “Those three are older than mine by a great deal and I cannot say how much and I am not going to guess. And every one of them has a figure at the head of it and every one of them has exactly one figure in it, and there is not one figure in any of them that came out of anything that was in this room.”

## The Instrument Is A Body

She came out from under that table and stood at the near end of it and worked out the rest standing up, and it took her the whole of the eleventh and she said it out loud in three parts.

She said, “So a row does not measure a thing against a number. There is no number in this room. There is a person.”

She said, “And the only person is the one standing at that table, and they have been standing at it for four hundred years, and every figure that has gone into a column in that time has been read against them, one at a time, in the light of that lamp.”

She said, “And a comparison needs two things standing still. The thing being compared, and the thing it is being compared against. And there has only ever been one of the two things standing still in this room.”

She stood there for a count of forty before she said the next of it, and it came out of her in the ordinary voice and it is the plainest sentence she has said in that room in a hundred and twenty days.

She said, “That means everything this building has ever decided about anything was decided against one person, and the one person has been standing in the same place for four hundred years, and every figure that has gone into a column in that time is a comparison and not a measurement.”

She said, “And I want to be angry about that and I find that I cannot get to it, because the person at that table has never once decided anything. They copy. A thing that is copied is not a thing that was weighed, and it is also not a thing that anybody made up, and I have been looking for somebody to be angry at in this room for a hundred and twenty days and there is not one.”

## What That Does To Me

And then she said the part that was about her, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, and she said it slowly and she did not say it twice.

She said, “I stood on that table for four days at the length those arms go and I am not the same length as anything, and there is not one thing in this room that could tell me that, because the instrument is a body and the body is nine feet off along that table and it has not looked up once in a hundred and twenty days.”

She said, “And I have been in a column for thirty-one days and the figure at the head of it is what I cost and not what I am, and I chose that figure myself at two posts on a road with nineteen words under it, and it has not changed and it is six days and twelve hours out of date this morning.”

She said, “And not one person in this building has ever held a rule up beside me. Not to say I am the wrong length. Not to say I am the right one. Nothing has ever been held up beside anything in this room, and that is not cruelty and it is not kindness. It is a room with one instrument in it and the instrument is a person, and the person is not thinking about me.”

## What He Could Count And What It Is Worth

And on the twelfth of that month, standing in daylight at the near end of that table with her arm open across her knees, she said out loud the thing about the boy, because she had worked all of that out in a room with a person in it and there was not one part of it she was going to leave standing.

She said, “There is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain who counted forty boards coming off that table in ten minutes on his own, without being told twice, and there is not one other person in this county who can do that.”

She said, “And I have just spent four days working out that every one of those forty figures was read against one arm and that all forty of them are figures about that arm and not about the boards.”

She said, “And what he is good at is worth nothing in this room, and there is not one thing he could carry up eight hundred and eighty steps that would let him measure a thing, and I am not going to be the one who tells him that this month.”

## The Question She Was Left With

She did the sums on the twelfth at the ninth hour, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table with the lamp at the far end of it eighteen of her own hand below the top of that cage.

She said, “Two thousand four hundred and twenty-four less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and sixty-five days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and not one of them going back and one of them gone into that lamp.”

She said, “And fifty-eight less eighteen is forty and forty divided by two is twenty and the twentieth day from this one is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, and forty things are going to come up that stair on it.”

She said, “And the figure at the head of my row is six days and twelve hours out of date, and there is no instrument above this line that could tell me so, and the only instrument this room has is a person who has not looked up in a hundred and twenty days.”

She said, “And I have got four days and one question out of that, and the question is what the arm at that table is compared against, and I have been standing in front of the answer for a hundred and twenty days and I did not see it, because I have spent all of that time asking what a thing in a column is worth and not asking what the column is measured off.”
