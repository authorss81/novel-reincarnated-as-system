# Two Things Standing Still

She spent four days standing at the length the arms in that room go and nothing in it looked at her once, and on the eleventh of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen she worked out that the thing at that table is not a counter and never was, and that it is a person, and that everything this building has ever decided was decided against that one person’s arm.

## Four Days At The Length Of The Arms

She started on the eighth of that month because it was the first day since the seventh with nothing on it, and she wanted a day with nothing on it.

She got up on the near end of that table with her left hand. It took a count of nine and it had not taken four tries since the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four. Then she went along the top of it on her hands and knees, twenty-two paces, with the grain of the wood under her palms, and knelt on the shelf in front of that lamp and sat down on it with her legs out over the light.

That is where the arms go. She worked that out on the ninth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four off the place the hands come down to, and she had put the wood under her own hips on it that day and nothing had happened, and she did not do it again for two months because of what she wrote on a board at that same place.

She sat there anyway. On the eighth she sat at the ninth hour and at the tenth hour and then until her breath was past nine and she had to sit down the rest of the way. On the ninth she did the same. On the tenth she took the whole day on her and the light came up onto her hands where they lay flat on the wood, sixteen of her own hand below the top of that cage, and it warmed the back of her right hand and she let it.

The person at the far end of that table did not look up once in any of it. Their hands stayed on the near edge. The light did not lean. The room did not have a shape to it that changed because a woman was sitting on the shelf where the arms go.

And on the eleventh she did not do it again, and she said out loud why, standing on the floor at the near end of that table with the boards under it and the bar nine feet nine flat against the wall on the left.

## The Plainest Thing She Has Said Here

She said, “I wanted to be measured.”

She said, “That is the plainest sentence I have said in this room and I have been in it a hundred and twenty-three days and I have not said it once.”

She said, “I said nineteen words on a slate at two posts in the open on the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, standing, in daylight.”

She said, “I said a figure out loud in this room on the tenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and a hand wrote it on a board. I said a name out loud in this room on the twenty-eighth of that month. I have said six terms one at a time in this room.”

She said, “I have never once said that I wanted to be measured, and that is the whole of what I came up here for.”

She said, “And there is not one instrument in this building that measures anything.”

She said, “I have looked for one for a hundred and twenty-three days. There is no rule and no mark with a number on it and there is no height and there is no length and there is no weight and there is nothing anywhere in this room that a person could put a thing against and say how much of it there is.”

And then she stopped, and she stood there for a count of a hundred and forty, and the person at the far end of that table went on doing the one thing they do.

## Where A Figure Gets Compared

She got under the table at the second hour of the eleventh with a cross-beam across her shoulders and lay on her back on the stone and put her hand flat on her own strip.

She said, out loud, on the flat of her back, in the ordinary voice:

“A row has two ends. One end is a head, and the head is a figure. The other end is a column, and the column is figures.”

She said, “And I have had both ends of mine in my hand more times than I have counted and I have never once asked where a figure gets compared with anything. And the answer is that it does not get compared with anything, because there is not one number in this room for a figure to be less than or more than.”

She ran her thumb along the edge of it while she said that. The cuts still caught. Beside hers were three older strips gone soft at every edge with a grey skin of dust on them, and there was a handprint standing in the dust on the top one where she had put her own hand down on the seventeenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four.

She said, “Every figure at the head of every row in this building was said out loud by a person in some other room, on some other day, and it came up a stair in an armful with forty other things, and a hand put it in a column.”

She said, “And a thing that is exactly what it was before it got here has not been measured. It has been moved.”

## The Thing Standing At That Table

She came out from under that table and worked the rest of it out standing up at the near end of it, in daylight, and she said it in three parts and she did not say any part of it twice.

She said, “So the instrument in this room is not a number. There is no number in this room. There is a person.”

She said, “And the person has been standing in the same place at the same table for four hundred years, and every figure that has gone into a column in that time has been read against them, one at a time, in the light of that lamp, in about eleven seconds.”

She said, “And a comparison needs two things standing still. The thing being compared, and the thing it is being compared against. And there has only ever been one of the two things standing still in this room.”

She stood there for a count of forty before she said the next of it, and it came out of her plain, and it is the plainest thing she has said in this room.

She said, “Everything this building has ever decided about anything was decided against one arm, and one arm has been the same arm for four hundred years, and every figure in every column in this county is a comparison and not a measurement.”

And then she said the part she was not expecting to find, which is that she could not get angry about it.

She said, “And I want to be angry about that and I cannot get to it. That person has never once decided anything. They copy. A thing that is copied has not been weighed and it has not been made up either, and I have been looking for somebody in this room to be angry at for a hundred and twenty-three days and there is not one, and there is not going to be one.”

## Forty Boards And Nothing To Weigh Them With

She said the next of it out loud on the eleventh at the ninth hour, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table, and it was about a boy four miles down that mountain who came up eight hundred and eighty steps three times and went down twice.

She said, “There is a boy of sixteen on a copying floor who counted forty boards coming off that table in ten minutes on his own without being told twice, and there is not one other person in this county who can do that.”

She said, “And I have just spent four days working out that every one of those forty figures was read against one arm in one lamp, and that not one of the forty is about the board it was on.”

She said, “And what he is good at is worth nothing in this room. There is not one thing he could carry up eight hundred and eighty steps that would let him measure anything, and he cannot come up here to be told that, because I cannot be given a thing and he cannot be given a thing either and the only way a fact gets from me to him is that I say it and he carries it, and I have got nothing short enough to carry down eight hundred and eighty steps that is also true.”

## Two Sums And A Question Left

She did the sums on the eleventh at the ninth hour, standing at the near end of that table with the lamp at the far end of it eighteen of her own hand below the top of that cage, and she wrote nothing down because there is nothing in this room to write with.

She said, “Two thousand four hundred and twenty-four less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and sixty-five days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and not one of them going back and one of them gone into that lamp on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four.”

She said, “And fifty-eight less eighteen is forty and forty divided by two is twenty and the twentieth day from the eleventh of this month is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, and forty things are going to come up that stair on it.”

She said, “And the figure at the head of my row is six days and twelve hours out of date this morning, and there is not one instrument above this line that could tell me so, and the only instrument this room has is a person who has not looked up in a hundred and twenty-three days.”

She said, “And I have been a figure at the head of a column for a hundred and twenty-three days and the figure is what I cost and not what I am, and in all that time not one person in this building has held a rule up beside me. Not to say I am the wrong length. Not to say I am the right one. Nothing has ever been held up beside anything in this room.”

And that was where she stopped, and it is where the question she went into that month with went wrong.

She had gone in asking what a thing in a column is worth.

She had come out four days later knowing that nothing in this room is worth anything, because nothing in this room has ever been weighed.

There is a person at the far end of a table who has been the measure of a county for four hundred years. They do not know it and they never will.

And she cannot ask, because the only way to ask a question in this building is to put it in a row, and she does not have one yet, and is not going to be able to make one by standing still at the length the arms go.
