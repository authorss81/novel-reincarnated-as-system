# The Hand Does Not Enter Anybody

She worked it out on the twentieth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four off the top of that table, and it took her three days, and the thing she found out at the end of it was that the room does not decide anything.

## Nothing On That Table To Write With

On the twentieth she went along the top of that table on her hands and knees for the first time in a month and put her face down level with the wood and looked at the near half of it from the side, at a count of six inches off it, for a count of three hundred.

There is nothing on it.

She has known that since the twenty-fourth of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, when she first went along it. She knew there was no dish and no reservoir and no wick and no key and no door in the side of it. She had not looked for the other things, and she looked for them on the twentieth, and there are not any.

No ink. No stone to grind. No sand to throw across a line. No pen, no holder, no knife, no rag, no water, no blotter, nothing of any kind on four feet of pale wood with the grain running up it.

She said out loud from up on the table, at the sixth hour of the twentieth, in the ordinary voice:

“There is nothing on that table to write with.”

## The Question He Asked

The boy had been sitting at the near end with his back to the leg of it for three days and he had been listening, and he asked the plain thing, which was the thing a person asks.

He said, “Then what does the hand write with.”

She said, “That is the whole of what I have got and I have not got an answer to it.”

She said, “There is a hand in this room that has put a figure in a column four hundred times and not one figure of them was made at that table. I have watched that hand go along forty boards in ten minutes and it came off every one of them with the same stroke in the same corner, and a stroke that comes out the same on forty different boards did not come out of what was on the boards.”

He said, “So it is a stamp.”

She said, “It is not a stamp either, because a stamp is a thing somebody made and carries and there is no such thing on that table or on any table in this building. And whatever it is, it is not in the room.”

## The Figure That Came Out In Her Own Hand

She got down off the table and lay on her back on the floor with her arm open across her and did the whole of it again from the beginning, in order, out loud, because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

She said, “On the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, at the first hour, in daylight, at two posts on a store road thirteen miles from a wall, I wrote nineteen words on a slate in my own hand with a nib I did not lift, and the figure in it was ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes.”

She said, “That went up a road in a bundle on the second of that month and it went up this stair on a board with forty others on the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four.”

She said, “And on the second of that month there was a strip of wood under that table a foot long and two inches across with a head on it and a column on it and one figure in the column, and the figure was mine, and it was in my hand.”

She lay still on the floor of that room for a count of forty and then she said the next part on its own.

She said, “That hand cannot copy a stamp out of forty different boards and it cannot write anything because there is nothing on the table to write with, and my figure came out of that row in my own hand, with three of my fingers on the nib and the strokes not all the same width.”

## The Reversal

She got up off the floor.

She said, “So the hand did not enter me into anything.”

She said, “A hand at a table cannot enter anybody into anything. It can only enter a thing somebody has already said, and it enters it exactly, and it has been doing that for as long as there has been anything for it to do.”

She said, “And I have been watching the hand for seventy-two days and waiting for it to do an entering, and it cannot do one, and the whole of what I have to work with is that it copies perfectly.”

He said, “Then what is it for?”

She said, “It is for reading.”

She said that, and then she stood at the near end of that table with her hand on the edge of it and did not say the next part for a count of sixty, and then she said it.

## What The Look Is Actually For

She said, “On the sixth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four I sat at this height with my cheek on that wood and worked out that the deciding takes one look and there is no second one and no appeal with and nowhere to stand and wait for it.”

She said, “And that was true, and I have got it written in the back of my head where I keep the things I am not going to be wrong about again, and it is still true.”

She said, “And what it is for is different. That look is not deciding whether to write. A thing that has arrived has already been decided on somewhere else, in a room somebody stood in, out loud, in daylight, standing up, and then it came here to be copied.”

She said, “And I have been sitting in the wrong room for seventy-five days waiting for a hand to decide about me, and there is not one deciding in this building at all.”

## The Second Wrong Instrument

She said it out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the ninth hour of the twenty-third of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four, with the four strips of wood still lying on the stone in front of her where she had left them.

She said, “There was a wrong instrument in the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four when I worked out that this room does not read boards. This is a second one and it is worse.”

She said, “The first one was a thing I had got the wrong end of. This one is a thing I did not know I was looking at at all. I have been counting a hand. I have been counting it for a year and it is not an instrument for anything that is about me, and it never was, and the thing that is about me is somewhere else and it is a person saying a thing in a room.”

He said, “Whose room.”

She said, “Mine.”

She said, “That is where the deciding is. Not up here. In a room where somebody is standing up and saying a thing out loud about themselves, and the thing comes up a stair after, in a bundle, and gets put in a column here by a hand that has got nothing to write with.”

She said, “And I have never once been in one of those rooms while it was happening, and I have done it twice in my life, and one of them was on the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four at two posts on a road.”

## What She Wants Said Standing Up

She said the thing she wants out loud at the ninth hour of the twenty-second, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, before she knew she was going to say it.

She said, “I want to stand up in a room and say a thing about myself and have it go somewhere. That is all of it. I have wanted that since I was nine years old and I have done it four times in my life and twice of those were about a number and once of them was a name on a board and once of them was a sentence to a voice that is not a person in this room.”

He said, “Why have you not done it here.”

She said, “Because until the twenty-third of this month I thought the doing of it was up here.”

She said, “And it is not. And the hand at that table has got nothing to write with and does not need anything, and the only thing it wants is to be given a thing that a person has already said, and there is not one person in this building who has said one.”

He said, “You have.”

She said, “I have said about nine hundred figures out loud in this room in seventy-three days and not one of them went anywhere, because a figure is not a thing about a person. It is what a thing cost.”

## What She Said About The Light And The Figure

She did the sums out loud before she went to sleep on the twenty-third, standing at the near end of that table, because the light goes down whether she says it or not.

She said, “Two thousand three hundred and seventy-six less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and seventeen days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and not one of them going back.”

She said, “The light at the far end of this room is forty-two of my own hand below the top of that cage. Fifty-eight less forty-two is sixteen, and sixteen divided by two is eight, and the eighth day from this one is the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four.”

She said, “And the figure on the strip under that table is four days, two hours and twenty-four minutes out of date, and it was out of date before I came into this room and it will be out of date after everybody in this building is dead.”

The boy was still awake at the near end of the table and he said, “Can you say something about yourself.”

She said, “Yes.”

He said, “Then say it.”

She said, “Not tonight. Tonight I have just found out that a room only gets a thing after a person has stood up in another room and said it, and I have been seventy-three days up here without knowing where the thing happens, and I am not going to use the first thing I understand about it on the first night I understand it.”

He said, “All right.”

She said, “Say that out loud again tomorrow and I will tell you why not.”
