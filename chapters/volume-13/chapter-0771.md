# Two Boards With One Figure

She tried for two days to stop two pieces of wood from going up a stair, and she could not do it, and on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four she found out that the reason is not a rule in this building. The reason is that she was the only thing in the world that could have stopped it and she did not stop it.

## What She Tried

On the twenty-fourth of that month she went under the long table and turned the forty boards over one at a time with three fingers and a thumb and she did not find the one with her price on it and she did not find the other one either.

She found the board she wrote five on. It came up that stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four with about forty other things, she wrote five on it at the tenth hour of the eighteenth of that month, she put it back under the shelf in the stack, and it came back off that stair empty on the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four and she has never been able to see it go.

That is the board she could find. It is the only one of the forty she can find by anything other than luck and it is a board that went up and came back with nothing on it, and it has been in that stack for fifty-six days.

She took a different one out and put it on the stone under the near end of that table, face down.

It did not matter.

## The Reason There Is No Procedure

She worked that out on the twenty-fifth, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table, and it took her most of the morning and she went over it four times because she did not want it to be true.

A thing that has not been read is a thing that has not arrived. That is the rule and she has carried it since the sixth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four.

A thing that has been read is a thing that has arrived, and once it has arrived there is no procedure in this county for a thing that has arrived and does not go where arrived things go, and there has never been one, and the reason there has never been one is that not one person in four hundred years has ever wanted one to go somewhere else.

She said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the ninth hour of the twenty-fifth:

“A thing that has been read cannot be un-read. There is nowhere on paper in this county to put that, and not one mouth in this building would take it back.”

And then she stopped and went back over the four ways she had tried it on her own in one night, and she said them out loud because that is the rule she has kept since she was nine years old.

She said, “I cannot be given a thing, so there is no one going to hand me a reason and I cannot take the board away by being given it.”

She said, “I cannot reach that table. I can reach the edge and I can get up on it and I have to go twenty-two paces on my hands and knees with a board in my hand and there is no one in this building to carry it for me.”

She said, “And a thing that has been read is a thing that has arrived, and I have been escaping being entered since I was nine years old and I have got exactly one of those in this building and it went up an armful on the first of a month and came back empty.”

She said, “And the fourth way is that I could say a figure out loud in this room, because the hand writes what a person says and nothing else, and I have done it twice and I know it works.”

And she stood there for a count of ninety after that last one and did not say the fifth way, and the fifth way is the one she had agreed not to use on the eleventh of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four and she has not gone near it since.

## The Fourth Mark On The Rail

She went down the low door on the twenty-sixth at the ninth hour and took the first four hundred and forty with her left hand on the rail and stood on the fourth landing in the dark with her hand on the flat of it.

There are four marks on that rail now and there were three on the tenth of this month.

The first three are groups of short strokes with a longer one across the top of them, and they are cut at the same angle and they are cut to the same depth, and she worked out on the tenth that they are one mark for each time a boy of sixteen came up that tower.

The fourth is one long stroke and it is not cut at the same angle. It goes across the flat of the rail and down the side of it and it is cut deeper at the near end than at the far end, and the wood has lifted along the whole of it.

She put her fingers in the lifted wood and could not have said how long ago it was done and she did not try.

She said out loud, standing on that landing in the dark with her hand in the groove of it:

“There is a mark on this rail that is not that boy’s.”

She said, “And I have no way on the face of this landing of telling when it was cut and I am not going to guess at a figure for it, and I am not going to go round this building looking for another one to compare it against.”

She said, “And I am not going down that stair to ask him, because I have said out loud twice now that I am not going to tell him what a row is and a thing I have said twice is a thing I have decided.”

## The Twenty-Seventh

And on the twenty-seventh she did the sums out loud at the ninth hour, standing at the near end of that long table, with the board still lying on the top of it where she had left it, and she got to the last of them and stopped for a count of ninety before she said it.

She said, “Fifty-eight less fifty is eight and eight divided by two is four and the fourth day from the twenty-seventh of this month is the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four, which is four days from here.”

She said, “And two thousand five hundred less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is nine hundred and forty-one days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and not one of them going back and one of them gone into that lamp.”

She said, “And the figure at the head of my row is ten days, seven hours and twelve minutes out of date this morning, and the figure in the second pair of cuts down under it went out of date the day after I put it there, and there is not one instrument in the world that puts either of them right.”

And then she said the last of it and she said it angrily, which is the only time in a hundred and ninety-nine days that she has said anything in that room in any other voice.

She said, “Two boards with my price on them are going up that stair in four days in one armful and I have had two days to stop it and I could not stop it and the reason is that two hundred and six days ago I said a figure out loud at two posts on a road and did not know what I was doing.”

She said, “And it is not that anybody has refused me. I want that on the record in my own mouth because it is the truest thing I have said in this room. Not one person in this building has refused me anything. Not one person in this building has refused me anything because there has not been one thing for me to ask for until the sixth of this month and what I said then was that I am not going back to a brother two days of road and six hundred feet below me.”

She said, “And what is going up that stair on the first is a copy of a copy of a figure I said in the open on a road at two posts when I was sixteen, and I am going to stand in this room and let it go, and I am not going to say one word while it goes.”

And then she got herself down off whatever she was sitting on and went and got down under that table and lay on the stone with her arm open beside her and put her fingers on her own strip of pale wood and did not move it, and there was a five in the second pair of cuts down under a price that was ten days, seven hours and twelve minutes out of date, and it was hers, and nothing in the world could read it.
