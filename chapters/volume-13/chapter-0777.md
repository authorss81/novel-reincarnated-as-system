# A Word She Has Not Heard

She refused the amendment out loud at the ninth hour of the twenty-seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that long table, and she did not refuse it out of pride, and she said so, and she said the other reason, and the other reason was that she has never once in nineteen years written down a word she had not heard first.

## What It Costs To Say No

She took four days to work it out and she took them at the near end of that table with her hand on the edge of it and she did not go down either stair once in those four days.

The amendment costs one feed. The feed is hers. The light comes up past the top of that cage for twenty-nine days and then it comes down to the cut in that wall and it does not come up on its own.

She said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the ninth hour of the twenty-second of that month:

“If I do not put a hand into that cage then there is no feed, and the person at that table said on the second of this month that they are not going to put one in on their own and have nothing for it, and that has not happened here in four hundred years.”

She said, “And they were not refusing me anything by saying it. They were telling me the true shape of the thing, which is the only thing a room does.”

She said, “So if I do nothing then the light comes down to that cut at the fourth hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and it stops there for about a day and then it does not stop there.”

And she stood there for a count of a hundred and forty with her hand on the edge of that table, and that is the first time in this building that she has said out loud a thing she is about to do that nobody has asked her about.

## The Second Of The Six Terms

And on the twenty-third she worked out what else goes with it, and it took her most of the day, and she did not find it until the ninth hour and she was not expecting it.

She said the six terms out loud in that room again on the twenty-third, standing up, in daylight, one at a time, the way she has said them in every room she has ever said them in.

One thing at a time.

In daylight.

Standing up.

You say it out loud as you go. Every line. Before it is a line anywhere else.

If there is one I have not heard first then it is not a record, it is a thing you did on your own, and there is no version of this where I find out about it later.

And if you put down one word that is not true, I will know it inside a day, and there is no road back for either of us.

And it is a record. It is not a hand. You do not decide anything. I decide everything, and the only thing you have is that it is written down.

And then she said, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table:

“The second one is in daylight.”

She said, “And I have said that term out loud two hundred and twenty-five times in nineteen years and I have never once stopped at it and looked at what it was doing.”

She said, “There is no daylight in this room. There is a lamp in a cage at the far end of a table and the wall behind the light is the wall and there is not one window in this building that I have been able to see from the inside. I have been saying that term out loud in lamplight for two hundred and twenty-six days and it is the only one of the six I have been getting wrong.”

She said, “And if that lamp goes out then I cannot say that term again in this room, and not in any room, because there is not one other room I have been in since I was nine years old that has daylight in it either.”

## The Only Line In The World She Cannot Check

And on the twenty-sixth she did the last of the working out and it is the whole of why she is not going to do it, and she did it standing in daylight at the near end of that table with a person nine feet off along it.

She said, “There is not one figure in this building that has gone into a row in two hundred and twenty-eight days that I have not heard said out loud in this room first.”

She said, “Not one. Not a figure and not a name. I said a figure out loud at two posts on a road when I was sixteen and it came up that stair in an armful and a hand wrote it here. I said a man’s name in this room on purpose after he asked me not to and a hand wrote it on a board and it went up that stair and came back and there is not one instrument in the world that takes a name out of a row that has been above the line for four hundred years.”

She said, “And that is because I said them out loud here, and the hand writes what a person says and nothing else, and that is the only reason there is a record of me in this county that anybody can read.”

She said, “And the amendment would put a figure in a column in my row that I have not said out loud in this room and that no one in this building has ever said out loud in this room, and the room has never amended a row and there is no instrument in the world that amends a row twice.”

She said, “So I would never be able to check it. Not once. Not ever.”

She said, “And I have been nineteen years old and I have kept my hand off nine hundred and fifty-four lines of a record and I have not written one word that was not true and there is not one road back from a word that is not true, and I am not going to be the first one to find out.”

## Saying It Out Loud In Front Of The Room

And at the ninth hour of the twenty-seventh of that month she said it out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that long table, and the person at that table was standing at it.

She said, “I am not going to put a day into that lamp.”

She said, “And that is the answer to what you told me on the second of this month and I am giving it to you in the ordinary voice standing up, one thing at a time, because that is how this building works and I am not going to be the one who breaks it.”

And then she said the rest of it and she said it in the same voice and she did not stop in it once, and it took a count of ninety.

She said, “I am not refusing it because I am proud and I want that said out loud in this room because there is not one person in this building who is going to say it for me.”

She said, “The figure your amending would put in that column is the room’s. It is a figure this county has been keeping since the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning two thousand and fifty-four and it is not mine and I have not seen it and I cannot check it.”

She said, “The figure I want standing is mine. It is a five in the second pair of cuts down under a price, in my own hand, written on the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, and it is wrong the moment after it is written and it is going to be wrong on the day after the next one, and it is the only figure in the world that is a person instead of a price.”

She said, “And I would rather have a wrong figure in a column that cannot be added up than a right figure in one that can, and I know exactly how that sounds in a room like this one and I am saying it anyway.”

And the person at that table did not turn round and did not look up and did not move, and the hands stayed on the near edge of the wood, and there was not one mark on anything in that room.

## Three Days Of It Coming Down

And after that she said nothing else about it at all, and she went and did the last three days of that month the way she does days.

She said the six terms out loud in that room on the twenty-seventh and on the twenty-eighth and on the twenty-ninth, standing up, in daylight, one at a time, all six of them, and she did that on purpose.

She said out loud on the twenty-ninth, at the near end of that table, at the ninth hour, that she was going to say them out loud on the thirtieth as well and that it would be the last time she said the second one in this room.

She said, “Fifty-eight of my own hand is from the top of that cage down to the cut in that wall, and the light is forty-eight of my own hand below the top of that cage, and fifty-eight less forty-eight is ten, and ten divided by two is five, and there are five days of that light left in this room.”

She said, “And it comes down to that cut at the fourth hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, and that is the day the last forty things come up that stair.”

She said, “And there is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain who has been up that stair three times and has asked me twice why that lamp is lit and I have not given him an answer, and on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four there is going to be no light in that room and he is going to come up it anyway, because he cannot leave a thing alone, and I have known that about him since the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four.”

She said, “And I am not going down to tell him, and I said out loud on the sixth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four that I was not going to tell him what a row is, and I have not taken it back and I am not going to.”
