# Forty Boards And Not One Of Them Hers

She worked out how to read a thing lying on a floor in the dark under a table four feet off the ground, and it took her four days and the use of three fingers and a thumb, and she got through four of the boards on the second of the month beginning 2,324 and nine of them by the fifth, and she has never in her life worked a thing out that was harder than that.

## Getting Under It

The space under that table is about three feet high at the near end and about two and a half at the middle, and the floor of it is stone that has not been swept in a hundred years, and there is no light in it at all.

She got in on her hands and knees at the second hour of the second of the month beginning 2,324 with her head going in first because there is no other way, and she was in a count of nine.

Her breath went to nine in the first count and stayed there and she was not able to turn round from the second count, because there is a cross-beam of the table’s frame across her back at about the height of her shoulders, and if she went on forward she would be under the middle of it with a board stack either side of her.

She lay still in the dark on stone for a count of two hundred, counting, because that is what she does, and then she worked her way back out backwards on her hands with her elbows, and she got out of it in a count of sixty, and she sat on the floor of the room at the near end of the table and had her breath down off nine in a count of a hundred and forty.

She did that four times over four days and she did not fall and nothing broke and she got exactly as far as the second board each time.

## Reading With Three Fingers And A Thumb

You cannot read a board in the dark. That is the first thing and it took two days for her to accept it.

You can read a board with your fingers. She has read her own hand with her fingers for nine years without knowing she was doing it, because she knows what her own writing feels like the way she knows a step is a step, and the little finger going in the middle of a line on a slate did not change the way a nine makes.

Forty boards lying flat in a stack in the dark can be turned over one at a time with the fingers of one hand, which is three fingers and a thumb and no more, and can be felt, and that is enough.

## Four Days Under A Table

She went under that table four times in four days and there is not one thing about those four days that she would tell another person about, and she has told him about all four.

She got her head in first, because there is no other way, and she was in it in a count of nine.

On the second of the month beginning 2,324 she lay on her side under the near end of it and put her hand on the stack and turned one board over and then could not get to the next one without coming out, because the boards are laid flat in a stack and a board that is under another board has to be lifted and she has three fingers and a thumb and one arm open and four feet of stone underneath her.

She got two boards.

On the third she worked out a way, which is to lie on her back under the stack with both knees up and lift the whole of it with her knees and read the top one with her fingers, and that got her nine, and she did that three times and got twenty-seven.

On the fourth she did it for four hours and got forty.

And on the fifth she did it for four hours again to be sure, because she was going to make a statement about forty boards and she is not going to make a statement about twenty-seven boards.

She said it out loud on the fifth of the month beginning 2,324 at the ninth hour, lying on her back on a stone floor under a table with her knees holding four pounds of pale wood off her face:

“Forty boards. Forty strokes in forty corners. Forty figures and every one of them the same figure. I have had my hand on every one of them twice.”

## What The Bundle Of Papers Is

And on the fifth, before she came out, she put her arm into the bundle of papers up to the elbow and did the same thing to that, and she could feel the leaves and the string and the cloth and she could not read a single thing with her fingers because she does not know what a leaf of paper in that bundle is supposed to feel like and a hand does not read.

The string is a cord and it is round the bundle twice and it is not tied in a knot. She could get her three fingers and a thumb under the second turn of it.

She could get them under it and she did not pull it, and she said out loud why, lying on her back with her arm in a bundle of papers on the floor of a room four miles above a mountain:

“Because the first time a person touches a bundle in a room where a hand copies figures exactly, that is a fact about the person and not about the bundle, and I am not going to be that fact. Not yet.”


## The Same On Every One

And on the third of the month beginning 2,324 she got to the eleventh board and she found the mark, and on the twelfth, and on the thirteenth, and it was the same on all three.

One stroke, cut or written in the top corner of the board, and one figure beside it.

The stroke on the eleventh was the stroke on the twelfth. The figure on the eleventh was the figure on the twelfth. And the figure was not a date and it was not a count of anything she could work out from the board itself, and it was the same figure on every board she put her hand on.

By the fifth of the month she had put her hand on all forty and there is not one of them with a stroke in a different place or a figure of a different shape, and there is not one of them with anything else on it at all.

She said it out loud lying on her side on the floor under a table with a cross-beam across her back and her breath at nine, at the sixth hour of the fifth of the month beginning 2,324:

“Forty boards went down this stair a month ago and forty boards went along that table at four to a minute, and at the end of it every one of them has one stroke in the same corner and one figure of the same shape, and the figure is the same figure.”

She said, “And nineteen words are on one of them with a date and a place on it in my own hand, and this room did not look at them.”

## What That Means

And then she worked out the rest of it, under the table, on stone, in the dark, with her fingers, and she worked it out out loud because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

She said, “A room does not read boards.”

She said, “It goes along them at four to a minute and it writes one stroke and one figure on every one of them and the figure is the same on every one, and a figure that comes out the same on forty different boards has not come out of what is on the boards.”

She said, “And the thing that came up that stair on the first of this month and went along that table for forty minutes was forty boards and a bundle of papers, and the boards are not what is read in this room, and there is a bundle of papers on the floor of this room with a string round it and I have not got to it, and I have been going at the boards for four days.”

## The Wrong Thing For A Year

And then she got herself back out from under the table and sat against the doorframe at the near end and said the thing that has changed the shape of the last nine years of her life, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice.

She said, “I have been counting the wrong thing for a year.”

She said, “I have spent a year believing that a figure in a column is how a thing becomes real. I put a number on a slate because a number cannot be taken off a person, and I carried it up a stair because I thought that was the way a thing gets into a room. And I have just put my hand on forty boards and not one of them is different from the other thirty-nine, and the room did not read one line of what was on them.”

She said, “A figure does not get a person into a room. It gets a person’s price into a room. And the difference between those two things is the whole of what I have come up a stair for, and I worked it out on my back under a table with my fingers and I am not going to be able to put it anywhere.”

That one went out loud on the stone under that table with the cross-beam across her back, and there is no part of this room that hears a thing said in it, and what came back was the same as what came back on the first of the month, which is a voice and not a person.

He said, “That is the first thing you have said up here that you did not know when you said it.”

She said, “That is the first thing I have said up here at all that you have heard.”

## What She Wants And Is Not Going Down For

And then she said what she wants, which is not a thing she wants in this room and is a thing she wants at the bottom of a stair eight hundred and eighty steps below this one.

She said, “There is a bundle of papers on the floor of this room with a string round it, and whatever this room reads is in it, and I cannot read it in the dark under a table with my fingers because I do not know what it is supposed to feel like.”

She said, “And I have not got anything to read it with. There is nothing in this room to read a thing with. There is a lamp that burns with nothing in it and a table I cannot reach and a person who does not turn round.”

She said, “And I am going to have to find out what a figure in this room is made out of before I can find out what anything of mine is made out of, and I am not going down those stairs this month.”
