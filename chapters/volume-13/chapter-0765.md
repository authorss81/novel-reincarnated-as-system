# The First Free Thing She Decided

The sixth bundle came up that stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four and went down it again with everything on it, and she stood four feet off the end of that table and could not see one thing that was hers, and on the seventh of that month she said out loud, standing, in daylight, that she is not going back, and that is the first thing she has decided in nineteen years that nothing at the bottom of a stair was going to come and undo.

## The Sixth Bundle

The thirtieth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four came and went and she did nothing with it.

The first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four came at the second hour, and the boots came up the turn, and the man with about forty boards under one arm and a bundle of papers in a cloth under the other came out of the place in the wall behind the lamp’s shelf and put them down and went back out without looking at anything.

She stood four feet off the near end of that table and watched it for the sixth time.

Ten minutes for the forty boards and thirty for the bundle, and the hands did not stop and did not hurry and did not go back, and the boards went under the table in a stack, and she went and got down on her hands and knees with her head going in first and put her fingers on her own strip of pale wood and it was where she had put it back on the twenty-second of that month, flat, face up, grain running up.

She read it with her fingers in the dark under that table.

Flat on the stone under that table, with her hand on her own strip of pale wood and nobody in the room with her, she said it out loud:

“The head is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes and the first pair of cuts down has the same figure in it.”

She said, “And the second pair of cuts down has five in it, in my own hand, and it has been there nine days, and it came up no stair and went down none.”

She said, “And twelve pairs of cuts under that are empty.”

And then she came out from under that table and stood at the near end of it and said the rest of it with a person standing nine feet off along it.

She said, “Forty boards went along that table in ten minutes and there are two things in this building that are mine and I could not tell you which two of the forty carried them.”

She said, “And one of them is on a strip under this table and one of them is on a board in that stack, and the board goes up that stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four, and I will not be able to see it and I will not be able to correct it, and that is what I decided on the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four and I have not been able to think of one reason since to take it back.”

## The Line On The First Of This Month

At the fourth hour of that first of the month the light came down to the cut in the stone behind that lamp and stopped on it.

She said, “There it is again.”

She said, “And that is the third first of a month that the mark has fallen on, and the second one in four hundred years that has been a first instead of a thirtieth, and both of them are mine, and there is not one row in this building with a word about either of them.”

And at the second hour of the second of that month a hand went up between the four bars of that cage and closed on the base of the light and it came up past the top of that cage in about eleven seconds, and the person at that table was standing at the near edge of it afterwards with both hands on it exactly as they had been standing.

She had her own hand at the end of that forearm before it and her own hand at the end of that forearm after it, and it was down by the width of the little finger on her left hand, and it was the same as the other times.

And then she went and got under the table and read her own strip again with her fingers and the two figures were where she had left them and the wood had not moved.

## What She Is Not Going Back To

She sat with it for five days. That is what she did from the second of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four to the sixth of it, and she did not go down that stair once in five days and she did not go down the other one either.

On the third she went and stood at the bar against the wall on the left of that room, nine feet nine, broken end towards the door, and she did not pick it up.

On the fourth she went down the low door and the first four hundred and forty with a rail on one side and her left hand on it, and she got to the fourth landing where the lamp in a cage has been out since the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and she put her hand into that cage and the iron was cold, and she went back up.

On the fifth she said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table:

“There is a bag at the bottom of that tower with a dish and a lump of clay and a ring the width of a finger and a key to a drawer and a strip of hand-made paper in it, and I have been down there four times in a hundred and seventy-nine days and I have not gone down for any of them, and I am not going to go down for the drawer, and I am not going to go down for the paper either.”

She said, “And I am not going down to tell a boy of sixteen what a row is.”

## Ning Wu

She said his name out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table on the sixth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four, at the ninth hour, and it was the first time in nine years she had said it out loud anywhere above the line.

She said, “Ning Wu.”

She said, “He is two days of road from the turn and six hundred feet below where I am standing, and he is nineteen years old, and he was nine years in a barrow ground and he has not given that up in nine years and he is not going to, and I have not asked him to and I am not going to.”

She said, “And I have not seen him in nine years and I did not see him on my way up that stair and I am not going to see him on the way down, and that is not because of anything he has done and it is not a punishment and not one mouth has told me to do it.”

And she stopped for a count of a hundred and forty and said the rest of it, and it is the sentence the whole five days came to.

She said, “I went up that tower because there was nothing at the bottom of any stair I was at, and I am still here, and every single day of it has been the same day over again with a figure on it, and I could go down two days of road and six hundred feet right now and stand in front of that boy and he would be exactly where I left him and he would not be glad to see me and he would not be sorry to see me either.”

She said, “And I am not going to.”

She said, “That is not a promise. That is a figure. I have got nine hundred and twenty-one days lighter than I was and every one of them has gone on its own at one hour and twelve minutes a day and one of them went into that lamp and did not come back, and I am not going to spend one of them on a road.”

She said, “And it is the first thing I have decided in nineteen years that no mouth in this building asked me about and nothing was going to undo for me and no reason in this building or out of it is going to come and fetch me back. I have been let and I have been lost and I have been decided about. I have not decided.”

She said, “And he settles nothing. He is not on a page of any book I have got and he is not going to be on one, and that is not a thing I have done to him. It is a thing I have done to me, and I did it at the ninth hour of the sixth of this month and I am not going to go and look at it again.”

## The Sums On The Seventh

She did the sums on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four at the ninth hour, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table with the light at the far end of it ten of her own hand below the top of that cage.

She said, “Two thousand four hundred and eighty less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is nine hundred and twenty-one days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and not one of them going back and one of them gone into that lamp.”

She said, “And fifty-eight less ten is forty-eight and forty-eight divided by two is twenty-four and the twenty-fourth day from the seventh of this month is the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four, and forty things are going to come up that stair on it.”

She said, “And the figure at the head of my row is nine days, seven hours and twelve minutes out of date, and the figure in the second pair of cuts down under it went out of date the day after I put it there, and there is not one instrument in the world that puts either of them right.”

And then she said the last of it, standing, in daylight, and she said it looking at the far end of that long table and not at the place in the wall behind the lamp’s shelf, where the stair she has gone up and down three times in nine hundred and four steps comes back down into the floor of this room.

She said, “There are two figures in a column under that table now and one of them is a price and one of them is not a price, and the room has had them there since the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four and it has not made a sound about either of them.”

She said, “And a column with two figures in it is a column that gets added up, and this one cannot be added up, and I do not know what a room does with a column it cannot add up.”

She said, “And there is not one person in this building who knows any of it happened, and there is a boy on a floor four miles down that mountain who still does not know what a row is, and a brother two days of road and six hundred feet below me who is not coming up, and a lamp ten of my own hand below the top of that cage that will be standing on that cut again on the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four.”

She said, “And I am not going to be able to work out what any of those three is for before that day, and I am going to be standing here when it comes, and I have said what I am not going to do and I have not said a single word about what I am going to do.”
