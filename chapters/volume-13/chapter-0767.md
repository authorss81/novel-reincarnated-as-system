# What One Look Is Worth

She worked out on the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four what one look costs, and the answer took her the whole of that day and part of the next, and none of it is about the look.

## What A Look Takes

There is a lamp in a cage at the far end of that table and a person standing at the near edge of it with both hands on the wood, and for four hundred years a thing has arrived, and the hand has come down over it once, and the thing has been what the thing said.

The hand takes about eleven seconds over a board. She has watched it six times and she has counted every one and the figure is the same every time and she has never been able to find out why a count of six comes out the same.

That is the whole ceremony. That is the whole of what four hundred years of a room consists of.

She said out loud on the twelfth, standing on the floor at the near end of that table with her right arm open in front of her:

“There is no second look.”

She said, “And there is no appeal, and there has never been anything to appeal with, and I have worked that out on the sixth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four and it has not moved a day in a hundred and fifty-six days.”

She said, “And what I am about to do is spend the first one, and there will not be another one in this building for me, and there is not one instrument in the world that gives one back.”

And then she said the part that made her put a hand flat on the near edge of that table and leave it there for a count of ninety.

“I have wanted one since I came up that stair.”

She said, “And I have never once said that out loud in nineteen years, and I have said every other number out loud in this room a hundred and eighty-two times.”

## Putting Down And Being Given

She spent the thirteenth of that month on a question that had been standing in the middle of everything since the twentieth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and that she had never once put in words.

What is the difference between a thing being put in front of a hand and a thing being given to a person?

She went over it three times that day with her hand on the edge of the table and got the same answer three times and did not like it any more at the end of the third time than she had at the end of the first.

A thing that is put in front of a hand, the hand reads. That is all it does. It does not become yours and it does not stop being what it was. You have not given it and you have not got it. You have put something where something else could look at it.

A thing that is given is taken. It goes out of the giver's hands and into somebody else's and after that it is a different thing with a different person holding it.

She said it out loud, standing, in daylight, at the ninth hour of the thirteenth, and she said it slowly because it is the sentence the whole two days came to:

“In a hundred and eighty-five days in this building nothing has been given to me.”

She said, “And the reason is not that there is nothing here to give. The reason is that there is no way in the world of my getting hold of one.”

She said, “And putting a piece of wood down on a table and having a hand read it is not being given a thing, and I have not worked out for four days whether that makes it better or worse, and on the third time I said it out loud I found out which way it goes.”

She said, “Worse. It is worse, because a thing that is put in front of a hand comes back having been read, and I will not be able to un-read it, and I will not be able to get it back into the wood.”

And then she said the other half of it, which took her the whole of the afternoon and which is the half that made her want to do it.

She said, “A thing that is put in front of a hand is the only kind of entering there has ever been for me. That is all of it. Every time in nineteen years that anything has come near me and gone away again, that is what happened, and it happened either because I did it or because I stood still while it did.”

She said, “And I have been escaping it since I was nine years old by being somewhere with no hand in it, and I have been in this room a hundred and eighty-five days and there has been a hand in it every single hour of every single one of them.”

She said, “And I have not felt it once, and that is the thing I am about to find out about.”

## What He Would Have Made Of It

On the thirteenth she went down the low door and the first four hundred and forty with her left hand on the rail, and she stood on the fourth landing where the lamp in that cage has been out since the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and she put her hand on the three marks on the flat of that rail again.

What he is good at is worth nothing in this room. She worked that out early in this building and it has not improved. He can climb four hundred and forty with a rail and four hundred and forty without one. He can count better than she can when he is frightened. There is not one thing any of that is worth at the near end of a long table with a hand at it.

She said out loud, standing on that landing in the dark with her hand on the rail, and there was nothing in the room to hear it:

“There is not one thing I could hand him that would let him measure anything.”

She said, “And I cannot be given a thing, and he cannot be given a thing either, and the only way a fact gets from me to him is that I say it and he carries it, and I have got nothing short enough to carry down four hundred and forty steps that is also true.”

And she said the last of it standing there, and it took a count of ninety:

“If I tell him what a row is on the morning I put a piece of my own on this table, then whatever happens to that piece of wood happens to him as well, and he is sixteen, and he will go down that stair and say so, and there is not one person in this building who could correct what he says.”

## Saying It Before It Is A Line

And on the fourteenth she worked out the last of it, and the last of it is why she had not done it on the eleventh.

Six terms, one at a time, in daylight, standing up, said out loud as she goes, every line before it is a line anywhere else, and if there is one she has not heard first then it is not a record, and if he puts down one word that is not true she will know it inside a day and there is no road back.

She said those six terms out loud in that room on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and wrote them onto the page of her, and they have been the way she has done everything since.

She said out loud on the fourteenth, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table:

“The fourth of the six terms is that I say it out loud as I go, every line, before it is a line anywhere else.”

She said, “And this room is a place where a line happens. That is the only thing this room does. And I have said a name out loud in it once and a figure out loud in it twice and I know exactly what a hand does with a word that is in the air in here.”

She said, “And I am not going to take the six terms to be a promise about rooms that cannot write.”

She said, “I am going to say it out loud anyway, and I am going to say it before I do it, and if the hand at the far end of this table writes it down then I will hear the stroke of it and I will not be able to un-hear it, and that is the price and I am paying it on purpose.”

## The Words Before The Words

And at the ninth hour of the fourteenth she said it, standing at the near end of that table with her hand on the edge of it and the strip of pale wood on the top of it and her right arm open in front of her.

She said, “I am going to put a strip of pale wood on this table at the near end of it, face up, with a figure at the head of it and a column under that head, and there are two figures in that column and one of them I wrote myself on the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four.”

She said, “And the hand at the far end of this table is going to come down over it once, and there is not going to be a second one, and I am nineteen years old and I have wanted this for a hundred and eighty-six days and I do not know what it is going to say about me.”

And then nothing happened for a count of ninety.

The person at the far end of that table did not look up. The hands did not come off the near edge. No board came out from under the shelf. The light came down that wall two of her own hand a day and it was twenty of her own hand below the top of that cage, and there was not one mark on anything in that room.

And she said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table:

“It is in the air in here.”

She said, “And nothing has written it down yet.”

And she went and got a board out of the stack under the middle of that table on her back with a cross-beam across her shoulders, because she has worked out that a thing that arrives has to arrive and not be laid there by her own hand.
