# Nothing Speaks To Him

He came up that stair a third time on the thirtieth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and he did not knock and he did not call and he did not come up in the dark this time, and the fourth bundle went along that table on the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen with a name in it, and nothing in this building has spoken to him.

## The Third Time Up That Stair

She heard him at the fourth hour and she knew it was him by the count of them and by the stop on the turn.

He came out of the place in the wall behind the lamp’s shelf wet through again and stood against the doorframe and got his breath, and it took less than it had on the eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four because he had done it three times.

He said, “I have not come up here to ask you again.”

She said, “You have come up here to get an answer you can carry down.”

He said, “I said I do not know twice and the carter has stopped asking me and started asking the man who counts the boards at the far end of the floor, and that man does not know what a lamp is and he is going to find out, and then it will not be me it is about.”

She said, “That is true and I have not got one word to put in front of it.”

He said, “I know you have not. I have come up eight hundred and eighty steps to be able to say I asked.”

She said, “You could have said that from the bottom of the stair and it would have been worth the same.”

He said, “It would not have. I asked from the bottom of the stair and the man counting the boards asked me what I meant by the lamp at the top, and I did not have anything, and I have had twenty-six days to think about having nothing.”

She said, “Get your breath. Sit down at the near end and do not touch that table.”

## The Bundle On The First

And she had two days to sit with that and she did not get any further with it than she had got on the fifth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four, which is that there is no answer small enough to be true.

Then the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen came.

She stood four feet off the near end of that table and the boy stood in the doorway and the boots came up the turn at the second hour and the man with about forty boards under one arm and a bundle of papers in a cloth under the other came out of the place in the wall behind the lamp’s shelf and put them down and went back out without looking at anything.

Ten minutes for the forty boards at four to a minute and thirty for the bundle and the hands did not stop and did not hurry and did not go back.

And that is the fourth time she has watched that, and it is the first time she has watched it with a figure of her own on a board somewhere in the middle of a stack, and there is not one way in the world she has of telling which one of the forty that is.

She said out loud at the end of the forty minutes, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“Forty minutes. Ten and thirty. And it has not been forty minutes since the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four and it has been forty minutes every single time.”

## The Lamp Standing On That Line

And on that day the light at the far end of that room came down onto the line cut in the stone behind it and stopped on the cut and did not go under it.

She said it out loud to the boy, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the fourth hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen:

“Look at that.”

He said, “It is on the line.”

She said, “It is on the line and there is nothing above it and nothing below it, and it has been two hands a day since the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four when a hand came into that cage, and this is the second time in four hundred years that a lamp in this room has stood there.”

He said, “What happens when it gets there.”

She said, “A hand goes into that cage and it comes down to that cut and stops, and then it opens and it comes up off the cut and past the top of that cage, and that person’s arm is thinner afterwards by the width of the little finger on my left hand.”

He said, “Whose arm.”

She said, “The one that goes in.”

He looked at that for a while and did not say anything and she let him.

## What She Waited For And Did Not Get

And on the second and the third of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen she waited, out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, and she said what she was waiting for so that it would be a thing in the room.

She said, “There is a thing that reads records. A name went into a board in this room on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, and that board went up that stair with forty others on the first of this month, and it is above the line now.”

She said, “So it has been read. That is the whole of what I know and I know it is a figure I worked out in her own voice on the floor of this room, not a figure anybody gave me.”

She said, “And on the second of this month I stood in this room from the first hour to the ninth hour waiting for something and nothing came, and on the third of this month I did it again and nothing came, and there was no mark on that table and no stroke and no figure anywhere and no hand came off the near edge except for the boards.”

She said, “And the thing that reads records does not speak when it finds a thing. That is what he told me in this room in the ordinary voice on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four in a count of about forty, and I have had eight days to find out whether he was wrong about it and he was not wrong about it.”

He said, “About what.”

She said, “About who found out. Not you. I am not going to talk about you.”

## The Cord Under The Far End Of That Table

And there is a bundle of papers on the floor under the far end of that table with a cord round it twice and not knotted, and she put her arm into it on the third of that month at the sixth hour and she did not pull it, and she had put her arm into it in the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four and in the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and she has not pulled it once.

She said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the sixth hour of the third of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen:

“There is a bundle of papers under that end of this table and a cord round it twice and it is not knotted, and I have had my arm into it three times in about a hundred and fifteen days and I have not pulled it once, and I am not going to pull it now.”

He said, “Why not.”

She said, “Because it is the only thing in this room that has not been looked at, and if I pull it then there is not one thing left in this building I could find out about, and I have been living on the not knowing for a hundred and fifteen days and I am not ready to stop.”

She said, “And because the cord is round it twice and not knotted, and a knot is a thing you cannot get a hand into, and this is not knotted so a hand goes straight in, and a room that has had four hundred years to shut a thing is not a room that shuts things.”

She said, “And I have not pulled it and I am not going to, and that is the same kind of decision as the bar against that wall and I am not going to improve on it.”

## A Boy In A Doorway Looking At A Room

And on the third of that month he stood in the doorway for about a count of ninety and looked along that table the way a person looks at a room, and she had not told him to and she could not have stopped him.

He said, “That is a long table for one person.”

She said, “It is.”

He said, “There is a place on the far side of it. Under where his hands are. It is not like the rest of the wood.”

She said, “I know.”

He said, “It is worn. Somebody sat there a great many times and there is no chair.”

She said, “There have never been chairs in that room.”

He said, “Then what is that.”

And she stood on the floor at the near end of that table with her arm open at her side and she did not say one word about it, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in daylight, in that room, and she has not said one word about it since.

She said, “Do not touch that table.”

He said, “I was not going to touch that table.”

## The Only Person Who Knows

And she said the last of it on the third of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen, at the ninth hour, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, with a boy of sixteen four feet off the end of a table in a room where one look decides everything and there is no second look and no appeal.

She said, “There is a row in this building with my price at the head of it and one figure in the column, and there is a figure in a board on that table that I wrote myself on the twentieth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, and there is a name in a board that went up that stair on the first of this month, and there is not one other thing in the whole of this building that knows about any of the three.”

She said, “And the figure at the head of that row is six days, two hours and twenty-four minutes out of date and there is no instrument above this line that could tell me so.”

She said, “And I cannot see the board. It went up the stair on the first with forty others and it came back down empty like all of them do, and I am not going to be able to see it and I am not going to be able to take anything out of it.”

She said, “And it has not spoken to him.”

She said, “And I am the only person in the world who knows what is in it, and I am standing in a room with a lamp in a cage and a bar nine feet nine against the wall on the left and a boy of sixteen in a doorway, and there is not one instrument in this building that can do one single thing about any part of it.”
