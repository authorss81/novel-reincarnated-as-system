# What The Room Stands On

She worked out on the ninth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four that a row is entered by the person it is about, and she worked it out from the furniture in six days, and the reason it exists is the reason she needs it and there is not one mouth in that building that is ever going to say so.

## The Room With One Person Missing

On the sixth of that month she got up off the floor at the second hour and the room was the same room it has been for ninety days and it was a different size, and she put the difference down out loud before she did anything else with it.

She said, standing, in the ordinary voice:

“There is not one person in this building now who can count the boards coming off that table at four to a minute, and I had that back for four days and I have not got it now.”

She said, “That is the second time in nineteen years and I have had it for four days out of nineteen years and I am not going to stand here and pretend that four days is not a thing that happened.”

She said, “The low door is standing open and the stair behind it is dark and it is a boy of sixteen going down four hundred and forty steps with no rail on them and I counted him down and he is the only other person who has ever counted anything with me and he cannot count and he got ten minutes out of forty boards on his own.”

She looked at the open door for a count of sixty and then she stopped looking at it and went and got under the table.

## The Dust On The Top Of A Row

She lay on her back on the stone under that table with the cross-beam across her back and her right arm open out in front of her, and she put her hand on her own strip, and then she put her hand on the floor around it.

And she worked out the first thing, which took her a whole day.

She said, out loud, on the flat of her back, in the ordinary voice:

“There is no dust on the top of mine.”

She said, “There is a grey skin of dust on the stone under that table and there is a grey skin of dust on the tops of those three old ones, and there is a handprint in the dust on the top of the second one where I put my own hand down on the seventeenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four.”

She said, “And there is not one mark on the top of mine except my own, and I have been in this room ninety days and I have watched that table from the near end and the far end and from underneath and that hand has never once been near a row.”

## What The Hand Writes And How It Knows

On the seventh she did the second half of it and she did it standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table, because it could not be done lying down.

She said, “A hand at a table cannot enter anybody into anything. There is nothing on that table to write with and a hand that is deciding what a thing is has to have something to write it down with, or it is not writing, it is keeping something in its head, and not one person in this room has been keeping anything in their head for four hundred years.”

She said, “And the figure on my row is in my hand. Three fingers and a thumb on a nib and the strokes not all the same width. That hand could not have made it. It could only have taken it and put it in a column exactly as it was, and that is not entering a thing, that is copying a thing that has already been entered somewhere else.”

She said, “And I chose it. At two posts on a road, on the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, at the first hour, in daylight, standing up, with nineteen words under it, out loud, before it was a line anywhere else.”

She said, “So the head of that row was chosen by the person the row is about, and it was chosen in a room four miles down a mountain before it was on a board, and the hand at that table wrote down what I said and nothing else, and the nothing else is the whole rule.”

## Who Keeps It

And on the eighth she worked out who keeps it, and that took her most of a day and it is the simplest thing she has come out with in ninety-one days and she was not expecting it.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the seventh hour of the eighth of that month:

“The table is for things that arrive. The row is for things that are about people. And the row is on the floor and the table is four feet above it, and nothing goes between them that I have ever seen.”

She said, “The table is twenty-two paces long and a row is a foot and two inches across, and a bundle goes on the table and a figure ends up in a column on the floor, and there is not one thing in this room that does either of those things and I have watched for ninety days.”

She said, “So the room does not keep the row. The room stands on it.”

She said that, and then she sat down on the floor at the near end of that table for a count of forty, because it is the plainest sentence she has come out with in ninety-one days and it took her two days and she was not ready for it.

## A Rule Four Hundred Years Old

On the ninth of that month she worked out the last of it and she worked it out out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, and it is the part of this that no mouth in this building is ever going to help her with.

She said, “That rule is old. The oldest of those four strips has gone soft at every edge and there is a grey skin of dust on the top of it with my handprint standing in it from the seventeenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four, and mine is two months old and the cuts in it still catch.”

She said, “The line cut in the stone of that wall behind that lamp is the oldest thing in this building that has a number attached to it and the number is four hundred years, and I cannot check that number against anything in this room, and I have never been able to check it, and I have had it in my head since before I came up this stair.”

She said, “And there is not one thing written in this building about why a row is entered the way it is. There is not one mark on any wall, any board, any row or any stone in that room that says so. I have been under that table four times and along that table four times and round that room four times and there is nothing.”

She said, “And a rule that has been kept exactly for that long has not got anybody left in the building who was in the room on the day it started. That is not a mystery. That is what four hundred years does to a rule. It outlasts the people who made it and it goes on working and not one living thing in the room knows what it is for.”

## Why Somebody Made That Rule

And then she said the last of it, and it is the reason the last thirty days happened, and she said it out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, in a room with a person at a table in it who has not looked up once in ninety-one days.

She said, “A row is entered by the person it is about. That means a hand at a table cannot put a person into a row. It means nobody in this building can decide what a person is, because the person says it, or it does not go in.”

She said, “That is why it was made. Somebody made it for the reason I need it and I do not know when and I do not know who and I am never going to find out, and the person at that table has stood in this room for four hundred years and has never once thought about it, and that is not cruelty and it is not kindness. It is a rule working.”

She said, “And nobody is ever going to say so to me, and I am not going to be able to ask, because the only way to ask a question in this building is to put it in a row, and I have not got a row, and the whole of what I have been doing for ninety-one days is getting one.”

## What It Costs Her To Know It

And then she did the sum of it out loud, standing, at the ninth hour, with the light at the far end of the room fourteen of her own hand below the top of that cage, and it is the last thing she said that day.

She said, “Two thousand three hundred and ninety-two less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and thirty-three days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and not one of them going back.”

She said, “And the figure on that strip under that table is four days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes out of date, and I am the only person in this building who could tell you that, and I have told not one person, because the only one in this building who could be told is the one who would have to carry the whole of it, and I am not going to put that on a hand that has never refused me anything in its life.”

She said, “And fifty-eight less fourteen is forty-four and forty-four divided by two is twenty-two and the twenty-second day from this one is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen, and that is the day the next forty things come up that stair, and on that day the lamp is going to be standing on that line again.”

She said, “And I have worked out what a row is and I have not said one word of it to anybody and I am going to say it out loud in this room in daylight on a day of my own choosing and a hand at that table is going to write it down, and that is the first thing in thirteen years I have done that has had a result at the end of it.”
