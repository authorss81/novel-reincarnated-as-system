# What Came Up The Stair

She waited three days for the first of the month beginning 2,324, and the waiting was the longest work she has ever done with nothing in her hands, and on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 2,294 she said out loud that it was twenty-three days since she came up this stair and that she was the only person in this building who knew that.

## Boots Before The Man

At the second hour of the first of the month beginning 2,324 she heard boots.

Not on the tower’s stair. Those are eight flights down behind a door a person the width of a door behind and you do not hear them from in here. These were on the second stair, on the turn, and they were going up, and they came down off the turn and onto the floor of the room at the far end out of the place in the wall behind the lamp’s shelf where the stair comes back to the level of this room.

She had been listening for that boot for twenty-three days.

## What He Carried

The man came into the light with an armful of boards under his left arm and a bundle of papers in a cloth under his right, and he was a man of about forty with his coat short for the mountain and a lamp nowhere.

The boards were the size of a slate and about the thickness of two fingers and they were pale wood with the grain running up them, and there were about forty of them, and he carried them with the ends up.

The bundle of papers was in a cloth with a string round it and it was a third of the size of the armful.

He did not look at her. He did not look at the door she came in by. He walked the length of the table, put the armful down on it in one movement without breaking his stride, put the bundle down beside it, and turned round and went back out of the place in the wall.

She said out loud to the empty room, standing, in the ordinary voice: “He has been on that road a year and he has not looked at one thing in this room, and he put forty boards and a bundle down in less time than it takes me to get up onto that table.”

And the boots went up the turn and were gone.

## Twenty-Three Days Of Waiting For A Date

She did not do nothing for twenty-three days. She wants that written down somewhere, because the whole of what came up that stair on the first of a month was a bundle, and she spent twenty-three days learning that a bundle is not going to arrive because a day has arrived.

She counted the days down. She counted them out loud at the seventh hour every day, standing at the near end of the table, because that is what she does with a thing that is going to happen and she has never been able to do it any other way.

On the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 2,294 there were twenty-three days to go and she said that figure out loud and it was the fourth number in a month and it was the only one in this building that changed.

On the second of the month beginning 2,294 there were twenty days.

On the twenty-eighth of that month there was one day to go, and she stood in the open door at the ninth hour and looked at a dark stair at the far end of a room with a lamp burning in a cage at the other end of it, and she had nothing to do with that night and she did not sleep it, and she counted it.

And on the morning of the first she got up off the floor at the first hour with the count of four hundred she had done in the night and she went and put her hand on the near edge of that table with her left hand and got a hand on it and stood there for a count of nine, and then she let it go and stood back four feet.

She said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, at the first hour of the first of a month beginning 2,324:

“I have been in this room twenty-three days for a thing that arrives once a month on the first, and the twenty-three days were not the waiting. The twenty-three days were me finding out that a first is not a date up here. It is a day something comes up a stair.”

He said, “You have said that before.”

She said, “I have never had it stand in front of me before. Down the line a first was a slate got taken out and turned over and written in and carried up a road. I worked that out at a frame in four days and I did not understand a word of it.”

## The Forty Minutes In More Detail

She kept count the whole of it and she kept it because she has never in her life let a thing happen in a room without a number attached to it.

Ten minutes for forty boards at four to a minute. She counted every one of them out loud from the near end of the table and she did not get one of them wrong, and the hand did not stop and did not go back and did not hurry once in ten minutes.

Then the string off the bundle and the cloth off it, and she could not see what was in the bundle from four feet off in the dark half of that room, and what she could see was leaves going onto the table one at a time and coming off again one at a time, and a head coming down over them the way it came down over the boards.

And the thirty minutes that took for the bundle was three times what the forty boards took, and she said out loud at the twenty-ninth minute, to the room:

“There are three times as many leaves in that bundle as there were boards on that table, and the hand went along all of them at the same rate, and the whole of it took forty minutes, and that is the whole of the work of this room for a month.”


## Four To The Minute

The one at the table went along them at four to a minute and did not stop.

It was a hand that went onto the first board and across it and a hand that came back and a hand that went onto the second board and across it, and the head came down over it, and then the next one, and it was the same every time and she counted forty of them out loud standing at the near end of that table at four a minute and she did not get one of them wrong.

Forty boards at four to a minute is ten minutes of looking.

Then the bundle, and the string came off, and the cloth, and there were more leaves in that bundle than there had been boards on the table, and the rest of it took thirty minutes.

Forty minutes. The whole of it, the armful and the bundle and the reading of them, took forty minutes, and it was done before she had understood that it had started.

## A Hand On The Table And Not On It

She got a hand on the near edge of that table before they went on it, at the first hour of the first of the month beginning 2,324, with her left hand and her weight on the arm that was closed that day.

She had it for a count of nine.

And she let it go and stood back four feet off the end of it, and she did not put a hand on that table once in forty minutes.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, to a room with a lamp in it:

“I can reach that table and I am not going to touch it while it has got forty things on it in front of the person who is standing at it, and that is not a rule anybody has given me. That is a thing I decided in a count of nine this morning because I could not think of one reason why it was mine to do.”

She said, “And I have been able to get a hand on that table since the twenty-third and there has been nothing on it to get it for, and I have never in my life had something to put on a table and this is the first time there has been something on one and I cannot reach it.”

## Four Boards That Are The Same

And then she spent the last nineteen minutes of the forty doing the only thing she could do, which was to look at forty boards from four feet off the end of a table in the dark half of a room and try to find one of them.

Every one of them is the same size. Every one of them is the same pale wood. Every one of them is about the thickness of two fingers and about the length of her forearm and they are all lying the same way up with the grain running the same way, and forty boards laid in a row by a man who has been on that road a year do not have anything about them that says which one was written on by a person and which one was not.

The one that is hers went up this stair at the second hour of the first of this month a month ago in a bundle with about forty other boards and it has nineteen words on it and a date and a place, and they are true, and from four feet off the end of this table it is the same board as the other thirty-nine.

She said, out loud, standing, at the seventh hour of the first of the month beginning 2,324:

“I have spent nine years making myself the only person in every room who could do a sum, and I got a line into a room four miles above a mountain and it went up among forty boards and it is the same board as the other thirty-nine, and there is not one thing on it that says a woman wrote it.”

She said, “And I cannot tell which one it is from here, and I am not going to be able to tell which one it is tomorrow either, and that is not a thing somebody has done to me. That is a thing I bought.”

## Where They Went

And then, at the end of the forty minutes, the boards came off that table.

They came off one at a time and went down onto the floor on the far side and were put under the long table, flat, in a stack, in the dark under it, by a person who did not have to crouch to do it and who did not make a sound doing it.

The last board went under at the seventh hour and the stack of them was lying in the dark along the left-hand wall under a table four feet off the floor, and she was standing four feet off the end of that table with an open arm and a lamp burning at the other end of the room and no light at all under it.
