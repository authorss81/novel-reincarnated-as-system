# The Place At The Far Side

On the fifth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four she stood at the near end of that table for a whole day with nothing on it, and in the room she was in, away from her hearing, there is a second place at that table that has his shape on it, and he does not get into it and he does not say no to it.

## What She Can Hear From The Floor

She worked out why she had never been able to stand still where a person stands still, and it took her most of that day and she did it with her body and not with a figure.

On the fifth of that month she stood in the middle of the floor of that room, which is the middle of nothing, and she put her hands down flat at her sides and she held still the way the person at that table holds still, which she had watched for a hundred and forty-seven days and had never once copied.

And she could not do it for a count of eleven.

Her breath went off nine on the fourth count and she got it back and it went off again. Her weight went onto the outside of one foot and she did not put it back. And she understood while she was standing there that the reason is not that the room would look at her. The room does not look at her. The reason is that being looked at is what holds a person still, and there is not one thing in this building that is looking at her, and there has not been since she came into it.

She said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table:

“There is not one thing in this room that has ever looked at me, and I have spent a hundred and forty-seven days trying to be measured by an instrument that would have to do it.”

And she said, “And the instrument does not even do that. It stands at a table for four hundred years and does not look at anything that has not been put in front of it, and I have not been able to work out what gets put in front of it except a board.”

And she said the next part out loud and it was about a boy on a floor four miles down that mountain.

She said, “There is a boy of sixteen who has been keeping a lamp in a cage on the fourth landing going since the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four with nothing to put in it, and I have never once been able to ask him what he thinks a lamp is for, because he would have to come up eight hundred and eighty steps to be asked and he would come up and ask me and I would not have an answer.”

She said, “And he cannot be in this room. And I cannot go down there. And there is not one thing in nineteen years that has ever let me ask a question and get an answer out of the asking.”

And she stood there for a count of a hundred and forty before she said the last of it, and she said it to herself and not out loud and not to a person, and it is the first thing she has said to herself in a hundred and forty-eight days.

She said, “And there is a stretch of the far side of that table the length of my own forearm, paler than the rest of the wood, with a shape worn into it that no tool made, and nothing in front of it and nothing on it, and I found it on the ninth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and I have not touched it and I have not said one word about it out loud in a hundred and forty-eight days.”

She said, “And I do not know what it is for and I am not going to find out by standing next to it.”

## Inside The Thing That Keeps Her

There is no floor in this place and there is nothing to put a hand on, and he checks both of those before he lets anything arrive, because he has been in one of these eight times and he has never once been in a hurry.

This time there is a room, and it is a real one, and he is in it.

The light is in a cage at the far end of a long table. There is no oil in it and nothing behind it and the wall behind the light is the wall, and the flame does not lean when anything moves past it in this room with a door standing open. There is a short line cut into the stone of that wall, a hand’s breadth to the left of the cage, and the light stops on it and does not go under it.

A person is standing at the near edge of that table with both hands on it. They are thinner than a person ought to be and they are smaller than the table they have been standing at, and they are not looking at anything.

And he is smaller than the table is tall.

He has not got a weight. The floor of that room does not take anything off him and does not know he is standing on it, the same way a floor does not know a thing that is being read.

## Two Places At One Table

There are two places at that table.

He has found that out and it took him about a count of a hundred and it is not a hard thing to find. The near side is where the person stands. The far side has nothing on it for twenty-two paces and then, where the light is strongest, there is a stretch of it that is paler than the rest of the wood and smoother, about the length of a forearm, and there is a shape worn into it that no tool made.

There is no chair in that room and there have never been one. There is nothing in front of that stretch of wood. Nothing has ever been set on it.

He stands in front of it.

He knows what it is. He does not have to be told and he does not have to work it out and that is the thing about it. A shape worn into wood by something that has been put in the same place a great many times over a very long time, and the shape is his shape, and the wood has taken it down the way wood takes a hand.

It is the right size for him.

It is addressed.

And nobody put him in it. He has been able to go and look at every part of that room in nine years of being in rooms and there is no instruction anywhere in it that says he is to come and stand here. There is a table and a lamp and a cut in a wall and a person at the near edge of the wood and a piece of the far edge that fits him, and the last of those is the only thing in the room that has ever been made for anybody.

## What A Shape Worn Into Wood Is

He goes round it once without touching it, and the shape in the wood is not the shape of a hand.

That is the thing he did not expect. He had come in with a hand in his head because a hand is what he has ever been, and there is no hand in that wood. There is a length, and a depth, and a curve at the near end of it about the size of a shoulder, and the grain of the pale wood has been pressed flat in a shape that is not any part of him that he can name.

He says, “That is not a hand.”

He says, “That is what a person leaves in a place when the person is not standing in it. And somebody has stood here enough times to press it into the wood, and I do not know how many times, and it is the oldest thing in this building by a very long way and I am not going to guess at a figure.”

He says, “And it is mine. I know it is mine the way I know my own count is mine. Not because anything told me. Because nothing else in this room is that shape.”

He says, “And there is no way on the face of this room for me to find out who stood here, or how often, or what they were doing while they did it, and there is no version of being in this place in which that matters as much as the other thing, which is that it was made for somebody and it was not made for the person standing at the near edge of it.”

## What He Does About It

He does not get into it.

There is no chair. He could not have sat in it if there had been one, and that is not the reason, and he does not spend the count of a hundred working out what the reason is.

He looks at it for a long time. He puts his hand nowhere near it. He does not put his hand on the wood and he does not find out what the wood would do.

And he does not say no to it.

He has had a thing offered to him in a room that was not a room in nineteen years, and it was six terms and a date and a hand to write with, and he asked for it and he got it and it cost her hearing it first, and he has never once been able to work out whether that was a good trade. And this is not the same kind of thing. This is a piece of furniture.

He says, in the ordinary voice, standing in that room, in the light off a lamp in a cage:

“I am not going to say yes to a piece of furniture.”

He says, “I am also not going to say no to it, because not one instruction in this room put me in it and there is not one person in this room I would be able to say no to, and I have spent nineteen years finding out what it costs to be the only thing in a room that anybody has noticed.”

He says, “There is nobody else in this room. There is not a woman in it and there is not a boy in it and there has not been one in four hundred years.”

He says, “And there was a woman standing in it earlier today, four feet off the near end of that table, with her arm open across her knees, for most of a day. She cannot hear any part of this. She does not know I am here and she is not going to know today or tomorrow.”

He says, “And I have just spent a count of a hundred in a room with one other thing in it that has not looked at me either.”

He says, “She stood at a table for four days this month trying to be measured by it. I am going to be able to hear about that from a long way off, when somebody reads this room. And the thing she could not get out of it is exactly the thing I have just got out of it.”

He says, “It does not look at you. That is all of it. It does not look at you and it is four hundred years old and it has never once had to, because in four hundred years not one thing has ever been put in front of it that it did not have to look at.”

He does not get into it.

And the room goes on exactly as it was going on before, in the way that rooms do, with a lamp in a cage at the far end of a long table and a person standing at the near edge of it with both hands on it and the light two of her own hand below the top of that cage and the rest of it going on the way it goes on.
