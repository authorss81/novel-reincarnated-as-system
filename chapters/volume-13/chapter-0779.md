# Nine Hundred And Four In The Dark

She went down nine hundred and four steps in the dark with nothing in either hand on the sixth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, and she came back up them, and she found on her way up that the bundle of papers under the far end of that table was against a different leg of it than the one she had put it against.

## The Second Morning Of The Dark

She got up at the fifth hour on the second of that month and the room was the same as it had been at the fourth hour the night before, which is to say that there was nothing to tell apart.

She stood in the middle of the floor of that room in the dark for a count of ninety before she did anything, because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists, and what she said was short.

She said, out loud, in the dark, in the ordinary voice:

“The light came down to that cut at the fourth hour of the first of this month and went under it and it has not come back and it is not going to.”

She said, “And I did that.”

She said, “And there has been no light in this building that was not in my hands for nine years until yesterday and now there is none anywhere in it.”

And she said the sums out loud in the dark at the ninth hour, standing, at the near end of that table, because she cannot let a day go past without putting its figures somewhere other than in her head.

She said, “Two thousand five hundred and thirty-five less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is nine hundred and seventy-six days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and not one of them going back and one of them gone into that lamp and one of them that light.”

She said, “And two thousand five hundred and thirty-five less two thousand three hundred and one is two hundred and thirty-four days in this room, and two thousand five hundred and thirty-five less two thousand two hundred and ninety-four is two hundred and forty-one days that my right arm has been open, and there is not one instrument in the world that is going to tell me that to my face.”

## The Bundle Against A Different Leg

She had her arm into that bundle of papers three times in this building and never pulled it. It is under the far end of that long table and it is corded round twice and not knotted, and she had felt the cord on it in the dark on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four and again in the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and again in the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four.

On the second of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four she got down on her hands and knees and went along the floor of that room on her hands and knees with her hand on the leg of the table until she found it, and the cord was under the second leg from the near end.

On the morning of the third she went and found it again with her hand because she could not see it.

It was under the third leg from the near end.

She said out loud, on the flat of her back on the stone floor of that room in the dark with her arm open beside her and her hand on the cord:

“Something in this building has been paid for and I did not feel it go.”

And she lay there for a count of ninety and said the rest of it out loud on the flat of her back on that stone.

She said, “And I have no instrument. I cannot tell you what it was or whose it was or what it was worth, and I am not going to stand in the dark in a room where nothing can be seen and pretend that a thing I cannot explain is a thing I understand.”

She said, “And it came off the ground this room stands on and not out of anything in my hands, and I have said out loud in this building before today that I am the only thing in it that can pay for anything, and that is not true and I do not know when it stopped being true.”

And she got herself up off the floor of that room and did not touch the bundle again.

## Nine Hundred And Four With Nothing In My Hands

She went down that stair at the second hour of the sixth of that month with nothing in either hand.

There is no rail on it and there is no light on it and it goes up out of the far side of that room and comes back down into the floor of the same room out of a place in the wall behind the lamp’s shelf. It is nine hundred and four and she has it. She counted it going down on the twenty-third of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen and she counted it going up on the third of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four and the figure is the same going up as it came down.

The four steps she could not hold are the first four out of the room and she found that out on the seventeenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen.

She said them out loud as she went, one at a time, the way she has said every number she has ever been afraid of losing, and she had the wall at every hundred and it was four inches off and she could not get anything off it.

Sixty-four turns. Nine runs. A hundred and forty in the long one.

She was on the floor of that room going up at the fifth hour of the seventh of that month with nothing in either hand and she came up the last four with her hand on the stone and not on anything else.

And she stood in the middle of the floor of that room in the dark and counted that second stair and it is nine hundred and four and it is not the same stair as the one in the tower and the two of them are not added together anywhere and have never been added together anywhere in four hundred years.

## The Cage On The Fourth Landing

On the seventh of that month she went down the low door and took the first four hundred and forty with her left hand on the rail, and she did not go down the second four, and she did not go down the second stair either.

There is a lamp in a cage on that fourth landing and it has been out since the third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and there is a day and a half of oil in a stone jar under a bench on that floor that ran out on or about the day two thousand two hundred and ninety-six.

She put her hand into that cage and the iron was cold.

And there is a copying floor thirty feet from a drawer with a lock on it, and shelves of paper going away from a person into the dark further than a hand can reach, and she stood in front of that drawer and put her hand flat on the grey in the wood in the front of it where a hand goes, and she did not open it, and she had said out loud on the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four that she was not going to and she gave a reason that day.

And she said out loud, standing in the dark on that landing at the ninth hour:

“There is a lamp in a cage on this landing that has been out for two hundred and forty-three days and there is a lamp in a cage at the top of that tower that has been out for two days, and I put one of them out.”

And she said, “And there is a boy of sixteen who keeps one of these going and there is no oil in this building and there has not been for nine years and he has been getting it from somewhere four miles down that mountain that I have not gone down to and am not going to.”

## Coming Back Up Into It

And she went back up the first four hundred and forty with her left hand on the rail and she came out of the low door into that room at the seventh hour of the seventh of that month and it was dark and it was the same dark it had been at the fourth hour of the day before.

She stood four feet off the near end of that long table in the dark and she put her left hand flat on the near edge of it, which she can reach, and she could not see her own hand on it.

She said out loud, standing, in the dark, at the near end of that table:

“There is a strip of pale wood under this table a foot long and two inches across and there is a price at the head of it and a five in the column in my own hand and twelve pairs of cuts under that are empty.”

She said, “And I know every one of those cuts by where it is under my fingers and there is not one instrument in the world that would give it back to me, and there is not one instrument in the world that could take a figure out of it, and that is not a thing anybody did to me. I did that on the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four with a nib and I did not do it to be rescued out of it.”

And then she said the last of that day out loud in the dark and it was short and she said it standing up.

She said, “There are three days left before the seventh of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four and I am going to be standing in this room when it comes, and I have not got one word for him, and I said out loud on the sixth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four that I was not going down to tell him what a row is and I have not taken it back.”

She said, “And I am not going to.”
