# What The Figure Is For

She worked out on the tenth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four what the figure she is lighter by is for, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that long table, and the answer took her most of the day and it is one sentence long and she did not like it.

## The Sums On The Tenth

She did them at the ninth hour on the tenth because she cannot let a day go past without putting its figures somewhere other than in her head, and she stood at the near end of that table with her right arm open in front of her and her left hand on the edge of the wood.

She said, “Two thousand five hundred and thirteen less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is nine hundred and fifty-four days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and not one of them going back and one of them gone into that lamp and not coming back.”

She said, “And the lamp is sixteen of my own hand below the top of that cage and it goes down two a day, and fifty-eight divided by two is twenty-nine, and the twenty-ninth day from the second of this month is the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, and it is twenty-one days from this one.”

She said, “And the figure at the head of my row is ten days, twenty-two hours and forty-eight minutes out of date and it gets more out of date every hour, and the figure in the second pair of cuts down under it went out of date on the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four and there is not one instrument in the world that puts either of them right.”

And then she did the one she had been putting off for four days, which was the arithmetic of the offer, and it took her most of the morning.

She said, “The amendment would go in the second pair of cuts down in that column, over the five, and it would go in at the second hour of the second of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four if I put a day into that cage myself, and the light comes down to that cut at the fourth hour of the first of that month.”

She said, “And between the fourth hour of that first and the second hour of that second there is a window of about ten hours in which that room would have light in it and no decision in it and that is the whole of the time there is.”

She said, “And I have twenty-one days to decide it and I have not done a single sum about it until this morning, which is the first thing in two hundred and twelve days I have let sit in front of me without working out.”

## What Each Of The Five Is For

She went through it the way she goes through everything, one at a time, out loud, standing, in daylight, and she asked of each one what it is for, and she had been asking that question since she was nine years old and she had never once asked it of her own things.

She said, “Four fingers and a thumb. What is that for.”

She said, “It writes. It holds a nib. It goes on a table and takes twenty-two paces of it on its own and it cannot be given a thing and it cannot have a thing put in it by anybody but me.”

She said, “That is what it is for and I have known that since I could hold anything.”

She said, “Two coppers I cannot count. What are those for.”

And she stood there for a count of ninety before she answered her own question, and the answer came out flat and it is the plainest thing she has said in that room in two hundred and twelve days.

She said, “Nothing. They are for nothing. I cannot count them and I cannot spend them and I cannot give them away and they have not changed in two hundred and twelve days and there is not one hour in two hundred and twelve days that I have thought about either of them.”

She said, “And I have been carrying them since I was nine years old and I have never once asked what they were for and that is the only one of the five I can answer in one sentence without stopping.”

## The Only Thing She Has Not Asked

She said, “Five blank leaves against my fourth rib. What are those for.”

She said, “They are for writing and there is a key to the drawer they are in at the bottom of that stair in a bag with a dish and a lump of clay and a ring the width of a finger, and I have been down to that bag four times and not gone down for it, and I said out loud on the nineteenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four that I was not going to open it and I gave a reason and the reason holds.”

She said, “And they are blank and they are going to be blank and that is not a thing that is for nothing. That is a thing that is for something I have not got to yet and I have not asked and I am not going to ask this month.”

And then she got to the two that are left and she did them in the wrong order on purpose, because she wanted to get the harder one done before the one that made it bear on anything.

She said, “Six terms. What are they for.”

And she stood at the near end of that table with her hand on the edge of it and did not answer it for a count of a hundred and forty, and the light came down that wall two of her own hand a day the whole time and did not lean in a draught.

And what she said at the end of that count was this, and she said it standing up and she said it in the ordinary voice:

“They are so that there is one thing in my life that I say out loud before it happens.”

She said, “And I have not said them out loud in this room since the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, which is two hundred and twelve days ago, and I have kept every one of them every day of that in this room without saying them, and I did not know on this morning until this morning that there is a difference.”

## What The Number Is For

And then she did the last one, and she did it out loud, standing, in daylight, at the ninth hour of the tenth of that month, and it is the sentence the whole day came to.

She said, “Nine hundred and fifty-four days. What is that for.”

She said, “It is for light.”

And then she stopped, because she had expected that to be the end of the sentence and it was not the end of the sentence at all, and she had to say the rest of it and it took a count of ninety.

She said, “Light in this room costs a day. I worked that out on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four at a hand in a cage and I have been carrying it since. And I am the only thing in this building that can pay it.”

She said, “And a figure that goes down is the only thing in the world that can pay for light, and that is not a fact about this room. That is a fact about everything that has ever had a light in it.”

She said, “A room is a place where a figure goes down. I worked that out on a barrow ground when I was nine years old with a bar in my hands and a man counting and I did not know then that it was a working out. I have been in rooms for nineteen years and none of them had one, and I could not be given a thing and I could not be measured and I could not be counted where I stood, and I did not know any of that was because of the figure.”

She said, “And I have been in this room two hundred and twelve days and there is a figure going down in it and it is going down at one hour and twelve minutes a day and it came out of me, and that is the whole reason a room upstairs can do anything about me at all.”

And she said the last of it standing, in daylight, with her hand on the edge of that table, and she said it without stopping in it.

She said, “And I did not put it there. I was nine. And I have been escaping it since I was nine, and on the tenth of this month I have worked out that it is the only thing I have ever had that anybody could spend, and I have been carrying it up a mountain in my own chest for nineteen years.”

She said, “And there is a room that has just told me it will put a name at the head of my column and take the five out of it and cost me one day, and I am not proud and I am not frightened and I do not know what I am going to do.”

She said, “And before I do anything I am going to say the six terms out loud in this room, all six of them, standing up, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, one at a time, and I have not said the sixth one out loud in this building in two hundred and twelve days.”
