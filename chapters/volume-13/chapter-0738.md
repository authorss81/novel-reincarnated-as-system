# Four Strips Under A Table

She went under that table on the sixteenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and found three more rows, and a boy of sixteen sat in the doorway and could not follow her, and that was the first thing she was better at than him in sixty-eight days.

## Going Under It With Him At The Door

The space under that table is four feet high at the near end and there is a cross-beam across it about a third of the way down, and the stone under it has not been swept in a long time and has a grey skin of dust on it that takes a handprint.

She has been under there four times in sixty-eight days, three of them before the first of this month. She goes in on her hands and knees with her right arm open ahead of her because she cannot get it back once it is in, and she comes out backwards, and it takes her a count of forty each way.

He could not get under it. He got down on one knee at the near end and put his head in and his shoulders stopped, and he got back out again without saying anything about it.

She said, out loud, from under the table, in the ordinary voice:

“That is the one thing in this building I am better at than you at, and it is because I am small, and I have never once been able to use it for anything.”

## Three More Strips

She had her own strip in her right hand, and she reached further in with her left along the stone, and about two feet past it her fingers went onto wood.

There was another one.

She worked her way out with it, and there was another one two feet past that, and there was another one two feet past that, and all three were the same size and the same shape as hers.

A foot long and two inches across, flat on the stone, face up, the grain running up them the length of them, the edges square and not broken anywhere.

She said, out loud, sitting on the floor of that room with three pieces of wood across her knees and a boy of sixteen sitting against the doorframe:

“There are four.”

He said, “Four of what.”

She said, “Rows. There are four rows under that table and I have been lying on the floor beside one of them for sixty-eight days.”

## The Column Is Not A List Of Things

She read her own first, because she can read her own with the flat of her finger in the dark, and she has read it four times and it has never changed.

A head at one end, and a column, and one thing in the column.

Then she put the three old ones out on the stone in a row in front of her, face up, in order, and she read them the way she reads her own, and she could not read a single figure on any of them, and after four days she worked out why and said it out loud.

She said, “A figure is made out of strokes and a stroke has a beginning and an end and a turn, and on these three there is not one stroke with a turn in it anywhere.”

She said, “And the width of the cut is the same on every mark in the column on all three of them, and on mine it is not, because I write one figure and the strokes in it are not all the same width, because I only have three fingers and a thumb and I hold the nib with three of them.”

She said, “So the column on these is a column of figures and not a list of things, because a list of things has a different shape on every line of it.”

## What She Could Not Date

The cuts on her own strip were sharp. She ran the flat of her finger along the edge of the head of it four times on the seventeenth and they caught, and she felt it.

The cuts on the other three had not caught anything for a long time. She put her finger on the end of each one of them and the end of it was not an edge any more. The wood had gone round. The dust on the tops of them had the same grey skin as the floor and it had been there a long enough time to have the print of her own hand in it when she lifted it off on the eighteenth.

She said that out loud at the ninth hour of the eighteenth, sitting on the floor with her back against the near leg of that table:

“I can tell you that those three are a great deal older than mine. Mine is two months old and the cuts in it are sharp and I can feel the difference between that and not being able to feel an edge anywhere on the other three.”

She said, “And I have got no instrument for how much older, and I am not going to make one up, and there is not one thing in this room with a date on it from before the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four.”

He said, “How old is yours, then.”

She said, “The second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four. It was not there on the first and it was there on the second, and that is a day I watched with my own eyes at four feet off that table, and it is the only date in this room I have ever been able to stand next to.”

## Four Rows Under One Table

And then she did the sum, on the nineteenth, at the seventh hour, with all four of them laid out on the stone in front of her in the order she found them in.

She said, “There are four rows under this table and every one of them is a foot long and two inches across and every one of them has a head at one end that is a figure, and every one of them has a column, and not one thing in this building is the size of a foot and two inches across except one person’s saying.”

He said, “What is the table for, then.”

She said, “The table is twenty-two paces long and four feet off the floor and it is made for arms longer than mine, and the rows are on the floor and they are face up, and the one at that table stands over them every day of its life and has both hands on the near edge of the table and not one hand has ever been on a row.”

She said, “And that is what a row is. It is a place a thing is decided about.”

## What He Could Not Do With His Hands

She tried to teach him to read one by touch on the eighteenth and it took a count of ninety and it did not work.

She gave him her own strip and put his finger on the head of it and moved it down the column.

She said, “That is a figure. That is the first mark and that is the second mark and that is what the two of them are.”

He said, “That is wood.”

She said, “Hold it the way I hold it. Flat. Edge on. And go slow, because a figure is not a shape, it is where the marks stop.”

He gave it back after a count of sixty and said, “I cannot do that. I have copied out a book of figures off a board in this building since I was fourteen and I have never once had to do that and I do not believe I am going to start tonight.”

She said, “That is the only thing I can do that you cannot, and I have been the only person who could do it in every room I have ever been in, and it has never once been worth anything to anybody.”

## What She Wanted Out Of Them

What she wanted, out loud, standing, in daylight, on the nineteenth at the sixth hour, was a name.

She said it before she had decided to say it and she let it stand where she had put it.

She said, “I want to know whether there is a name on one of those three. I have been in this room seventy-one days and there is not one name in it anywhere, not on a board, not on a row, not on anything, and I did not know that was remarkable until I had been here seventy-one days.”

She said, “And if there is one, then this room has decided about a person before and it did not need the person in the room to do it, and I want that to be true more than I want anything in this building.”

He said, “Why.”

She said, “Because if there is not one, then a thing gets into a row in this room without anybody standing in the room saying it, and I have got a figure in one of them that I wrote myself and I would like to know what else is in here that no one said.”

## The One She Could Not Say Out Loud

And then she said the part of it that she had been holding since the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four, and she said it out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, with a boy of sixteen in the room, because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

She said, “Four rows under one table and three of them have gone soft at the edges and one of them is mine, and it is two months old, and the other three are the only other rows in this room and they are the only three things in this building with my figure anywhere near them that are not forty boards going along a table at four to a minute.”

She said, “And the three old ones have nothing on them that I can read, and I am not going to guess at them, and I have been in this room seventy-one days and the only figure in this building that is in my own hand is the one I brought up that stair myself on the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four.”

She said, “And I have not worked out yet how one gets in.”

He said, “Gets in what.”

She said, “In one of those. In a row. I have got four of them under a table and not one word on any of them that came out of this room, and the figure on mine got in here on a board with forty others, and I do not know who put it in the row and I do not know what puts a thing in one.”

The light at the far end of the room was thirty-four of her own hand below the top of that cage and she said the number out loud, and then she said the other number, standing there with four strips of wood on the stone:

“Two thousand three hundred and seventy-two less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and thirteen days, and the figure under that table is three days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes out of date, and it is out of date every day and there is not one instrument in this building that could tell me so.”
