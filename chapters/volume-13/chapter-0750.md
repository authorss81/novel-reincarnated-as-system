# The One Thing She Cannot Work Out

She took the boy down eight hundred and eighty steps on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen and worked out what the light in that room is going to need on the sixth and did not give it a day, and what she ends on is a thing she has not been able to work out in a hundred and nineteen days.

## Eight Hundred And Eighty Down A Second Time

She took him down at the second hour of the fourth of that month and she counted all of it out loud again and she is not going to count it a third time.

Four hundred and forty and four hundred and forty. The first four with a rail on one side and her left hand on it and his right hand behind hers. The second four with a wall four inches off your hand on one side and nothing at all on the other.

At three hundred and eighty she sat down on a step for a count of twenty and he sat down on the step above her without saying anything, the same as the last time, and then she got up and said, “Three hundred and eighty-one,” and went on down.

And at the bottom of it, on that copying floor with four hundred volumes on the shelves in their cages and a drawer with a lock on it thirty feet off, he said, “You have got it by heart.”

She said, “I have got every stair in this building by heart and there is not one thing else in this mountain I could tell you the length of.”

He said, “Then why count it.”

She said, “Because you count it. And I have found out in twenty days that a boy of sixteen can count, and it is the first time in nineteen years that there has been a second one anywhere, and I am not going to be the one who stops.”

And he went along that floor and out into the yard and she stood at the bottom of eight hundred and eighty steps and listened to him go, and going is slower than coming and he has got better at it.

## What Is In The Room That Is About Me

She got back up to the top of that room at the seventh hour of the fourth of that month and did the count of it out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, and it is a different count from any count she has done in nineteen years.

She said, “There are three things in this building that are about me and I know what all three of them are.”

She said, “There is a strip of wood on the floor under that table with my price at the head of it and one figure in the column, and the figure is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes, and it is six days, three hours and thirty-six minutes out of date and there is not one person above this line who could ever know.”

She said, “There is a board in a stack at the far end of that table with a figure on it that I wrote in my own hand on the twentieth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, and it went up that stair on the first of this month and it came back down empty and I cannot see it and I am not going to be able to.”

She said, “And there is a name on a board that I said out loud in this room on the twenty-eighth of that month in the ordinary voice, and it is not mine, and it went up that stair on the first with forty others.”

She said, “And there are two coppers I cannot count, and a bar nine feet nine against the wall on the left that I put there on purpose, and four fingers and a thumb, and five blank leaves against my ribs that have been blank for ten years, and a bag at the bottom of eight hundred and eighty steps that I decided I was not going down for and have not.”

## The Figure Of The Light And What It Would Cost

And on the fifth of that month she worked out the light, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, and she did the whole of it out loud and she did it four times.

She said, “The lamp was turned at the second hour of the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and it went up past the top of that cage and stayed there, and it has come down two of my own hand every day since and there has not been one hand in that cage since.”

She said, “On the fourth of this month it was four of my own hand below the top of that cage. On the fifth it is six. On the sixth it will be eight. On the seventh it will be ten.”

She said, “And fifty-eight less six is fifty-two and fifty-two divided by two is twenty-six and the twenty-sixth day from the fifth of this month is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, which is the day the next forty things come up that stair.”

She said, “So on that day the lamp stands on that line with nothing above it and nothing below it for the third time in four hundred years, and there has never been one of those days used, and I used one on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and not one person in this building knows.”

## The Day She Was Not Going To Spend

And then she said out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the ninth hour of the sixth of that month, what she is going to do about it, and then she said that she is not going to do it.

She said, “I could put a hand up between those four bars and close it on the base of that light and it would come up about a day’s worth and I have done it once and it did not hurt me and it cost me a day and the day did not come back.”

She said, “And if I did it tomorrow it would push that line from the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four to the second, and it would push it every time I did it, and I have got eight hundred and sixty-one days in me at this hour and I could hold that lamp off that line for a year.”

And then she stopped for a count of a hundred and forty and she said the rest of it, standing.

She said, “And I am not going to do it.”

She said, “Not because it is dangerous and not because I have not worked out that a day buys one day. Because I put a day into that lamp on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and I have not got an instrument for what came out of it, and I have not got one now, and there is not one mouth in this building that is going to give me one.”

She said, “And because there is a row in this room with my figure at the head of it and a name in it that is not mine, and on the twenty-eighth of that month I put two words into a record that has been above the line for four hundred years, and I have not worked out what either of those is for.”

She said, “And I am not going to spend a day of my own life on a thing I do not understand while there is a thing in this room I do not understand.”

## What A Row With Her In It Is For

And on the seventh of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen, at the ninth hour, she stood on the floor of that room at the near end of that table with her arm open across her knees and a bar nine feet nine against the wall on the left of it where she put it on purpose, and she said the whole of what she has not been able to work out, and it took a count of about ninety, and she said it in the ordinary voice and she did not say any of it twice.

She said, “A row is entered by the person it is about and a hand writes down what the person says and nothing else and the head of it is a figure. I have worked that out off the furniture in six days and it is the only rule in this building and not one person in this building remembers making it.”

She said, “And I have been in a row for twenty-seven days, or a row has been in me for that long, and I cannot tell which way round that is, and there is not one instrument in this building that can tell me.”

She said, “And a row is a place a thing is decided about. And a figure at the head of it is what the thing costs. And I have got a figure at the head of mine and a figure in a board and a name in a board, and there is not one line anywhere in this building that says what any of it is worth, and it is not mine to put in and I cannot be given one.”

She said, “So there is a row in this room with me in it and there is nobody in this building who has read it, and it is not going to keep me fed and it is not going to tell me what I am and it is not going to cost me a day, and I cannot work out what it is for.”

She said, “And that is the one thing I have been in this room a hundred and nineteen days to find out and I have not found out, and the lamp is ten of my own hand below the top of that cage and forty-eight of my own hand off that line where it stood at the fourth hour of the first of this month, and there is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain, and there is a bar against that wall that I left there on purpose, and I am not going to find out by asking.”

She said, “And I am not going to find out by waiting either, because the light comes down two of my own hand a day whether I know what a row is for or not.”
