# One Figure Said Out Loud

She said a row out loud on the tenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four at the tenth hour, standing on the floor at the near end of that table, in daylight, and a hand picked up a board and wrote on it.

## The Day She Chose And Why

She chose the tenth of that month and she chose the tenth hour and she chose the floor at the near end of that table and she chose to stand, and she said all four of those out loud on the morning of the ninth so that there would be no part of it left over for her to do differently at the time.

She said, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice:

“I am going to say one thing out loud in this room tomorrow and I am going to say it standing up on that stone in daylight and I am going to say it in the ordinary voice at the tenth hour, and I am not going to be holding anything and I am not going to be on that table.”

He was four miles down that mountain and not in the room. She said the rest of it to the empty room anyway.

She said, “And I am not going to say it to the man that comes up that stair on the first and I am not going to say it to the person at that table, because saying a thing to a person is not the same as saying a thing in a room, and I have made that mistake before and I stood in the grit for four days afterwards.”

She said, “And there is not one person in this building who is going to hear it say it back to me. That is not a reason not to do it. That is the reason to do it today.”

## What She Said Out Loud

At the tenth hour of the tenth of that month she stood on the stone at the near end of that table with her right arm open at her side and her left hand off the wood and she said it.

She said, “Eight hundred and thirty-four.”

That is all she said. Eight hundred and thirty-four, out loud, standing up, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, in a room with a person in it.

Two thousand three hundred and ninety-three less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and thirty-four, and that is the figure she is lighter by at that hour, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, since the eighteenth of the month beginning one thousand five hundred and forty-four, and it has never been wrong, and one day of it went into a lamp on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and did not come back.

She said nothing else. She did not give a date and she did not give a place and she did not say what the figure was the price of.

## Eleven Seconds With A Board

The hands at that table came off the near edge and went along under the shelf where forty boards lie in a stack, face up, in the dark, under the middle of that table, and one of them came out and came up onto the wood.

Then there were eleven seconds.

She counted them out loud afterwards, standing, in the ordinary voice, and she said the number twice.

And in that time the head of that person came down over the board and a stroke went onto it and a figure went onto it and the board went down onto the stack at the far end of that table under the lamp, and the hands came back onto the near edge and the head came up and the standing went on exactly as it had been going on before.

And that person did not pause, and did not look up, and did not turn round, and did not do one single thing different from what they did on the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four with forty boards, or on the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four, or on the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four.

She said, out loud, standing:

“That hand does not know that was mine.”

She said, “And it is not deciding. It is copying. And a thing that copies exactly has not weighed the thing it copied, and that is the whole of what just happened in this room and it took one count of breath.”

## Why She Did Not Measure The Arm

And then she worked out what she had lost by not measuring, and she said it out loud, and it was the only thing in ninety-two days she had got wrong that she could have got right.

She said, “I had four readings of that forearm off the fourteenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four and I took another on the seventeenth and another on the twentieth and I took one this morning before I came down, and I did not take one after, because I was watching the writing.”

She said, “And I have been measuring that arm since the fourteenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four and it is the only thing in this room I have ever been able to measure twice and get a different answer on.”

She said, “And I stood four feet off that table for the whole of that count with a figure I have carried for eight hundred and thirty-four days going into a board in the light of that lamp and I watched it happen and I did not put my own hand up against the end of that arm.”

## The Figure She Is Lighter By

On the eleventh and the twelfth and the thirteenth of that month she went over it four times and she got the same answer every time and she did not improve on it.

She said, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the ninth hour of the thirteenth:

“I said one figure. I did not say my name and there was not one word of anything else in it, and there was not a date on it and there was not a place on it and there was not one thing in it about what I am.”

She said, “And a figure is not a person. A figure is what a thing costs. And this building has been putting figures in columns for four hundred years and it has never once put a person in one, and if a person has to be put in a column by hand out of a number then the hand is deciding, and the hand does not decide.”

She said, “So what has happened today is that I have been entered into a row at this table with a figure at the head of it, and the figure is the price of me and not me, and there is not one instrument in this world that could tell the difference between the two of them.”

She said, “And I knew that this morning before I said it. I have known it since the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four when I read that strip with my fingers in the dark and found my own hand on it.”

## What She Is Now That She Has One

She worked out what had changed on the twelfth of that month and it took her a count of ninety and she said all of it out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the seventh hour.

She said, “Ninety days ago there was a strip of wood under that table with my figure on it that I had not put there. Today there is a board on top of that table with a figure on it that I did put there. Those are not the same thing and not one person in this building can see the difference and I cannot see it either.”

She said, “And the difference is that I cannot find out. For ninety-two days I was a thing that had arrived in this building without a row and I could go and stand where the table was and see that nothing had been written about me, and that was the whole of what I had and I did not know that was a thing I had until it stopped.”

She said, “And now I have gone from a figure I could check to a figure I cannot check, in one count, on a day of my own choosing, and I did not know that was on the table when I said the number and I know it now.”

She said, “And the one person in the world who could have told me what a row does to a thing in it is four miles down that mountain, on a floor with four hundred volumes on the shelves in their cages, and he is sixteen, and he does not know what a row is, and I am not going to be able to ask him because he does not know.”

## Why She Was Not Going To Read It

The board she had got written on was lying face up on a stack at the far end of that table under the lamp, about eighteen paces away and four feet up, and she could have read it with her fingers in the dark in the time it takes her to get up onto that table.

She did not do it and she said out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the ninth hour of the thirteenth, why not.

She said, “There are two things on that board and I cannot get at either of them, and I am going to leave both of them.”

She said, “If it is the figure I said, then a room has my price at the head of a column and nothing else in it, and I have known that since the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four and it has not stopped me sleeping. If it is not the figure I said, then I have put a true thing into a record I cannot read, in a building where things are decided by one look and there is no second one, and there is not one thing I could do about it if it were wrong.”

She said, “And I have never in nineteen years held a figure I could not act on. Not one. And a figure I cannot act on is the one kind of thing in the world I am not able to carry, and I have been carrying everything else since I was nine.”

She said, “So I am going to leave it.”

And then she said the whole of what she actually wanted, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the tenth hour of the thirteenth of that month.

She said, “I wanted it to be me. I have wanted one thing to be in this building that is not a figure since the eighth of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and I have said a figure out loud in daylight at the tenth hour and got a figure written down, and that is what a row is.”

She said, “And the board it is on goes up that stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen with the next bundle, and it comes back down empty like every other one of them has, and I am not going to see it again.”

She said, “And I am not going to find out what is in it for a very long time, and there is not one thing I am going to be able to do about it, and I said it anyway.”
