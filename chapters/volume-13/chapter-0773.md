# What She Has Got

She counted what she had on the sixth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that long table in a room with two people in it, and it came to five things, and not one of the five is a thing she chose, and she found that out while she was doing it.

## What She Counts Out Loud

She started with the ones that are easy and she went the whole way round and she came back and did them a second time because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

She said, standing, in daylight, at the ninth hour of the sixth of that month, at the near end of that table, four feet off the wood:

“I have four fingers and a thumb.”

She said, “That is five on the right hand and I have had it since I was born and it is the hand I write with and it has not closed since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and it is not going to.”

She said, “I have two coppers and I cannot count them.”

She said, “I have worked out on the eighteenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen that I cannot count them and I have not been able to do it since and there is not one instrument in the world that would help, and they have not changed in two hundred and eight days and neither has my hand.”

She said, “I have five blank leaves against my fourth rib that I have not written in and am not going to.”

And then she stopped and put her left hand against the fourth rib on the right side, where a stub of a nib and a cake of ink have been kept against her skin since the seventh of the month beginning one thousand eight hundred and seventy-four and have never once been let dry, and she stood there for a count of ninety with her hand there.

She said, “They have been blank for ten years and they were blank on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four when I put a day into that lamp, and they are blank now, and I am not going to write in them, and that is not a promise, it is a figure.”

## The Six Terms

And then she said the last of the easy ones and she said it more slowly than the rest.

She said, “I have a record.”

She said, “Six terms, said out loud in this room on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four, one at a time, in daylight, standing up, every line before it is a line anywhere else, and if there is one word of it that is not true I will know it inside a day and there is no road back for either of us.”

She said, “And it is a record and not a hand. He does not decide anything. I decide everything. And the only thing he has is that it is written down.”

She said, “And there has never been a seventh one and I have not asked for one.”

And then she said the thing about it that she had not worked out until she got to it, and it took a count of ninety and she said it standing in daylight at the near end of that table with a person standing nine feet off along it.

She said, “That is the only one of the five that somebody else put there.”

She said, “Everything else on this list I got on my own or it was done to me. The hand is mine. The coppers are mine or they are nobody’s. The five leaves are mine because no one has ever been able to give me a page. And the record is six terms that a man said out loud in this building and kept, and he kept them because I asked him to and he asked me not to and I did it anyway.”

She said, “And it is the only thing on this list that would cost somebody else something if I lost it, and it is the only thing on this list that is worth anything, and I counted it last.”

## Nine Hundred And Fifty Days

She said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that long table, and she said it as arithmetic and not as anything else:

“Two thousand five hundred and nine less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is nine hundred and fifty days.”

She said, “At one hour and twelve minutes a day. It has been the day less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine since the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four, and before that it was the day less one thousand five hundred and sixty from the eighteenth of the month beginning one thousand five hundred and forty-four, which is eight hundred and thirteen days, and it was never wrong in that time either and not one of them has gone back.”

She said, “And one of them went into that lamp at the fourth hour of the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and it did not come back and it is not coming back and that is the only one of nine hundred and fifty that has ever gone anywhere in a direction I chose.”

And then she said the part that made the whole count worth doing, and she said it flatly, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table:

“That is the fifth thing I have, and it is the only one of the five that can be spent.”

She said, “A bar cannot be spent. A hand cannot be spent. Two coppers I cannot count cannot be spent. Five blank leaves cannot be spent and are worth nothing and I have never once been able to give one away.”

She said, “Six terms cannot be spent, because they are a record, and a record is the only kind of thing I have ever had that does not go down when it is used.”

She said, “And nine hundred and fifty days is a figure that is going down, and a figure that is going down is the only kind of thing there is that can be put into a light in a cage.”

And she stood there for a count of a hundred and forty and what she was feeling was not grief and it was not relief either.

What she was feeling was that she had been carrying a purse she had never opened and that it had five things in it and that four of them had been dead weight since the day she got them, and that the fifth one was the only thing in the world she could actually spend, and that she had known that since she was nine years old.

## The Bar She Does Not Count

And then she said the sixth thing, which is the bar, and she said it standing up and she said why she is not going to count it.

There is a bar nine feet nine flat against the wall on the left of that room, broken end towards the door, with no strap on it, and she left it there on purpose on the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four, and she took it up when she was nine years old and she has carried it for ten years and that is the whole of what is known about where it came from.

She said, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table:

“There is a bar nine feet nine against the wall on the left of this room.”

She said, “And I am not going to count it as something I have.”

She said, “Not because it is not mine. It has been mine since I was nine and there is not one person alive who could tell me different.”

She said, “Because I put it down on purpose. On the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four, standing, in daylight, in this room, with a hand on it, and I left it against that wall because there was nothing in this building I could put it down for.”

She said, “And the putting down is the only thing about that bar that is mine. Everything else about it belongs to a man who got it when I was nine and to a barrow ground nine years and to a road I cannot get back to, and there is not one thing in nineteen years that has let me ask about any of that.”

She said, “And if I count it I am counting his, and I am not going to.”

And then she said the last of it and it was about a boy and not about a bar.

She said, “There is a bar on this wall and there are nine hundred and fifty days in me and there is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain, and he could not carry the bar up eight hundred and eighty steps and I could not carry it down and I have not got a copy of it.”

She said, “And there is not one figure on that list I could put in his hands and have him hold it, and I have worked on that for two hundred and eight days and there is not one and there is not going to be one.”

## What The Count Comes To

And she said the last of it at the ninth hour of that day, standing in daylight at the near end of that table with a person standing nine feet off along it, and she said it in the ordinary voice and she did not stop in it once.

She said, “So it is five things and four of them are dead weight and one of them is the figure I am lighter by, and I have been carrying them around a room in a tower four miles up a mountain for two hundred and eight days and I have not put one of them on that table and I have not got one of them into my hands and I have not been able to give one of them to anybody.”

She said, “And there is a room that has just told me it can put a name at the head of a column in my row over the only figure in the world that is mine, and it costs one of the five, and I have twenty-nine days.”

She said, “And I am not going to decide it on the strength of a purse. I have never in my life decided anything on a purse. I am going to decide it on the sixth term and I have not said the sixth term out loud in this room since the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and I am going to say it out loud again before I do anything about it, whatever I do.”
