# Four Hundred And Forty With No Rail

She found out on the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four that he could not do the second four flights, and she did it by taking him down them and watching, and she had asked him nothing at all.

## Four Hundred And Forty Down

There are eight flights in the tower’s own stair. The first four are four hundred and forty steps and there is a rail on them on one side only, and the second four are four hundred and forty steps and there is nothing on that side at all, because the wall is the wall and there was never room for a second one.

She has been up and down both halves nine times since the seventh of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and she has never once gone down the second four with her hand on anything, because there is nothing to put it on.

He had been up them once in his life and that was on the morning he came to ask.

At the second hour of the twelfth she went down the second four in front of him and counted. Forty steps and she turned and looked up at him, and he was on the landing above her with one hand on the frame.

She said, “That is forty.”

He said, “I know how many they are.”

She said, “I know that you know. I am saying it out loud because I have got no other instrument and because you are out of breath again and this is the only thing I have got to give anybody.”

## The Part He Could Not Do

He got a hundred and ninety steps down in the first hour and then had to stop and sit on a step for a count of forty, and he sat the way a person sits who has not sat on stone in a while, which is with both arms round his knees and his head down.

She went up eighty steps to him and sat down on the step above with her arm open across her knees and her feet on the step and did not say anything at all for a count of a hundred and forty.

Then he said, “It is not the steps.”

She said, “I know.”

He said, “It is that there is nothing on that side. On the first four you can put your hand on it and you do not have to think about it and you get to the bottom. On the second four you have to put your hand on the wall and the wall is four inches off and you cannot get anything off the wall and if your hand goes out it does not have anything in it.”

She said, “That is the stair. That is exactly the stair. I have been telling you for four days that there is nothing on that side and you have got there before me.”

## What She Gave Him Instead Of A Reason

At the fourth hour of the thirteenth she got him down the whole of that second four with her going first and him behind her, and it took a count of nine hundred, and at the bottom of it she turned round and counted him up.

He said, “You do not have to do that.”

She said, “I have been doing that since I was ten years old and it is the only thing I have ever been able to do for anybody and I am going to do it for you whether you want it or not.”

At the ninth hour of the fourteenth she got him down the first four with her hand on the rail and her own hand did not go on the rail, because there was one hand there and it was his, and she kept hers at her side the whole way.

Eight hundred and eighty from the door of that room to the floor of the copying floor.

She said it out loud at the bottom of it, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice:

“Four hundred and forty and four hundred and forty, and the first four have a rail on one side and the second four have not got one, and there is not one lamp on any of them and there has not been one in this building for nine years.”

## Two Hands On An Edge

And that is the day she found out the thing about being small that nobody else in nineteen years had seen.

The boy put his hands on the edge of that long table at the near end of it and got up on it in one movement, the way a person gets up on a thing they have got up on a thousand times, and he stood on it with his weight on his arms and looked along the twenty-two paces of it.

She could not do that.

She had four tries at it between the twentieth and the twenty-third of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and on the fourth one she got a hand on the edge and held it for a count of nine, and she has gone along the top of that table on her hands and knees ever since, and her breath is at nine the whole way.

He came down off it in one movement as well and stood on the floor and looked at his own hands.

He said, “You cannot reach it.”

She said, “I can reach it.”

She put her left hand up and her fingertips got over the edge and about a hand’s width of her palm was on the wood and she could not get her weight onto it and she let go before her shoulder went.

She said, “I can reach it. It took me four days in the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four to get a hand on it and I am not able to get up on it the way you just did, and that is the whole of what is different about me and about you.”

## What The Table Is For

He asked her at the sixth hour of the fourteenth what the table was for, standing at the near end of it with his hands at his sides.

She said, “I do not know.”

He said, “You have been in here sixty-seven days.”

She said, “Sixty-eight days when you came up and seventy now.”

She said, “And I can tell you what is on it and I can tell you what has gone along it, and I can tell you what comes up that stair and when it comes, and I cannot tell you what it is for, and there is not one thing in this room that has the making of it on it.”

## What She Said About Not Being Seen

And then she said the truest thing she had said about that room, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the seventh hour of the fifteenth, with a boy of sixteen four feet off the end of a table.

She said, “You got up on that table and stood on it and looked along it and not one thing at that end of it moved.”

She said, “And I have been up on it twice and gone twenty-two paces of it on my hands and knees in the light of that lamp and that person did not turn round once, and this morning you stood on the middle of it for a count of nine and it did not look up.”

She said, “Nothing in this room sees anybody standing on it. Not me and not you. That is not an answer about a lamp on a landing and I am not going to pretend that it is, and it is the truest thing I have got about this place and I have had it for three days and I did not know it until you got up there.”

## What She Was Trying To Do

What she was trying to do for three days was stop him going back down that stair with a question and no answer in him, and she could not do it, and she worked out why in a count of sixty on the thirteenth and said it out loud on the fourteenth.

She said, “A person who asks me a thing and does not get an answer goes down that stair and says so. And there is no one in this building who can correct what he says and no one in the county who would ask him twice, and in about a year somebody will put a ladder up this stair because that is what happens to a thing somebody wants to get at.”

She said, “And I have been doing that for nineteen years. I have never once in my life been in a room where a person said a true thing and it stopped where they said it.”

She said, “So the thing I am trying to do is get him an answer, and I have not got one, and the only thing I have got is that he can climb four hundred and forty steps and count better than me when he is frightened.”

## Three Days On A Stair And A Count

On the sixteenth and the seventeenth she did not go down that stair again and he did not ask to go down it.

She worked on the three of them what she could do. She could not get a light. She could not get oil and there is none. She could not get anything to put in a lamp and there is nothing in this building to put in one.

She could give him the count.

On the fifteenth of that month she said it out loud to him standing at the near end of the table, and she said it in the ordinary voice and she said all of it:

“Two thousand three hundred and sixty-eight less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and nine days. And the light at the far end of this room is twenty-six of my own hand below the top of that cage, and it goes down two a day, and fifty-eight less twenty-six is thirty-two, and thirty-two divided by two is sixteen, and the sixteenth day from this one is the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four.”

She said, “And I am going to be standing in this room on that day, and there is going to be forty boards coming up that stair, and you can come and watch them or you can go down and not, and I am not going to tell you which one you ought to do.”

He said, “I will come.”

She said, “I know you will. I said it out loud because I could not let it stand that I had not said it.”

He said, “Is that all you say out loud?”

She said, “Almost all of it. Everything except the two things I have not worked out, and you are the first person in nineteen years who has been in a room while I said the rest of it.”
