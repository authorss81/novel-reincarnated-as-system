# Down The Way It Came

She did not put a hand into that cage at any hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, and the light came down to the cut in the wall at the fourth hour of it and did not stop there, and that is the first time in four hundred years a thing has gone back down that stair the way it came.

## The Twenty-Ninth

On the twenty-ninth of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four she said the six terms out loud in that room one last time in daylight, standing up, at the near end of that long table, in the ordinary voice, one at a time.

She said the first one and the third one and the fourth one and the fifth one and the sixth one without stopping between them.

And then she said the second one and stopped, and stood there for a count of ninety with her arm open in front of her and the light fifty-four of her own hand below the top of that cage, and then she said it and got through it.

She said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table, at the ninth hour of the twenty-ninth:

“That was the last time.”

She said, “And I have kept that term out loud in nineteen years in rooms that had daylight in them and in rooms that did not, and it has never once occurred to me that a term can be kept in one place and not in another, and I found that out on the twenty-third of this month at the ninth hour and I have had six days to do something about it and there is nothing in this building I can do about it.”

She said, “And I am not taking the other five back. They are a record and not a hand and I decide everything and I have decided this and I have said it out loud before it was a line anywhere else, which is the fourth one, and I have kept it in this room for two hundred and thirty-one days by saying everything out loud and I am not going to stop doing that because a room has stopped.”

## The Thirtieth

On the thirtieth she did the last day of it the way she does a day, and she got up at the fifth hour and ate at the second and the seventh and she took a reading of her own right forearm, which has been down by the width of the little finger on her left hand since the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four and has not come back.

It was the same to the width of a hair.

She said the sums out loud at the ninth hour, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table.

She said, “Fifty-eight of my own hand is from the top of that cage down to the cut in that wall and the light is fifty-six of my own hand below the top of that cage and it goes down two a day, and two thousand five hundred and thirty-three less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is nine hundred and seventy-four days, and the figure at the head of my row is eleven days, twenty-two hours and forty-eight minutes out of date.”

And then she said the last of it out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table, and she said it to the room and not to the person at it, and it took a count of ninety.

She said, “There is one thing left that can be put in that lamp and it is nine hundred and seventy-four days of me at one hour and twelve minutes a day and one of them went in there on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four and did not come back.”

She said, “And the person at this table has told me the true shape of it and they were right about every part of it, and they have said in a flat voice that they are not going to put a day in there on their own and have nothing for it, and that has not happened here in four hundred years, and I have believed them and they are correct.”

She said, “And so it is me or nothing, and I am not going to, and I am not saying that out loud for the room to hear because the room does not decide anything about a thing no one says, and I have said it out loud already on the twenty-seventh and it is in the air in here and that is where it belongs.”

## The Man On The Turn

The man came up the turn at the second hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four with about forty boards under one arm and a bundle of papers in a cloth under the other, and he has been on that road a year, and he came out of the place in the wall behind the lamp’s shelf.

He put them down.

That is the whole of what he does. He has done it seven times and he has been on that road a year, and he does not look at anything and not one person in this building has ever asked him a question.

And the hands at the near edge of that table did not come off the wood.

They did not go along the forty boards at about four to a minute. They did not go along them slowly either. They stood there with both of them on the near edge of a table in a room where the light was two of her own hand above the cut in the wall and the wall behind that lamp was black.

Forty things had arrived and the room had not looked at one of them.

She stood four feet off the near end of that table and said out loud, in the ordinary voice, and the man heard it and turned round:

“Take them back down.”

And he did not ask her why.

And she said the rest of it out loud because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists, and it is the only time in four hundred years that anybody has said that sentence in this room.

She said, “You have brought them up eight hundred and eighty steps and I am sending them down eight hundred and eighty steps, and the cord on that bundle is round it twice and not knotted, and I have had my arm into that bundle three times in this building and I have never pulled it, and there is not one instrument in the world that takes a thing back out of a bundle that has been untied.”

She said, “And I am not going to untie it now.”

And the man took up the forty boards under one arm and the bundle of papers in a cloth under the other and went back out of the place in the wall and went down that stair, and the sound of a person going down eight hundred and eighty steps in a tower went away for about nine minutes and then it did not.

And that is the first time in four hundred years that a bundle has gone back down that stair the way it came.

## It Did Not Stop On The Cut

At the fourth hour of that first of the month the light came down the last two of her own hand to the cut in the stone behind that lamp.

She was standing at the near end of that table and she watched it do it.

It has stopped on that cut four hundred years. Not because the cut stops it. Because a hand has gone into that cage within a day of the light reaching that cut, every single time, in four hundred years, and the one thing that cut has ever been is the place they stand and watch from.

And no hand went into that cage.

The light went on down.

She said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table, at the fourth hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and thirty-four, and it is the plainest thing she has said in this building in two hundred and thirty-three days:

“It did not stop on the cut.”

And then the light in that cage went out.

And there is nothing in this world that can put it back.

There is no oil in this building and there has not been for nine years. The person at that table is the lamp and the lamp is the person and neither of them has anything to put in it. There are five blank leaves against her fourth rib and a bag at the bottom of a stair with a dish and a lump of clay and a ring the width of a finger and a key to a drawer in it.

There is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain with a lamp of his own. There is not one thing in this building that goes into a lamp in a cage.

## The Room That Has Never Decided Anything In The Dark

And she stood in the middle of the floor of that room with her right arm open in front of her for a count of a hundred and forty, in the dark, and nothing about her had changed.

She said out loud, standing, in the dark, at the near end of that table:

“There is a bar nine feet nine against the wall on the left of this room and it is still there and I did not move it.”

She said, “There is a strip of pale wood under that table a foot long and two inches across with a price at the head of it and a five in the column in my own hand, and twelve pairs of cuts under that are empty, and there is not one instrument in the world that can read any of it now.”

She said, “And there is a row in this room that says what I am, written by me, true, and there is no light in it.”

She said, “And the deciding takes one look and there is no second look and there is no appeal, and in four hundred years there has never been anything to appeal with, and there has never once been a need to, because this room has never had to decide anything in the dark.”

She said, “And it has not decided anything about me. Not today. Not in two hundred and thirty-three days.”

And then she said the last of it, standing, in the dark, at the near end of that table, and it took a count of ninety and she said it without stopping in it once.

She said, “I did that.”

She said, “And I am not going to be able to un-do it and I am not going to be able to un-count what it cost, and a boy of sixteen is going to come up eight hundred and eighty steps in six days because he cannot leave a thing alone, and I have not got one word for him.”
