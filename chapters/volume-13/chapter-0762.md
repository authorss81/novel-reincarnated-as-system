# Under The Table With The Nib

She went under that table on the nineteenth, twentieth and twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four with a nib and a cake of ink against the fourth rib and came out without having written anything, and what happens in here is the decision and not the doing.

## What Is Under That Table

She went under it at the second hour of the nineteenth with a cross-beam across her shoulders and lay on her back on the stone and put her hand on her own strip.

She has had it under her hand more times than she has counted and she has never once moved it off that stone. A foot long and two inches across, pale wood, the grain running up it, face up, and a head at one end that is a figure and a column.

She got her hand round the wood and said it out loud, to the floor of that room:

“The head is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes, and it is in my own hand, and I chose that figure at two posts on a road at the first hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, standing, in the open, with nineteen words and a date and a place under it.”

She said, “And the column has one figure in it and it is the same figure, and it went into that column because I said it out loud in a room in this building and a hand wrote it, and the head and the column say the same thing and they have said it for one hundred and seventy days.”

And then she did the thing she had not done in one hundred and sixty-one days, which was to run her finger down the column past the figure that was in it.

She said, “There is another pair of cuts under it.”

She said, “And there is another pair under that one, and another under that, and they go down to the bottom of the wood and they are all the same apart and they are all empty, and the grain is not cut across any of them.”

## Reading It With A Nib In Her Hand

She lay there under that table with a nib and a cake of ink against the fourth rib and read that strip with her fingers for the better part of two hours, and she came out on the twentieth and went down again on the twenty-first and did it twice more, and on the twenty-first at the seventh hour she came out and said the whole of it.

She said, “There are fourteen pairs of cuts in that column and one of them has my price in it and thirteen of them are empty, and the wood is a foot long and two inches across and the cuts are the same apart from the head to the foot.”

She said, “And not one person in this building has ever entered a second figure into that column, and not one of those thirteen pairs has a mark in it of any kind, and there is no dust on any of them that has been disturbed.”

She said, “And that is not an accident. A hand that copies figures exactly is a hand that has been putting figures into columns for four hundred years, and if one of them had ever gone into two of them I would have found it.”

She said, “So this is the first column in this building that has ever had room left in it after a figure went in, and it is mine, and it has had room left in it for one hundred and seventy days and the room has not sent a single board up that stair asking me to fill it.”

And then she stopped, and she lay still under that table for a count of a hundred and forty, and then she said the part that is about her and it is the longest sentence she has said in this room.

She said, “There are two kinds of figure and I have worked that out in three days and it is the whole of what I have got. A figure that is a price can only be added up. You can add a price to another price and you get a price and there is not one thing you can do with the answer except keep adding. A figure that is a person can be counted.”

She said, “And being counted is the thing I have been escaping since I was nine years old.”

She said, “And there is no way in the world of knowing that is what I am doing at the moment I am doing it, except that I have got an empty column under a table with fourteen places in it and I am lying under it with a nib in my hand.”

## What It Would Take To Correct It

And on the twentieth of that month she did the other half of the work, which is to go through every way there is of being wrong about this, and there are only three of them, and she did them standing at the near end of that table with her arm open across her knees.

She said, “The first way is that I am wrong about what a figure is. I am not. A price can only be added to and a person can only be counted and I have been counting things since before I could walk and I have been wrong about it once.”

She said, “The second way is that a column with two figures in it cannot be added up and so the room will not use it. That is true and it does not matter, because that is not the same as the room being able to take a figure out of a column, and there is not one instrument in this building that has ever taken anything out of anything.”

She said, “And the third way is that the room will send something down that stair about it. It will not. There is no procedure in this building for a thing that has not been asked for and there has not been one for four hundred years, and the person at the far end of that table does not know my name and does not know my hand and has not looked up in a hundred and fifty-eight days.”

And then she stopped for a count of ninety and said the part about the drawer, which is the only other thing in her life she could change on the same afternoon and cannot.

She said, “There is a key in a bag at the bottom of that tower that opens a drawer thirty feet from a copying floor, and in that drawer there are four sheets of rice paper and five blank leaves, and the five blank leaves have been blank for ten years and I have never found out what is on the four sheets and I am not going to find out this month.”

She said, “And I have just found out that a second figure in a column cannot be corrected, and it took me four days to find out and it cost me an arm on the twelfth of this month and it is going to cost me another one when I do it, and there is a drawer four hundred and forty steps down this tower that I have never opened and that might contain the one thing in the world I could put in a column that would be true and about me and could still be changed.”

She said, “And I am not going to open it. Not because I am afraid of what is in it. Because if I open it and there is a page in there with a true line on it in a hand, then the last thing in this world that has never been written on is not blank any more, and I would rather be wrong in a column than right on a piece of paper that has not been read in ten years.”

## The Decision And Not The Doing

And she got herself out from under that table on the twenty-first at the ninth hour and stood at the near end of it with her arm open across her knees and the nib and the ink against the fourth rib, and she said the decision, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, and she said it once and she did not do it that day.

She said, “I am going to write a figure in the next pair of cuts down in that column.”

She said, “And it is going to be five. Five stages into the seventh realm, taken at the tenth hour of the eighteenth of this month at the near end of that table, on a board, with a nib out of a bag I carried up eight hundred and eighty steps in my own shirt.”

She said, “And it is going to be in my own hand and it is going to be under the figure the room put there, because that is the only place in this building where a figure of mine can go where the room will keep it.”

And then she stopped for a count of ninety and said what it costs, and she said it slowly.

She said, “Once that figure is in that column, that column has two figures in it. A price and a count. And a column with two figures in it is a column that gets added up, and that is not me being found out. That is me being counted, by a room that has never once been asked to count anything.”

She said, “I have been escaping that since I was nine years old. I got a bar at nine and I have carried it ten years and I put it down once, on purpose, and I am not going back for it. And there is not one instrument in the world that takes a line out of a record that has been above the line for four hundred years, and this one is under a table on a stone floor and it has been above the line for one hundred and seventy days.”

She said, “And I am not going to be rescued out of it. There is not one mouth in this building that knows I am in this room, and not one mouth is going to come up that stair about it, and there is no voice in my head that is going to ask me not to.”

She said, “And I am not going to do it tonight, because I took a stage off that table on the eighteenth and I have not found out yet whether a column can take a second figure without taking something else out of whoever put it there, and I am not going to find that out on the same row.”

And she put the nib and the cake of ink back inside her shirt against the fourth rib, and she went and stood in front of that wall at the far end of the room and looked at the light, which was thirty-eight of her own hand below the top of that cage.

She said, “Fifty-eight less thirty-eight is twenty and twenty divided by two is ten and the tenth day from the twenty-first of this month is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four.”

And then she said the last of that day out loud, standing, in daylight, and it was about a boy and not about a stair and not about a lamp.

She said, “There is a boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain who has never been in a row in his life and who does not know what one is, and there is not one thing I could hand him on the first of that month except a number, and a number is not a hand.”

She said, “And there is no way on the face of it that he is going to find out what a row is from me, and I am going to be in one before the first of that month, and I am not going to be able to tell him.”

And after that she did not go down either stair, and the second pair of cuts down in that column was still empty.
