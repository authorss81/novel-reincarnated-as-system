# The Same Substance

The lamp in the cage was turned on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 2,324 at the second hour, two days before she had worked out that the line cut in the wall would be reached on the thirtieth, and it took eleven seconds, and she has not been able to put it down since.

## What She Was Doing When It Happened

She was on the floor at the near end of that table with her back against the doorframe and her legs out in front of her and four numbers in her head in the order they were in, and she had been watching the far end of the table for two days because that is all she has to do and there is nothing else in this room.

The hands came off the near edge of the table.

That is the first thing and she was not ready for it, because in twenty-two days the hands have not come off that edge once, not for a count of a hundred, not in the middle of nothing.

One hand went up above the head and past it, and she could not see where it went because the lamp is in a cage on a shelf and the shelf is above her own head where she is sitting.

She got up off the floor.

## Eleven Seconds

The light went down.

She was standing at four feet off the end of that table when it started and it did not flicker and it did not go out and it went down the way water goes into a cloth, steadily, and the bright place it makes on the wall behind it came down with it and got smaller at the bottom edge.

It came down to the line cut in the stone and it stopped there, exactly on the cut, and it did not go under it.

And then the light went up.

She watched the bottom edge of the bright place come off that cut and go back up the wall past where it had been, and up past the top of the cage, and higher than the light had ever been in twenty-two days, and then it came back down and settled where it usually is.

And the whole of that was eleven seconds and she counted them.

## What Was Different Afterwards

She had been measuring that person for twenty-two days without knowing it, because she measures everything that is in front of her, and she had four hundred counts on the fourteenth of this month when she tried to put them in a column and gave it up.

The forearm on the table’s edge is smaller.

Not thinner. Smaller. She put her eye on it from four feet off with her own hand held up at arm’s length beside it the way she has held a slate up against a line on a board, and the round of it is smaller by the width of her thumb nail.

The face is different at the cheek. The hand that came down and went back onto the table edge is a hand she would have measured differently yesterday.

And the person did not stop. The hands went back onto the near edge of the table and the head came down over it and the whole of the standing went on exactly as it had been going on before, and there was not one thing in the whole of it that a person standing at that table would have looked up for.

## What She Worked Out

She got back down onto the floor at the near end because her breath had gone to nine and she had been standing, and she lay on her back on the stone with her arm open across her and she worked it out in a count of sixty, and then she said it out loud because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

She said, “The light came off that cut and went up and the arm went down at the same time.”

She said, “And I measured both of them. I put my own hand up at the end of that arm before it and I put my hand up against the wall at the bottom edge of that light before it and I did both again after, and the round that came off that forearm is the same distance the light came up that wall.”

She said, “And it is not a thumb nail and it is not a hand. It is the width of my little finger, and I do not have a little finger any more, and I used the one on my left hand to measure it and it was the same.”

She said, “And that is the same substance. That is all I have got and it is not a guess, because a thing that goes up by a distance and a thing that goes down by the same distance in the same seconds is one thing and not two, and I have been measuring things for nineteen years and that is a measurement.”

She said, “And it is not that the light came up because the arm went down. I do not know that and I am not going to say it. What I know is what happened, and what happened is that one of those two things got smaller and the other one got bigger, and it happened while I was standing there with my mouth open, and the one that got smaller is a person.”

She said, “And that person has been standing at that table for four hundred years, and nothing in this room is going to be different tomorrow.”

## Two Hundred Counts On A Forearm

She had two hundred counts of that forearm before the lamp was turned and she had them the way she has every figure that has ever mattered to her, which is in order and out loud.

She had put her own hand up at the end of it on the fourteenth of the month beginning 2,324 and again on the seventeenth and again on the twentieth and again this morning, and the four readings were the same, and she had said that out loud at the near end of the table each time and there was not one person in the room who had heard any of them.

She had also worked out, and said out loud on the twentieth, what it is about a forearm that can be measured by eye from four feet off by a person who can do nothing else, and the answer is that it cannot, except in one direction.

You cannot tell whether a forearm is bigger.

You can tell whether it is smaller, because you have had it in front of you before and your head has it, and she has been carrying numbers in her head since she was ten years old and this is the first thing she has ever carried in her head that was about another person.

She said that out loud, lying on the floor of that room on her back with her arm open across her, at the fifth hour of the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 2,324:

“I have four readings of an arm and every one of them is the same and I am telling you that it is smaller because of that and not because I have an instrument, and I have been counting things since I was ten years old and I know exactly what that is.”

She said, “And I have watched that arm four times without the lamp being turned and it has not gone down once. And I watched it go down and I watched it come back up and it did not come back up.”

He said, “You are going to be able to tell in about two months.”

She said, “I am not going to be here in two months.”

She said, “I am going to be in this room until the light in that cage is on a line cut in a wall. And by then there will not be one count left of that arm that is the same, and that is going to be the only figure about me in this building that has been true for longer than a month.”

## What She Said To Him About It

And then, at the ninth hour, she said the one thing in this room she has not said to the person at the table, and she said it to him and not out loud, which is a thing she has done four times in nineteen years and never in a room.

She said, “I could do it.”

She said, “I have got a table I can get on and a lamp in a cage with four bars in it and there is a light in there that I have measured at fifty-eight of my own hand from the top of the cage to a cut in the wall, and I have watched a hand go up between those bars and close on the base of it and come away eleven seconds later with less arm than it went in with.”

She said, “And I am not going to, and I want you to be quite clear that that is not because I am frightened of the room.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “Then say what it is.”

He said, “You do not know what it is.”

She said, “That is a reason.”

He said, “It is the only one you have got.”


## What It Is Not

And then she said what she does not know, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the sixth hour of the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 2,324, in a room where a hand copies figures exactly and where she has been keeping things out of words for a month because she will not say a guess.

She said, “I do not know what it is.”

She said, “There is a light in a cage with no oil in it and no wick in it and nothing behind it, and a person stands at a table and puts a hand up into the cage and the light comes down and goes up, and I have watched it once and I have been in this room twenty-two days and I do not know what any of it is made of and there is not one thing in this room that is going to tell me.”

## What She Can Compute

And then she said what she can compute, and it is the only thing in the whole of this that is a number, and she said it standing, in the ordinary voice, at the seventh hour of the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 2,324.

She said, “One turning costs that person the width of a thumb nail across the forearm.”

She said, “And I have no figure for how many turnings there have been, because a hand turns a lamp as often as it has to and it is not written down, and I cannot get at it by the light either because I have measured the light in my own hand and the light has fifty-eight hands to fall and there is not one step in that where a person is in it.”

She said, “And I can compute mine exactly. Two thousand three hundred and fifty-one less one thousand five hundred and sixty is seven hundred and ninety-one, and one hour and twelve minutes a day, and not one of them going back, and it started on the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544, and I was not able to see it start.”

She said, “And that is a version of the same thing and I have been paying it every day since I was nine.”

She sat down.

She said, “There is a person at that table who is less every time that lamp is turned and there is a person at this end of it who is less every day, and there has not been one hand in this building that has put either of them back.”

## The Part She Cannot Say

And then she stopped, and she stopped for a reason she said out loud at the ninth hour of that day with the light at the far end of the room standing higher than it had stood in twenty-two days.

She said, “I have got a hand and a table I can get up on and a lamp in a cage with four bars in it.”

She said, “And I have not got an instrument for what goes into that cage and no mouth in this building is going to tell me and I am not going to find out by guessing.”

She said, “And I am not going to do anything about it until I know what it is, and that is a thing I have never once said out loud before in my life, because everything I have ever lost was taken off me in a place I was not in.”

She lay on the floor of that room with her arm open and looked at the light.

It was higher than it had ever been.
