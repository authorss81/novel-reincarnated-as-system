# One Day Into A Lamp

She put a day into that lamp at the fourth hour of the fourth of the month beginning 2,354, standing, in daylight, in a room with a person in it, with nobody asked for it, and it is the first thing she has ever spent.

## What She Had To Put In It

She did the whole of the count out loud first, standing on the floor at the near end of that table, because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

She said, “There is a bar nine feet nine against the wall on the left and I am not putting that in a lamp.”

She said, “There are two coppers I cannot count and a thing in a bag at the bottom of a stair that has not been let dry since the seventh of the month beginning 1,874 and nine inches of hand-made paper with eighty-one lines on it, and I decided on the third of this month that I was not going down for any of them and I have not gone down.”

She said, “And there is a key and a ring the width of a finger and a lump of clay and a stub of a nib and a dish, and none of those is a day.”

She said, “And there are five blank leaves against my ribs and they have been blank for ten years and they are going to be blank when this is over, and that has not changed in four weeks and it is not going to change now.”

## The Difference Between Losing And Spending

And then she said the thing that made her do it, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the third hour of the fourth of the month beginning 2,354, and she said it slowly.

She said, “Everything that has ever come off me has come off me in a place I was not in.”

She said, “It has been taken off me from a hand that was not mine, on a day I did not choose, at a frame or on a board or in a column, and I have never once been where it happened and I have never once been able to hand one of them anywhere.”

She said, “I have never lost anything either. Losing is a word for what has been done to me. I have never spent one hour of one day out of my own life by my own hand with my own hand open.”

She said, “And there is a hand in this room that puts a day into that lamp every time it needs light, and it is the same substance, and I have measured it, and I am the only thing in this building that has anything I could put in there.”

## The One Question He Asked

He said, “You are not going to be able to feel it go.”

She said, “I know.”

He said, “You will put your hand in there and you will feel nothing at all and you will come down off that table not knowing whether you have done a thing or not.”

She said, “Then I will do the sum out loud afterwards and I will find out whether I have done it.”

He said, “That is the first thing in nine years you have said to me that is a plan.”

She said, “It is the first thing in nine years I have said to anybody that is a plan.”

## Her Hand In The Cage

She got onto the table at the fourth hour with her left hand and her weight on the arm that was closed that day, and she went along the top of it on her hands and knees, twenty-two paces, with the grain of the wood under her palms and her breath at nine the whole way, and the light coming up on her from the far end.

She knelt on the shelf in front of the lamp and put her hand up between four bars of iron into the cage.

She closed it on the base of the light.

The light went up.

It did not go down to the cut in the wall and stop and come back, the way it did when the one at the table does it. It came up off the cut and up off the level it had been standing at and up past the top of the cage, and it did not stop where it had been higher on the twenty-eighth, and it came up and it stayed up.

It was the highest that light has ever been in this room.

She took her hand out from between the bars and the light stayed where it was.

## What It Cost

And she came back along the top of that table on her hands and knees and got down off the near end onto the floor, and her breath was at nine and her right arm was open and it had been open since the first of the month beginning 2,294 and it was open now in the same way and nothing about it was different.

She stood at the near end of that table and did the sum out loud, in the ordinary voice, standing, in daylight, in a room with a person in it.

She said, “Two thousand three hundred and fifty-seven less one thousand five hundred and sixty is seven hundred and ninety-seven.”

She said, “That is what I was at the top of this hour.”

She said, “And it is seven hundred and ninety-eight now.”

She said, “That is two days in one day.”

## The Change In The Oldest Number

And then she said the part of it that is arithmetic and is going to be true for the rest of her life, and she said it standing, out loud, one item at a time.

She said, “The figure I am lighter by has been the day less one thousand five hundred and sixty since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544, and it has never been wrong, and it was seven hundred and ninety-six yesterday and it was seven hundred and forty-one on the eighth of the month beginning 2,294.”

She said, “And from today it is the day less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine.”

She said, “Two thousand three hundred and fifty-seven less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is seven hundred and ninety-eight, and it is that for every day from here, and there is not one day going back, and there is not one instrument in this building or in this county or in the whole of the world that takes a day back once it has gone into a lamp.”

She stood there and let it be a thing in the room.

## The Day That Goes By Unused

And then she said what she had done to the arithmetic of that room, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the fifth hour of the fourth of the month beginning 2,354, because she has not been able to leave one of these alone since the eighth of that month.

She said, “Twenty-six days from this hour to the thirtieth of this month is what was left in that lamp, and I have just put a day of my own into it.”

She said, “And so it is twenty-seven days now, and the line cut in the wall is on the first of the month beginning 2,384, which is a day of the month that is thirty days long and is the day the next forty things come up that stair.”

She said, “And that is the first day of slack in the month.”

She said, “There has been one of those in every month for four hundred years and not one person has ever used one, and there is not one row in this building with my name at the head of it that has got anything in it about what I did at the fourth hour this morning.”

## Three Days After

And on the fifth of the month beginning 2,354, and the sixth, and the seventh, she did not do it again, and she went and put her hand against the wall every day at the fourth hour and worked out where the light was.

On the second of the month beginning 2,354 the light was two of her own hand below the top of that cage and on the third it was four, and it was six on the fourth, and she put a day into it and it came back up to four, which is where it had been on the third.

On the fifth it was six below the top and on the sixth it was eight and on the seventh it was ten, and the rate had come back to two a day and was going down the same as it always had, and she stood on the floor at the near end of that table and said it out loud and got seven hundred and ninety-nine and then eight hundred and then eight hundred and one.

And then she did the other sum, out loud, standing, because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists. Fifty-eight less ten is forty-eight, and forty-eight divided by two is twenty-four, and the twenty-fourth day from the seventh of the month beginning 2,354 is the first of the month beginning 2,384, and that is the day the next forty things come up that stair.

And she said why she is not going to put a second day into that lamp, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the fifth hour of the fifth of that month, with her hand on the near edge of that table.

She said, “I could go up that table right now and put my hand between those bars and I am not going to, and it is not because it is dangerous. I went in there once and it did not hurt me and I have had worse from a slate and from a stair.”

She said, “Because I put one in at the fourth hour yesterday and I have not got an instrument for what came out, and there is not one mouth in this building that is going to give me one, and I am not going to put a second one in until I have got the first one measured. And I am going to be here on the thirtieth of this month and on the first of the month beginning 2,384, and I am going to watch what that lamp does on its own.”

And the person at the table did not turn round once, and there was no mark on anything, and no stroke and no figure anywhere, and the strip of wood under the table with the head and the column on it had the same figure on it that it had on the first of the month beginning 2,324.

And it is three days, three hours and thirty-six minutes out of date now, and she reads it with her fingers every day, and there is not one person in that building who could ever know.

## What Is On The Page

And she said the whole of what has happened to her since the eighth of the month beginning 2,294, at the ninth hour of the seventh of the month beginning 2,354, sitting on the floor with her back against a doorframe, in the ordinary voice, and it took a count of ninety and she did not stop in it once.

She said, “Day eight, a room with a person in it that I had counted nine times without a person in it. Day twenty, a hand on the edge of a table. Day twenty-four, a lamp with nothing in it and nothing to put in it, and a light that does not lean in a draught.”

She said, “The first of the month beginning 2,324, forty boards and a bundle of papers and forty minutes and not one hand on the table. The twelfth, a strip of wood under the table with a figure at the head of a column that is mine and is in my hand and is three days out of date. The twenty-seventh, a lamp turned down to a cut in a wall and turned back up, in eleven seconds, and an arm smaller afterwards.”

She said, “The second of the month beginning 2,354, the same thing again. Fifty-eight of my own hand to a cut in a wall, and two of my own hand a day, and twenty-nine days, and one day in every month that has never once been spent.”

She said, “And today, one day of mine into the cage at the fourth hour, and it did not come back, and the figure I am lighter by is the day less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine from here and it is going to be that for the rest of my life.”

She said, “That is sixty days. And there are five blank leaves against my ribs and a bar against the wall on the left and a lamp at the far end of this table higher than it has ever been, and none of the sixty days has gone into the book and not one word of any of it has gone out of this room.”


## What She Has Found Out

And she said the last of it at the ninth hour of the seventh of the month beginning 2,354, sitting on the floor with her back against the doorframe and her arm open across her knees and a bar nine feet nine against the wall on the left where she left it on purpose.

She said, “Light in this room costs a day. That is the price of it and I can compute it exactly and it is a day a day and it has been a day a day since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544.”

She said, “And I am the only thing in this building that can pay it, and there is no one going to bring me anything, because there has never been one thing in nineteen years that was given to me and I have not been able to be given anything since the day I was born.”

She said, “And there are eight hundred and one days of it in me at this hour and twenty-seven days of light in that lamp and one day of it is already spent and it does not come back.”

She looked at the light at the far end of the room, which was higher than it had ever been and higher than it was going to be on the thirtieth of this month, and she had a count of a hundred with the next thing she was going to say and she said it anyway.

She said, “I do not know what it is paying for. And I have been in this room sixty days and there is not one thing in it that is going to tell me.”
