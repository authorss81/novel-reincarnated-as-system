# The Fifth Vessel

She took the fifth stage off that table herself at the tenth hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, standing on the wood in the light, writing with a nib she had carried up eight hundred and eighty steps in her own shirt on the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen, and it is fifty-eight days, and the whole of what it cost her is that she cannot be measured with a person.

## The Second Board She Took Herself

She worked out on the fourteenth and the fifteenth of that month that she could do it, and on the sixteenth she worked out how, and she did not do it on either of those three days.

The fifth bundle went along that table at the second hour of the first of that month and the boards went under it in a stack of forty, and she had got one out of that stack on the twentieth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four on her back with a cross-beam across her shoulders, and it took two tries then and one try now, and there was no reason on earth to wait.

What there was, was that she had said a row out loud in that room on the twelfth of that month and a hand had come down that table, off the near edge, down the leg of it, and taken her open right arm at the wrist and gone round it once and stopped on the wrist bone for eleven seconds, and her forearm had gone down by the width of the little finger on her left hand.

And she did not know yet what that was going to have to do with a stage.

She said out loud on the sixteenth of that month, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table:

“There is not one mouth in this building that is going to tell me whether a stage can be taken against a row that has something wrong in it, and I am not going to put a figure on a board until I have got past the day before.”

## Along The Table In The Light

On the seventeenth she got the board out and on the eighteenth she wrote on it.

She took the top one off the stack on her back with the cross-beam across her shoulders and it took one try.

She got up on the near end of that table with her left hand and it took a count of nine. She went twenty-two paces along the top of it on her hands and knees with her breath at nine the whole way and the light coming up on her from the far end the whole time, and the bar nine feet nine lying flat against the wall on the left of that room where she put it on purpose.

And then she knelt on the shelf in front of that lamp with the board flat on the wood and she took the nib and the cake of ink out from against the fourth rib where they had been for seven days, and she put her right hand round it with three fingers and a thumb, on an arm that has been open since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and is not going to close.

She had not written with ink since she was seven years old. The nib was a stub and the ink had been open since the seventh of the month beginning one thousand eight hundred and seventy-four and had never let dry and it took a count of nine to make it do what a nib does.

There is nothing behind that light and the wall is the wall and the flame does not lean when her hand moves past it.

She wrote one figure.

She said it out loud, kneeling on that shelf in the light:

“Five.”

And then she stood up off the shelf on top of a four-foot table in daylight in a room with a person in it and said what that was, standing, and she said it slowly.

She said, “Immortal Vessel 5. Five stages into the seventh realm, and ten stages past a ceiling that has not existed since before I could hold a bar.”

And nothing happened.

The person at that table did not look up and did not turn round and did not move, and there was no sound, and the low door stood open on a stair that goes down eight hundred and eighty steps, and there was not one thing in that room that knew it had happened.

## Fifty-Eight And Not A Hundred And Thirteen

And she said the interval out loud on the floor at the near end of that table at the sixth hour of the eighteenth of that month, standing, in daylight, because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

She said, “The fourth was at two thousand four hundred and three days and the third was at two thousand two hundred and ninety and the second at two thousand two hundred and six and the first at two thousand one hundred and twenty-eight.”

She said, “Two thousand four hundred and sixty-one less two thousand four hundred and three is fifty-eight. And it is not a hundred and thirteen and the a hundred and thirteen was two thousand four hundred and three less two thousand two hundred and ninety.”

She said, “And here is why it is fifty-eight, and I worked that out standing up in daylight at the near end of that table and not because anybody told me: the first three were taken against a room, and a room is not a row.”

She said, “The fourth was a hundred and thirteen because there was no row to take it against. On the ninth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four I said a figure out loud in this room and a hand wrote it on a board, and that board is the row I have got, and everything since has gone against it.”

She said, “So a stage can be taken against a row that is hers instead of against a room that is not, and a person can be measured against her own record, and it does not matter one hour that the record is a price and not a person, because the room does not know what it is measuring.”

She said, “And I am not going to say that I waited fifty-eight days for it. I did not know it was there. I have been in this room a hundred and fifty-eight days and there was not one row in this building about me until the tenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four.”

## What It Took Off Her And What It Took Off Nobody Else

And then she said the price, out loud, standing on the floor at the near end of that table at the seventh hour of that day, in the ordinary voice, and she said it once and she did not say it again in that room and she is not going to say it again in any room.

She said, “It has cost me the ability to be measured with a person.”

She said, “A comparison takes two things standing still. It took one of them off me on the twelfth of this month and it took the other one off itself every time that lamp has been turned for four hundred years, and a comparison that has run off me cannot be run again with a person standing next to it.”

She said, “And so from today, in every room there is, the thing that does the measuring will be me and the thing being measured will be a record, and if there is a person in it, that person is furniture.”

She said, “And that is the whole of what it cost and there is not one other part of it.”

And then she took her own account of herself out loud, standing, in daylight, because there were two figures in that room that had moved and she is not going to let one of them go by unsaid.

She said, “Seven of eleven holds. It was seven this morning and it is seven now, and I did not lose it going up that table and I did not lose it coming down it, and it did not move at all this time and I have not worked out what that means and I am not going to guess in a room where a hand copies figures exactly.”

She said, “And my right forearm is still down by the width of the little finger on my left hand from the twelfth of this month, and it has not come back, and there is not one instrument in this building that would put it back.”

## The Sums On The Eighteenth

She did the sums at the ninth hour of the eighteenth of that month, standing, in daylight, at the near end of that table with the lamp at the far end of it thirty-two of her own hand below the top of that cage.

She said, “Two thousand four hundred and sixty-one less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is nine hundred and two days, at one hour and twelve minutes a day, and not one of them going back and one of them gone into that lamp on the fourth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four.”

She said, “And fifty-eight less thirty-two is twenty-six and twenty-six divided by two is thirteen and the thirteenth day from the eighteenth of this month is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four, and that is the day the sixth things come up that stair.”

She said, “And the figure at the head of my row is eight days, eight hours and twenty-four minutes out of date, and there is a board on the top of that stack with a figure on it in my own hand written with a nib that has been in a bag for thirteen years, and it is going up that stair on the first of that month with forty other things, and I cannot see it and I am not going to be able to.”

And that was the last of that day, and it is what she said standing in daylight on the floor of a room four miles above a mountain with a person in it who had not looked up in a hundred and fifty-eight days.

She said, “A boy of sixteen four miles down that mountain is not going to be able to be measured either. He has nothing in this building that would weigh him and he has nothing in his own two hands that would weigh anybody else, and the two of us are the only two people I have ever met who cannot be counted where they stand, and neither of us has told the other.”

She said, “And there is a board on that stack with five on it in my own hand, and a column with one figure in it that is a price, and I have got a nib and a cake of ink against the fourth rib and a room that copies figures exactly.”

She said, “And I am going to write a second figure in that column before the first of that month, and it is not going to be a price, and there is not one instrument in the world that takes a line out of a record that has been above the line for four hundred years.”

And then she got down off that table the long way, on her hands and knees, twenty-two paces, and put the board she had written on back under the shelf in the stack of forty, and she could not see it go.
