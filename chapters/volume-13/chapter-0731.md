# Left Against The Wall

She decided what to do about the bar on the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,324, at the fifth hour, standing over it, and it took her three days after that to do it and she has not been able to make herself do it faster than that and she has not been able to make herself leave it either.

## What It Costs To Put It Down

Nine feet of ash, four inches by six, a hold-iron at each end and no strap on it and no notch cut for a hand anywhere along it, and it has been against the wall on the left of that room since the seventh of this month and she has not been near it once.

She picked it up on the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,324 at the second hour, with three fingers and a thumb on one end and her left hand in the middle of it, and it cost her twenty-nine counts.

Then she carried it to the middle of the floor and turned round and put it down again and it cost her fifty-four, which is more than it has ever cost her to set anything anywhere and is what it costs to set down a thing you have decided to set down.

Then she did it twice more that day on purpose, to be sure of the figures, and the third one was fifty-four as well and she said that out loud standing over it.

## What It Is For

A bar is not furniture. She has never in her life thought about that and she thought about it for a whole day on the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,324, lying on the floor of that room next to it.

A bar is a thing for carrying a load along a thing that is not flat, and the whole of what it is for is the shoulder, and the shoulder is the part of it.

There is nothing in this room to put a bar down for. There is no rack and no hook and no beam and no bench. There is a table four feet high with a lamp on it and a stair that turns and a person standing at the far end of it with both hands on the edge.

There is not one object in this room that a bar belongs to. There is not one object in this room that anything belongs to, except the lamp and the table and the floor and the person, and she cannot put anything on any of them.

## What Carrying It Up There Would Be

She worked out the whole of it standing on the floor on the nineteenth and the twentieth of the month beginning 2,324, out loud, in the ordinary voice, and she got it right twice.

She said, “That stair goes up nine hundred steps and turns, and I can hold nine hundred as a number for about a count of four and then I cannot hold it at all.”

She said, “And I have to stand still at the top of anything, and I go to nine, and I go to nine standing still on the seventh step of that stair and I go to nine at the top of eight flights of another one, and I am not carrying a bar nine feet nine up nine hundred steps at nine counts of breath in a place where I have never once been able to put a hand on the wall and the wall on both sides.”

She said, “And I would have to do it with an arm that has been open since the first of this month and is not going to close, and three fingers and a thumb, and I would have to do it coming down again with the same.”

She said, “And I am going to be standing on the top of that table on hands and knees twenty-two paces from a lamp in a cage.”

## What A Shoulder Is For

She worked that out on the eighteenth of the month beginning 2,324 with the bar in her hands, out loud, in the ordinary voice, standing over it in the middle of that floor.

She said, “A bar is nine feet nine of ash four inches by six and there is a hold-iron welded at each end and no strap and no notch and nothing cut for a hand anywhere along it.”

She said, “And every one of the nine feet nine of it has been on my left shoulder since I was nine years old, which is ten years, and there has not been one single day of that in which I did not have it on me when I went up a hill.”

She said, “And I have never once thought about where a bar is supposed to go. There is no bar in this county. There is not one person in this county I have ever met who has told me what a bar is for, and I have been carrying one for ten years because it was in my hands when I was nine and there was not one person there to take it off me.”

She said, “And I have just worked out in a count of a hundred and forty that it is not a thing you own. It is a thing you put under a load on a thing that is not flat, and the whole of it is the shoulder, and the shoulder is the part of it that gets the worse.”

She said, “That is all of it. And there is not one other object I have got that works like that, and I have got a ring a finger’s width and two coppers I cannot count and a dish and a lump of clay.”

## The Third Time She Picked It Up

And on the twentieth of the month beginning 2,324 she picked it up a third time on purpose and carried it to the far end of the room, and it took her twenty-nine counts to get it up and she had her breath at seven going across and at nine standing still at the far wall, and she stood there with it.

And then she said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, standing against the far wall of that room with a bar across both hands and an open arm:

“If I carried this to the foot of that stair I would carry it down nine hundred and four steps of a stair that turns and then eight hundred and eighty steps of another one that has a rail on the first four of them and nothing on the second four of them, at seven counts of breath on anything that rises and nine standing still, and I would do it with three fingers and a thumb and an arm that is not going to close.”

She said, “And it is not the weight. It has never been the weight. It is that there is nothing at the bottom of those stairs to put it against, and nothing on this table to lay it on, and nothing in this room that a bar is for, and I am not going to be able to stand at the top of nine hundred and four steps and hold nine feet nine over my head to hand it to a person who has never been in this room.”

She said, “And I am not going to be able to put it down.”

She put it back where it was and it cost her fifty-four counts, and she stood in the dark near the door with her forehead against the frame and her hands on the wood.


## What She Did

And on the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,324, at the second hour, she put it down for the last time and it cost her fifty-four counts and she did not pick it up again.

She put it flat against the wall on the left of the room, the way it was, with the dust on the top of it broken at one end and not at the other, and she put it down with the broken end towards the door because that is the end that was nearer her hand when she came in and she is not going to change which end it is.

And then she stepped back and looked at it and it is nine feet nine of ash lying flat on a stone floor in a room four miles above a mountain, and it is the first object she has ever owned that is not against her ribs or on her shoulder or in a bag at the bottom of a stair.

## What She Said To It

And she said it out loud to the bar, in the ordinary voice, standing over it, and not to a person, because there is one person in this room and that person is at the far end of a table and does not turn round.

She said, “I am leaving you here.”

She said, “I have carried you since I was nine years old and you are the only thing in this room that has ever been mine, and I am putting you down flat against a wall in a room where things are decided by a hand, and I am not coming back for you today.”

She said, “And I am not going to be able to come back for you at all if it turns out that this lamp is going down two of my hand a day and I have to be on that table for a piece of it, and that is the reason I am doing it and it is the only reason.”

She stood there for a count of ninety with her hand on the top of it and she did not look at the dust on the top of it.

## What She Has Instead

And then she said the other half of it, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the third hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 2,324, and it is the first thing in nineteen years she has been able to say about a thing she owns.

She said, “I have never once put anything of mine down in a room on purpose.”

She said, “Everything that has ever come off me has come off me in a place I was not in, from a hand that was not mine, on a day I did not choose. And this is the first time in nineteen years I have taken a thing I am holding and set it down where I could not reach it in a count of nine.”

She said, “And the bar is going to be there when the light goes, and when a person stands at that table in four hundred years, and I am not going to be here to see it and that is the first thing I have ever left behind that I chose.”

She got up off the floor and went and sat at the near end of the table with her back against the doorframe.

And she could go down those eight hundred and eighty steps with nothing in her hands for the first time since she was nine years old.
