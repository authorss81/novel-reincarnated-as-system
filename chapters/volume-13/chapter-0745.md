# The Arm That Wrote It Down

She went back to the readings of that forearm on the fourteenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and eighty-four and found out what her figure cost, and it did not cost her.

## Five Readings And A Sixth

She had six readings of that forearm and she took the sixth on the fourteenth of that month at the seventh hour and it is the only set of six she has ever taken of anything.

The fourteenth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four, at the seventh hour.

The seventeenth of the same month, at the seventh hour.

The twentieth of the same month, at the seventh hour.

The twenty-eighth of the same month, at the second hour, before the hand went up into the cage.

The second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four, at the second hour, before the hand went up into the cage.

The fourteenth of this month, at the seventh hour, which is after a figure I said out loud in this room went onto a board.

She said, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the near end of that table, holding her own left hand up at the end of that forearm the way she has held it up six times:

“It is down again.”

She said, “And it is down by the width of the little finger on my left hand, and I do not have a little finger on this hand any more and I have not had one since the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four, and the one I used is the same as the other two times.”

She said, “So a figure I said out loud in this room on the tenth of this month cost the person who wrote it down exactly what a lamp costs, and not one hour and twelve minutes came off me.”

## Forty Minutes That Cost Nothing

And then she went and checked the other one, which is the one that made the whole of it, and she checked it on the fifteenth and the sixteenth.

She said, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice:

“I have four readings of that forearm from the twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four and I took one at the near end of that table on the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four and one on the first of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four, with forty boards and a bundle of papers in front of it.”

She said, “And I measured that forearm before and after both of those and it is the same to the width of a hair both times, and the whole of those two was eighty minutes of that hand going along a table at four to a minute.”

She said, “So the room’s ordinary work is free. Forty boards in ten minutes and a bundle in thirty and not one round of a finger off that arm.”

She said, “And one figure copied exactly cost the same as one lamp, and the difference between those two is that forty boards are looked at once and a figure is copied, and this room has never looked at a board in four hundred years and has copied for it every day of that time.”

## The Saying Is Free And The Writing Is Not

On the seventeenth she put the whole of it together out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the ninth hour, with the light thirty of her own hand below the top of that cage.

She said, “A row is entered by a person saying a thing, and the saying is free.”

She said, “I have said a figure out loud in this room every day for ninety-nine days and about nine hundred of them have gone into the air and out of the door and nothing has come off me for any of them, and I have never once paid for saying one.”

She said, “And the writing is not free. And the cost of the writing comes off the hand that writes. Not off the person it is about. Off the hand.”

She said, “I have been in a row for four days and I am lighter by a figure and a day a day at one hour and twelve minutes, and that is the same figure and the same rate as the ninety-four days before I said anything, and not one hour of it is on account of that board.”

She said, “And the only person in this room who has ever paid for a row is the person standing at that table.”

## The One Who Is Standing There

And she said the next part, out loud, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, and it took a count of ninety and she said it slowly.

She said, “That person has stood at that table for four hundred years.”

She said, “And every row in this building has had its column entered by somebody saying a thing out loud in a room somewhere, and every one of those has been written down here, and every one of those has come off that arm.”

She said, “And that person has never once asked whose it was. And they could not. It is not written anywhere in this room, and it was never written anywhere, and if they asked there is not one mouth in this building that would answer them, and the room would go on exactly as it is going on.”

She stopped there and stood on the floor at the near end of that table for a count of a hundred and forty.

And then she said the sentence, and she said it out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and it is the only sentence in ninety-nine days that has a person in it.

She said, “Somebody stood at that table for four hundred years and put their arm into a lamp for words other people said out loud in rooms they were not in, and they never found out whose words they were, and they never once thought about it.”

And then she thought about the one person who had come up eight hundred and eighty steps to ask her a question and had gone back down with a number instead of an answer, and he was four miles down that mountain on a floor with four hundred volumes on the shelves in their cages, and he was sixteen, and he could count forty boards coming off a table in ten minutes without being told twice, and he did not know what a row was and he was not going to be told by her in any room she has got.

She said, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, and it is the second time in ninety-nine days she has said a thing about him out loud:

“He asked me what a row was for and I did not answer him and I have not got an answer now. And there is not one thing I have found in this room in ninety-nine days that I could hand down eight hundred and eighty steps to a person who has not asked me twice yet.”

## Three Charges And A Number

And she did the sum of the ones she could see, out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, at the eighth hour of the sixteenth, and then she did the one she could not do and she said so out loud rather than leaving it out.

She said, “The twenty-eighth of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four, at the second hour. The second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and fifty-four, at the second hour. And the tenth of this month, at the tenth hour.”

She said, “That is three, and each of them is the width of the little finger on my left hand off the same forearm, and I cannot divide a width into a time and I have never been able to add two counts in different kinds of thing.”

She said, “And there are four hundred years in that wall behind that lamp, and I have got no figure at all for the other ones and I am not going to make one up, and there is no instrument in this building that would give me one.”

She said, “So the whole of what is standing at that table has gone into that lamp, on a day the room chose, at a rate no hand has ever written down, and the only person alive who could tell me the figure is me and I have been in this room ninety-six days and I have not seen it.”

## Twelve Days Of Six Terms

And on the seventeenth of that month she worked out the other half of it, and it is the half that is about her, and she said it out loud standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, at the seventh hour.

She said, “On the twenty-first of the month beginning two thousand one hundred and fourteen I agreed with a voice in a ground with two hundred people in it to keep a record of me, and there were six terms and there is no seventh, and I have not softened one of them and I am not going to.”

She said, “And the fourth one is that he says it out loud as he goes, every line, before it is a line anywhere else.”

She said, “And that is the price. That is the whole of what the six terms cost and it is the same price as the lamp.”

She said, “The lamp costs a hand, and the lamp does not decide anything, and the hand comes off it afterwards. The record costs me hearing it first, and it does not decide anything, and I do not get to know a thing about myself before it is said.”

She said, “And so there are two instruments in the world that write down what a thing is and are not allowed to decide what it is, and I have one of each, and one of them is a person at a table four miles above a mountain and one of them has been with me for nine years, and I did not go looking for a second one and both of them arrived.”

## The Worst Thing About It

And then she said the last of it out loud on the eighteenth, standing, in the ordinary voice, and it is the reversal and she did not see it coming until she had it in her mouth.

She said, “It does not cost me anything.”

She said, “That is what four days of a row has taught me and it took me four days to learn it and seven days of measuring to be sure of it. A thing said out loud in this room costs the person who writes it, and the person it is about does not pay a thing, and the saying is free, and I have been free in this room for ninety-nine days and I did not know it.”

She said, “And there is no limit on it.”

She said, “I can say a row out loud as many times as I have got days for, and every one of them costs that arm a little, and nothing in this room keeps count of how many times I have done it, and no one will stop me, and nobody will be angry, and there is not one part of this I am going to be able to stop myself doing.”

She said, “And that is the worst thing anybody has ever handed me, and it was not handed, it was worked out, in four days, off a forearm, by me.”

## The Sums

She did them standing at the near end of that table before she sat down, and she had a count of a hundred with the last of it and she used it.

She said, “Two thousand four hundred less one thousand five hundred and fifty-nine is eight hundred and forty-one days, and the figure on the strip under that table is five days, seven hours and twelve minutes out of date, and there is not one person in this building who has any idea that either of those numbers is moving.”

She said, “Fifty-eight less thirty is twenty-eight and twenty-eight divided by two is fourteen and the fourteenth day from this one is the first of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and fourteen, and I have said that sum out loud four times in ten days and it is the same every time and it is going to be the same for a long while yet.”

She said, “And on the first of that month the next forty things come up that stair and I will be in this room and I will not have put a hand on that table once, and I have not worked out what I am going to say out loud that day.”
