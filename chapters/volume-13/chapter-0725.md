# No Oil In The Building

She got onto the table again on the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,294 and went along the top of it on her hands and knees into the light, four feet up, with a hard floor under her the whole way, and she did that because there is no other way to get at a lamp in a cage on a shelf at the far end of a table that is made for arms longer than hers.

## Going Along The Top Of It

Twenty-two paces along the wood on her hands and knees, and the grain of it under her palms, and the light coming up on her from the far end the whole way, and the lamp getting bigger.

She had her breath at nine standing still and she kept it there the whole of the twenty-two paces, and there is no rail on the top of a table and there has never been one.

The shelf is a plank let into the wall at the height of her own chest when she is kneeling, and the lamp in the cage stands on it, and the cage is iron with four bars round it and a ring at the top of them and a collar round the belly of it.

## Twenty-Two Paces Into The Light

She got up onto the table at the fifth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 2,294 and went along the top of it on her hands and knees, and it took her a count of two hundred and forty, and that is the first time in nineteen years she has put her weight on a thing that was made for a person with arms longer than hers and found that it held.

There is no rail on the top of a table. There has never been one.

The light off the lamp comes toward her down the length of it the whole way, and it came onto her hands first and then onto her face, and by the twentieth pace she could see the far wall and the shelf and the cage in it plain, and by the twenty-second she was kneeling in front of it.

She said, out loud, on her knees, at arm’s length from the cage, in the ordinary voice:

“I have been in a room for twenty days being unable to reach anything and I have got to the end of it in a count of two hundred and forty and the end of it is a lamp with nothing in it.”

## What She Looked For And Did Not Find

She went through that cage the way she goes through everything, one item at a time, and she said each one out loud as she got to it, standing on her knees with her face against the bars.

She said, “Four bars and a ring at the top of them and a collar round the belly and a plank under it. That is the whole of what that lamp is made of.”

She said, “No dish. No bowl. No reservoir. No wick. No wick-hole. No key. No little door in the side of it. No spout, and I know what a spout is because there is one on the oil lamp on the fourth landing of this tower and I have watched it every day for two years.”

She said, “No flask, no jar, no bottle, no can and no stone of anything on the shelf it is standing on, and the shelf is a plank let into the wall and there is not one mark on it where a thing has ever stood.”

She said, “And there is nothing behind the light. I put my hand in behind it through the bars at the back and there was wall.”

She put her hand in behind it again to be sure, and it was wall.

She said, “That is the whole of it and I have got it down in a count of a hundred and it took me twenty days to be able to say it because I could not get to it.”


## What Is Not In It

She put her face against the bars and looked into it for a count of a hundred.

There is no dish under it. There is no bowl and there is no reservoir and there is nothing in the belly of the cage to hold anything. There is no wick and no wick-hole and no key and no little door in the side of it and no spout.

There is nothing in that lamp to put anything into. There is nothing in it to take anything out of.

She put her hand flat on the shelf and turned her head about so that she could see the whole of the belly of that cage from the side, and there was nothing on the shelf either, no flask and no jar and no bottle and no stone of oil and no can.

She said it out loud with her face against the bars, in the ordinary voice:

“There is nothing to put in this lamp and there is nothing in it to have been put in, and I have looked at it from the side and there is no place on this shelf where a thing that feeds a lamp has ever stood.”

And she stopped there, because there is no second half of that to say, and she is the only person in this building who has said it.

## A Flame That Does Not Lean

And then she did the thing she does, which is to hold a thing still long enough to watch it, and she put her open hand flat on the shelf at the side of the cage and moved it in and out and in and out over a count of sixty.

The flame did not lean.

There is a draught in that room. The door has been standing open the width of a person since the seventh of this month and the whole of the outside of a mountain is on the other side of it, and a flame in this cage that is not being fanned would have gone over every time her hand came past it.

It did not go over once in sixty counts.

She said, “This does not lean.”

And she sat back on her heels on the table four feet above the floor of a room at the top of eight flights and eight hundred and eighty steps and worked out what she had just been looking at for four days, which is a light that is standing in a cage with nothing behind it and is not being blown.

## The Line Cut In The Wall

Behind and a hand’s breadth to the left of that lamp there is a short line cut into the stone of the wall, about as long as her hand, cut deep and old and with the same cut in it as the edge of the table.

The light in the cage falls on that wall and the bottom of the bright place it makes is above the line, and it is not much above it.

She put her own fingers against it and worked out how far the bottom of the light was above that cut, and it is a hand’s breadth and a little over, and she has no instrument for what that means and she is not going to make one up out of a guess.

She said it out loud with her hand against the wall, once, and then she left it alone.

## The Lamp Four Miles Down This Mountain

And then, with her knees on the top of a table in the light, she thought about the other lamp, and it took her a count of a hundred and she had to put it into words afterwards because it is the only thing in this room that has a person in it.

There is a lamp in a cage on the fourth landing of this tower, and it is fed with oil out of a stone jar under a bench on a copying floor with four hundred volumes on the shelves, and there has been no oil in this building for nine years and a day and a half of it went out on the third of this month, on the last of it, about the ninth hour.

And there is a boy of sixteen four miles down this mountain who has been keeping that one going for two years and who said, on the sixth of this month, that he would keep it going after she stopped asking, and she did stop asking, and he was right, and it went out.

That is the whole of what she thought about, and she did not say one word of it out loud.

She said, standing up on the table with her arm open and the light on her face, in the ordinary voice:

“There is one thing in this building I have never said out loud and it is about a boy at the bottom of this tower and an oil jar, and I am not going to say it here, and I am not going to improve on that later either.”

## What The Lamp Is Doing

And then she got off the far side of the table onto the floor and stood in front of the thing that is burning.

It is burning now, and it was burning on the third of this month when the oil four miles down this mountain ran out, and it has been burning every hour of every day since, and there is no oil and there is no wick and there is nothing behind it and it does not lean in a draught.

She said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, at the sixth hour of the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 2,294, standing at the far end of a long table in a room with a person in it:

“So it is not fed out of a jar. A jar runs out in a day and a half and the lamp at the top of this stair has been going for thirty days and there is nothing in this building to feed it with.”

She said, “And there is a person standing at that table who has been standing at it for four hundred years, and I have watched that person for twenty days and the only thing they have done that I can see is stand at the near edge of that table with both hands on it.”

She said, “And that is all I am going to say about how this lamp is lit, standing, in daylight, in this room, in front of the person who is standing at it.”

She went and sat down on the floor at the near end with her back against the doorframe.

The light went on not leaning.
