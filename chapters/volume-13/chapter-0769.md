# The First Look

She said a row out loud at the seventh hour of the seventeenth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and seventy-four, standing at the near end of that table with her strip of pale wood on the wood in front of her, and the head at the far end of it came down over what she had said, and it was the first look she had ever had at anything of hers.

## What She Said Out Loud So That The Hand Could Hear It

She got herself up onto the near end of that table at the seventh hour with her left hand and her weight on the arm that was closed that day and she went along the top of it on her hands and knees, twenty-two paces, and knelt on the shelf in front of that lamp with the strip flat on the wood.

The light was thirty of her own hand below the top of that cage. She put her hand flat on the strip, a foot long and two inches across, pale, grain running up, face up, with a head at one end of it and a column cut into it.

She said it out loud, kneeling on that shelf in the light, in the ordinary voice, because the rule in this building is that the hand writes what a person said and nothing else, and a hand cannot be given anything and has to be told:

“The head of this row is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes. That is what I cost. I said it out loud at two posts on a road at the first hour of the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four and it came up that stair in an armful and a hand wrote it here.”

She said, “And the column under that head has the same figure in it in the first pair of cuts, put there by the hand at that table on the first of the month beginning two thousand two hundred and ninety-four out of words I said in the open at two posts a long way from this building.”

She said, “And the second pair of cuts down under that one has five in it, in my own hand, written on the twenty-second of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four at the ninth hour with a nib and a cake of ink against my fourth rib.”

And then she stopped and did not say anything else, because she had said out loud every word of it before it was a word anywhere else and there was nothing left to add.

## The Head Comes Down Over It

The hands at the near edge of that table came off the wood.

They went along the top of the table to the strip and they did not pick it up and they did not turn it over. The head came down over it the way it comes down over the forty boards that go up that stair on the first of every month, and it stayed there.

She was on that shelf on her knees with her right arm open in front of her and her left hand flat on the wood and she did not move.

She has not been able to stand still in this room for a count of eleven since the fifth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, and she worked out on that day that the reason is that being looked at is what holds a person still and there was not one thing in this building looking at her.

Something had its attention on the shelf she was kneeling on.

Her weight went onto the outside of one foot and she did not put it back. Her breath went off nine on the fourth count and she got it back and it went off again and she got it back again. And she stood on the shelf of a four-foot table with the head of a person bent over a piece of wood a foot long, and she did not go anywhere at all.

It was about eleven seconds, which is the same figure it has always been, and neither has hers moved in four hundred years.

And then the head came up and the hands went back to the near edge of that table and stood there on the wood exactly as they had been standing.

She stayed on that shelf.

There is a thing a person can do on a stone shelf four feet off the floor with a lamp burning at the other end of it and a room that has just done the only thing it does, and what she did was stay exactly where she was and count.

She counted it again with her breath and it did not go off nine on the fourth count. It did not go off at all. She went through it twice standing on her knees with her hand flat on the wood and the grain of it under her palm and the light coming up on her from the far end of the table.

Then she said a word out loud, on her knees, at the top of that table, and there was no one in the room to hear it, because she has never once in nineteen years let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

And the word was the one she has been trying to get at since the fifth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, and it took her three goes and on the third go it came out whole.

Being looked at is what holds a person still.

And then she stopped, because the head at the far end of that table had come up, and there was nothing left in the room to be looked at.

## What Was Written On The Board

Nothing happened for a count of ninety.

Then the hands went along the top of the table again, past her, to the middle of it, and came back with a board.

It came out of the stack of forty that lies face up in the dark under the middle of that table. She heard it come out and she could not see where it came out of, and it was put down on the top of the table at the near end of it, in front of her, face up.

The light from the lamp at the far end came down that table and there was a stroke and a figure on it.

She got her hand on it and turned it round so the grain ran the same way as the grain of the other one and she could read it with her fingers, kneeling on that shelf with the light behind her.

There is a head at one end of it. It is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes.

And under that head there is a column cut into the wood and there is one figure in it, in the first pair of cuts, and it is ten days, twenty-one hours and thirty-six minutes again.

And there is not one stroke in the second pair of cuts.

She read it three times with her fingers and then she got herself back along the top of that table on her hands and knees and got down off the near end onto the floor and stood four feet off it and looked at it lying on the wood where she had left it.

## Two Of The Same Thing

She went back under the table after that and lay on the stone with a cross-beam across her shoulders and put her hand into the stack of forty and turned them over one at a time with three fingers and a thumb, face up, flat, the way she has done since the second of the month beginning two thousand three hundred and twenty-four.

She could not find the one the board had come out of.

She said out loud, lying on the flat of her back under that table with her arm open on the stone beside her:

“There are forty boards in this stack and two of them are mine by my own count and I have never been able to tell which two and I have just found out there are three.”

She said, “The one with the price on it that went in on the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four. The one I wrote five on at the tenth hour of the eighteenth of that month and put back under the shelf, and that came back off that stair empty and I could not see it go. And the one that is lying on the top of that table with the price on it again, that no one in this building has read and that I read with my fingers in the light coming the wrong way up the wood.”

She said, “And I cannot tell them apart and there is not one instrument in this building or in this county that would tell two pieces of pale wood apart, and it is going to be four days until two of them go up that stair and I am not going to be able to see which two.”

And she got herself out from under that table and stood at the near end of it and put her left hand flat on the strip of pale wood that was still lying on the top of it where she had left it, and she did not pick it up and she did not put it back under there.

## The Part She Cannot Work Out

She said out loud, standing, in daylight, at the ninth hour of the seventeenth, and she was careful to say all of it before she said any of the rest of it:

“There is a new board in this room with my price at the head of it and one figure in the column and the second pair of cuts empty.”

She said, “And the other board with my price on it, that the room wrote on the twelfth of the month beginning two thousand four hundred and forty-four, has been in that stack since that morning, and there are two boards in this building with the same figure on them and they are the only two things in this room that are the same.”

She said, “And both of them go up that stair on the first of the month beginning two thousand five hundred and four in an armful, in ten minutes, and I will not be able to see either of them go and I will not be able to correct either of them.”

And then she got to the part she had been standing in front of since the seventh hour and could not get round, and she said it flatly and she said it without stopping.

She said, “I do not know whether it saw the five.”

She said, “And there is not one instrument in the world that will ever tell me. A room does not report what it looked at. A room reports what it wrote, and what it wrote is a price, and I have been a price in this building for a hundred and eighty-nine days and this morning I found out what that is worth.”

And she stood there for a count of a hundred and forty with her hand on the edge of the near end of that table and did not say one word about it, because she has not got a way of saying it yet and she is not going to make one up on the same day she has it.
