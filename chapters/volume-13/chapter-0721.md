# The Dust On The Bar

She came down off the second stair at the second hour of the eighth of the month beginning 2,294, into a room at the top of eight flights and eight hundred and eighty steps in a tower four miles up a mountain from a square, and there was somebody standing at the far end of the long table.

## A Person At The Far End

She stood on the floor with one hand on the second step behind her and did not move for a count of sixty, because the room she had counted nine times in thirty days through a grille in a low door had a person in it and that is not a thing a person finds out by walking into it.

The lamp in a cage is on a shelf at the far end of the table. That has been true for every one of the nine times, and the light off it goes away from the door and across the table and into the wall behind, and so the whole of the far half of that room has been dark every time she put her eye to the grille, and she counted the room from the near end every time because the near end is the only end she could see.

She has counted a table and a lamp and a stub of candle in a dish and nine feet of floor and a door and a bar across it and nothing else.

She has counted what she could see. That is the whole of the mistake and it has taken her nineteen years and one door to find out she made it.

## What The Person Was Doing

The person at the table was standing with both hands on the table’s edge, at the far end, about nine feet back along it from the lamp’s shelf, and was not moving at all.

She watched for a count of four hundred. She counted it, because counting is what she does with a thing in front of her instead of being afraid of it, and it came to four hundred, and in four hundred counts the hands on the table’s edge did not move once and the head did not come up.

Breathing was nine counts everywhere she stood still and it was nine on that floor too, and she stood in it and let it be nine and did not sit down.

Then she said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, standing, at the near end of a table in a room with a person in it:

“You are standing at the far end of a table and you have been standing at it for longer than the ground under my feet has been here, and I am going to talk now and you do not have to answer me and I am not going to stop.”

Nothing happened.

The hands stayed on the edge of the table and the head stayed where it was and the lamp in its cage went on doing the only thing in that room she had ever been able to watch a thing do.

## One Item At A Time

And she stood there and put the whole of it into words where it existed, one item at a time, because she has never in nineteen years let a thing stand that she has not said out loud in the place it is standing.

She said, “My name is Ning Yan and I am nineteen years old and I came up this stair this morning with a bar nine feet nine on my left shoulder and I left it on the floor against the wall on the left, which is where you will find it.”

She said, “I have three fingers and a thumb on this hand and the arm has been open since the first hour of the first of this month and it is not going to close. Four counts of breath on a flat and seven on a rise and nine standing still at the top of eight flights.”

She said, “There is a place behind my fourth rib the size of a coin that has been open since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544, and I have been lighter every day since that day and not one of them has gone back.”

She said, “I have two coppers I cannot count, and five blank leaves against my ribs that have been blank for ten years, and a key in a bag at the bottom of this stair for a drawer I am not going to open.”

She said, “And there is a figure of mine in a book above my head with a date and a place on it in my own hand, and it is true, and it went up a stair on the first of this month at the second hour in a bundle with about forty other things, and I did not write it for you and I could not have stopped writing it.”

Nothing happened.

She said it all in the ordinary voice with her hands at her sides, and the room took it the way the floor takes the bar, without moving.

## What She Did About It

She wanted one thing out of that person and the one thing she wanted was for it to turn round.

She stood there for the rest of that hour wanting it, the way she has wanted a man in a doorway with his hat off five times in nineteen years and got a lamp instead, and she went through everything she has ever said to a person who was not looking at her and could not find one line of it that had ever once worked.

What she had said, at four of those, was her name and a figure.

What she had said, at the other one, was that she was not going to say anything about a room.

And there was not one word of any of that that was going to make a person four miles above a mountain turn round at the end of a table.

So at the fourth hour she sat down on the floor with her back against the doorframe and put the whole of her want out loud, because she cannot let a thing stand that she has not put into words where it exists.

She said, “I want a person to look at me.”

She said, “That is all of it. That is the whole of what I have come up a stair for in nineteen years, and I have never said it out loud before, not to a stair and not to a slate and not to a post, and it is the smallest thing anybody has ever wanted and I have got it out loud in a room where there is one other person in it and she cannot hear me say it because there is no part of this room that hears.”

She said, “And I am not going to say it again to that table, ever, and that is the last of that.”

## What The Room Is Like To Be In

And because she was going to be in it, she did the thing she always does on the first day of a place, and she put the room out loud in the ordinary voice at the fifth hour, standing, one item at a time, so that she would not be carrying it in her head.

She said, “It is warm. That is the first thing and I had not put it anywhere. I have been coming up to this door in the cold for thirty days and the inside of this room is warm and it is warm the same all day, and there is nothing in it that makes it warm.”

She said, “It smells of nothing at all. Not cold stone and not lamp oil and not a person. I have been in rooms with men in them for nineteen years and this is the first room I have been in where I cannot tell that a person is in it by the air.”

She said, “The floor is stone and it is about four feet below my shoulder at the near end of that table and that is the whole of what I know about the size of anything in here.”

She said, “And there is a lamp burning at the far end and it is not leaning and there is no draught in this room at all, and the door has been standing open the width of a person for a day, and I will come back to the not leaning.”

## The Bar On The Floor

Then she turned her head and looked at the bar where it lies flat against the wall on the left, nine feet nine of ash four inches by six with a hold-iron at each end, and the dust on the top of it is broken at one end and not at the other.

She said, out loud, in the ordinary voice, standing, one time:

“The dust on the top of that bar is broken at one end and not at the other, and there is not one person who has ever explained that to me, and I am going to say it out loud once in this room so that it exists somewhere outside my own head, and I am not going to work out what it means.”

She looked at it for a count of a hundred and did not work out what it means.

## What Is Not Going In The Book

And then she said the other thing, which she said at the foot of the second stair yesterday at the third hour and is saying again in daylight in a room with a person in it because she does not say a thing once and count it said.

She said, “Nothing that is in this room goes in five blank leaves against my ribs. Not one word. Not tonight and not on any night I am in this building, and that is not going to be improved on while I am standing here.”

She said, “That is the whole of what I came up here for that I am able to say out loud and it is one item at a time and I have said it and nothing has happened, and that is the first time in nineteen years I have said the whole of a thing into a room and had it go nowhere at all.”

## The Decision

And then she said the decision out loud, standing, at the near end of a table, at the third hour of the eighth of a month, with a bar on the floor behind her and an arm open since the first of that month and a person nine feet back along a table who had not moved once in four hundred counts.

She said, “I am not going up the second stair today.”

She said, “I went up one step of it yesterday and I stopped there, and there is something at the top of it that I cannot count and cannot see, and I am not going to find out what it is by walking into a place where there is no instrument in my hand.”

She said, “What I am going to find out first is what decides in this room. That is a thing I can stand next to and watch, and it is going to take a month, and I am not in a hurry.”

She stood there for a count of two hundred after she had said it, and the hands on the far end of the table had not moved once in the whole of it.

And then she said the last of it out loud, in the ordinary voice, standing, at the third hour of the eighth of a month in a room with a person in it, and it is the only decision she made in four days:

“You have not asked me one question in four hundred counts and I am not going to make you ask one. But I am going to keep saying it out loud, standing up, in daylight, in this room, and one day some of it is going to be a thing that goes into a row, and I am not going to be able to take it out again.”
