# Ledger For A Crossing

The arithmetic started on the seventeenth and it took her four days, and it is worth setting down because every part of it was true and not one part of it changed anything.

There are two books. One of them ends on the seventeenth of a month that is two months back and the other one has three empty dates in it, and there is not one stretch of continuous work in either of them on nine days of road.

There are five stubs in an oilcloth against her ribs, with five marks on them, and not one of the five can be compared with either book, and a man at a desk would have to hold two books and five papers and decide which of them he believed, and there is no version of a rule about hours that lets a man do that and be honest about it.

There is a licence in the same oilcloth with a ceiling on the front of it at Foundation the first stage, and she has never touched it, and the seven conditions are on the back of it and are not re-listed anywhere, and the claim is the seventh and the last thing on the paper.

And there is a quarter of a mile of ground past the cart turn that is on nobody's book and nobody's job and paid worst, and it is a foot and a bit deep on the low side all the way along, and it is quiet, and the quiet is because there is nobody out there.

## What Does Not Follow From Two Books

She went at it from four directions and it came out the same every time.

Hours in order with dates do not cross a boundary no county paper crosses. That is not a rule anybody made and it is not a thing she can argue with, because the countersignature that put her at Foundation the first stage came from an office past the boundary and it came on a schedule read aloud out of a book by a clerk in a room she was not in the middle of, and the schedule was the wages of a stretch she worked on this reach at six coppers a day, and it is the only instrument the world has ever given her and it is made of one thing.

A continuous run. Her hours, in one hand, in order, with dates.

And a run is a thing two books cannot make, and she took them apart herself, on purpose, in daylight, at a trestle, in the time it takes a man to turn a bar over, and she has not asked for it to be put back together and she is not going to.

The other direction is the same.

The one instrument that has never been spent on a stage, or on a name, or on a man she needed to find, is sitting in the place where she keeps the things she has not decided about. And it is the only thing in her life that has ever moved the machinery itself rather than moving her through it, and it is hers to order and his to hold only on her word, and the word has a price and the price has to be said before the word.

She got as far as that on the seventeenth and stopped, and did not go near the ground again for two days.

## The Ticket

She keeps the count on the back of a dead weight ticket.

Four marks. It has been four marks for a while and it has been going down on its own for about three months and she is the only person in the world who is keeping it, and she has not spent one of them since the tenth of the month before.

The rest is written on the same tickets in the same hand with the flat of a second finger: eleven holds that hold to the end of a stroke and about fifty that do not; the four stage tests; the cost of the fifth, which is a name struck out of a wage-book in a foreman's own hand; the cost of the sixth, which is a hand flat on a board and nothing written in it; the cost of the seventh, which is three empty dates and nine men who stood a stretch for nothing.

She writes those out because they are hers and because a thing that costs a person is a thing that ought to be written down by somebody, and there has never been anybody else.

There are three dead weight tickets in the oilcloth now and two of them are full and one of them has the ration on the back of it, and the Clay Plate is under all of them against her chest, and it has been there for four years and it is the only thing she owns that was put in a sealed place and let out.

She has never told him that he is the only other person in the world who knows any of it, and he has never asked, and the not-asking is the closest thing to a kindness between them and neither of them calls it that.

## The Word Is Not Said

She did not order it on the twentieth, and she did not order it on the twenty-first, and on the twenty-second at about the fourth hour, at the trestle, before she picked the bar up, she spent one of the four.

She had not spent one since the tenth of the month before and she did not want to go on to the bad flat without knowing what was there, and he is the only thing in her life that reads anything at all, and the four marks do not last as far as the day that is in front of her if she spends at the rate she has been spending at.

She let one go and it bought her exactly this, in the ordinary voice, in her head:

“Say it and I hold it as long as you want it held.”

She stood at the trestle with the bar in her hand and about ten coppers in the other one.

“Not for a stage,” she said.

“No,” he said.

“Not today.”

“You know the day.”

## Fear

The fear came on the twenty-second at about the seventh hour and it did not go away again that night.

It was not of the bad flat. She has been afraid of worse ground for four years and she is seven stages past Condensing and the strongest body on nine days and she has a left hand that has to build itself before a bar lands, and she will take that ground if somebody will put her on it.

She was afraid of spending him on purpose.

She did it once before and it was eleven months ago in a counting room in a county nine days west and it cost eight days out of ten of a man who had about ten days, and she did it with no ceremony and nobody else in the room and she has not been sure since that day whether it was a purchase or a theft, and the difference between the two turned out to be what she felt about it and not what it cost.

That was a name. A name she had bought and had got and that had pointed her at a room at Stony Ford and a hand that wrote a date against a standing claim, and it had answered nothing and had cost eight days and she had used it anyway.

This time there is no name at the end of it. There is a quarter of a mile of quiet ground on nobody's book at the end of it, and if it works then she will be somewhere the layer can read, and if it does not work then she will be a woman who stood in a field for nothing four days past the cart turn with not one line of it on any board on earth.

And it comes out of him.

That is the sentence she has been turning over since the seventeenth, and she turned it over about nine times on the twenty-second and it came out the same every time.

Every stage she has taken in four years has come out of her own body. The base went in, the breath went from three to four, the order went out of the left hand, the heel went, a name went out of a wage-book in a foreman's own hand.

And the one thing she would be spending him on is the one thing that has not come out of her body at all, and it comes out of him, and it comes out of the foundation she is standing on while the foundation is still being built.

She is not asking whether it is fair, because fair is a word that needs two people in it and she has been using this one on purpose since a sentence she kept back once a year stopped being a thing she said out loud. That is not forgiveness. It is a purchase, and the account is still carrying, and it will be carrying at the end of the book.

She is asking, at about the seventh hour of the twenty-second, with her boots off in a hut past the last stake, how much of him there is going to be on the far side of it — and the answer is that she does not know, and that she is going to do it anyway, and that she did it once before in a counting room and came out the other side of that as well.

Which is worse.

That is the fear, and it took about nine minutes to get hold of and it took the rest of the night, and it is not a reason. It is a thing she went and looked at and put down and picked up about four times before the light came.

## What She Decided

She decided it on the twenty-second and she did not say the word, and the price is still unsaid, and it will be said at a trestle in daylight before she says anything else.

The day she chose was the fifth of the next month, which is thirteen days off, and the reason she chose it is arithmetic and not temper: twenty-eight days from the seventh, the base turns again on the seventh of that month, and she wants the crossing inside the turn and not outside it, because a base that is moving is a base that will carry anything she can put into it and will not carry anything at all if she tries to hold it still.

She has never hired onto ground nobody is paid to record. She is going to say her name out loud at a trestle and ask for the worst ground on nine days, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, and put her hand on the standing and say one word, and then she is going to be where the layer reads.

That is the whole of the decision and it was made in her own head with nobody in the room, and she has not told the sub-contractor, and she has not told the store, and she has not told the woman with the cart, and she has not told him either, because there is nothing to tell him until she says the word and the price comes out of her.

At about the seventh hour on the twenty-third she walked past the last stake with the bar over her shoulder and stood on the rise for about nine minutes looking at the ground beyond the cart turn, and came back and put the bar against the hut and went and slept.

Across the ground between the trestle and that rise, about two hundred yards off, there was a fire going at the course's own trestle, and a man standing at it in a plain coat with a notebook shut against his side, not writing.
