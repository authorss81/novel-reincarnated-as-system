# The Noise In The Ground

She found it on the seventeenth of the month, on the low side, at about the fourth hour, with a bar in her hands and about two feet of the stuff under it.

The bar goes under and comes up and the ground makes a noise while the bar is moving.

That is not a thing she had ever noticed in eleven days of standing in the worst ground on nine days, and she noticed it on the seventeenth because she stopped for about a second and a half to get her breath on the pull and stood still with the bar in the hole.

The ground kept making it.

She stood still with a bar in a hole in the ground at the fifth hour of a working morning and the ground made a noise under her hands for about as long as it takes to count to eleven and then it stopped.

Not faded. Stopped.

## What It Was Not

It was not a loud sound. That is the first thing to say about it and it is the thing that took her two days to get past.

A loud sound is one you turn your head for. This was low, under the bar and inside the ground and up the inside of the arm, and it had no edge on it, and it was about the volume of a man breathing hard four yards off in a hut, and if eleven men had been standing on that ground at the fourth hour she does not think one of them would have heard it.

She put the bar back in and pulled and it came again, and she stood still again and it came again.

## The Word For It

She asked, on the second day, at the trestle, and she asked it in the ordinary way, the way a person asks for the name of a tool.

She asked the man with the broken thumbnail first and he looked at her and said, “That is a loud stretch.”

“I have heard it at the ring.”

“That is a loud stretch,” he said.

And that was the whole of what either of them said about it, and he went and oiled a hinge.

## The Word For It, Again

The girl of nineteen said it first, on the third day, without being asked, because she came past at the fifth hour and stopped for about six feet of it because there was a bar across the path she was using, which is not a reason to stop.

“Forty is loud,” she said.

“Forty,” said Ning Yan.

“The thirty-nine above it is worse. You will find out.”

She was right. She was right about that as she is right about a pace.

She was also faster than Ning Yan in that ground and had been since the second week of the month before last, and the difference between the two of them is not strength.

It is never strength. Nobody on that course has ever thought it was strength, including the woman who has been standing in it.

## Why It Is Not Strength

A bar has to be turned over at the top of the pull, in one movement, and the turn has to be there before the arm knows it is going, because the ground is wet to about two feet and if the bar comes up flat the wet under it has nothing to hold to and it comes off in the foot and the boot goes.

It is a thing about timing and the timing is a thing a body knows.

Ning Yan had a body that could carry a hundred and seventy quarry loads in thirty-four days and it took her forty minutes to do eight yards that a girl of nineteen did in eight, and neither of them had ever said one word to the other about that and neither of them was going to.

That is not a thing anybody on a course argues about. It is a thing a foreman puts on a board and a woman does not put her hand up for.

## What The Thirty-Nine Was

The thirty-nine is a stretch about four hundred yards long on the low side, and the low side of the thirty-nine is a cut bank with a soft floor under it and a lip at the top, and the lip is where it wants to come over.

That is the part nobody can explain to a person who has not stood in it. It is a foot of grey stuff, wet all the way down under the grey, and it holds a boot exactly as much as it holds a stone, which is to say not at all. Your weight goes through the grey into the wet and the wet takes the boot, and if your weight goes forward the boot stays where it is and the leg keeps going.

Nobody falls in it. That is the thing nobody tells you. It is not deep enough to fall in and it will not hold a person up at all.

So the whole of working it is putting your weight over the pull. That takes about forty minutes to learn and about a year to stop thinking about, and she had done about four hundred yards of it in two months and was still thinking about it.

## The Sixteen Days

She was on the low side of the thirty-nine and the low side of the forty for sixteen days, which is a fortnight and a bit, and she did not get a bar's worth of it down and nobody expected her to.

The parts of it she did get down dried in an hour and looked exactly like the ground they came from, which is why nobody from outside has ever caught anybody.

And for sixteen days she kept something.

She did not write it down. There was nowhere to write it down that was not the back of a dead weight ticket and she already had three tickets with a ration on one of them and a stage's shape on another, and a person can only put so much on a card and still read the card.

She kept it in her head, which is a thing she had done four hundred times in four years and had never once done in the field.

Two of those sixteen days were ordinary days with nothing in them at all, when the ration did not come and she did not go looking for it, and the four that she went out on were at the fourth hour with about nine hundred yards of bank in front of her and eleven men a quarter of a mile off on the high side.

## What She Kept

What the board said, against what she remembered of what nine counties had heard.

Not what they had heard of her. What they had heard, in order, over four years, out loud, in the words of whoever read it out, at the moment it happened.

She was the only one. There is a person in this world who was inside a bound when every word of it was said, and who stood in a foot of the residue on the far side of nine days of road, and those two people are the same woman and she was standing in the drift with a bar in her hands.

So: what the board said.

She did it the way she used to do an audit, at night, on a table, with a wedge of paper, except that this time the table was three boards in a bunk and the wedge of paper was in her own head and there was nobody to read it out to.

The loud stretches are the stretches nearest the inside end of the working reach.

The worst of them is the worst of them, and it is the worst of them because it is the one that has been worked worst, and the schedule for it is three seasons old and eleven men, and they are right about that and have been right about that since the trestle in the second week.

And going out along the reach from the inside end, it thins.

And the far end of the working reach is not thin.

It is quiet.

She had been putting a bar into the far end of the reach since the second week of a month that is now three months back, one stretch at a time, as the work came, and she had never once in all that time put a bar into a foot of anything at the far end, because there has never been a foot of anything at the far end. There is half a hand's depth in two places and it does not ring and it does not even want to.

## The One Sentence

She said it to herself once, on the second of the month, at the fourth hour, standing at the edge of the quietest ground on nine days with a bar she had not needed to bring.

**The quiet is where there is nobody.**

That is the whole of what she said and she did not build anything on it and she did not go back to it for another three days.

There is a difference between a thing that has been found and a theory, and she built three theories in four years and two of them were wrong and one of them was about a man in her head, and she knows what a theory costs and she knows exactly what hers would cost here.

It cannot be proved. That is the first thing.

She would have to say out loud, to a foreman or a tender or eleven men, that the ground under a line of work gets louder the more has been said, and there is not one instrument in nine days of road that would show it, because the only control in the world is a woman standing in a different place with a different number of things in her.

She would have to be a licensed one explaining what is in her head to a man with a broken thumbnail who has never asked her a question since the day she came out here.

She kept it.

That is not a thing she decided in a room. She decided it in the ring on the eleventh, and she did not decide it because she was afraid, and she thought about it for two days afterwards looking for the fear and did not find it where she expected it.

It is that a fact she cannot prove is worth exactly nothing to a person who is paid by the day, and the moment she said it out loud she would have stopped being a hand on a board and become a licensed one with an opinion, and in a place where people are paid by the day the two of those do not both get to happen.

She did not say it to the foreman and she did not say it to the tender and she did not offer to fix anybody's schedule.

And she did not say it to the voice, because he cannot read a board, and the only thing he could have done with it was spend something she had not got, and she would have had to pay for it out of a column.

## What That Left Her With

She was standing in a foot of the residue of her own announcements in the ground of a country that has never heard one.

That is the sentence for it and she did not say it out loud and was never going to say it out loud to anybody.

Nine counties has a word for *servant-born*. Nine counties has a word for *irregular* and a word for *the Chosen One* and a word for the eleventh of a list of eleven designations that two heads hold and no paper does.

There is no word anywhere within nine days of road for a woman whose advancement has leaked into the ground of people who never heard it.

There is not one. She looked for one for two days and there was not one, and the not-finding of one is the worst thing that has happened to her in four years and it is not a thing anybody has done to her.

## The Thing She Said Instead

On the fourteenth of the month she spent one of the ration standing at the fire with eleven men in a ring and the argument going about a man who had done a thing on the thirty-one and got the credit for it.

He said three things. Two of them were about the thirty-one and one of them was about a man who had left.

She had wanted, for about four minutes, to ask the man in her head one question, and the question was a good one, and it was the first good one she had had in about six weeks.

She spent it and it bought three sentences about the thirty-one.

She knew what it had bought while he was saying it.

That is what she got for it: the noise in the ground, and eleven thousand things said in nine counties in four years, and a correlation that only one person alive can compute and that one person alive cannot prove, and eleven words about a man who had left.

She put the ration away for the rest of that day.

She has never said to anybody what the drift is loudest where the most has been said, and there are eleven men and a girl of nineteen and a foreman and a tender inside the working reach and not one of them will ever hear it from her.

And every one of them is right about it. They are right about the schedule and right about where it collects and right about what happens to a stretch that is not kept, and they have been right since a trestle in the second week of a month that is now two months back, and she stood inside the ring and watched the proof arrive in the only form proof ever arrives in.

The tenth part is hers and she cannot show anybody a line of it.
