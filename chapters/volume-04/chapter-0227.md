# The Stretch With No Hire

There is a load of stone that goes past the cart turn and it goes there because the cart turn is the short way and the road round is two days.

It is not a big load. It is two barrows on a handcart and two men on the handles and about a third of a ton between them, and it goes over the rise and past the bad flat and down into a yard on the far side, and the sub-contractor has been running it for nine years.

He sent two of his men over it on the first of the month and she went at the back of the cart because she was there and a person walks beside a cart.

## Over The Rise

It takes about forty minutes from the trestle to the place where the road turns down on the far side, and about a third of that is on ground that belongs to somebody.

The rest of it is not on anybody's plank.

That is the thing she learned on the first of the month, walking beside a handcart with her hand on the side of it and eleven hours of the day already in a book behind her. There is ground between a sub-contractor's last mark and the bad flat that is not a stretch and is not anybody's job and is not on a board, because a board carries stretches and this is not one.

It has drift on it. It is thin, and thick in the two low places, and it goes over a boot in about one place in six and not in the other five.

She looked at it for about a year and a half of her working life, which is about eleven seconds at a walk, and then she looked at the rest of it.

## The Bad Flat

It is three-quarters of a mile and there is no mark on it anywhere.

That is the first thing. A course paints its stretches. A sub-contractor paints his. This has no paint on it, at either end or in the middle, and there is no stake in it and no board, and the only place on nine days where a person can see where a piece of ground stops being anybody's job is where the ground stops being anybody's job.

She saw it whole from the top of the rise at about the fifth hour and did not go down into it.

The drift is a foot and a bit on the low side, all the way along, and it is the thickest drift she has stood next to in four months and she has stood next to it on the worst of the ring, and it is quiet.

That is the second thing and she made herself stand with it for a while before she went on.

It is quiet the way the ground past the last stake is quiet. There is no ring in it. She put the toe of her boot into the low side about a yard in and nothing came back, and the boot came out clean, and there was nothing underneath it afterwards.

She had known that on the third of a month in a field with a bar in her hands and it had taken her a minute and a half to find.

It took her about as long as a person can stand still and did not enjoy a moment of it.

## Where A Boot Takes

She went down into the low side about a hundred yards in and stood in it for about five minutes, which is more than she meant to do and the whole of what she learned about it.

A foot and a bit of quiet ground holds a boot the way a boot holds a man in a ditch, and the thing about it is not the depth. The depth is a foot. The thing about it is that it does not tell you which way it has moved while your weight was on it.

She turned her boot about ten times in about five minutes and the ground answered every time and gave back the same answer, and at about the eighth turn the low side went out from under the outside of her foot about a hand's breadth and she was down on one knee in it before she had decided to be.

She got up in about the time it takes a person gets up.

There is no ring in that ground at all. Under the drift there is nothing that comes back through a boot, and a boot that is going to move does not announce itself, and the whole of the low side's danger is that it is as quiet as a field at the far end of the reach.

There is one place about a fifth of the way along where it has happened before. She did not have to be told about it and she did not ask.

Two men were in the ground about four inches deep and flat out, with a bar laid across the both of them, and the low side came up over the head of the man at the front. The drift had closed over the both of them within a week or two, which is what drift does, and there was a bar laid there still, which is what people do.

Neither of them was nine days old.

There is no board and there is no name and there is no wage paid on that ground and there is no man who stands a stretch on it, and if a thing goes into that low side it is a thing that two men have to find, and they find it by walking, and it takes a day and a half to walk three-quarters of a mile of ground in a straight line.

That is the whole of what is on that flat and she got it standing on a rise at the fifth hour of the first of a month with eleven men and a girl on a course a quarter of a mile behind her.

## The Cart Turn

The road round is two days and the cart turn is forty minutes, and everybody who runs stone to that yard takes the turn, and they take it by going along the high ground on the north side and not by going through the low side.

She did not know that and worked it out from the wheel ruts in about four minutes on the way out.

The ruts on the high ground are four inches deep and two sets and they have been going into the same ruts for nine years. The ruts through the bad flat are shallow and there is only one set and they are not recent.

Somebody went through there. Once. A long time ago, and then somebody came back and took the high ground instead, and everybody who has run stone since has taken the high ground, and nobody has ever written down the reason because there is nothing on that road to write it on.

A course holds a line because people tend it. A road goes round because a man went round it once and told the next man, and that is the same mechanism and it is about four hundred years old.

She was still standing there when the cart came back up, and she got out of the way of it in the ordinary way, and one of the two men looked at her boots and said, “That is the flat,” and she said, “Yes,” and he said, “You are the one off the thirty-nine,” and she said, “I was,” and he nodded at the cart and did not say anything else.

That is the whole of the conversation she has had about that ground with anybody who works on it.

## What It Would Take

She worked it out on the walk back and she worked it out honestly and she did not like any of it.

It is not strength.

That is the thing she had expected to be the thing and it is not. She has got more of it in her right arm than eleven men have between them, and her left hand has the heel of it gone and the order gone out of it and has to be built from nothing before a bar lands, and that is a slow hand and not a weak one.

The shortage was never in what a hand can do. It is in how long a person can stand in the low side and be wrong about it and not go in.

That is a thing that comes back wrong. Four counts, all the time, on the flat and on a grade and in a foot and a bit of ground that is quiet and does not tell you anything about what is underneath it.

And the breath is the whole of it, and the breath has been four counts for a month and three is not coming back. In a foot and a bit of quiet ground a person gets four counts out of a pull and no more, and at the end of a long run of it there is not a fifth count, there is a place where the counting stops being possible and you put the bar down and stand there.

She has done that on a worked stretch four times since the fourth of the month before and she has not done it in front of anybody.

Eleven men on the ring would not be better at this than she is and she has thought about that for eight days and she cannot make it come out any other way, and being clear-headed about it is not the same as being glad about it.

A man who had done this for nine years would be at the end of the bad flat at the sixth hour and would not be on one knee in the low side at the fifth.

And there is a version of this where she goes down there and is on one knee in the low side at the fifth hour, alone, in the dark, three-quarters of a mile past a trestle where a man with a book is going to write her name in a book at the fourth hour the next morning, and nobody walks that ground for a day and a half.

She stood with that on the rise for about four minutes and it did not go away, and then she went back down to the trestle and picked her bar up off the ground where she had put it.

## She Did Not Go On It

She worked the eight days.

The first was the first of the month and she had gone over the rise with a cart and she had stood at the top of it and she had not gone down into it, and the other seven were the same stretch she has worked since the fourth of the month before, at five coppers, eleven hours, the mark of somebody else's stretch on the plank at the trestle.

She put her hours in the book in order with dates on them and she kept the counterfoils in the oilcloth and she built four more holds and lost two of them.

The sub-contractor named it once, on the sixth, when he was talking about where a man goes when he is not here.

“The bad flat is past the cart turn,” he said, “and the only thing anybody has ever come off that is off it, and not one of them could tell you afterwards.”

He said it about the ground and he went on eating.

She did not go on it and she did not ask him what it is worth and she did not ask him what a day on it pays, because the answer to the second of those is that nobody would pay for it, and the answer to the first of those is that he told her on the fourth of the month before last not to be clever at it, and he has not said it since because he does not say anything twice.

She is ashamed of the ground and she is jealous of it and those are the same thing and she has not told anybody either.

It is the worst ground on nine days and it is on nobody's book and no hour she works on it will go into a book anywhere at all, and it is the only ground within nine days of road where that would not matter.

At the seventh hour on the eighth she was standing past the last stake with the bar on her shoulder, looking at the rise, the way she has looked at it about nine evenings running, and she had three days' worth of nothing in her hands and no way to make a run of it out of a thing nobody is paid to record.
