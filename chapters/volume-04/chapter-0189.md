# Member Present

Four miles down in the dark with three counts on the stair and four miles back up it in the last hour of the light, and she had done it twice a week since the middle of the summer and had never once got better at it.

The third flight of the county building is the worst of it. There is no landing on the third flight and the turn at the top of it is a left, and a left at the top of a flight in the deep cold at the third hour of the sixteenth of the month is a thing that goes wrong in the back of the ribs and not in the legs.

She went up it on three counts.

There was a queue in the room and the queue went out into the square, which it does on the sixteenth because the county examines her monthly and every member of this county who wants to see a member examined can stand in it and has been able to since the summer.

She was the ninth in the queue.

## The Man Eight Feet Off

He was not eight feet off.

He was beside her, at about the distance a person stands at a counter when two people are using it, and he had come down the mountain on the same road at the same time and had not said one word about it, and she had told him in a hall about six weeks ago, in front of eight men, that he was not to do this any more.

There is nothing about that in a county. There is no rule, and the county keeps no paper on it, and nothing in that building knows or cares where a man stands in a queue.

Nobody in the square looked at the two of them and nobody in the room looked at the two of them.

He did not ask her a question. He had not asked her a question since a cold end in the middle of the autumn and he did not ask her one now, and he stood at a counter in a queue in the deep cold for about forty minutes because a man who has decided he is going to stand somewhere does not afterwards require a reason for it.

She could hear him breathing.

That was the whole of him. Eleven minutes of her hand in front of a room full of people who did not shuffle, and one man four feet off her left shoulder breathing at the ordinary rate of a man of twenty-two who has walked eight miles in a day.

## The Counter

The young clerk took her sheet and looked at the date on it and then looked at the board behind him, which has the month on it in chalk in a hand that is not his.

“Sixteenth,” he said. “You are the ninth.”

“I am the ninth.”

He wrote the attendance in and turned the county column round.

It was a long sheet folded in three and the crease of it was soft, and it had been folded and unfolded twice a year for nineteen years, and she had stood at the side of that table and signed it four times.

Sun He came round from the press with his coat on.

He put his thumb on the column and went down it and stopped at the fourth line and ruled it, and then at the eighth line and ruled it, in the ordinary hand, in the county's own four words, which are short and have been on every form in that office since before he came to it.

*No stage entered for this county at this date.*

Three times, down the margin, beside three gaps.

He did not say it out loud.

She noticed that he did not say it out loud, and she worked out why while she was standing there, and it was because he has said that ruling three times in nineteen months, twice with a man in the room who had said the opposite thing at a public counter in the summer, and there is no fourth sentence in that county that would be better than the first three.

So he ruled the three gaps in the ordinary hand and wrote *Member present* at the foot of the column and dated it and did not add anything at all.

## Eleven Minutes

She put her left hand flat on the table.

The heel of it was not there. It had not been there since the fourth of the month in the middle of the winter and every person in that room could see the shape of what was not there from where they stood, and one man in particular could, and he had said so out loud in a hall to nine men about six weeks ago.

The first figure took about fifty seconds.

She had never timed one and she timed it then. The second took less. The fourth took less than the first by a little. By the seventh her hand had found the drag and stopped needing the decision about it, and the ninth went down in about twenty seconds, and the whole of it came to just under eleven minutes.

The room did not say one word during any part of it.

The clerk did not look at his watch and Sun He read something else.

Nine people in a queue and about six more out in the square stood in a low room with eleven windows in it and watched a woman with no heel on her left hand write nine figures on a county column. Not one of them understood that what they were watching was a thing she was doing because there was no other way to do it, and she would not let anybody else do it for her.

At the end she put the pen down and pushed the sheet two inches toward him.

## The Man At The Back Of The Queue

He had been in the queue the entire time and had come to the table when the signing was done, and he had a sheet of his own out and folded and he had been standing at a distance with his hands loose in front of him.

He put the sheet on the counter and did not open it.

“You have not been at this counter since the nineteenth,” he said.

“No.”

“You bought one certified copy off this county in the whole of the winter and you have not been back for it,” said Bai Zhu. “That is not a complaint. I am telling you what I have done and what I have not done, because I have come to tell you one thing and I would like it understood that I have come for that and not for anything else.”

He put his hand flat on the counter.

“The answer on the shelf by that door is still what it was,” he said. “Four words and a blank. The district has not written in the space. I have been at the counter twice since the nineteenth and asked what the district has said, and both times a clerk has read me the same four words, and one of them read me the space as well, out loud, because I asked him to look at it, and he did, and it is empty.”

“I know it is empty.”

“I am telling you because you did not ask me and I would rather you had it from me than from a shelf,” said Bai Zhu. “I have never asked you a question. I am not going to start now and I am not going to start later, and I have said that in the same breath as this so that you do not have to spend a morning working out whether this is the beginning of something.”

He picked his sheet up and did not open it.

“I have bought nothing at this counter since the certified copy,” he said, “and I have paid for no force, and I have not been up your stair, and the referral is still where it went, and I will tell you what I think it is going to be answered with when it is answered, if you ever want it, and it is going to be the same four words on a different piece of paper.”

“You have never told me what you think the answer will be.”

“No,” said Bai Zhu. “You would have taken it as a thing to argue with and I have watched you argue with a calendar.”

## The Sentence She Wasted

She spent one of the day's four on him, on the way down the four miles, on a question that did not matter at all.

She had known for about two days that she was going to spend it. She had known while she was writing the rule out on the back of a dead ticket, and she had known while she was standing in a hall with no roof being told by nine men that her eleven minutes were worth nothing, and she had gone on knowing it, and she went four miles down a mountain and asked anyway.

“Can you feel it going?”

That was the whole question. Nine words.

“Which of it,” said Yuan.

“The channel. When it is spent. Can you feel the difference between a day you have used and a day you have not used?”

He took a while, and she counted it, and the count was about as long as a sentence she had wanted to say to the man beside her at a counter and had not said to a person who could not hear it.

“Yes,” he said.

“That is all of it?”

“That is all of it,” he said. “I can feel it. It is not like a wound and it is not like being tired and I have been able to feel it since the first night and I have not told you because you never asked and because you would have spent the asking better than this.”

She walked about forty steps on a mountain road in the dark.

“So that is a quarter of a day’s worth of the day gone,” she said. “On nine words.”

“Yes.”

“You could have said no.”

“I could have said no,” said Yuan, “and then you would have spent two sentences on it instead of one, and you would have been angrier, and the answer would have been the same, and I would have got the same amount of nothing. I chose the way that cost the least. Do not write it down as generosity.”

## What She Carried Down The Hill

One sheet with her hand on it, and a column with three ruled gaps in it, and an attendance on the sixteenth that a clerk wrote down because a clerk has to.

The four miles back up took until the light had gone and she did three flights of it on three counts and never got back to two.

There was a room four miles up a hill of a thousand feet with nine men asleep in it and a licence nailed straight over the second bunk and a fire that went down twice between the fourth hour and the light and about a third of a day of a voice gone on nine words at the bottom of a mountain.

She got up the last of the stair on three counts and went in and fed the fire because it was down, and one of the men at the near end said something about the cold end, and she said something back, and that was the whole of what the crew of the Qinghe furnace had to say about its licensed member going down a mountain and coming back up it on the sixteenth of the month with a sheet in her coat.
