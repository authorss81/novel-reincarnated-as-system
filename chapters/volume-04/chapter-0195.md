# The Working Reach

The third day she left the cart at the turn, where the driver put her down on high ground with the last of his load still shifting, and told her the rest the way he had told her the other things.

“The ground does the rest of it,” he said. “Nine days on foot, if you go at it, and eight if you have a bad night.”

He said it the way a man says a thing about stone.

She had seven days of road left and about four coppers and a tin and a bake wrapped in a cloth, and she went up.

## The Grade

The climb out of the Verge starts in a place where nothing looks like a climb and turns into a climb about half a mile in, without warning and without any change in the trees, and she was in it before she had worked out that she had started.

It is not stairs. That is the thing that had taken her four volumes and it took her three days to learn properly. On a stair the body is told what is coming and answers; on a grade the body is told nothing and finds out, and by the time it has found out it has already been doing the work for a quarter of an hour.

At four hundred feet she was at three.

She held three for two hours and it was three and it was fine, and then the ground came up over a lip and into the wind, and the wind took the heat out of the front of her and the grade came up another eight in the hundred, and four is what she went to, and she went to four and stayed there for most of the rest of that day.

She never went to five. She was aware the whole way up that five was there and available and that the only reason she did not go to it was that she had decided in advance that she was not going to spend anything to find out, and that was the whole of the difference between the two numbers.

## The First Day Up It

The first day on the grade she made three distinct mistakes and she knew about all three of them by the time the light went.

The first was a boot. She had come off a working crew with good boots on her feet and a road with stone in it, and the stone got into the toe of the left one at about the second hour and stayed there, and she did not find it until the second hour was well over because it was not a pain, it was a thing she was working around.

The second was water. She had a bake and four coppers and she did not go into a hamlet to fill anything, because a hamlet is a place with people in it and she had spent four volumes learning what happens to a woman who stands in a room where people are looking at her, and she went another three miles before she crouched by a run-off ditch and drank out of it, and it was fine, and it was the wrong decision and she made it anyway.

The third was the arm.

She carried her bag in her right hand for six hours on the first day because her right arm had been open since the second hour and she had not shifted it, and when she finally shifted it at the sixth hour the change in the whole left side of her was so immediate that she had to sit down on the ground for a while.

She did not do the third one again.

That is what three days of hard road actually is. It is not one bad afternoon. It is finding out three times in one day that you have been holding a thing wrongly, and being able to correct each one, and not being able to correct the first two, and going on to bed at the top of the rise with all three of them settled and none of them any use.

## The Wanting On The Second Day

The second day was the worst of the three and it was the worst of it about four hours after she had started, on a long false rise with nothing at the top of it.

She wanted to know what was past the next rise.

That is the whole of it and it is not a large want. It is not the office name above this county and it is not the eleven and it is not her mother's face. It is a shape, in front of her, on the ground, about six hundred yards away.

Her hand was on it.

She did not order it.

Nobody said a word about it, out loud, to anybody, on that hill or afterwards. No one had asked her about it and she had offered it to no one, and the only record of it anywhere was that on the second day of a nine-day road she wanted a thing very badly and did not buy it.

He did not notice, which is correct, because he was not supposed to. The whole of the discipline the two of them had built in a cold county over a year was that neither of them made a noise about a price.

## The Cold

The cold at the end of a day on high ground is not the wash house cold and it is not the cold of a room with ten sleepers in twelve bunks.

The wash house cold is wet and it gets into the cloth and it is the same every day. The quarter cold is a bed of ash and nine men and a bucket somebody filled. This one comes down off the ground after the light goes and it comes down through everything, including through a coat with a licence in the lining of it and an oilcloth and a plate, and it does not stop at the cloth.

She stopped twice on the second night because she had to, and both times it was for the arm and neither time was for the breath.

That was the thing she had not known she would find, and she found it on the second night and wrote it down in her own head with no figure attached: the arm went before the breath did, and the arm going was the cheap one, and the expensive one had not started yet.

The third night was colder still and she found the roof.

There is a shape that high ground gives a traveller when the weather has not decided: a building with one wall left and no roof on it, set a little off the track, with the standing wall square enough to sit behind.

She found one about an hour before the light and went into it and put her bag down inside the wall and lay down with her back to the stone, which was still holding the day's heat the way a thing holds heat when it has been in the sun all day and then got cold.

She got eight hours there, and she did not frost, and she did not hurt, and the only thing she had to do to earn all three of those was to walk for nine days.

## What She Rationed

The ration had been the day's ration since the cold county and it was not a number on any page now, and on the grade she could not afford it at all.

On the second day she spent none. Not one. There was a point in the middle of that false rise where a single sentence would have bought her the shape of the ground beyond the next rise, and it would have been a good buy, and she went without, and the reason was that she could not tell what it would cost and she was nine days out of a county with no money in it and there was no way to ask.

On the third day she spent one, and it bought nothing at all, and she had it back the next day at one and then had nothing at all for two whole days.

That is what the falling-off does when nobody is counting it. It does not go down evenly. It goes down where it is used.

She had been using it twice a day in hours since the spring, on a foundry floor, where two sentences was a shift and four was a discipline she had written on the back of a dead weight ticket in her own hand.

Out here two of them was a shape she wanted and four was a day she could not afford to have, and she was spending it the way a woman spends coin, which is badly, because she was the only one who knew how little there was.

## What A Body At Nine Is

There is a thing that happens to a person at the ninth of a realm and nobody tells you about it, because there is nobody to tell.

At Condensing nine she was the strongest body she had ever had. She had carried a hundred and seventy quarry loads in thirty-four days and she had worked a furnace shift at the cold end for five weeks and she had got a block off a lip backwards without her right hand closing on anything at all.

She had also believed, somewhere underneath about four months of use, that this was a thing that would go on being true.

It is not the same limit as a person who has not done anything, and the difference only shows on the third day.

A body that has been filled is a body that has to be carried. That was the whole discovery and it took nine days of hard road to find out, and it is not in any writing she had ever read: the fullness does not make the work cheaper, it makes the work heavier, because there is more of her in every single thing she lifts and the lifting has to carry all of it.

So: eight hours of sleep, because she was a bigger body. Three counts of breath on a climb, because she was a bigger body. The arm going before the breath, because she was a bigger body with one more thing to hold up.

On the second night she lay in the lee of a wall that somebody had built for a reason four hundred years ago and worked out that she could not have done those three days four months ago, and that a person who has never done anything could have done them without noticing, and that this was going to be true of every single thing she was going to do for the rest of her life.

## Alone

She was on her own for three days in a way she had not been since the quarry closed.

That was the rest of it, and it was a rest, and she had not had one in three volumes.

Nobody was asking her anything. That was the whole of what the three days were.

There was no counter, no queue, no sheet coming round, no monthly thing, no clerk taking the name of an agent off a form, no stair, no lamp, no frame held by a man who had said he would not say a thing a third time.

There was a road and a body on it and a man in her head with about nothing left in him, and the entire content of what she had to ration was whether she wanted to know the shape of the next hill.

She slept eight hours a night for the first time since the winter started. That is what happens to a person when there is nobody in the room who wants a thing off her.

She got to the top of the rise on the third evening able to stand and unable to be sure for how long she would be able to stand, and she stood there in the last of the light looking east over ground that was somebody else's ordinary country, with fields on it and a roof with smoke out of it about two miles off, and she was not going to get to any of it tonight and had four coppers and a bake.
