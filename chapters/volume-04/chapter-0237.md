# What Goes Past The Line

Twenty-two days out from the eighth, in the ordinary way, and the twenty-ninth of a month is the day it comes.

She was at the far end at about the fourth hour with the bar in her left hand, because the bar has been in her left hand every day since the twenty-first, and the two marks in lime she had laid on the twenty-first were still white enough at the edges to see from nine feet off.

She took it standing up.

She had done the eighth standing in a hut with three boards in it in the dark because there was nowhere else to take it. She did the ninth in the open on ground she had tended herself, at the fourth hour, in the ordinary voice, the way she has taken every stage for two years.

## The Word

She said it and she did not hold it and she did not put a hand over her face or shut her eyes or turn away, and she let it go.

[Ning Yan of the county of Lowwater, servant-born, under a ceiling written in her own hand. Foundation, stage nine, is held.]

It announces and it stops there.

## What It Did Here

Nothing.

That is the first thing and it is the thing she had not been ready for, because in four years every one of them has gone through the ground she is standing on and arrived somewhere, and this one arrived and there was nowhere for it to arrive.

There was nobody within nine days of road to be told. The course is nine days west with nine idle men on it and a girl of nineteen and a foreman and a tender with a board. There is a store with a woman behind the counter who has never asked her anything. There is a man with a boot off his foot and a man with a book and a pot and a wind off a rise.

None of them heard it. She looked at all of them, one after the other, going out over the rise at about nine yards a second, and not one face on nine days of ground had changed.

The drift under the bar on that ground is six inches and it is quiet and it does not ring, and the announcement went into it the way everything she has ever announced has gone into the ground, and about a quarter of a second after it did, she felt it go along.

It went along the ground.

She felt it the way she has felt the ring under a bar about as long as it takes to count eleven and then it stopped, and in the time it took to get her breath back she worked out that the whole of it had gone in one direction, east, away from the nine days she knows, past the post at the top of the rise and out into country she has never been in.

She did not build a theory on it. She stood on ground she had tended with a bar in her left hand and said nothing, and the ninth stage of a realm she has no paper for came up under her the rest of the way while she was standing there, and the base underneath it finished, and it is heavier and colder than eight ever was.

## What It Did A Mile Past

The man who keeps the board puts one up every nine or ten days and has done for nine years whether or not he has anything to put in it.

She was at the post about two hours later at about the sixth hour, because there was nothing else to do with a morning, and she was about forty feet off when he came up with the cart.

He was not hurrying and he was not looking for her.

He took a strip off the back of the cart against a rule and squared the end of it with his thumb and came up to the board and wrote a date at the left.

Then he stopped with the pen down about an inch above the cloth.

She watched a man of fifty-five stand at a plank for about as long as it takes to read a line, with a pen in his hand and nothing to write, and then he wrote something, and she could not read a single word of it because it is not in any hand within nine days of road, and he pushed the strip up behind the plank where the weather does not get at it and squared the plank with his thumb and went down the rise.

He did not come and ask her what the sound was. He did not come and ask her anything at all. He has not asked her one thing in two visits and she has been on this ground twice.

He wrote it down because that is what the board is for.

## Eight Hundred

The building is about four hundred yards past the post and she had seen it both days she had been this way and had not gone near it. It is a low room with a counter in it and a stove and about six men in it, and it is the only room within nine days of road where anybody writes down the hours of ground that is not the course's.

She went to it at about the seventh hour on the twenty-ninth because she had a day with nothing in it and because she wanted to see what a wage-book looks like when it is not hers.

There was a man at the counter with a sheet of paper in front of him and six men standing about nine feet back, and he was reading.

Not out of a book. Off a paper, in the ordinary voice of a man checking a thing out loud so that the people behind him can hear which part he is on.

“Fourth,” he said. “Nothing above the fifth, in person, with your own hand, and no surety again without relicensing.”

“Sixth,” he said. “The certified hours return with the quarter on the same crate in the same order.”

And then he said the seventh one, and he said it the way he had said the other two, and he had the paper in his left hand and the room behind him going quiet about a half a second before he got to the end of the line.

“A standing claim of eight hundred jin,” he said, “entered on this licence, travelling with it, and not affected by it.”

She was nine feet off, outside the door, in the wind, and she did not move.

Eight hundred jin.

She has carried that figure since she was sixteen. It went into four county books in a season of a year that is four years back and it is the seventh and the last thing on the back of a paper against her ribs that she has never once taken out of the oilcloth and looked at, and it is in a lease and a register and a standing sheet and a watching barrier board with a name on it, and a man has just read it out loud, in a room, a mile past the last stake, in a country where nothing is announced.

Nobody in that room knew she was standing outside it.

## What It Means For One Person

She stood there for about as long as it takes to read a line and then she went and sat on a stone outside and put her hands on her knees.

Four counties write what is heard. Nine counties hear it.

There are nine counties inside a line and one of them has just been read a figure out loud in a room that is not on their side of anything, and there is no book in any of the nine that has a column for a claim that has crossed out past the working reach.

The figure was read out. That is all that has happened to it.

And the people in that room can act on it. That is the part that is new.

It is in the first office within nine days of road that has ever been in a position to do anything about it at all, and it got there in the ordinary way, on a piece of paper carried east by a cart that was doing another job, and it was read out loud because a man at a counter reads the conditions out loud, and not one person in that room knows it is about a woman standing in the wind outside the door.

She had wanted it on that board at the far end and she had got it, and the same week the thing that has been riding her since she was sixteen walked past every office she has ever stood in front of and landed in a room with a stove in it.

She was not frightened and she was not proud.

She was angry, in the plain and complete way of a woman who has been carrying a thing for four years and has just watched a stranger carry it past her, and she sat on a stone outside a building on the far side of the last stake and let it be the whole of what she felt, and it took about four minutes and it did not need anything else.

## What She Did Not Do

She did not go in.

She did not go in and say the figure is mine, because there is no version of that in which a woman with four fingers' worth of grip on one hand and eleven holds in the other and no pot and no household and a licence she has never opened becomes somebody that room has to act for, and she worked that out on a stone in about nine seconds.

She did not go in and ask them what they are going to do about it, because a line is a place where people are paid by the day and questions are not on the wage-book.

She stood up off the stone at about the seventh hour and walked back up the rise to the post, and the man who keeps it was not there, and there was one more line on the board than there had been that morning, dated, and she could not read a word of it and she did not need to.

The ninth went out at the fourth hour from the far end of nine days of working ground into a country where nothing has ever been announced, and it arrived, and a man wrote it down, and a claim of eight hundred jin has been read out loud in a room that can act on it, and none of it is an answer to anything.

Nine stages in a column in four counties, and one of them not heard, and the ninth heard a mile past the post by eleven men who have never heard one at all.
