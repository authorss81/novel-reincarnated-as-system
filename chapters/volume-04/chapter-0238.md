# A Name In A Book

The thirtieth of the month she was at the far end at about the fourth hour and she went past the marks she had laid in lime on the twenty-first and kept going.

The drift is six inches there and it is quiet and it does not ring, and the last of the working ground runs out about two hundred yards short of the post at the top of the rise, and she walked off the end of it at about the fifth hour with the bar in her left hand.

He said it before she was half a mile.

“There is a line going into the board,” he said. “It has a date on it and it is in the narrow column and it is the ninth thing in that column since before you came east.”

She stopped on the ground.

“It does not stop,” he said. “I cannot see you go. I can see the line going in and I cannot see any part of you on either side of it and that is the whole of what it looks like from where I am.”

That is the first of the two ways the boundary came down and it did not cost her anything at all.

## The Body

It took her the rest of the day and most of the next one.

The bad flat is three-quarters of a mile and there is no mark on it anywhere and the drift is a foot and a bit on the low side all the way, and it was a foot and a bit on the twenty-first and it had come back to about that in nine days because nobody tends it except the one woman who is going across it.

She went along the high ground on the north side, the way everybody who runs stone to that yard has gone since somebody went through once and came back and took the high ground.

That took about an hour and a half and it was the easy part.

Then she came back down into the low side at the top of it, because the high ground does not go all the way, and she went three-quarters of a mile in about a foot and a bit of ground that is quiet and does not tell anybody anything, with four fingers on one hand and a bar in the other and four counts of breath and a base under her that is finished and colder than stone.

She came out at the bottom on the second of the month at about the fifth hour with her hands cut across the two places a bar holds a hand, and she had been in it about four hours and she had gone down once, on the knee, at about a third of the way along, and got up.

Nobody found her and nobody was sent. There are two men in the ground on that flat with a bar laid across them and a bar still there, and there is not going to be a third.

## The First

The trestle is about four hundred yards past the post on the cart track and there is a fire under it and a book open on a plank and about eleven men on that side of it, and none of them has ever heard a stage announced in his life.

She came up to it at about the sixth hour on the second with her hands cut and said the thing she came out here to say, in the ordinary voice, to a man with a book.

“My name,” she said. “Ning Yan. And I want the hours.”

He wrote it. That is the whole of what happened at that trestle on the second of the month and no man there asked her a single question.

He wrote her name in the column that is on the near side of the plank, which is a column of hours, and it is hers, and it goes in the way a column goes in.

It took her about eleven minutes to write the date, because the writing hand is the left one and it has the heel of it gone and it goes with the flat of the second finger and it has gone that way for about three years and it is not going to change.

It took her longer than that because of the other hand. The man at the plank kept the book flat for her with his palm because the plank is not level, and she held the corner down with four fingers and a thumb and the corner went twice.

She did not ask him to hold it the second time.

## What Is Said On A Line

On the fourth she was hired on and the hours began to go in in order with dates on them, and on the seventh she was paid, and the price is said out loud at a trestle in the ordinary voice because that is what is said on a line and it is not said differently for anybody.

“Five,” she said.

The man at the trestle wrote the five in the price column, because that is what the price column is for, and did not look up.

Six is the rate at the course and five is the rate off it and nobody on either side of the world has ever explained the difference to anybody, and she has said a number in the ordinary voice to a man with a book and he has put it in a column and gone on to the next line.

That is the whole of what is said between a woman and a wage-book in a country where nothing is announced.

## The Warden's Hand

He was at the post on the fourth, about nine feet off, in a plain coat, with a notebook shut against his side.

He had come past the post from the east, which is not the direction he came from four months back, and he had been on the far side for six days and had not gone into that building and had not spoken to a man in it.

She had about eleven minutes of a column of hours written in her own flat-of-a-second-finger hand before he said anything, and then he said the whole of it in one breath in the ordinary voice.

“I have not bought anything, I have not threatened you, I have not touched you, I have not entered anything and I have not asked you a question,” he said, “and I have not in six days spoken to a man on this side of the post, and I want that in the same breath as the rest of it.”

Then he took the notebook out from under his arm and gave it to her.

There was a page in it with a line on it and the line was in a Warden's hand, and it certified that a person had left the working ground of the line on the thirtieth of the month and come to the far side of it, and it was dated on that side of it, and at the bottom of it, where a person signs a thing, there was the office.

Not his name. The office.

She read it twice with the flat of her second finger on the edge of the page and did not understand it and put it back and he took it and shut it against his side.

“That is the second one I have signed,” he said. “The first one is in a county book nine days west and it is the reason your course shut its ground against you and it is true and I would write it again.”

“It does not have your name on it.”

“No,” he said. “It has never had my name on it. I am not on any of them. There is an office and there is a hand and there is a date, and that is what a thing looks like when it comes out of where I am.”

## What Went In The Space

She asked him one question and it was the only question she had asked him in about four months and it was about a shelf.

“The thing you asked for,” she said. “Is it answered.”

He said no at once, and not unkindly, and the way he says things.

“It is not answered. It is still pending. There is no instrument in my hand and there never has been and there will not be one for me.”

He put his thumb on the notebook.

“The writ for it is issued by the people whose line it is, to somebody standing on it and carried by it, and I have said that to you in the same words on a rise four months back and it has not changed in one particular.”

“They closed their ground against me.”

“They did, and they were right, and I would write the referral again.” He looked down the cart track at the trestle and at about eleven men standing about it. “They still do not know you exist. They have never once been told your name by anybody and there is no way of telling them and there is no writ.”

“Then what is in the space.”

He was quiet for about the length of a man reading a line.

“A countersignature,” he said. “On your petition. The relicensing. It came from past the county boundary and it is dated from out here and it went into the space on the sheet at the district, because it is the only thing anybody above me could put there.”

She stood in the wind and looked at the post.

“Is it an answer.”

“No,” he said. “It is not an answer to my request and my request is still pending and the space is not empty and nothing in the space has ever been about the thing I asked for. That is the whole of it and I would rather you had it from me on the fourth than from a sheet in nine weeks.”

Then he said: “I am going to stand about nine feet off here for a while and then I am going to go back past the post, and I am not going to ask you to come.”

He did not ask her to come.

## Nine Lines

Behind her, up the rise, there is a board on a post, and it is dated at the top, and it has nine lines in it.

Eight of them have something on them. One of them has nothing on it at all.

They are in a hand that nobody on that line can read, and every person standing at that trestle can look at it and tell you it is a hand from the other side, and not one of them could write a word of it, and eleven men on this course have never heard an announcement at all in their lives.

She has seen a column like that before and nobody else in this country has ever seen one.

The seventh hour was going. The man at the trestle had the book open on the plank and the column on the near side of it was a column of hours and it was hers, and she put the pen down in it with four fingers and a thumb and the flat of a second finger, and behind her the board stood on its post with nine lines in it.

Nine days of road behind her, and a floor, and nine men, and a tower with a light in it.

And it was quiet. It was quiet the way the ground past the last stake is quiet and the way a room is quiet at the end of a working day, and it was the first silence she had ever stood in that was not the inside of her own head.

She was Foundation nine. There is nothing above nine anywhere in the world. Her base is finished and it is the coldest thing about her and she will not be able to put it down.

She had about six weeks of a voice and a standing that is hers to order and she did not order it.

The register still reads reconciled. The plate is still against her ribs. The sheet went west with a foot and a half of blank paper in it and it is never coming back.

And a claim of eight hundred jin has been standing since she was sixteen, and it has been read out loud in a room about four hundred yards from where she was standing, and it is not answered, and the man who asked the question about it nine days and a good many weeks west of here has not been given the thing he asked for and is not going to be.

The man at the trestle tore a strip off the book at the rule and put it on the plank face down and went and got a pot.

There were six of those in an oilcloth against her ribs now, and they were in three different marks, and none of them could be checked against anything.
