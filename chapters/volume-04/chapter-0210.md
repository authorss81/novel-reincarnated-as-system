# The Carrier

She went to the board in the back of the hut on the first of the month and stood in front of it the way a person stands in front of a thing that is not going to change.

“You told me there was a west-going in about three weeks,” she said.

“There is,” said Ma Sheng.

“After that one.”

He turned round. He did not ask why she wanted it, because a man who keeps a board and has somebody standing in front of it reads out whatever is on it.

He put his thumb on the board and ran it along a line and stopped, and he read it the way he had read it for her once before, in under a minute, in the voice a man reads a thing he has read every week for nine years.

“After that one it is a man of the lower valley and he runs it on his own book,” he said. “He has a house on the far side of that valley and he takes nothing but what his own book says, and he leaves when he leaves. He went about nine days back and he will go when he goes.”

He took his thumb off the board.

“And after him there is nothing written for the rest of the season.”

She looked at the board for a while.

“You have said that is the last of the west-going twice now,” she said.

“There is not another west-going till the season turns and there is no date on that board for the turn,” he said. “A board is what has happened and what is going to happen on the days somebody put down. That is the whole of what it is.”

That is a different thing from a closed road and she understood the difference while he was still putting the board down, and it was the worse one.

A road that is shut can be waited out. A road with no times on it is not shut and has not been shut; it has simply stopped being written down, and it will start again when somebody with a board decides it starts again, and that is not anybody on this course, and it is not going to be decided this season by a woman standing in front of a plank.

## The Third Of The Month

He came back east on the third.

She was at the trestle at the fourth hour when the cart came up the track with the county's paper on it, and the whole camp came out to look at it, because everything that comes off a cart is looked at, and there is not a great deal in a camp like that.

A carrier runs the reach because there is work at both ends and a man runs a team. He had been out past Stony Ford and half of a county he had not heard of and he came back with two satchels and a bundle and a crate of lamp oil, and the bundle went on the trestle and the satchels went to the hut.

Then he said, in the middle of putting a lamp down, that the round had come round.

## A Round Comes Round

A county's monthly sheet travels with the work, because the county's paper goes out with whoever is carrying paper, and whoever is carrying paper goes out and comes back on a schedule that has nothing to do with the county's.

The sheet with her name on it went west on the sixteenth of the last month, in the satchel, with the wedge of paper and the tally sticks and a strip of oiled paper with something off a stone on it.

It came back east on the third of this month with a carrier who had a reason to be on the road that had nothing to do with her.

She stood at the trestle and let it be put in front of her, because a person does not get to decide that a piece of paper is not going to be in front of her when it is in front of her.

## What Came Down The Stair

The member's sheet was folded in three and it was heavy with ink, and it went onto the trestle and somebody turned it round, and in the attendance column on the line for the sixteenth of the last month, in the hand that writes *Member present*, there were the county's own two words where she had known they would be.

She had four days' hours in the column beside it and they were in a contractor's hand and they were in order.

She did not look at them.

## The Other Slip

Underneath it, folded smaller, at the bottom of the bundle, there was a slip of county paper about two inches by three, and it was not hers, and it was not anybody's in that camp.

It was the district's.

She had last seen that piece of paper nine days and four hundred miles west of that trestle, pinned to a public shelf by a door beside eleven other things, the eleventh of which had had four words on it and then an empty space the width of a line where an answer was meant to go.

The four words had gone up the stair and come down again, the way everything goes up and everything comes down, and this was the way it had come down.

She picked it up. It was warm from the satchel.

**Received: the twelfth.**

**Held.**

The space the width of a line, which had been empty since a clerk in that county read four words off a form and stopped, had something in it.

A date, and one word.

*Held.*

It is an office word and it means that the paper is in a room and has been looked at by somebody whose business it is to look at papers, and that nothing has been done to it.

That is all it means. That is the entire content of it. A clerk wrote it in a county nine days and four hundred miles west, in the ordinary hand, because a form has a place for it and the place was empty and somebody had to put something in the place.

That clerk did not write it for her. That clerk does not know her name. It is possible that clerk did not read the form.

She stood at a trestle in the open on a working morning with a slip of county paper in her hand and eleven people about forty feet off doing a morning, and she looked at the six letters of it for a length of time that a person who was only reading would not have needed.

There is another way that word is used.

Not in a county. Not in an office with a stair. In a place where a thing goes in and is not got out again, and where what is held stays held, and where the holding is the whole of what happens to it.

She was the only person within nine days of road who could hear both of those in one word, and there is nobody else within nine days of road who could hear the second one at all.

She put the slip down on the trestle, squared it, and pushed it about four inches to the left of the book, which is where paper goes when it is not being dealt with.

She did not say it out loud. Not to the camp, not to the foreman, not in the ordinary voice a woman uses when she finds something. Not in her own head in a sentence with a verb in it.

It stayed where she had put it, on a plank in a country where nothing is ever announced, and it was the first thing that had ever come back to her out of a stair in a room she was not standing in.

## The Morning He Went

She stood at the trestle on the morning he went anyway, at the fourth hour, because she was at the trestle at the fourth hour every morning, and because she was doing the board, and because it was a working morning.

He loaded two satchels and the crate and one crate that was not lamp oil. He did not take passengers, because he had said so about four days ago in a lane for no reason at all.

He went east at about the fifth hour and the camp went on.

There is no money.

That is the half of it and the half of it is not interesting and she had it four days after she left a bag under a hill at a dressed stone. She was paid six coppers a day and there was a place at the fire, and the fire and the bunk and the bread came out of the same six, so there had not been anything spare in it since the morning she came over the last rise.

She would not have gone.

That is the other half and she turned it over about nine times standing at that trestle with the bar against her shoulder, and it came out the same every time, and the answer was not the road and it was not the money.

She had a licence with a claim on the back of it. She had an attendance gone against her that would be in a file in Lowwater this year and in a file in Lowwater five years from now. She had four stubs of paper in an oilcloth against her ribs, and a stage on a document she had never touched, and a crew nine days west who could not be told anything by anybody.

If she went west on a carrier tomorrow she would be standing in a square in Lowwater in three weeks with nothing in her hands and everything in her head, and there was not one office on that square that could do anything she could not get done by sitting still where she was.

She worked that out on the fifth morning and had it again on the ninth.

The part that stayed was not that.

The part that stayed was that she had been a woman with a date and a plan for about four years, and on the third of a month at the fifth hour she was a woman on a line, and there was no longer anything on any board anywhere between here and Lowwater that would put her in a county.

## What She Spent

She spent one of that day's ration at the end of it, standing at the trestle with the light going on the boards, asking him the ordinary question about the carrier's schedule — when he went, what he carried, whether anybody rode with him.

He answered all of it in under a minute, exactly as it was written on the board, and it was the same information she had stood in front of him and been given for nothing on the first of the month.

That is what a ration is. You do not get to choose what it buys, and she knew it while she was spending it.
