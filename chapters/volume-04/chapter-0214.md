# Four Counts

She had been putting four counts into a grade with a bar in her hands for thirty-five days and had been getting away with it, because four counts is what a grade takes off anybody and the grade is the shortest part of the day.

Three was the number that carried her between one thing and the next.

Three on the flat. Four on a line. Five if she went into the ring on a bad night, and she had never once taken the five.

The three was not a reserve and it was not a size. It was the count she had between the end of one thing and the beginning of the next.

The walk from the bunk to the wire. The walk from the wire to the ring. The walk back at the seventh hour with a bar over her shoulder.

Every working day for four years had been built out of those three, and since the thaw she had been quietly spending them without noticing that she was spending them, the way a person spends a thing they have had so long they have stopped counting it.

## The Third Of The Month

The interval is twenty days from the last one and she did not go looking for it and did not have to.

It arrives the way the interval arrives, which is that the body begins putting out about four days early the sort of cold it is going to be in, and she had had that on the thirtieth of the month before, in the middle of a bucket of water, and had not put it anywhere.

She took it at about the fourth hour on the third, sitting on the edge of a bunk with three boards on it, in a hut with nine other men asleep in it at the third hour, which is the hour she had been taking things at since she was fourteen.

The stage and what it cost were the same event, and neither of them was the bracket.

She put it into the air the way she puts everything into the air, which is the way a person says a thing out loud in an ordinary room where nobody needs anything.

## What It Took

Short breath.

Three on the flat. That is what it was and that is what it is now.

Four on the line, where it was four. Four on a grade, where it was four. Four on a flat, where it was three.

Three is not coming back and she said so once, out loud, in the hut, to nobody, at about the fourth hour of the morning of the third of a month, and did not say it again.

The fourth count is not a prize.

It is the number the ground takes off a woman with a bar in her hands, and she pays it in the number that let her walk from one job to the next without stopping.

A base gets heavier to carry. That is why the intervals lengthen and it is why there was twenty days and not ten, and it is why the thing that goes is never a reserve and never a size.

It is a count.

## Both Of Those Are True

She went over the wire at the fourth hour on the same morning, with the same bar, and put it into the ring on the low side of the thirty-nine at about the fifth hour, and worked eleven hours.

She was stronger on that ground than she had ever been.

That is not a thing anybody can do anything with. A body at the third Foundation is not a body that can carry more; it is a body that has something under it, and what is under it does not show up in what you can lift.

She lifted more than she ever had. She got a bar under two feet of the worst of it and moved it and the ground under it was sound and black and it took a boot perfectly.

And at about the seventh hour she had to put the bar down and stand still for a moment with her hands on it, and the count that had been four since the thaw was four on a flat, and the three that had been quietly paying for the walking was gone.

She was better and she was worse and both of those were true at the same time and there was nobody within nine days of road who could tell her which of them she was.

The girl of nineteen came past at about the eighth hour on the same morning, did her eight yards in eight minutes, and stopped at the end of it with her bar on her shoulder.

“You are stopping,” she said.

“I am not stopping.”

“You are stopping on every pull. You were not doing that in the last month.”

That was true, and it was the first thing anybody had said to her about her own body in four years, and there was no answer to it that did not begin with a sentence she was not going to say at a ring at the eighth hour of a working day.

“Then I will leave it,” the girl said, and went down to the far end of it.

## What She Did About It On The Fourth

She tested it twice on the fourth of the month, which is the number of tests a day she gave herself and had given herself since the quarry.

The first was the walk from the bunk to the wire, which is about two hundred yards on flat ground with a bar over her shoulder.

Four.

The second was the low place on the thirty-nine, where she put the bar in and got her weight over the pull in one movement with a boot full of it, and stood up out of the ring at the other side of the pull.

Four, and it did not go to five, and her arm did not open, and she got out of the boot.

She wrote both of them on the back of the third dead weight ticket under the ration and the stage, in her own hand, with the flat of a second finger, in about nine minutes for the four lines.

Flat, four. Grade, four. Ring, four. Flat, four.

Then she put the ticket back in the oilcloth above the Clay Plate and did not look at it again until the eighteenth of a month that was to be the worst of the whole season.

## The Fire

The fire in that hut belongs to the eleven men and the foreman and it is lit at the second hour out of what goes on the plank at the west end.

She had been out of it for five days in the middle of the month before and she went back into it on the eighteenth and then came out of it again inside a week and has not been in it since.

What she settled on was her own bread bought at the store in the mornings and no share at all in a fire that is lit for everybody else.

That is the arrangement and it has been the arrangement for about a month and nobody on that course has ever once asked her about it, because it is not a thing anybody on a course asks about, and because she made it in the ordinary way that people make things that are none of anybody's business.

She was warm on one side of a working day. She was not warm on the other side, and had not been since the middle of the month before.

She was not hungry either, and that had not changed, and she had stopped expecting it to and had not had the time to put anything underneath it. The four counts were the same on both sides of the fire.

## What It Is Worth

There is a short answer to this and it is true and it is worth about what it sounds like.

She was at the third Foundation, and there was a base under her that was not there at the second, and a base is a thing later techniques get put on top of. Nothing she has already spent on technique goes back on top of it.

That is the whole of what a stage is at this point in her life. There was no other content in it and she had gone over the list four times.

It took twenty days. It took the number she had been living on between one thing and the next. It took the fire.

On the morning after the third she went out at the fourth hour and it was four all the way to the ring and four all the way back, and the walk that had been the free part of the day was now a thing she did on four, and it was going to be four for the rest of the season.

There is nobody within nine days of road to tell her whether that is a good trade.

There is a man in her head who can read a person through her eyes and cannot read a board, and who would have told her what the trade was if she had spent something on it, and she did not spend anything, because she already knows how he will read a thing like this and she did not want to hear it in his voice while she was carrying it.

She put the bar down on the boards and lay down on a mat with nine men asleep around her who have never heard a stage said in their lives and would not know one if it went off in the room.

In the morning the count on the flat was four, and it did not come back to three.
