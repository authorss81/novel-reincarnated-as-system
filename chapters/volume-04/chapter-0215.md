# The Man With A Notebook

He was in the camp on the fourth of the month.

She saw him from about two hundred yards, on high ground above the cart track, standing where a man stands if he wants to see the ring and the board and the trestle without being in any of them.

He had come down in the night. There was no cart and no carrier's line in his account of himself and he had a plain coat with the mud off it and a notebook under one arm, and the face was one she had last seen about a quarter of a mile off a lane on high ground nine days and a good many weeks west of here.

She had said *you will be here on the course when I am*, and he had said it back to her in the same voice, and neither of them had said what it meant.

He did not come to the trestle.

He stood on the high ground and he came down to the wire in the middle of the day and he stood about ten feet outside it and watched eleven men and a girl work, and he wrote.

That is all he did, for thirteen days, and it is more than anybody else in nine counties has done about her in four years.

He watched the board being read at the sixth hour. He watched the foreman's thumb go down a line and eleven men get up. He watched the ring on the low side of the thirty-nine, and on the fourth day of the thirteen he was down in it, to the ankle, in good boots, with the notebook shut against his chest, and a man came out of it beside him and did not say anything about it and went back over the wire.

He did not buy anything. There is a store at that camp and he went past it every day of the thirteen and he did not go in.

He did not threaten her. He did not touch her. He did not come into the hut or the bunk or the store or the space behind the cart wheel where she slept, and he did not stand near her at the fire on any of the thirteen nights.

On the ninth of the month he said the thing, unasked, in the ordinary voice, to nobody in particular, about nine feet off.

“I have not asked you a question,” he said, “and I am not going to start, and I want you to have that in the same breath as the rest of it so that you do not have to spend a morning on it.”

## The Rest Of It

That was the whole of what he said to her on the ninth and it was the same sentence in the same order that he had said in a hall with no roof and on a step in a lane and in a market, and she had heard it four or five times and it had never once been anything else.

The rest of it arrived on the eleventh, at about the fifth hour, on the high ground above the cart track, with the notebook shut, and he said it because he had been walking for nine days and he had worked out that he had about three days of reason left in him and he did not want to spend them being careful.

## Why He Walked Nine Days East

A field man of a Warden's office cannot unmake a System inside a bound.

That is not a matter of courage or of a better office. It is the instrument. There is one instrument for it and it is a Wall writ and it is issued by the line's own people to somebody who lives on it.

He said all of that standing up, in the ordinary voice, with the notebook under his arm, in about four sentences, and he said it the way a man reads out what is on a form.

“A writ for that is issued by the people whose line it is, to a person on it,” he said. “Not to an office nine days west. Not to me. To somebody who is standing on it and is carried by it.”

He looked down at the camp for a moment.

“You are on it. You have been standing on it since you came out here. You are not carried by it. It does not know you are here, it has never heard of you, and it has got no way of putting a writ on a person who is not on the roll of it, because a line of work does not have a roll of everybody who stands on the ground.”

## What The Line Can Do

“What the line can do,” he said, “is close its own working ground against a person it does not carry.”

He said it flatly and he did not enjoy it and he did not put anything on it.

“It has a rule for it. Nine years old. It has been used twice and both times it was right, and a rule that has been used twice and been right twice is not a secret and is not going to be argued with.”

She was standing on the high ground about eleven feet off him and she had said nothing at all, and she had said nothing at all because there was a straight answer to the whole of what he had come out here to do and she had worked it out standing on the low side of the thirty-nine on about the eleventh, in the time it takes to turn a bar over, and she had not wanted to be told it by a man with a notebook.

## The Part He Does Not Want

Then he said the part, which is the part she has gone over most since.

“I do not want it executed.”

He said it in the same voice he had said everything else, on high ground, at about the fifth hour of a working afternoon, in front of the woman it was being done to, as a professional disagreeing with his own office in an open place.

“I want it pending. A pending answer is a question somebody above me has to answer, and I have never once believed that the answer would be the right one, and I have been wrong about things before and not once about this.”

He put a thumb on the notebook.

“I have not asked you a question. I am telling you a thing because you are going to find out what I am in about four days and I would rather you heard it from me and in this order.”

## What She Asked Him

One thing, at about the sixth hour on the thirteenth, standing with about nine feet between them, and it was not a question about the writ or about the ground or about the rule.

She asked him whether anybody comes west out of that country.

He answered it in the ordinary way, in about three sentences, and the answer was that there is no run. That a man runs his own book and leaves when he leaves, and that a board in a hut at the west end of a course has whatever somebody has put on it, and that there will not be another west-going written on that board before the season turns.

She had that off the same board on the first of the month, from the same man with the same thumb, for nothing.

She paid for it in a ration and she knew what she was paying for it while she was standing there paying for it, and the difference between then and the first of the month was that on the first of the month it had been a thing about a road and now it was a thing a man from a county office she had never stood in had walked nine days to confirm.

## The Eleventh Of The Month, At The Wire

At about the second hour of the eleventh he came to the wire, where a man comes to the wire, and he asked the foreman a question and the foreman answered it.

She was about forty feet off, at the plank, with a day's line half written, and she saw the whole of it inside about ten seconds.

Cui Bao had asked him what a rule was, in the way a foreman answers a man with a notebook, because a rule is a rule and it is nine years old and it is not a secret.

And the man with the notebook had asked the question in the ordinary way a man asks a foreman, and the foreman had told him, in the ordinary voice, standing at a wire, with his hands at his sides, exactly what the rule was and how many times it had been used and that both times it had been right.

The man with the notebook wrote it down while the foreman was talking.

She stood at the plank and understood it in the time it takes to put a board down, and then she went back to her line and finished the day's readings and it went up about an hour late.

She did not go and stand in front of him. She thought about going and stood there until her arm was open and did not, and there was no version of standing in front of him that changed anything at all, and she had worked that out at a trestle in a county four hundred miles west about two years before she ever heard of him.

It was not fear. She wanted that written down because she had expected it to be fear every day for four years and it had never once been fear.

It was the specific and entirely adult humiliation of being told, by an honest man who did not want to tell her, that the only thing that can be done about her is going to be done by strangers who have never met her and who will be right.

She had spent four volumes learning to be competent at paper. She had got a leg on a counter and a leg in a room and a leg on a stair past every office she had ever stood in front of.

And the paper was about to be read by a foreman of about fifty, correctly, and the thing it described was the thing that was wrong, and there was not one thing she could produce, not one hour in the best book in the world, that would say anything different, because the book was not the question.

The question was whether a line of work keeps its working ground open to an unauthorised thing.

She was the unauthorised thing. That was in a certificate in a Warden's hand that a clerk entered into a book nine days and four hundred miles west, and it was true, and she had relicensed above it three times and had not touched the paper.

And there is a licensed one in good standing at the centre of it. That is on the same paper. Both are on the same paper.

He said he does not want it.

She did not look at that. She put it where she puts things that are not useful until the back of a very long day, and she did not look at it, and it was still there when she got up.

It was the only thing anybody within nine days of road had said to her in about four months that had not been about getting a thing done.

That is the whole of what it was, and she did not look at it, and she has not gone back to it.

## The Sixteenth

The month's sheet went west that evening in the satchel with the lamp and the tally sticks, and it had eleven words of hours in it that were all good and about a foot and a half of paper with nothing in it.

She did not go and stand at the wire again that day and she did not go near the high ground, and in the evening the man with the notebook was at the far end of the camp writing by the light off the hut, and eleven men were in a ring about forty feet off doing the thing they do, and about nine hundred words of it went past her.

She sat with her back against a cart wheel and listened to all of it and worked out that on the eighteenth of a month, or somewhere near it, a thing would happen that she could not stop and would not be able to argue with, and that the man who had caused it had told her about it a week in advance in the ordinary voice and had said he did not want it.

That is what she had for it.
