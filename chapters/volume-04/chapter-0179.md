# Answer Pending

She went down the four miles on the first morning of the rest of it and stood at the shelf by the door of the county office for eleven minutes, and she read the same slip of paper three times.

That was one more than the last time and it was not an accident. On the last time she had read it twice, once to understand it and once for the fact of it, and both of those had been about understanding.

This time she stood in front of it with her hands inside her coat and her right arm held close and read it three times, and by the third one she was not reading it any more. She was checking whether it had changed.

The shelf was to the left of the door and it was about as long as a man's arm and it had eleven things on it when she last went past.

It had twelve.

She counted them without meaning to. Three notices about the tithe quarter. Two notices about the winter fuel allotment, one of them from the autumn and one of them corrected. A sheet about a bridge on the Verge road that had been off since before the snow. A slip about a surety she had not asked for and had not refused and did not want. A thing about the price of salt. Two district circulars, folded. One with a county seal on it that had been there since the thaw.

And eleven things, and the twelfth, and the twelfth was the one she had come to look at.

The slip was narrow, about two fingers wide, and it had come down the same stair as everything else and been pinned at the same height as everything else, and it had the district's mark on it and not the county's.

Under the mark, four words, in a clerk's hand.

*Referred upward. Answer pending.*

And under that, a ruled space the width of a line where an answer was meant to go.

The space was empty.

## What An Empty Space Is

It had been empty the last time too, and she had known that the last time, and she had walked out of this building and gone up a hill four miles and lain down in the cold with a licence nailed over her head, and none of it had gone into the space.

What she had not done, in all the days between, was look at it properly as a thing on a form.

A question that nobody has answered is a question. She had spent nineteen months learning what questions were and they were the things people asked out loud at counters and in rooms and in lanes, and every one of them had an answer somewhere and the somewhere was usually a man with a book.

That was not this.

A ruled space on a form is not a question. It is the place where an entry gets made. Somebody prints that space because somebody is going to write in it, and the width of it is the width of one line of writing, and it is ruled in the same ink as the rules above it and below it, and it has been ruled since before the district that printed it was a district.

So the space was not empty because there was nothing to say.

It was empty because there was something to say and the writing of it was somebody's job, and that somebody worked somewhere that was not in this building.

She had known that on the second reading. She had said as much to herself in a lane in the middle of the winter, more or less, in worse words.

Knowing it and standing in front of it were not the same act, and the second one took eleven minutes and there was a queue behind her in the room and about nine of them had come in out of the cold for the same reason she had.

## The Queue

There were nine people behind her and the room had eleven windows in it that let in about as much light as eleven windows let in at the fourth hour of a morning in the deep cold.

Sun He was at the table at the back with his coat on, which he did in deep cold and which that room read as a coat and nothing else. The young clerk was at the counter. A man she did not know was signing a column at the end of it with a whole hand and taking four minutes over it, and there were two people behind him who did not shuffle either.

None of them were going to move for four minutes, and she had eleven minutes' worth of standing in front of a shelf in her, so she read the space a fourth time and then stopped.

## The Thing That Was New

The twelfth thing on the shelf was a county notice, and it was not from the district.

It was on the same paper as the county's other notices and it had the county's seal on it in the ordinary way, and it had been pinned there some time in the last two days, because there was a thumbprint of frost melted and refrozen on the corner of it, and it had not been there the last time she stood at this shelf.

*THE SEASON'S ROAD EAST OUT OF THIS COUNTY WILL BE REOPENED ON THE EIGHTH OF THE NINTH MONTH, OR LATER, AND NOT EARLIER.*

*THE QUARRY FACE AT THE VERGE IS SHUT AND NO LOADS WILL BE ENTERED AT IT UNTIL THE ROAD IS OPEN.*

*THE TITHE QUARTER CLOSES ON THE LAST DAY OF THE MONTH.*

Three lines, a seal, and a clerk's signature, and the clerk's signature was the young one at the counter, which meant somebody had sent for him, which meant it had been written on purpose and not thrown up in a hurry.

She read it twice.

She had been waiting for it since before the middle of the winter without ever knowing that was what she was waiting for, and it had been sitting in an office somewhere for the whole of that time waiting for the thaw to be far enough off to be worth posting, and it had come down the stair on the ordinary morning the ordinary way.

A day.

That was all of it. It was one day on a piece of paper with a seal on it, and a person who wanted to know when the road opened would have gone to this shelf and read it and gone away, and it would have been the whole of what he got.

She stood there and understood that she had been waiting since the day the road shut for a date to put on a wall in her own head, and that the county had had the date on a shelf the entire time, and that the reason she had never found out was not that it was withheld from her.

It was pinned up where anybody could read it.

## The Shape Of The Thing

And then, underneath the noticing, in about the time it takes to draw a breath, the other thing arrived, and she felt it arrive, and it was the reason she had really come down the hill.

The county had published a road.

Not a road to somewhere. A road to the edge of the county, and past that edge a thing this office could not describe in three lines, and the notice had managed the describing by not doing it, and she stood there and looked at the two lines of it and saw the whole of her own position drawn in the outline of a road notice.

*This county's boundary is a place where a road stops.*

The county knew where that was. The county put a date on it. The county sealed it and sent it up a stair and had it read out by a man in a lamp-mender's coat in nine counties where nothing is announced, and every one of those nine counties heard that the road east out of Lowwater opens on the eighth of the ninth month.

And there was the same boundary at the other end of it.

Her petition had been barred on the ground that this county holds no schedule of work done past its boundary and is not able to certify one, and the last line on it had been written slower than the rest, and it had said that the answer to her application was not that office's to write.

The Hunter's request had gone up the same stair with it. The district had answered with four words and a ruled space.

Three documents, three offices, one line on the map that all three of them were standing on, and she was standing on the wrong side of all of them at once.

She could see the shape of the thing from inside it.

That was new. She had spent nineteen months learning what each office would and would not do, one at a time, and she had never once stood still long enough to look at all of them together and see that the boundary was the only thing in the picture, and that the boundary was drawn on a road notice in a clerk's hand, and that a clerk in this county had known where it was and sealed the answer and gone home.

## The Silence

He did not say anything the whole way down the hill or the whole way round the square.

She noticed it at about the third hour, in the lane behind the wash house, standing with her hand on a board, and she did not ask him why, and the not-asking was the first thing about this week that she had chosen rather than found.

She has spent a year and a half finding out what a day of him is worth by using it. She has never once in nineteen months had a whole morning in which he did not say anything at all, and there is no rule about that, and there is nothing in the spring's standing that says he must.

She stood in a lane in the third month of the cold and let a man in her head be quiet for four miles and did not spend one sentence of it finding out why.

## The Wash House

She did not go in at the counter.

She went across the square and down the lane behind it to the wash house and took the board up in the corner where the water does not get, and the tin was under it, and she counted it on the bench under the lamp with the flat of her right second finger because that is the finger that can still do a turn at the end of a stroke, and the heel of the other one is not there any more and has not been since the ice.

It took longer than it should have. It took the better part of a quarter of an hour, because a person cannot count quickly with the side of a finger, and there is no faster way to do it and there is not going to be one.

About a hundred and forty coppers.

She put the board back down and put the stool back and wiped the bench, which nobody was going to notice and which she did anyway.

There was a girl at the trough with a sheet in front of her and nothing on it, because a wash house's copying work is carriers' run lists and the road had shut a hundred days ago, and so there had not been a list to copy in a hundred days and there was not going to be one until the notice on the shelf came true.

“You are standing in my light,” said the girl.

“I am.”

“You always stand in my light.”

“Yes,” said Ning Yan.

The girl turned the sheet over and looked at the blank side of it, which is a thing a person does with a sheet when there is nothing on the front.

“It is going to be the eighth of the month,” she said. “My cousin came in from the square with the notice in his hand and read it out at the trough and three people told him he had it wrong, and he has not got it wrong, because it is the county's own and it has a seal, and if it is wrong then it is wrong in writing.”

“It is the county's own.”

“Then it is the eighth,” said the girl, “and I will have a list inside four days of it, and there will be more in the month after, and you may stand in my light for the rest of the winter if you have got nothing better to do.”

She did not thank her and the girl had not needed to be thanked and had arranged in advance not to be, which was a thing she had been arranging for two years and had told three washerwomen about so that it could not be an accident later.

## What She Carried Back Up The Hill

She went up the four miles at the ordinary speed with three counts on the stair still to do at the top of it, and she was at the foot of the crew stair before the light went.

The eighth of the ninth month.

A hundred and forty coppers under a board in a corner where the water does not get.

Nine men in a floor and a Hall with no roof and a word on the front of their own schedule.

And a ruled space the width of a line, on a public shelf, where an answer goes when it is somebody's job to write one.

She had come down the hill wanting two things. She had got two things, and one of them was a date, and the other one was an edge she could stand at and look across and had no way round to.

A clerk in this county had sealed a date onto it and gone home for the night.

She stood at the foot of the crew stair and worked the last of it out with her thumb against her teeth, the way she had worked out every date since the spring.

The road opens on the eighth of the ninth month.

And everything she had to do in this county before that — the space on the shelf, the bar on the petition, the eight hundred jin with a date written into somebody else's book by a hand that is not that book's — is a thing she has to do before the eighth, or after it, or from the other side of it.

There was no third thing to do with a ruled space. It was ruled, and the office that fills it is up a stair she has never seen the top of, and there is nothing whatever she can do to it from four miles inside a line that a clerk in this county sealed a date onto in the afternoon.

She went up the four miles with a hundred and forty coppers and a date, and she got up the last of the stair on three counts and never got back to two, and she did not tell one person on that floor about any of it.

There was a month in front of her now that had a shape in it.

That was new. Every week since the middle of the winter had been a week she was spending in the same county being unable to do the one thing in four counties that could put a thing in a blank, and the week had had no shape at all.

This one had a day at the end of it, and the day was the eighth of the ninth month, and she was going to be standing on this hill when it came unless she did something about it.
