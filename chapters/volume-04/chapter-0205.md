# Member Absent

The sheet came round on the sixteenth with the carriers, which is what it does, because a county's monthly member sheet travels with the work the way a roster travels on a line.

It was not addressed to her and it was not brought to her and it was not given to her.

It was on the trestle in the morning with the day's other paper on it — a carrier's docket, a tally stick, a strip of oiled paper with something off a stone on it — and it stayed there through the whole of the middle of the morning with about five men walking past it, and at about the fourth hour somebody turned it round and looked at it and left it where it was.

She saw her name from about six feet off, standing there doing nothing in particular.

## Two Columns

It is a sheet that any county can read and no county does. There are four columns. The first is a date. The second is a name. The third is an attendance, and the fourth is hours, and the third and the fourth are **not joined**.

That was in the form and it had been in the form for years, and she had never seen it from the wrong side.

The sixteenth was ruled in the first column. Her name was in the second column, in the same hand as everything else on the sheet, and it was the only name on the sheet she recognised, and it was sitting in a column next to a rule that had been filled in at the other end by an office nine days west.

In the attendance column there were two words.

**MEMBER ABSENT**

It was the county's own two words and it was in the hand that writes Member present at the foot of a winter return in a hall with no roof.

The same hand. The same pen. The same four words in the same order in a building nine days up the road, except that the other ruling was made by a man standing in a room with her in it, and this one had been made by nobody at all.

Beside it, in the fourth column, in a contractor's hand, in ink that was two days old, were the hours she had worked since the eleventh of the month.

Five days of them. They were in order. They were in good order. Every one of them had a date against it and a stretch against it and a mark against it, and the total at the foot of her own line was in six columns of something that were the only part of the whole sheet that had ever been anybody's business.

Five days, and every one of them good, and not one hour of them had anything to do with the two words in the column next to them.

She stood and looked at that for a long time.

There is no quantity of hours that reaches it. That is not a rule anybody made. That is what the two columns *are*: one of them is money and one of them is a person, and the county put them side by side in about the fourth year of the thing for a clerk's convenience, and there has never been a number that crosses from one to the other in nine counties.

She would have needed to be there at the third hour on the morning of the sixteenth, in person, in her own hand, at the county office on the square in Lowwater.

## The Word He Offered

Ma Sheng came and looked at the sheet because he came and looked at everything that comes round.

He read her line and he read the fourth column and then he stood there for a bit with his hands at his sides.

“I will tell you what that is,” he said, “and I am telling you because nobody has told you and it is in front of you.”

“Tell me.”

“Any person standing on the course may write a word in the reason column of a member's absence.” He put a thumb on the sheet, well clear of the ink. “That is not a county thing and it is not a form of office. That is the rule of a place where people are paid by the day. Any man on this course can write a word against your absence and it goes back west with the sheet and the county reads it.”

“Does it take the attendance out.”

“It does not take the attendance out.” He said it flatly. “It does not take it out, it does not put anything in the other column, and it does not put one hour on either side of it. It says why. That is all a word does in that column. It tells a clerk in a county what a person was doing and it does not change the thing that has happened.”

He took his thumb off the paper.

“You would want to think about it. Most people who could write there have not got anything to write.”

Then he went and wrote a man's hours against a stretch, and he did not say another word about it, and he was not framing it as anything.

She did not thank him. She did not know then why, and she turned it over afterwards, and the answer was that she was not thanking him then and was not now — what he had done was put a thing on a table in a hut, and the thing was still hers to pick up.

## The Column

Here is the whole of it.

**The reason column is the column her hours go in.**

Not beside it. Not under it. It was the same width of the sheet, on the same lines, on the same page, and every hour she had ever worked out here was written on that sheet in the space that a word would go in.

So a word that a county would read, and that would put a reason on her member's sheet next to MEMBER ABSENT and would be true — she was on a contract nine days east, she was working, she was paid by the day, she was not within reach of any office — is also a line in the only book in the world that can certify an hour she worked out here.

A book read in order, with dates, by an office on the far side of a boundary no county paper crosses.

And she was about to be relicensed on the strength of that book — on the strength of ten days out of it, read aloud in a room by a man she was not shown and could not check.

A word in that column would be in it. Every word. For the rest of the season and after the season, in order, with the dates on.

She worked it out in about four seconds, standing at a trestle in daylight, in front of people.

## What Was In Her Mouth

It was about seven words long and it was true and she had it ready, and she never put it anywhere.

She stood there and had it, and she was aware of the exact shape of it, and she understood that if she said it she would be saying it to a man who would write it and to a county who would read it and to an office that would keep it under the number it is allowed to keep.

She did not say it.

She stood at the trestle for a bit longer and then went and got her bar and went over the wire.

By the time she was in the ring, the carrier had the sheet in the satchel and the satchel was on the wall, and the sheet was going west on the next carrier whenever the next carrier went.

It was going with two words in one column and five days of good hours in the other and about a foot and a half of empty ruled paper in the middle, where a county clerk in nine days would eventually look, and see the space, and rule it, and stop.

## The Whole Of It, In Order

She worked it out standing up, in daylight, with a sheet in front of her, and it could not be done anywhere else.

She had gone east on the morning of the first of the month.

She had not once counted the sixteenth. Not on the flat after the fourth bridge, where the reach was on the back of her hand in a carrier's shorthand. Not on the climb, when there was a whole morning when she could not have spent a voice on a hill shape and did not. Not in a lane on high ground where a man who could not do anything for her had told her the truest thing anybody had said to her in a year.

She had not counted it and she had not weighed it and she had not decided against it.

There is no version of the last nine days in which anybody took the counting out of her hands.

## It Is Not Payable

That was the thing about it, and three months had not got her used to it. Hours did not buy it. Money did not buy it.

There was a column about four hundred miles of paper away where the word went, and it was the width of a line, and there was exactly one line in it, and she did not fill it in.

## What She Refused To Say Out Loud

Nobody stopped her.

That is the sentence. She went back over it afterwards about forty times, on a ring in the worst ground on nine days, with a bar in her hands at the fourth hour of a day that had nothing else in it, and she could not get it to come out any other way.

Nobody stopped her. There was no order. There was no county clerk who had refused to paper her and there was no man in a good coat who had put a hand on her arm and there was no letter and no rider and no threat of any kind.

She walked off the top of a hill on the morning of the first of the month, with about thirty coppers and a plate and a licence and a document about eight hundred jin in a bag, and she did not count the sixteenth, and she got on a cart at the fourth bridge the next day and went on east.

Nine men in a cold room four miles up the other side of that hill were nine days behind her, and she was not going to see them before the thaw, and she could send nothing and tell them nothing.

That was the cost.

It was the reason she did not write the word. It was not the only reason, and she never pretended it was the only reason, and she knew exactly what the other one was and had not said it out loud to anybody either.

## Nine Men Nine Days West

She thought about them at the fourth hour on the ring, which is where she thought about everything that had no place to be thought about.

Nine men. A cold room with a bed of ash in it and a bucket that Qin had filled on his own initiative in the dark, and a fire that had gone down twice a night for a fortnight because she had stood in the daylight at a board and chosen a road over their heat.

Gao holding the frame. He had said the sum twice and had promised them he would not say it a third time and had then said something else instead, which was worse, and the something else was that the second bunk was kept.

Qin standing four miles down a mountain to be eight feet off in a queue and then not eight feet off.

None of them had said anything to her about a date.

That was the part, and it was not the suffering.

Nine men cannot see a county's monthly sheet, and would not know to ask about it, and would not have suffered. The part is that in eleven months she had built a life out of being the only person in a room who kept her own hand on everything she had.

And the one item she had lost in four years was the one they could never have helped her with, because the entire mechanism of it was that it happened in a room with nobody in it.

She could have written to them. There was no way to write to them.

She could have told Qin on the morning of the first at a dressed stone with nothing written on it, with a flat of bread going warm in a coat, and she had said nothing, and it had not occurred to her to say it, and that was not a decision she had made either.

And she was not going to let it become the road's fault, or the winter's, or a county's.

She had not counted it, and she had not been prevented from counting it, and there were four separate days in that road where she could have turned round at a shoulder and had it.

She put the bar into the ring and moved four feet of ground and did the arithmetic about how many feet were left.
