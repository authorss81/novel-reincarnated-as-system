# Three Days Missing

She went out on the ninth of the month at the fourth hour and she was not at the trestle at the fourth hour, or the fifth, or the sixth, or the seventh, and she was not at the trestle on the tenth or the eleventh either.

That is the fact of the three days and it is the only fact of them that anybody within nine days of road could have seen.

The sub-contractor wrote her down at the fourth hour on the ninth because he writes every day down at the fourth hour, and he wrote the mark of the stretch she works on, and then she went past it.

## The Fourth Hour

She took the seventh stage at about the fourth hour on the first of the three days, sitting on the ground with her back against a rise about three hundred yards past the last stake, in the ordinary way, which is the way she has taken every stage for two years.

[Ning Yan of the county of Lowwater, servant-born, under a ceiling written in her own hand. Foundation, stage seven, is held.]

It went out. It does not say where she was and it does not say what heard it.

Nine hundred yards inside the wire, a course with nine idle men and a girl of nineteen and a foreman and a tender heard a stage go up at about the fourth hour, and every one of them looked at the ground they were standing on, and not one of them knew where to look.

Nine men and a girl and a man with a board, on a course whose board has had a hole in it for a month, and the sound arrived and it did not carry a direction.

She got up about a minute later and went on out past the last mark, because a rise is not ground that anybody is paid to work.

## The Ground In Between

She went about two hundred yards and stopped.

There is ground between a sub-contractor's last mark and the bad flat. She had walked it once with a handcart on the first of the month and had not gone down into it, and it is not on anybody's plank, and the drift on it is thin and thick in the two low places, and it is the worst of what is left within two hundred yards of a trestle.

It took her about two hours to work out why she was standing on it.

It is not the drift. It is the quiet.

There is a thing happening under her that has been building since the thirteenth of the month before, and on a worked stretch with eleven men and a girl on it and a painted mark on the plank and a ring in the ground under a bar, it does not turn.

She has run four years on ground that talks.

She stood on the quiet ground for about ten minutes and felt the thing under her come up half a step the way a thing comes up when it is offered the ground it was built for, and she went to the wall for the bar.

## The First Day

She worked it by hand, by herself, with a bar, from about the sixth hour until the light went.

Her left hand is a hand that builds every grip from nothing, one at a time, before the bar lands, and that is a slow thing on a worked stretch where a man can set his feet. Out here there is no worked stretch, and the bar lands wherever it lands, and she had to build a hold, and put the weight over it, and take the weight off, and build the next one, about forty times in an hour.

Her hands were cut by the time the light went. Not badly. In the ordinary way, across the two places where a bar held a hand.

She was out there in the dark of the first night with her hands cut across the two places a bar holds a hand, and the base under her had gone another step heavier and colder, and the thing she had been feeling come up half a step since the thirteenth of the month before came up the rest of the way, all at once, in about the length of a breath.

And the first thing she felt about it was relief, and the second thing she felt about it was that she had four marks on the back of a dead weight ticket and had not spent one of them since the tenth of the month before.

She slept in the hut with three boards in it because the hut is hers and the trestle is not and there is no man on that ground with a book who would have a place in it for a woman who did not come in.

## What Ten Hours Costs On It

The ground in between is worse to work than the worked stretch and better in one way, and she learned both of those in the first hour of the first day.

Worse: there is no mark, so there is no end of a run, so a body that wants to stop at the end of a hundred yards has to decide to stop, and she stopped about forty times a day in places where nobody would have known.

Better: it is quiet, and quiet ground does not tell a person anything, and after four months of a ground that rings under every bar she puts into it she found out that she can work twice as long on it without knowing how far she had come.

That is not nothing. It is the only advantage the place has and it is the one that made it worth going to.

The drift is about a foot in the two low places and about six inches everywhere else and it is thin in the middle of the run, and there is a lean-to about four hundred yards off the second day's end with two bars in it, and somebody put them there and did not come back for them, and she took the better one and left the other one where it was, because there is a man who owns the lean-to and she is not going to be a woman who takes a man's bar.

## What The Board Said

She came in on the third evening.

She came in on the outside of the wire, past the trestle and past the last stake and along about a hundred and forty yards of the course's own ground, at about the seventh hour, with her hands cut and her breath at four counts and eleven hours of nothing behind her that no book on earth is going to be told about.

The board stands at the wire on the course's own ground and it faces out, because a board that a foreman reads aloud has to face the men.

She stopped about forty feet off it and read it, the way she reads a board, which is from the bottom up.

Three new lines on it.

Dated the ninth, the tenth and the eleventh. A stretch in the column that a stretch goes in, written in Cui Bao's own hand, in the marks the men use.

And no figure against any of the three.

Not a figure of hours. There is no hours figure on a foreman's board; a board carries the stretch and the man. And on those three lines there is no man.

Nine men are idle and a girl of nineteen is idle and a course that has nothing to do pays a man by the day for being on a stretch, and there is no day in that that can be paid for, and a foreman who is right about everything wrote three lines of a stretch with no man on them because the stretch had to be kept and there was nobody on it to keep it.

## Unwilling

They did not tell her.

That is what she went looking for afterwards and that is what she did not find, and she went back over it for about three days before she accepted it, because a person would have mentioned it.

Nine men who are idle, and a girl of nineteen who is idle and is somebody's daughter, went out on three days of a stretch that is on a foreman's board, for a person they struck out of a wage-book in the open eight weeks ago, and not one of them said a word about it then and not one of them has said a word about it since.

They were not paid for it. There is no line on that board that pays for it and there is not going to be one, because a course that has nothing to do pays nobody, and a man who stands ground on his own hour is a man who has thrown away a day and knows exactly what he threw away.

The girl of nineteen was out on the second of the three days. She saw her at about the fifth hour, about four hundred yards off, on the low side, with a bar, working alone, about half a mile inside the wire from where Ning Yan was sitting with her back against a rise.

The girl of nineteen does not know what is in Ning Yan's head. She has read a refusal, and a number, and the word on the front of her father's own schedule, and a line drawn through a woman's name in a wage-book at the second hour of the seventeenth of a month that is two months back. She is somebody's daughter and she is nineteen and she is idle and she went out and did a day's work that is not on a board for a woman she has spoken to twice.

And she did not look in the direction of the rise.

## Worse Than A Refusal

Being refused by them was better than this and by a long way.

She had worked that out about a week ago and had not expected to have it worked out for her by a board.

A refusal is a thing a person does to you and it means a person has looked at you and decided and is standing behind the decision. She has had four years of those and she knows exactly what to do with one: stand in front of it, be in the room, do not let it be the last word.

There is no facing in this. Nine men and a girl went out on three days of a stretch that is on nobody's book for a person they struck out of a book in the open, and they were not paid for it, and they did not ask to be, and they did not want her to find out and they made sure she had to work it out of a plank from forty feet off.

And they are right. The line holds where it is kept. A course stands ground it does not own when a hand is missing from it, and that is not a kindness and it is not a rescue and it does not make them any less correct about the paper that closed the ground in the first place.

They did the only thing they are built to do, and she was three hundred yards off the whole of it, and it cost them three days of a run they are not paid for and it cost her the instrument.

## Three Empty Dates

The sub-contractor squares his book at the end of a run and a book that is squared is a book other men can be told to read.

He did not have hers to square for three days, and she had been in it for three days, and both of those were true on the same page.

He had written the ninth, the tenth and the eleventh down at the fourth hour, and the date column was full, and the mark of the stretch was beside every one of them, and the hours column was empty on all three because the hours had not been worked on the stretch he had put her on.

She came to the trestle on the twelfth and he was at it.

He looked at the three dates and he ran a thumb down past them and stopped, and looked at her.

“You want the tenth, the eleventh and the twelfth filled in,” he said.

“No.”

“They are your three days.”

“They are three days I did not work your stretch.”

“That is what I said,” he said, and went and got a pot, and did not say anything else about it, and he has not said anything else about it since, and she has not asked him to.

Three dates in a book that is true.

She is eighteen years old and she has the seventh stage and the order gone out of her left hand and about five coppers and there is not one stretch of continuous work in either of the two books on nine days of road, and there is not going to be one now, because the only run she ever had was put in two places by her own mouth on purpose, and three of the days that would have gone into the making of one went into ground nobody records.
