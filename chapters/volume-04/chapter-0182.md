# Not Herself

She did it on the mat in the second bunk on the fourth night, with the fire a bed of ash and nine men asleep and the licence over her head with its head side to the room, because it was the only hour in a day when a sentence of hers could not be heard by anybody on that floor.

She had the instrument. It had been hers to order since the middle of the winter and not his to refuse and not hers to spend, and the standing had changed hands in a winter room with nobody in it and both of them knowing exactly what they were doing, and since then she had had it in her hands for weeks and had not used it once.

She knew, without ever having counted it, that he was finite. She had known that for a while and had not looked at it, on the grounds that looking at it is a thing you do once and then you have to carry it, and she had a great deal of carrying to do.

On the fourth night she looked at it.

And then she did the thing she had actually come to the mat to do, which was not to decide what to buy.

It was to decide what she was not going to buy.

## Five Things

There were five.

She had been carrying them about since the reading in the middle of the winter and she had never said any of them out loud, because a thought that is said out loud in a room with nobody in it is still a thought that has been said out loud, and she had learned in the middle of the summer that a sentence she has said can be repeated back to her by a person she did not intend to repeat it to.

She took them in order, and she refused four of them in the ordinary voice, to nobody, one at a time, and it took four sentences of him and she spent every one of them and she did not get anything at all for any of them.

## The First

The first was herself.

Not her name. Her. The thing that was in her head, read out of her, written down about in a Warden's hand by a patient man who had never lied to her, and taken out of her on a date inside the winter by an office that would have paid a creditor she had never met in full and left her licence and her stages standing and it would have worked.

She had refused it in a room in the middle of the winter with two witnesses in it and had signed the refusal in three lines, and she had put her name to it, and it had gone up a stair and been entered in three books, and a fortnight ago she had stood in a low room and checked that she did not wish she had said yes.

“I am not taking it out,” said Ning Yan, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to nobody. “Take it out and put it in a hand and date it and I will say the word again.”

That was the first thing she refused, and she refused it on the same grounds and for the same reason, and she did not go near the other side of it, because there is no version of this where a person stands on a mat in a cold room at the ninth hour and looks at the other side of that decision.

It cost one sentence.

He did not say anything about it, and that was the correct thing for him to do, and she noticed the correctness and hated it and used it anyway.

## The Second

The second was the claim.

Eight hundred jin, standing, the seventh and the last thing on the back of a licence, travelling with the paper, going up every quarter with a schedule, sitting in a counting room in Lowwater that she had never been in against a date entered by somebody who was not the man who counted.

It would have gone in a breath. That was the thing about it. It would have been the cheapest of the five, because a claim is a debt and a debt is a number and a number is the sort of thing a ledger is good at, and there is a man who is owed and has been owed for a year and has not asked and has not met her and has a room within a mile of where she sleeps.

“Not the eight hundred jin,” said Ning Yan, out loud, to nobody. “He has not asked me for it once and I am not going to go into a counting room in this town and buy it off him with him in my head.”

She refused it on a mat in the cold and she did not say why to anybody, and the reason was four months old and had been made in a county office in front of two witnesses, and it was that she had signed a thing at about the fourth hour in the spring saying that she would not stop a man who was doing his job, and she had said it about him and not about what was in her head, and she had meant it about both.

It cost one sentence.

## The Third

The third was the rule his speech runs on.

That was harder, because it was not a fact about her life. It was a fact about him, and it was the reason there was a channel at all, and a woman who can buy the rule a voice runs on can make that voice say anything, and a man who has been in her head for a year and a half knows exactly what that is worth and would not have to be asked.

She refused it because the price of that is not eight days.

It is everything, eventually, in a shape neither of them would recognise, and she had read that far in the middle of the summer in about nine seconds and had not wanted to look at it since.

She spent a sentence on it and he said almost nothing at all, and the almost-nothing was the loudest thing he had ever not said.

## The Fourth

The fourth was the crew.

Nine men. An irregular schedule with a word stamped on the front of it in a clerk's hand for the fourth time in a year. Hours that were asked and worked and entered on a sheet that is not a claim. A room that goes down twice between the fourth hour and the light. A girl with a crooked leg at a trough. A keeper in a tower who was warm.

She refused it out loud, one sentence, at the fourth of five.

Not because she did not want it. That is the whole of what she said to herself and she said it once and let it stand: she wanted it, and there was a version of the purchase that would have put a name on nine men's winter and a name on a girl's leg and a name on a tower, and it would have taken the last of what he had and left a man in her head with about nothing, which is a thing that does not end.

She did not ask him about the fourth one at all. Asking would have been the first half of buying it, and she had known that before she got to it, and she refused it standing up in her own head with her hands in her coat.

## The Fifth

And then she stopped and lay still in the cold for a while and let the four refusals sit where she had put them, which is on a mat, out loud, in the ordinary voice, at the ninth hour, with nine men asleep and a fire that was not there.

She had refused four things she could have bought and every one of the four had been about her.

Not one of them had been about somebody else.

And the fifth thing was a hand.

A hand had written a date into a counting room in Lowwater, and the date was a date to answer by, and the claim was a claim against her, and the hand that wrote the date was not the counting room's and was not hers and had never in its life been hers, and a person who keeps a schedule for other people does not stop keeping it because somebody in a cold room four miles up a hill has decided he is interesting.

That was the only one on the list that was somebody else's day to keep.

And it was not an escape. It bought nothing. It bought no hours for nine men and no winter for a room and no stage and no answer in any space on any shelf. It was a name, and a name with a household on the other side of it, and a direction.

She lay there with the four sentences spent and the fifth not said, and she worked out that she had decided, on a mat, in the cold, two days before she was going to say the word, what the word was going to be for.

## What She Did Not Do

She did not say it.

She did not tell the nine men, who had read a nine and three holes in a column and a word on the front of their own schedule and had never asked her for anything. She did not tell the girl at the trough, who had a crooked leg and had arranged not to be thanked. She did not tell a keeper in a tower who had just told her that he had gone back to copying.

She did not tell him either, and that was the fourth refusal and she had not counted it as one, and he had noticed her not saying it, and he said nothing about that as well.

There were four sentences of him gone on four things she did not buy, and a finite quantity of him in her head that she had not counted, and a hundred and forty coppers under a board in a wash house four miles down, and a word that was now two days away from being said.

She lay in the cold and put her right hand flat on the oilcloth over the shape of the plate under it and did not think about what she was going to ask for.

She was going to ask for a name. That was all. She had worked her way down five things to a name in the space of an evening and she had not once, in the whole of the working, said anything about herself.
