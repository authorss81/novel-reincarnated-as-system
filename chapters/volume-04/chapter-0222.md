# The Far End

She paid a working day's rate to be set down in a field.

Five coppers to the man with the cart, on the third of the month, in the morning, in front of nobody, because a carter will take a person out past the last stake for money and money is what it costs and it is not a thing anybody arranges in advance.

He went over the rise and set her down in the middle of a field about a quarter of a mile past the last stake and went on to a yard with a gate in it, and said he would come back at the seventh hour if he was coming back that way, and he was.

That is the whole of the arrangement and it took under a minute and there is not one thing in the world she could not have done instead.

## The Ground

The drift is thin.

That is the first thing and it is the whole of the surprise and there is no other surprise on that ground. There is half a hand's depth of it in two places in fifty yards and it is dried out on top and it comes up over a boot about as far as a boot goes and nowhere else.

There is no ring.

She walked fifty yards of it with a bar and stopped and looked along it, and it is not a ring, and there is not a bar's worth of work in fifty yards of it, and a course that sent eleven men here would be paying eleven men to stand about in a field.

She put the bar into six inches of it and pulled.

It did not ring.

It made the sound a bar makes when a bar goes into six inches of anything, and that is the sound, and it stopped when the bar stopped, and there was nothing underneath it afterwards.

She stood in the middle of that field in the middle of the morning with a bar in her hands and did not move for about a minute and a half.

On the seventeenth of a month that is now two months back she had stood at the far end of the working reach with a bar she had not needed to bring and had said one sentence to herself in the ordinary way a person talks to themselves in the open.

The quiet is where there is nobody.

She was standing on it.

She had worked it out sixteen days of a bad stretch with a bar in her hands, in the worst ground on nine days, out of the residue of things that nine counties had heard out loud over four years in the order they were heard, and the only control she had ever had was herself, and there was no other person in nine days of road who could have done the arithmetic.

And she could not have proved a single line of it, and she has not said it to anybody, and she is standing in it.

There is no ring here because there is nothing here. There is half a hand of the stuff in a country that has never had a stage said out loud in it in the whole of its life, and the reason the ground is quiet is that there is not one person on it to be announced.

That is all. She had not built anything on it and was not going to, and if it is wrong then she is a woman of eighteen standing in a field with a bar in her hands on a day she paid five coppers for.

## What The Quiet Was Doing To Her

There is a thing that happens to a person who has spent two months in the ring and then stands in a place where the ground does not take a bar.

The noise in the ground was under her feet. She had been living with it — not hearing it, living with it, the way a person lives with a mill four streets off — for something like five weeks, and she had stopped noticing it the way she had stopped noticing the three counts on the flat.

And then she stood in a field where there was not one, and the place where it had been was empty, and it took her about a minute and a half to find it.

It was the loudest thing in that field, and it was in her own head, and it is not a thing anybody else on earth can hear.

She stood there with that for about a minute and a half and then she put the bar on the cart rail and let it be.

## A Woman With A Team

There was a team on the far side of that field at about the second hour and it was not a course team.

Two horses and a low flat cart and four men loading stone off a cart-road, and the loading was being done at a rate that was not fast and was not slow and did not need anybody watching, and the woman doing it was twenty years old and had a rope's end in her hand and was calling the two horses by two names.

She was about as tall as Ning Yan and about four times as brown and she had the hands of a person who has been outside since she was old enough.

She came over, because there was a woman standing in a field holding a bar and not working, and that is a thing you go and look at.

“You are not on this,” she said.

“No.”

“You are a long way from not being on this.”

“Yes.”

The woman looked at her for about as long as a person looks at a thing they are going to buy or sell.

“Wei Ran,” she said. “I run one team out past the far end and back. If you want to be picked up, you stand at the gate at the seventh hour and I do not take anybody who is standing in a field.”

She asked it once, in the ordinary voice, at about the second hour of the afternoon, sitting on the flat cart with her boots off because she had been in them since the fourth hour and the ground out here is not a ring.

“How did you get through.”

That is the whole question and she asked it the way a person asks a person, which is directly, and she had not asked anybody anything on that course in four months and she asked a stranger in a field in under four words.

Wei Ran was sitting on the wheel with the rope's end round her hand and she looked at her for about three seconds.

“I did not,” she said.

And then she went on loading, and she did not elaborate, and she did not ask her back, and she did not tell her anything else about it.

## What She Said About The Line

Later, when the stone was on and the two horses had been quietened and the four men had gone for water, Wei Ran said a thing about the ground that was correct.

“You are looking at that like it owes you something,” she said. “It does not. It is thin out here because nobody works it, and it is thin because nobody works it and not the other way round, and whatever it is that makes it thick up there is not coming from out here.”

She nodded back up the rise with her jaw.

“You have got a bar and you have been on the ring. I have got a cart and I have been on the road. Both of those are the wrong end of it and both of those are worth five coppers, and the ground up there goes quiet about a mile before anybody hears a horse on it.”

She said it as a fact. She was not comforting anybody and she did not look at Ning Yan while she said it and she did not look at her afterwards either.

It was the correct thing about the line, and it is worth saying what correct meant, because she had been on the wrong side of correct for a month and had got used to it.

Wei Ran was right. The drift is thin out here because nobody works it, and it is thin here and not thick up there for a reason that has nothing to do with the ground and everything to do with which side of it you are standing on, and the one thing she had found out in two months on nine days was that she was not the only one who could stand on that ground.

She had thought, for about four weeks, that a fact about the drift that eleven men could not work out was hers alone.

There was a woman with a cart who had worked out the other half of it on a road, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nine words, and had not thought it was worth anything to say.

## Two People Who Went Through

They sat on a flat cart in the middle of a field in the middle of an afternoon with their boots off, about nine feet apart, and neither of them said one word about either of the two things they had in common.

Ning Yan has a licence. It is in an oilcloth against her ribs with a ceiling on the front of it that has never been touched and a claim on the back of it, and she did not ask for anything she was carrying and had never once been asked for it either.

Wei Ran has nothing and went through anyway, and it is in her hands and in a cart and in four men who do not ask her anything, and she said four words about it in a field and then she loaded stone.

Neither of them said any of it out loud and the afternoon was not about it.

## What She Spent

She spent one of the ration at about the fifth hour, sitting on the wheel with the field going long, and it bought her nothing at all.

She did not find out what it bought. She let it go the way a person lets a thing go that they did not need and that they are not going to look at, because the cart comes back at the seventh hour and a woman who has spent her ration out loud in a field with a stranger in it has spent something she cannot get back and did not want.

The ration goes down on its own now. It has been going down on its own for about three months and she is the only person in the world keeping a count of it and the count is a set of four small marks on the back of a dead weight ticket and she has not added to it in four days.

He does not grow back. There is about as much of him as there was at the beginning of the month and a little less, and the voice in her head does not say anything that gets her anything, and she is not going to keep it up, and this is not a decision and it is not a cost and she had not looked at it for eleven days.

## The Seventh Hour

The cart came back at about the seventh hour, as it generally does.

Past the ground she had been standing in all day there is a rise and over the rise the ground goes down and on that ground there is a stretch that is on nobody's book and is nobody's job and is the worst of it, and a man at a trestle past the last stake named it to her in the ordinary way a week ago and told her not to be clever at it.

She had not been near it. She was not going to be near it before somebody paid her to be near it.

She got on the flat cart at about the seventh hour with her bar and her boots on and she was set down in a stand of ground about two hundred yards past the last stake at about the light, and there was a fire going at the sub-contractor's trestle and four men at it and a book open on the plank.

The quietest ground on nine days, and a woman of twenty on a flat cart who went through without permission and said four words about it and loaded stone.

And a fact she has worked out in sixteen days with a bar in her hands, which is that the ground is loud where the most has been said, and she cannot prove a line of it and she has told nobody and is not going to.

And a foot of drift on the low side of the thirty-nine that makes a noise under a bar and keeps making it after the bar stops, which eleven men and a girl of nineteen have an ordinary working word for.

There is no word within nine days of road for either of the two things she has learned this season.

The noise, and the quiet.
