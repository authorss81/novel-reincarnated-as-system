# Underneath Her

She worked six days and the shape of them was flat, the way days get when there is a column and a trestle and a bar and a girl of nineteen who works in front of her every morning without comment.

The ring got cleared and the ring filled and the ring got cleared, and the foreman moved her onto a bar and then onto a bar and a bar, and the days went the way days go when there is a column with her name in it and a trestle and six coppers and a girl of nineteen who works in front of her without comment every morning.

What she was not doing was eating.

That is the plain way to say it and it is not accurate, and the inaccurate version is the one she would have given to a doctor in a county office. She was eating. She ate twice a day for six days and the tin came out lighter every evening at exactly the rate of six coppers.

She was not hungry.

That is the part that went wrong, and it went wrong on the second of the six days and it did not go wrong all at once, it went wrong the way a cold goes into a wall.

## What Went Wrong

Condensing had made her full.

She had put that in her own head at the ninth stage, standing at a mark in a hall, and it was true: top to the soles of her feet, with no gap anywhere in her, for the first time in her life, and she had eaten more that week than in any week of her life and had been hungry every hour of it.

She had been full for four months. She had not known that full was a separate thing from warm, because she had never been both, and now she was on high ground in the first working season of her life with a chest that had not been cold since she was fourteen years old.

On the second of the six days she noticed that she was cold in a place that has nothing to do with the weather.

It is under the ribs. It is not the arm and it is not the legs and it is not the hand with the heel gone.

It is a cold that starts about two inches under the breastbone and goes down and forward and settles in the middle of her. It is there at the fire and it is there under three blankets, and it is there in the middle of the afternoon when the sun is on the far side of a camp and eleven men are taking their coats off.

She was not short of breath in any new way. Three counts on a climb. Four on a grade.

She was not hungry. She ate everything she was given and could not have told anybody what any of it tasted like.

And she was going to be cold like this for four weeks.

She did not say it to anybody. There was nobody to say it to. That is not a complaint about the course and it is not a finding about the world; a place where people are paid by the day does not have a word for what a hired woman feels at the fourth hour, and she was a hired woman.

## What It Was

On the fourth of the six days she worked out what it was, at about the seventh hour, on the ring.

Something is being built underneath her.

She felt it. That is the only honest verb available. There is a thing under the ribs and around the base of the spine that was not there in the second year of her life and that came in with the ninth and that is being worked on now by something that does not need her to agree with it, and it is heavier than what she was before it started, and it does not get warm, and it is going to take four weeks.

The work above the ninth was not being built. It was not being built at all. She had thought for about a week that it was the schedule — ten days in a column, ten days of her own hours going up a stair she could not climb — and then on the fourth day she stopped being able to tell whether it was the schedule or her.

A base is a thing later techniques get built on.

That was what she had, in her own words, from four years of being at the bottom of something. Everything she had spent on technique so far went on top of what she was, and none of it could be put back on top of what was going under her now, and the work underneath did not care what was on top of it and did not pause.

She wrote it on the back of the third dead weight ticket that evening and put it with the other two.

## The Pay

She was paid the same as everybody. Six coppers, on the plank, out of a fold, the book open beside it with her line in a hand that was not hers and a mark against the stretch.

Nobody on that course knew there was any difference between her and the man of forty at the far end of the wire. There was not. That was the entire social arrangement.

She had never in her life been so thoroughly outside a set of rooms as she was every single evening that month.

She had been inside nine counties' worth of rooms. She had been the only woman in each of them. She had been examined in one, audited in one, hunted in writing by one, refused by one, and there was not a single day of the previous four years in which she had walked into a room and not been the subject of it.

Here there were eleven men and a foreman and a girl of nineteen and a tender and about nine hundred words a night about a man whose name she did not catch.

She found this out about herself on the fourth of the six days, and found it funny for about a second and a half, and then found out that it was going to cost her something later, and it did, and she had not yet worked out where.

## The Days In Between

Two of the six days have no content at all and are therefore short.

She got up and went over the wire and worked nine hours and came back and ate and lay down in a bunk with three boards on it, and in the middle of either of those days there was not one thought in her that was about herself, and she noticed it at about the sixth hour on the second of them the way you notice a room you have been in for a while.

He had not a word either.

That was what two whole days in the middle of a ration was. It was not that he was quieter than he had been. It was that there was nothing left to spend and nothing she wanted, and she had been getting toward that for a fortnight and had not noticed it happening, because a ration that goes down by itself does not make any noise.

She did not order anything.

She did not reach for it, which she had done once on a hill nine days behind her and had not done since, and she was not going to write about that.

## Nine Days West

The crew was nine days west and nothing was going either way, which is what a line of nine days does, and which is the ordinary condition of every person on that course who has a family on it.

She thought about that more than she expected to.

Nine men in a cold room four miles up a hill, a bucket by the grate, and a girl at a frame with forty-two run lists in a pile, and a tower with a light in it and a stair carried this quarter by a district clerk's arms, and a board behind a well that had lost four loads a week on purpose, and a keeper of sixty-two who had not asked her a question since the autumn.

Every one of those was nine days away and reachable on foot by anybody with two weeks and a tin.

She had not thought of them as a line until she was standing inside one.

## The Second Blanket

Ma Sheng brought her a second blanket on the third of the six days, at about the seventh hour, and put it at the foot of the trestle and went and wrote something on a board.

No word with it.

She did not thank him.

She did not thank him then and she did not thank him afterwards, and she turned it over — not much, and not every day, and never as a warm thing. The reason was not that there was nothing to thank. There was something to thank.

The reason is that on a course where a man keeps a board and reads it out to whoever is standing in front of it, a blanket at the foot of a trestle is not a personal thing.

It was a thing a camp did for the thinnest person in it, and she was not the thinnest person in it, she was the broadest. He had brought it because he was a man who noticed that somebody was cold. And if she thanked him for it she would be thanking him for noticing, which is a thing that gets a person thanked and then thanked again.

She folded it and put it on the bunk and used it and never said anything about it, and that is the correct handling of it, and it did not make the cold go away for even one night.

## The Account

There was a thing she had been carrying since the deep cold of the winter and she touched it once in those six days, standing at a trestle, at about the eighth hour, in the ordinary voice she used for arithmetic.

It was a purchase and it was still carrying.

She was going to keep spending him for as long as there was him.

That was the shape the account had taken in the middle of the winter, and there is not one line anywhere in it that says settled. She had stopped arguing with him in a cold county on a night in the deep cold and she had not gone back to it, and the relief of that was still the largest thing she had ever got out of him and its price was that she had agreed to do it.

He was quieter at the end of this stretch than he had been at the start of it and so was she, and she noticed, and she moved on, and it was not a moment.
