# Nine Days Out

The road opened in the dark and she was on it before the light was off the hill.

The last of the wash house was behind her at the hour when the troughs stop running and the water in them goes back to being cold all the way through, and she had a bag with four things in it and about thirty coppers in a tin, and the tin had been under a board in that corner where the water does not get since the morning of the two hundred and fortieth day that she had stopped buying stone.

There was no speech at the wash house either. She had put the frame down off the pile and left the girl's four coppers on the frame's corner where the girl would find them in the morning, and she had not said why she was leaving, because the girl had not asked and had once told her out loud that asking was the thing that put a person under a line.

## The Forty-Second List

The girl was at the copying frame with her leg out straight under it and a sheet of carriers' writing face down beneath her hand, and she had been awake since before Ning Yan came down the yard.

The sheet was one she had copied the night before for four coppers. There were forty-one of them in the pile by the frame and this was the forty-second.

The carrier had wanted it copied because a carrier's sheet gets used again, and the next hand that reads it wants to know what the last hand wrote, and that is the whole of the trade. He had come in on the night before with it and gone out again before the light.

Along the bottom of it, where his own entries had been and had then been rubbed out for reuse, somebody had written a roll of hands.

Eleven of them. A foreman's name set above the list. A day rate in the shorthand carriers use when they are writing for carriers and not for clerks. And under the day rate, in the same shorthand and not in a carrier's hand at all, a reach.

**Nine days.**

She read it twice with two fingers flat on the frame and she did not ask the girl anything about it, because the girl copies lists and had copied this one for four coppers and was owed nothing further for having copied it.

She did not thank her either.

The girl had four coppers a week and four more out of a district fund for the cold months and a quarter paid forward to the thaw, and a leg that had been set crooked and would stay crooked, and three washerwomen had been told in advance that there was nothing here to thank. Ning Yan had known all of that before she came down the yard and said none of it.

What she took off the sheet was the reach and the rate. She wrote neither of them down anywhere, and she stood at the frame long enough to be sure that a roll of hands on a reused carrier's sheet was not a thing anybody was going to come back looking for.

Then she took her bag and went up the hill, and the girl went back to the forty-first.

It occurred to her on the way up, halfway, that a roster had reached a wash house in this county for the first time in her life because a woman with a bad leg is paid four coppers a week to copy things nobody wants, and that the whole of the reason she knew anything at all about the end of that road was a piece of rubbish with a foreman's name on it that a carrier had turned over and reused.

She did not think this was a sign. She thought it was a job, and she was grateful for it in a way she had no way of showing, and she went on up the hill.

## The Shelf

The county office is on the square and the shelf is by the door, and a person can walk from the wash house to that shelf in eleven minutes at an ordinary pace.

She stood in front of it in the cold and read it once.

The district's answer was four words and a space the width of a line, and the space was empty.

She looked at it the way she had looked at it on the two hundred and twentieth day and again on the two hundred and thirty-ninth and again on the two hundred and forty-second, which is to say she let it be a ruled space with nothing in it, because that was what it was, and the office that would ever put something in it was up a stair she could not climb.

Above it on the same sheet, going up past the warden's own hand and past the clerk's, was the notice. Sealed, with the frost on one corner melted once and frozen again.

*The season's road east out of this county will be reopened on the eighth of the ninth month, or later, and not earlier.*

The carriers had said a week after that. The carriers were the ones who went and looked, and the county was the one that published, and the carriers had been right and the county had been early.

That was now a fact she carried east in the same pocket as the tin, and she was not going to argue about it on a road.

She read the shelf once and she turned round and left it, and that was the last time in this whole stretch of days that she stood inside reach of a county office at all.

What it cost her to walk away from a shelf was not the shelf. It was that every document she had ever owned had a room behind it, and a room meant an address, and an address meant that a thing could be found by a person who wanted to find it, and she had spent four volumes learning that the only way any of it had ever stayed hers was that she could stand in the room.

In the morning she could not stand in any of the rooms. She would be nine days from the nearest one and there was nothing between her and whatever was in the nearest one except ground.

## The Cold Room

Nine men in a cold room four miles up a hill, and a fire that had gone down to a bed of ash again on the last two nights and had been filled twice on the last night by somebody who did not say he had filled it.

She came in with her bag, and there was a bucket already standing by the grate, full, and the ash raked flat, and nobody in that room made anything of either.

The nine men were all awake. They had all been awake since the two hundred and forty-ninth, which was the night the cold came back, and they had watched the fire go down twice a night for a fortnight and had done nothing about it, and then one of them had filled a bucket and still done nothing further about it.

She had prepared three things to say. Two of them were about the tin and one of them was about a schedule with a word on the front of it.

She said none of them.

She stood in the middle of a cold room with nine men in it and said, “The road is open and I am going east in the morning,” which was a fact that had cost her nothing to learn, and then she stood there some more in the quiet while the fire did what it was doing.

Gao held the frame.

He had said the sum out loud twice, in a hall with no roof, so that nine men would hear it from a mouth instead of off a wall. He had told them he would not say it a third time, and he was a man who did not say a thing three times to the same nine men.

So he did not say it.

He said, “The second bunk. We have not moved anything off it.”

She looked at him and could not think of anything to do with that, and did not try to find anything, and that was the end of what was said in that room.

It was a kindness she had not asked for and could not refuse cleanly, and it was also an admission, made out loud by a man who did not make admissions out loud, that nine men had spent a fortnight being cold on purpose and had decided that the reason was hers and were not going to say it to her face.

## The Marker

Qin walked her out of the valley.

Four miles down and then up onto the shoulder to a dressed stone that said nothing she stopped to read, where the county's ground stops and the county's paper stops with it, and neither of them has ever announced where the stopping is.

He walked the whole way and said nothing for the first three miles of it, which was exactly how he had been in a queue on a square in the deep cold.

At the stone he took something out of his coat and put it into her hand. It was a flat of something baked, wrapped in a cloth, and it was still warm from being inside a coat on a cold morning, and he did not say where it had come from and she did not ask.

Then he turned round and went back up the hill.

She stood there holding it until he was over the ridge, and then she put it inside her coat against the oilcloth, above a plate that had been against her ribs since the sixth day of a winter before any of this had started.

She did not call after him. He had not asked her for anything, and he had filled a bucket in the night, and a man who fills a bucket in the night has told you where he stands and does not need to be answered about it.

## The Road

The road out of the county went east along the Verge with the water on its left hand. It was a working road. There was stone going along it and men going along it and men in carts going along it, and nothing on it was sorry to be there.

By the time she had the marker behind her she had counted three, and she held it at that for a while, and then it went to four over the first rise, and then it settled at three again on the flat, and she let it settle.

She had about thirty coppers, nine days of hard road in front of her, and a body that took three counts of breath on a stair and had never once gone back to two.

She spent one of the day's ration on the first grade before the light was properly up.

She asked him what he made of the nine men in that cold room, and he gave her a true answer in four words, and she could not use it for anything at all, and there it was gone.

Then she walked.

Behind her, from that hour onward, were a wash house, a square, a shelf with a ruled space on it and nothing in the space, a tower with a light in it, a board behind a well, a girl at a copying frame, and nine men in a cold room with a bucket standing by the grate.

Every paper in her life had been written by somebody she could walk to.

She was walking away from all of them at once, on purpose, towards a book she had not seen.
