# Say It Slower

He told her the price before she asked him what it was, which is one of three things he does and the only one of the three that costs him anything, and he did it at about the ninth hour on a mat in the second bunk with the room asleep and the fire a bed of ash and the light off.

“Eight days out of about ten,” said Yuan. “That is the whole of the price. It is not more and it is not less and it is not going to be less because you are careful. Eight days, and you will be left with about two, and you will know what two is worth the day after you have spent them and not before.”

She lay still.

“You have about four counts to decide,” he said, “and I am telling you inside them, because that is when you would want to know. If it turns out I have been wrong about this, I would want to have said it before you paid and not after, and I have thought about that for two days and I have decided I would rather you had a bad four counts than a good four counts and a bill you did not know about.”

Eight days out of about ten.

She had known it would be that. She had known it for about two days and she had let it sit where it was while she did four other things on a mat in this room and refused four of them out loud, and the number had not changed while she refused them, and it was not going to change in the morning, and and there was no version of her waking up the next morning with more of him than she had on the night before.

That was the entire arithmetic of it. There was no second column and no other line and no office that could put an entry in it.

## The Form

She said it back to him slower than he had said it, which is what she does, and which is the whole of the standing that was fixed in the spring between them and has not been written out since.

“Eight days,” said Ning Yan.

“Out of about ten.”

“Out of about ten,” she said, “and about two left, and I will not know what two is until it is gone.”

“That is correct and it is the correct way round.”

“Price before the word.”

“Price before the word,” said Yuan. “Every time. I have never once done it the other way and I am not going to learn it in a cold room at the ninth hour.”

## The Sentence She Had Brought

She had brought one up the hill with her.

That is the thing about the fourth of her four, and she had kept it in the same place for a year, and the place was the ration: she kept one back every year out of what he could have said, and she spent it, once, on a day, in a room, out loud, saying that she had not forgiven him for the price of a reading in the middle of the winter.

She had done it on a wall in a lane in front of a man in a winter's coat, out loud, with witnesses. She had done it a second time in a county office, in a quieter voice, with two witnesses. And after the second one she had written down in her own hand that she was not going to say it again for a while.

A while had not run out yet. It would run out in about five days, and she knew roughly when, and she had been not-thinking about it since the middle of the winter.

The day came round the way it comes round every year, which is not on a date she keeps and not on a day of the month, and it came round somewhere in the middle of a week in which the fire was going down twice a night and a girl had been paid four coppers and a county notice had come down the stair, and it came round on the night she was lying on a mat about to spend eight days of a man on a name.

She had brought it up the hill to spend.

That is what she did with it.

She spent that day's reserved sentence on the price instead — not on the accusation, not on the wall, not in a room with witnesses in it, but saying a number back to a voice that nobody else on that floor could hear, at the ninth hour, on a mat, in the cold.

## What That Cost

It was not a big thing to anyone watching.

There was nobody watching. Nine men were asleep in twelve bunks and the second one on the left had the window board in it and the third one from the end had a cough that has run since the autumn, and not one of them turned over, and not one of them would have heard four words said in an ordinary voice at that hour if four words had been said.

She lay in the dark and did not say it.

She had been carrying it since a morning in the middle of the summer in a room with two witnesses in it, and she had gone on carrying it for a year on purpose and out loud, and she had used it once a year as though the account were being kept in his name and in a room, and she had never once examined whether it was.

It was not in his name.

It had been hers since the middle of the summer, and a claim she is keeping for somebody else is a different object from a thing she is refusing to put down, and she had gone on calling it the same because calling it the same was cheaper than looking at what it actually was.

And what it actually was, she worked out in the dark on that same night, in about the time it takes to count nine, was that she had been keeping a record of an injury in order to be able to say out loud once a year that it had not healed.

Nobody was auditing the record.

That was the shame of it, and it was a small shame and a specific one, and it was the first thing in nineteen months that she had been ashamed of that had not happened to somebody else.

## What She Did Not Do

She did not say the word.

She lay there with the price named twice and the ration on the back of a dead ticket and about four counts gone and the night's one sentence already spent on the number instead of on the sentence, and she did not say the word.

It cost her nothing. She had gone into that room with the word in her mouth and she came out of it with the word in her mouth, and the only thing that had changed was that she had said a number out loud slower than he had said it, and one of the four of the day had gone on the saying of it.

It bought her a day.

She had bought a day before with a thing that cost more than that, in a warehouse off a lane in the middle of the summer, with two witnesses in the room, and she had never once thought of it as a purchase, and she thought of it as that now, lying in the cold, and the difference between the two words was the whole of the last year.

## The Morning After The Price

She went down the hill on the second morning of that pair of days with a bar in her right hand and the word still in her mouth, which is a thing to carry down a hill at the seventh hour and up a hall floor to a cold end, and she carried it the whole way and did not say it.

Gao had the slate at the near end. She signed her own hour off the end of it with the flat of a second finger, in the ordinary hand, at the ordinary speed, and he took it and looked down the column and did not say the thing he had said in a hall on the twenty-second.

He had said it once. He was not a man who said a thing twice to the same nine men, and she had worked that out about six weeks ago and had never once tested it.

At the far end she worked forty feet of a setting bench that does not get the sun until the second hour, and the stone at the cold end is cold all the way to a hand's depth in this month, and a person's breath goes up in front of her face and hangs there.

She spent two of the day's four in the morning.

She had not meant to. She asked him one thing about the drift at a cold end that she already knew the answer to and had asked about before, on a floor, in the autumn, and he answered it in one sentence, and two marks went onto a dead card in the lining of her coat under the oilcloth in about the time it takes to put a bar down.

That was the whole of what rationing him looked like in practice. There was no discipline in it at all. There was only a thing she already knew, asked anyway, because the asking was cheaper than the not-asking and she had not known that until she had started keeping the card.

## Nine Men Asleep

The room did not change. That is worth saying because it is the thing that happens to a person who is afraid of being heard and is not heard.

Nine men slept in twelve bunks. One of them coughed, and one of them turned over at about the second hour and said something about the wind that he had said on the night before.

One of them was twenty-two, and he had walked four miles down a mountain that day, and his boots were under the bunk with the dust of it on them, and he did not wake, and nothing at all happened in that room.

The fire went down once before the light and she got up and fed it.

He put it down when it was over. She had not asked him to and he did not offer to hold it again, and that was the whole of the evening.

She lay back down in the cold with the price named twice and the word still in her mouth and a sentence a year gone on a number, and she did not forgive him and she did not tell him to stop.

She had not said the word. That was the whole of what she had done that night instead of it, and she lay there and understood, for the first time in a year, that she had been carrying a thing that was hers, and that carrying it had cost him four sentences every year that he had had no idea he was paying.
