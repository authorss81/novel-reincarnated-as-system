# What The Ear Can Tell

The thirteenth of the month came in the way the fourth and the seventh and the tenth had come, which is that she put the bar on the wall at the fourth hour and took it off it at about the seventh and eleven hours of work went into a book in a man's hand with a date on it.

The base under her got heavier through that day and she did not notice it until the afternoon, because that is what a base does. It does not arrive. It is there on one side of a day and it is heavier on the other side and the thing that has changed is not a thing you can put your hand on.

The interval had been running for twenty-six days.

## The Thirteenth

She took it at about the fourth hour in a bunk with three boards on it, before she went out, in the ordinary way, which is the way she has taken every stage for two years: she said the word and did not hold it and it went out.

[Ning Yan of the county of Lowwater, servant-born, under a ceiling written in her own hand. Foundation, stage six, is held.]

It announces and it stops there. It does not say where she was and it does not say what heard it.

## The Ninth Hour

What heard it was about nine hundred yards off, inside the wire, on ground that belongs to a man she has not spoken to in eleven days.

Nine men and a girl of nineteen and a foreman about fifty and a tender with a carriers' board, all of them idle, because a course that has closed its own working ground against a person has nothing to do with eleven men until the contract picks up again, and the contract is smaller.

They heard it. It is not a thing anybody inside the bound does not hear.

And then, before he had got his hand properly down, the tender turned about ninety degrees and looked out past the wire, at the trestle.

That is how she knows the order of it, because she was standing at that trestle at the time with a bar in her hands, and she saw the sound arrive here and then she saw him move.

He put his hand flat on the board. Not on a line, not on a column. Flat, the way a man puts a hand on a thing to feel whether it is true.

And he did not write anything.

## Two Hundred Yards

She saw the whole of it and she was working the whole time.

Two hundred yards is not far. It is far enough that a face is not a face and near enough that a body is a body, and she has spent two months learning to read men at that distance over a wire.

Nine men looked up. All of them, inside about the same part of a second, the way men look up at a thing that arrives in the ground and not in the air, and eight of them looked at the man nearest to them.

The girl of nineteen did not look up.

That is the part she went back over. Everybody else on that course has a reason to have a reaction — nine men who are idle and bored and have been idle for six weeks, and a foreman who closed his own ground against her and is still thinking about it — and the girl of nineteen had her bar in her hand and her back to the wire and she went on working for about six seconds after it came.

Then she stopped. She stood with the bar in the air the way a person stands, and she turned round and looked down her own stretch in the direction it came from, and there is nothing out that way except nine days of ground.

Cui Bao came out of the hut and stood about ten feet off with his arms at his sides and looked up and then looked down the run for about as long as it takes a man to decide there is nothing out there to find, and went back in.

Then the hand went flat on the board.

She put a bar into the ring at about the ninth hour on purpose, out of a thing she has not admitted to anybody, and it rang under her arm for about as long as it takes to count eleven, and it stopped when the bar stopped.

Same passage. Same ground. Same way of arriving.

## What An Ear Knows

She worked it out over the rest of that day and the next one, and it took about a day and a half, and it is the plainest thing anybody has ever worked out about herself.

The drift under a bar rings. She has known that since a month that is three months back and eleven men and a girl have an ordinary working word for it, and they say it as a fact about ground: a stretch that has got loud.

That is work. That is what the ground is doing.

And a thing else arrived at the ninth hour of the thirteenth, and it is not work, and it goes through the same ground and comes out of the same ground, and eleven men with eleven years of their ears on a line of work can hear the difference between the two.

They can tell.

That is what the hand on the board said. He put it flat because he had felt something come up through the props the board stands on the same way it comes up through the ground when the drift is thick, and then he stood there, and he did not write a line, because a board is a board and it carries dates and stretches and hours and prices and there is not one of those things that a sound can be entered against.

## A Wage-Book Is For Hours

She got it in the end and it took her about a day and a half, and she got it standing at the trestle over a pot with four men at it, and it is worth saying plain because it is the plainest thing anybody has said to her about herself in four years.

Cui Bao came out past the wire on the fourteenth, in the afternoon, on the ordinary business of a man walking to a place where he has to be.

He did not come to her. There were about two hundred yards of ground between the trestle and the wire, and he had crossed all of it, and he stopped at the trestle because there is a pot at the trestle and a man with a book at it, the way a man stops at anything.

“Twenty-two,” the sub-contractor said. “That is the one that is slow.”

“She is slow,” Cui Bao said. “She is slow and she is the strongest thing on nine days and I have watched her for a month and both of those are correct and I cannot make them fit and neither can you.”

The sub-contractor looked at her.

“Twenty-sixth,” he said, to her. “Five. Same as the rest.”

“I will be out before the fourth hour.”

“You will be out before the fourth hour.”

Cui Bao stood at the end of the plank with his hands at his sides for about as long as a man stands at a trestle who has not come to say anything, and then he said the thing.

“You have not asked me a single question in a month,” he said, “and I would like that on your side of this plank before I say this.”

She did not say anything.

“I closed my ground against you on the eighth of the month before,” he said. “A man in a plain coat read a referral out of a notebook at my table and said the thing in your head is unauthorised and carried by a licensee in good standing, and both halves of that are on his paper and both halves of it are correct, and I acted on it inside a week. My run is a fortnight shorter. Nine men are idle and my daughter is idle and the contract is smaller than it was on the first of this month, and every hour of that is right.”

He put his thumb on the plank the way he puts it on a book.

“And I heard your stage go yesterday at about the ninth hour,” he said. “I have heard nine of them in four years. I have not written one of them down, because a board carries dates and stretches and hours and prices, and there is not one line on this reach that a sound goes into.”

He looked at the two hundred yards of ground between the trestle and the wire.

“So that is my half of it and I would do the whole of it again on Friday,” he said, “and you may stand there and be angry about it and I would rather you did than that you thanked me for it.”

Then he said: “I am not going to say it out here again,” and walked back inside the wire.

## Anger

The anger came at about the seventh hour of the fourteenth and it was clean and it was not at him.

It took about four minutes to find where it was pointed, and it was not at a man. It was not at nine idle men and a girl of nineteen who have a rule nine years old that has been right twice, and it was not at a tender who put his hand flat on a board and did not write, and it was not at a sub-contractor who pays five and is right about the difference.

It was at the fact that all of them are right about her.

The ground is loud where it has been worked and quiet where it has not, and the crews know that and are paid by it and are correct. The line holds where it is kept. A wage-book is for hours and prices. A man may close his own ground against a person he does not carry.

And every one of those is true, and there is not one version of being right that leaves her name in either book with a run in it.

That is what the sixth stage cost.

Not the four counts, and not the cold, and not the eleven minutes a line and not the two feet of it in one movement on ground that eleven men cannot follow her onto. What it cost is that every person who is right is right about her too, and she is standing on ground that has stopped paying her, and the strongest body on nine days of road is standing at a trestle at the fourth hour being the slowest hand on somebody else's stretch.

She wrote it out that night on the back of a ticket with the flat of a second finger and took eleven minutes over the two lines and did not write it again.

She is six stages past the front of a document she has never touched, and the ear on that ground knows it.
