# One Name and a Column

The road does not open in any of the three days this was counted over.

It opens in the morning after it, and the notice on the shelf by the door of the county office says the eighth of the ninth month, and the carriers' word — which is not a notice and is not sealed and has never once been wrong — says nearer a week after that.

She is going on the county's arithmetic.

There is no other arithmetic. A notice with a seal on it is a thing a person can act on, and a thing a person can act on is worth seven days of being wrong, and there is nothing else in nine counties that is worth acting on.

## What She Had

She counted it out on the back of a dead weight card in the yard behind the Hall on the second of those three days, in daylight, with the sun on the wall, because a thing you have only done in the dark you have half done.

A licence with nothing above nine on the front of it anywhere in the world, nailed straight over a second bunk, seven conditions on the back that she had read once and has never read again.

An eight hundred jin claim with a date written against it in a counting room in this town by a hand that is not that room's, entered on the seventh and last thing on a piece of paper, and the date is still in there.

A ruled space the width of a line on a public shelf, still empty, that the district has left empty since the middle of the winter and has put nothing in and will put nothing in.

A quarterly schedule with the word on the front of it that no clerk on that square is going to take off while nine men are on it.

Nine men.

A girl at a trough with four coppers a week and four more out of a district fund and a quarter paid forward to the thaw and a bad leg.

A keeper in a tower of sixty-two who is warm, and whose stair is carried this quarter by a district clerk's arms, and who has not asked her a question since the autumn and has asked her nothing about any of this.

About thirty coppers under a board in the corner of a wash house where the water does not get, in a tin, chosen on the sixteenth of the month, and not spent.

A Clay Plate against her ribs above the oilcloth, and a register in a building four miles away that still reads *reconciled*.

A dead weight card with a column of marks on it that thins as it goes down, and a working name written under the column in her own hand at the ninth hour in the cold.

And him.

She came to that last one and stopped, and she did it on purpose, because she had learned in the last ten days that a person who counts a voice like a sack of stone is going to get a number and then argue with the number.

There is not much of him.

She wrote that on the card, in her own hand, with a pencil stub, in the sun on the back of a scrap of dead work.

**There is not much of him. Enough.**

That is the whole of the count and she did not put a figure on it and she was not going to, and if anybody ever read that card they would find a woman who had counted a thing she owned and had refused to put the count in writing because writing it down makes it a schedule and a schedule is a thing somebody else can use.

## Why East

Not the hunt.

That was the first thing she put on the card and then scratched out. It was true and it was not the reason, and she was not going to carry a reason into a cold county that was a kind of running.

The hunt is behind her and out of reach, and out of reach is not safe; it is only somewhere she cannot get to from here.

A referral has gone up a stair past every office she has ever stood in front of. It carries her name and her stages in order and her hours, and it carries no agent's name, and the only thing that can execute it is past a boundary no county paper crosses.

Staying inside keeps her out of its reach. It also keeps her under a ceiling of nine with nothing above it on any piece of paper in the world.

Both halves are true and she had been living inside both of them for a season.

So it was not the hunt.

It was this.

A relicensing needs a schedule of hours worked at the ceiling asked for. She knows that, because a registrar in this county wrote it on a bar in the ordinary hand, on the ground that this county holds no schedule of work done past its boundary and is not able to certify one, and then wrote a last line slower than the rest saying the answer to her application is not that office's to write.

And that is the whole of the trap, and she had turned it over and looked at it from every side in three weeks:

She needs hours worked past this county's boundary. No office inside this county will certify them, because this county does not employ anybody out there, does not examine anybody out there, and does not hold a second hand on anybody out there, and a county cannot certify a thing it has no hand on.

And she has a name.

**Luo Ping.** A working hand. A schedule-man, thirty-nine, who keeps a schedule for somebody else's office and is sent to put dates into it, four or five offices in a season, with a wife and a boy of eleven who live past this county's boundary and have for some years.

She did not buy the claim and she did not buy the answer and she did not buy herself. She bought a direction, and it points east, and east is the only direction in the world in which an hour she works could go into a book that an office above this county can read.

She stood in the sun against the wall of the Hall and held the card and worked out that she had spent eight days of a voice on the cheapest useful thing she could find in the whole of her life, and that it had pointed at a road.

## The Frame

On the third of those three days she worked a shift at the near end and came in off it at about the ninth hour and wrote the Hall's own sheet for the quarter out in full, with the flat of a second finger, in the ordinary hand, in a hall with no roof.

Eleven minutes.

Gao held the board and said nothing, because he said a thing out loud in a hall about six weeks ago so that nine men would hear it from a mouth, and he had also said it a second time a fortnight ago and had told them he would not say it a third time, and he was a man who did not say a thing three times to the same nine men.

He did not ask her what she had done with the tin. Nobody had asked her about the tin. The fire had gone down twice a night for a fortnight and had been going down twice a night for a winter, and the difference between those two things is the difference between a thing that has happened and a thing that has been arranged, and nine men had let it be arranged.

She put the pen down and squared the sheet.

## The Thing She Was Not Going To Say Out Loud This Year

There was a sentence she had brought up the hill on a night in the middle of this month to spend, and she had spent it on a number instead, and then there was the day it came round, and then there was the lane and the wall at the end of it and four minutes of standing there.

She was not going to say it.

Not this year. Not on a wall, not in a lane, not at about the seventh hour on a bench, not on a mat at the ninth hour with nine men asleep.

What had replaced it was not forgiveness. She had gone over that in three weeks and had it exactly where it belonged: it was a purchase, and she had made it on purpose, and it cost a sentence she had been saving for a year, and she was going to keep spending him for as long as there was him.

And the account was carrying. It was carrying at the end of this month and it would be carrying in the spring, and there was not one line anywhere in it that said settled, and the whole of what she had done in the last ten days was to change the shape of a thing she was keeping rather than to put it down.

She did not forgive him. She did not tell him to stop.

She had stopped arguing with him.

That is the difference and she noticed it standing at a frame in a hall with no roof on the last night of three days, and it is not a small one. For a year and a half she had argued with him, out loud, in an ordinary voice, on mats and in lanes and once in front of a wall, and the arguing had been the whole of the relationship and she had thought that a person who is in an accounting with something spends the accounting arguing.

She had stopped.

What she was doing instead was budgeting. She had a card with a column on it and a name under the column and a rule about what not to buy, and she went through the days spending him the way a woman spends a tin of thirty coppers, and the tin was never going to last past a road and neither was he and she had stopped telling him so.

## What She Did Not Do

She did not pack.

There is nothing in four miles to pack nine days of hard road out of. There is a coat with a licence in the lining of it, an oilcloth, a plate, a card, and about thirty coppers.

She did not say goodbye. She did not say it to a girl with a bad leg or to a keeper with a cleaned lamp chimney or to a board behind a well, and she was not going to say it to nine men either.

She did not tell them what was coming in a way that would be a speech, because a speech is a thing a person makes when they are frightened of a room, and she was not frightened of that room, and because there is nothing in this county she can offer nine men that is better than what they have and she has no authority to make nine men anything.

She counted at the frame.

She wrote a column of marks on a dead weight card in her own hand, and under the column a working name, and under the name a rule about what not to buy with him, and she put it inside the oilcloth against her ribs above a plate that has been there since the sixth day of the winter before any of this, and she lay down in the second bunk in a cold room with the licence over her head.

The road opens in the morning.

She is going east, on purpose, with a name and a rule and about thirty coppers, and the last thing she did before the light was put her right hand flat on the oilcloth over the shape of the plate and work out, one more time, the exact size of what she had left to spend.
