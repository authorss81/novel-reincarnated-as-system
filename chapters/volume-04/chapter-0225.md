# The Blank Reason

The sheet comes round once a month.

That is the arrangement and it has not changed in four years, and it is a strip of county paper about the length of a forearm with an attendance column on it that is not joined to the hours column, and it travels east on a man who runs his own round and goes back west on the same man four days later, or eleven, or whenever he gets to it.

It came round past the last stake on the fifteenth of the month with a cart of oil and salt behind him, and it came at about the second hour, which is when it always comes, and it is still folded in three when he got off the cart.

## The Carrier

His name was Pei and he was about sixty and he carried things for a living on the far side of the cart turn, and he had a coat that had been repainted twice and a way of standing that came from thirty years of getting off carts.

He put the sheet on the plank of the trestle face down, the way a man puts a thing down that has not been written on yet, and stood over it with one hand on it.

The oil went into the store in four rounds by the two of them, about eleven coppers, and the salt after it, and the woman behind the counter did not look up once, because it is a thing that happens on that round and she has seen it every month for years.

“There is you,” Pei said. “Twenty-two. I am the hand that writes in that column.”

She said nothing.

“She is absent,” Pei said. “And after that column there is one that is a foot and a half wide and it has been empty three months running and it is not mine to fill, because it travels down the same road as your hours do and I am not a man who writes in your hours.”

He put his finger on the empty column and left it there for about the length of a cart being unloaded.

“I have said that to you before,” he said. “I will not say it again until I have to write in it, and when I write in it I will write *member absent* and leave the other one as it is, and that is not me being hard on you. That is me being a man who does not write in a column that is not his.”

“Who told you to leave it.”

“The office the month you started.”

“You did not ask them why.”

“No,” Pei said. “It is a column and I am a carrier and about eleven coppers of oil is what I am paid, and I have carried this strip four times now and it is the fourth time I have seen that column empty and it will be the fourth time I have left it.”

He put the sheet in the inside of his coat, folded in three, against his chest.

## Why Not

There was a word she could have written and she did not write it, and she had not been offered it a second time by either of the two decent men who had offered it once, and she was not going to be offered it a third time by anybody, and the reason is not modesty.

The word would have gone in the column after the attendance column and it would have travelled west nine days to an office that had already written a date against a standing claim in a counting room, and the reason column is the column her hours go in.

That is the entire reason. It is one sentence and it has been true for four years and it is the single reason there is.

She had been offered the word twice, once by a decent man at a trestle inside the wire and once by this man, on an earlier round of his, and each of them had offered it exactly one time, and she had not taken it either time, and both of the sheets had gone west with a foot and a half of blank paper in the reason column.

“Twice,” she said.

“Twice.”

“You have not asked.”

“No,” Pei said. “If I ask then I am a man asking a question about a column, and in a year I will be a man who asked one, and there is not one thing I want out of you that is worth that.”

She looked at the empty column for about as long as it takes to read a column and did not put anything in it.

## The Sixteenth

On the sixteenth he wrote *member absent* in the attendance column in the hand that writes *member present*, and the reason column went west a foot and a half wide and blank, and that is the third time, and it is not payable in hours.

He did it standing at the plank with a pen out of his coat and it took him about the length of a man writing a date, and he read it out afterward because a man reads what he has written when there is a woman standing there.

“Member absent,” he said. “Sixteenth. Nothing after it.”

“That is three.”

“That is three,” Pei said, “and it will go again in thirty days and again in thirty days after that, and I will carry all of them and I will not be asked to write one word in any of them.”

He folded the sheet into three and put it inside his coat against his chest, which is where a man keeps a thing he is carrying west, and then he went and got his coat off the rail and swung it up onto his shoulders with the strip in it.

He was going west that afternoon.

She walked out past the last stake with him about a hundred and forty yards, which is as far as a person can walk with a man and still be walking with him, and then she stopped and let the cart go on.

It went out over the rise at about the fifth hour with eleven coppers' worth of oil sold and a strip of county paper against the driver's chest, and it went down the road past the cart turn and out of sight, and she stood on the rise and watched it until it was gone and then stood there for a while after it was gone.

## The Road West

There is a board at the course's trestle with dates on it, and the dates are when a carrier goes west, and a woman who is nine days east of a county office can stand in front of that board and learn everything there is to learn about whether anybody is coming for her.

There is no date west on it after this man.

She had known that since a month that is three months back, when the carrier came back east on the third and went west again and there was a date, and the man after him runs his own book and leaves when he leaves.

So that was the last strip of paper with her name on it that will leave this reach for the rest of the run.

Not a carrier was coming back either. A carrier comes east on a round and the round is a round, and the round does not come back unless there is a reason and there is not going to be a reason, because the only reason there would ever be is her and the county does not know where she is and the county has not asked.

## Bread

She bought bread at the store that evening and it was five coppers and she had about five coppers, and she ate it sitting on a stone past the trestle with her back to the wind, and the sub-contractor's pot was going about eight feet off with four men at it.

That is the arrangement and it has been the arrangement for two months.

The sixth stage had cost her something in the afternoon three days before that was not the cold and was not the hand. Eleven men on a course with nothing to do had heard a thing go up out of the ground at about the ninth hour, and not one of them had been able to write it down, and she had watched a tender put his hand flat on a board and take it off again.

That is what a stage costs on ground where people are paid to hear ground.

She had learned to work inside a blank column going west about three months ago and the learning took about two minutes, and she had been proud of it in the way a person is proud of a thing that costs nothing.

There were three of them now, at thirty-day intervals, and the reason column was a foot and a half wide every time, and she was carrying all three the way she carries the missing order in her left hand, which is to say she had stopped expecting any of it back.

At the seventh hour the four men at the pot were laughing at something, and she turned her face away from the firelight on purpose, in case it was a thing that wanted a person in it, and went and slept in a hut past the last stake with three boards in it.

The strip went west without her in it and she lay on three boards and listened to the wind coming off the rise, and there was nothing in that country to write down about her at all, which is a different thing from what she had expected, and is the first good week she has had in four years in the way that a thing being out of your hands is a good week.
