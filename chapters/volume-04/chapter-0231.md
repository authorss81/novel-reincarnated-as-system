# The Price Before The Word

He was on the high ground at about the fifth hour on the twenty-fourth, and he was on it on the twenty-fifth and the twenty-sixth and the twenty-seventh, and he was on it on the twenty-eighth and the twenty-ninth and the thirtieth.

Seven mornings. He came down off the rise about two hundred yards out from the trestle and stood where a man stands to see the ring, the board and the plank without being in any of them, and he wrote, and stopped, and stood there, and wrote again.

That is the whole of what he did for seven days. It is more than anybody else in nine counties has done about her in four years, and he did it standing up, in weather, and he did not come past the last stake once.

## What He Did Not Do

He bought nothing. There is a store at the cart turn and he came down the road past it at about the seventh hour every evening of the seven, at the hour she comes out of it with bread under her arm, and he went by the front of it at about nine feet and did not go in.

He threatened nothing. He touched nothing. He did not come into the hut with three boards in it, or the store, or the eight feet of fire she pays five coppers a day to stand near, and he did not stand near her at it on any of the seven nights.

He entered nothing and he asked her nothing, and on the twenty-seventh, at about the sixth hour, standing about eleven feet off in the ordinary voice of a man reading the day out, he said all of that in one breath so that she would not have to spend a morning on any of it.

“I have not bought anything, I have not threatened you, I have not touched you, I have not entered anything and I have not asked you a question,” he said, “and I am not going to start, and I want you to have that in the same breath as everything else so that you do not have to spend a morning on it.”

He said it the way he had said it in a hall with no roof and on a step in a lane and in a market and once on high ground about four months back, and she had heard it five or six times now and it had never once been anything else.

She stood where she was and did not go over to him, which she had wanted to do at about the second hour of the second day and did not do then and did not do on any of the other five.

## The Week Of Holds

She worked the seven days on the same stretch with the same mark painted on the plank beside it, at five coppers, eleven hours, the fourth hour to about the seventh, and the man at the far end of the plank with the boot came apart again on the twenty-fifth and said it wanted doing twice.

She lost four holds and built seven that held to the end of a stroke, and she wrote the seven on the back of the dead weight ticket with the flat of a second finger at about eleven minutes a line, in a hut with three boards in it, in the dark, while four men at the pot eight feet away were telling a story she turned her face away from on purpose.

She had about five coppers in her hand on the twenty-fourth and about five on the thirtieth, and the number was the same both times and she had stopped finding that remarkable somewhere in the third week of doing it.

And she was at four counts of breath on the flat by the second of the seven, which is a thing a body does at seven stages and which is not coming back to three, and the bar wanted more of her on the twenty-ninth than it had wanted on the twenty-fourth, and she did the same eleven hours anyway.

## What It Costs

She spent one on the twenty-sixth, at about the fifth hour, sitting on a stone past the trestle with the bar across her knees and the wind coming off the rise behind her.

There were three marks on the back of that ticket and there had been four for a fortnight, and one of the four went on the twenty-second of this month and bought her four sentences, and there had not been four before that for about three months.

She let one go.

It bought her the price, in the ordinary voice, and he said it once, and he did not attach anything to it.

“One day out of the three you are keeping,” he said.

That was all of it.

He did not tell her not to do it. He did not tell her to do it. He did not tell her what a day is in the ordinary sense of the word, or where it comes out, or what it is when he is holding something he is only holding on her word.

He had said the number and he had stopped, the way a layer stops when it has announced something.

Three marks on the twenty-second. One of them on the twenty-sixth. Two left.

She sat on the stone in the wind and worked that out on her own, in about the time it takes to count out a day's coppers.

One of those two is the price of standing it for the crossing, and the crossing is under a day, and after it there will be one mark left in her and she will be spending it anyway, and there is not one thing in the world that puts any of it back except work.

She got up off the stone and carried the bar back and put it against the hut and did not write it on the back of the ticket with the flat of a second finger, because it was four minutes to write and eleven to write properly and it was not going to be different in the morning.

## What Went In The Notebook

Three lines went into it over the seven days and she never saw the page and never would.

The first: that a course on nine days of road, lawfully and under a rule nine years old used twice and right twice, had closed its own working ground against a person on the seventeenth of the month before and that the closing was correct.

The second: that the person in question had been hired on the same day by a sub-contractor outside the wire, at five coppers against six at the course, and had put her hours in his book in order with dates, and that the course's book holds her to the seventeenth and nothing after.

The third: that she was working past the last stake on ground that was not the course's, on a stretch that carried somebody else's mark, and that she had said her name out loud at that trestle in daylight, and that she had asked for nothing since except bread.

He wrote all three down on the seventh evening and closed the book and went up the rise, and he was not two hundred yards from the trestle any of the seven times he wrote.

## The Part Of It That Was Not A Threat

She went over it for two days afterward and it came out the same every time, and what it came out was the worst thing he has ever done to her, and he did not do it.

Four months back he stood on high ground in front of her and said, in the ordinary voice, that he did not want his own order executed, and he wanted the answer to stay pending, because a pending answer is a question somebody above him has to answer and he had never once believed the answer would be the right one.

He is at the fifth hour on high ground seven days out of seven, and he is writing down that the thing he does not want has been done, and he is writing down how well it was done and by whom and at what price, and he is not lying in any of it, and there is not one word in the whole of him that she could put in front of a man with a plank and call a lie.

She wanted him to lie once. That was the want and she had not known she had it until about the second hour of the twenty-eighth. Four years and eleven documents and two offices that wanted something out of her, and she had never once wanted a thing from the man in the plain coat except that he would be wrong about something, so that she could be angry at a person instead of at a procedure.

He said it four months back on high ground that he did not want it. He was attending it. He was keeping the minutes.

That is the difference between a betrayer and an honest man who is losing, and it is the whole of why she cannot leave and cannot come closer, and she stood at the trestle on the thirtieth at about the seventh hour with five coppers on the plank and understood that she had spent one of the last three of him on the price of it, and that it was the right purchase, and that it was going to cost more than the three of them together.

## Five Days

The fifth of the next month is five days off the thirtieth.

She had said the word and not the price at the trestle on the twenty-second, and she had said neither on the twenty-third, and she had heard the price on the twenty-sixth, and there was nothing standing between her and the bad flat now except five working days and one of them being the first of a month, when a man with a book writes a day's line before the day is worked.

Three marks, one spent on the twenty-second and one on the twenty-sixth, and one day of standing to pay for on the fifth, and one left.

And one mark was four sentences, and she had been keeping the count on the back of a ticket since the tenth of a month that was now two months back, and she knew to within one exactly what four sentences bought, and a quarter of a mile of quiet ground past the cart turn was not going to be done on four sentences.

She had one mark left in the world and she was going to spend it on the morning of the fifth, standing it in one go, and then she was going to go out past the cart turn with a bar and stand in a foot and a bit of ground that does not tell anybody anything, and the only thing that lowers what he owes is a thing that gets finished.

That is the whole of the thirtieth and it is where she went into the next month.
