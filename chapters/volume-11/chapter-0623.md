# The Rule She Wrote In The Ash

She worked on it from the sixth hour of the sixth of the month beginning 1,874 and she gave it six days, and the six days are the whole of it.

The rule was this and she had it standing in a yard at the fourth hour of the first, in four words she could say standing up. A told thing cannot be written. Only a true thing can go in her own hand.

That was the whole of what six days of standing in a yard with a furnace in it produced by the second day. After that she had nothing to work out, she had a decision to put off, and that is a different kind of day and it is much harder to get through.

## What Her Hand Does

She tested it on the seventh, at the seventh hour, in the north corner of the yard, out of sight of the low.

She had a scrap of the heel of the loaf — the paper under it, the part a person does not eat — and the stub of a nib, and she opened the cake of ink, which had not been opened in two months, and it was still good.

She wrote six.

And what came out on the scrap was five.

She wrote six again and five came out again. She wrote it four more times and got five three times and four once, and she understood exactly what had happened, and it had been happening for four years and she had had five days to think about it.

Her hand writes what she means at the moment it goes down. It does not write what she intended when she picked the nib up. She had meant six hundred and forty, and six hundred and forty is a number she has carried since the twenty-eighth of a month beginning 1,814, and her hand had gone down and meant five.

She said it in the north corner of the yard with her back to the low.

“If my hand writes what I mean and not what I plan, then I cannot write a line in a hurry. And I cannot write a line about a thing I have been told, because I will mean the being-told and not the thing.”

## The Three Things She Was Frightened Of

She got all three of them out on the eighth of the month beginning 1,874, sitting on the stone wall with the nib in her hand and the cap off, and the third was the real one and the first two were only the shape of it.

She was frightened that the page would turn out to be about somebody else.

That is the oldest one and it has been her trouble since a month beginning 1,784, when a carter put four names on a board and took them east and she was not one of them and has never been able to follow a name that anybody else hands her anywhere.

A name she has carried thirty-one days in the air came out of a woman’s mouth on a made road. It fits nothing. It cannot be fitted to a furnace and a number and a boy of nineteen without a person in the middle of it holding it in place.

She was frightened that the page would be ordinary.

That one was harder and she said it anyway, to the stone wall, at the fourth hour of the eighth of the month beginning 1,874.

“What if it is a line. A date and a weight and a boy, and it is all true, and it is all of it, and there is not one word in it about what it was like to be him.”

She sat with that for a while.

“A thing written down truly can be a very small thing,” she said. “The man with the shut notebook told me on a slope that a true thing is not the same as a thing that is yours, and he was right, and that is the whole of what anybody has said to me about this in thirty-one days.”

And the third one was the real one and it took her two days to get to it.

She was frightened that she would read it and it would not matter.

## The Third One

She got it at the sixth hour of the ninth of the month beginning 1,874, standing at the mouth of the furnace with her face turned away from it because she was not going to do it twice in thirty-one days.

It was this.

If she read the page and it held, then she would have a true morning in her hand. And a true morning would be the only thing anybody has ever given her about him that was worth the walking. And she would have it, and she would not be able to do one single thing with it.

Because a told past does not reach her, and there is no reason on this earth to think a read past is different, and she had proved the told one at the sixth hour of the fifth of a month beginning 1,844, standing in a made road with the words perfect and the whole thing a hand’s width from the nib.

She said, to the furnace, with the heat on the side of her face:

“Then I can write a name and I cannot write a morning, and the name is the smaller of the two, and I am going to spend the six leaves I have on the smaller one because it is the only one I can do.”

He said, “You have got that exactly backwards and I am not going to correct you.”

She said, “Say the part of it that is not the correction.”

He said, “I am not going to say it. If I say it now you will write the smaller one to get at the larger one and you will have used a leaf on a day you were frightened.”

She said, “That is true and it is the second time in six days you have let a thing sit in my hands.”

He said, “It is the fourth day of six and you have two days to spend and I am going to let you spend all of them.”

## The Practical Part, Which Took A Whole Day

On the tenth of the month beginning 1,874 she found out how to hold a page and write at the same time with one arm, and it took her the whole of it, and it is here because it is what actually happened in the six days and not one word of it was about a boy.

A page propped against a wall goes over when your hand goes across it. She tried it a dozen times against the low wall in the north corner and it went over nine times and did not go over twice, and both of the times it did not go over she had got the figure wrong.

She borrowed a board from inside the low. She asked for it and the woman gave her the flat end of one, a thing two foot by a foot and a half with a chalk figure on the side of it, and she did not thank her, and the woman did not appear to want her to.

She put a stone under the back of the board so it was at a slope, and she put the page on it, and she got the nib down on the first line and it worked, and that was the most useful day she has had since the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,814.

## Six Leaves

She counted them on the last of her six days, at the sixth hour, sitting on the stone wall with the board across her knees.

Eight leaves, stitched at the fold of a blanket and soft as cloth. The first one carries a date and a place. The second one carries four sentences she has read aloud once and will not read again.

Six that are hers and blank.

She counted them once. She did not count them five times the way she did on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,814, because there was no point in it. She got six, and one of them was going, and she knew which one.

## The Decision, Said Out Loud

She said it at the seventh hour of the last of the six days, standing in the doorway of the low house, to a woman of forty-five who had been up since the fourth hour.

“I am going to read the page tomorrow and I am going to write down one line of it in my own hand in my own book, and I am going to write the date the page gives me and the place I am standing in and the name on the page, and I am going to write them because I have read them with my own eyes and I will know all three of them to be true while the nib is going down.”

The woman said, “And if your hand does not agree with you.”

She said, “Then I will not write it, and I will have lost a leaf, and I would rather lose a leaf.”

The woman said, “You have not asked me for the time.”

She said, “Sixth hour of the twelfth. I will bring the board and a stone and the thing I write with and I will not open it on the ground.”

The woman said, “Come at the sixth. I will turn it and I will leave the book open on the table and I will go out to the yard, because I have read this kind of thing done to a page before and I am not having it done in front of me.”

Ning Yan said, “I would not have let you stay.”

She said, “I know that, which is why I am going out.”

## The Night Before

She got an hour and a half, and sat on the stones outside the gate that night with the bag open beside her and the leaves folded back and the nib uncapped and the cap in her teeth, because that is a thing her hands can do.

He said, “Say it once before you do it.”

She said, “I am going to put a name into a book of my own where no hand can take it out of it and no fire at a furnace will burn it, and I am not going to be able to be a person who does not know it afterwards.”

He said, “That is the whole thing and you have had it since the seventh of the month beginning 1,844.”

She said, “I have had it since the sixth of a month ten years back, when a page came out of a shut book with my own name at the end of it and the word *lost*, and I have spent ten years being careful.”

He said, “Yes.”

She said, “And I am not going to be careful now. I am going to be exact. That is a different thing and it is the only one I have.”

She put the nib cap back on with her teeth, and lay down on the stones with the book under her coat on the side where the paper is, so that the two things against her ribs lay side by side and neither of them moved.

She was lighter at the seventh hour of the last of the six days than she was at the seventh hour of the seventh, which is three hundred and twenty-four days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first.

And she went to the sixth hour of the twelfth, because she had one day, and she had been given it by a woman who was owed nothing and had asked for nothing and had said a name out loud for the first time in nineteen years on a made road to a stranger with a bar.
