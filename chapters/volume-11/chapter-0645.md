# What She Said In The Yard

He put it in front of her at the sixth hour of the second of the month beginning 1,994, at the gate, standing, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, with the furnace going over behind him and the smoke straight up.

He said, “I was a piece of a thing and the thing was burned out of me at nineteen in a place that wrote it down true.”

He said, “I am not a keeper and I am not an office and I have never had a book that anybody could take off me, and there is not one thing about me that is being kept by anybody including me.”

He said, “I am telling you this now because you are going to say a thing in this yard in two days and I am not going to let you say it to a fragment.”

She said, “Say the rest of it.”

He said, “There is no rest of it. There are three sentences and I have given you all three and I am not going to give you a fourth one, and the fourth one is a name I am not giving you today.”

She said, “Why not today.”

He said, “Because you have not asked me for it and because in two days you are going to say something in the open and I am not going to be standing in your head with a name in my mouth at the same time.”

And then he was quiet, and he stayed at the gate, and she said nothing else for four minutes, and neither of them said anything else about it that day.

## The Day Before

She worked on the second and she worked on the third of the month beginning 1,994 and she did not say it anywhere, and the man of fifty-one hung the board up at the sixth hour on the third with her figures on it where anybody could see it.

And she went out of the east gate at the seventh hour of the second and walked east for three hours on the flat and up on the rising ground, and she came back at the tenth hour and did not do the count, which is the first time in ten years.

She said out loud at the gate on the third, at the ninth hour, to a yard with a furnace in it, and there were thirteen people on that ground inside ninety feet:

“Tomorrow at the sixth hour I am going to stand where the board is and say a sentence in this yard. It is going to be about me. It is not going to be about the woman with the book or the man on the bench or the man with the board or the man who carries the ash, and every one of those four has been exactly right the whole way.”

“And not one of them is going to be able to un-say it or answer it or stop me.”

He said, “You are telling them in advance.”

She said, “I am telling them nothing. I am standing here talking to myself where four people can hear me and not one of them is going to know what it was about.”

He said, “That is not the same thing.”

She said, “It is the only thing I have got.”

## The Sixth Hour Of The Third

She stood at the west end of the long window in the low at the sixth hour of the third of the month beginning 1,994, with the board on the nail behind her and her own figures on it, and the man of fifty-one sitting at the plank table writing, and the boy of fifteen at the stack calling the first figure of the day.

And there were thirteen people on that ground inside a hundred yards. The man of seventy-one was on his bench at the north end. The woman of forty-five was in the doorway of the low house with the book. The man of fifty was coming off the floor with a bar over his shoulder and ash on both his forearms. Three men were at the mouth. Seven more were on the kiln floor and along the north wall.

She did not ask any of them to be there.

She said it in the ordinary voice, standing, in daylight, once.

“I am going to say one thing and it is going to be true and I am not saying it to anybody in this yard.”

“The tax is designed.”

“It was made to a figure. It was made before there was a ground to put it on, by somebody who chose twenty, and it is one in twenty on every ground and it has never changed and not one person who collects it can change it, and it is counted in days and not in money so that it comes off the same whether you have earned a great deal or not.”

“Not one person on any ground has ever been told their own total.”

She said, “That is the shape. That is all of the shape. And I worked it out on the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,964 with a stick in the grit, out of four counts and a figure off your board and the day count of a furnace that has not been let out in nineteen years.”

She said, “And here is what it is for.”

The boy of fifteen stopped calling figures.

She said, “It is for finding people like me.”

## The Silence After It

Not one person said a word for nine seconds and she counted them because she counts, and they were nine, and there was a furnace making noise the whole time.

He said nothing.

He had said in advance that he was going to say not one word and he said not one word, and he was in her head, and not one word came out of him, and she went on standing there with her hands at her sides and let the nine seconds be nine seconds and did not fill them.

The man of fifty-one put his nib down on the plank table.

He said, “I did not know that.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “I have written that figure on that board every day for nineteen years.”

She said, “I know you have.”

He said, “I could not have told you any different one. There is not one person on this ground who could.”

She said, “No. And that has never once been anybody’s fault on this ground and it is not anybody’s fault now.”

He said, “What do you want me to do.”

She said, “Nothing. I want you to go back to the board.”

And he did, and he picked the nib up and went back to the board, and that is all that happened to him.

## What The Ground Did With It

The man of fifty came four feet off her and stood there with the bar in his two hands.

He said, “Second hour.”

She said, “You put that in the yard.”

He said, “I have carried that barrow out nineteen years and I have not said one word about it to one person, and a girl of nineteen has just stood in a yard and said what was in it and where the sentence ends, and I am fifty and I am not going to be the only one on this ground who knows what came out of that gate.”

She said, “You were not the only one.”

He said, “No. And you did not ask me.”

She said, “I did not ask you.”

He said, “Right,” and he turned round and went up to the stack and took his bar.

The woman of forty-five said one thing from the doorway and it was short.

She said, “It is a good thing you did it and I am not going to say any more about it,” and she went in and shut the door.

## What She Said Next

She said it at the seventh hour of the third of the month beginning 1,994, at the gate, in daylight, standing, to a gate and a furnace and a man who has just been told what she found out and is not going to answer it.

She said, “I have said it in the open in front of thirteen people and none of them asked me anything about me and one of them has gone back to his board and one of them has gone back to work and one of them has shut a door. And not one of them is wrong and not one of them was ever hiding anything and that has been true for ninety-eight days.”

He said, “What are you going to do.”

She said, “Stay.”

She said, “I am not going anywhere. I have got no road behind me that goes back, no road in front of me that goes anywhere, no wages, no place, no board I am on that is mine, nine coppers I cannot count, a brother two hundred yards north of here on a made road who walked west past me at dusk and has not walked since, and a voice in my head that came off the same thing that made the levy.”

She said, “I am nineteen years old and I am nine stages past the ceiling and I have been lighter every single day since I was a child and I am going to be here when it is tomorrow.”

She said, “You told me in the space on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,964, or you told me here, or you have been telling me since the eighth of the month beginning 1,964, that you were not going to un-make it.”

She said, “So here is what has happened, and it cannot be put back.”

“There is not one person on this earth who does not know that a girl of nineteen stood in a yard on the third of the month beginning 1,994 and said that the tax on this ground was made to find people like her.”

“I said it once. I am not going to unsay it. I am not going to soften it. I am not going to be the person who found it out quietly and kept it, because I did not keep it, I said it, and there are thirteen people on this ground who heard it and one of them has already gone back to his board.”

He said, “Say the last of it.”

She said, “I am staying.”

She said, “And I am staying knowing what it is for, and I am not going to be able to un-know it, and I am not going to be able to un-say it, and there is no road out of here that would give either of those back, and I have walked out of four places in ten years and I am not going to walk out of the fifth.”

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the third of the month beginning 1,994 than she was at the fifth hour of it, which is four hundred and thirty-six days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back.

And she slept on the stones outside the gate with her bar under her head, on a furnace ground, at the foot of a two-acre line of stone, four hundred yards from a bench where a man of seventy-one had a book shut under his two hands, and the furnace did not let out, and the smoke went straight up.
