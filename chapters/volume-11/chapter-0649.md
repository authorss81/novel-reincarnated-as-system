# The Sky Says Nothing Back

She went out of the east gate at the fourth hour of the eleventh of the month beginning 1,994 and lay down on the flat with her face to the east and her ear against the ground, because that is the thing she has done nine times in nine years and there is not one other thing a person can do with a stage.

She lay there until the light came up off the flat and there was nothing.

Not a small thing. Not a thing that was late. None of it, and the ground under her cheek was cold in the ordinary way that ground is cold, and there was no eighth minute of any hour, and there was no tenth minute of a tenth hour coming.

She sat up at the fifth hour and did the arithmetic of the morning out loud to the two miles of flat, standing, with the bar across her knees.

A bar nine feet nine. A skin. A bag with a dish and a lump of clay and a ring a finger’s width and a stub of a nib and a cake of ink she opened on the seventh of the month beginning 1,874 and has used four times since.

Nine coppers she cannot count. A book of eight leaves with five of them blank. And a piece of paper in a clerk’s fair hand on a board at the north end of the yard behind her, and a line on the same board in the same hand a thing that happened on the fourth of this month at the tenth hour in the open.

And a place nine paces by six on a kiln floor that she has not written down anywhere.

And nine stages under her feet and nothing above them.

## Seven Days Of It

She did it seven days running.

At the fourth hour every morning of the eleventh to the seventeenth she went out through the east gate and lay on the flat with her ear down and stayed there for an hour and a half, and on the morning of the seventeenth it rained four hours’ worth in twenty minutes and she lay in it and it did not make any difference to what she was listening for.

She worked the days in between. Five days of barrows at a fifth of a man’s rate. The long hold at the pit twice, at seven each, and four days of work on the leg after it. The board went up at the tenth hour every day with her figures on it in a fair hand and the man of fifty-one did not ask her one question about any of it.

The boy of fifteen called every figure and called her figure and it was right.

The woman of forty-five kept her door shut on three days out of seven.

The man of seventy-one sat on his bench at the north end with his hands on his knees and did not open the book, and on the fifteenth she sat down four feet off him for twenty minutes and neither of them said a word, and when she got up he said, “The morning after was the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,964.”

She said, “It was.”

He said, “That is all I have got and I am not going to open the book.”

## What The Silence Is

This is the one thing she has never had to think about, and she thought about it once, on the fourteenth, at the fourth hour, on the flat, in her own head, and she did not say it out loud then or afterwards.

The layer announced her three times in this life and the third one was the last one it had.

Not because it ran out. Because nine was the end of the count, and it had been the end of the count the whole time it was doing it, and it told her so in the only language it has, which is a sentence with a number in it.

She was the last address it ever had.

That is all of it and she did not explain it to herself and she did not put it in her book, and there was no one on that ground she could have said it to except a man of fifty-one with a board, and it is not a figure and it does not go on a board.

## What She Could Not Say Out Loud

At the gate on the sixteenth, at the ninth hour, in daylight, standing, with a yard behind her and a furnace going, she got as far as the start of it and then stopped.

She said, “The last one was on the fourth and it is the last one there is going to be.”

And she stood there for a dozen seconds with the furnace making noise.

He said, “Say the rest of it.”

She said, “I cannot say the rest of it out loud here.”

He said, “Why not.”

She said, “Because it is not about me and there is not one person in this yard it is about, and if I say it here then it is a thing I have said in a yard, and I said one thing in a yard two days ago and I am not doing it twice in seven days.”

He said, “You are not going to put it in your book either.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “That is a thing you have been doing for nine years.”

She said, “Yes. And I have got five blank leaves and I am not going to use one on the last thing the layer ever did, because the day I write that down it stops being the last thing and starts being a line I have got.”

## The Patch On The Kiln Floor

On the fourteenth she went and stood on the nine paces by six.

Off the east slope, four paces down, nine paces along the north side, in through the one way on to the floor, because there is one way on to the floor and that is why a page written by a person standing there did not have to say where it was. Four courses of stone from the bank at the east. A gap the width of a nail in the whole of the north joint.

She stood on it for a minute and a half with the furnace going behind her and then she stepped off it and walked out of the yard and did not write it on a leaf.

He said, “You stood on it.”

She said, “I stood on it.”

He said, “You went to it.”

She said, “I have gone to it twice and I have not stopped on it once, and I am not going to stop on it, and the reason is that it was put in for him and he was not a person who had a place put in and the laying of it did not ask him.”

He said, “That is the first reason you have given for anything that has been the true one.”

She said, “It is the first one that has been about me.”

## The Seventeenth At The Second Hour

She packed at the second hour of the seventeenth and there was nothing to pack.

The bar on her left shoulder with a bar of fire bound into the lining of it. The skin. The bag. The book of eight leaves. The paper against her ribs on the side where the book is, and she did not call either of them anything.

Nine coppers in the front of the bag, and she put her hand in and took them out onto the flat of the bar and counted them and got nine, eight, nine, and she put them back.

She went and stood at the long window and said, “I am going at the fourth hour.”

He said, “I know. You said you were going on the fourth.”

She said, “Your figures are on the board for six more days and then the board goes down on the second and I am not here for it.”

He said, “I told you what I would do.”

She said, “I know what you will do. I am saying it out loud so that if somebody asks you in four months where the girl with the bar went, there is one person in nine days of road who can answer them, and you will not have to guess and you will not have to be kind about it.”

He wrote nothing down, and he did not look at her for two seconds, and then he went back to the columns.

She did not say the other thing. She has not said it to a living person since she was a girl and she was not going to start on a man who is going to rub her out of a board on the second of a month.

And at the fourth hour she went out of the east gate on a furnace ground with the bar on her shoulder and the furnace going over behind her and the smoke straight up, and the man of fifty was at the mouth with a bar of his own and did not look up, and the boy of fifteen was on the low roof with a bit of chalk in his hand.

She was four hundred yards off the ground and on the two miles of wet ground with no carts on them when she looked up, for the first time in ten years that she could remember doing on purpose, and there was nothing up there over that ground that had ever been named to her by anything at all.

The bar came off her shoulder at the four hundred yards and went onto her right, because the right is the arm that is open four days in five and it was the fourth day of five, and it came off at the four hundred yards on purpose and it stayed off for the rest of that day and for the next two, which is what she has done since she was fifteen.

She walked the two miles of wet ground with a rut in it and nobody on them and then the wall with the opening standing open in it, four feet and a bit, and then the two miles of flat, and there was nothing at all to lie down on between the gate and the wall and there had not been for as long as she could remember, and she had used that fact nine times in this life and this was the tenth.

She was lighter at the fifth hour of the seventeenth of the month beginning 1,994 than she was at the fourth hour of it, which is four hundred and fifty days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back, and she was walking west with her hands empty and nine coppers she could not count and there was not one person in the world who was going to tell her anything.
