# The Count Holds

She counted on the third of the month beginning 1,904 at the fifth hour, sitting on the stone wall on the west side of the yard with her feet in the ash, because it is what she does instead of anything else, and because she had decided at the fourth hour of the second that she was going to count what it had cost her to get here and she was going to do it properly or not at all.

It took her four hours and it is the whole of the third of the month beginning 1,904.

## The Days

Sixty-four days from the turn to this wall.

She got that out three times and it came out sixty-four three times, and she wrote it in the ash at the foot of the wall with the end of one finger and did not rub it out.

Twenty-five days of that was on this ground. Forty was on her feet on nine days of road and then on ground going up. Two was at the turn.

She said out loud, to the ash, at the seventh hour:

“That is the only thing I have lost that anybody can name.”

He said, “That is not true and you have got an hour of this morning left to work out why.”

## The Coppers

She put them on the flat of the bar on the wall, all nine, and did it four times, which is the most she has ever done with a row of metal.

Nine, nine, eight, nine.

Nine, nine, eight, nine.

Nine, nine, nine, ten.

Nine, nine, eight, nine.

She got seven four times, one twice, ten twice, and she never once got nine standing in the sun on a wall with a bar under them.

And the whole of that is a person handing a number over, which is the one thing she has never in her life had to do with money before the twenty-second of the month beginning 1,814.

A number you keep is a number that stays where you put it. Nine, five times.

A number you hand over goes into another pair of hands as well and you have not got it on your own any more. That is not about being slow and it never has been and it was never going to be.

She said, at the seventh hour and twenty minutes:

“Nine coppers. Two a loaf. Four days.”

She said it out loud twice and got four both times, and four is a number that holds, and she has not checked why it does and one of them is a day she has spent.

## The Bricks

She had not done the multiplication in nine days.

Two hundred and forty courses. Six hundred and forty to a course.

She did it on the wall with her left hand and a stick, because her right was open, and it took her a while and she got it three times.

One hundred and fifty-three thousand, six hundred.

She said, out loud, on a stone wall on a furnace ground, at the seventh hour and forty minutes of the third of the month beginning 1,904:

“One hundred and fifty-three thousand six hundred bricks a day.”

He said, “That is right.”

She said, “Every one of them counted by a boy who was not being paid for counting them.”

He said, “That is also right.”

She said, “For how many years.”

He said, “You are not going to ask me that one today.”

She said, “I am not. I am going to sit with a figure.”

## Nineteen Years

Three hundred and forty-six days of it.

She has been lighter every single day since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 and not one of them going back, and she counted that on the wall and got three hundred and forty-six, and it comes every day.

Nineteen years at that fire, if a year is three hundred and sixty-five days, is six thousand nine hundred and thirty-five.

She wrote that in the ash under the first figure and it took her a while and she had to start again, and she got six thousand nine hundred and thirty-five twice.

And then she sat with the two numbers side by side for about ten minutes.

Three hundred and forty-six.

Six thousand nine hundred and thirty-five.

She said out loud, at the eighth hour of the third of the month beginning 1,904, to a yard and to a furnace and to a man who has no day and no name and a hundred and fifty-three thousand six hundred bricks in him:

“I have been coming apart for three hundred and forty-six days and he was at that fire for six thousand nine hundred and thirty-five, and I am the twenty-fifth part of it, and there is a hole in my chest that has been open since before he stopped counting and it is not going to close, and I have been carrying it around for ten years like a trade I did not ask for.”

He said nothing.

She said, “Say that again and see if I have it.”

He said, “That is the ninth time you have said that since the seventh of the month beginning 1,844.”

She said, “It is the tenth.”

He said, “No. The seventh, the eighth, the ninth, and this one. Four.”

## The Test She Set Herself

She did the rest of it at the eighth hour and twenty minutes with a stick in the ash, on purpose, arranged like a row of barrows, and it took her the better part of an hour and she has never gone over anything so carefully in her life.

She put down six figures and said six things and she did it in the order they had come to her, oldest first, and she was checking one thing only: does it come back when I ask.

One hundred and fifty-three thousand six hundred bricks. It came.

Six hundred and forty to a course. It came.

Nine coppers. It came back nine four times and ten once.

Three hundred and forty-six. It came.

Six thousand nine hundred and thirty-five. It came.

And then:

Ninety-one courses laid along the top of a kiln wall in the second year, taken up again on his own time, and neither of them said a word about it.

It came. She said it back word for word, out loud, on a stone wall.

And there was nothing behind it.

A furnace east of here, a long way east of here. It came as words. There was a place behind it.

Nineteen, when he stopped counting. It came as a word. There was no boy behind it and there has never been.

Two syllables a woman said on a made road on the seventh of a month beginning 1,844. It came, four times, perfectly. There was nothing behind it and there never was.

## The Finding

She got it out at the ninth hour with the stick in her hand and the ash going cold, and she said it out loud once and did not say it twice, because a thing said twice is a thing somebody can change and she was not letting it change.

“The count is the only instrument I have got and it works on everything, and it works on money, which I did not believe until the twenty-second of the month beginning 1,814, and it does not work on one single thing in the world, and the one thing it does not work on is a person.”

He said, “Yes.”

She said, “A number is a thing about the world and it stays where I put it. A thing somebody has done to a person is not a thing about the world. It is about him. And it goes through me.”

He said, “Yes.”

She said, “And I have been counting bricks and courses and coppers and days for ten years because they are the only things in this world I can hold, and I did not know that was the rule. I thought I was counting because I liked it.”

He said, “You count because you are frightened and it is the oldest thing you can put your hands on. The counting came second and the frightened came first and it has never once been the other way round.”

She said, “That is a cruel thing and it is true and I am not going to be pleasant about it.”

He said, “I know. It is why I have never said it.”

## The Hour After

She sat on the wall at the ninth hour and thirty minutes and she had not eaten since a quarter of a loaf and she had not been hungry since the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,544 and she was not going to start now because the arithmetic had started, and the arithmetic was the only place she could stand.

And then she worked out the last of it, and it took her about ten minutes, and it is the last thing she did on the third of the month beginning 1,904.

The count holds.

The telling does not.

And there is one page in the world where that morning is written down true, and it was shut on the first of the month beginning 1,874 and the woman who keeps it said no to her once and told her exactly why and was right.

She said, out loud, to a yard that had gone quiet on her in about nine days without her noticing it had gone quiet:

“I am going to go and ask her tomorrow, and I am going to have one reason, and the reason is not that I am a girl who has walked forty days to ask.”

He said, “Say the reason so that you have said it.”

She said, “The reason is that I read a day out of my own book at a wall on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,874 and it did not reach him, and she has had it in ink for nineteen years, and she is the only person left on this ground who can put a true thing in front of him, and I have to find out whether that is worth anything at all.”

He said, “That is a good reason.”

She said, “It is the only one I have got and it is nine days old.”

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the third of the month beginning 1,904 than she was at the fifth hour of it, which is three hundred and forty-six days of it, and she had a name on a leaf and a day on a leaf above it and a figure in the ash she was not going to rub out, and she went and got four hours of sleep on the stones outside a gate with the bar under her head.
