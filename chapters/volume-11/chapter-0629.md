# A Yard With A Fire In It

She noticed the rest of it on the twelfth of the month beginning 1,904, and she put it on the stone wall at the sixth hour so that there would be two of us holding it, and she was not aware for another two days that she was doing it in a voice.

The boy of fifteen still called her figures. The man of fifty-one still wrote them on the board and hung the board up on the nail where anybody could see it. The ten men and two women in that yard still came in and out of a gate she was standing next to.

And in four days on that ground not one person had spoken to her.

The board went up with her entry on it and got read by four people and not one of them said a figure to her at the end of a day. The bowl had stopped coming out at the sixth hour on the second of the month beginning 1,904. There was no bowl on the step on the ninth, the tenth, the eleventh or the twelfth.

She said it at the sixth hour of the twelfth, on the wall, in the ordinary voice, to an empty yard.

“Nobody has said a word to me in four days and I have not been thrown off and I have not been told anything and I have not been given anything, and I do not know yet which of those I am reading as the thing that has happened.”

He said, “Say the one you have not said.”

She said, “I think I did this.”

He said, “Say it plainer.”

She said, “I think I asked a woman for her husband’s book and it has gone quiet on me because of it.”

He said, “That is not what happened and I am not going to let you build a story out of a thing you have not checked. Check it.”

She said, “I am going to.”

## Across A Working Yard

She crossed the yard at the seventh hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 1,904 to get to the trough, which is about nine steps from where she sleeps, and a man of forty with a long bar came round the corner of the furnace at the same moment with a course on it.

She did not hear him. That is the part she has gone over.

Her right arm was open and the heat came off the mouth at her side and she was in front of a square hole in a brick stack with a bar of fire two inches thick coming down about four feet from her head, and the man with the bar shouted, and she moved about a foot and a half and not far enough.

The bar came down where she had been standing.

She went on to the trough and drank out of it with her left hand and put her back against the stone and shook for four minutes, which is not a figure she put anywhere.

He said, “You did not hear him.”

She said, “I did not hear him.”

He said, “That is your fourth one and your fifth.”

She said, “Explain that to me.”

He said, “I am not going to. You have four fingers and a thumb on one hand and a right arm that is open four days in five and four counts of breath that go to seven inside a loaded reach, and you have walked across that yard nine times in a month and the last two of them you did not hear a man shouting at you from twenty feet because your attention was on something else.”

She said, “That is not a cost anybody has named.”

He said, “It has not been named because it is the fourth stage and it has been yours since a month beginning 1,724 and I am not spending a stage on it. That is not what stages are for.”

## The Doorstep, The Fifth

She asked at the seventh hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 1,904, standing in the doorway with the bar against her hip and the yard behind her going on the way a yard goes on.

She did not ask for the book.

She said, “I read you a day off a leaf of my own book at a wall on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,874 and it did not reach him.”

The woman said, “Say the rest of it.”

She said, “I have had that day in a book against my ribs for thirty days and it came off a page in your low house in a good hand and I read it out loud in daylight at a wall and there was nothing on the other side of it.”

She said, “And you have had it in ink for nineteen years. And you are the only person left on this ground who can put a true thing in front of him. And I want to know whether that is worth anything at all.”

The woman stood in the doorway and did not go in.

Then she said, “Come in and shut the door, because that is the only reason you are getting an answer and not the street.”

## The Question She Was Asked

Sitting at the table with the stove going, the woman asked her the first question anybody on that ground had asked her in two months.

She said, “What are you going to do with it.”

Ning Yan said, “Read it.”

“That is not what I asked.”

Ning Yan said, “I have got a name on the third leaf of a book of eight leaves with a day above it and a place above that, and I wrote it at the sixth hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 1,874 in my own hand off your page, and it cannot be put back. And I have wanted one thing since the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,874 and I have not been able to get at it out of a mouth.”

She said, “Say the thing.”

She said, “I want him to have one morning. Any morning. Nineteen years of a man counting bricks is not a person and I have got a hundred and fifty-three thousand six hundred bricks a day and it came out of the count and the count was the only instrument I have got.”

The woman said, “And if he has it and nothing changes.”

She said, “Then he has it.”

The woman said, “Say it once more. I have never asked anybody for anything and I want to know what asking for it is like.”

She said, “Then he has it.”

## What The Woman Said Back

She said, “I have thought about that since you stood in the road and I have not got an answer and I am not going to pretend I have.”

She said, “It is my husband’s page as well as the boy’s.”

She said, “I know it is. I have not come here to forget that and I have not come here to make it be only one of them either.”

She said, “If I open it again I am going to turn it and go out to the yard, and you are going to read the whole of the entry yourself, and then you are going to sit at that table and find out what you are going to do with it, and I am not going to be in the room for any of that.”

She said, “All right.”

She said, “Not now. Tomorrow, at the sixth hour, and the fire is drawn at the seventh and I do not have it out of the ground for a girl with four counts of breath.”

She said, “Why.”

She said, “Because I have had a bar come down where you were standing this evening and I am not having you at my table with an open book on it.”

## The Whole Of It

She lay on the stones outside a gate with the bar under her head and did the thing her mind does when it cannot be still, which is the count, and she put all of it in a row and got every figure.

Sixty-five days from the turn. Forty-nine of them on this ground. Nine coppers. Two a loaf. A fifth of a man’s rate and the same board. Two hundred and forty courses at six hundred and forty to a course, which is a hundred and fifty-three thousand six hundred bricks. Three hundred and fifty-five days of the hole in her chest. Nineteen years at a fire, six thousand nine hundred and thirty-five days of it if a year is three hundred and sixty-five.

Five leaves that are hers and blank.

And one of them was going to be used, and she did not know on what.

He said, “That is the whole of it.”

She said, “That is the whole of everything I have.”

He said, “That is a good deal less than you had a month ago.”

She said, “It is a good deal more than I had on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,814, and I know which way that is and I am not going to be argued out of it by a count.”

## The Fifth Hour And Forty Minutes Of The Thirteenth

She was at the door at the fifth hour and forty minutes of the thirteenth of the month beginning 1,904 and the woman opened it before she knocked, which she had not done once in two months.

The book was on the table.

The string was off it and lying beside it and the cloth boards were open and it was open at a page and the woman stood beside it for four seconds and then went out and shut the door behind her.

Ning Yan stood at a table on a furnace ground with a bar of light coming in through the long window at an angle, and a book open at a page, and ten feet of it, and a door on the other side of it with a woman standing outside it.

And she did not put her hands on the table.

She put them behind her back, the way a person does at the front of a fire, because that is what her hands wanted to do, and she stood there and looked at a page in a good hand that had been dry for nineteen years and had never once been read out loud to anybody.
