# Second Hour, Nineteen Years Ago

He came to the gate on the fourth of the month beginning 1,964 at the sixth hour in the morning and stood at the ten feet he had stood at the seventh hour six days before, and he had not come to the west end of the floor.

She said, “You came over here.”

He said, “I came over here.”

She said, “Why.”

He said, “Because you did not follow me. There is not one person on this ground who would have followed me. The last one who might have followed me has been dead nineteen years and she was not going to follow a barrow either.”

She said, “That is not a reason and you know it is not a reason.”

He said, “It is the only one I have got and I have had six days with it and I have not thought of a better one.”

## What He Would Not Say

She said, “You are not going to tell me what was in it.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “Say all of that so I do not come back.”

He said, “I am not going to tell you what was in the barrow and if you ask me again in a month I will not tell you then either, and you can go and ask the woman and she will not tell you, and the book will not tell you, and there is not one person in the world who is going to tell you, and that is not a thing I have decided. That is a thing I have found out.”

She said, “Then why are you here.”

He said, “Because there is one other part of it and I have not said it out loud to one person in nineteen years and I am fifty and I would like to have said it.”

She said, “Say it.”

He said, “Tomorrow.”

## Six Things He Said, Over Three Days

He said them on the fifth, on the sixth and on the seventh of the month beginning 1,964, at the gate, standing, in daylight, one at a time and not all in one go, and she did not write one of them down.

On the fifth:

“I carried it out at the second hour.”

He said, “That is the only hour in the day I have ever carried anything out of that mouth at, and I have carried something out of it at the second hour every day for nineteen years, and on the morning after that one it was not the same barrow.”

On the sixth:

“There was not one person at the gate.”

He said, “And there was not one person on the track for a mile and a half after it.”

On the seventh, in the last of the light, with his forearms in front of him and the ash from the seventh hour still on them:

“I did not know what was in it and I did not look down into it.”

He said, “That is what I saw. A morning going out of here the way ash goes out.”

He said, “That is all I saw, and it has taken me nineteen years to be able to say it, and I have not said one word of it to your woman or to the man with the board or to the man on the bench, and I am saying it to a girl of nineteen who asked me one question at the west end of a floor and walked away after it, and that is why I have said it.”

## Where Ash Goes

She walked to it.

That is on the third of the month beginning 1,964 at the seventh hour, and it is a mile and a half down the east track to where the ash from that ground is put, and it took her two hours and twenty minutes going and she did it with the bar off and the bag on her hip and she got there at the ninth hour and stood there for about ten minutes.

It is a heap in the bottom of a flat with a low wall of stones round it that is not nine feet high and is not stone, and it is about nine paces by six at the top.

And the same black ash goes on it every day from every ground that sends ash down this track, and the man of fifty-one has a figure for how much comes off this ground in a month, and it is on the board once a year, and it is the only figure on that board that is ever struck out.

She said it out loud at the top of the heap, at the ninth hour and ten minutes of the third of the month beginning 1,964, to nine paces by six of black ash at the bottom of a flat:

“It is the same shape.”

And then she sat down on the low wall and worked out what that meant, and it took her about half an hour and she did not like any of it.

Nineteen years ago the thing that went out of that gate came down this track to this heap. And this heap is nine paces by six at the top, and it is a different nine paces by six from a different thing on a different floor four hundred yards north of where she is sitting, and it is a shape that a heap of things makes when a heap of things is not looked after.

She said, “Two nine by sixes. One of them is four courses of new stone that a man cut for a place. The other one is the shape that happens when nothing is done on purpose.”

She said, “They are not the same and I am not going to sit here and make them the same because it would be a better story.”

And she got up off the low wall and walked a mile and a half back to a gate at the tenth hour with her arm open and did not say anything to anybody about it for two days.

## What The Book Has, And What It Does Not Have

She got it out on the seventh after he had gone, with a stick in the grit outside the gate, and it took her an hour and it is a short sum and it is the largest thing on this ground.

The ground’s book has the day, and the two weights, and the difference, and the name at the foot of it in a good hand. It is accurate. It has been accurate for nineteen years and a man of seventy-one has never been wrong once in it.

The woman’s book has the same day and the same weights and the same difference and it is her husband’s page.

The board has a figure on it every day and the board has been hung on a nail where anybody can see it for nineteen years.

And not one of the three has ever said what went out of that mouth at the second hour on the morning after.

Not because it was kept. There is nothing on this ground to keep it with. The book does not have a column for it. The board does not have a line for it. It was not written because it was not seen, and the not-seeing was not done by anybody.

She said it out loud at the ninth hour of the seventh of the month beginning 1,964, sitting on the stones outside the gate with her back against the wall and her bag under her hip:

“Nineteen years of true writing and not one hour of it is a record of a thing going out of a gate at two in the morning, and the reason is not that somebody decided. It is that there was not one person there.”

## The Morning She Was Not In

And then she found the thing she had come for, and it was not the one she had asked for.

A boy of nineteen was taken at the sixth hour of a morning on this ground. She has got that, on a page in a good hand, in three books, in two of them by two different hands that agree.

The next thing that is known anywhere on this earth about that boy is that a barrow went out of that mouth at the second hour the following morning, through an east gate and down a track for a mile and a half, with not one person at the gate and not one person on the track.

That is a morning.

It is not the morning he was taken in. It is the morning after, and it is an hour long and a mile and a half long and it has got a man in it who was thirty-one and did not look down, and it is the first morning anybody has handed her about that ground that she can put her hand on.

She said, to the stones, at the ninth hour and twenty minutes of the seventh of the month beginning 1,964:

“I have been asking for one morning for seventy days and I have been asking for the wrong one. I have wanted the hour it was taken in, and that hour belonged to him and to a man who was doing a work on himself for nine years, and it does not belong to me and it never did.”

“And no book was ever going to hold it for me, because I was not in it, and I have known that since the seventh hour of the thirteenth of the month beginning 1,904, and I did not do one thing about it.”

She said, “The hour after belongs to the man with the ash on his arms. And he has given it to me. And it is the only hour of it that has ever been given to anybody.”

## What Cannot Be Proved

And she put the last of it down flat at the tenth hour, because she is a person who puts the last of it down flat, and because she has learned in eight days that a thing half said is a thing somebody can change.

She said, “I am not going to stand here and tell a man of fifty that he carried something out of a gate. I am not going to tell the woman her husband’s page is wrong. I am not going to tell the man of seventy-one that his book has a hole in it. Every one of those three has written down what they were given, true, and not one of them was asked what it was for and not one of them could have known.”

She said, “And I am not going to tell anybody that the morning went out of here unwatched on purpose, because I do not know that and there is not one thing on this ground that would let me find out.”

She said, “What I am going to say, in the open, once, is that not one person on this ground watched, and that is a different sentence, and it is the true one, and I have got a man of fifty to back it.”

He said, “You have got me to back it and I am fifty and I am not going to be asked again.”

She said, “I am not going to ask again.”

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the seventh of the month beginning 1,964 than she was at the fifth hour of it, which is four hundred and ten days of it, and she lay down on the stones with her bar under her head, and she did not do the count twice, and she slept for the first time in six days without waking at the fourth hour.

And the question she did not ask him, and is not going to ask him, and has told him she is not going to ask him, is whether a thing that goes out of a gate in the morning is gone, and that question is the only one left in this that has no book in the world with an answer written true in it, and she is nineteen and it is hers.
