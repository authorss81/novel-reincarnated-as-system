# The Third Thing He Owes

She worked the ladder out on the eighth of the month beginning 1,964, sitting on the wall at the west end of the yard with her feet in the ash, and it took her most of a morning and it comes out the way it always comes out, which is short and plain and does not improve.

One thing he owes: what he was before he started counting. Asked on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,814, at dusk, at a turn, out loud, alone, and it cannot be un-asked.

Two things he owes: the day before that one. Asked on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,874, at the seventh hour, standing, in daylight, and she got three things where she asked for one and took no fourth.

And three: what it was for.

That is the one she has not asked and she has had it ready since the seventh of the month beginning 1,874, and she has not put it out loud in seventy days because every time she got near it she heard herself asking a second thing with it.

She said out loud, to the ash at the seventh hour and twenty minutes of the eighth:

“I have been carrying a why of him about a furnace ground for seventy days and I have not said it once, and the reason is that the why and the who are the same question on this ground and I do not know how to ask one without the other one standing next to it.”

He said, “Ask it in the terms.”

She said, “That is why I have not asked it.”

## The Question, In The Terms

She asked it at the sixth hour of the tenth of the month beginning 1,964, at the gate, in the open, standing, in daylight, with the furnace going over two hundred yards behind her and the smoke straight up and about four people on that ground within ninety feet.

She said, “What was the tax for.”

One at a time. In daylight. Standing.

And then she waited, which is what she has done on every question she has put to him for four months.

He said, “You asked me what it was for.”

She said, “I did.”

He said, “You did not.”

She said, “That is not a fair thing to do to a person who has kept the terms for seventy days.”

He said, “You said what was the tax for. That is a what. A what is smaller than a why and it is the one I have.”

She said, “Say that again so that I have got it.”

He said, “You are asking me for the reason. A reason is a thing I have not got and have never had, and if I had it I would not hand it over on the strength of a question asked standing in the open in front of four people, and you would not want it if I did.”

She said, “Say the last of it again.”

He said, “You would not want it if I did.”

## Three Things Where She Asked For One

He gave her the shape instead, and he gave it in six pieces over two days, and he did not give it as a speech.

On the tenth, at the seventh hour, at the gate:

“It is not a sum. That is the first thing and it is the thing almost everybody has wrong. It is counted in days.”

On the eleventh:

“One day out of twenty.”

On the twelfth, in the morning, at the trough:

“The same on every ground. That is not a figure anybody on any ground sets. It arrives.”

On the thirteenth:

“It comes off the board. Whatever a person has been put down for that month, the day comes off it before the board goes up, and a person who has earned nothing pays it the same as a person who has earned a great deal, because it is counted in days and not in anything.”

On the fourteenth:

“No person is ever told what their own total is. Not once, not on any ground, not by any office or any keeper or any man with a board.”

And on the fifteenth, in the last of the light, with her sitting on the stones outside the gate and him standing at ten feet with ash on his forearms:

“It has not changed since before I was at that fire and it cannot be changed by anybody who collects it, and that is not a complaint. I have said that it is not a complaint. I am telling you the shape of it, because you asked a what, and that is all of the what.”

## The Woman Who Married The Man Who Paid It

She went to the low house at the seventh hour of the fourteenth of the month beginning 1,964 and she had not been in that doorway since the twenty-second and the woman knew it before she got there.

She said, “Did your husband pay it.”

The woman said, “Nine years. Every month, off a board, in days, and he never once told me what a day was for and I never once asked him, because he was at it nine years and a person does not ask a man that at that fire.”

She said, “And now I am telling you I asked somebody else.”

The woman said, “You asked a man who has been in your head for eight years and you are nineteen years old. That is not a criticism. I am telling you what I would have said if I had asked him on the day he started, and I would not have said it, and we would both have got to where we are.”

She said, “He is not a man.”

The woman said, “He was a man on that floor for nine years and he had a book he was not allowed to read out and a name he did not put on anything, and I married him, and he did not tell me one thing about it in nine years and I have had nineteen years without him and not one of the nineteen has been about a levy.”

She shut the door and it was not a slam.

## The Figure Off The Board Again

She went back to the long window in the low at the sixth hour of the sixteenth of the month beginning 1,964 and asked him for one thing and it was the smallest thing she has ever asked a person for.

She said, “Read me the figure under my own entry for the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,874.”

He said, “Why.”

She said, “Because I read it four times that day and it came out the same four times and I could not hold it, and I have not been able to produce it since, and I have been carrying it as a failure for forty-six days.”

He read it out.

She said it back. It held.

She said it back a second time. It held.

She said, “That is the first figure in my life that has ever come back the second time and it is the first one I ever read and could not hold.”

He said, “It is a fifth of a copper.”

She said, “Say it again so I have it as a sentence.”

He said, “A fifth of a copper off a man’s day, every day, on every ground, and no person is told and no person can change it and it is not on anybody’s board as anything but a fraction under a weight.”

She wrote that on the back of her board in her own hand at the seventh hour of the sixteenth and it was still there on the nineteenth when she divided it, and it is the only sentence in any of this that she has ever had to write down.

## What The Shape Is

She said it out loud at the ninth hour of the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,964, standing at the gate, in the ordinary voice, to a yard and a furnace and a man who is not in the room.

“A day out of twenty. Every ground. Counted in days and not in money. Taken off the board before the board goes up. The same to a person who has earned not one copper and a person who has earned a great deal. No person has ever been told their own total. And not one person who collects it can change it.”

He said, “That is right.”

She said, “Say the part about not being told again, because that is the part that is not like a tax.”

He said, “No person has ever been told their own total. In nineteen years at that fire and in eight years of a girl’s head before it. It is taken, and the taking is accurate, and the total goes up and the book does not come back.”

## The One She Did Not Ask

She stood there for a while after that.

Then she said, “You have given me a what.”

He said, “You asked for one.”

She said, “I have spent seventy days being told things by people who were right, and not one of them told me this. I have been on the far side of a line for four years paying it every month on a board I could not take my own name off, and I have never once asked what it is.”

“And that is not because it was kept from me. It is because it is the one question in this life that has never once looked like it belonged to me.”

He said, “It belonged to you from the first day you crossed.”

She said, “Then why did I not think of it until I had run out of people to ask.”

He said, “Because you were busy.”

He said, “You have been asking a sequence and the sequence had a next one on it and the tax was not on the sequence. You put it there on the eighth of the month beginning 1,964 at the seventh hour and twenty minutes and it will stay there now.”

She said, “That is all I have got.”

She said, “I asked you why and you gave me a shape, and I am not going to be angry about it, because you told me in the terms that you would answer the smallest true thing and you have kept to it every time for seventy days, and I would rather have a small true thing in my hand than a large one I could not check.”

She said, “And I have got something to do with it.”

He said, “Say what.”

She said, “I have got a fraction. A fraction is a thing I can count, and I have not counted one since I was a child, and I am going to count this one in the morning with a stick in the grit.”

“And if it comes out the way I think it is going to come out then it will be the largest thing anybody has ever handed me. And you handed it to me because I asked a small question in the open in front of four people.”

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,964 than she was at the fifth hour of it, which is four hundred and eighteen days of it, and not one of them going back, and she went and got four hours of sleep on the stones outside the gate with her right arm open and did not wake at the fourth hour for the fifth night running.

And at the fifth hour of the sixteenth she is going to take a stick out to the gate and put the shape on the ground in four counts, and one of the four is nine coppers she cannot count, and that one is going to cost her more than the other three put together and she does not know yet how to do it.
