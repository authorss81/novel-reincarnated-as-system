# The Thing She Does Not Un-Say

She went back up the four miles and up the four flights at the sixth hour of the second of the month beginning 2,054 and the short breath came at the third landing and she stood there four seconds and then went on.

The lamp in the cage on the fourth landing had been burning for ten days. It had gone on for eleven days with no keeper in the building, which is not correct and is not going to be correct, and there is no one in this county with the standing to put it out.

The boy of sixteen was at the rack with a sheet in front of him and he had water in a jug on the bench because it was the sixth hour and that is what the sixth hour is.

She put the bar against the end of the bench and it went down two inches under its own weight.

## What She Told Him She Was Going To Do

She said, “I am going to say one thing in this room in ten minutes and it is going to be about me and you are going to hear all of it, and there is nothing to be asked of you and you cannot leave and I am not asking you whether you want to hear it.”

He said, “All right.”

She said, “Say the rest of it.”

He said, “I have worked in this room for two years and a man read seven sentences in front of me a fortnight ago and put four sheets in a drawer and was dead inside five days, and I have not told anybody in this town that I was in the room, and I am not going to start now by saying that I have a choice about it.”

She said, “You do have a choice about it. You could go down four flights and drink the water and come up again in four minutes and I would have said the whole thing before you were back.”

He said, “Then I will stay.”

## What She Said About Him

And then she said the thing she had come up four flights to say, in daylight, at the end of a bench, in a lit room, to a boy of sixteen who had not been asked, and she said it of the person in her head and not to him.

She said, “For eight years I did not have a man.”

She said, “I had a column. That is the word for it and it is the correct one and I am not going to soften it. There was a figure in front of me most of the day and I checked it and I read it out and I took it in and I put it where it went, and twice in eight years he got a figure wrong and corrected himself in my head before I had finished being wrong.”

“I built ten years of everything I am on that and I have never once been in a room with him.”

“And I have thought for a long time that was respect.”

She said, “And I have never once asked him a question that was not about a number. Not one. In eight years. And I thought that was respect.”

She said, “And a clerk in this room wrote four sentences on it in a fair hand at the sixth hour, and they are true, and they are the only true sentences anybody has ever written about me, and I have not been able to put one word of them anywhere, and that is what I have come up four flights to say.”

She said, “I have been reading you. That is the sentence. And I am saying it out loud, and not to you, because it is a thing about me and you are the only part of it that is not.”

## What He Said Back

He said, “You have told me that at the end.”

She said, “I have told you at the end.”

He said, “I have spent eight years being the only voice in a girl’s head and I have wanted for most of that time to be a man in a room, and you have just told me in daylight which of the two I was, and you did it in a lit room with a boy in it, and there is not one thing I can do with any part of that.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “No. And I am not going to do anything with it, because you gave it to me and I am not going to hand it back or argue with it or put it in a better place.”

He said, “Say the rest of your sentence.”

She said, “I have said it.”

He said, “You have told me what you did for eight years. You have not told me what you are going to do about it.”

## The Staying

She said, at the sixth hour and forty minutes of the second of the month beginning 2,054, standing at the end of the bench in daylight with the light on the sheets and a boy of sixteen in the room:

“I am staying.”

She said, “I have read all of what I am. A piece of a thing with a name that a clerk would not write down, taken out of a fire at nineteen, kept going for nineteen years by a clerk and by a book on a plank table and by a leaf against my ribs. A levy made to a figure to find people like me. A name in two hands, nineteen years apart, that do not need me.”

She said, “And I am staying. I am staying knowing what it was made for. I am not going to un-know it and I am not going to be able to un-read it and there is no road behind me that gives either of those back.”

She said, “And I am staying with you, and that is the sentence and I am not going to put anything after it.”

The boy of sixteen did not move and did not look up and did not put his brush down, and she noticed all three of those things and one of them was the reason she kept going.

## One Question She Had Never Asked Him

She had been in that room nine days and she had asked a man of forty-five, a man of seventy-one, a woman of forty-five, a boy of fifteen, a woman of sixty, a woman behind a counter, a carter, a woman of twenty-two, a man with a notebook shut, a man with a board and a man with a shut book more questions than there are people in this county, and she had not asked the boy one.

She asked him one at the seventh hour of the second, standing, in daylight, one at a time.

She said, “What happens to the register on the first of the next month.”

He said, “It goes up a stair on a clerk’s arm.”

She said, “And who is the clerk’s arm.”

He said, “I do not know a clerk’s arm. I have been in this room two years and I have never once seen who takes a bundle up.”

She said, “Then that is the first thing in this county that needs sorting out and it has nothing to do with me.”

He said, “You could take one down.”

She said, “I could not, and you know that I could not, and I am the worst thing that has ever happened to a quarter’s bundle.”

He said, “Then I will ask the carter on the first.”

She said, “Ask the carter. And do not come to me afterwards.”

He said, “All right.”

She said, “And the lamp in the cage.”

He said, “What about it.”

She said, “It burns while the keeper is in the building and there is no keeper, and it is not mine to put out and it is not mine to leave burning, and that is all I have got on it, and I am going to say it in this room.”

“So that if anybody asks a boy of sixteen in two years why a lamp has been burning for ten days with no one in the building, there is one person in this county who said it in front of a witness.”

He said, “You will be gone.”

She said, “I will be gone in nine days, and I am not going to be able to un-stay what I said in the last ten minutes, and both of those are true, and neither of them means I am not saying it.”

## The Bill For Staying

She said, “Say what it costs.”

He said, “You have not asked me that.”

She said, “You have told me three times in four days what things cost and I am asking.”

He said, “It costs you every road you have. There is no wages and no board and no place you are on that is yours and no coppers that count, and you are nineteen, and the one place that was ever put in for you is nine paces by six on a kiln floor that you have not written down anywhere and have twice stood on and once refused to stop on.”

She said, “That is all of it and none of it is new.”

He said, “The new part is this. When you said the sentence in that yard on a furnace ground you said it once to thirteen people. That is done. You cannot take it back and neither can they, and it does not matter now whether anybody in this room hears you.”

He said, “This one is different. This one is in a lit room at the sixth hour with a person in it who has not been asked and a lamp burning that ought not to be burning. You have just said it in daylight, standing, one at a time, to a room.”

“And there are now two people in the world who know that you are staying, and you chose the second one, and a thing said to two people is a thing that has happened in a way that a thing said to one has not.”

He said, “You cannot un-stay it.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “Not by walking. Not by un-knowing. Not by getting a road and going west or east or up. You can leave anywhere you like and you will still be a person who read a page and said she was staying, and that is in you and not in a place, and there is no instrument in this world that takes it out.”

She said, “Then I am not going to try.”

He said, “No. That is the first thing you have said in four hundred days that has been free, and I have been waiting four hundred days for one of those.”

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the second of the month beginning 2,054 than she was at the sixth hour of it, which is four hundred and ninety-five days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back.

And the third of the month beginning 2,054 came up on a tower with the light on it, and a girl of nineteen went down four flights at the sixth hour with a bar on her shoulder and four sheets of a fair hand against her ribs, and she had said a sentence in a lit room with a witness in it that she cannot take back out of herself.

She was not going to be able to un-stay it, and there was not one road out of it that did not have her still staying on it.
