# The Instrument In His Hands

It came out of the cloth at the second hour of the eleventh of the month beginning 2,024 and it is about the length of a hand and it is grey, and it has no moving part on it that she could find, and she has looked at three of a kind before and none of them looked like anything and this one gave nothing away, which she found more frightening.

He had turned the lamp away from the bench before it was out of the cloth. He did that first. She wrote that down in her own head, because it is the first thing a man who has been working for twenty-two years does when something is going to happen on his bench.

Feng said, “Before it goes on, I am going to tell you three things, and I am not going to stop you asking me any of them and you will not ask me any of them.”

She said, “Go on.”

He said, “One. It shows me what you are looking at. It does not read your column and it does not read the gaps in your column and it is not a reading of anything you have written.”

He said, “Two. It costs the person who has it on. Not their arm and not their eyes. It costs them a year of what they have left, and I am sixty-seven and I have four years in me by the coal’s account, and I have had a very good run of this and I have nothing a year is going to be taken off.”

He said, “Three. I am going to look at him and not at you, and he is going to be in the room as much as you are, and that is the only reason this instrument is worth having and I have known it was worth having for a year.”

## What She Said About The Three

She said, “You have left out the one that matters.”

He said, “Say it.”

She said, “That it does not shut.”

He said, “It does not shut.”

She said, “Then you have not got a year, you have got the rest of your life, and there is not a version of the rest of your life that does not have one hour of this in it.”

He said, “Yes.”

She said, “Say it again.”

He said, “Yes. It is not going to shut. I have had it in a drawer for five years and I have not been able to work out how to want it, and I have worked that out in nine days since a girl of nineteen came up four flights with a bar.”

She said, “Then I am not going to say no.”

He said, “I did not think you would.”

She said, “Do not be pleased. I have not said yes yet, and I have one question and it is standing up in daylight at the end of your bench and it is the same question I asked the man on the rise and it is about this.”

She said, “Is this the same as being kept.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “Say all of it.”

He said, “It is the opposite of being kept. In five years I have had a piece of paper that reports in full and it has gone up that stair on a clerk’s arm and into a book that shuts, and I could not take one hour of my own life back out of it.”

“This is not that. This is me, in this room, on this bench, looking at a thing and then putting it in my own hand, and after that no one on any floor above this county has it and it is mine and it is the only thing I have ever done that is only mine.”

## The Moment It Went On

She said, “Tell me what you need.”

He said, “I need you to think of him.”

She said, “That is it.”

He said, “That is it. Not out loud and not as a name and not for me. I am asking you to do the thing you do ninety times a day and that you have never once done in front of a person, and I am asking you to do it for an hour in a lit room at the end of my bench, and I am telling you what I am asking for before you do it, because I have not read a person doing it for anybody.”

She stood at the end of the bench.

She put her left hand flat on the edge of the bench and she thought about him.

Not his name. Not the two syllables. Not the yard, and not the furnace, and not the tax, and not the thing he said at a gate two days before she walked off a ground for nine days.

Him.

And she went on doing it for an hour and three quarters, standing up, with a bar of fire against the end of the bench and a lamp turned away and a boy of sixteen copying two sheets in a quiet room four flights above a town, and she did not stop and she did not lose it once, and she did not put it down for one minute of it.

And at the end of it she found that she was shaking in the left hand and that it was not fear.

He said, “That is enough.”

She said, “How much of it did you get.”

He said, “All of it. Not the parts. The parts are yours. I got the shape of it and I got the part that is in the world and not in your head, and there is more of that than I expected and I want to write it down while I have got it.”

## The Stool And The Bench

He had the thing off his face in a minute and he put it in the cloth on the bench and then he put the cloth in the drawer and shut the drawer, and the drawer was not going to be opened again in his life and both of them knew it while he was doing it.

Then he sat down on the stool.

That was the part she was not ready for. He sat down on a stool four feet from her with his back against the edge of the bench and his hands on his knees, and before the cloth went back in the drawer he had been able to get up off a stool without a hand on anything, which she had watched him square a brush nine hundred times in fourteen months.

He said, “Do not do that face.”

She said, “I am not doing a face.”

He said, “You are standing at the end of my bench looking at a man of sixty-seven who cannot get off a stool, and I have had people look at me like that twice in five years and both of them were in a county room at the second hour.”

She said, “I am looking at you because you are the only person in the world who has ever agreed to look at anything for me.”

He said, “Then look at me differently. I have been in this building twenty-two years and I have four hundred volumes on those shelves and I have never once been a person who looked at anything for anybody, and you have come five days and asked me and I have said yes, and if I am going to be looked at I would rather be looked at as a man doing a piece of work than as a man doing a kindness.”

He got up off the stool at the fourth try, with a hand on the bench, and stood there.

He said, “Now go down four flights and come back up in an hour.”

She said, “Why.”

He said, “Because I have forty minutes in which I can write and I am not going to spend any of them on the stair with you standing in it.”

## Why He Wrote It Instead Of Saying It

She was on the third landing with her hand on the rail when she heard him start, and she came back up an hour later and he had four pages of a fair hand on the bench with the brush down square beside them.

She said, “Say it to me.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “Say it to me now, in this room, with an empty room.”

He said, “I will say it to you and it will not arrive, and then we will both have spent an hour and you will have asked me for something twice and I will have been wrong twice.”

She said, “Say that again.”

He said, “I will say it to you and it will not arrive.”

And she stood at the end of the bench and understood that the thing a person can say to her about him does not come, and that the thing a person can write about him does, and that in ten years she has believed the first kind four times and been failed by it four times and had not known there was a second kind.

She said, “Then write it.”

He said, “I am writing it. That is what forty minutes is for.”

And at the eighth hour of the eleventh of the month beginning 2,024 a man of sixty-seven with a cloth-wrapped thing in a drawer under a working rack that was never going to be opened again had four pages of a fair hand on a bench.

And the fourth of them said that a thing seen truly and written truly keeps after the person who saw it is gone, and that this is the first of them, and that he was going to leave the rest of it out of the report because the report goes up a stair and this does not.

## Four Days Of A Man Writing

She did not read any of it and it took her four days not to read any of it.

On the twelfth she sat on the floor with her back against the end of the bench from the sixth hour until the ninth and he wrote with his shoulder half turned and did not once put his hand flat on the sheets, which is what he does when a figure is wrong, and so she knew that about four of the pages and no more.

On the thirteenth he was at the sixth hour with the brush and at the eighth hour he was still at the bench and she went down four flights and up four flights twice and he did not know she had gone and did not know she had come back.

On the fourteenth he coughed.

That is all of it and it is not dramatic and she has written it down as it happened. He coughed nine times over four minutes and it went on past the point where a cough has anything left in it, and when it stopped he put the brush down square with the edge of the bench and was quiet for half a count and then he picked it up and went on writing at the same line he had been on.

She said, “Stop for the day.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “It has been four minutes.”

He said, “It has happened four times a day since I was fifty-one and I have copied a quarter’s bundle in a worse than this at the end of a season and if I stop now then I will have looked at it, and I am not going to have looked at it.”

And then he said, “That is not true and you know it is not true, and I have said it because a man of sixty-seven who has just been told to stop by a girl of nineteen needs a reason that is not in his chest. Ask me the true one again.”

She said, “The true one.”

He said, “I am frightened that if I stop for a day I will find out that I have nine days of writing in me instead of a year, and a fair hand does not take seven days to do what it has to do.”

On the fifteenth he finished at the third hour in the afternoon and put the brush down and squared the sheet and did not turn it round, and the first thing he said was that she was to have the whole of it alone, with an empty room, and the second thing he said was that she was not to have it that night.

She said, “Why not that night.”

He said, “Because a person reads a page like this at the fourth hour in a cold room by herself and it does not go in as the person it is for. You have got five days’ habit of a thing arriving in daylight. Come back at the sixth hour tomorrow and read it with the light on it.”

She said, “And the boy.”

He said, “The boy is sixteen and he is going to be at that rack at the sixth hour because he is always at that rack at the sixth hour, and I am not going to have one thing said to you by me and the same thing seen by him at the same time, and that is not a rule I have thought up, that is a rule about what a hand is for.”

And he put the pages in a drawer, and it was a different drawer, and he locked it, and he gave her the key and she put it in the front of her bag with two coppers she cannot count.
