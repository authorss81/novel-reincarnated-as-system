# Before You Started Counting

The light went off the stones at the turn at about the eighth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 1,814 and there is no shed at this turn and no lamp and no wall, and she had known both of those things at the seventh hour and had said the sentence in her own head forty times since and had not said it out loud.

She got up off the kerb and picked the bar up off the stones with two hands and carried it a hundred yards off the road to the broken ground on the north side, where there is nothing to lie down on and nothing to see a person by, and set it down again.

Then she checked.

She stood in the dark and looked at the turn, and at the bench, and at the two posts four hundred yards east with nothing on the board, and at the store road going west and the made road going east, and she listened for ten minutes, and there was a carter four miles off on the east road who was still four miles off when she stopped listening.

There was not a soul within four hundred yards.

She said that out loud to make sure of it, and a boy of eleven told her in a month beginning 1,694 that a thing said out loud where two people are is out, and she had asked him then whether it was out where one person is, and he had said: *it is out, and nobody has got it.*

## What She Had In Her Hand

Six sentences about a boy who weighed lime.

She went over them once with the dark coming, standing, not reading them because they are not written anywhere and she has not written them down and she is not going to.

A boy who counts what nobody is paying him to count is a boy the furnace will use.

And it is not the same as keeping, and not one man at a fire thinks it is the same, and the only people who ever found the difference out were the ones it was done to.

And it happened without anybody meaning it, and not one man there was cruel, and not one of them can be named.

That is what she had. It was given to her at a turn by a man of forty-five who wanted nothing for it, and there was not one question in it, and it was the only thing she had in nine days that was not a question.

## What She Was Wearing For It

She had a bar nine feet nine on her left shoulder and a skin and a bag with a dish and a lump of clay and a ring a finger’s width and a stub of a nib and a cake of ink she has not used in two months.

And against her ribs, on the side where the paper is, a book of eight leaves made out of a blanket: a date and a place on the first leaf, four sentences on the second she has read out loud once and will not read again, and six that are hers and blank.

That is everything she has and she took none of it off to ask. She stood in the dark with the whole of it on and a bar on the ground at her feet, and she has thought about that since and the part that has stayed is that she did not even consider taking it off.

There is a way of asking a question with your hands empty and it means you expect to be given something.

She was not expecting to be given something. She was nineteen and one copper lighter than she was at the first hour, with a bar and a skin and a book with six blank leaves in it, standing in broken ground a hundred yards north of a turn with no lamp on it.

## The Asking

She said it at the ninth hour, in the ordinary voice, standing in broken ground a hundred yards north of a turn on nine days of road, alone, with the bar on the ground at her feet.

“What were you before you started counting.”

It took two seconds and it went out of her mouth and into the air over eleven miles of made road and nine of strip road and a gate and a wall and a flat, and there was nobody in any of it, and it was out.

She stood there afterwards with her heart going and her mouth dry and four counts of breath going to seven and staying at seven, which is what it does inside a loaded reach, and she waited for the thing that happens when a question is answered.

## What He Did Not Do

He did not answer.

That is the first half of it and it is the half she was not ready for at all, because she had spent nine days building a shape where a shape was not required and she had expected a shape.

He did not go quiet. That was what she was watching for and it did not happen and she wrote it down later as the finding.

He went careful.

She could hear it. For two seconds the voice in her head went the way a hand goes careful on a wet floor, and then he started again, and everything he said after that was picked.

He said, “Say it again.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “That is the correct answer and I want it on the record that I asked.”

She said, “You asked me to say it twice.”

He said, “That is correct.”

## What He Said Instead

He said, “I am not going to answer that at the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of a month in the dark on a turn where there is no shed and no lamp and no wall, and I am going to tell you why, and then you are going to do what you do with it.”

She said, “Say the why.”

He said, “Because I have had eight years of being the only thing in your head that you do not ask. If I answer that in the dark on a turn, it is a thing that happened at night to two people who were not on the ground, and in a month you will be standing on a road in daylight telling somebody about it and it will have a shape.”

She said, “It already has a shape. It is a question in the dark.”

He said, “Then it has the shape of a question in the dark, and that is worse than what I am offering you, and you know it is worse, and that is why I am being careful.”

## The Terms

He said, “Two things.”

She said, “Say them.”

He said, “One at a time.”

She stood there.

He said, “In daylight.”

And then he stopped talking and did not say one word for four minutes, which she counted, and she has counted four minutes twice in her life on purpose.

## What The Terms Cost

One at a time. In daylight.

The old terms had been one at a time, at the wall, in daylight, and she had kept all three of them for a hundred and seventy-six days and never broken one.

The wall is gone. A man of nine hundred years shut a book on a plank table, took a bag, and walked out of it thirteen miles to a turn with a girl, and went back east twenty-two miles to a door on eleven feet of boards.

So there is no wall. There is no book. There is not one page left in the world she is permitted to put a question on.

Eleven days each way to nowhere. That is what a question costs now, out and back, to a place that is not a place, because there is no longer anywhere to walk it to. A question used to be two days of made road and a stranger’s table. Now it is done standing.

And she asked at the ninth hour in the dark, and the answer comes in daylight, and the daylight on this ground starts at the fifth hour and there are three days of it left before she runs out of standing.

## The Lever, Pulled And Not Pulled

She stood in the broken ground with all of that in her and she could have asked him again.

That is the lever. It has been in her hand since the month beginning 1,634 and she rubbed it out in the dust at the foot of a wall on the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,784 and has not written it since, and at the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth she had it in her hand with the whole of it open.

Ask twice and he has to come to it tonight.

She did not.

She worked out why standing up, in the dark, with her hands empty, and it took her under a minute and she has not gone over it since without it coming out the same.

A thing you can ask twice is a thing you can be refused twice. And she wants a thing she can ask once.

And if she asks it in the dark tonight she has spent the only askable thing she owns on the first half of a question, and he has told her in eight years of talking that he does not answer a thing twice, and she has believed every figure he has ever given her and she is not going to start on this one.

He said, “You had it.”

She said, “I had it.”

He said, “You are not going to tell me why.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “That is the second time today.”

## What Cannot Be Un-Asked

She said the terms out loud at the tenth hour so that there would be two people holding them, because a figure that only one person has got can be changed.

“One at a time,” she said. “In daylight.”

And she did not write them down. She thought about writing them down in the dust with a finger and she did not do it, and the reason is that a thing in the dust goes, and a thing in the air does not, and she wanted it in the air.

Then she picked up the bar with two hands and put it on her left shoulder and walked the hundred yards back to the road and stood on the stones of the turn in the dark.

She said it out loud, in the ordinary voice:

“You have been in my head for eight years and you have counted with me every day of it and you have never once been asked what you were. And I have asked you. And I am not able to take it back and I was never going to be able to, and I did it in the dark on a turn with no lamp because I could not do it in daylight and I have just been told that I cannot do it in the dark either.”

She stood there for a long time.

It cannot be un-asked. There is no second asking that will take it back, because a second asking is a second question and she will not be spending one of those, and because a boy of eleven told her in a month beginning 1,694 that a thing said out loud where two people are is out, and she has just worked out that a thing said out loud where two people are not is out too, and the difference is only in who has it.

He is the other one. He has it.

And that is the first time in ten years that there is a fact about him in two places, and one of them is him, and she did not know that was going to happen when she got off the kerb at the seventh hour and walked a hundred yards into the broken ground to be alone with it.
