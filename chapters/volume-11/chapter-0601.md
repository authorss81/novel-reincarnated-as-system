# The Bar Laid Down

She got up off the stones at the first hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,814 with the cold in her back and her right arm hanging out in front of her where it had gone in the night and had not come back.

The bar was on the ground at her hip. Nine feet nine of it, with the strap bound out of her own coat lining because she took the lining out on purpose in the month beginning 1,754, and the stone end of it had put a mark in her thigh that she would be carrying until dark.

There is not one thing at a turn at the first hour. The bench was grey and cold. The trough had ice standing in the rim of it. The woman of sixty was not on it yet.

He was talking.

She had got so used to that she did the listening before she did the standing, the way a person listens for a stove in another room, and it was there, and it had been there for every hour of six days and the six days before those and for eight years, and she had never once in eight years gone and looked for where it comes from.

## What She Owns At First Hour

She went through it on the stones in the cold with the sun not up yet, because she does it every morning and it takes as long as it takes.

A bar nine feet nine and a strap bound out of her own coat. A skin. A bag with a dish in it and a lump of clay and a ring a finger’s width and a stub of a nib and a cake of ink she has not used in two months.

Against her ribs, on the side where the paper is, a book of eight leaves made out of a blanket: a date and a place on the first, four sentences on the second she has read out loud once and will not read again, and six that are hers and blank.

And a sheet of paper against the same ribs, opened with her own two hands four years back and never once called anything.

Two things against her ribs and she has called neither of them anything.

That is ten things and one bar. It has been ten things and one bar for four months and before that it was ten things and no bar, and she does not remember when the bar arrived and neither does he.

## Two Things On The Ground

There were two things on the stones at the turn on the morning of the eighteenth and she had put both of them down at the dusk of the day before, and neither of them had moved.

The bar. And a bag with nine coppers in the front of it that she cannot count, and a quarter of a loaf she has been eating a piece at a time for six days.

At the dusk of the seventeenth she had stood on these same stones and watched a brother of nineteen go west up the road with nothing on him, and she had said one sentence out loud to the empty road, because she does not carry a thing in her head that she has not said once.

The sentence was that she does not ask him yet.

And then he had gone on talking, and the light had gone, and she had laid the bar down, and here is the eighteenth.

## The Board Four Hundred Yards East

There is a bar on two posts four hundred yards east of the turn with a board nailed to it, and loads get chalked on that board when they go out.

She walked down and looked at it at the second hour, because she had looked at it every day for six days and it is the sort of thing a person looks at.

Bare. The chalk on the rail beside it had gone soft at the edges, which is what four days of weather does to chalk lying still.

He said, “The last load that went on that board left here at the fourth hour of the second of a month beginning 1,754. It was stone. I know the weight of it because you asked me for the weight of it.”

She had not asked him for the weight of it since.

She said, out loud, to the board, “Then you know that board better than I do.”

He said, “I know everything that has ever been on it. I have never once been able to tell you so, because you have not asked me a question about it, and you are not going to, and that is correct.”

She stood there for a while with that.

## Three Directions And None Of Them

She stood at the post and worked the eighteenth out in her head the way she works every day out, and here is all of it.

East, nine miles, a post with a keeper of fifty-five on it and a chain in a burn in a cut. West, four miles, a cottage with a shut door at the mouth of a lime bed that stopped nine years back. Four hundred yards east, a board with nothing on it.

Nine miles east, four miles west, four hundred yards. That is what nine days of road offers a person who is standing still.

And the wages were the other half of it, because she is a person with no place and no board and no road, and a board goes down to the store on the second of a month, and the store gives out what the board says. The next one is fourteen days off.

She worked that out at the second hour and had not known it at the first, and knowing it was the first thing on the eighteenth that was any use to her.

## What Standing Is Worth

Then she did the one that took longer, and it took her forty minutes standing in the frost with her hand on the post.

She can stand at this turn for a fortnight and on the fifteenth she has to go and do something about bread, or she has to be somewhere a person might feed her, and she has worked out that both of those are the same walk in four different directions and she does not want to do any of them.

Fourteen days to a board. Eleven days to the end of standing.

That is the shape of it. Standing runs out three days before the work does, and there is not a thing on this ground that is going to chalk a single figure on that board in between.

She wrote the two numbers in the dust at the foot of the post with the end of one finger, side by side, and did not rub them out, which she does not do any more.

## Why She Put It Down

She laid the bar down at the third hour and it took two seconds and she did not decide to do it so much as find that she had done it.

Flat on the stones at the side of the road, parallel to the road, the stone end pointing east, the way a thing is laid down by somebody who means to pick it up again and not by somebody who has finished with it.

And then she stood with her hand still on it and worked out why.

Things happen to her. That is the shape of the whole thing. Things happened to her at a wall twenty-two miles east and things happened to her in a barrow ground past that wall and things happened to her at a counter with nine coppers on it, and she has never in her life had to stand still and let a thing happen to her, because standing still is the only thing she owns that nothing can be done to.

So she is going to find out where it is.

Not today. Today is the eighteenth, and the arm is out, and there are nine days of road in three directions and none of them is a direction the talking is in.

## The Bench On The Eighteenth

The woman of sixty came at the fourth hour and sat down at the far end of the bench with her hands in her lap and did not look over, which is what she does.

They sat thirty feet off each other until the light went, the way they had sat six days running, and neither of them said anything, and Ning Yan did not ask her a thing, and would not have asked her a thing if the woman had been the first person out of her mouth in four months.

And when she got up off the bench to go and see whether the bar was still where she had put it, the woman of sixty moved six inches along the seat, away from the end she had been sitting at, and did not look up.

She has not done that before, and it was six inches.

## The Water In The Trough

She went and broke the ice in the trough at the fifth hour and drank out of the rim with her left hand because the right one is not there in the mornings.

She is not hungry. She has not been hungry since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 and that is finished and has been finished for years, and a body that is going lighter every day of its life does not want anything put in it.

What it wants is to be worked. She worked out on the eighteenth, standing at the trough with her hand in the cold water, that she has not carried a full bar a mile since the month beginning 1,754, and that everything wrong with her at this moment is that she is stopped.

That is not sad. That is the first true thing about her body she has had in a month and she let herself have all of it before the light came up.

## A Team Going East

There was a woman with a team on the road at the sixth hour going east with four of stone on and no board.

She came through at a walk and did not stop and did not look at the girl standing at the post, and Ning Yan stood four feet off the near wheel and let her go past, and the two of them said nothing at all to each other in four seconds.

It is worth putting down that she was four feet off the wheel and that the woman did not take her arm off the reins and did not change her pace by any amount.

That is what nine days of road is. A person with a bar on her shoulder and a person with a team do not stop for each other.

## Two Hundred And Seventy-One

She was lighter at the eighth hour than she was at the first, and she said the number out loud standing over the bar because she counts and because there is one count she has never once been able to make stop.

Two hundred and seventy-one days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first.

He said, “Two hundred and seventy-one.”

She said, “Out loud.”

He said, “You do not always do that.”

She said, “You are the only thing I have ever met that gets the figures right when I have not asked.”

He said, “That is a low bar and I am going to leave it there.”

That is all that was said between them on the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,814. A number each way, and neither of them said thank you, and neither of them was going to.

## What She Took Off The Stones With

She got the bar up onto her left shoulder at about the ninth hour and stood there with it and did not go.

Tomorrow she is going to find out where the talking comes from.
