# The Two Dates

She did the arithmetic on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,814 at the fifth hour, standing at the trough with the bar across her knees, because she counts and because for the first time in ten years she had a date to count back from.

Nine years back from the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,814 is the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,805, and she wrote that down first because it is the half of the sum that comes out even.

She wrote it in the dust at the foot of the trough with the end of one finger and sat and looked at it.

## Four Things On Two Dates

She went through what she knows about this ground and she put three of them on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,805 and they all fitted.

The lime at the cottage went hard that year. The miller has said nine years and says it the way a man says a thing that happened to a wheel he was standing next to.

The wheel on the mill has not turned since. Four people live in the building. Two of them are under sixty and one of those two has a cough you can hear from the road.

And a boy of ten went east that year, behind the last barrow, because he would not get up on one. He was fed on the road. He was nine years in that ground before she found him in person and he said *you are shorter*, and he does not know what a list is.

And the fourth thing is hers, and hers is nine days earlier than the other three.

The small open place low behind her fourth rib, the size of a coin, opened on the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 and has not closed, and she has been lighter every single day since that eighteenth and not one of them going back.

## The Thing She Did Not Expect

She sat on the kerb with that for a while and worked out what it meant and it took longer than the sum.

Nine years is not a length on this ground. It is two dates, and there are nine days between them. Everything on this road that stopped, stopped on the twenty-seventh of a month beginning 1,805, and the place in her opened nine days before it.

She had put those two things in different places for nine years. She had a body and she had a road and she had never once set them down next to each other, and it turns out that they are the same nine years and nine days, and it turns out that she is the only person on nine days of road who can date any of it.

Nine years of a lime bed and a wheel and a boy and a hole in her chest, and nine days of nothing at all in between the last of them and the first, and one number.

## The Boy Behind The Last Barrow

She went back over that one in her head on the kerb, because it belongs on the same date and it is the only one of the four she has never once been able to hold still.

A carter came down this road with a board and put four names on it and took them east. The four went east with it. The carter came back without them and was not the next one back for a long time.

And the boy went with them, behind the last barrow, because he would not get up on one.

That is nine years back. There is nothing on this page that corrects it and nothing on the ground that agrees with it either: a carter does not put a boy of ten on a board, and a boy of ten does not walk four hundred yards behind the last barrow of lime on purpose and then turn up nine years later in a ground past a wall.

And yet he did it, and he has been fed, and he has hands like their father’s, and he did not know what a list was when she stood thirty feet off him and told him a number.

She has had nine years to turn that over and it has never once come out even.

And she wrote the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 in the dust underneath the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,805, so that the boy and the hole in her were on the page together, and the gap between the two lines was nine days, and she stood looking at nine days for a long time, and nine days is the width of a barrow track and it is not worth a line of a woman’s hand.

## The Bench

She sat down at the fourth hour on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,814, thirty feet off the woman of sixty, and she had two hours.

The woman of sixty has said four sentences in four months. She said four of them in the month beginning 1,754 and she has been on that bench every day since, and she did not look up.

Ning Yan did not ask her a thing.

She has wanted to ask the woman of sixty something since the month beginning 1,754. There is a list of it about nine items long and she has never spent one of them, and the reason is not respect and it is not manners, and it is that the woman of sixty has never once in four months put anything over a sill for her without being asked, and a person who has done that is not a person you take something from.

So she sat thirty feet off and did the sum again and got the same answer and looked at the ground.

## The Looking Back

And at about the fifth hour and forty minutes the woman of sixty turned her head.

Not far. About a foot, and she did it without hurrying, and she looked at the place low behind Ning Yan’s fourth rib through the front of the coat, and she knew exactly where to look, and she had known where to look before she turned her head.

She held it for four seconds.

Then she turned back to the road and put her hands in her lap and said nothing at all, for the rest of that day and the next.

## What She Did Not Say To Him

He did that at the ninth hour and then he waited for her to do nothing about it, and she did nothing about it, and that is the correct answer and both of them know it.

Here is what she wanted to say and did not say.

She wanted to say thank you to a voice in her head for not telling her at the fourth hour that a woman on a bench was going to turn her head at the fifth hour and forty minutes, because she would have moved, and if she had moved she would not have been thirty feet off in the right place.

She has not said thank you to him in nine years. She said it once, to a man of nine hundred years, for four sentences about a house, and it bought nothing and she has not said it since.

And what she wanted to say this time was not for something done. It was for something not done, deliberately, for a day, for her benefit, and she has never in her life had a word for that and she is not going to invent one at the ninth hour on a kerb by a trough.

So she said, “You let me sit thirty feet off for a day so that you could tell me about it afterwards.”

He said, “Yes.”

She said, “That is the most useful thing you have ever done and it is a thing I cannot thank you for, and we are going to leave it there.”

## What Ning Yan Did

She did not ask.

She sat there for another hour and a half and she did not ask her one question, and she has been over every hour of the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,814 and she did not ask her one question, and she is putting that down because she knows what it costs her to do it.

Because there is a woman on a bench who has just told her, without saying a word, that she knows what is in her.

And the whole of ten years is that nobody has ever known what is in her. A man in her head has counted with her for eight years and cannot get out of her. Every keeper, every clerk, every wall, every list, every door has wanted a figure off her and has never once looked at where the figure comes out.

Four months of a bench and a woman of sixty has been looking at the exact place on her where all of it comes out.

## What She Is Going To Do With A Morning

She got that out at the sixth hour on the kerb with the last of the light off the road, because she has to know and she is not going to find out tomorrow morning.

One morning. The sixth hour of the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,814. Daylight, the turn, one at a time, out loud, and a carter can stop and look, and the only witness she will have is a man saying good on a cart.

And she has been carrying a question since the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,634 and has never once known what she wanted out of it, and on the twenty-seventh she worked out that she has been carrying it as a shape and not as a want.

She wants one number. That is all she has, and it is too small to say to him, and it is not one at a time.

He said, “Say it.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “You have said no twice today and you have said it once this year and it has not gone well with you.”

She said, “I am not going to work it out at the sixth hour on a kerb. I have one morning and I am going to use the whole of the night on it.”

He said, “That is the correct answer and you have known it since the fifth hour.”

## The Part She Cannot Say

Here it is and she is going to have it said in the open at least once.

For nine years she has been looked for. She has been on a page and a wall and a board and a list nine hundred years deep and in a man’s own keeping and in the hearing of a man with a shut notebook who buys nothing, and she has been wanted by every one of those things and looked at by none of them.

And on the twenty-seventh of a month she was looked at by a woman of sixty on a bench for four seconds, over a road, out of daylight, for no reason at all.

It did not feel like being found. It felt like being the only person in a room.

She was lighter at the ninth hour than she was at the fifth, which is two hundred and eighty days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first.

And she has one morning of daylight left on this ground, at the sixth hour of the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,814, out of a turn with a bar on it, and she has never once in her life had less idea what she wanted.

He said, at the ninth hour, “She has been at that bench four months and she has never looked at anybody.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “And you have never asked her a question.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “Two people on nine days of road, four months, not one word, and today one of them turned her head.”

She said, “And you knew.”

He said, “I know at the fourth hour and I do not tell you, and I am telling you now because it is daylight and it is before the sixth hour tomorrow, and that is the last time I am going to be able to get away with it.”
