# The Man With Ash On His Arms

She could not get up off the dirt outside the gate on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,934 the second time, having lain on it every day since the twenty-second.

She got up on the fourth try on the morning of the twenty-eighth, and on the twenty-ninth she put up nine lengths of broken stone in a whole day and had to sit down on the stack twice, and the man of fifty-one wrote nine up on the board at the sixth hour and hung the board where anybody could see it and did not say one word about it in front of anybody, which she has been grateful to him for in a way she cannot put anywhere.

## What The Walk Took

Not the arm. The arm is its own thing and it is open four days in five and it was open that day and it was open the day after.

The breath.

Four counts at rest, everywhere, always, since before she can remember, and seven on a rise and inside a loaded reach, and she has spent ten years learning to live inside that and she has never once had to hold seven for two hours and forty minutes over hot stone with nothing in her hands and no bar and no way of stopping.

It did not come back to four until the second of the month beginning 1,964.

That is eleven days.

And on the twenty-ninth she was at the trough at the sixth hour and she put her left hand in and she could not get it up to her mouth, and she drank out of it by getting down on one knee in the grit in front of nine people, and she has not done that in front of anybody since she was nine years old, and she did not think about it while she was doing it and she thought about it for two days afterwards.

## What She Did About Being Seen

The knee in the grit in front of nine people is the part she has not put down.

She worked out on the thirtieth of the month beginning 1,934 that she was not going to do it again, and the working-out took four minutes and the answer was short.

She is nineteen. She has four fingers and a thumb on one hand. Her right arm is open four days in five and it was open that day. She cannot lift her left hand to her mouth when her breath is at seven and it does not come down to seven at a trough on a hot afternoon.

There is no third thing to work out.

And the second thing she worked out is harder and she got it on the first of the month beginning 1,964, at the sixth hour, sitting on the wall:

For ten years every room she has been in has been a room she was brought into, and in every one of them there has been at least one person who could see her at the worst moment and every single one of those people has used it — a figure, a rate, an entry, a date, a hand on her arm and a question about whether she could do it again.

And nine people on a furnace ground watched a girl of nineteen go down on one knee in the grit in front of a trough, and at the seventh hour that evening not one of them said one word about it, and the next morning the man of fifty-one wrote nine lengths on a board and hung it on a nail.

She said that out loud at the wall on the first of the month beginning 1,964 and then she said the part that was the hard bit.

“They have been right the whole time and I have been the one doing the seeing, and I am not going to do it any more, and I do not know what is left of me on this ground if I am not the one doing the seeing.”

He said, “A lot.”

She said, “Say it.”

He said, “You can put a bar up a wall. You can read a board in a good hand at thirty feet. You can hold a number that a man of fifty-one gives you. That is a lot.”

She said, “And you cannot count nine coppers.”

She said, “That is the sixth stage and it is yours and it is not going anywhere. So: a lot, and one hole, and it is a better trade than four years of a line on a board.”

## Four Days Of Eleven Lengths

She counted it out with the stick in the grit on the first of the month beginning 1,964 at the seventh hour, and it comes to the same thing two ways.

Thirty-one lengths on the sixteenth of the month beginning 1,934. Nine on the twenty-ninth. Eleven on the thirtieth, and she had to stop twice, and she had to stop twice on the twenty-eighth as well and she did nine.

Two hours and forty minutes of walking with nothing on her cost her four days of work.

She said that out loud on the stones outside the gate at the seventh hour and twenty minutes of the first of the month beginning 1,964, to a man who has never once told her the price of anything in his life:

“That is the rate. That is the exchange on this ground, and it is not a bad rate, and I would do it again, and I am saying it now while I have got it, because I know exactly what I am going to want to say about it in nine days.”

He said, “You are not going to regret it.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “Say why.”

She said, “Because a regret is a thing I keep and I have not got room. And because if I regret it then the twenty-first did not happen, and it did happen, and I was on that floor at the mouth with my hand on the wall and my heart going and nothing in my hands for the first time since I was twelve years old.”

## The Man In The Ten

She had counted him nine times.

That is the thing she said out loud at the gate on the second of the month beginning 1,964, at the sixth hour, sitting on the wall with her feet in the ash because she was still sitting on things eleven days after a walk.

“Ten men and two women and a boy of fifteen. I have counted that yard since the fourteenth of a month beginning 1,844 and I have counted him every time and I have not seen him once in a hundred days.”

“He is fifty. He has ash on the two of his forearms from the wrist up and it is not new ash, it is nineteen years of the same, and he works the mouth end, and he is the one who pushes the barrow out when the floor is swept.”

He said, “You have not seen him because he does the job that does not get called.”

She said, “What is the job that does not get called.”

He said, “Out. He takes the ash out. Everything that comes off that floor goes out with him at the second hour and the seventh, and it goes out through the east gate and down the track, and not one person on this ground calls it anything, and it is not on the board.”

She said, “How long has he done it.”

He said, “Nineteen years. He started it seven days after.”

She said, “Seven days after what.”

The man of fifty-one looked at the board and did not answer that and went in.

## What She Worked Out By Herself

She got it out herself over a day and a half, standing at the east gate with her arm open and the bar against her hip, and it is a short sum.

He is fifty. Nineteen years ago he was thirty-one. He was the man at that mouth end who had pushed the ash barrow out three times a day for twenty years before that and has pushed it out three times a day since, and on this ground people call him the boy who carries the ash, which is not a thing anybody said to his face, and it is the only name he has there.

And on the second hour of the morning after the harvest of nineteen years ago, he pushed a barrow out of that mouth.

Not the ordinary ash. The floor had not been swept since the previous seventh and the second hour of that morning was not a second hour a barrow went out at, and there was nothing on it to take out.

He pushed a barrow out at the second hour on the morning after, through the east gate and down the track, and he has never said one word about it to one person in nineteen years.

She went and found him at the mouth end at the seventh hour of the second of the month beginning 1,964.

## The One That Did Not Come Back

She said, “What was in the barrow.”

One question. In daylight. Standing. At the seventh hour, on a furnace ground, twenty feet off the mouth, with the heat coming across four feet of open air and hitting her in the chest.

He looked at her for a long time.

Then he turned round and walked up to the stack, and he took his bar, and he went to work, and he did not say one word.

She stood there for four minutes in the heat with her arm open and seven counts of breath and four fingers and a thumb on one hand and a bag on her hip with a book in it against her ribs.

Then she said, out loud, to a man walking away from her up a kiln floor with a bar in his two hands:

“Nineteen years is a long time to keep one thing shut when there is not one person on this ground who would ever do anything to you about it.”

And he did not turn round, and he did not put the bar down, and he worked the second length, and he worked the third, and at the seventh hour he went out of the east gate with an ordinary barrow of ordinary ash.

She could not follow him. She has worked out since that she could not have followed him if he had stopped, because at the seventh hour of the second of the month beginning 1,964 she had been able to walk nine paces of a kiln floor and get back off it, and that was what the twenty-first had cost her.

She said that out loud to the stones outside the gate at the ninth hour of the second, and she said it once:

“A man of fifty has carried one question of mine out of a gate nineteen years and three days and he did not answer it, and I cannot get up off this ground to find out where he took it, and the finding out would not have cost anybody but me a single hour.”

And then she lay down on the stones with her bar under her head and her bag under the bar and she was lighter at the tenth hour of the second of the month beginning 1,964 than she was at the sixth hour of it, which is four hundred and five days of it, and not one of them going back, and she lay there a long time and did not do the count twice.

And the thing she has not got is the only thing on that ground she cannot get on her own two feet, which is a man of fifty coming back out of an east gate and standing at ten feet and saying the rest of it, and he has nineteen years of practice at not doing that and she has four days of not being able to walk.
