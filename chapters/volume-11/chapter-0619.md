# He Does Not Know

She was a mile above the furnace ground at the fifth hour of the thirteenth of the month beginning 1,844, on the rising ground with the heat coming up at her out of the dip, when she saw a man walking up the slope toward her with a notebook shut under his arm.

She knew him at three hundred yards by the way he walks.

That is all she has ever had of him. Nine days of road and a shut notebook and a man who buys nothing, and she has met him four times in ten years and has nine sentences out of the whole of it.

She did not go into the road. He came up past her at a walk.

She went along at nine feet off his left shoulder for a quarter of a mile before she said anything, and he let the quarter of a mile happen, and she counted it because she counts.

## The Question

She said it at the seventh hour, in daylight, on open rising ground, in the ordinary voice, to a man nine feet off her left shoulder, walking.

“Whether a person who knows what a voice was is safer than a person who does not know it is there.”

He did not slow and he did not speed up and he did not turn his head, and he let her get to nine feet again before he said anything at all.

Then he said, “No.”

She said, “You have not had a minute.”

“I have had four days,” he said. “I have known the shape of that question for four days and I have not got an answer to it and I am not going to invent one at you at the seventh hour on a slope.”

She walked nine feet off his left shoulder for another two hundred yards.

“Thank you for saying so plainly.”

“Do not do that,” he said. “I have told you a thing I do not know and you are being pleasant over it and I would rather you were not.”

## The Other Thing

He said at the eighth hour, without any change in the pace:

“That is the fourth question anybody has ever asked me that I would rather not answer.”

She said, “What were the other three.”

“They are not about a road.”

That was the whole of it and he did not offer one of them, and she did not ask again, because one at a time is a rule she has kept for the voice in her head since the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 1,814 and it turns out to be the rule she has been keeping her whole life.

She said, “You have said you do not know. That is the fourth one.”

“Yes.”

“Then I have asked you a question and got the same answer I got from a voice in my own head, and I want to say out loud that you are the first person in four days who has not tried to be useful to me.”

He said, “I am not trying to be useful to you. I have not been trying to be useful to you since a month beginning 1,694.”

## Two Hours Of A Slope

They walked two hours and it is the longest she has ever been within nine feet of another person on this ground and it had nothing in it.

He put no question to her. He has never asked her one in ten years and he did not ask her one at the eighth hour on a slope nine days east of a gate.

## The Heat On The Rise

At the ninth hour they were down to the level of the yard wall and the heat came off the low ground in a way she had not felt since she was ten years old.

Her breath went to four counts and stayed at four, and then it went to seven and stayed at seven, because that is what it does on a rise with a fire in it and inside a loaded reach.

He said, “You are at seven.”

“I am at seven.”

“You have been at seven for two hours and you have not slowed down once.”

“I have slowed down twice,” she said, “and you did not see either of them because they were both behind a rise.”

He said, “That is a fair answer and I am not going to argue with it.”

She said, “How far do you walk this road.”

“I do not walk this road. I came up it four days ago and I am going down it and it will not have me on it again.”

“Why did you come up it.”

He said, “That is the fifth question and you did not ask it and I am not going to be thanked for the not-answering, and I would rather you asked it than say nothing.”

## What He Said Going Down The Slope

He got four hundred yards below her and stopped at a place where the ground breaks and there is a stone in the middle of it, and he stood on the low side of the stone with the notebook still under his arm.

He said, “You are going down to that furnace.”

“Yes.”

“You have a name.”

She stood still. The bar was on her shoulder and the ground was going down and there was a woman with a shut book thirty miles behind them and she had just heard herself say it to a man on a slope.

He said, “You did not say the name and I am not asking you to. I have been walking beside a person for two hours who has a name and has not said it, and that is not unusual and it is not mine.”

“Then how do you know.”

“Because a person who has come nine days east for one thing does not come nine days east for the air,” he said. “And because you asked me whether knowing is safer, and not one person asks that who has not got something.”

She said, “I am not going to ask you for anything.”

“No,” he said. “You have not asked me for anything in ten years, and it has been the most expensive thing about you.”

## The Part He Would Not Say

He stood on the low side of the stone with the notebook under his arm and the sun off the top of it and he did not say the thing.

She waited for it. She had been walking for two hours and she had a name she could not keep and a question he would not answer and she wanted one thing out of him before he went.

He said, “I am not going to tell you what is down there and you did not ask me and I want that on the record, because in two days you are going to want a person to have told you, and the only one who could have is walking away from you down this slope.”

He said, “There is one thing I will say and then I am going.”

“Say it.”

He said, “It has been nineteen years. That is a long time for a thing to be true without anybody checking it, and I have checked nothing, and neither have you, and when you get down there and somebody tells you a true thing that is the answer to your question, you are going to find out that a true thing is not the same as a thing that is yours. I know that because I have been the other one. That is all I say on it.”

## The Night Short Of The Yard

She came to the yard wall at the ninth hour of the thirteenth of the month beginning 1,844 and stopped on the outside of it with the bar against the stone, and she did not go in, and she stayed there three hours and did not go in.

The yard wall is knee high. It is a line of stone put up in a hurry a long time back to stop barrows going out of the yard in the dark, and a person could step over it with one boot.

She stood on the outside of it and looked at the barrows standing nose up inside it and at a low building with a door and a woman of forty-five standing in the door with her arms down at her sides, forty feet off, not moving.

The woman had been standing in that door for the length of time it took Ning Yan to get down the rise.

They looked at each other across forty feet and a knee-high wall for nine seconds, and neither said a word, and then Ning Yan sat down on the outside of the wall with the bar across her knees and the woman went back inside and shut the door, which was not a refusal, because a door shut at the end of a day is a door shut at the end of a day.

She got four hours of sleep on the ground on the west side of the yard wall with the heat coming over the stone at her, and it is four hours and not three, which is what she gets on stone.

And she worked all of it out before she slept, standing, and it took her the better part of an hour, and here it is.

She is going to ask that woman one question.

Not the name. She cannot use the name, because she has proved she cannot carry it across a yard and use it on arrival, and a person who opens a book because a stranger gave her a name in the road gets a different answer from a person who has something to ask for.

She is going to ask whether the book exists.

And if the answer is yes, she is going to ask to see it, and if the answer is no, she is going to ask why it was never opened.

One at a time. In daylight. And the third one is standing, because there is nowhere to walk it to and it is a door forty feet off and she is on the wrong side of it.

She said out loud, on the stones, late, with a yard wall between her and a shut door:

“I have come nine days east on a sentence a woman said to me in the road, and I have not got one thing to show for it, and tomorrow I am going to ask a person for something, and it is the first time in ten years I have asked a person for anything at all.”

He said, “I know.”

She said, “Say that again and see if I have it.”

He said, “I know.”

## Going Down

She watched him go west for a quarter of a mile until he was a figure and then a mark, and he did not turn round and he did not call out, and he had not asked her one question in two hours.

Then she went down.

That is what she got out of the ninth hour of the thirteenth of the month beginning 1,844, and she turned it over on the way down and it came out like this.

Four people in nine days. A man with burnt hands. A woman with a basket. A man with a shut notebook. And a voice in her head.

And not one of them has given her a thing she can keep, and one of them gave her a thing she wants very badly, and she asked him a question to find out whether she was safer or not safer and he has been walking for four days with an answer and he does not have one.

He said, “You asked me anyway.”

She said, “I asked you anyway.”

He said, “Why.”

She said, “Because I have never once asked a person a question I was frightened of the answer to, and I asked you one at the seventh hour on a slope, and I am going to keep asking until somebody answers it. It is not going to be you. You knew that when you let me walk nine feet off your shoulder for a quarter of a mile before you said a word.”

The bar was heavy on her shoulder and the ground was going down and there was a furnace a mile off with a smoke coming off it, and she had never in her life asked for a thing and been told plainly that it was not available, and it turned out to be the cheapest thing anybody had given her in nine days.
