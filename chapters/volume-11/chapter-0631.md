# Seventy-Five Days On A Wall

She did not go and lie down.

That is the first of it, and she said it out loud at the last hour of the light on the thirteenth of the month beginning 1,904, standing in the middle of a yard with a furnace two hundred yards behind her and not one person in it, because she worked out in the last month that a thing she only thinks is a thing she can lose.

He said, “Say the rest of it.”

She said, “I am going to be on this ground tomorrow when the eighth stage comes, and I have decided not to be asleep when it does, and that is not a decision about the stage. It is a decision about the ground. I want to be standing on it when I have got it.”

He said, “Why.”

She said, “Because I have got a name and a day and a place that says furnace ground, and not one hour of the morning, and after tomorrow I do not know what I will be able to do about that.”

## The Number On The Wall

She got the stick out of the ash at the north end by the windbreak and put it on the flat of the bar on the stone wall, and she did the sum standing, and it took her a quarter of an hour and she got it twice.

The seventh stage came on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,814. That was day 1,842 and it was seventy days after the sixth.

Tomorrow is the fourteenth of the month beginning 1,904. That is day 1,917 and it is seventy-five days after the seventh.

Seventy-five.

And the last time was seventy, and the one before that was seventy, and there was a run of three the same length before that and then a run of four the same length before that, and she has been counting intervals since the eighth of a month ten volumes back because it is the only arithmetic that has never once come out wrong.

He said, “You are looking for the next one.”

She said, “I am looking for the next one and I have already got it.”

She wrote it in the ash under the bar with the end of one finger and she did not rub it out.

Eighty.

She put a finger under the last figure in the ash and left it there, and then she said it out loud to the wall, with the light going off the stone.

“The spaces are getting longer and the reason is in the table in my own head and not in anything anybody has told me. Seventy, seventy-five, eighty.

“One of these days there is going to be one of ninety and I am not going to be here for it, and there is not one thing on this ground or on any road I know of that gets shorter.”

## What She Wants Before It Comes

She worked out what she wanted at the second hour of the fourteenth, sitting on the wall with her feet in the ash and the furnace going over behind her.

Not the morning. She has given up the morning. She tried three times to put one down in her head and there was nowhere to put it and she is not going to keep knocking.

A place.

That is all of what she has wanted since the sixth hour of the seventh of the month beginning 1,874, when she stood in the middle of an empty yard and said it to an empty yard, and the book assumes everybody who reads it already knows where they are standing.

And she has been sixty days on a ground where everybody knows where things happen.

## The Rule Of A Ground Like This

Here is the rule and she got it out of a stone wall and not out of a mouth.

On this ground a thing does not happen in a general place. It happens at the mouth, or at the west end of the kiln wall, or against the third course of the stack, and if a person does not know where a thing happened then they have not been here, and there is no way to write that down for somebody who has not been here because there is no word in it for where.

The boy of fifteen calls every figure out loud and he knows which course a figure came off. The man of fifty-one writes the figures on a board and knows which length they came up. The woman of forty-five has a page in a shut book with a date and two weights and a name in a good hand and not one word in nineteen years about where in it a boy stood.

She said that last part out loud to the wall.

“I have read the page. I read it in two minutes on the thirteenth and I have got every figure on it and not one place on it. And the woman who wrote it has looked at it four times since the thirtieth and did not see that until a girl of nineteen said it in her low house.”

He said, “It is not a fault in the page.”

She said, “It is a fault in who the page was written for.”

He said, “That is the same sentence.”

She said, “It is, and I have been standing on the ground it was written for for sixty days.”

## The Ground In The Dark

She walked the boundary at the second hour, in the dark, with her left hand on the top of the stone, because there is a knee-high line of it all the way round two acres and it is the only thing on this ground that has an edge you can learn.

A stone every two paces, going round. Ninety-four paces the long side and fifty-eight the short, and she got it twice going round in the opposite direction, and she put both figures side by side in her head and left them there.

And in sixty days on this ground she had never once asked where in it a thing happened.

Not because it was kept. Because it had never occurred to her that she did not know. She had walked past the mouth a hundred times. She had carried broken stone to the stack four lengths and up, and broken stone does not care where it came from, and she had eaten at a trough nine steps from where she sleeps and slept on stones outside a gate, and every single part of sixty days had been the part of being here that does not require knowing where here is.

He said, “You have been counting the fence for an hour.”

She said, “I have been counting the fence because it is the only thing on this ground that has a number in it that is not written on a board.”

He said, “That is a poor reason to walk a fence in the dark.”

She said, “It is the reason I have got.”

## What Is On The Third Leaf

She got the book out at the third hour, on the stones outside the gate, and held it open on her knees with the string round two fingers.

Eight leaves made out of a blanket. A date and a place on the first. Four sentences on the second, and she has had them out of her mouth once and is finished with them. A date above a place above four capital strokes on the third, in her own hand, put there on the twelfth of the month beginning 1,874 and not erasable by anything on this ground.

Five blank ones.

The yard had gone dark by then and the smoke was going straight up over her head. She said it out loud to the gate:

“If somebody tells me where it happened, I am going to write it on the fourth leaf.”

And then she sat with that for a while and found out that she did not want to.

Because a place in a book is a place in a book. A name in a book held for thirty-one days and a day in a book held for thirty-one days and both of those were true and both of those were a person at a fixed point doing one thing. If she wrote a place down she would have made a thing with edges out of the one thing in her life that has never had any.

He said, “You have worked that out in about a minute.”

She said, “I have had it since the seventh of the month beginning 1,874 and it took me a day to find the fourth leaf and a night to find out I did not want to put it there.”

## Who Has Not Asked Her A Question

She counted them at the fourth hour, standing at the gate where she sleeps, and the count came out the same as it came out yesterday and the day before.

Ten men and two women in the yard. A boy of fifteen. A man of fifty-one with a board. A woman of forty-five with a shut book. That is fourteen days of work and sixty days of a yard and a bar of fire and broken stone off a kiln floor at a fifth of a man’s rate, and in all of it not one of them has asked her one question about her.

She said it at the third hour and ten minutes, to the gate and the stones and the dark coming down off the rising ground.

“In ten years, in a dozen rooms, on a stone face, at a counter, in four counties, every single person who has ever given me anything has wanted a figure out of me first. And on this ground for sixty days not one person has wanted anything, and I have not known what to do with it, and it turns out the reason I have not known what to do with it is that I was waiting to be asked.”

He said, “That is a good thing to find out the day before.”

She said, “It is a bad thing to find out the day before, and I am going to use it anyway.”

## What She Decides At The Fifth Hour

She was at the west end of the yard at the fifth hour of the fourteenth with the bar off her shoulder and the stick in her right hand, which was open, and she stood where the sun comes in flat over the stone line and looked at the floor of the kiln.

There is one man on this ground who writes down where a person was.

That is not the man of fifty-one. The man of fifty-one writes down a weight and a course and a length, and a weight does not have a place in it. That is the boy. That is the woman of forty-five, and she will not open it again and she was right not to.

It is not anybody in this yard.

She got up off the stones at the fifth hour and twenty minutes. The light was coming across the ground and there were thirteen people not asleep behind her in the low houses along the north side, and she said it standing, in the ordinary voice, where the low houses could have it.

“I am going to ask one person on this ground a question tomorrow at the sixth hour, in daylight, standing, and it is the third one I have ever asked a person, and the first was for work and the second was for a book, and this one is not for anything. It is: where in this ground did it happen. And if they say no, then I will go and work out the answer out of the stone myself, because that is what I have got.”

He said, “Say why that one and not him.”

She said, “Because he cannot keep a place. I asked you that this morning and you said it had no force, and you were right, and I have been sitting with it for four hours and I have worked out that a person is the only instrument left in the world that can tell me where something was, and there are fourteen of them on this ground and not one of them has ever asked me what I am.”

She was lighter at the sixth hour of the fourteenth than she was at the fifth hour of it, which is three hundred and fifty-seven days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back.

And she went and put the bar down against the wall and got four hours of sleep on the stones outside the gate with her right arm open and the sky going from black to a colour she has never had a word for, and the furnace did not let out once all night, because it has not been let out in nineteen years and it is not going to be let out tonight for a girl with four counts of breath.

And at the fifth hour of the fourteenth she is going to be standing at the west end of that yard with the bar in her two hands, waiting for the eighth thing, and she has one question and one person to put it to, and she has never once had that before.
