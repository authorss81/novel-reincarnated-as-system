# What He Wrote While He Can

He wrote the last of it at the sixth hour of the sixteenth of the month beginning 2,024 with the light on it and the boy of sixteen at the rack and the lamp turned away from the bench, and he read every sentence out loud as he put it down, in the ordinary voice, at a working pace, the way he reads out a figure he has just written to a man at a window.

He did that because she had asked him to on the second day, and he did it because of what happened on the second day, which is the reason he read it to her instead of to an empty room.

## The Second Afternoon Of It

On the second afternoon she had gone out of the room at the eighth hour to walk the four miles to the square because her legs had gone in the stair, and she had come back in at the ninth and he had said the first sentence of it to the empty room.

Not to her. He had said it out loud to a copying floor at the ninth hour on the thirteenth of the month beginning 2,024, in the ordinary voice, to check whether a sentence holds when it is said.

She said, “You could have waited ten minutes.”

He said, “I have been saying things to an empty room for twenty-two years and it is the only rehearsal a fair hand gets, and I have never once been caught at it before and you are standing in the doorway.”

She said, “Say them all again to me in the morning.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “Why not.”

He said, “Because you will hear them and you will not be able to un-hear them, and you have told me that a thing you are told does not arrive, and I have believed you for five years without ever once having cause to, and this morning I am going to be the first person in ten years who proves it, and I would rather do it once in a good light than four times in an hour.”

## The First Five

He wrote them at the sixth hour on the sixteenth with the boy at the rack and the light on them, and she stood at the end of the bench the way she had stood at the end of it for six days, and she did not read down over his shoulder, and she counted them without reading them, which is the only way a person counts anything.

*He has shown me a man. He is nineteen years old and he has been nineteen for nineteen years, and he is the fourth piece of a thing that had a name, and I do not have the name and it is not mine to write.*

*He was burned out of it at a fire in the east nineteen years ago and what woke up was the piece of him that was not burned, and that is all a fragment is and it is not a soul and it is not a man and it is not nothing.*

*The levy on this ground is one in twenty. It is counted in days and not in money, it comes off a board before the board goes up, no person on that ground has ever been told their own total, and it was made to a figure by somebody who chose the figure and it has never changed.*

*The levy is not for the ground. I have been in this county forty-five years and it is not for the ground.*

*A girl has been counting with him for eight years and no one has ever told her one word of him, and everything she has got she worked out on her own with a stick, and she has written his name on a leaf of her own book in her own hand, and there is a second copy of that name in a good hand on a plank table at a furnace ground to the east that does not need her to be alive to be right.*

He came to the end of the fifth one and put the brush down and looked at it for a count.

He said, “There are two more and I am not going to do them in a hurry.”

## The Last One

She was at his elbow and she was not going to move, and he did not ask her to move, and he wrote the sixth and then he wrote the seventh, and the seventh is the one she has, because she was standing there and she could not have got round the bench and turned my back on a hand in seven.

*The sixth is that the thing I have been shown was made to find people like her, and I have had the tally of it in front of me since the ninth hour of the thirteenth and I did not know what a tally was until this morning.*

*The seventh is this. A thing seen truly and written truly is the only instrument in the world that can take a person through a wall. It is not the layer’s and it is not mine. Nobody writes one twice, and this is the first of them, and I am not going to write a second one in this room.*

He put the brush down square with the edge of the bench and squared the sheet with the back of his hand and read all seven of them back in a low voice without stopping, because that is what you do with a fair hand, and it was right, all seven of them, and there was not one false line on it and there was not one line on it he could not stand behind.

Feng said, “That is a page.”

She said, “Say what it is for.”

He said, “It is a report of a thing I was shown, in my hand, on a sheet that is going in a drawer in a building, and it goes up no stair, and it is not entered, and it is not on the quarter, and it is not in any of the four hundred volumes, and there is not one address in this county or any other that can be given it.”

He said, “And it is the only thing I have ever written that no floor above this county can hold.”

## The Boy At The Rack

The boy of sixteen copied two pages in a quiet room for an hour and three quarters and did not look up once, and afterwards, when she asked him whether he had heard anything, he said that he had heard a man writing, and that he had heard a man reading, and that in his two years in that room not one person had ever read anything out loud to anybody.

She said, “You were four feet away.”

The boy said, “I was copying. When I am copying I am not listening. That is not a thing I decided, that is a thing that happens.”

She said, “Did you hear what it was about.”

The boy said, “No. And if I had, I would not have been able to do anything about it, and he would have stopped, and you would have stopped, and then the quarter would have gone up late.”

And she thought about that for the rest of the day and could not get round it, because a boy of sixteen in a copying room four miles above a town had worked out in two years that the correct thing to do with an hour of two grown-ups saying true things in a low voice is to go on copying, and that it is not rudeness and it is not obedience, and that no one ever told him so.

## What She Said About It Standing At The Bench

She said, “You have put a thing about him in a book in a county.”

He said, “No. A sheet in a drawer. There is a difference and the difference is that a book has a spine and a drawer has a lock, and I have given this one a lock, and the key is in the front of your bag.”

She said, “Say the last of it again.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “Say the sentence about the wall again.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “Then how am I going to know that I have it right.”

He said, “You will know it that way in ten years, or you will not know it at all, and either of those is better than a person putting it into your ear at the fourth hour in a cold room.”

And she stood at the end of the bench with the lamp turned away and the light on the sheet and a boy of sixteen copying two pages four feet away who had not looked up once in an hour and a half and who was not going to look up once in his life.

And she understood all of what he had done, and it was this: he had taken the only instrument in the world that could carry a person through anything, and he had put it in a building instead of in her, because a building does not go anywhere and she does.

## The Days After The Sixteenth

He worked on the seventeenth and the eighteenth and the nineteenth, at the sixth hour, in fair hand, on the quarter, because the quarter closes and the quarter closes whether a man is well or not.

On the twentieth he could hold the brush for twenty minutes at a stretch and no longer, and he put it down three times in one hour, and the second time he put it down he had a cloth on the bench beside him that had not been there at the start of the hour.

She said, “You are going to stop.”

He said, “The quarter goes on the first and it is the twentieth and there is a bundle on it.”

She said, “I can carry it down four flights.”

He said, “You can carry a bar up four flights and you have carried one since you were a child and you are standing in my copying room telling me what you can carry. Take the sheet out of the light, that is all. The light is on it and the ink on that paper is twenty-two years old in my hand and it goes yellow in four hours.”

And she took the sheet out of the light and put it in the drawer and turned the key, and it was the first time in five years that a thing she had done had been done for him rather than by him, and she noticed it and said none of it, and she has still not.

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the twentieth of the month beginning 2,024 than she was at the fifth hour of it, which is four hundred and eighty-three days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back.

And there were four sheets of a fair hand in a locked drawer four feet from her and a key in the front of her bag, and he had not said one word to her about when she was to read them, and she was not going to ask him, because he would tell her, and she would listen, and she had four days of her own about telling.
