# What She Said To A Woman With A Book

She asked it at the seventh hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 1,844, standing in the doorway of the low house with the bar against her hip and the yard behind her going on the way a yard goes on.

“Is the book here.”

The woman said, “Yes.”

And then the woman stood in the doorway of her own house at the seventh hour of a working day and did not go back in and did not ask her to come in and did not say anything else, and Ning Yan stood there and counted, because one at a time is a rule and the rule means the second question waits.

## The Second Question, The Next Day

She asked it at the seventh hour of the twenty-fifth, in the same doorway, in the same voice.

“May I see it.”

The woman said, “No.”

She said it without any weight on it at all. That was the thing Ning Yan went over for two days afterward, because a refusal you can argue with is a wall and a refusal that is simply the truth is a floor, and there was no way at all to get under this one.

“May I say why I am asking.”

“You may say anything you like,” the woman said. “It has not got anything to do with it.”

## Why

She said it standing in the doorway with one hand on the frame and she took her time about it, and she was not cruel and she was not slow and she was not enjoying herself, and she gave the whole of it in a minute and a half.

“It is my husband’s. It came to me in the hands of a clerk and there is no other one of them on this ground and there is no other one anywhere he was, and it is the only object in the world that has my husband in it.”

She said, “I know that.”

“Nineteen years, and no one has ever asked to see it. Not the men on this ground. Not the ones from the sect. Not one, in nineteen years, and I have had people come up that track.”

She said, “I know that too.”

“And if you see it you will not take it away. You will put it back exactly as it is. And it will not be exactly as it is, because you will have seen it, and you will have been in this house, and you will know what is on the shelf.”

And then she said the one that ended it.

“You told me in the road that a name will not stay in you. You said it to me at the sixth hour on a made road. I have been turning it over for six days and I have decided I am not going to be the reason a thing gets into a person who cannot hold it.”

## What Ning Yan Said

She said it at the sixth hour of the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,844, standing in the doorway, and she said it in the ordinary voice, and she knew while it was going out that it was the worst thing she had said to a person in ten years.

“Nineteen years and you have never once opened it to find out whether it is true.”

The woman did not move.

“That is not a question,” the woman said, “and it is not an unkind thing, and I am going to take it as the second and not as the first.”

She said, “You are going to take it however you like.”

“I am going to take it as the second,” the woman said, “because you did not come here to be cruel to me, and because you said it out loud on my step where four people work, and because that is the third cruel thing I have been said in nineteen years and it is the first one that was any use.”

## What She Did With The Twenty-Eighth

She worked the whole of the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,844 on the stone at the north end of the yard with a hand on a barrow that was not hers and she was not asked to touch and she was not turned off.

The heel of the loaf had been gone since the first hour of the twenty-seventh. She has not been hungry since the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,544, and a body that goes lighter every day of its life does not want anything put in it. What it wants is to be worked.

So she went and worked, because there was a barrow on the ground and she is a person with a bar on her shoulder, and the boy of fifteen watched her for an hour and then brought her a second one and then left her alone with the wall, which is the only thanks anybody on that ground has given her in nine days.

## What A Ground Like This One Pays

She worked that out standing at the gate on the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 1,844, watching the man of fifty-one take the numbers the boy calls and put them on a board hung inside the low, and it took her twenty minutes and it is the only thing on that ground she can use.

The board is a board the way every board she has ever worked for is a board. It goes down to a store when the month turns and the store gives out what the board says, and the whole of the arrangement rests on one thing, which is that the figure is called out loud as it goes on so the men in the low can hear it.

She stood at the gate and listened to a boy of fifteen call weight after weight for an hour and she counted how many he called and got thirty-one and then got thirty again.

That is her, that is her whole trouble, and it has been her whole trouble since the eighth of a month ten years back, and it has never once been about being slow.

A number you keep is a number you keep.

So she worked out the rest of it standing at the gate in the heat.

The second of the month beginning 1,874 is the day the next board goes down, and that is eleven days off the twenty-sixth, and to be paid off it she has to be at a face on the first, and the first is day 1,873, and she will be on this ground.

That is the whole of her situation on the twenty-sixth of a month and it took her twenty minutes at a gate to arrive at it.

She said out loud, to a yard that did not answer her, at the sixth hour of the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 1,844:

“A woman has told me the truth and there is nothing behind the truth. There is a board on the wall in that low and a boy who calls the figures and a man who writes them, and if I put a figure on that board I will eat, and I have been walking nine days for a name that will not stay, and the name is worth less to me now than the bread would be.”

He said, “The name is worth exactly what it is worth. You have not lost it.”

She said, “I cannot get at it.”

He said, “Then go and get bread and come back to the name with a full hand. That is the order the two of them have to be done in and it is not the order you would have chosen.”

## What She Is Left Holding

She did that at the seventh hour of the twenty-eighth, sitting on the stone wall on the west side of the yard with her feet in the ash.

Two syllables. She can produce them word for word as many times as she wants and there is no place they go.

One copper she cannot count, and a heel of the loaf that went on the twenty-seventh, and a body that does not need it and a person that does.

Nine days east. A ground that does not want her and lets her stay. A woman in a low house who has said no to her correctly and in her own doorway and without any weight on it at all.

And one sentence, said out loud, on a step, where four men work, that she is not going to be able to put back where it was.

He said, “You wanted that book.”

“I wanted that book.”

“You do not have it.”

“I do not have it.”

He said, “Say the rest of it.”

She said, “And I have said a thing to a woman on her own step that I would take back at a cost, and she did not take it back, and she is not going to, and I have known better than that for ten years and I said it anyway because I had walked nine days.”

## The Night Of The Twenty-Ninth

She slept on the stones outside the gate on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,844 and got four hours of it, and she lay there and looked at the sky through a dead branch somebody planted forty years ago and did not think about anything, which is what she does when she cannot.

And at the third hour she got up and sat up in the ash and said one sentence out loud, to the dark and to a yard with a furnace in it and to a woman asleep thirty feet inside a wall that is knee high.

She said, “I do not know why I said it and I have known all day why I said it and I am not going to sit here at the third hour of the twenty-ninth of a month beginning 1,844 and pretend that I do not.”

He said, “You said it because you had walked nine days and you had asked for one thing and been told the truth.”

She said, “I do not want to be that.”

He said, “You are nineteen and you have one copper and you are nine days from a bread you have not bought, and there is not one other instrument available to you this evening, and you have used it on a woman.”

She sat in the ash and did not answer him, and the furnace went on making its smoke that goes straight up and does not lean, because there has not been a wind on that ground since the winter before she came.

She was lighter at the sixth hour of the twenty-ninth than she was at the sixth hour of the twenty-eighth, which is three hundred and twelve days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and it went the other way after that.

At the sixth hour of the thirtieth of a month beginning 1,844 the woman came out of the low house and crossed the yard and stood over her where she was sitting with her back against the stone wall, and she did not say good, and she did not say anything else either.

She said, “I have been up since the third hour.”

And then she said the thing Ning Yan had put into her with six words on a step the day before.

“I have looked at it.”
