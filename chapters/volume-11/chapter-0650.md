# Whether Staying Is Keeping

She had walked six days west off that furnace ground and she had put the barrow ground behind her on the second day of them, which she did not go into.

She stood on the crest of the rise above it at the sixth hour of the second and counted two hundred rows with a stone at the head of each and did not go down off the crest, and there was one figure moving along a row and not down one and it did not stop this time, because it knew who she was.

She said out loud to the rise, standing: “I will come back when I know what I am going to say in there.”

He said, “You have not said anything in here.”

She said, “I have not.”

## Six Days West

The six days were the worst walking she has done in this life and she did them at a walk because a loaded reach costs four days of work after it and she had nine coppers she could not count and nowhere to spend them.

Two miles of flat. The wall with the opening standing open in it. Two miles of wet ground and the made road after that, and then ground she knew, and then a rise at the fourth hour of the sixth day that put seven counts of breath into her mouth and kept them there for ninety paces.

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the sixth day than she was at the fifth hour of it, which is four hundred and fifty-nine days of it, and not one day of the six went back.

On the fourth day a carter going east with an empty cart asked her whether she wanted a mile and she said no, and he said, “There is no work west of here that anybody is going to want you for.”

She said, “I know. There is a building.”

He said, “There is a building.”

She said, “Five days on.”

He said, “Then you have time.”

## The Man With The Shut Notebook

She saw him at the seventh hour of the seventh day on open rising ground a mile short of the turn, coming up toward her at the pace of a man who is going somewhere and has been going there for a long time, with a notebook shut under his arm.

She knew him at three hundred yards by the way he walks.

That is all she has ever had of him. Nine days of road and a shut notebook and a man who buys nothing, and she has met him five times in ten years and has a dozen sentences out of all of it.

She did not go into the road. He came up past her at a walk and she turned off the track onto the grass and stood there and let him get nine feet off, and then she waited until he had gone ten paces past before she spoke.

She said, “Whether staying is the same as being kept.”

One question. In daylight. Standing.

He stopped.

## The First Two

He came back four paces and put his weight on one foot and did not put the notebook down.

He said, “No.”

She said, “Say all of it.”

He said, “It is not the same thing and I have watched the same thing turn into the other one three times and I can tell you about two of them and I am not going to tell you about the third.”

She said, “Tell me about the first.”

He said, “A woman in a town by a river stayed in it for years because her boy is in the ground in it, and she said every day of them that she was not able to leave him, and what she was doing was keeping him, and the keeping was in her own head and it would have gone with her if she had walked out of the town and left the box on a hill.”

She said, “That is a grief and not a rule.”

He said, “It is a grief and it is also the shape, and the shape is the same one every time, and the difference between the first and the second is that in the first one somebody had put her in the town.”

He said, “The second one was a man on a ground nine days of road from here who had a fifth of a day taken off his work out of every seven and had never once been told what it was for, and he stayed on that ground for nineteen years because the ground was the only place anybody knew his name at, and when the board came down off the wall in the winter he went four hundred miles and came back in the spring.”

She said, “He did not know.”

He said, “No one ever tells them. I am not telling you either, because you did not ask me and because I do not know.”

## What He Did Not Say About The Third

She said, “Tell me about the third.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “Why not.”

He said, “Because I do not want to and because you are not going to do anything with it. You are going to stay there, which I can see you have already said out loud to thirteen people in a yard, and the third one did not stay and I have not worked out why and it has taken me nine years.”

She said, “You have thought about it for nine years.”

He said, “I have turned it over for nine years and I have got no answer and I have never invented one, and you are the fifth person who has asked me about staying and four of them wanted me to tell them it was the same thing.”

She said, “You have not raised your paper.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “Why not.”

He said, “Because you asked me a question, and a man with a paper pending does not get to put a second thing into the same hour. And because it makes no difference to what you are doing.”

She said, “It still makes no difference.”

He said, “It does not make the slightest difference to whether staying is being kept, and that was your question and it has been answered. I have not lied to you at any hour of this walk and I am not going to start by telling you the paper does not matter. It matters. It is just not that.”

## What She Did With It In The Next Two Days

She walked the next two days with it and she did not like it, and by the second evening she had worked out why.

On the first day she kept hearing it as an answer and she kept putting it next to the sentence she said in the yard, and the two of them did not go together. Her sentence was a weight she had picked up. His sentence was a question about which way a sentence runs.

She said out loud on the second evening, sitting on a wall four miles short of the turn with the bar across her knees and her arm hanging:

“If I am not being kept then there is nobody keeping me, and that is not the same as no one being able to put me somewhere. That is worse. That means anybody can.”

He said, “That is right.”

She said, “Do not say that like it is a thing you have been waiting to say.”

He said, “I have not been waiting for anything. I have been walking west and you have been catching up.”

## What She Answered Him

She said, “Why is it not the same thing.”

He said, “Because a kept thing has somebody who put it somewhere. That is the difference and it is not a moral point, it is a fact about which way a sentence runs.”

He said, “You have never once been anywhere on purpose that you were put in. You were put in a crew at ten and you were put in a line at twelve and you were put in a bound at a wall, and every single one of those places took you in and let you out on your own two feet, and the difference between those and this is that you have never been owed.”

She stood in the grass with the bar in her hand and did not say anything for half a count.

She said, “That is the second time a person has told me the true shape of a thing and both times it was to my face and neither time it was any use to me at the time.”

He said, “Then it is a shape and not a gift, and I have not given you anything, and I am not going to ask you a question about it.”

She said, “I know. You have never asked me one.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “Can I say one thing.”

He said, “You can say one thing.”

She said, “I am not staying because it is safe. There is nothing there. I am staying because the last sentence I have got in the world is a true one and it was said in a yard in front of people and I cannot take it back, and I have found out in the last three days that a thing you cannot take back is the only kind of weight there is.”

He said, “That is not what I asked you about.”

She said, “No. It is what I answered.”

He said, “All right.”

And he took the notebook out from under his arm and did not open it and put it back under his arm, and she saw him do it and she has thought about that for six days since.

## The Turn At The End Of It

She said, “My request is still open and I am not going to raise it.”

He said, “That is mine and not yours, and I have not raised it, and I am not going to talk about it on a rise.”

And he went on west at the pace of a man going somewhere and she stood in the grass and watched him go for two hundred yards and then she took the bar onto her left shoulder and walked after him because it was the only direction there was.

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the seventh day of the month beginning 1,994 than she was at the fifth hour of it, which is four hundred and sixty days of it, and not one of them going back, and she was five days out from a building with a register in it.

And a man nine hundred years old had just told her that the sentence runs the other way, and she had two days of walking left and no idea at all what she was going to ask a clerk when she got there.
