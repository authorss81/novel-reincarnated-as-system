# What Her Hand Went Down On

She was outside the gate at the fifth hour and forty minutes of the twelfth of the month beginning 1,874 with the bag open and everything out of it laid on the stones in a row, which is what she does before anything she has to do with two hands.

The blanket book. The board with the stone under the back of it. The nib. The cake of ink, opened. The bar, laid flat, because she cannot hold a board and a bar at the same time and the bar weighs what she weighs.

And the copper, which she put at the end of the row without any reason except that it was in the bag and it was the only thing in the bag that was not for the writing.

## The Door At The Sixth

The woman opened the door at the sixth and did not say good and came out onto the step with the book in her hands, cloth boards, string round it, nineteen years of dust gone off the top of it with the sleeve of a coat.

She put it on the table inside and she left it open and she went out to the yard, and she shut the door behind her, and that was the whole of the terms and she had chosen them and not been asked.

Ning Yan stood at the table for four minutes and did not touch it.

That is the part she has never got past. Four minutes, a page, and a pair of hands she has had since she was six, and she stood there and did not put a finger on it.

Then she read it.

## The Page

She read it with her own eyes in a minute and twenty seconds, and she read it the way she has read fine hands since she was twelve years old, which is quickly and then again, because the first time is for the shape and the second is for the figures.

It is one entry. It is not a page of names.

It is a date. It is a place, and the place is this ground. It is a line that says a work was begun and stalled, and a line that says it was filled, and a line that gives a boy, his age, his father’s trade, and the weight of what was taken and the weight of what came back.

The two weights are in it and they are not the same, and the clerk who wrote them wrote the difference out as a figure and did not put a word next to it.

There is not one word in the entry that says why.

Ning Yan stood at a table with her hands flat on either side of a page and she read it, and the figures went in and stayed, because figures always go in, and the date went in, and the place was a place she had stood on for twenty-eight days.

And at the end of the line there was a name.

## The Name On The Page

She read it and it took her a second and a half to be certain, and she was certain.

Two syllables. The first of them short and the second one falling.

It is the same as the one a woman of forty-five said out loud on a made road at the sixth hour of the fifth of a month beginning 1,844, with a basket of lime on her arm and nineteen years of not saying it behind it.

Her hand, when she wrote it in the road on the seventh, had not gone down on a true thing. It had gone down on a sound.

This one is ink that dried nineteen years ago on a shelf in a low house on a furnace ground, and it is the handwriting of a man with a good hand who was told what to write.

She said, out loud, alone, in a low house with the door shut:

“It is in ink.”

## Coming Back With It

She carried it out of that low house at the seventh hour and it went east in her head and stayed.

She said it to herself once at the gate, in the ordinary voice.

“Yuan Sheng.”

She waited for it to go through her and out the way it had gone through her and out four times on a made road with a woman four feet off her.

It did not go out.

It sat down.

That is the whole of the difference and it is thirty-seven days and one page, and she has never in her life been able to write the difference out any plainer than that, and she tried twice in the next three days.

A thing said to you is a thing that happens to you.

A thing read with your own eyes is a thing you found out on your own.

And a thing found out is a thing your hand can put down.

## The Line

She wrote it at the eighth hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 1,874, sitting on the stones outside the gate of the furnace ground, on the board with the stone under the back of it, with the bar laid flat beside her because it weighs what she weighs.

She wrote on the third leaf of a book of eight made out of a blanket, and the first two have been full for four years and are not going to be read again.

She got the nib down on the first line at the eighth hour and she did it slowly, and her hand writes what she means at the moment it goes down, so the whole of the work was in arriving at each line meaning exactly that line.

The date. The one on the page, in the same order, in the numerals the clerk used.

A line under it, and then the place, and she put down *furnace ground* and nothing else, because there is no name on this ground and there has never been one and she was not going to invent one in a book of her own.

And then the name, in capitals, in her own hand, four fingers and a thumb.

YUAN SHENG

It took her four seconds to write. She has been at it since to say what it felt like and the nearest she has got is that her hand was not alone on it.

## What Cannot Be Put Back

She looked at it for a while.

Blanket does not take ink back. There is no solvent in the world she can reach and there is no woman on that ground who would take a blade to a page for her and no fire she would put it into, and she knew both of those at the eighth hour while it was still wet.

She said, out loud, to the yard, to the furnace, to the ash and the barrows nose up against the inside of a knee-high wall:

“That is the whole of it. I have said for ten years that a book is the one thing a person cannot take off me and I have been right and it has taken me four days to understand that the reason is that you cannot put it back either.”

She said, “You are not going to say thank you for it.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “You cannot thank me for it either.”

He said, “No,” he said, “and I have not got an instrument for it, and there has never been one in eight years, and I have said nothing about it.”

## Is That Your Name

She said it out loud at the ninth hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 1,874, standing up, in daylight, on open ground, with a book of eight leaves on the stones at her feet and the ink not dry.

She said, “Is that your name.”

He said, “Yes.”

And then she said the rest of it out loud, because a name in a book of her own is a thing that has been done to a person and she had done it and she was going to say so in daylight where it could be heard.

“I have put your name in a book of my own against my ribs, in my own hand, with a date on it and the place on it, and there is no hand in the world that can take it out of it and no way to put it back un-written, and I did it at the sixth hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 1,874, in daylight, out loud, on open ground.”

He said, “Yes.”

She said, “That is not a question and it is not an answer to a question and I am saying it so that there are two of us holding it.”

He said, “There are two of us holding it.”

She waited for him to say what it was, and he said, “Say the rest of it.”

She said, “I am not going to say the rest of it out loud and I am not going to say it to myself either, and you have heard me not say it, and that is the only thing that is going to happen about it in this year.”

## The Woman Came Back In

She came in from the yard at the ninth hour and twenty minutes and put her hand flat on the table and looked at the page she had left open, and she did not ask to see what was in the book against Ning Yan’s ribs, and she did not ask a question about the ink, and she said one thing.

She said, “Did it say the same one.”

Ning Yan said, “It said the same one.”

The woman said, “Then I have been carrying it for nineteen years and you have been carrying it for thirty-seven days and it is a good deal of trouble for one line of a man’s hand.”

She said, “It is the only true sentence anybody has said on this ground in nineteen years.”

The woman said, “That is not a compliment. That is what it is. There is not a great deal to say about most mornings.”

She said, “There are five leaves left.”

The woman said, “There were six. I watched you count them on the yard wall with a stone and a board and I did not come out.”

## What She Is Left With

She was lighter at the ninth hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 1,874 than she was at the fifth hour and forty minutes of it, which is three hundred and twenty-five days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and the first one went out of the ground under her feet and did not come out of her hands, and she is not going to say any more about that than that.

And here is what she had at the ninth hour, all of it, standing up, on open ground.

One name in her own hand in a book no hand can reach, with a date on it and a place on it.

Five leaves that are hers and blank.

One copper she cannot count.

One bread, which was bought at the store behind a mill five miles west of a turn, twenty-nine days ago, and which she is not going to get back.

A ground of two acres with a furnace in it that never wanted her and has let her stay.

And a woman of forty-five who has said a name out loud twice in nineteen years and shut a book once and opened it once, and who is not going to open it again.

He said, “You have written it and it is done.”

She said, “It is written.”

He said, “And what are you going to do now that you cannot un-know it.”

She looked at the yard, and at the barrows, and at the smoke going straight up off a furnace that has not been let out in nineteen years, and at a ground kept for nineteen years by people doing an ordinary day.

She said, “I am going to find out what a fire is. I said on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,814 that I would, and I have been on this ground twenty-eight days and I have not done it.”

He said, “That is not what I asked you.”

She said, “I know what you asked me, and I am not going to answer it today, and you will have to ask me again in a month.”
