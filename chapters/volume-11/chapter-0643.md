# Rubbed Out Again

She wrote it out on the twenty-second of the month beginning 1,964 at the seventh hour, in the ash, at the gate, standing, with a stick, because she has done it twice before and both times the writing down is what makes the not-doing of it a decision.

One question, asked once.

And then the same question, asked again.

That is the lever. There is no third part to it and there never has been, and it has been in her bag since the eighth of a month ten volumes back and she has carried it through a wall and a gate and thirteen miles of made road and sixty-five days on foot and four months of a woman asking for her husband’s book.

## What The Lever Is

At the seventh hour and ten minutes she was crouched over the ash with the stick in her hand, and she said it out loud, in the ordinary voice.

“Ask him once and he answers the smallest true thing and it takes as long as it takes. Ask him twice and he gets to the next one sooner. That is what it does and it is not a trick and he has never once pretended it is anything else.”

He said, “That is right.”

She said, “Say the other half.”

He said, “The first one is gone. That is the other half and it has always been the other half and I have told you so twice in the open and I am telling you so a third time because you have just spent a day and a night working out that a thing was designed, and a person who has just worked that out is at the exact place in herself where it is most expensive.”

She said, “You have been waiting for me to get here.”

He said, “I have been waiting since the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,814.”

## What The Eighth Stage Took Off It

She stood there and worked it out, and it took her about four minutes, and it is the third thing the eighth stage took and she had not seen it until that minute.

On the fourteenth of the month beginning 1,904 the asking stopped holding.

Before that, a doubled question was expensive. She would lose the first answer and she would have to go and get it again later, if she could, and a person can sometimes go and get an answer again.

Now she cannot.

She put it out loud, standing, in the ash.

“If I ask you a question twice I will get two answers and I will not be able to order them and I will not be able to say which one I asked first, and I have proved that on five days this month in front of a man with a board. So the lever is not a thing that costs me the first answer any more.”

He said, “It costs you both.”

She said, “It costs me both.”

She said, “That is the eighth, working on the one thing I have been carrying since I was a child. It has not stopped me carrying it. It has taken the ground out from under the one thing I was carrying it for.”

## The Double Question, In The Ash

She wrote it anyway, which she has done twice before and has never regretted doing.

Two lines, side by side, in the grit at the gate, the way you lay out two rows of barrows.

*What was the tax for.*

*What was the tax for.*

The same four words, twice, about ten inches apart, and the distance between them is the entire instrument.

She looked at it for a while.

Then she said it out loud to the two lines in the ash at the eighth hour and ten minutes of the twenty-second of the month beginning 1,964.

“You are not even a question any more. You are a way of asking twice so that one of them counts. And the only reason I have ever wanted you is that I wanted to be answered sooner, and I have been answered twenty times in seventy days and every single one of them I am still carrying.”

She said, “That is all of it. I do not want to be answered sooner. I want the one I asked.”

Then she put the flat of the bar on the ash and rubbed it out, both lines, and the grit under it came up black and she left the black there.

## The Two Other Times

She has done it twice before and she went back over both of them on the twenty-third, in the light, sitting on the stones outside the gate with the stick across her knees, and it took her about half an hour and it is worth putting down because the third is the one that counts.

The first time was at dusk on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,814, in broken ground a hundred yards north of a turn, with no lamp and no person, and she had put one question into the air and had wanted a second one and had not said it.

The second time was on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,874, at a wall on the west side of the yard, at the seventh hour, standing, in daylight, with a furnace working two hundred yards behind her, and she had put one question into the air and had wanted a second one and had not said it.

Both times she wrote it down afterwards so that the not-saying of it would be a thing she had done and not a thing she had failed to do.

And both times she was in the same place in herself, which is standing in daylight with a question half out of her mouth, and she has now been in it three times, and the third time is the first one where the cost of not pulling it is not hers.

She said it out loud on the stones at the sixth hour of the twenty-third, to the stones, because there was not one person near her:

“The first two times I did not pull it for you. This time I am not pulling it for me, and that is the difference and it took me ninety days to be able to tell it.”

## The Leaves

She got her book out on the twenty-fourth, at the seventh hour, on the stones outside the gate, and she opened it to the fourth leaf and held it open on her knees.

Five blank ones. The fourth, the fifth, the sixth, the seventh and the eighth.

A date and a place on the first leaf. Four sentences on the second leaf, which she said once into the air when she was a girl and has never repeated. A date above a place above four capital strokes on the third.

Five.

She has had five since the twelfth of the month beginning 1,874 and she has had a use for four of them in the eighty days since and she has not used one.

At the seventh hour and twenty minutes of the twenty-fourth she was still on the stones outside the gate, and she said it out loud, in the ordinary voice.

“I am not going to write it on a leaf. Not the doubled question and not the single one. If I write it down then there is a line in the blanket book in my own hand with two copies of the same four words in it about ten inches apart, and in about a year I will read it and I will not remember putting it there, and then it will be a thing in the world that I cannot tell apart from a thing I did on purpose.”

He said, “You will still know.”

She said, “Will I.”

He said, “You will know because you have just said the reason, and the reason is in here and not on a leaf.”

She said, “That is a real answer and I did not expect it and I am going to sit with it.”

## The Boy Of Fifteen And The Man Of Fifty

She got the last of it out of the boy of fifteen on the twenty-fifth, at the stack, at the seventh hour, and she asked him one question and got a straight answer in the ordinary voice, because a person leaves a mark.

She said, “What is the man with the ash on his arms called.”

The boy said, “The boy who carries the ash. That is what we call him and he does not like it and he is fifty.”

She said, “Why does he not like it.”

The boy said, “Because he was thirty-one. My father says he was thirty-one and he has been called the boy since before I got here and I asked him about it once and he said he would tell me when he was sixty and he is not going to be telling me anything for ten years.”

She said, “Has anybody on this ground asked him what he carries out.”

The boy said, “No. You are the first one and you are not going to get an answer either because he did not give you one and he does not give anybody one.”

## What She Decides

She tied the string on and put it back against her ribs on the side where the paper is and she lay down on the stones outside the gate at the tenth hour of the twenty-fourth, and she did not sleep, and she got up at the fourth hour and she walked out of the gate and east on the flat for two hours and came back at the sixth hour of the twenty-fifth and did the day’s work.

And at the seventh hour of the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,964 she said all of it at the gate, in daylight, standing, and there were four people on that ground within ninety feet and she had not asked any of them to be there.

She said, “I know what that tax is for.”

He said, “Say it.”

She said, “Not here. Not on this ground, in the open, with a furnace going and four people inside ninety feet, and not yet.”

He said, “Why not yet.”

She said, “Because I have got a shape and a fraction and four counts and a figure off a board and a man of fifty-one who writes what he is given. That is a thing I have found out on my own with a stick in the grit. And what I have got in my head this morning is not a finding out. It is a woman of nineteen who has been lighter every day since she was a child putting a sentence together and being pleased with it.”

She said, “I am going to say it to you once, in daylight, standing, where it cannot be taken back off me. And I am going to hear what you do when I say it. And then I am going to say it again in the open in front of people, because a thing said once to one man in a head is not a thing that has happened.”

He said, “And if I do nothing.”

She said, “Then I will know it is true, and I will say it in the yard anyway, and that will be on me and not on you.”

She said, “You have had no part in this since the fifteenth and you are not going to have any part in it on the day I say it. That is the arrangement and I am telling you what it is in advance so that you cannot be the reason for any of it.”

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,964 than she was at the fifth hour of it, which is four hundred and thirty days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back.

And on the second of the month beginning 1,994 he is going to put three sentences in front of her at a gate in daylight, and she is going to know what he is before she says what the tax is for, and the reason she has arranged it that way is that she has spent eight years being the only person in the world who knew what he was and she is not going to hand the last of it to a house full of people.
