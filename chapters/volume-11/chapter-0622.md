# The Woman Who Opened A Book

She read the first hour of the thirtieth of the month beginning 1,844 inside the low house for the first time in nineteen years, which she had not done, because a person who is let in is a person who is owed something and she had said no to a girl on her step four days before.

There was a table, a stove with its door open, two stools, a bed against the far wall, and a shelf.

The book was on the shelf at the end, standing up between a folded blanket and a bowl, and it was cloth boards with a string round it, and it was nineteen years of dust on the top edge of it.

The woman took it down and put it on the table and did not untie the string and did not open it, and she stood with her hand flat on the cover for four seconds.

Then she said, “You asked me whether it was true. I have checked whether it is true.”

## What She Says

She said it standing at the table with her hand on the book, and she said it in pieces, and she did not read anything out of it and she did not turn a page, and Ning Yan let her take the length of time she needed because one at a time is a rule and this was the first sentence of it.

“A harvest in that sect is written down when it is taken.”

She said, “Taken how.”

She said, “That is the second one and you will let me get through it.”

Ning Yan said, “I will let you get through it.”

“They wrote it down in the same seven days. They wrote it down true.”

“My husband did not write the order and he did not carry it out, and I have never once said otherwise and I am not going to start with you.”

“The boy was nineteen and he was the son of the man who keeps the fire.”

“Not one person on that ground has ever said it was going to save anybody.”

And then, after a while:

“It did not save my husband and it did not save the boy, and the writing never said it would.”

## The Six Of Them

She has gone over the six of them since. They were across a minute and a half with two pauses in it and a woman of forty-five said every one of them standing up, and she did not say one of them twice, and there is not a speech in it.

She said, “They wrote it down.”

She said, “They wrote it down inside seven days and they wrote it down true.”

She said, “My husband did not write the order and he did not carry it out.”

She said, “The boy was nineteen and he was the son of the man who keeps the fire.”

She said, “It did not save my husband and it did not save the boy.”

She said, “Not one person on that ground has ever said it was going to.”

And that is the second mouth and it is six sentences long and it is the only time in nineteen years that anyone on that ground has said any of them out loud.

## What A Harvest Book Is

She said that after it, sitting down on the second stool for the first time in four days, and this is a thing she had thought about and not been asked.

“It is not a register of the men. It is a register of the works. Every work that gets made at that sect is written, and if a work stalls, the filling of it is written too, and the date, and the hour, and the weight of what is taken and the weight of what comes out.”

She said, “The weight of what comes out.”

“That is in it. That has always been in it. They are a sect that weighs what it puts in a fire and they weighed him.”

She said, “Was the writing true, then.”

“It was true when I wrote it and it is true now and I have stood in this yard for nineteen years and I know the ground and it is true.”

She said, “You have checked it.”

“I checked it in the days after I wrote it. I went out and stood where it says and it was where it says.” She looked at the table. “I had forgotten how to read. I read it last night with my husband’s name at the front of it and a lamp, and I got to the second page and shut it, and it was the third hour that I sat up.”

## The Part That Is Not In The Book

She said, “I will tell you what is not in it, because you are going to go and look and I would rather you did not spend a day.”

Ning Yan said, “Say it.”

“It is not a book of reasons. There is not one line in it that says why, and there is not one line in it that says whose fault, and there has never been a word in that book that is about anybody being cruel, because not one of them there thought anybody was being cruel.”

She said, “And they were not.”

“They were not,” the woman said. “I have said that to three people in nineteen years and you are the second one to ask me.”

She said, “Who was the first.”

She looked up and said, “My husband’s brother, ten years ago. He came up that track four times and never once asked me to open it, because he said he would rather not have it than have it wrong.”

## What Was Not Said

She did not say the name.

Ning Yan waited for it, standing at a table in a low house with a book on it, and the woman saw her waiting and put two fingers on the cover of the book.

“No,” the woman said.

“You said it on the road.”

“I said it once on the road and it has been nineteen years and it was the first time in nineteen years and I am not going to make it two because a girl has come sixteen days with a bar.”

She said, “That is fair.”

“It is not fair,” the woman said. “It is nineteen years of practice at not saying a thing and I have got very good at it and you have come sixteen days.”

She said, “You will say it again.”

The woman said, “I will say it again when there is a reason on the ground and not before, and you have not got one yet, because you have got a name and not the morning.”

## Six Hours In A Low House

She sat in that low house for six hours across two days and she heard a great deal that was not about a book.

The woman made a pot of something and put a bowl of it on the table in front of her without a word, and Ning Yan ate a third of it, and she did not thank her, and the woman did not appear to want her to.

And it was the first thing anybody had put in front of her in six days and it was not a gift and it was not a rate and it was not asked for, and she has not been able since to put it into any of the shapes she has for what people do to her.

She said, at the fourth hour of the first of the month beginning 1,874, sitting at the table with a bowl in front of her:

“Three people in twenty-nine days have told me something true and I have got one name out of the three of them and it will not stay in my head.”

The woman said, “That is a strange thing to say about your own head.”

“I can say the name. Any number of times. Word for word. It comes back perfect every time.” She turned the bowl round on the table. “It just does not get in. It goes in as far as the words and stops.”

The woman said, “Then write it down.”

Ning Yan looked at her.

She said, in the ordinary voice, inside a low house on a furnace ground thirty miles east of a turn, at the fourth hour of the first of a month:

“I have been not writing it down for twenty-nine days because I have not been able to get a single true word about it that is mine.”

He said, “That is the second time you have said a thing and been closer to it than you were the first time.”

She said, “The nib is in my bag and the ink has not been opened in two months and there are six leaves of a blanket book against my ribs that have been mine and blank for four months.”

He said, “Yes.”

She said, “And the one thing I have said three times in this room is that I cannot put a told thing in my own hand.”

He said, “That is true and it is the correct answer and you worked it out standing up in the road on the first and you have been carrying it for four days without once acting on it.”

She said, “I have not been carrying it. I have been carrying a book I cannot have.”

“No,” he said. “You have been carrying a decision you have not made yet. That is what the six days have been.”

## Where She Was Left

At the sixth hour of the fifth of the month beginning 1,874 she was standing outside the low house in the yard with the bar on her shoulder, and the woman was in the doorway, and neither of them had said good.

And here is what she had, and she put it down in the yard in the ordinary voice so that it would be in two places.

A book on a shelf in a low house, nineteen years shut, opened once for six hours across two days and shut again on the first of the month beginning 1,874.

Six sentences, said once, in nineteen years, by the correct person, on the ground where it happened.

One name she can produce word for word as often as she likes and cannot get in.

A rule she worked out standing up in a road, and it is the whole of what she has: a told thing cannot be written, and only a true thing can go in her own hand.

And no morning.

That is the whole of what she got on the fifth of the month beginning 1,874, and she was lighter at the sixth hour than she was at the fourth hour of the first, which is three hundred and eighteen days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first.

The woman in the doorway said, “You are going to go and read it.”

Ning Yan said, “I am going to go and read it.”

The woman said, “Then say out loud what you are going to ask me for when you come back.”

She said it on the yard stones at the sixth hour of the fifth of the month beginning 1,874, to a woman in a doorway and a yard with a furnace in it:

“I am going to ask you for one page. And I am not going to take it out of this house. And I am going to read it with my own eyes and I am going to put down in my own hand one line of it, and no word of anybody’s mouth is going anywhere near that line, and you are not going to help me with it.”

The woman said, “You have read a hand.”

She said, “Since I was twelve years old.”

The woman said, “Then come back when you have worked out why you are frightened of it, and not before.”
