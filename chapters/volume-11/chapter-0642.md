# One In Twenty

She counted it on the seventeenth of the month beginning 1,964 at the fifth hour, sitting on the stones outside the gate with a stick and the ash off the seventh hour of the day before, and it took her most of the day and it is four counts and they go one after another in the order she found them.

## Days

Four hundred and twenty.

That is what she is, in the only unit anybody has ever used on her. Four hundred and twenty days of the little open place low behind her fourth rib, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back.

She said it out loud twice and got it twice and then she did the only thing in the world she has wanted to do with a figure since the eighth of a month ten volumes back.

She divided it by twenty.

It came out even. Twenty-one.

She sat in the grit with her mouth open for about ten seconds.

She said, “Twenty-one days of me. Gone out of the world, one out of every twenty, for four hundred and twenty days, and I did not know it was being taken, and it came out exactly.”

She said, “It came out exactly and that is wrong. Four hundred and twenty is a number I have got because I have counted it every single day and it is not a round number by accident.”

## Coppers

Nine coppers in the front of a bag.

She put them on the flat of the bar on the wall and she did it four times, which is the most she has ever done with a row of metal, and she got nine, nine, eight, nine, and she has never once got nine standing in the sun with a bar under them.

A fifth of a copper.

That is the unit of the smallest thing the tax has ever taken off anybody in the world, because not one person on this ground earns enough in a day for a twentieth of a day to come out in a piece of money, and so what a person actually loses is a fraction they cannot hand over.

And the one instrument in her possession that could show her a fifth of a copper is nine pieces of metal on a bar, and it will not do it.

She said that out loud at the seventh hour to the wall and she said it once and did not say it twice, because she has said it before on this wall and it does not get any truer.

## Ash

This is the part she did not expect and it took her an hour and a half and she got it wrong twice before she got it right.

She took the ash off the kiln floor, which is cold by the fifth hour and has been scraped to the north wall since the seventh hour of the day before, and she laid it out in twenty heaps along the base of the wall in a row, the way you lay out a row of barrows.

Sixteen inches across each. The same sixteen inches. She made them the same.

And then she sat back on her heels and looked at twenty heaps that were all exactly the same size and said out loud to a furnace ground at the seventh hour and twenty minutes of the seventeenth of the month beginning 1,964:

“That proves nothing. I made them the same because I had been told they were the same. That is not a count. That is a thing I did with ash.”

She kicked the row apart and she counted them again, properly, with the flat of the bar, and she got nineteen heaps of between fourteen and nineteen inches and one heap of about nine, and that is a real row of ash on a real floor and it says not one thing about anything at all, which is what she wanted.

## Nineteen Years

Six thousand nine hundred and thirty-five days, if a year is three hundred and sixty-five, and she has had that figure since the third of the month beginning 1,904 and she has not looked at it since.

She put it in the ash under the twenty heaps and divided it by twenty.

Six thousand nine hundred and thirty-five goes into three hundred and forty-six and a bit.

Six thousand nine hundred and thirty-five into twenty is three hundred and forty-six, and a bit over.

It does not come out even.

And that is the first thing in four counts that has not come out even, and she sat looking at it for about ten minutes, and the thing that came out of it was not a figure. It was a question.

She said, “Three hundred and forty-six and a bit. In nineteen years, at a furnace, on the biggest job there has ever been on this ground, a man has not come out even once.”

She said, “That is not an accident of arithmetic. That is a shape.”

## The Figure On The Board

She went and got the man of fifty-one at the sixth hour of the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,964, at the long window in the low, and she had not asked him for anything since the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,874 and she stood four feet off and did not ask him for anything.

She said, “There was a figure on the board with a fraction against it on the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,874 and I read it four times and it came out the same four times and I could not hold it, and I went out of that low and sat down on a wall and I have never been able to produce it since.”

He said, “I remember it.”

She said, “What is the fraction.”

He said, “One twentieth of what a man gets for a day.”

She said, “That is the tax.”

He said, “That is the tax.”

She said, “Say the rest of it.”

He said, “It is not mine. It came with the board. It has been on that board since before I came to this ground and it has been the same every day of nineteen years and there is no way on this ground of changing it, and I write what I am given and I have never once written a different figure.”

She said, “Then how do you pay it.”

He said, “The day comes off the board before the board goes up. You work the day and the day is not yours. It has been like that as long as I have been here and it will be like that when I am not.”

## What She Said To The Boy Of Fifteen

She told the boy of fifteen at the stack on the twentieth of the month beginning 1,964 at the seventh hour, and she told him because he had been bringing her a second barrow out of the stack on three days out of four for twenty-one days in the month beginning 1,874 and had stopped, and had told her to her face that he was bringing them out and did not know what she thought he was doing.

She said, “There is a figure on the board with a fraction under it and the fraction is one twentieth, and I worked out on the eighteenth that that whole fraction was made by somebody before there was a ground, and I have not told one person on this ground that except you and I am telling you at the stack because you are the only one here who has ever brought me anything that was not a figure.”

The boy said, “What do you want me to do with it.”

She said, “Nothing. I want you to know it so that in about four years when you are nineteen and there is a board up somewhere and a figure with a fraction under it, you are not the only one standing in front of it.”

The boy said, “I will be on this floor at nineteen.”

She said, “Then you will be standing in front of one and you will not be the only one, and that is what I have bought with ninety-eight days and a hole.”

## What Designing Looks Like

She got it out at the eighth hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,964, standing at the gate, with a stick in her hand and twenty heaps of cold ash behind her and the numbers in her head in a row for the first time in her life.

She said, “I have read boards since I was twelve years old. That is the only thing anybody has ever paid me for and I have done it in nine counties and I know what a thing that grew looks like and I know what a thing that was fitted looks like, and they are not hard to tell apart.”

She said, “A thing that grew has a join in it. It goes wrong somewhere. It is heavier on one ground than another because one ground is better than another. It changes when there is a bad year and it changes when the man in it changes and it changes when somebody dies.”

She said, “A thing that was fitted has no join. You cannot find where it was made. It is the same on every ground and it is the same in every year and it does not change when the man in it changes, and the people who carry it are the last people on the earth who could have made it.”

She said, “One in twenty. There is no join in that.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “Then somebody chose twenty. And the choosing is all of it, because a tithe is ten because ten is a hand and a fifth is five because five is what a man can carry in one go and one in twenty is not a thing anybody arrives at by being careful. It is a number somebody wrote down before anybody was counting.”

And then she stopped, because she had four counts and a figure off a board and she was going to say the word, and she stood in the grit at the gate on a furnace ground for about twenty seconds and said it.

“It is designed.”

She said, “It was made. By somebody, on purpose, to a figure, before there was a ground to put it on. And in four hundred and twenty days it has taken twenty-one days of me and not one person has come to ask me for them, and I did not know it was being taken, and there is no join in it anywhere.”

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,964 than she was at the fifth hour of it, which is four hundred and twenty-two days of it, and not one of them going back, and she had a stick in her hand and did not put the twenty heaps of ash back in a row, and left them lying along the base of the wall in the dark.

And a designed thing can be looked at. That is the part she had not thought about until the ash was cold, and she has to find out how to look at one with two eyes in nineteen days, because a thing that was made to a figure was made to be seen by somebody, and she has never once in her life been the somebody.
