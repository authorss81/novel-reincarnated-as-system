# Waiting With No Road In It

She got off the stones at the first hour of the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,814 having slept two hours and ten minutes, which is the worst she has done since the third night of the road in a month beginning 1,784, and she had not slept because she was waiting for something to be different.

Nothing was different.

The turn was there. The trough with ice gone out of it. The bench with the woman of sixty on it at the far end at the third hour. The bar on two posts four hundred yards east with a board on it and nothing chalked on it. Her own arm, hanging.

And he was talking, in the ordinary voice, about a carter who had gone east at the fourth hour of the night with a load of nothing in the cart.

He said, “You are going to ask me whether anything is different.”

She said, “I am not.”

He said, “You are standing there deciding whether to.”

## The Waiting Has No Road In It

She did that on the twenty-fifth in the morning and it took her most of an hour and it is the whole of what the terms bought.

One at a time. In daylight.

One at a time she has kept for a hundred and seventy-six days. In daylight she has kept for the same hundred and seventy-six days. Neither of those has ever cost her a mile.

The third one is new and it is *standing*.

She used to put a question into a book nine hundred years deep and walk twenty-two miles to the wall and stand in an opening four feet in and say it at the wall, and the wall was a place. Two days of made road to a place, and the place held it, and she knew exactly what she was doing when she got there.

There is no wall now. There is no book. There is no place on nine days of road where a question goes.

So the waiting is not a walk. It is a turn, and a bar, and a bench, and the hours between the fifth and the ninth, and she stands in them.

He said, “You worked that out faster than I thought you would.”

She said, “I have had all night to work it out and there was not one single other thing to think about.”

## What He Has Not Done Since The Ninth Hour

He has not avoided her. That is the thing she was braced for and it did not come.

He has not been careful. He has not shortened what he says. He has not brought up the terms again or looked for a way to prepare her or warned her of anything, and at the sixth hour he said a thing about the number of stones in the third row of the wall by the trough being nineteen, in the ordinary voice, because she had counted them on the nineteenth and it is a number she owns.

That is the cruellest thing that has happened to her since the finding.

She had a thing in her head that was not the count. And the count went on exactly as it had gone on for eight years, and the man in it went on doing what he has always done, which is reading out figures before she has finished taking them in, and the world did not open anywhere.

He said, “I am not going to treat you differently.”

She said, “Why not.”

He said, “Because you would work it out inside a day and then you would have nothing to stand in.”

## The Bench On The Twenty-Fifth

She sat down at the fourth hour, thirty feet off the woman of sixty, and stayed two hours, and she did not ask her a thing.

On the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,814 the woman of sixty had moved six inches along the bench away from the end she was sitting at, and had not looked up, and it had been the largest thing that had happened to her in four months.

Today the woman of sixty did not move.

She sat where she sat and she kept her hands in her lap and she did not look over, and after two hours Ning Yan got up off the bench and stood in the road and felt something she had not felt on this ground before, which is that being noticed once is worse than not being noticed at all, because now there is a day it did not happen on.

That is not a complaint. The woman of sixty has said four sentences in four months and every one of them was correct.

## The Cost Of Daylight

Here is the bill and she wrote it out on the stones at the seventh hour.

The answer comes in daylight. Daylight on this ground is the fifth hour to the ninth. A question takes twenty seconds to say out loud.

So a question about the oldest thing in the world is going to be said in the ordinary voice on a made road at a turn, in the open, within four hundred yards of a board and a bench and a bar on two posts, and any carter on nine days of road who comes along between the fifth hour and the ninth will slow down and look at a girl of nineteen talking to an empty road.

He knows that. She is certain he knows that, because she stood in broken ground a hundred yards north of the turn on the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth and got told he was being careful.

He did not name a wall because there is not one. He named daylight because daylight is the one term left that cannot be kept.

And she has to stand in it.

He said, “That is what you asked for.”

She said, “That is not what I asked for. That is what you said you would do.”

He said, “Yes.”

## The Carter At The Sixth Hour

She went and stood on the stones at the sixth hour and made herself do it, because on the twenty-fourth she had found out that a person who does not look for the price of a thing pays it, and she had never once looked for the price of this one.

She stood on the side of the turn a carter comes down and she said a figure out loud in the ordinary voice, to an empty road, with her hands at her sides.

Forty-one. It was not anything. It was the first number that came into her head.

And then she waited for the road.

There is a carter on nine days of road every nine days and there is not one scheduled. She stood for an hour and twenty minutes and one came at the seventh hour with boards on, and he came up to the turn at a walk the way they do, and he saw a girl of nineteen standing on the stones talking to an empty road with a bar on the ground at her feet.

He slowed.

He looked at her for four seconds and then at the bar and then at her, and she said one more number out loud while he was doing it, because she had decided to be looked at.

He did not stop the horse. He did not get down. He said, “Good,” and went on east.

That is the whole of it and she stood in the road and worked out that in eight years of every kind of door and keeper and keeper’s table and wall, a person on nine days of road has never once stopped for her, and that is going to be true on the morning she gets an answer as well, and that the only witness she will have is a man saying *good* on a cart.

He said, “You rehearsed.”

She said, “I priced it.”

He said, “And?”

She said, “And it is survivable. That is what is survivable. Not asking it in the dark. I cannot do it in the dark.”

## The Six Sentences She Has Not Written Down

She went over the six sentences at the seventh hour, sitting on the kerb by the trough with the loaf out and a piece of it in her hand.

They are the only thing anybody has given her in a year. A man of forty-five told her six sentences about a boy who weighed lime and wanted nothing, and she has them in her head, and there is nowhere in the world she is permitted to write them down.

There is one place. There has been one place since the month beginning 1,724, and it is the second leaf of a book of eight made out of a blanket, against her ribs, with four sentences on it that she read out loud once and will not read again.

She got the bag open and she got two fingers in and she felt the edge of the blanket book and she put her hand back.

Not because of the room. There is room. Six of them are hers and blank and have been hers and blank for four months.

Because that leaf is the only page in the world she owns, and if a stranger’s six sentences go on it then it stops being the place she keeps herself and it starts being the place she keeps things, and she has worked out in the last nine minutes that she does not want that.

He said, “You will forget them.”

She said, “I will not.”

He said, “You will not have a way of proving that you have not.”

She said, “No.”

## Three Days Of It

She worked out what she had on the twenty-fifth.

The twenty-fifth itself, which is today and which she has already spent.

The twenty-sixth, and the twenty-seventh, and the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,814. Three days of daylight with four hours in each of them, and one at a time, which means one of those days and one of those four hours, and then there is nothing at all, because the board goes down on the second of the next month and she has to be at a face on the first.

So she has three mornings.

And she stood on the stones at the eighth hour of the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,814 and made the decision, out loud, in the ordinary voice, to a woman of sixty on a bench who did not look up.

She said, “I am not going to spend one of them tomorrow.”

He said, “Why not.”

She said, “Because I asked it at the ninth hour last night with six sentences in my hand and nothing else, and I did not know what I wanted. A thing I do not know what I want is a thing I am spending on the first shape I can find, and I have spent the first shape I could find on four walls and a furnace and a hand.”

She said, “I have three mornings. I am going to find out what a fire is on foot before I spend one of them asking what you were.”

And that is the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,814, and it is the first time she has had something to wait for and no road to wait on, and she was lighter at the ninth hour than she was at the first, which is two hundred and seventy-eight days of it.
