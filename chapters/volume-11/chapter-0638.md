# Past The Patch Of New Stone

She left the bar against the wall at the sixth hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 1,934 and she left the bag at the gate and she took nothing, not the skin and not the dish and not the book, which is the first time the book has been out of her coat since the twelfth of the month beginning 1,874 and she noticed that and did it on purpose.

Three men were at the west end when she came out of the gate. The shift goes at the sixth and they had nine or ten minutes of walking off the mouth before the fire was drawn.

He said, “You have not got the bar.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “You are going to the floor.”

She said, “I am going to walk round the ground.”

He said, “There is no reason to walk round this ground.”

She said, “There is one and I have had it since the seventh of the month beginning 1,934 and I am not going to say it out loud in front of three men who have to work here after the seventh hour.”

He said, “That is fair.”

## The Six Days Before It

She did not sleep well on any of the six nights before the twenty-first and she did not lie about that to herself, which is new.

The sixth of the month beginning 1,934 she took the bar down off the wall and carried it round the yard on her shoulder to find out how heavy it felt at the end of a day rather than the start, and it felt exactly the same, and she did not write that down and it was the most useful thing she did in those seven days.

The seventh she worked out the hours. The sixth hour is when the men shift and the seventh is when the fire is drawn and the second and the third are when the ground is hottest, and she wanted the sixth.

The eighth she went and asked the boy of fifteen one question, which she could do because a person leaves a mark, and it was: how long does the boy take to walk a floor from the gate to the west end and back.

He said, “Forty minutes if you are not stopping and an hour if you are.”

She said, “Right. That is the number I wanted.”

He said, “It is not a question, it is a floor.”

The ninth, the tenth and the eleventh she left it entirely alone, on purpose, because she worked out that a thing she has been preparing for six days is a thing she is going to be disappointed in, and she wanted no disappointment waiting at the end of it.

The twelfth she washed her feet in the trough at the sixth hour, which she had not done since she was twelve, and the boy of fifteen saw her do it and said not a word, and the man of fifty-one saw her do it from the long window and said not a word, and she has thought about that more than the floor.

And on the thirteenth she did not get up off the stones until the fifth hour and she lay there for two hours with the sky going from black to a colour she has never had a word for and she was not frightened and she was not calm either and she has never been in the middle place before.

## Four Paces Down

The east slope is four paces and it is the only way on and she had it from a man of seventy-one at twenty feet standing in the open, and she got on to the floor at the sixth hour and fifteen minutes of the twenty-first.

The floor came up hot through her boots.

Not warm. Hot. Two hundred and forty paces of stone that has had a furnace worked off the west end of it every hour of the day for nineteen years, and the sun coming in flat on it from the east on the twenty-first of a month, and the second hour of the afternoon still four hours off.

She got her breath back to four inside two minutes and it stayed at four for the first thirty paces, which she did not expect and was glad of, and then it went to seven at the thirty-first pace and stayed at seven and she did not get it back once after that for two hours and forty minutes.

## Nine Paces Along The North Side

She walked the north side with her right hand on the top of the kiln wall, which is the only thing on that floor a person can hold on to, and the wall is warm too, and by the sixtieth pace her hand was the hottest thing about her.

Nine paces along the north side is where the nine by six is.

She came to the north edge of it at the sixth hour and thirty-one minutes with her heart going and seven counts of breath and her right arm open and the floor under her doing what a floor does when somebody has been on it for two hours and a half.

Four courses of new stone. Grey out of the bank at the east. Not the grey of the pit.

A gap in the north joint the width of a nail the whole length of it.

She did not stop.

She did not slow down either, and that was the part she had to work at, because everything in her body wanted to turn onto it and she has four fingers and a thumb on one hand and a right arm open and she could not have turned onto it fast enough to stop if she had started.

She went along it. Nine paces by six on her left hand side and four feet off it, and her boots on the old stone, and the gap in the north joint went past her from the sixth hour and thirty-one minutes to the sixth hour and thirty-four minutes.

And at the sixth hour and thirty-four minutes she was past the end of it and she was still walking and she said out loud, in the ordinary voice, on a kiln floor on a furnace ground, with her hand on a warm wall and not one person within thirty feet:

“Right.”

## The Whole Of The Floor

A hundred and thirty-one more paces to the west end.

She counted them. She could not have told anybody afterwards how she counted them and she has thought about it a great deal since.

The boy of fifteen’s barrow track goes along the working edge and it is worn about two inches below the rest of the floor and she found that with her boots and stayed in it for a hundred paces and went back up onto the flat twice and came off the worn line both times.

At the hundredth pace she stopped.

Not to rest. She could not have told anybody why. She put both hands on the kiln wall and she stood with her face down for twenty seconds and she breathed at seven and counted and the twenty went by and she did not have anything in her head at all, which has not happened to her since she was nine years old.

Then she went on.

And at the two hundred and tenth pace, on a kiln floor on a furnace ground, at the seventh hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 1,934, she came to the west end and the mouth was there.

## The Mouth At The West End

It is a square hole in a brick stack four feet high and it has iron going in and out of it all day and it has never once been let out in nineteen years.

The heat off it is about the heat off the inside of an oven with the door shut and it comes across four feet of open air and hits you in the chest.

She stopped eight feet short of it and stood there and breathed at seven and let it come.

She said out loud, to a furnace two hundred yards from the gate she had walked out of at the sixth hour:

“This is the thing he named.”

She said, “A furnace. East of here. Not this sect and not any sect you have stood in.”

She said, “I walked sixty-five days to get to this ground from a turn where I stood and got no board, and I have been on this ground ninety-seven days and this is the first time I have been eight feet off it, and I got here with two feet and nothing in my hands and I am not holding anything.”

She stood at the mouth for nine minutes.

The three men came back along the floor at the seventh hour and a half and one of them came within ten feet of her and stopped and looked at her for a while and then went on up the floor to work, and he did not say one word, and she has never known which of the two things was the reason.

## Past It

And then she turned round and went back down the floor.

Not the way she came, which was the north side with her hand on the wall. The other side. Down the working edge, in the worn barrow track, past the nine by six on her right hand side and not four feet off it this time but about nine, and past the low houses and the bench and the trough and the stack, and out of the gate at the eighth hour and forty minutes of the twenty-first of the month beginning 1,934.

Two acres. Nine paces by six, four courses of new stone, a gap the width of a nail in the north joint, and a place that has an edge you can learn.

She said it out loud on the flat outside the gate at the ninth hour, sitting down, which she had not planned to do and could not have stood up for.

“The one thing I wanted was a place,” she said. “I said that in this yard on the thirteenth of the month beginning 1,904 at the tenth hour with a page in my hand that had not got one in it, and it was true then, and I have got one now.”

“And I have just walked round this ground in two hours and forty minutes without my bag and without the book and without stopping anywhere, and I am sat down in the dirt outside a gate and I do not want the place.”

He said, “Say why.”

She said, “Because a place is a thing you can get to. And I have got to it. And I have got to it twice, once when a man of seventy-one walked me onto it and once just now on my own feet, and the second time was better and the second time was on my own feet and it did not hold either.”

She said, “And I want to say the part that is worse, and I am saying it because I have got not one person on this ground to say it to except three men at a mouth and a boy of fifteen and a man with a board.”

She said, “There is a place where he stood. I have got it. And I have not got one hour of what it was like, and I did not get one hour of it today, and I walked across the top of the day he had and I came off the far end of it, and that is what I have been doing for eight days and it turns out I am good at it.”

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the twenty-first of the month beginning 1,934 than she was at the sixth hour of it, which is three hundred and ninety-four days of it, and she could not get up off the dirt for four minutes, and when she got up she went and put her hand on the bar against the wall and left it there for a while.

Two hours and forty minutes of hot stone with nothing in her hands has bought her nine paces by six, a furnace mouth at eight feet, and four days on a stack she cannot get off, and she has until the twenty-fifth to find out whether that is a price or a bill.
