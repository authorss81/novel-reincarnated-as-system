# A Permission That Does Not Hold

She was at the stack at the sixth hour of the fourteenth of the month beginning 1,904 with the bar across her right shoulder and the sun not yet over the stone line, and she was on time.

She had thought about the hour for two days. Every stage she has taken since the sixth came between the fifth hour and the seventh, and the seventh was the hour she had set for a question to a man, and she had decided before she got up that the stage did not get to have that hour as well.

The little open place low behind her fourth rib, the size of a coin, went quiet somewhere in the eighth minute of the sixth hour and then did not.

There was no sound in it. There has never been any sound in it, and she has been through this nine times, and the sound goes into the ground.

It took ten seconds.

And after it she was exactly as tired as she had been before it and exactly as hungry as she had been before it, which she was not at all, and what it took came out of the ground under her feet and not out of her hands.

She stood at a stack on a furnace ground with her hand on the bar and waited for the sound that a thing going into the ground sometimes makes for other people, and there was not one on that ground and there was not going to be.

The furnace went on. The smoke went straight up. A man came out of the low at the north end with a length of iron and went past her toward the mouth and did not look at her, and she was standing in the middle of a working yard at the sixth hour and forty minutes of the fourteenth of the month beginning 1,904 and nothing had happened that a person could have put in a book.

## What She Asked, And What Came Back

At the seventh hour she walked to the west end of the yard and put the windbreak behind her and a working furnace ground in front of her, and said it out loud, standing, in daylight, the way he named the terms and the way she has kept them since the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,814.

“Is the permission you gave me still binding.”

One question. In daylight. Standing.

He said, “No.”

She said, “Say all of it.”

He said, “It had force on the twenty-eighth of the month beginning 1,814 and it does not have force now. You asked me for a thing and I gave you a way of asking and the way of asking is a way of asking and nothing has ever been on the other side of it holding. That stopped being true about two hours ago and I did not do it.”

She said, “You did not do it.”

He said, “I did not do it and I cannot put it back and I am not going to stand here and be blamed for a shape that has moved.”

She said, “Then it is the eighth.”

He said, “Yes.”

## The Man With The Board

He came out at the seventh hour of the fourteenth with the board under his arm and a cloth in his hand, and he said the first question anybody had asked her in sixty days.

He said, “Why did you put fourteen up on the second length and not thirteen.”

She stood in the grit and found out that she could not answer it for the count of four, and it was not because she did not know.

She said, “Thirteen and a half.”

He said, “Thirteen and a half is not a figure that goes on a board.”

She said, “It is the figure that came up the wall and the bar is nine feet nine and it does not stop where the stone stops. And I did not put the half on, because a half is not a thing you have ever had room for on that board.”

He looked at the board and then at her and then at the board.

He said, “Then put the half on from tomorrow and I will take it.”

She said, “You have never asked me a question about my work.”

He said, “I have had nothing on the board worth asking about for nineteen years and I have had you on it for sixty days.”

She said it out loud to the ash afterwards, at the ninth hour, and she said it twice in her own head before she said it out loud once.

“That is the thing I said at the wall on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,874, that I was waiting to be asked, and it has taken me sixty days and a stage and a hole in the asking to find out that the waiting was all I had left on this ground.”

## Three Hours Afterwards

She went and got her board off the wall and turned it over and put the flat of it against the stone and she got the stub of the nib out of the front of the bag with her left hand, because her right was open and had been open for two days of the four in five.

She wrote one line on the back of a board that a man of fifty-one had lent her.

*Asked at the seventh hour of the fourteenth of the month beginning 1,904, standing, in daylight: is the permission still binding. Answer given: no.*

Then she put the nib away and tied the string on the bag and hung the board back on the wall and she went and did the second length of the day’s work with the bar of fire out of the pit, and the boy of fifteen called her figure and she called it back and it was right.

And then at the ninth hour she came to the board to write the second line and she could not find the first one.

She stood at a wall on a furnace ground and read thirteen lines of a man of fifty-one’s figures and there was nothing on the back of it in her own hand, and she went through it three times, and the third time she knew that it was there and she could see it and it was a sentence she had written at the seventh hour with a stub of a nib in her left hand.

She said it out loud to the wall, and nothing came back off it, and the line on the board was the same line it had been at the seventh hour.

“There is a line on this board in my own hand that says I asked a question at the seventh hour today, and I can read it, and I cannot put one word of the question back together except the four words I have just said, and I have got them from the line and not from having asked.”

He said, “That is the whole of it.”

She said, “Say all of it.”

He said, “The answer is in you and it will be there tomorrow. The asking is not. You cannot point at it.”

## What She Named It Once

She put the board down off the wall and held the flat of it in both hands and stood in the grit and said it once, at the ninth hour and thirty minutes, in the ordinary voice, to a furnace and a yard and a man who was in the room nineteen years ago and is not in this one.

She said, “I cannot keep him by asking.”

“That is what the eighth took and it is not a limb and it is not a count of breath and it is not a hole. It is that I had a way of holding on to him and it was the asking, and a permission that held a list open cannot hold a fragment open, and there is nowhere asked left to reach, and he is findable by knowing and not by asking, and I am going to have to work with that for the rest of my life.”

And she did not say it again that day or the next.

## The Days After

She worked on the fifteenth and the sixteenth and the seventeenth and the eighteenth and the nineteenth, five days, and she put up barrows at a fifth of a man’s rate off the kiln floor and the boy called every figure and the man of fifty-one wrote them up and hung the board where anybody could see it.

And on each of those five days at the seventh hour she asked him one question. Small ones. The kind that can be checked.

How many lengths is the third course of the kiln wall.

What was the day of the month seven days before the one in the book.

How many men were in the yard in the second year.

And every one of the five came back true and she could not produce one of the five questions at the start of the following morning, and on the nineteenth she tried for twenty minutes at the wall and produced three out of five and could not order them.

She said that out loud to the man of fifty-one at the sixth hour of the nineteenth, because he was writing on the board and she wanted it in the air in front of one other person on this ground.

“I have got five true things and I cannot prove how many questions there were or what order they came in, and every one of them came out of the ordinary voice of a man who is in my head, and I am not saying that to you to make you say something. I am saying it because it happened and this is the only ground in nine days of road where anybody would write a figure down if I asked them to.”

He said, “Who asked you.”

She said, “Not one person. I asked myself, and I am writing it on the back of your board and I am going to rub it out, because a line in my hand that says I asked is not the same thing as having asked, and I have found that out today and I am not going to hold a false instrument.”

He wrote, “You asked yourself.”

She said, “That is the first true thing anybody has written on a board on this ground about me and I am going to leave it up.”

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,904 than she was at the fifth hour of it, which is three hundred and sixty-two days of it, and she had got a name on the third leaf of a book of eight against her ribs and a date above it and a place above that and eight stages past the ceiling under her feet in a yard where the furnace had not been let out once in nineteen years.

And she said it out loud at the gate with her right arm hanging and her bag on her hip, and she said it once and did not say it twice, because a thing said twice is a thing somebody can change:

“I have got him in a book in my own hand. I have not got him by asking. Those were the same thing eight days ago and they stopped being the same thing at about the sixth hour this morning and there was no sound.”
