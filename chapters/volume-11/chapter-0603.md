# A Voice With No Address

She went east at the second hour of the twentieth of the month beginning 1,814 with the bar on her left shoulder, because on the eighteenth she had decided that she was going to find out where the talking comes from and a girl who says things like that and then does not do them is a girl who has found out about herself in the worst possible way.

Thirteen miles. Eleven of made road from the turn to the gate, and two past the gate to the wall, and nothing at all to lie down on for the whole of it.

## Eleven Places She Stands In

She did it properly. That is the thing that is worth putting down, because she is a person who counts and she gave this four hours before she gave up.

She picked a dozen places on the way out where she has heard him say something she has never repeated to anybody, and she stopped in each of them and stood still and waited for him to stop talking, because a man who is in a room stops talking when you walk into it.

Four hundred yards east of the turn, where he said a thing about a bar.

A mile out, where the made road comes off the low ground and you can see the wall standing up against the sky for the first time.

The third mile, at the place the ground goes flat and you can hear a cart four hundred yards behind you.

Six of them on the made road itself.

The gate.

The two flat miles past the gate.

And the opening.

She stood in every one of them, and he talked in every one of them, and not one was different from any other, and she wrote the dozen down on the back of her own wrist with a finger and some of the sweat off her arm and by the time she got to the wall there was no room left on her.

## The Gate With Nobody At It

The gate was standing open.

That is the whole of what a gate is on this ground: it is not shut, and there is no one on it, and it has not been shut and no one has been on it for as long as she has been coming here, and she has never once worked out what a gate with no man at it is for.

There is a bar across the inside of it that a girl of fifteen carries boards under, and there was no girl, and there was no bar, and there was nothing in the road.

She went through it at the fifth hour and there were two miles with nothing to lie down on, which is what those two miles are, and she has done them nine times this year and she did them in an hour and fifty minutes.

He said, a third of the way along it: “You have asked me one question about this road in eight years and it was whether it was open at night.”

She said, out loud, to two miles of nothing, “It is.”

He said, “I know. I say so.”

## The Wall And The Opening

The wall stands open and has stood open since a man of nine hundred years shut a book on a plank table and walked out of it with a bag over his shoulder.

There was no plank table. She has never seen a plank table out here and there is not one out here.

There was no man, and no bag, and no door on eleven feet of boards behind anything, and no brush, and not one thing in an opening eight feet high except a girl standing in it with a bar on her shoulder and the light coming over the eastern jamb.

She went four feet in, which is as far as she has ever gone and is also as far as the floor is level, and she stood there for an hour and twenty minutes.

He talked the whole time.

She said, out loud, in daylight, to a wall: “Say something.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “You have never once said no.”

He said, “I have said it to you forty times. You ask me a figure every day of your life and every day of your life I have to look up a number for you and on this road there is not one number in me this morning.”

She stood in the opening with that, and then she went out through the far jamb, because a person who has been told no in daylight does not get a better answer by waiting for the light to change.

## A Quarter Of A Mile Past

She walked the quarter of a mile out onto the flat and stood on the place where a rut in wet ground came out on the far side of a wall, in a month beginning 1,754, and where she has not found it in her head since the twenty-fourth.

The ground beyond the flat goes up. It went up then and it goes up now and from where she was standing she could not see a barrow, a road, a stone, a person, or a single thing that has ever been described to her by anybody.

She stood on it for an hour.

He talked.

She said, “Are you here.”

He said, “I am not anywhere.”

She said, “You are talking.”

He said, “I am not anywhere that you can get to. That is not the same as being far away and you have known the difference between those two things since you were eleven years old, and you have never once asked me which one it is.”

## The Count She Could Not Start

She sat down on the flat at the eighth hour and did the one she has not been able to start since the third night of the road, when she got four days into her head and stopped.

She got four.

And then she got something else.

Twenty-two.

It came the way the eight came on the nineteenth, except that it was not a count of the coppers, and it had no unit on it, and she sat there in the dark on the far side of a wall with a bar across her knees and held it for ninety seconds and it did not turn into days and it did not turn into anything.

Twenty-two.

And when she stood up to go back it was gone, and it did not come again on the eleven miles back, and she has not had it since.

She does not know what it is a count of. It may be a number of days inside his keeping and it may be a number of days inside somebody else’s, and it is not twenty-two days from the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,814, and she wrote that on her wrist too because she is not going to stand in a place and let a number go by unrecorded.

## Thirteen Miles Without A Figure

There is a thing she does on every walk of this road and she found out she was not doing it.

Counting. She has counted every mile of every road she has ever walked since she was eight years old — the ruts, the stones in the middle, the turns, the places where the ground changes, the number of cart-loads a stretch of road would take. She counts the way other people breathe and she cannot help it and she has never once tried to stop.

She walked thirteen miles and did not count one figure.

She did not notice she had stopped until she was two miles past the gate, in the dark, on ground with nothing to lie down on, and she went back through the whole day looking for where it had stopped, and it stopped at the bar on two posts four hundred yards east of the turn, on the eighteenth, when a man said a number back to her in the ordinary voice.

That is what it costs her. A number of two hundred and seventy-two days of being lighter and she can hold that, and four hundred yards of road and she cannot, because she spent the number on a voice.

## The Barrows On The Other Side

She turned the ground on the other side of this wall over on the flat for an hour, and she is putting it down because it is the only thing on her that she cannot get round.

There are two hundred people or near it in a ground past this flat, in rows, with a stone at the head of each, and she has been in it once for four days and a night and she did not get one word out of anybody there and she did not ask.

Her brother was thirty feet off her for four days and he did not ask her one question, and she has gone over that on a turn at dusk a dozen times since.

And the thing she cannot get round is that she does not know whether he was quiet in that ground or whether she was.

He talks in her head at a turn on nine days of road and in a barrow ground with two hundred people in it, at a wall, on a face with nine barrows in it, on a road at night with a bar under her head. There has not been one hour in ten years in which something in her head has not been counting.

And she stood thirty feet off her brother for four days and got nothing.

She is not going to be able to walk to him and she is not going to be able to hear him and those may be one fact.

## What She Said To The Bar On Two Posts

She came back onto the turn at the second hour of the twenty-first with a bar she cannot get down and four days of no sleep in her, and she went out to the two posts four hundred yards east and stood in front of the board with nothing on it and said it out loud.

“You are not in the book,” she said, “because the book is shut, and you are not at the wall, because the wall has been walked out of, and you are not in the barrow ground, because I have never once heard you say a word while I was standing in it.”

She put her hand flat on the post.

“And you are not in my head, because you are in my head, and there is no distance between me and you at all, and I have spent eight years behaving as though there were.”

The board was bare. The chalk on the rail had gone entirely.

She said, “I am not going to be able to walk to you.”

And he did not say that she was wrong, which is the first time in eight years that she has made a statement about him out loud at a place where she could be proved wrong and has not been contradicted.
