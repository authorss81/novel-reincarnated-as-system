# The Kind Of Person Who Asks

She sat on the bench at the turn at the second hour of the twenty-third of the month beginning 1,814 with three-quarters of a loaf in a cloth in her lap and three days of walking in her legs and did the one piece of work she had left, which was to sit still and count.

She had been carrying a question since the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,634. She had never put it anywhere. On the eighteenth of a month she had said out loud to nobody that she does not ask him yet, and on the twenty-third she sat down at a turn and counted everything she has ever asked him, in order, because that is what she does instead of guessing.

## Every Question She Has Put To Him

Here they are. She got them out in a quarter of an hour and there are not as many of them as she has spent eight years assuming.

What is it for. The thirtieth of the month beginning 1,604.

What does the word do. The fifteenth of the month beginning 1,634.

Will you tell me when. The same month, the twenty-first. Answered: *there is a day.*

What was in it. The twenty-fourth of a month beginning 1,784, and she did not say it.

How much. On a face, at the ninth hour, more than once.

Whether the long way round the cut is open at night.

How many went over the chain on the twelfth of the month beginning 1,784. Answered: four.

What else is on it. The seventh hour of the nineteenth of the same month. Answered: six, and it was not her brother’s name.

That is all of them. Eight questions and every one of them asked for a figure.

## What Every One Of Them Had In Common

She did that at the fourth hour and it took her twenty minutes and it is the whole chapter.

Every one of the eight wanted a number, or a day, or a yes, or a no, or a word with a meaning that could be looked up. Every one of them could be answered in a sentence a person says without thinking.

And every one of them had somewhere to go.

What is it for goes into a column. What does the word do goes into a column. How much goes into the front of a bag. How many went over the chain goes against six on a shut page. What else is on it goes into a book nine hundred years deep and has been standing in it ever since.

She has asked every kind of question there is, and every single one of them had a place on the other side of it, and she has never once in her life asked a thing into empty air.

## The One With No Place On The Other Side

The question she has carried since the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,634 is what he was before he started counting.

It does not want a number. It does not want a day. She does not know what shape the answer is going to be and she has never in her life not known what shape an answer was going to be, and that is the reason she has carried it since the fifteenth of a month beginning 1,634 and never once put it down.

And there is nowhere to put it.

The book is shut. It has been shut since the nineteenth of a month beginning 1,784 and there is not one page left in the world she is permitted to write a question on. The board four hundred yards east takes a weight of stone. The figure the man with the chalk writes down is a weight of stone.

There is nowhere to put a column of it and there is no book for it and there is no wall to say it into, and there has not been one in ten years.

That is the thing that has been stopping her. It was never the asking. She worked that out on the bench at the fifth hour with the sun on her hands and it took her nine minutes.

It is that she has never asked anybody a question whose answer has nowhere to go.

## Why She Was Wrong About That For Eight Years

And here is the part she had to sit with, and she sat with it for an hour and did not get up.

She has been putting every answer she has ever had into something. A shut book. A column in her head. A figure on a board. A man’s own keeping.

And none of that has ever been what happened.

Not one answer in eight years has ever been in a book. The list was a thing on a wall and the word *lost* is still at the end of her own line in a shut book and it has never once been in her head. Six went over a chain at the fourth hour of the twelfth of a month beginning 1,784 and she has counted those six in her own head every day since, and the shut page is shut.

She has been the filing for eight years. That is what she is. She has been standing in ten years believing that a question has to be written down somewhere to be a real question, and it is her own head that has been keeping all of it, and she is the only place any of it has ever been.

She is not a column.

## What She Remembers Of The Fifteenth

She has gone back over it a great many times and there is no moment in it. She can put the date on it and that is all.

What she has is this. There was a place with a face and a man with a chalk on a string, and the man asked her for a figure, and the figure arrived before she had it, and she said it out loud the way she had been taught to so the men at the low could hear it.

And then, after, in the ordinary way, with the chalk going back on its string, she asked it where it had learned to count.

And nothing came back.

Not a refusal. Nothing. The way a floor does not answer a person who says something to it.

And she decided then, on the spot, standing there, that she was not going to ask it anything else, and she has kept that for every day since, and she has never worked out until the fifth hour of the twenty-third of the month beginning 1,814 that she did not decide it because she was afraid of the answer.

She decided it because the answer to a question of that shape is not a thing a person can put down.

She did not have anywhere to put it. She was young and she had a face to work and a figure to say and not one person on nine days of road who would have known what to do with an answer of that shape, and so she never asked, and she has kept a thing shut for ten years over a gap in the floor.

The gap was in the floor.

## What He Said While She Counted

He let her get through all eight of them without saying anything, which is not what he usually does, and at the end of it he said:

“You have made a list of it. In your head, in order, with the dates.”

She said, “Yes.”

He said, “You could have written that down. You have a nib and a cake of ink and six leaves of your own and you did it in your head instead.”

She said, “Because it is not a thing to keep.”

He said, “Then what is it.”

She said, “It is a thing I have just done, and I will know it until the day I do not, and there is nobody I owe it to.”

He said, “That is the first sentence you have said to me in eight years that has no question in it.”

She said, “I noticed.”

## The Lever, In Her Hand

Asking twice.

That is the lever and she has been carrying it since the month beginning 1,634 and it has been in her hand through every one of the questions she counted this morning.

Asking twice is the only thing in the world that would make him get to the first answer sooner. It is also the only thing that would make the first answer never arrive at all, because a question asked twice is a demand and a demand is not a question, and she rubbed it out in the dust at the foot of a wall on the eighteenth of a month beginning 1,784 and has not written it since.

She took it out in her head and turned it over and put it away again, and she is putting down that she did not pull it, and she is also putting down that she has worked out a second thing about it, which is the reason she is never going to pull it.

A thing you can ask twice is a thing you can be refused twice.

She wants a thing she can ask once.

## What She Decided At The Seventh Hour

She said it out loud on the bench at the seventh hour of the twenty-third of the month beginning 1,814, in daylight, to a woman of sixty thirty feet off who did not look up, because she says things out loud and she had to hear the shape of it in the air.

“I can ask him,” she said.

And she did not say what, and she did not ask it, and she sat there with the loaf in her lap and let that stand.

Here is the whole of the decision and there is a second half to it and the second half is the one that matters.

She is not going to do it on a turn with nothing in her hand.

She has walked eight miles in two days and got a shut door, a brother going west, and a loaf she bought with a number she said out loud. None of that is anything she can put a question on.

She wants to ask this thing with something in her hand that is not a question, and she does not have that yet, and she is not going to spend the asking until she does.

That is the middle of the twenty-third of the month beginning 1,814, and she has six days of standing left after this one, and she has not spent one of them, and the question is sitting on the bench beside her where she put it.
