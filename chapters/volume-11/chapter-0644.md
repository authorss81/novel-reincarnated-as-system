# The Price Of Being Known

It is not a field and it is not a room with a floor and a table in it.

It is a space with nothing in it that he can stand on and a thing in it that he can look at, and those are the first two things he checks, because he has been inside one of these four times and he starts every time by working out what kind of place he is in before he lets anything arrive.

There is no light in it that comes from anywhere and there is no shadow. That is not new.

What is in it this time is an arrangement.

## What Is In The Space

She makes one at the seventh hour of the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,964, at a gate, standing, in daylight, with four people inside ninety feet that she has not asked to be there.

She says a sentence out loud to him in her head, once, where it cannot be taken back off her, and then she says it again in the open in front of people, because a thing said once to one man in a head is not a thing that has happened.

And then she tells him what his part in it is going to be, in advance, so that he cannot be the reason for any of it.

Nothing.

That is the arrangement. He is to have no part in it on the day she says it. She gives him that in advance on purpose, and he can see the reason she gives it in advance, because she has been four months working out that a thing told to a person by a man she cannot pay and cannot keep is a thing she cannot put anywhere.

So she takes him out of it before it happens.

That has never been done before. In ten years she has never once taken him out of anything, and she has put him into almost everything, and this is the first time she is putting him out on purpose and on her own side of the work.

## The Question He Goes Looking For

He has one question and he has had it since the seventh of a month beginning 1,874, and he has been carrying it wrong for four months.

He thought it was whether a fragment can be known without being used up by the knowing.

He has been carrying it as though knowing were the thing that does the using.

He looks for the answer the way he looked for the morning and did not find it, and he does not find it in the first hour, and by the second hour he has worked out that he has been holding the question upside down and has been doing it since she read a date out of her own book at a wall and it went through him and out.

## What She Arranged, And What It Does To The Keeping

He reads the four days.

A day of walking round a ground with nothing in her hands, on the twenty-first of the month beginning 1,934, and four days afterwards on nine lengths and then thirteen, and a rate of two hours and forty minutes against four days of work, said out loud on a floor with a stick in the grit, in a yard with nine people in it.

A man of fifty carrying a barrow out of a gate at the second hour on the morning after, saying six things over three days and not saying them all in one go.

A shape with a fraction in it. One in twenty. The same on every ground. Counted in days and not in money. Taken off a board before the board goes up. No person was ever told their own total.

Four hundred and twenty days divided by twenty, and it comes out even.

And then, on the second day of it, sitting on stones outside a gate: a figure divided by twenty that does not come out even, and the sentence *that is not an accident of arithmetic, that is a shape*.

And then the thing that is in this space, which is why he is in it:

*It is designed.*

She works that out on her own. With a stick. On a ground, in the open, in nine days, with a man beside her who writes what he is given and cannot change it and has never once been asked what he was given for.

He is not in any of it.

That is what being known past his own keeping does.

## What Being Known Past His Own Keeping Costs

Here is the price, and it comes out clean, and it is the opposite of what he has been assuming for eight years.

It costs nothing.

Everything she has about him in this world is now in somebody else’s book. A date and two weights and a name, in a good hand, in a cloth book on a plank table, kept by a man of seventy-one who has not read it back and does not need to.

That one is not hers and never was hers and it does not know her name. Four capital strokes, in her own hand, on a leaf of a blanket against her ribs, and that cannot be un-written and does not need her to be alive to hold it. Two hands, nineteen years apart, and the two of them agree, and if one of them stops tomorrow the other one is still right.

She can lose him. She has known that since the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,904, on a kiln floor in eight minutes.

And the keeping does not go with her.

That is the price. It is not a cost to him and it is not a cost to her and it is the cheapest thing he has ever found out, and he has been spending eight years building himself into an entry in order to be findable through her, on the understanding that an entry can be lost.

An entry cannot be lost. It can only be filed.

## The Thing He Was A Fragment Of

He looks at the last part of it for a long time and does not go near it, and then he goes near it, because he has been in four of these spaces and he has never once looked at the one thing he could have looked at in any of them.

There was a thing that had a name.

He does not have the name and he is not going to have it in this space and it is not hers to be given and it is not anybody’s to say out loud in a yard.

He was a piece that came off it. Burned out of it in a mortal sect at nineteen by people who wrote everything down truly and were not hiding.

He has been a stray piece of it for nineteen years and the Scribe is the word for the whole it came off, and he is not going to put it in a book she has to carry, and he is not going to say it to her on a furnace ground either, and he knows exactly how long he is going to hold that, and it is not forever, and the not-forever is new and he does not like it.

What he does with it here is this.

It is not an enemy. It does not act. It has not spoken once in nineteen years and it is not going to.

It is a thing that was made, and it was made to a figure, and one of the figures it was made to is a girl who counts.

And that is the part that has been in this space the whole four days and he has not let it arrive until now.

## The Two Days In Front Of Him

He looks at those two days the way he looks at everything, which is from the outside and in order.

On the second of the month beginning 1,994 he is going to stand at a gate at the sixth hour in daylight in the ordinary voice and say three sentences, and not a fourth one, and the fourth one is a name he is not giving her.

And he works out here, in a place with no floor in it, what the fourth one is, and it is not a name.

It is this: that there was a person in the thing he came off, and that the person did not know either.

That is the piece he has never let himself look at, and it is here in the fourth visit, and it is worse than the being burned out, and it is the reason he has gone round it four times in nineteen years.

Somebody made a levy with a fraction in it so that a certain kind of person would be counted, and then somebody in the middle of that machinery counted, and the counting reached him at nineteen at a fire in the east, and it reached that person too, and neither of them was told, and that person did not get out.

And he is a stray piece of the thing that did the counting, and he has spent nineteen years being the only voice in a girl’s head, and he has never once let the other one into the room.

He looks at it for about an hour.

Then he does the thing he has come here to do, which is not to decide what it means.

He decides what to do about it.

## The Answer

He looks for the exception, the way he looked for a second door in a space at the fourth of a month beginning 1,634, and there is no exception, because there is no rule in here at all.

A fragment is not used up by being known.

A fragment is not used up by being known, because the knowing was never what used it up. He has been treating himself as an entry since the twelfth of a month beginning 1,764, and an entry is a thing that gets filed, and the filing is what takes it, and a girl who reads a name in a good hand does not file a man.

It can.

That is the answer. He has gone looking for it for nineteen years and it takes nine lines in a place with no floor in it, and it is the cheapest answer in this world and it is four hundred and thirty-four days old in the ground where she is standing.

## What He Is Going To Do About It

He is going to stop being an entry.

That is the decision and it is the first decision he has made on his own account in this space in four visits, and it costs him the only thing he has ever been able to use, which is the fact that she has been able to find him.

He is going to stop being an entry and he is going to tell her what he is, in daylight, on her own side, in the ordinary voice, at the gate, before she learns any of it, and he is going to do it as a person handing a thing over and not as a keeper producing an entry.

And he is going to do it in the two days before she says her sentence in the yard.

Because if he does it after, then what she says in the open will be a thing she said to a fragment, and all of what she has built in nine days with a stick in the grit is that she said it to a man.
