# A Rate With An End In It

Wei Ran’s team came along the four miles at the fourth hour of the twenty-third of the month beginning 1,814 with stone on and no board, and stopped where there is room to stop, and she said the rate before Ning Yan had the bag off her shoulder.

“Six coppers the four miles and you have the bar.”

“Six.”

“Six, and the bar goes in the cart and not on the seat, because the seat is for sitting in and the bar is not for sitting in, and if somebody comes looking for a bar in my cart at a gate I am going to be annoyed about it.”

That is a trade. There is not one thing in it that is not a trade, and she has been in a book ten years and there has never been one of those in anything.

## What She Said Going Out

She said the number out loud on the cart track where the lime is in the ruts.

“Six.”

Wei Ran said, “Six. That is the rate and it does not go up for having a bar. A bar is a bar whichever arm carries it.”

She did not ask why not.

The four miles took an hour and ten minutes and the team went at a walk and did not hurry and did not stop.

Ning Yan sat in the back of the cart with her feet up and her back against the side and the bar lying across her knees where Wei Ran had put it, and it took four minutes off her going lighter, which she knows, and which is why she sat in the back of carts for the whole of every run she has ever made.

## What She Said Coming Back

She said the number out loud again on the stones at the turn with her feet on the ground and her arm hanging.

“Six.”

Wei Ran said, “Six. Same rate both ways, because I say the rate both ways and you repeat it both ways, and a number that is only said once is a number somebody can change.”

Ning Yan put her hand into the front of the bag and took out six of the seven and gave them over one at a time, and did not count them, and did not look at them after the second one.

Wei Ran did not count them either. She put them into a box under the seat without looking down, which is what a person does with money a passenger has said a number to.

“A fortnight of carrying at that rate,” Wei Ran said.

“I have not got a fortnight.”

“No,” Wei Ran said. “You have got none. You have got until the twenty-ninth of this month and not a day past it, and I have carried for men who thought the same thing on the eighteenth.”

## The Opinion

They sat in the cart with the team unloaded and the horse drinking at the trough four hundred yards east of the turn, and Wei Ran said the thing she came out west to say, and she took her time over it.

She said, “I do four runs a month. Nine years I have done them. I go west to the mill and I bring stone and I take nothing back east, because there is nothing east to take and there has been nothing east to take for nine years.”

She said, “There is no board on the two posts and there is not going to be one. I have been the only team on this road for a year and a bit and I have not said so to anybody because there is nobody to say it to.”

She looked at the bar on the stones.

“Six coppers the four miles is the last rate on this ground. When this team stops, there is no rate, and there is no road behind it, and there is not a single man west of this turn who would carry a bar for money.”

She said, “That is not a thing I am saying to be kind and I am not saying it to be cruel either. It is a rate. It has an end in it. You will find the end of it standing at that turn with a bar on your shoulder.”

## What Ning Yan Said Back

She said, “I know. I worked it out on the twenty-first.”

Wei Ran said, “Then why are you sitting in my cart.”

“I have seven coppers that I cannot count and a loaf and six days of standing left,” Ning Yan said, “and this is the only arrangement I have made with anybody in a year.”

Wei Ran put the reins down and looked at her for a second.

“Six coppers is not a rescue.”

“I know it is not.”

“It is a mile and a bit each way and four of your arm and I would have taken the bar.”

“I know you would.”

They sat there for a bit. Wei Ran did not ask her a single question. She has never asked Ning Yan one question in ten years and she is not going to start, and she did not ask her where she had been for four days at the end of the month beginning 1,814 or what she had been doing east of a wall or who was in the barrow ground.

She said, “I am going west again in nine days and there is stone on and there is no rate.”

“I know.”

“You will be here.”

“Yes.”

## What She Did Not Say To Her

She did not say thank you. She has said it once in nine years, to a man of nine hundred years, for four sentences about a house, and it bought nothing and she has not said it since, and she is not starting with a woman of twenty-two who has said a number out loud at her.

She did not ask her a single question. Wei Ran came along the four miles and did not ask her one either, and there were forty odd available on each side and not one of them was taken up, and that is a whole conversation in a cart.

And she went back over the four miles on the way down trying to work out what it is about the two of them that means nothing gets said, and the answer is that neither of them has anything to say to the other, and the second answer is that a woman with a team and a girl with a bar are two pieces of equipment on one road and have been since the month beginning 1,754.

She did not say her mother’s name, which was in her mouth for an hour and ten minutes with the lime going past the wheel.

## The Nine Days

“I am going west again in nine days and there is stone on and there is no rate.”

“I know.”

“You will be here.”

“Yes.”

“That is not a question and I am not asking it.”

“No,” Ning Yan said. “It is not.”

And that was the end of it, and Wei Ran went west at the eighth hour with an empty cart behind a horse that was not tired, and neither of them said one word after that, and there was nothing to say, and neither of them was pretending there was.

## The Four Miles Back And The Arm

She went back east four miles on the cart with her back against the side and the bar across her knees and her right arm out in front of her where it had been since the first hour, and it did not come in once for the whole of the way.

Four counts of breath. Seven on a rise. She has not had a different number of anything since the sixth stage, and she checked it twice in the cart by counting Wei Ran’s turns of the reins, which were regular, and she knows how that sounds and she did it anyway.

At about a mile out the arm went properly and stayed gone, and she could not have got up onto the cart off the ground if the cart had stopped, and she worked that out and then worked out the other thing, which is that if the cart had stopped she would not have tried to get up off the ground, because there has not been one occasion in ten years when getting up off the ground was any use to anybody.

## What Is Left On Nine Days Of Road

She did that on the kerb with the light going, because she counts and because a woman with one copper has to know the shape of the ground.

The board. The second of the month, nine days off, and to be paid she has to be on the face on the first, and the face is four miles west and it is stone by weight and it is not a thing a girl with one arm open does in a day and then walk four miles east.

The lane. Four miles west with a woman of fifty at forty feet of it, who put a loaf in her hand once without being asked and has not done it since, and who is not going to, and who would not say why.

The mill. Five miles west, four people, a wheel that has not turned in nine years, and no work, because a wheel is the work.

The post. Nine miles west, past the mill and the lane, and a keeper of fifty-five who keeps a chain and has a flat stone at the foot of a ladder that is bare and is going to stay bare.

The barrow ground. East past a gate and a wall, thirteen miles, about two hundred people, nobody paid, no board, no chalk.

Five places. Four of them are places where a person might feed her and all four of them have a price she does not have.

Six coppers a carry is the sixth thing on nine days of road and Wei Ran has just told her it has an end in it.

## What She Was Left With At The Turn

One copper.

She worked that out on the kerb by the trough with the light going and she had to do it twice, because a number she cannot hold is a number she does not hold, and the first time she got two and the second time she got one.

Both times there was one piece of metal on her knee.

One copper, three-quarters of a loaf, a bar nine feet nine, a bag with five things in it, and a book of eight leaves with six of them hers and blank.

And that is the whole of what is left of everything she has.

He said, at the ninth hour, “One.”

She said, “One.”

He said, “You will not last until the board on that.”

She said, “I am not going to the board.”

She said, “One copper and six days of standing and no rate on this ground after today, and I am not going to a lane to be fed by a woman who has already decided what I cost.”

He said, “Then that is six places and five of them are shut.”

She said, “Yes.”

He said, “And you are going to sit on that kerb and go through them again.”

She said, “I have already gone through them twice.”

He said, “Then you are going to do it a third time, and that is what you do, and I am not going to stop you.”
