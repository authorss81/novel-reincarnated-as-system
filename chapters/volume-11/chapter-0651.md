# The Road Stops At The Turn

She came out of the trees onto the flat at the sixth hour of the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 1,994 with the bar on her left shoulder and the board on two posts standing four hundred yards east of her with nothing on it.

Nothing on it. She stood and looked at it for a minute from further off than she needed to, because she had stood days in that yard months ago and had gone west off it and had come back east twice since, and it has been bare, and it has been up on the first of every month since bare, because there is not one person on that face who has been on it since she left.

The woman of sixty was on the bench.

She said, “You have got a bar.”

She said, “I have got a bar.”

The woman of sixty said, “That is not news and I am not going to make it one.” And then she said, “You have got nine days’ walking in you and four days’ worth of anything else,” which was correct, and she was on the bench at the ninth hour of the day before and she was on the bench at the tenth hour of the day after, and that was all of it.

## Six Coppers The Four Miles

The team came up from the west at the eighth hour with an empty cart behind a horse that was not tired, and Wei Ran put the reins down and looked at her for a second, and did not ask her a single question.

She has never asked Ning Yan one question in ten years and she did not ask one now, and it took a second and a half for both of them to establish that.

She said, “Six.”

She said, “Six.”

She said, “Six coppers the four miles, and it is the last time the number is going to be said on this road, and I am saying it going out and I am going to say it coming back at the turn, because a number that is only said once is a number somebody can change.”

And she said it again at the turn an hour and ten minutes later with the horse standing in the trough and the cart empty behind her.

And it was six.

That is the trade and there was not one thing in it that was not a trade, and there has not been one thing in ten years that was not a trade, and this is the last one that will ever be said on this ground.

Ning Yan took six out of the front of the bag without counting them and did not look at them after the second one and put them into the box under the seat without looking down, which is what a person does with money a passenger has said a number to.

Then she put her hand in and took the rest out onto the flat of the bar and counted them and got three.

Then she counted them again and got two.

She sat on the kerb by the trough with the bar across her knees and did it a third time and got three, and there was one piece of metal on her knee both times, which is not a fact about the money.

## What Wei Ran Said Going Back

She did not ask for the last mile. She has thought about that afterwards more than she expected to.

Wei Ran said, “The bed is finished.”

She said, “The bed.”

Wei Ran said, “The stone at the mill. Nine years I have been going west to it and bringing it east to a turn with no ground east of it, and there is no more stone in that bed than there is in the wall you are leaning on, and the man of seventy-nine knows it and has known it for a season and has not said it out loud because a man of seventy-nine does not say a thing like that while there is still one cart to bring.”

She said, “And when the cart stops.”

Wei Ran said, “Then there is no rate. And there is no road behind the rate, because a road that only goes one way is not a road, it is a habit that four people have, and I am one of them and there are three others.”

She said, “You will go west for work.”

Wei Ran said, “I will go west for work and there is none within nine days of this turn and there is a store behind the mill that buys in small and has a woman behind it who has been buying in small for nine years.”

“I am twenty-two and I am going to be twenty-three in the winter and that is all of my thinking said out loud, and I have said it to you because you have got a bar and not because I want anything from you.”

She said, “I am not asking you for the last mile.”

Wei Ran said, “Good.”

She said, “Why good.”

Wei Ran said, “Because if you had asked I would have had to say whether I would or not, and I would have said yes, and then it would have been a thing I did for you, and I have not done a thing for anybody since the thawing and I am not starting in the middle of a year with no stone in it.”

## The Second Time She Has Been Carried

She worked it out sitting on the kerb by the trough while the horse drank and while a woman of twenty-two put six coppers into a box under a seat without looking down.

Twice. That is the whole number of times in ten years that another person has carried her a distance for money, and both of them were four miles on this road, and one of them was a fortnight of carrying and one of them was the last one.

She said it out loud, sitting on the kerb, in the ordinary voice, to a woman in a cart.

She said, “That is two.”

Wei Ran said, “That is two.”

She said, “And both of them are you, and one of them is the last time, and I have got two coppers left and I cannot count them and I am going to walk five days.”

Wei Ran said, “That is the correct thing to have worked out and I am not going to pretend it is a sad thing, because it is a rate and the rate has ended and that is what rates do.”

She said, “It is a sad thing.”

Wei Ran said, “It is a sad thing for four seconds and then it is a rate, and you have nine years of practice at turning things into figures and I am not going to be the one person on this road who interrupts it.”

## Five Days West

She walked five days west off that mill and she did not get a lift and she did not ask anybody for one.

The store road goes west off the flat and there is a lane four miles along it with a woman of fifty at forty feet of it who put a loaf in her hand once without being asked nine years ago and has not done it since and was not going to, and she went past at the third hour on the third day of the month beginning 2,024 and the woman was at her door and they looked at each other and neither of them said anything at all.

There is no shed at the turn. There has never been a shed at the turn.

The ground went up on the second day and she took the rise at a walk with seven counts of breath and the arm off and out in front of her where it had been since the first hour, and on the fourth day she slept nine hours in a barn on a floor of broken stone with the bar under her head, which is the longest she has slept since she was a child, and she did not enjoy any part of it.

And on the fifth day, at the seventh hour of the third of the month beginning 2,024, she came over the last rise and Lowwater was in the bottom of it with the smoke off a hundred chimneys and a tower standing at the foot of a square at the west end of it.

She had not been in the town in five years and she went down the rise the whole way looking for one thing, which was her own face in a shuttered window, and she found it in the third shop along and stopped there for two seconds.

And what she saw was a girl of nineteen with one arm out in front of her and her hair gone short on the left where a bar has sat for two hundred and forty days, and she had forgotten what she looked like and she was not pleased with it and she went on down the rise.

She stood on the rise for ten minutes with the bar on her shoulder and did not go down.

She said out loud to a town, standing, in daylight, at the seventh hour of the third of the month beginning 2,024:

“There is a building four miles down the other side of this with a lamp in a cage on the fourth landing and a man in it with a cough, and I have got two coppers I cannot count and an arm that is open four days in five and one question, and I have not worked out what the question is yet.”

He said, “You have. You worked it out on the ninth hour of the seventh of the month beginning 1,994 on a rise with a man with a notebook.”

She said, “That was two days ago.”

He said, “It was two days ago and you have been walking since and you have not changed your mind once, and the question is going to be the same one, and you are going to be frightened of it in a stair in two hours.”

She said, “How do you know what the stair is going to be like.”

He said, “Because I have never once been in that building and I know exactly what it is going to be like.”

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the third of the month beginning 2,024 than she was at the fifth hour of it, which is four hundred and sixty-six days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back.

And she went down the rise into a town she had not been in for five years with two coppers she could not count and a bar nine feet nine and a question she had not said out loud yet.
