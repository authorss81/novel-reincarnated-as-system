# A Trade And Not A Kindness

She asked for work on the twenty-third of the month beginning 1,874 at the sixth hour, standing at the mouth of the long window in the low where the man of fifty-one writes the figures, and she said it out loud in the ordinary voice.

She had told a man with a shut notebook on a slope on the thirteenth of a month that she had not asked a person for anything in ten years, and she had said it as a fact about herself, and she had no idea it was going to be the next thing she did.

“Can I work.”

He said, “Can you read a figure out loud.”

“Yes.”

He said, “Then you can work.”

He said, “There is broken stone off the kiln floor and it goes up the wall to the stack by weight. The boy calls it and I write it. It is seven before the fire is drawn and four after.”

She said, “Say the rate.”

“A fifth of what a man gets and the same board.”

She said, “Say it again and see if I have it.”

He said, “A fifth of what a man gets and the same board.”

## What A Barrow Is Here

The stone off the kiln floor is not the stone on a stone face and she worked that out in the first hour.

On a face it is a barrow of whole rock with a bar in it and it comes up a slope and it is the heaviest thing a person moves. Here it is broken stone, three-quarters of the weight, and it goes four lengths of nine paces, and it is loose and it runs.

She said, at the seventh hour of the twenty-third, at the stack, to a boy of fifteen who was about to call her figure:

“I can do that.”

The boy said, “Everybody can do that. The heavy one is the one from the pit and we do that two of us.”

“Give me that one.”

He said, “You have not got a second one.”

She said, “Give me the pit one anyway.”

He said, “All right,” and he said it in exactly the tone a person uses for something that is going to go badly and is not worth arguing with, and she has thought about that tone a great deal since.

## The Third Of The Arm

It went at the second trip and stayed gone for the rest of the day, which is the ordinary arrangement and not an injury and not a thing anybody in that yard has ever noticed her do.

Her breath went to four and then to seven on the third length and stayed at seven for the rest of the day.

She put up fourteen barrows of pit stone between the fifth hour and the ninth hour of the twenty-sixth, which is more than a boy of fifteen has done in the same four hours, and the boy was on the floor with a shovel most of it and did not put up a single one of them himself.

Not one person remarked on it.

She said, to the yard, at the ninth hour and twenty minutes of the twenty-sixth, with her hands grey and her right arm hanging:

“Say that figure out loud to me.”

The man read it off the board.

She said it out loud to find out whether she still had it.

She got it once.

She said it again after a minute and she got it once.

She said it the third time and she got it twice, which was a figure that has never been on that board in nineteen years of a boy calling figures at that wall, and the man of fifty-one looked up off the board for a second and then went on writing.

She put down the truth of it standing in the grit at the ninth hour and thirty minutes of the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 1,874: she can be paid and she cannot keep what she has done.

## The Telling That Went Past Her

At the seventh hour of the twenty-fourth he gave her a thing, the way he does, unasked, in the ordinary voice, while she was pulling the fourteenth barrow up the third length.

He said, “There was a course laid along the top of the kiln wall in the second year and it was not straight and I took it up again on my own time and the man who laid it never said a word about it and neither did I.”

She said, “How many courses.”

He said, “Ninety-one.”

And she kept on pulling.

That is the thing. She has gone over that hour more than any other in the batch and the whole of it is that she did not stop, and she did not say it out loud, and she did not say it a second time to see whether she had it, and she did not put it anywhere.

It went through her and out at the fourth length and it is gone, and ninety-one is a number she can still say now if she wants to, and the shape of the ninety-one courses on the top of a kiln wall in the second year is not in her anywhere at all.

She said, at the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth, at the stack:

“You told me a thing and I have not got it.”

He said, “No.”

She did not check it.

He said, “That is the whole of what nine days on that ground has done to you and I am not going to take any of the credit for it, because you worked it out on a wall on the seventeenth and I had nothing to do with it.”

## What It Is Worth

Two coppers a loaf at the store behind a mill five miles west of a turn, and the board goes down on the second of a month, and she has to be on a face on the first.

The second of the next month is the second of the month beginning 1,904, which is day 1,905.

The first is day 1,904.

She said, at the sixth hour of the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,874, on the stone wall on the west side of the yard with her feet in the ash:

“Four days to the first and five to the day it goes down, and four days of this. And I have got one copper I cannot count and no bread since the twenty-seventh of a month beginning 1,844, and I am not hungry, and a person is not the same thing as a body and those are two different kinds of running out.”

He said, “You have been here a month and a person has not offered you anything on this ground.”

She said, “The woman has left a bowl in front of me twice and the boy of fifteen has carried me four inches of a barrow perhaps a dozen times and the man has given me a job, and none of it was asked for and none of it has a word on it.”

He said, “That is what a ground is when it is run properly.”

She said, “It is. And I have spent ten years being put in rooms by people who wanted a figure out of me, and this is the first place anybody has put me to work without once asking me what I am, and I do not know what to do with it.”

## The Second Barrow, The Second Time

She noticed it on the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,874 at the fifth hour and wrote it down in her head without knowing what she was writing down.

The boy of fifteen brings one barrow. On the good mornings he brings two, out of the stack on the far side of the yard, and he sets the second one down at her feet and then takes the first one back.

On the twenty-first he did it. On the twenty-second he did not. On the twenty-third, the twenty-fourth and the twenty-fifth he did it, and she did not see the shape in that at the time.

On the twenty-sixth he did not and on the twenty-seventh he did not, and by the seventh hour on the twenty-seventh she had stopped waiting for it and had not noticed she had stopped.

She said, out loud, to a yard with thirteen people in it, at the sixth hour and ten minutes of the twenty-seventh:

“You have been bringing me a second barrow for three days out of four.”

The boy said, “Have I.”

She said, “Out of the stack, and then you take the other one back. Three days out of four and then not at all.”

He said, “I bring them out. I did not know what you thought I was doing.”

And that was the end of it, and he went back to the floor with his shovel, and she stood at the stack with her hands in the grit.

The thing that has stayed with her is not that it stopped. It is that it went on and off first, and that there was no pattern in it she could read.

She has gone over that exchange a great many times since and she has not got past it and she has not found a way to be angry about it, and that is what has been going wrong with her for six days and she will not know what it is for another three.

## The Twenty-Seventh And What It Is

At the sixth hour of the twenty-seventh of the month beginning 1,874 the man of fifty-one put the board up on the nail inside the low where anybody can see it, and under her own entry he wrote a figure, and she could not read it off the nail from the yard because it was in the good hand and she was thirty feet off.

She did not go and read it.

She went and read it, at the sixth hour and twenty minutes, because she is a person who reads boards and has been for ten years, and there were three people inside the low and they let her.

It was a figure with a fraction against it.

She read it four times and it came out the same four times and it was not a number she could hold, and she did not try to hold it, and she walked out of the low and across the yard and sat down on the wall.

She was lighter at the ninth hour of the twenty-seventh than she was at the fifth hour of it, which is three hundred and thirty-seven days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first.

He said, “That is the best day of work you have done since the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 1,814.”

She said, “It is.”

He said, “And you cannot produce it.”

She said, “I cannot produce it and I did not try and it has cost me something and I cannot tell you what, and I have not said anything on this ground that has not been a trade except a thing I said to a woman on her own step, which was not a trade and cost me a leaf and a month.”

He said, “Say the rest of it.”

She said, “The rest of it is that a copper in a bag is worth more to me on a road than a figure on a board in a yard, and that is not true about money and it is true about me, and I have known it for nine days and it has not gone away.”
