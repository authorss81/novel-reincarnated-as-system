# Two Hands, Nineteen Years Apart

She went back at the seventh hour of the eighth of the month beginning 1,934 and stood at twenty feet from the bench in the open before she came the last ten, because that is what she was told and because she has found out that a rule given to her by a person on a ground is usually a rule about that ground and not about her.

He said, “You have a reason.”

She said, “I have one and it is a bad one and I am going to give it to you before you open anything.”

She said, “You told me to come back when I had a reason. My reason is that I have learned this floor has one way on to it, and that is why a page written by somebody who stood here did not have to say where, and I want to know whether the ground’s book says where.”

“If it does not say where then nothing has been kept about this place at all. And if it does then you have been sitting on a bench in front of one for nineteen years.”

He said, “That is a reason.”

She said, “It is the only one I have.”

He said, “It is better than the last four people.”

## What Is In The Book

He went in through the opening with no door in it and she stood outside for a minute and a half and then she went in, because he had said that she could and because she has not once in ten years been asked to wait for a thing she was going to be given.

It is a low house. A plank table. A stool. A lamp with a stone on a dish of oil, because there is no other light in it and he is seventy-one.

The book is cloth boards, an inch and a bit thick, with a string round it, and the string is tied and untied nine hundred times and has been untied and retied so many times that it has a shape of its own.

And it is in a good hand. Every line of it is the same hand. She could tell that from six feet and she could tell it from any distance because she has been reading boards since she was twelve.

He untied it and opened it on the plank table and turned it round so that the writing was the right way up for her, which she noticed, and which was the first thing anybody on this ground had done for her that she had not had to ask for.

There are entries. One for a thing taken. A day at the head of it, and two weights, and the difference, and then a name at the foot of it in capitals.

And the names at the foot of them are not all of them the same size and they are not all of them spelled the same way and one of them is in a different ink.

## The First Two Days Of It

She did not get anything on the eighth. She looked at it for an hour and she got the shape of the thing and not one thing else, and she said so at the seventh hour and forty minutes, standing in a low house with a lamp in it, which is not daylight and which he had said was a thing he was not going to do again.

He said, “Come at the seventh hour and read it and do not ask me anything on the first day.”

She said, “Why not.”

He said, “Because you will read it as a thing about you and it is not a thing about you. It is a thing about a ground.”

She came at the seventh hour of the ninth and the tenth and read it for two hours both days and said not one word to him at all, and on the eleventh she came at the seventh hour and sat down on the stool across the plank table from him and put her own book out on the wood between them.

## What He Said, And When

He said, on the eleventh of the month beginning 1,934, at the seventh hour, with the book open and not turned round:

“I kept it.”

She said, “I know you did.”

He said, “You do not know that. Not one person has ever written down that I kept it, because a thing that is kept is not a thing that is done.”

On the twelfth, with the lamp going:

“I wrote it true.”

She said, “I have read four hands in my life and one of them was yours and I could tell.”

He said, “Then you can tell whether it was true.”

She said, “It was true.”

On the thirteenth, in the last of the light at the bench:

“I have not read it back.”

She said, “Once in nineteen years.”

He said, “Once. It is a page of figures and a name at the foot of it. There is not one word in it to read.”

## What He Said About The Keeping

On the fourteenth of the month beginning 1,934, at the seventh hour, sitting on the stool with the book shut and his two hands flat on the cloth boards, he said the rest of it, and she wrote none of it down and has not needed to.

He said, “A name in a book is kept whether or not a person comes for it.”

She said, “Say the next part.”

He said, “The keeping does not have to be understood to be accurate. I did not know what any of them were for. I was not asked and I did not ask. I wrote what I was given and I wrote it true and I have not been wrong once in nineteen years and I have had no reason at all.”

He said, “Not one person has come for that book in nineteen years. Not for one of them. And not one of the names in it has ever been read back by a person who was in the room.”

He said, “It has been right the whole time.”

And then he put his hands on the book and did not open it again.

## What She Did With The Six Things

She wrote none of it down, and she wants that on the page, because she has written down every figure she has ever been given and there is not one sentence of it in her own hand anywhere.

Here is what she did with it instead, and it took her two days and it is the only method she has left.

She said each of the six things out loud, one a day, at the gate, at the seventh hour, in the ordinary voice, to a man of seventy-one sitting on a plank on two stones twenty feet away who has not opened a book since the fourteenth and is not going to open one tomorrow.

And when she had said one she checked whether she could say it again on the following day without being told it first.

Five of the six came back.

The sixth did not, and the sixth was the one that said that not one person has come for that book in nineteen years.

She worked out why on the fifteenth, sitting on the stones outside the gate at the fifth hour, and it took her ten minutes.

He had not said it in the presence of anything. Everything else he had said about the book had the book in front of him on the plank, and the keeping was in the room with the keeping. And that one he said with the book shut under his two hands, off the top of his head, at the seventh hour on the fourteenth of a month beginning 1,934, with no book in the world in reach of either of them.

And a thing said off the top of a head with no book in the room to hold it is a thing she cannot keep.

She said that out loud at the fifth hour of the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,934, and she was frightened of it for four minutes, and then she got hold of the other half.

The other half is that it does not matter. Because of what the man of seventy-one said on the fourteenth, in the same three days, standing, in front of a girl who can read a hand: a name in a book is kept whether or not a person comes for it.

She does not have to keep it. That is the first instruction anybody has ever given her that she is glad of.

## The Same Four Strokes

She had her own book out on the plank table from the eleventh and it had been out on the wood, open, face down, the whole three days, with the third leaf turned back and the string round her fingers, and he had looked at it once and had not asked her about it, which she has thought about a great deal since.

She opened it to the third leaf at the seventh hour and twenty minutes of the fourteenth and put it flat on the table next to his.

A date above a place above four capital strokes.

*YUAN SHENG*

And she took his hand — not his hand, the book, and turned it round so that the foot of the entry on the fourteenth of a month nineteen years back was facing her — and the name at the foot of it in capitals in a good hand was four strokes long and they were the same four strokes.

She did not say anything for two minutes.

Then she said, “Spell it.”

He said, “I cannot spell it. I have never been able to spell it and I have copied it nineteen times off other hands and got it right every time because there are only two reasonable ways to write it and he has one of them.”

She said, “That is not what I asked.”

He said, “Then ask it again properly.”

She said, “Is it the same.”

He said, “It is the same.”

## What She Took Away

She sat on a stool in a low house at the north end of a furnace ground with two books open on a plank table and she worked it all out in ten minutes, and it is not a complicated sum, and it is the largest thing that has happened to her in ten years.

For eight years, the only record in the world of that man was inside one girl, and she has spent eight years being careful with it — careful not to say it, careful not to lose it, careful not to put it where a person could use it.

And a man of seventy-one on a bench wrote it down in a good hand nineteen years ago because he was given it to write, and he has not read it back, and he has no reason, and he does not know what any of them were for.

Two hands. Nineteen years apart, and she does not know the day to the day and has never asked and is not going to.

At the eighth hour of the fourteenth of the month beginning 1,934 there was a lamp in the low house and the man of seventy-one on the plank table under it, and she said it out loud to the three of them, once.

“Everything I have spent eight years being careful about was careful for nothing, and all I have got is that a thing can be true and be written true and be right for nineteen years and not one person ever has to read it.”

He said, “That is what I said.”

She said, “You said it better.”

She said, “You said it better because you have been sitting on a bench inside it for nineteen years and I have been carrying it around inside my own head and calling that the dangerous one, and the dangerous one is the one that can be lost.”

She stood up off the stool and tied her own book and put it back against her ribs on the side where the paper is, and she put her hand flat over it for a second, which she has not done since the twelfth of the month beginning 1,874.

And she said, at the eighth hour and twenty minutes, standing, in a low house, with a book shut on a plank table that she is not going to see the inside of again:

“There are two of them now. And if one of us stops tomorrow the other one is still right, and that is the first time in my life that a thing about him has been held by anything that is not me.”
