# Walking Back Into The Rows

She went east out of Lowwater at the sixth hour of the fourth of the month beginning 2,054 with the bar on her left shoulder and four sheets of a fair hand folded four times inside her coat against her ribs on the side where the book is, and she walked the four miles down off the mountain and out onto the store road and kept going.

Because the wall is thirteen miles east of the turn and the rows are another four past the wall and she had two days.

She had told the boy of sixteen that it would be nine days. It is nine days to somewhere that is not here and that is the only arithmetic she has.

## The Old Man On The Road

She saw him at the second hour of the fifth of the month beginning 2,054 on the long flat about four miles short of the wall, going east at the pace of a person who has nowhere particular to be and has been going east for a very long time.

He was nine hundred years old and he was carrying a shut book under his arm and he had a staff that was not a staff and he was going her way, and the road was wide enough for both of them and they went past each other at nine feet.

Neither of them said anything.

That is the whole of it. She did not go into the road and she did not slow and she did not speed up, and he did not look at her and she did not look at him, and they were nine feet apart for four seconds and then they were past each other.

She has met him three times in ten years and he has said a dozen sentences to her across the lot of them and four were on a house, and she said the words once, nine years ago, to a man of nine hundred, and it bought nothing, and she has not said them since, and she was not going to start on a flat four miles short of a wall.

He said, out loud, to the road, on the far side of her, without turning round, and she has thought about it for two days:

“He is still here.”

She said, to herself, out loud: “That is four sentences about a house in ten years and that is the fifth, and he has said five sentences on one thing and not one about himself.”

## The Crest At Dusk Of The Fifth

She came up onto the crest at the seventh hour of the fifth and there were two hundred people in the bottom of it in rows with a stone at the head of each, and she stopped walking at the top of the rise two hundred and fifty yards short of the first row.

Two hundred and fifty yards.

She had stood on that crest on the ninth of the month beginning 1,844 at the ninth hour and forty minutes of the light with sixty-one rows counted and one figure moving in it, and she had wanted to go down into it more than she had wanted anything since she was a child, and she had not gone in, and the reason had been that she had come nine days east with one copper she could not count and a stub of a nib and no ink.

She said out loud, standing on the crest at the seventh hour of the fifth of the month beginning 2,054, to two hundred yards of rising ground and a clerk’s lamp in her coat and four sheets of a dead man’s handwriting:

“The reason I did not go in last time was a pen. I have got four sheets in a fair hand in my coat and I have got five blank leaves and a nib that I have used four times, and that is a worse reason, and I am going in.”

## Going In

She went down the rise at a walk with the bar on her shoulder and no one stopped her and no one said anything.

There is no man over any of them. There is no board and there is no chalk and nobody is paid, and that is all that ground is and it has been all of it for nine years.

She walked up the second row and then stopped and turned round and looked at all of it from the inside, which she had not done, and there were two hundred stones at the heads of about two hundred rows and a light coming on over the rise behind her and a man standing about forty feet off in a third row looking at her.

He had hands like their father’s.

She said his name out loud, once, in the open, in front of about two hundred people, which she had stood on a crest nine years ago and sworn she would not do.

She said, “Ning Wu.”

## What She Said In There

She said it at the eighth hour, standing in the third row between the stones, in daylight, with a light coming on over the rise and about two hundred people in the ground, and she had not asked any of them to be there and they were there because they are there.

She said, “I am going to say one thing and it is going to be true and I am not saying it to any one of you and I am not going to be asked a thing on it.”

She said, “There is a fraction under a figure on a board on every ground in this world. It is one in twenty and it is counted in days and not in money, and it comes off the board before the board goes up, and no person on any ground has ever been told their own total, and it cannot be changed by anybody who collects it.”

She said, “It was made to a figure. Somebody chose twenty. It is the same on every ground and it has not changed in nineteen years and there is no join in it anywhere.”

She said, “And it is not for the ground. It is for a kind of person.”

She said, “And a clerk in a tower wrote it down in a fair hand a fortnight ago, on four sheets that go up no stair, because he saw it and believed a girl of nineteen who came to him with it and he had not asked her one question in seven years.”

And then she stopped, because the rest of it was a piece of a thing with a name that a clerk would not write down, and that is not hers to give away in a ground with two hundred people in it.

## What Her Brother Said

He came up the row to about eight feet and stopped, and he said it in the ordinary voice, and it is the first thing he has said to her since a road five miles west of a mill nine years ago.

He said, “I know what a fraction under a figure is.”

She said, “You do not.”

He said, “I have been in this ground nine years and there is a board at the north end of it where the men write what they have done, and there has been a stroke and a number under the stroke every day of it, and I have carried stones for four men for nine years and I never once asked what the number was because I could not read until the third year.”

She said, “And when you could read it.”

He said, “When I could read it I went and found out and I did not tell anybody.”

She said, “Why not.”

He said, “Because it is in my four days and not in theirs and I was ten when I came here and I am nineteen now and I have worked out in nine years that if a man on this ground knew that a fifth of a day was coming off him for something he was not part of, then there would be not one person on any of these rows.”

He said, “And there would be not one person on these rows in a month.”

She stood in the third row between two stones and did not say anything for forty seconds.

She said, “That is the truest thing anybody has said to me in nine years and it is also the worst.”

He said, “I know. I have known for five years and I did not say it then.”

She said, “You did not say it to me.”

He said, “No. You walked past me on a road nine years ago with a loaf in your hand and you did not stop and I have not had one word with you since, and I have not been waiting for one, and I am not going to tell you that I have.”

## The Forty She Did Not Take

She did not ask him one question. She has not asked him one question since she was ten years old and she did not ask him one now, and there were forty available and she took none of them, and she has gone over every one of them in the two days since.

She did not say the other thing. She has not said it to a living person since she was a child and she did not say it to a boy of nineteen in a row of stones.

She did not say she was sorry, and she did not say she had been wrong, and she did not say anything at all a year, because there is not one thing she could say about it that would be true and there is not one thing she could say about it that he would forgive, and she has known both of those since she was a child.

She said, “I am going to be at the turn at dawn on the sixth.”

He said, “There is nothing on the turn.”

She said, “There has not been anything on the turn since the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,814.”

He said, “I know. I have walked past it in nine years.”

And she went out of the rows and up the rise and stood on the crest in the dark for twenty minutes with her back to two hundred stones and a bar on her shoulder, and she did not count them, because she had counted sixty-one on this crest nine years ago at the ninth hour and forty minutes and she had not counted them now, and there was nothing to be gained by it that she could put in a book.

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the fifth of the month beginning 2,054 than she was at the seventh hour of it, which is four hundred and ninety-eight days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back.

And she walked west off the crest in the dark with eleven miles of made road in front of her and nothing between the gate and the wall to lie down on, and she had said one true sentence out loud in a ground of two hundred people and it had been heard by two hundred and one and it had settled nothing, and not one person in that ground had said her name, and he had not either, and she had said his twice.
