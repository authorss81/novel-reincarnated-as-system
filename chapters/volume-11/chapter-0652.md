# A Fair Hand And A Cough

She came into the square at the seventh hour of the fourth of the month beginning 2,024 and crossed it with the bar on her shoulder and went up the four miles to the tower the long way round the tanner’s, because the short way goes past a door she has not gone past in five years and she did not have the hour for it.

The stair is four flights and the short breath comes at the third landing and it came there, and she stood on the third landing for four seconds the way she has stood there since she was a girl, and then she went on, because going up is the better half and going down is the worse half and she had four hours of it.

The lamp in the cage on the fourth landing was burning.

She had not seen it in five years and she looked at it for as long as it takes to go up one flight and it was exactly the same, which she had expected and did not enjoy.

## The Man On The Bench

He was on the copying floor with a sheet in front of him and the lamp turned away from it and his back to the stair, the way he sits.

Feng Lushi. She had not said that name to herself since she was a girl of thirteen and she had not said it to him since she was a girl of thirteen either.

He turned round.

He is sixty-seven. He was sixty-two, and a hundredweight of coal came up a road in four days on the ninety-seventh day of that winter five years ago, and the fire has been in that room every winter since, and the chair has been at the second hour on the county’s reading days for five years, and he has got four years out of it and he knows to within a season what it bought and he has never once said the number out loud to anybody including her.

He has a cloth in the left side of his coat and there is blood in it, and she looked at it for a second and a half and he saw her look at it.

Feng said, “That is not new and it is not interesting and you are going to ask me about it.”

She said, “I am not.”

He said, “Good. Sit down. You have got no blood and you have got a bar.”

She said, “I have got a bar.”

He said, “Put it against the end of the bench. It has been four flights and you came up them with it and you have been doing that since you were nine.”

She put the bar against the end of the bench and it went down two inches under its own weight, and he looked at that too.

## What He Said, And What He Did Not Say

He did not ask her one question. He has not asked her one since the ninety-seventh day and he did not ask her one now, and the two of them sat at opposite ends of a copying floor with four hundred volumes on the shelves behind him and a working pile on the bench and four sheets on the rack.

Feng said, “You have not come for the coal.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “You have come a column.”

She said, “I have.”

He said, “Say the column.”

She said, “You said you could not read the gaps in it and you stopped trying.”

He said, “I did. In the third year. I had four years of trying and it did not go into a fair hand any better at the end than it did at the start, and a thing that will not go into my hand is a thing I have stopped trying to write down, and I was right to stop and I would have been wrong to keep going.”

He said, “That was five years ago and it is the reason you have come up four flights with a bar, and I have thought about an hour of it in five years, which is not much for a thing a person comes five days for.”

She said, “What have you thought about.”

He said, “That whoever could read it would want to, and that not one person who could read it would come up a tower for it, and that you would.”

He said, “And I have been wrong about two thirds of things I have thought about in this building and I would rather you did not tell me which thirds.”

## One Question, In Daylight, Standing

She stood up off the stool and stood at the end of his bench in daylight, with the lamp behind him and the volumes on the shelves and four flights under her, and she asked him one question, one at a time, the way she has kept it since the ninth hour of the twenty-fourth of the month beginning 1,814.

She said, “Is there a thing in the world that can show a person what another person sees.”

He said, “Yes.”

She said, “Say all of it.”

He said, “There is one and there is only one and it does not make a copy and it does not write anything down, and it shows one man what another man is looking at while it is on, and it cannot be put down once it is on, and it is not made for a girl of nineteen who has walked five days to ask for it.”

She said, “Who has it.”

Feng said, “I have.”

And he put the brush down square with the edge of the bench and took his hand off it, which is a thing she has watched him do about nine hundred times and which means he has stopped working for the length of the conversation.

## The Thing In The Drawer

It was in the drawer under the working rack, in a cloth, and it is smaller than she expected it to be, which she had not expected.

He said, “It came up the road in the same year as the coal, with a man who had a list and did not have a name on it that I could put in a sentence, and he left it and went down the stair and I have had it in that drawer for five years.”

She said, “You have had it for five years and you have not used it.”

He said, “I refused it. On the ninety-seventh day, before the coal, in front of you, and my reason was a hundredweight of coal and a chair at the second hour and my chest, and I meant every word of it and it is the correct reason.”

She said, “Why are you using it now.”

He said, “Because the fire is in and the chair is at the second hour and the coal has been coming up that road for five years and I have been warm, and a man who has been warm for six winters is a man who is about to stop being warm, and I am sixty-seven.”

She said, “That is not a reason. That is two figures.”

He said, “It is all of the reason and I have not dressed it up for you, because I have never dressed anything up for you in fourteen months and five years and one afternoon.”

She said, “It has got a name.”

Feng said, “It has one name and it is the only one I am going to say.”

He said, “Lung.”

She said, “Say the rest.”

He said, “The rest is that it is a thing a man signs and I have signed a different one for five years and it reports in full and it does not expire, and the two of them came out of the same place, and the one I have is in a drawer because I did not want to see anything before the coal.”

He said, “And the rest is that you are the only person in ten years who has ever asked me for one.”

## The First Line On The Day’s Sheet

He took a sheet out of the rack at the ninth hour and squared it with the edge of the bench and wrote at the top of it, in a fair hand that has copied four hundred and eleven volumes, one line, and turned it round and put it where she could read it, which is not necessary and is not allowed and which he has done three times in five years.

*This is not an announcement. I have written announcements for twenty-two years and this is the first thing I have written in twenty-two years that is not going up a stair.*

She read it twice.

She said, “You are not going to send that anywhere.”

He said, “No. That is the point of it. Everything I have written for five years has gone up that stair on a clerk’s arm and into a book that shuts, and this is the first thing I have written in twenty-two years that stays in this room, and I have been waiting a year to have something worth that for.”

She said, “You have known of me since the eighth hour of the day I came up that stair.”

He said, “I have known a great deal of it since the eighth hour of the day you came up that stair and I have not asked you one question about any of it, and you have never once said so to me, and I have never once asked you to, and that is the arrangement and it has not moved in five years.”

She said none of it to him, and he did not want any of it.

She said, “What happens to the thing in the drawer.”

He said, “It is going to be open at the second hour of the eleventh of this month and it is not going to be shut again, and that is not a thing I am deciding in the night. I have had it in that drawer since the coal came and I have known every morning for a year that I was going to take it out before the end.”

“The reason I have waited this long is that a girl of nineteen came five days up a road with a bar, and I wanted to be asked.”

## Six Days Of An Unopened Drawer

She stayed in the tower for six days and she did not ask him one question in any of them.

That is harder than it sounds and she would not have believed it before she started. She has asked a question of every person she has met in ten years, forty or fifty available in any room, and there was one man in this tower with an instrument in a drawer six feet from her boot and she asked him not one question for six days, because a thing that is going to be opened on the eleventh must not be asked at on the fifth.

He worked. He copied the quarter’s bundle in a fair hand at the sixth hour and carried it down four flights himself, because he had asked to be suspended from the stair by his own written request a quarter at a time for five years and a quarter falls four times a year, and he came back up empty and went to work.

A boy of sixteen came up at the eighth hour and filled the lamp and copied two sheets in a hand that is not as good as his and did not talk, and he looked at her once, on the second day, and she said, “He knows I am here,” and the boy said, “He does not tell me anything. I have worked in this room two years and I have never once been told anything by him that I did not read off the sheet in front of me.”

She said, “That is a good way to be brought up.”

The boy said, “It is a quiet way.”

She slept on the floor of the copying room on the fifth, sixth and seventh nights with the bar under her head and her boots on, and she worked nothing here, because there is no work here for a girl with one arm that pays, and she went out at the ninth hour each evening and walked the four miles to the square and back because the four flights had got into her legs.

On the eighth she took her book out at the sixth hour on the floor beside the lamp and opened it to the fourth leaf and looked at it for a long time.

Five of them. The fourth, the fifth, the sixth, the seventh and the eighth, and they were blank and they had been blank since the twelfth of the month beginning 1,874.

She put the book away without putting anything on any of them, because a man had a drawer six feet from her boot and she was not going to write anything down a man who had not opened it yet, and because there is not one thing in this world she could write that would be worth as much as one line of his.

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the fourth of the month beginning 2,024 than she was at the seventh hour of it, which is four hundred and sixty-seven days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back.

And she was standing at the end of a bench in a tower with four hundred volumes behind a man who had written one line on a sheet and turned it round so that she could read it, and she had not asked him for anything and she was not going to, and a thing was going to be open in this room at the second hour of the eleventh of the month.

On the eleventh a man of sixty-seven took a cloth out of a drawer under a working rack and put it on the bench beside his brush, and it was not going to be shut again.
