# Carrying It East

She carried it out of the gate on the fifteenth of the month beginning 1,934 at the fifth hour with the bar across her right shoulder and her bag on her hip and her right arm open, and she walked east for three hours and came back at the eighth, and she has done it every day since and she is going to do it for the rest of her life.

That is the method and it is worth six coppers a day of nothing.

## What Came Out Of The Ground

On the sixteenth she put up thirty-one lengths of broken stone off the kiln floor in one day and it is the best day she has done on that ground and the man of fifty-one wrote it up on the board in a good hand at the sixth hour and hung the board where anybody could see it.

And at the seventh hour and forty minutes of the sixteenth, out in the yard, with the bar of her right shoulder under her bag and thirteen people inside the low houses and the furnace going over behind her, the little open place low behind her fourth rib went quiet and then did not.

There was no sound in it. There has never been any sound in it, and the sound goes into the ground, and she has been through this ten times.

It took ten seconds.

And after it she was exactly as tired as she had been before it and exactly as hungry as she had been before it, which she was not at all, and what it took came out of the ground under her feet and not out of her hands, and there was not one piece of stone in her hand and there had not been one piece of stone in her hand all day.

The man of fifty-one wrote thirty-one up on the board and she said it to him at the seventh hour and fifty minutes and he said, “That is the best day you have had here and it is not going to be the best one you have, and I have had thirty-one before.”

She said, “From a man.”

He said, “From a man.”

She said, “You have had thirty-one lengths and I have had thirty-one lengths and there is a difference in that and it is not the lengths.”

He said, “Say it.”

She said, “No.”

He looked at the board.

## What The Yard Did About It

Four people saw the ten seconds and not one of them said a word about it, and that is not the same as not one of them saw it.

The woman of forty-five came out of her door at the seventh hour and fifty minutes of the sixteenth and stood on her step in the last of the light and looked at where she was standing in the grit with her arm hanging, and she stayed on that step for four minutes.

Then she said, “There is a stage every so often.”

She said, “Not many people here have seen one and I have seen one and my husband saw two.”

She said, “Do you go down.”

She said, “Go down where.”

She said, “Never mind. It is not a thing to ask a person on a step.”

And she went in and shut the door and it was not a slam, and it is the only time in ninety-eight days that a person on that ground has said one word to her about her body, and she has not been able to think about it since without having to stop.

He said nothing about it at all, which she has also not been able to think about since without having to stop.

## The Problem With Two

She got it out at the gate that night with a stick in the grit, and it took her the better part of an hour, and it is a short sum and it does not come out.

One copy is a thing she is responsible for.

Two copies is a thing two people are responsible for, and one of the other people is a man of seventy-one who has not read his book back since the year it was written, and who does not know what any of it was for, and who is not going to find out from her.

And the trouble is in one sentence, which she said to the stones outside the gate at the ninth hour of the sixteenth of the month beginning 1,934:

“If those two ever disagree, I have got a problem about him that there is not one person alive I can put it to, because he is in my head and he cannot be asked and I will not know whether I asked.”

He said, “They will not disagree.”

She said, “You do not know that.”

He said, “I know it because I have never once been wrong, and you know it because you read the other book yourself with your own eyes on the seventh hour of the thirteenth of the month beginning 1,874, before you put it in your own hand, and you had it off the same page I had it off.”

She said, “We had it off the same page. That is not two sources. That is one page and two hands, and if the page is wrong then I have got two wrong copies and a woman of forty-five who wrote it and has looked at it four times.”

He said, “Then you have got three, and one of them is a woman who was in the room.”

She said, “I know.”

She said, “I have been arguing with a thing that cannot happen for two days and the reason I am doing it is that I have not got anywhere to put the argument, and that is what the asking was for.”

## Six Days Of Carrying It

She worked the days out. That is what she did with the eighteenth and nineteenth and the fifteenth and the seventeenth and the twentieth, and she did it with the stick in the grit outside the gate in the dark, and the figures are these.

A telling goes through a person and out in the length of four lengths of a kiln floor.

A true thing she is told she can say back and cannot say where she heard it.

A true thing she reads she can say back and cannot say when she read it, unless she read it herself, and she has read four pages with her own eyes in her life and two of them are about him.

A name in her own hand in a blanket book against her ribs holds.

A name in a good hand in a cloth book on a plank table at the north end of a furnace ground holds.

And there is no instrument in the world for holding the two of them, and there never has been, and she has been inventing one out of asking since the eighth of a month ten volumes back and it stopped working eight days ago.

On the twentieth she stood at the gate with the stick in her hand and the figure of the lighter in her head at three hundred and ninety-three days, and said it out loud.

“Knowing is heavier than asking. It is heavier because a asking has an end to it and a knowing does not, and because a thing you are told is a thing that happens to you and a thing you know is a thing you have to hold up.”

He said, “You have worked out why it is heavier.”

She said, “It is heavier because I am the only instrument on this ground and everywhere else. There is no second column.”

## East Of The Gate

She walked east on the fifteenth and on the eighteenth and on the twentieth, three hours out of the gate and back, and the track runs on the flat for a mile and then goes up on the rising ground, and not one thing on it, and the smoke of the furnace goes straight up behind her and is out of sight inside four hundred yards.

The third time out she carried it as far as the rise and sat down on the ground at the top of it with her back to the wind and did it all again, and this is the part she has kept.

She thought: what is the heaviest thing I am carrying.

And she went through it, in order, the way she has gone through everything since the eighth of a month ten volumes back, oldest first.

Nine coppers she cannot count. Nine feet nine of bar. A ring a finger’s width. A lump of clay. A stub of a nib and a cake of ink she opened on the seventh of the month beginning 1,874. A dish. A skin.

A bar that is not hers and a board that went down without her on the second of the month beginning 1,844.

A brother east of her on a barrow ground with a stone at the head of each. A woman of forty-five and her shut book. A man of fifty-one and his board. A boy of fifteen who calls figures. A man of seventy-one with a good hand. Nine paces by six of new stone with a gap in the north joint. Six thousand nine hundred and thirty-five days. One hundred and fifty-three thousand six hundred bricks a day. A morning she was not in.

And at the bottom of the list, under all of it:

His name, in two hands, nineteen years apart.

She said that out loud on the rise at the third hour of the twentieth of the month beginning 1,934 and then she got up and walked back in at the eighth hour with her arm open and did the fourth length of the day’s work.

## What She Cannot Set Down

The thing she found out at the top of the rise, and it is the last thing she found out on that ground, is that she had it backwards for ten years.

She thought the hard part of carrying a person was that they can be lost.

They cannot.

A name in a good hand in a cloth book on a plank table cannot be lost by anybody at all, not by fire and not by water and not by a girl of nineteen dying on a rise in the dark, and it was there before she was born and it will be there after, and the man who wrote it will not read it back and does not need to.

The hard part is that there is no putting it down.

She said it out loud on the rise, with the wind off the flat and the smoke behind her and four hours of road in any direction with nothing on it.

He said, “That is a strange thing to have found out.”

She said, “It is the strangest thing anybody has ever said to me and I have been counting since I was eight, and it took me eight days of not being able to ask a question and a walk out of a gate three times to get it, and I could have got it in a minute if I had ever once been in a room with two people who were carrying the same thing.”
