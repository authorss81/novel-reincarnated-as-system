# A Tower With A Light In It

She went out of the east gate at the seventh hour of the fourth of the month beginning 1,994 with the bar on her shoulder and her hands empty, and the ground she had been standing on for a hundred and forty days got smaller behind her at about four hundred yards, the way a thing does when there is not one thing in it taller than you.

She had told no one where she was going and she had not decided yet, and both of those were true at the same time on the two miles of flat with no cart on them.

The morning was cold enough that the four counts of breath came faster than she wanted them to and she let them come, because seven is for a rise and this was flat and there was no reason to pay for it.

## What She Went Out There For

That was the honest answer and she had it ready before she had gone a mile.

Not the ash heap. The heap was a mile and a half down the east track and it was nine paces by six and she had said out loud on the stones that it was not the same thing as the nine paces by six on the kiln floor, and she was not going to stand over it again.

She went past the turn-off for the track and kept going, and got to the place where the ground begins to rise, and stopped there, and stood.

“What am I doing.”

She said it out loud to two hundred yards of rising ground in the ordinary voice, and then she stood there for a minute and a half while a wind came off the flat and went through the lining of a coat that had been mended a dozen times.

She said, “I have a book of eight leaves with five of them blank and a stub of a nib and an open cake of ink, and I have a man in my head who cannot be given anything, and I have got one figure on a board in a fair hand that has been there for nineteen years, and neither one of those is mine and I cannot write either of them out.”

She said, “I have been counting things since I was a child and it is the only thing I have ever been able to do, and there is not one thing left in the world that I can count.”

## The Tower With A Light In It

The thing that arrived while she was standing there was ten years old and it had been in her head at the back of everything for all of that time, and it turned up the way a thing turns up when you have stopped holding it down.

A tower with a light in it.

A hauler at a pot had said it to her in a county four days of road from anywhere, five years back, and had not looked up while he said it, and had gone on eating. A light in a tower that comes on at the fourth hour and does not stop. A bundle going down a stair and a clerk’s arms going up four flights with it and coming down empty. A floor of nine men and the same nine and all of them still on it.

She had never seen that tower. She had walked past the foot of the post nine days west twice and the light had been off both times and she had not gone in.

And under all of it, older and hers and with a man in it, was a building four miles down a mountain with a lamp in a cage on the fourth landing that had burned from the sixth hour in autumn through a winter and out the other side.

And a copying floor with four hundred volumes on the shelves, and a keeper who had been sixty-two then and had a cough that had got worse every year for eleven years, and who had never once asked her a single question in fourteen months and had written down what she said as though it were a thing that had happened.

She said out loud to the rising ground, at the eighth hour and twenty minutes of the fourth of the month beginning 1,994:

“They are not the same building.”

She said, “I have never seen the first one and I am not going to walk nine days west to look at it. I am going to the second one, and the second one is five days west of the turn on foot and I have done it in four with nothing wrong.”

She said, “And I have not told a living person that I am going, and I am about to, and that is the part I have not got past.”

## What She Told The Man With The Board

She was back inside the line of stone at the ninth hour and she went straight to the long window and the man was still there with the cloth and the board on the nail behind him with the line on it that he had written at the fourth hour.

She said, “I am going west in the morning.”

He said, “How long.”

She said, “Nine days to the turn and five more after that, and I do not know what I am going to do when I get there and I have not worked it out, which is the first time in four months that I have said that of anything.”

He said, “Then say the other half of it.”

She said, “The other half is that I am going to a building with a register in it, because there is a man in it who copies out what he sees in a fair hand and has been doing it for twenty-two years and has never asked me one question and is dying, and I am going to ask him to look at the thing that is in my head, because there is no other instrument in the world that can do it.”

He said, “You could have said that in four words.”

She said, “I have wanted to say it in four words for four months and every time I got to four words I was talking to a wall at the second hour.”

He wrote nothing down.

That was the part she had not expected and it is the part she told him about afterwards. He wrote nothing down, because a board takes figures and a date and a line about a stage, and a girl going west for nine days is not a figure and it is not a date, and he is a man who writes what he is given and does not invent columns.

He said, “Then I will leave your figures up for a month.”

She said, “Why a month.”

He said, “Because a board with your name at the top of it and a bare space under it for a month is a lie, and I am not going to have your name on a board in this yard being a lie for longer than a month.”

She said, “You are going to rub them out.”

He said, “On the second of the next month, the same as everything, and I will not tell you when that is.”

## What The Boy Of Fifteen Said

She stopped at the stack on the way to the gate because the boy was there doing the last count of the day with a bit of chalk on the back of his hand, and he had brought her a second barrow out of the stack on three days out of four for twenty-one days in a month beginning 1,874 and had stopped, and had told her to her face that he was doing it and did not know what she thought he was doing.

He said, “You are going somewhere.”

She said, “West.”

He said, “How far west.”

She said, “Far enough that I will not be on this floor when the next board goes up.”

He put the chalk down and looked at the stone line and then at her.

“Will you be standing in front of a board with a fraction under it.”

She said, “I will be standing in front of one if there is one, and if there is not one then there will be, and you will be nineteen in four years and you will be on a floor.”

He said, “I have told you I will be on this floor at nineteen.”

She said, “Then you will be the only one of the two of us who knows what a fraction under a figure is.”

She did not say anything else to him and she did not put her hand on his head, because he is fifteen and she is nineteen and neither of them would have known what to do with it.

## The Sentence She Said In A Yard Yesterday

She went out to the gate at the ninth hour and twenty minutes of the fourth and she stood in the grit with the furnace going over behind her, and she said it out loud, standing, in daylight, to no one in particular and to the thirteen people who had heard her the day before.

She said, “I said in this yard on the third of this month that I was not going to walk out of this ground, and that I had walked out of four places in ten years and this was going to be the fifth place I did not walk out of.”

She said, “I am walking out of it in the morning. It is nine days and then five and I have not worked out what is at the end of it, and I said the sentence about the fifth place in front of thirteen of you and I am standing in the same yard telling you it did not hold.”

He said, “You are breaking a sentence you said out loud in front of people.”

She said, “I am breaking it in front of the same people. That is the only part of it I could not have done any other way.”

He said, “Say why. Not the long reason. The one that is true in a sentence.”

She said, “Because everything I know about him I worked out in my own head, and the head is nineteen years old and it is going, and there is one building left in this world where a person writes down what they see in a hand that is not theirs, and a man in it is dying and has never asked me for anything.”

She said, “And when I come back — or when I do not — there is going to be one line on the ninth stage on a board at the north end of this yard in a fair hand, and it is going to be the only thing in the world outside my own head that says any of this happened.”

She was lighter at the tenth hour of the fourth of the month beginning 1,994 than she was at the seventh hour of it, which is four hundred and thirty-seven days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back.

And she went and lay down on the stones outside the gate with the bar under her head and a pack on her back that had a bar of fire in the lining of it, and the furnace did not let out, and the smoke went straight up, and the day had half an hour left in it, and the ninth stage was in the last of it.
