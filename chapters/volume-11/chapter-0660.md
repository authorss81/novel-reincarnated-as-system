# A Board With Nothing Written On It

She walked the seventeen miles in the dark.

That is all of it and it is the last thing she does in this life that costs her anything that is not in her head. Four miles of made road off the rows, the two miles of flat between the gate and the wall, and the rest of it made road from the gate to the turn.

A wall with an opening standing open in it four feet and a bit, a fence on the north side that goes on for a mile and then stops, and a row of stones on the south side at intervals set up by somebody for a reason she has never worked out.

And nothing between the gate and the wall to lie down on, and no shed at the turn, and a bar nine feet nine on a shoulder that has had one arm open four days in five for ten years.

She was on the flat at the turn at the first hour of the sixth of the month beginning 2,054, in the dark, with the light coming grey off the east.

## The Woman Of Sixty

The woman of sixty was on that bench again.

She has been on that bench every morning of the nine months Ning Yan has been able to remember and she was on it at the first hour of a morning in the dark with a blanket over her shoulders and she did not get up.

She said, “You went east.”

She said, “I went east.”

The woman of sixty said, “You came back at the turn.”

She said, “I came back to the turn. The bar is four feet nine in the other direction and it is not a place.”

The woman of sixty said, “That is the first true thing anybody has said at this turn in nine years and it has taken a girl two hundred yards and two days to say it.”

And then she said, “You have got two coppers in that bag.”

She said, “I have got two coppers in that bag.”

The woman of sixty said, “I am not going to give you a third and you are not going to ask me for one and I have said that out loud so that neither of us has to do the other thing at the third hour on a morning.”

She did not ask.

## Nothing On Two Posts

The board is a bar on two posts four hundred yards east of the turn and it goes up on the first of a month and it came up on the first with nothing written on it and it has gone up on the first of every month since with nothing on it.

She stood in front of it at the second hour of the sixth of the month beginning 2,054, with the light coming up behind the store road and her breath going white, and there was nothing on it.

That is all of it and it is the oldest thing on that road and it was there before her and it will be there after.

She had stood a long stretch of days in that yard and had gone west off it on the twenty-ninth of the month beginning 1,814, and had forfeited a board that came back up the track on a cart with eight coppers in a cloth on it, and she has read boards since she was twelve and it is the only thing anybody has ever paid her for.

And there is no board anywhere in nine days of road with her name on it, and there is not one person in this world who is writing her hours down in a fair hand.

## What She Was Carrying

She got the list out in her own head at the third hour, standing in the grit at the foot of the two posts, because she counts and because it is what she does in the first hour of a morning.

A bar nine feet nine. A skin. A bag with a dish and a lump of clay and a ring a finger’s width and a stub of a nib and a cake of ink she opened on the seventh of the month beginning 1,874. Two coppers she cannot count.

A book of eight leaves against her ribs. A date and a place on the first. Four sentences on the second that she said once into the air when she was a girl and has never repeated. YUAN SHENG on the third, four capital strokes in her own hand, and five blank ones after it, and they are blank.

And four sheets of rice paper in a fair hand, folded four times, against her ribs on the side where the book is, from a clerk of sixty-seven with a cough that had got worse every year for nineteen years, who is three feet from the back wall of a tower at Lowwater in the ground since the fourth hour of the twenty-second of the month beginning 2,024, who never asked her one question in seven years and who was the only person in the world who ever believed her.

She said, out loud, standing, in daylight, to a board with nothing written on it:

“I have not got a name on a board and I have not got wages and I have not got a shed at the turn and I have got a man’s handwriting in my coat and a brother thirteen miles east of this turn who walked west past me at dusk nine years ago and has not walked since, and I have got a piece of a thing in my head that a clerk would not write the name of, and I am nineteen.”

And she was lighter at the tenth hour of the sixth of the month beginning 2,054 than she was at the fifth hour of it, which is four hundred and ninety-nine days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back.

## The One Thing Nobody Can Point At

She looked up at the sixth hour.

There is a ceiling over a person for as long as there is a wall and a board and a figure on the board and a house that puts them there.

And she had been under one at every hour of her life from the age of ten until the seventh of a month beginning 1,934, and it had been taken down in stages, three at a time, and the last of it had been taken down on the fourth of the month beginning 1,994 at the tenth hour with a fair hand writing a line on it on a board at a furnace ground sixty-five days east of here.

And there is nothing over her now.

Not a thin thing. Not a limit. Not one thing that anybody can point at, and nothing that has ever been named to her by anything at all, and she stood on a store road at the sixth hour of the sixth of a month with her head back and her eyes open and waited a minute and a half for something to announce itself, the way she has waited nine times in nine years at the sixth hour, and there was nothing.

She said, out loud, on a road, at the sixth hour, standing, in daylight:

“There is not one thing in the sky over this road that has ever been announced.”

## A Day And A Night On A Store Road

She stayed on the turn through the whole of the sixth and through the night.

There is no shed at the turn and there has never been a shed at the turn.

And she had said in a yard on a furnace ground that she was not going to walk out of the fifth place in ten years, and had then walked out of it inside ten days, and a woman of nineteen who has said a thing twice does not get to say it a third time by sitting down on a bar with nothing on it.

The woman of sixty was on the bench at the fourth hour and at the ninth hour and at the second hour of the morning and did not speak to her, and that is the correct way for a woman of sixty on a bench at a turn to treat a girl with a bar who has been there two days.

Four men came west in the day and none of them looked at the two posts, and a cart went by at the seventh hour with stone in it, which is the only cart that has gone east in a year, and she watched it go and did not ask anybody anything.

She slept four hours on the stones by the trough with the bar under her head and woke at the second hour and lay there until the light came, and the light came up over the east off the same road she had come in on.

## What She Said On A Store Road

And then she said the thing, once, out loud, standing, in daylight, at the sixth hour of the seventh of the month beginning 2,054, on a store road with a bar on two posts four hundred yards behind her and the bench empty in front of her and no other person inside a hundred yards.

She said, “I am going up.”

He said, “Say the rest of it.”

She said, “I have not got the rest of it.”

He said, “Say what you have got.”

She said, “I have got a piece of a thing with a name that a clerk would not write down, that was burned out of a fire nineteen years ago and has not spoken once since, and I have got forty-one strokes in the margin of a dead man’s sheet that neither of us knows what they are.”

“I have got a name in two hands that do not need me, and I have got a brother who has carried stones for four men for nine years for a fifth of a day he found out five years ago and told no one.”

“And I have got nothing else in the world.”

She said, “And the rest of it is a road up and I do not know what is on it and no one in nine days of road has ever been up it, and there is not a ceiling to go through, because the last of the ceiling went into the ground on the fourth of this month sixty-three days ago in front of thirteen people, and the thing that is left above my head is the sky, and I have never read the sky in my life.”

He said, “That is the first thing you have said in five hundred days that is not a figure.”

She said, “It is.”

He said, “Say one more thing and then I am going to let you have the road.”

She said, “I am not going up because he is up there. I do not know whether he is up there. I am going up because a thing that was made to find people like me has been counting them for nineteen years and no one in the world has written down what it is for, and a clerk in a tower wrote down in a fair hand that it is not for the ground.”

“And that sheet is against my ribs and there is no one else alive who has read it.”

She said, “And if it turns out there is no road up there then I will come back down and I will have been the only person who went, and that is a thing I can pay for.”

He said, “You cannot pay for that with two coppers.”

She said, “No. I am going to pay for it with the next one.”

He said, “Say the last of it.”

She said, “I have asked him what he was.”

And she stood at the turn on the store road at dawn with the board on two posts behind her and nothing written on it, and two coppers she cannot count in the front of a bag, and a bar nine feet nine across her shoulders, and four sheets of a fair hand against her ribs.

At nineteen years old, nine stages past a ceiling that no longer exists, with a fair hand against her ribs and five blank leaves against her ribs and a sky over all of it that has never once been announced to anybody in this world.

She did not say what was going to answer.

The sky did not.

He was still talking.
