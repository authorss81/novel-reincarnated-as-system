# She Did Not Go In

She was at the gate at the seventh hour of the eighth of the month beginning 1,844, and the gate was a bar on two posts and a chain across it and a stool, and there was a man of sixty sitting on the stool with his feet in the dust and a bowl beside him.

He lifted the chain for her without being asked and without getting up.

She said, “East.”

He said, “I know which way that is.”

She said, “Two days on the ground that goes up.”

He looked at her then, properly, and at the bar, and then at the road behind her that she had come the length of.

“How far have you walked.”

“Two days.”

He said, “Nobody goes east.”

## Two Miles With Nothing On Them

She went through the gate at the seventh hour and twenty minutes and the wall was two miles on and there is nothing between the two of them to lie down on.

That is the whole of what is on those two miles and it has been the whole of it every time she has walked it, and she has walked it twice, and both times at dusk, and this is the third.

A fence on the north side that goes on for a mile and then stops. A flat of stone that has no grass on it because the carts turn there and there are no carts. A row of stones on the south side at intervals, set up by somebody, for a reason she has never worked out and has stopped trying to.

And no shed, and no tree, and nothing that would hold a person off the ground for six hours.

She took the two miles at a walk with the bar on her left shoulder and the light going, and she said the name out loud four times on them.

“Yuan Sheng.”

She said it at the half-mile, at the mile, at the mile and a half, and at the wall. The road took it and the road kept nothing, and every one of the four came back perfectly when she turned her head and repeated it, with the same two syllables in the same order, which is the part she has not got past.

She was not going to be told anything about him that stayed. She had worked that out standing in the road on the fifth and she had a woman’s basket going west behind her and a shut book on a shelf she had not seen.

And she walked the two miles anyway.

That is the thing about the road east of the gate. You can be told a direction and you can take the direction or leave it, and what you cannot do is be *given* one. A direction is not a fact about you. It is a fact about the ground, and the ground is still there at the sixth hour of the eighth of a month, and she had eight days of boots on it behind her and one more in front of her.

He said, “You are walking on a woman’s sentence.”

“I am walking on the made road and on two miles of stone.”

“You are walking on a woman’s sentence and you know it is one and you have taken it anyway, and I want you to notice what you have just done, because it is the first time you have taken anything out of a mouth without a count on it.”

She said, “It is.”

## Through The Opening

The wall came up out of the last of the light and the opening in it is standing open, four feet and a bit in, and she has been through it twice.

Nothing between the gate and the wall to lie down on, and nothing behind the wall either. That is what the flat is. It is flat and it is a quarter of a mile and the ground at the far side of it goes up, and there is a rut in wet ground coming out from under the wall, and she has not found it in her head since a month beginning 1,754 and she has not gone looking.

She went through the opening at the ninth hour of the eighth of the month beginning 1,844 and stood on the far side of it in the dark with the bar up.

The air on this side is different and she had not noticed it at dusk on either of the other two times because she had been in a hurry both times and she is not in a hurry tonight.

## The Rows

She saw them at the ninth hour and forty minutes of the ninth of the month beginning 1,844, on ground that is going up gently, and she stopped walking and stood still on the crest of it for a quarter of an hour.

Rows. A stone at the head of each. Between two hundred and three hundred yards from where she stood, along the slope and into the dip past it.

She counted the rows, because she counts, and because there was nothing else in her to do with her hands, and she got sixty-one before the light went and she stopped and did not start again.

There was one figure moving in it at that hour and it was going along a row and not down one, and it stopped when it saw her, and waited until she had gone, and she went.

She did not go in.

She wants that written down in the plainest possible words, because it is the second time and she is not going to have it softened in her own head later. She stood on the crest of a rise a quarter of a mile from two hundred people and she wanted to go down into it more than she has wanted anything since a boy said *you are shorter* to her in a ground she had walked into on her own legs.

And she did not, and the reason was not duty and was not kindness and was not the year.

The reason was that she had come nine days east with one copper she cannot count for one thing, and the one thing was two syllables that do not stay, and the only instrument in the world that would hold them was a stub of a nib and a cake of ink she has not opened in two months, in her own hand, while they were being said.

She was not going to sit down in a ground of two hundred people and do that. Not here. Not with a brother nine years in that ground who has hands like their father’s and who said five sentences to her on a store road and has not said a sixth.

She did not call out.

She did not say his name.

She stood on the crest and she said his name out loud exactly once, to herself, in the ordinary voice, and then she said the two syllables after it, and they were both there, both perfect, and neither one of them is why she did not go in.

## Whether It Is A Remembering

She got up on the tenth at the second hour and she did the one thing she had come east to be able to do, which was to find out what a name is when you cannot put it anywhere.

She said it to the stones in the dark.

“Yuan Sheng.”

She waited. She said it again.

“Yuan Sheng.”

She waited longer, sitting on the ground with the bar across her knees, and she said it four more times over the length of a quarter of an hour, spread out, the way you would test a thing you were not sure of.

It came back every time. Word for word. Two syllables, the first of them short and the second one falling.

That was the finding and she sat with it in the cold for a while, because it is worse than losing it, and losing it would at least have been an ordinary failure.

She is not forgetting anything. Her head is exactly as good as it was at the turn, and it was good at the turn, and it held two hundred and forty courses a day at six hundred and forty to a course and three questions standing in a shut book and nineteen stones in the third row of a wall by a trough.

It holds the name perfectly.

And there is no difference at all between her and the road. A road also holds what is said to it perfectly. A road gives it back to you exactly, in the same order, as many times as you ask, and it has never once had it.

She said out loud, to the stones, at the second hour of the tenth of the month beginning 1,844:

“I have got him and I have not got him, and those are the same thing, and it is going to be the same thing until I put it down in ink in my own hand.”

He said, “That is the first sentence about this you have said that is not a complaint.”

She said, “It is not a complaint. It is a bill.”

## The Ground That Goes Up

She camped on the rise on the tenth of the month beginning 1,844, which is a day she has never in her life had to name because she has never once been this far east of the turn with nothing to go back to.

She lay on stones with the bar under her head and she got four hours of it, which is what she gets.

She was lighter at the seventh hour of the tenth than she was at the seventh hour of the ninth, and that is two hundred and ninety-three days of it, counting the eighteenth of the month beginning 1,544 as the first, and not one of them going back.

He said, “You did the right thing.”

She said, “I did a small thing and it cost me a great deal and I would like it recorded in those words and not the other ones.”

He said, “It is recorded in those words.”

She said, “Do not thank me for it.”

He said, “I have not and I am not going to.”

## The Air Changes

She saw it at the seventh hour of the twelfth of the month beginning 1,844, coming down off a rise with the bar on her shoulder and nothing in the bag but the heel and the copper.

The air changes.

That is how she knew. There is stone dust in it and there is a heat in it that has not been in the air on this ground since she was ten years old and standing in a lime bed, and there is a smell coming up off the low ground of something burning that is not wood.

Below her and a mile off there is a low building and a yard and a wall round the yard that is not a wall anybody builds to keep anybody out, and a line of barrows standing nose up against the inside of it.

And a furnace, with a smoke coming off it.

He said, “You have said in daylight that you are going to find out what a fire is on foot before you spend a morning asking what I was.”

She said, “On the twenty-fifth of the month beginning 1,814. Nine days ago.”

He said, “Nine days.”

She stood on the rise above it with the air going hot on her face and she could smell it from a mile out, and she had a bar on her shoulder and one copper she cannot count and a heel of loaf and a name that would not stay in her head.

She had walked nine days on a woman’s sentence and on the made road and on two miles of stone with nothing on them to lie down on, and she was not going to be told what any of it was for.

She went down the rise.
