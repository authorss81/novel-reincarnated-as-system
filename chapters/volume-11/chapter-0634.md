# Nine Paces By Six

She went at the seventh hour of the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 1,904 and she stood in the open at twenty feet from a bench in front of a low house at the north end of the yard for about two minutes before she went any closer, because a man of fifty-one had told her to and because it was the first useful thing anybody had told her in seventy-two days that was not a figure.

The bench was a plank on two stones. The low house had a door on the north side and an opening on the south with no door in it and a curtain of hide across it, and there were nine or ten hours of the day in it and a man of seventy-one sitting on the plank with his feet on the grit and his hands on his knees.

He watched her come the last ten feet. He did not get up.

## What She Asked

She said, “Where on this ground did it happen.”

One question. In daylight. Standing. At the seventh hour and five minutes of the twenty-sixth, in the open, ten feet from a bench.

He said, “Say it again with the thing in it.”

She said, “Where on this ground did a boy of nineteen get taken at the sixth hour nineteen years ago for a senior who had been at a work on himself nine years.”

He sat on the plank for a while.

Then he said, “You have got that off a page.”

She said, “I have got that off a page and a woman’s mouth and forty-nine days of walking and I have got it in my own hand on a leaf of a blanket book against my ribs and it has held for forty-four days and the page has not got one hour of it in it and not one place.”

He said, “Say why you are asking me and not her.”

She said, “Because she keeps her husband’s book and she wrote it true and she is right not to open it, and you keep the ground’s, and a ground’s book has to say where, because a ground is a place and a place is not a place until somebody says which part of it.”

He said, “No. But it is close, and you have said it out loud to a person and it has not gone through you and out, which means you did not get it off a page, which means you worked it out yourself, and that is the first thing about you I have believed since you came on this ground.”

## The Book He Will Not Open

She said, “Will you open it.”

He said, “No.”

She said, “Why not.”

He said, “Because it has been shut nineteen years and the last person who opened it was the woman, and she opened it once and closed it and told you no in a doorway and she was right, and I am not going to be a second person telling a girl the same thing for the same reason.”

She said, “Then why did you come out of that house.”

He said, “You were stood in my yard at twenty feet at the seventh hour and you had a bar on your shoulder and a bag on your hip and you were going to come and ask me and I have been asked by four people in nineteen years and I would rather be asked than have it be somebody else’s.”

He got up off the plank, which took him a while, and he came out through the opening with no door in it and he crossed the grit toward the kiln floor and did not look back to see whether she was coming.

She was coming.

## Where The Floor Lets You In

He stopped at the west end of the kiln floor and put one hand down on a course of stone.

He said, “Do you know how a man gets on to this floor.”

She said, “No.”

He said, “You cannot get on to it from the west. That is the stack side and there is a nine-foot drop and no lip on it and no man has ever walked in off the west in nineteen years and not one of them has tried twice. You come off the east, off the yard, where the slope is, four paces down onto the second course and then forward nine paces along the north side to here.”

She looked at it. The whole floor is worked from the east. Every barrow of broken stone goes up the east slope and along the north side, and the boy of fifteen walks it forty times a day and has never once had a choice about it.

She said, “So it is one way.”

He said, “It is one way, and it has been one way since before I came to it, and a man standing on this floor has his back to the stack and his face to the yard, and he cannot be got at from anywhere except along that run.”

She said, “And that is why the page does not say where.”

He said, “That is why the page does not say where, and I will tell you the other half of it, because you are going to work it out and I would rather you had it right. The page does not say where because anybody who has stood on this ground has got it in their legs. It is not an omission. It is a thing that was left out on purpose by a person who assumed the person reading it was standing where I am standing.”

## What The Patch Is Made Of

It was nine paces by six.

That is what it is. That is the finding, and she got it twice going round it and a third time with the boy of fifteen, who came over at the seventh hour and a half because the boy comes over at the seventh hour and a half, and who said, “That is not our stone.”

She said, “I know it is not our stone.”

The boy said, “Ours is grey out of the pit. That is the stone off the bank at the east and it has been laid since I got here.”

She said, “Since when.”

The boy said, “I do not know. It was laid when I came.”

The man of seventy-one said, “It was laid in the seven days after the morning.”

She said, “You wrote that down.”

He said, “I wrote everything down. There is a day on it and there is a figure on it and there is the amount of stone, and it is in the book, and I am not going to open the book, and you can come back for it when you have a reason and not before.”

She said, “You said no and then you gave me the place.”

He said, “I said no to the book and yes to the floor. They are two different things and the floor is not in the book.”

## The Only Way On

She stood on it at the eighth hour of the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 1,904 with four courses of new stone under her boots and a woman of forty-five’s shut book twenty feet away in a low house and a man of seventy-one standing at the edge of it not telling her anything.

And she got the thing about the asking.

It went at once. Not the answer — the answer was nine paces by six and it was true and she would have it tomorrow. The asking went. She reached for the question she had put at the seventh hour and five minutes and there was no handle on it, no sense of having opened her mouth in the open in front of a man on a bench with the light on her back.

She had been handed a place. It had cost her nothing. She had asked a person a question and got a true thing and put it on the ground and it had not held her at all.

And the reason it had not held her was that holding was never what asking did. Holding was the knowing. She had always known that. She had worked it out on a stone wall on the third of the month beginning 1,904 and written it in the ash, and she had gone on using asking as though it were a second way in, and it was not, and it stopped being anything at all eight days ago.

She stood on the four courses of new stone in the eighth hour and said it out loud to the man of seventy-one, in the ordinary voice.

“I asked you one question eight minutes ago and I cannot prove I asked it, and I have got your answer, and I would have got your answer if I had asked it a second time or if you had come out of that house and put it in my head without my asking, and the asking was never the thing that held anything.”

He said, “It is not.”

She said, “Then what is a person for.”

He said, “A person is for the thing you cannot do yourself. You counted that floor yourself. You could have found that patch in a morning. I have been sitting on that plank for nineteen years and I could not have told you which nine paces it was, because I only ever see it the one way and it is the way you cannot walk.”

He said, “I have been able to tell you that since you got here.”

She got down off it on the east side, off the run, the way a person comes off a floor.

And then she knelt down again on the north edge of it and put two fingers into the joint between the third course and the fourth, where the stone from the bank at the east meets the stone out of the pit, and the two of them do not fit. There is a gap about the width of a nail in the length of the north edge, and it is not wear, because the rest of the joint is tight as a hand laid.

Somebody cut that stone for it. Somebody wanted that patch to be exactly nine paces by six and put four courses down where the floor was taken up, and the man who did it is not a man who works this floor, because a man who works this floor lays a barrow’s width and takes what the barrow gives him.

She said, out loud, kneeling on a kiln floor on a furnace ground at the eighth hour and twenty minutes of the twenty-sixth of the month beginning 1,904:

“There was exactly one place on this ground to stand and that is why a page written by a person who knew did not have to say.”

And she stayed on it until the ninth hour, and then she came off it and went and did the last length of the day’s work with her right arm open.

She did not write it down anywhere, and on the first of the month beginning 1,934 she was still not writing it down, and on the fifth she walked over to look at the north joint again and then walked away from it, and the question that was standing in the yard with her from the eighth hour was not the one she had gone out to ask.
