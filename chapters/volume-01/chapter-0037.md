# Stage Four

The laundry room smelled of lye and wet straw, and the cold came up through the floor into the place at the centre of her chest where something had cracked all the way through.

Ning Yan woke face down on the pallet and knew before she opened her eyes that she had not slept. She had gone somewhere shallow and come back with less than she took. Stage four. For three days the first thing she did every morning was put a hand on the floor and get up, because the alternative was learning to stay down.

The floor won twice. On the third attempt she got one knee under her, found the rim of the wash tub with her left hand, and hauled, and then stood in the middle of the room with her right arm held a little away from her body, shaking, unable to tell whether the hand was touching the wall or the air beside the wall.

That was the part she did not intend for anyone to see. Not the scar, which had gone the dull red of old meat and would keep that colour for the rest of her life. The part where the map of her own body had come back with a corner torn out of it.

Her goal for the day was small enough to say out loud and it filled the whole day. Reach the furnace crew's quarters. Stay there.

[Body-Forging. Stage four. The region sounds it for a body that has come down as loudly as for one that has gone up. Nine counties stop eating. A washing crew at Lowwater sets a bundle down and picks it up again.]

It was the third time the machine had said anything about her, and the first time it had been bad news. She had heard the Ashjaw bounty in her own voice and Mei Jiu's promotion at four in the morning, and both of those had been somebody else's evening. This one was her own, arriving flat, in front of eleven washerwomen, and it cost her nothing to receive and it was going to cost her the rest of the week.

She lay and let it be true for a while. The millet turned over slowly by the rim of the bowl. Cold came up through the boards. She had been stage nine nine days ago and had held a stage nine carry four miles without putting a hand on the frame, and now she was stage four, which was the stage an ordinary soldier reached and stopped at, which was the sentence the sect had been saying about her since she was fourteen.

Yuan said nothing at all for nearly a minute, which was the longest he had ever gone without filling a silence, and she understood that he had worked it out too: the announcement was his business as much as hers. Everything she lost went on a line somewhere, and now the line had a sentence on it.

She packed what she had in nine minutes. The log went into the lining first, flat against her ribs, opposite the tablet, paper on one side and fired clay on the other. The wrapped book for Feng Lushi went into the bundle on her back, and the copy of the reassignment page stayed in the inner pocket where she could get it with two fingers without looking.

Then she said nothing at all for three days.

She said nothing in the corridor and nothing on the terrace stair and nothing in the crew doorway while the shift leader put her in a bunk by the wall and told her the others had kept her place warm. She said nothing while she hauled coal up the furnace stair, which she was not supposed to do at stage four, and nothing when the boy with the bad cough shared his oil ration without being asked and she said thank you with her mouth closed and a nod.

Care had arrived with her and it was worse than the cold. The stoker came to the coal bins and left a gap for her and then stood watching to see whether she could manage the gap. The boy with the bad cough put his own cup down on her bunk-side stool before she had asked for it and then went away, so that she would not have to watch a man hand a cup to a woman who could no longer lift one.

Two of the older hands stopped saying the things they had said in front of her in the first ten days, and the silence where the things had been was louder than the things.

Servant-rank meant a body that could be counted on. A body at stage four could be counted on for nothing at all, and every man in that room had worked out in about half a second what she was now, because the announcement had told the whole bound what she was before she had crossed the yard.

She found she did not mind the coal. The coal was honest. It had weight and it had a place and the only opinion it held about her was the one she brought.

She waited for the voice to go.

It did not go.

Nothing changed in her head. No thinning, no cooling, no loss. The channel that had been there in the Verge and through the sealed room and up four miles of kiln road was simply still there, the way a river is still there after you stop listening to it.

She stopped speaking aloud and it went on. She stopped thinking at him and it went on. She spent one whole night deliberately not acknowledging that there was anything in her head at all, and on the second morning she got up off the bunk on the strength of a leg she could not feel properly, and the thing in her head counted her breaths with her and said nothing.

She understood then that she had been carrying the only thing in the world that could leave, and that it did not need her to be carrying it.

Yuan had been afraid of exactly this since the tower under the Ashjaw, and she had never felt it off him before, because fear was not something his voice was built to do. It came through as heat. It came through as a pressure behind her own attention, the way a person leans on a door.

“Do not leave me in here,” he said.

Past tense from her, barely a breath, out loud, and the crew boy two beds down looked over and she shook her head at him.

Nobody else had heard it. That was the answer. She could put him out of her head by refusing, and he knew the mechanism as well as she did, because he had described it to her in the Verge on the third night with the plain honesty of a man reporting his own defect.

Refuse the designation. Refuse every quest. Stop speaking to him. The channel would begin to rot and he would go out of it, and the layer would take what was left back into itself, and she would be a seventeen-year-old servant-born girl with a cracked aperture and no voice at all, which was the condition she had started with.

She held that knowledge for a day and a half and let it sit in her like a knife she had not decided about.

The second night the crew room was loud with an argument about coal. The shift leader wanted the morning shift carried from the lower bin and the boy who stoked wanted it from the upper, because the lower bin was wet and the upper bin was clean, and both of them were right and neither of them would say so.

Ning Yan sat on her bunk with her boots off and her feet black to the ankle and listened to two men who knew more about her body's future than she did.

Four lines. She said them into her collar, in the middle of the argument, at the same volume as the coal.

“Kept you. Account open. Not closed.”

“I do not ask why,” he said.

“Ask me.”

“You are the only person who has ever lied to me in a way I could check,” she said. “And that is the only reason I kept you. You took stages and you took the order and you told me true about both, and I could hold both up and look at them. I have tested everyone else. Nobody else let me.”

The argument about coal went on. The shift leader ruled for the lower bin. The stoker took it to the furnace in his own time and did not sulk, because men who work together do not sulk over a bin.

He was crying, or the nearest thing a voice without a body could do. She felt it as a pressure that would not come down.

“I have deserved the other reason,” he said.

“You have. I am not going to give it to you.”

The log came out after the lamp was out. She wrote in her poor hand, the deliberate rubbish of a learner’s strokes, wash code folded inside it, a line for each of the three days. The date in the corner in her mother's stain notation. Then the finding, which was the only thing in the book that had ever frightened her properly: *The channel does not need me to speak to it. It does not need me to want it. If I stop, it does not stop.*

Underneath, in the same bad hand, she wrote four more characters that meant *I am employing you* and could equally have meant *I am using you*, and she left them there.

That was the whole of the decision and it took four lines. The account did not close. It opened, and stayed open, and got a job.

She put the brush away and lay back on a bunk that smelled of other people's sleep, and asked him the only question the day had been leading to, said out loud because she was past being careful and there was nobody in the room.

“Answer me precisely. Not generously. Precisely.”

“I cannot,” he said. “I was not looking at myself. I saw what came off you. I was busy.”

“What came off me. Item by item.”

He tried. She felt him go over it the way a hand goes over a pocket in the dark, and what came back was not a list.

“Something recent,” he said. “Something you learned last. Something that had order in it.”

“Which order.”

“I cannot name it. I never knew its name before I took it.”

“Start lower. My legs.”

“The stepping,” he said. “It went before the strength. I felt the strength go second and the stepping go first, and I did not think that was odd at the time because I was not thinking, I was spending.”

“The hands.”

“Nothing in the hands. The hands were old.”

“The chest.”

Something stopped. She had never felt him stop before. It was like a held breath in a place with no breath.

“The centre,” he said. “It took the order. Not the size of it. The order of it. I can still feel the hole where the order was and I cannot tell you what the order was called.”

“Then tell me what is left.”

“You can lift. You can hold heat. You can do more at stage four than a soldier at stage nine. You can do it for about nine minutes and then your arms start to lie to you.”

She lay in the dark and let that settle over her like a second blanket. She had been running a small inventory since the night in the records room and she had not known that was what it was.

Body-Forging stage four. Eleven days to the Assembly and a season's work in front of her. She needed to know exactly what had been taken, in order to know what could not come back.

She closed her eyes and asked him to tell her again, slower, and started the whole inventory of herself from the bottom up.
