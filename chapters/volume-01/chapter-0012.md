# The Reading of the Ledger

The outer yard on the eighth morning was full.

Ning Yan stood in the ranks of the returned with her hands folded and her scar bound tight under clean robes, stage six steady in her chest, Feng Lushi's copy sewn inside her sleeve. A hundred outer disciples packed the snow-trampled square below the Nine Steps Terrace. Above them the Elder Hall's canopy stood empty in the cold sun, furnace smoke tasting of iron at the back of every throat.

She had one goal, small and whole. Stand in the yard. Hear her own quarter-token settled. Be counted as a person who came back.

The Ledger Reader climbed the dais with the winter book in his arms, a bound volume of rice paper in Feng Lushi's fair hand and nineteen others like him, copied upward from every tower. The Bureau's seal hung from its spine. The Reader was a fat man with a kind voice, and he read the way rain fell, steady and without interest.

“Quota twenty-eight, Qinghe Sect, winter of the Low Water. Harvests received twenty-eight. Hands received—”

Names followed, rank after rank, credits and debits, beasts filed and men substituted. Du Hong's name came with the Ashjaw jaw, four hands whole, and he flushed crimson in the ranks while boys clapped his shoulders. Pei Lan's name came as hurt, carried whole, and a murmur went around. Han Shu and Lu Mei were read as lost, and their mothers, somewhere below in Lowwater, would hear it by nightfall.

Then the Reader turned a page, and his voice changed a fraction.

“Furnace column. Unsettled accounts carried forward.”

Ning Yan's spine went cold before her name came, the way animals knew weather.

“Ning Bo, furnace-man, taken for a stalled ascension six years past. Account unsettled. Balance carried to the nearest unspent thing.” A pause. Paper rustled. “Ning Yan, outer disciple, third rank. Credit held.”

Silence in the yard. A hundred heads turned.

Her name was in the furnace column. Not as a person's entry, as a credit. Her name had been sitting in Qinghe's furnace books for six years as an unsettled account, and the reading this morning was the moment it got noticed publicly by the wrong people.

Death by harvest left an account. It followed the family three generations unless someone with authority wrote it off. Her father's death had been running through the sect's books under her name because she was the nearest unspent thing. She had spent her whole life trying not to be in that column, and the column had held her all along.

Mei Jiu, fourth-rank inner, standing with the inner girls at the terrace edge, looked at her with sudden sharp interest. Lu Fen smiled. Shen Xiu stood by the gate post with his face unreadable, his list-board closed.

The Reader closed the book. “Quarter-token of Ning Yan, settled by proxy of Du Hong's filing and sheath bounty. Person counted as whole.”

She was whole. She was a credit. Both at once.

She did not speak. She stepped forward, took the token with both hands, bowed to the dais, and walked back into the ranks with her legs steady under her, though the scar burned and stage six went thin as paper all at once. She stopped wanting a document that said her brother was dead and started wanting one that said what actually happened to him. That was the change, cold and complete, and it would carry her further than any quota.

After the reading broke, Du Hong found her by the wall, furious on her behalf. “You were used,” he whispered. “Six years as a credit and no one told you—”

She shook her head. “I am going to find out what a furnace column is for,” she said. “Not the word. The thing.”

The voice spoke, low, in the noise of the dispersing yard.

“I hear your name in their book. I am angry. I stay.”

She touched the copy in her sleeve through the cloth. Her account was now a public object. The run was survived. The thing waiting at home was a ledger line.

She went to find out what the furnace column was for, and the road she saw led to a kiln nobody was supposed to enter, where a tower plate bore a date after a file was closed, and where her brother's name waited in ink that someone had to look at.
