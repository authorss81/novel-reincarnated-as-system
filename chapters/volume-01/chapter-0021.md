# What They Take

The procedure wall was written for somebody who already agreed with it.

Ning Yan stood before it in the dark assessment room with her copy drying inside her coat and her hands still shaking, and read by fingertip and by the last grey from the doorway. Bureau hand, fair and small, lines of instruction for the kiln crew. Intake. Assessment. Firing. Filing. The language of housekeeping, each step numbered, each tool named.

She wanted to understand what was written. Resistance was the writing itself, which made it very hard to read as anything other than routine.

*Stalled disciple to be opened. Useful part to be moved into failing one. Both to be filed under same number.*

She read it twice. Then a third time, slower, because her mind refused the plain meaning.

The kiln was not a place where people were killed. It was a place where cultivators were manufactured. A stalled disciple was opened, the useful part moved into a failing one, and both filed under the same number. The furnace was a supply chain, and everyone in it was a servant. Core-stone fired with qi. Qi fired with people. The assessed who were not processed were not freed. They were material.

Disgust came up cold. She let it come. Disgust was a cold, useful thing to have. It steadied her hands better than hope.

On the work table lay a single fired plate apart from the shelves, as if left for weighing and never filed. She lifted it.

It was the size of a hand. Its surface was like skin, warm from the room despite the cold, smooth with faint pores, giving slightly under her thumb. She had never touched anything like it. Clay should have been hard. This yielded, then returned.

She knew without being told what it was. A tablet made of the assessed. Fired qi holding shape. The kiln’s output, partly made out of people it described.

Her stomach turned. She set it down. Picked it up again. The texture stayed with her, terrible and precise, the grain of a palm, the warmth of a wrist.

She had forty minutes before the seal’s marker reported her weight. Wen warned her. The layer logged removals. She understood that now with her body, the way she understood stage and scar. Stay too long with weight that was inside, and the room noticed.

She looked at the shelves of children in clay. At the procedure board. At the plate in her hands, which described somebody the way her brother was described, assessed and filed.

She decided.

She said it out loud to the empty room, which was the first time she talked to a building.

“I take this.”

Past tense, flat, final. An irreversible decision, made in a sealed room, with nobody listening. She wrapped the tablet in cloth and put it inside her coat against her ribs, where Feng’s copies lay. It sat warm through the cloth, wrong and alive.

The room did not answer. Dust settled. The doorway showed black now, night full outside.

She turned with the tablet against her skin and walked the eleven steps back through the passage, alone, uncounted, carrying the first thing out of a sealed place in sixteen years.

Her breath came seventeen to the minute. Her legs remembered the way without instruction.

She did not look back at the shelves. Looking back would have made her set it down, and she had already said she would not.
