# What the Layer Says

Ning Yan did not trust herself to sleep.

The tower after midnight funneled wind and ash through every gap in the lashed poles, and her scar throbbed with the cold. Du Hong snored faintly against the wall with the jaw-plate wrapped in oilcloth under his head like a pillow. Outside, the Verge breathed, vast and patient, eleven disciples and a Team Elder scattered along the water in fires that looked from above like sparks on black paper.

She needed four hours. She did not dare take them. At stage four, with Lu Fen still circling, sleep was a thing other people did.

So she counted. That was what she did when fear would not leave room for rest. She counted breaths, counted heartbeats against breaths, counted seconds between the wind gusts to keep her mind from running back to the blade stopping a finger from her throat and the cold pouring out of her chest.

The voice kept her company without speaking, a low presence at the back of her skull, dimmer than before the spending. She felt him counting with her. That frightened her more than she wanted to admit, that he was lonely enough to count.

Then the night split open.

It was not sound. It was the absence of all other sound, as if the wind and the fires and Du Hong's snore had been lifted away at once. A sentence arrived, whole, in the air and in the bone, audible to every living person at the same moment, in a voice that was not a voice.

[Inner disciple Mei Jiu breaks to Body-Forging stage eight. Her diligence is recorded.]

Ning Yan sat bolt upright. Pain knifed through her scar. Du Hong woke choking. Outside, someone shouted. Someone else laughed in disbelief. Up and down the water, fires flared as people stood.

The sentence hung in the cold, then faded, leaving the wind to rush back in.

Ning Yan stared at the pole roof with her heart hammering. Mei Jiu. Fourth-rank inner disciple, the girl who had beaten her in the yard last autumn with clean sleeves and a sponsor's token. Breaking to stage eight at Qinghe, miles west over the ridge, in the practice hall with its braziers and its maps.

And every person in the bound had heard it. The lamp-menders in Lowwater. The clerks in their towers. The farmers in counties she had never visited. People three counties away with no reason to care about one inner girl's diligence had just been told, at four in the morning, in the middle of their sleep.

The layer was not a sky. It was a room with people in it.

She understood it all at once, cold and complete. Announcements reached everyone inside the bound and no further. They were audible, not written. They reported and never advised. The layer spoke to everyone equally, which meant it was not on her side. It would announce her death as readily as her breakthrough, to the same audience, in the same flat tone.

Du Hong whispered, “Did you hear—”

“Everyone heard,” she said. “That was the point.”

The voice spoke, thin with awe and fear.

“I hear it too. I am inside it. I am small inside it.”

She pressed her palm to her sternum, feeling the chipped rim of her aperture ache. He was inside the thing that had just spoken. A man with no hands lodged in the machinery that told the whole bound when a girl breathed wrong. The scale of it made her dizzy.

Then her mind, stubborn and servant-born, did the absurd thing it always did at four in the morning. She counted the seconds the sentence had taken to arrive and fade, measured it against the distance to Qinghe over the ridge, and worked out how fast the sound must have traveled to reach the far counties at once.

Too fast. Faster than any horn, any drum, any runner. It had not traveled at all. It had happened everywhere.

She lay back against the cold stones with that knowledge like a weight on her chest. The world had gotten larger and colder in the same breath.

Outside, the camp settled slowly, voices buzzing. Lu Fen laughed too loud about Mei Jiu's luck. Shen Xiu sat long over his list-board, brush unmoving.

Ning Yan did not sleep. She listened for whether the night would speak again, and hated that she now wanted it to.
