# What It Cost Him

They wanted to put her in the anteroom and she said no, and then they wanted to put her in the Hall's second room and she said no again, and in the end an old steward of about seventy with a burnt hand walked her up the Nine Steps Terrace at the twelfth hour with her right arm in a sling made out of a coat, because nobody had thought to do a sling and the coat was somebody else's.

She made him stop at the sixth step.

From there you could see the street. Forty feet of dressed oak and two hundred years of roof lay across it in three pieces and a fourth was on top of the well, and the ash was coming down onto all of it in a grey column that had begun to look like wet cement.

Men were already up on the undercroft roof trying to get at the beams, and both great doors were propped open on nothing at all, and forty people stood in the street looking up at the shape of the gap where a building had been a building.

She sat down on the sixth step. The stone went cold through her skirt and up her back into the place the fever had been sitting in, which had been the height of a bad cold since the second day past and had gone somewhere else entirely in the last hour.

He did not say anything.

She waited for him to say something for the length of a count to sixty, which she did, and at sixty he was still not saying anything, and she noticed somewhere in the middle of the count that this was the first time since the ninth week that he had gone quiet while she was awake.

## The Explanation

“You held it.”

He was there. He had been there the entire four seconds and he had not left in the last nineteen minutes.

“Four seconds is not a pause,” he said. “There is no pause. The sentence was made and it was finished and then it sat where made sentences sit until it is let out. That is all four seconds was.”

“You held it.”

“Yes.”

She put her left hand flat on the step. The stone under it was the same stone she had put her hand flat on at the fourth hour of the fourth night past, with a hollow in her chest and an open hand, in a different kind of weather.

“Tell me the rule you used.”

He told her. He told it flatly and in order and without any apology in it anywhere, because he had decided during nineteen minutes in which he had said nothing at all that if he was going to do this he was going to do it properly, which was, she understood about a third of the way through, the most frightening version of him available.

“A report does not go out on its own,” he said. “It is made, and then it is released, and the release is the act of whoever is holding the sentence. That is not a rule of this sect or of that office you have been reading sheets from. It is older than the sect.”

“Every advancement inside this bound for a hundred years has gone out the instant it was ready, and not one of them has ever waited, and that is not because nobody has ever held one. It is because nobody who was holding one has ever had a reason to.”

“Why would a reason exist.”

“Because holding one costs nothing and letting one go costs the whole of the room.” He was quiet for a moment. “Ning Yan, look at what you are actually asking me about.”

“If I had held that report, then the only record of that morning in nine counties would be nine clerks with pens in a building with no roof, writing down that a girl was struck five times and got up four times and lay on a floor for two minutes and forty. And no voice. Nothing in any chest in any village. A Hall full of men arguing about whether it had happened.”

“So nothing would have happened.”

“Nothing would have *been reported*,” he said. “And there is a difference, and the difference is the whole of your life, and I want you to hear the difference before you decide what you think of me.”

He gave it to her in pieces.

“You are sealed for Condensing. The seal runs a year from the eighth day past. You were on the floor at the ninth hour today and you did not go through, or you went through and four men in this building ruled that you did not. Either way the register of Qinghe says a member who was sealed has not attempted. That is a hole, and it is the exact shape a furnace looks for, and it has been that shape for six hundred years.”

“Inside a month there would be a form. The form would be about the seal, because a seal that has not been used is a seal in dispute, and there is a clause for that which somebody thought about carefully.”

“It would go to a room with two men in it. You would be called to a room with two men in it, and one of them would be kind about it, because that is how they keep it quiet, and he would tell you the account can be settled in service and your brother's would be looked at, and he would not be lying about any of it.”

“And if I had gone through and they had ruled that I had not.”

“Then there is no report and there is a finding that you did not,” he said, “and a girl who is not in any office in nine counties is a girl that four offices can do nothing with, and I would have spent nine months keeping you out of every room that had your name on the wall of it. That is what I would have been doing. That is the work I would have had.”

He stopped.

“I decided,” he said, “that I would rather have you be a target for the rest of your life than be invisible for one month more.”

## The Arithmetic

The street was full of ash and men on a roof and she watched a beam go over and come down in three pieces, and the arithmetic took about ten seconds and she did it the way she did everything.

She had asked the Hall, in a corridor, for nine hundred witnesses, on the grounds that a sentence four women in a market square could check was worth more than a sentence nine hundred people had seen. She had built a whole petition out of the idea that a thing which cannot be checked is a thing that can be done to a person in private.

She had asked, in other words, to be counted.

And he had sat inside the machinery for four seconds and let the count happen.

If he had held it, the only record of that morning in nine counties would have been nine clerks with pens in a building with no roof, writing down that a girl was struck five times and got up four times and lay on a floor for two minutes and forty.

No voice. Nothing in any chest in any village. By eleven years from now nine counties would have four different sentences about a morning that had actually happened in one room in front of everybody the sect could put in a building.

That was the whole trade. It had been made for her by a thing with no hands, in four seconds, in a building that was falling down, and it had cost her the only thing she had ever wanted, which was to do a thing on purpose and have it be real.

“You did not ask me,” she said.

“No.”

“You have never asked me. Not once in four months, and you have spent me four times, and one of them woke me up four months lighter with a hole in my chest and a technique I will never have again.”

“Yes.”

“But you are right,” said Ning Yan, “and I am not going to argue with you about it, and I want that said now while we are on the step, because if I do not say it here I will spend nine years being unable to say it.”

He did not answer.

“You will be right about this again. Not about this. About the next one. There will be a next one inside a month and it will be worse and you will be right and I will not be able to prove a single word of it, and you will do it again because it works. That is what you are. I have known what you are since the eleventh day and I have kept you anyway.”

“Yes,” he said.

“Then we go on.”

“Go on where.”

“With the arm I have,” said Ning Yan, and did not look at him, because looking at him was not available.

## The Name

She sat on the sixth step through the middle of the afternoon with the ash going down on her and the men shifting beams in the street, and at about the fourth hour she said, “What was the four seconds for.”

“You have just told me what they were for.”

“I know what they were for. I want to know what you were doing.”

And he did not answer that one.

That was the moment. It was not a silence and it was not a pause; he gave her four other sentences in about two seconds, all of them true, and one of them repeated a thing she had already been told, and she heard the shape of a man answering a different question very well indeed.

*The release is the act of whoever is holding the sentence. Every advancement for a hundred years has gone out the instant it was ready. There was one morning in four months when it did not. He told me he held it. He has not once said when he let go.*

Four seconds.

She did not say it out loud. She sat on a cold step on a mountain and worked out that a thing with no body had been the only person in nine counties who could have stopped the one announcement she had wanted her entire life, and had chosen not to, and had told her about the choice without ever once telling her the length of it, and had watched her work it out.

“You could have told me it was four seconds,” she said.

“No.”

“Why not.”

“Because you would have asked me why,” he said, “and I would have had to tell you that I did not know.”

[A name is entered in the layer beside an entry that has stood empty for six years. The layer does not explain a name it has not written.]

“It knows your name,” said Ning Yan.

“It has a name for me.”

“That is not the same thing and you know it is not the same thing.”

“No,” he said. “It is not.”

She waited.

“Yuan Sheng,” he said.

He said it in the flat present voice, without a number attached, in the tone of a man handing over a piece of paper he has been keeping for six years because it was the only thing he had that anybody else had not given him.

She did not write it down. The log was against her ribs and the log was evidence and this was not evidence; it was a thing a dead nineteen-year-old had carried out of his own life and she was not going to put it in a book that could be found.

“Yuan Sheng,” she said once, out loud, on a step, with the ash coming down, so that she had heard it in her own mouth as well as in her own head.

“Do not use it lightly.”

“Why not.”

“Because it is the only thing about me that was ever mine,” he said, “and if you use it, you will be using that.”

## Ninety Seconds

It came at about the fifth hour, while men were coming up the terrace with a stretcher she did not need.

He went away in the middle of a sentence. Not gone — she could feel the difference now and had learned it — but *away*, the way a man goes out of a room and is still in it.

He came back about four minutes later with a different silence.

“Settlement has passed two thirds,” he said.

“What does that do.”

“It means that in about four months I stop being able to hold the channel open, and after that there is nothing in your head and no bargain was ever made and you spent four months talking to a wall.” He said it in the flattest voice he had.

“And it has made me an offer. There is a version of this where I can read every cultivator inside the bound and not just you, and issue the announcements myself, and choose what goes out and what does not. It is open now. It will be open for about ninety seconds.”

“Take it.”

“No.”

“Ninety seconds is a long time to say no to.”

“I have had four months to think about it and you have been in the room for three of them,” he said. “If I take that, then I will not be a voice in your head. I will be an office. And an office is a thing that is better off than you, and that is the whole of what has gone wrong with me since the first night, and I am not going to fix it by being given a desk.”

“You might have to.”

“Yes,” he said. “I might. I have refused it and I have lost the ability to see past your own skin and I am not going to pretend that was free.”

The ninety seconds went.

After it she asked him one question, on the step, in the ash, with the street full of men and beams.

“If it is too public to be taken quietly,” she said, “then how many people know my name now?”

“Nine counties,” he said. “Every person in every one of them, at the same moment, including every person who is breathing that air as I say this. And by tomorrow, four offices, and a mark in each. And by the end of the week, a price.”

She sat with that.

Two years ago a ledger had put her name in a column in a yard and it had cost her nothing but a chain. This had cost her the same name, and it had cost every other person in nine counties nothing at all, except one who had decided for her, in four seconds, in a room she was not in.

She was alive.

She was also, from that morning onward, a thing with a line in four books that had never had a line in them before, and that line would be there when she was forty, and no door in any building was going to close over it.

“Why are you telling me this now and not in the middle of it.”

“Because in the middle of it I was working,” said Yuan, “and I have been sitting on this step for three hours waiting until I was not, and that is the most honest thing I have got and it is not nearly enough. You are not going to forgive me. I have known that since the fourth second. And you are still here.”

He was right. She was still there.

She had not forgiven him. She had understood him completely, in the way that you understand a rule in a building that has just taken your roof off, and she had gone on sitting on the step beside him anyway, and the explanation she had was one she had been given in a corridor in the middle of the winter by a decent man who could take it back.

*Being rescued is not the same as being owned.*

It was also not the same as being left.

She got up off the sixth step with her left hand and her knees and went up the remaining three to the rail, and stood there in the ash over Qinghe with the whole of the sect below her looking up at a girl in a stranger's coat.

Four offices had a line about her now. Somebody would be sent to find out what a Condensing stage one with a cracked aperture and no sponsor was worth. And the thing that had made her famous was the same thing that had made her safe, and she would spend the rest of her life unable to separate them, and she was not going to be able to ask the only person who knew why not to explain.

Visibility arrived at the fourth hour of the fourth afternoon, it cost nothing, and it was the most expensive thing anybody had ever done for her.
