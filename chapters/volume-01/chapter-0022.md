# The Weight at the Marker

Night on the kiln road was black under trees.

Ning Yan came out of the Broken Kiln yard with the tablet against her ribs and the copies in her lining, and the cold struck her sweat cold. Her goal was simple and held her upright. Get clear of the marker before the night watch walked the Lowwater turn.

Resistance was the seal itself. The moment she left with an object that was inside it, the layer made a note, and the note went somewhere she could not see. She felt it as a pressure change, the way air changed before an announcement, a listening stillness that settled and lifted.

She walked. Ash muffled her steps. Stage seven burned thin to keep her warm. The plate was heavier than it looked, pulling at her coat, warm through cloth in a way that made her skin crawl.

Half a mile down, Yuan came back.

He returned all at once, a rush of presence, counting fast, frightened, loud in her head after forty minutes of silence. Relief went through her so sharp it was pain.

“I am here. I am back. I cannot undo this.”

She kept walking. Branches caught her sleeves. Her breath smoked.

“I cannot tell you what it costs,” he said. “I do not know where the note goes. I am sorry.”

She did not ask him to pay for it and he did not offer. That was the new ledger between them. The layer logged removals. The Clay Tablet was the first thing in sixteen years that came out of a sealed place, and the cost of carrying it was hers to carry, not his to spend.

She kept walking the four miles with the plate against her ribs and did not sleep and did not stop. Her feet found the path by habit. Her thighs remembered step fragments when her head was too tired to lead. The second mile broke her. The third remade her. Somewhere in the fourth, with dawn still hours off and Wen’s marker post ahead pale in starlight, the deep shift came, a small crack through exhaustion, heat racing down her arms.

Body-Forging stage eight.

Built from carrying, like stage six, and for the same reason. Four miles of walking with a plate heavier than duty and of not sleeping. No quest. No price stated. Her foundation tax untouched for this one, which she noticed and he did not comment on. That silence was its own entry.

Yuan spoke once more, low with wonder and fear.

“I rise again. I feel it. I stay with you.”

His Settlement rose again. She felt it the way she felt weather, a tightening of the channel, a warmth that was also a weight. They were both keeping books now. She held one end in her coat. He held the other in his coherence.

She reached the marker post before first light. The cloth strip hung still. Wen’s cart was gone. The road beyond lay empty toward Lowwater. She stopped, set her hand on the post for a moment, breathed seventeen to the minute, and understood that she crossed from sealed ground back into counted ground with contraband against her skin.

[Removal from sealed ground is logged. The object leaves. The weight remains.]

The sentence arrived whole, in air and bone, flat, reporting, never advising. She heard it alone on the road. No one in Lowwater stirred. It was not for the bound. It was for the office behind it.

She stood with her hand on the post and felt partnership turn into accounting, cold and clean. She did not ask him to carry it. He never volunteered. This pattern would repeat until Volume 5 and it was the worst thing about him, that he watched her carry and called watching love.

She lifted her hand from the post and walked on toward Lowwater with the tablet warm against her ribs.

She had the tablet and no idea who she could show it to. That was the next question, and it hurt worse than her feet.
