# The Turn

The outer yard three days later held winter light like a blade.

Ning Yan was summoned to the Elder Hall for the ordinary business of a stage-nine outer disciple with no sponsor. She went in her cleanest coat, ledger flat to her ribs, tablet against skin, hands washed raw. Her goal was narrow. She meant to sign nothing and lose nothing.

Resistance was procedure. The Hall sat in session, nine elders on low seats, clerks at the sides, braziers smoking. Qiu sat third from the centre, hands folded, face calm. Shen Xiu stood by the door with his list, eyes on the floor. She felt his report sitting unspoken behind his teeth.

The Speaker read her name, her stage, her lack of sponsor. Past stage nine needed a seal. Servants were not sealed. The words were said the way weather was said.

Then Qiu rose and petitioned. He asked the Hall to admit Ning Yan to the furnace crew as servant-rank inner disciple, bed indoors, stipend, family account deferred pending good service. He spoke of her winter work, her carrying, her stage nine reached by labour. He did not mention tablets or kilns or reassignments. He offered the sect a strong girl for the furnace floor, and offered the girl a reason to stop. His voice stayed even throughout, the voice of a man requesting stores, not mercy.

It was approved in eleven minutes. Genuine promotion. Real advancement, enormous for servant-born, a locked room with a bed in it. Brushes scratched. Seals thumped. Somebody congratulated her.

She accepted, because refusing would have been pure, and she was not in a position to be pure. She bowed, past tense formal, thanked the Hall, took the token. Her hands did not shake until afterwards.

Trapped inside a kindness. Much worse than being hunted.

Outside, on the terrace steps, Shen Xiu caught her sleeve.

“Did not report,” he murmured, past tense, low. “The scheduling moved. I held my tongue. You went inside anyway.”

She looked at him, tired man with a coughing son, and felt no relief. Inside was inside, whether by report or by promotion.

Yuan spoke, present, worried.

“You win. I fear. I stay close.”

She walked to the furnace crew quarters with her new token in her sleeve and understood the reversal. She had won the argument—stage nine, noticed, wanted—and lost the fight, because wanting meant housing, and housing meant the building, and the building meant the floor above the records room. Qiu was never going to destroy her. He was going to give her a reason to stop, kindly, with paper to prove it.

In the crew doorway she stopped, turned, looked back at the yard where Du Hong watched from the rail, face strange.

She kept the log. That was the part of the decision nobody in the Hall had voted on. Bed, stipend, deferred account, and underneath, paper and clay against skin.

She stepped inside the building. The records room sat under it, and the furnace crew went down there twice a month, and she was now crew.
