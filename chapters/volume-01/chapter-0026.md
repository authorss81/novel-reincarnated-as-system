# Rebuilding

The training yard at dawn smelled of ash and cold iron.

Ning Yan took the far corner where the snow had been beaten to ice, and began the work of getting back to stage nine. Eleven days stood between her and the winter drill. Three days to the drill itself when she started counting in earnest. Her body sat at stage eight, thin from the kiln road and the nights writing, with a chipped place at the centre of her chest that caught when qi moved fast.

Her goal was simple enough to say and hard enough to bleed for. She needed stage nine before the drill, because stage nine outer disciples were weighed twice and given oil, and stage eight girls with no sponsor were given advice and a longer run.

Resistance was the missing part of her. Four months gone in one night under a tower, and the order of things with it. Her legs remembered step fragments when they chose. Her head remembered nothing of the Nine-Step as teaching. The aperture rim pulled whenever she pushed past comfort, like a cup with a cracked lip that would not hold hot tea.

She worked forms until her shoulders shook. She carried water. She ran the terrace steps with sand in her coat. Du Hong met her on the fourth morning and beat her without cruelty, and on the sixth morning she lasted half an hour before he tapped her ribs. At night she oiled her knees with kitchen fat and slept with the ledger under her pallet, waking to check the string before dawn count.

Hunger worked on her worse than cold. The outer kitchen cut portions in winter, and stage eight burned through what little she ate. She traded mending for extra millet, hemming two crew coats by lamplight with fingers that shook. Her mother would have scolded her for sewing tired. Her mother was not there to scold.

On the seventh day she understood something she had been circling for a week.

She had been watching Mei Jiu drill on the upper terrace, the inner girl moving through a condensing preparation form with her sleeves tied back. The movement was clean. The shoulders dropped at a place Ning Yan's own shoulders did not drop. She tried to copy it and failed, and tried again and failed, and felt the familiar anger come up.

Yuan spoke, careful.

“I see it. I find it. I do not speak it well.”

She kept her face still, sweat freezing at her hairline. “You saw what I did wrong,” she murmured, past tense, breath short. “You told me before when my elbow strayed.”

“I see the shape. I hold it. I lack the words.”

She went to the ash-flats after drill, alone, where wind moved grey snow in sheets and nobody watched long. She stood in the cold and let him look through her eyes at her own hands.

“Showed me where it sat,” she said. “You did it in the tower with shoulders. You did it again at the sheath.”

He guided her the way he always did, not by telling but by locating, a pressure at the edge of attention, warmer when her hand neared the true line, colder when it strayed. Left elbow lower. Breath later. Weight forward half a finger. She adjusted, and the form settled, and heat moved through her calf without catching.

It was the first time she had used him as a teacher instead of a door. The knowing landed hard. She stopped mid-form with snow in her shoes and stared at nothing.

She told him to stop.

“Stopped it,” she said aloud, flat. “You did enough. I wanted it done alone.”

Silence. Hurt, quickly hidden. She felt him withdraw, counting to himself, trying to be small.

“I stop. I wait. I am here.”

Shame burned through her colder than wind. He had no body, no hands, no way to stand in weather, and the one thing he could give was sight without touch, and she had taken it and then told him to stop as if he were a servant. She stood on the flats with her hands shaking and understood that grudging trust was still trust, and the only kind she had ever offered anybody.

She asked anyway.

She lifted her hands again, took the stance, and said, low, “Helped me once more. I asked it plain.”

He came back at once, bright, trying not to show how glad he was.

“I help. I find. I stay.”

They worked until light failed, locate and adjust, locate and adjust, until the shoulder dropped the way Mei Jiu's dropped, until breath came seventeen to the minute and held. She felt the deep shift gather the way weather gathered, pressure then release, a small crack loud through her ribs, heat racing down her arms and legs.

Body-Forging stage nine.

Built from eleven days of losing and carrying, from being located rather than told. No quest. No price stated. The foundation tax sat untouched, which she noticed the way she noticed weather. Her aperture rim ached at the chipped edge but held.

She stood on the flats with snow to her ankles and laughed once, short, startled. Stage nine. Where ordinary people stopped. She had no idea whether she was ordinary, and for one breath she did not care.

Yuan spoke, wonder and fear together.

“You burn bright. I see you. I remain.”

She pressed her hand to her chest where the tablet lay against skin under layers, and the log against ribs opposite, paper and clay, and felt the weight of both under stage nine strength. She wanted the drill now. She wanted the weighing, the oil, the second look. She wanted what stage nine outer disciples were given, and she knew it would not include the seal.

The sect's winter drill stood three days off. She walked back toward the lights with her legs shaking and her breath steady, located, rebuilt, trusted against her will.
