# The Map She Was Not Given

The outer disciples’ scripture hall was a long low room above the laundry, warm with brazier heat and smelling of old paper and wet wool.

Ning Yan went there on a rest afternoon with her hands clean and her token in her sleeve, stage seven still strange in her chest, and asked the keeper for the map to Condensing. She asked it the way she asked for lamp-wick, as if it was a thing that could be handed over, because she had decided that morning that wanting it obliquely had never worked for anybody servant-born.

The keeper was an old woman with ink on her cuffs who had handed books to outer disciples for twenty years. She looked at Ning Yan’s token, looked at her face, and shook her head with genuine regret.

“The map is not kept here for general lending,” she said. “Inner disciples receive it at sealing. You need a sponsor’s writ to see it.”

Ning Yan bowed. “I was told the hall held all practice pages.”

“It holds what it is given to hold.” The old woman gestured at the shelves, rows of bound manuals for Body-Forging forms, breathing exercises, knife work, none of them past stage nine. “The Condensing map is not locked. It is simply not in this room, because this room hands books to people whose names are on a list.”

Not locked. Simply absent. Ning Yan stood among shelves of things she was allowed to read and understood that permission was a kind of wall, smoother than stone and harder to climb.

She spent an hour copying a breathing page she already knew, to be seen working, and watched who came and went. Inner disciples entered the side room with writs. A clerk checked names against a list-board. The door closed. The door opened. Nobody servant-born went through it.

Mei Jiu came in near the end of the hour.

Ning Yan knew her by reputation and by announcement. Fourth-rank inner, Body-Forging stage eight, the girl the layer named to the whole bound at four in the morning. She had beaten Ning Yan in the yard last autumn with clean sleeves and a sponsor’s token, efficiently, without malice, the way a person closes a shutter. Since the ledger reading she watched Ning Yan from the terrace edge with sharp interest.

Now she stopped at Ning Yan’s table, looked at the breathing page, and said, “You copy that like somebody who already breathes.”

Ning Yan set down her brush. “I was taught to copy fair. The tower keeper praised my hand.”

“Feng Lushi praised anybody who brought him paper.” But Mei Jiu sat, uninvited, which was itself a kind of answer. She was eighteen, thin, tired around the eyes in a way no stage eight should have been. “You want the map.”

“I want to know why it is not here.”

Mei Jiu glanced at the side door. “Because the Condensing breakthrough past stage nine is not a technique problem. It is a document problem. It requires a sect seal, and a servant-born cannot be sealed.”

The words landed flat. Ning Yan had expected a locked chest, a secret manual, a test of worth. Not paperwork.

“A seal,” she repeated.

“A seal and a sponsor and a name in the right column.” Mei Jiu’s voice stayed low, past tense, careful. “I broke to eight in the practice hall with braziers and maps and a sponsor watching. The layer announced my diligence. Nobody announced the three days I spent in the Elder Hall waiting for the seal clerk to find my writ. Nobody announced what it took from my family account to get him to find it quickly.”

Ning Yan heard the anger underneath, worn thin by carrying. She had come to hate this girl for a year in the yard. Now she sat across a table and recognized the set of the shoulders, the line where a person decided to lose on purpose long before the hands knew.

So she did not ask Mei Jiu for help. Help would have been refused, and rightly. She asked the harder question.

“Did you get yours honestly?”

Mei Jiu stared. Then she laughed, once, short.

“No,” she said. “I got mine the way everybody gets theirs. My mother paid a harvest balance with two years of furnace laundry. My sponsor took a fee in oil. The seal clerk kept me waiting until I understood the waiting was the fee.” She rubbed her eyes. “I wanted the same thing you want and I got it earlier and it has gone badly for me. Stage eight sits in my chest like a stone. I cannot sleep past second watch. The seal itches. If you think the map is freedom, you have not read the permit.”

Ning Yan sat with that. Her rival turned out to be a person with her own account in somebody else’s column, not a bad person, not a villain, just a girl who paid and kept paying.

“I am sorry,” Ning Yan said, and meant it, which surprised them both.

Mei Jiu stood. “Don’t be. Be careful.” She hesitated at the table edge. “The address for paper that is not the sect’s is Lowwater. There is one set of records in the nine counties that is not the sect’s. The tower keeps what the air says, not what the Elder Hall tells it to say. If you want to know what a seal costs, ask what the air said on the night you were sealed.”

She went into the side room with her writ, and the door closed.

Ning Yan finished her copy, paid the keeper a half-cash for the paper, and walked out into the cold with stage seven aching and a new understanding sitting cold in her stomach. The obstacle was not strength. It was paperwork, and paperwork had an address.

Yuan spoke as she crossed the yard, quick with excitement and fear.

“I hear her account. I hold it with you. I do not forget.”

She did not answer where others could see. But she held Mei Jiu’s truth alongside Du Hong’s warning, two tally slips that did not match, and knew the next road went down the mountain.

Lowwater. The tower. The records that were not the sect’s.
