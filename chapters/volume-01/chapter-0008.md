# The Tree Line

The inner tree line was where the Verge stopped pretending to be a forest.

Ning Yan saw it at noon on the fourth day, past the second water, where the ashwoods grew so thick their crowns knotted together and the light came down in grey shafts full of falling ash. The boundary mark ended here. Beyond was sheath country, where Ashjaws denned in the roots, and the quota parties turned back unless desperate or ordered.

She was desperate. Du Hong's filed plate had made her quarter-token whole on paper, but paper at the boundary needed a record plate to back it, and the pine splinter in her sleeve was only half proof. She needed the sheath cleared and the tower tally confirmed, or Lu Fen's competing claim would eat her alive at the reading.

Shen Xiu had let them go with a long look. “You came back by dusk,” he said, “or you did not come back.”

Nine of them in the sheath. She counted the breathing through the roots before she ever saw a body, the way the voice taught her to listen, low bellows-rasps overlapping in the dark under the trees. At stage four, with a leg that still forgot step three, it was suicide. With Du Hong beside her, who had never fought one, it was murder.

The voice spoke, grave and quick.

“I offer a quest. You clear the sheath. You bring back the boundary mark. The price is a full stage. There is a real chance the aperture opens. You decide. I cannot lie.”

A full stage. She stood at Body-Forging stage four, thin as paper. A stage was everything she had rebuilt since the night he spent her. And the aperture, chipped at the rim already, cracking open meant stopping wherever she stood, forever, a cultivator who could never hold qi again past what she reached.

But the layer could be bargained with, badly, and the price was always paid by her and never by him. That was the only leash either of them had, that he had to say it plain before she accepted.

Du Hong nocked his knife wrong-handed. His shoulders screamed fear and determination in equal measure, the set of a boy who had decided to lose bravely on purpose.

She made the second decision of the run.

*I accept,* she thought, *but he stays outside the kill radius.*

“You accept. I hold the door. You do the work.”

She turned to Du Hong and put her hand flat on his chest. “You waited at the mark stone,” she said. “You carried no blade past it. You lived to file.”

“I fought beside you,” he said. “I owed you—”

“You owed me living,” she said. “You stayed.”

She refused to let him inside and it cost her the only advantage she had, a second blade and a pair of eyes. She went into the trees alone, lame and scarred and stage four, with habit instead of technique and a pine splinter for luck.

The fight was not clean. Nothing at stage four against nine was clean.

She took the first Ashjaw with a rock and a knife, turning inside its lunge the way her thighs remembered when her head stayed quiet. The second caught her shoulder and tore her sleeve to the skin. She screamed and kept moving, because stopping was dying, step-fragment, step-fragment, heat flashing through calves that had no names for what they did. Qi answered her half a step faster, the quest's lean, and she used it to run along the root-wall and drop behind the third beast and open its hinge from above.

Blood steamed. Ash stuck to blood. Her scar burned as if reopened. Somewhere beyond the trees Du Hong shouted her name and stayed where she told him, which was the bravest thing he had done.

The breakthrough came mid-fight, with a crack loud enough to hear through the snarling. Body-Forging stage five. Heat poured through her, new and raw, the aperture holding, rim aching but whole. She sobbed once with relief and drove her knife home in the same breath.

When it ended she stood in a circle of carcasses with her knife broken to the hilt and her hands empty, shaking so hard she could not wipe blood from her eyes. Nine. She had cleared nine with habit and spite.

The warmth settled into the voice, faint but real.

“I am clearer. You live. I am glad.”

Then the layer spoke, to everyone, about her.

[Sheath cleared at the inner line. Nine Ashjaws taken. The bounty is settled.]

She had filed nothing. She had carried no plate to any boundary. The layer announced a kill she did not file, because the quest itself was filing, because the machinery honoured what its Chosen did whether any clerk wrote it down.

The announcement put a bounty on the sheath and the boy in it. She understood that as she stood bleeding among the dead, hearing her own work said aloud to every county at once. She was visible now in a way she had never chosen.

Du Hong ran in, white-faced, and caught her before she fell. “You were dead,” he kept saying. “You were dead and you are not.”

She leaned on him, stage five unsteady under her, aperture throbbing at the rim, and felt fear of dying for somebody she met four days ago settle cold in her stomach. She could not make herself stop.

They walked out with the boundary mark pried from the root-wall, and the trees closed behind them without sound.
