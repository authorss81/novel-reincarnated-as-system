# Sealed Ground

The Broken Kiln yard after dark was ruin held together by quiet.

Ning Yan crossed the fallen wall where the gate had been, pack tight, knife loose, Feng’s copies pressed against her ribs. Her goal was simple and entire. Get inside the assessment room. Copy what was there. Leave before night watch.

The building was ruin. The door was ruin, wood rotted to lace, hanging from one hinge. Beyond it a passage went dark toward an inner room where a lamp should not have burned and did not. She smelled fired clay and old smoke and wet stone, and underneath, faint, the iron taste of furnace air.

She stepped over the threshold.

Yuan went silent.

Not quiet. Silent. For the first time since the staging ground below the Lower Gate, the low presence at the back of her skull vanished, cut as if a door closed between breaths. No counting. No warmth. No embarrassed quick voice in the present tense. Nothing.

She stopped mid-step with one foot in the air.

She waited. Snow settled on the ruined lintel. Somewhere in the trees an animal moved and stopped. Her own breath sounded loud, sixteen to the minute, then seventeen, shallow with fear.

*Yuan,* she thought.

Nothing answered.

She spoke aloud, softly, to the empty passage. “Yuan.”

Her voice came back off stone. No second string under it. No tone beside her head. She was alone in a way she had not been since she was seventeen and heard a man count her breaths, and the aloneness had a taste, metallic and cold, like water from a cup left out overnight.

Fear rose hot. She forced it cold. She had forty minutes of light left in the yard, perhaps less inside. The seal was not masonry and not a door. It was an instruction, and he was inside the thing that issued it. The layer would not let him read inside sealed ground. He was not blocked by a wall. He was not present at all.

Then, for the length of a breath, he came back wrong.

A flicker, dim, miscounted. A sense of numbers scattering, of a readout trying to form and failing. She felt him try to say her stage and get it wrong, try to say her name and lose the second syllable. Then gone again.

She understood. He came back with a count that was wrong because the seal refused him the room. The layer could refuse a place to the voice that lived inside it.

She had a choice. Go back to the marker, to Wen’s cart, to daylight and paper and safety, with Feng’s copy and no more. Or go in anyway, alone, with no voice at all, into a room where her brother was assessed at thirteen.

She went in.

The passage took eleven steps. Her feet found broken tile, then packed earth, then fired brick worn smooth. The assessment room opened beyond, low and square, shelves along three walls holding fired clay tablets in rows, arranged by year, no names on the outside of anything. A procedure board hung on the far wall in Bureau hand. A work table stood in the centre with brush-rests and a weighing dish, dust thick.

She stood in the doorway and listened to her own heart. Forty minutes. She had about forty minutes before full dark made the road back impossible and before the seal’s marker did whatever it did to weight that stayed too long.

No voice counted with her. No warmth leaned toward her. Her hands shook as she lifted the first tablet.

She set it down. She moved along the shelves, checking year marks cut into the clay edges, looking for the year Ning Wu was thirteen, the year the kiln took him. Her fingers left trails in dust. The quiet pressed on her ears until she heard blood.

This was the most frightening thing that happened in this volume. Not the knife at her throat. Not the sheath. Being without him where she needed him, discovering that partnership was a thing that could be taken by a room, and going on working anyway.

She found the year row. She knelt on the cold brick with dust in her throat and began to read by touch in failing light.

Outside, the wind moved through the broken chimney and made a low note like a cracked pipe. Inside, a girl alone turned clay in her hands and kept breathing, sixteen, seventeen to the minute, counting for herself because nobody else could.

She was still counting when the light went grey and the room went dark around her hands.
