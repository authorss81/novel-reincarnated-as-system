# The Report

The records room below the furnace held dust and cold that heat never reached.

Ning Yan went down with the tally party carrying a lamp, new crew as lamp-bearer, and saw it whole for the first time. Shelves of bound books by year. Clay tablets in racks. Loose sheets in Bureau hand tied with string. The air smelled of paper and iron. Her goal burned plain. She needed the records book out, because it held the only copy in nine counties of the reassignment page with the unknown hand.

Resistance arrived early. Shen Xiu reported her three days early, for his own reasons, for his son, for the clerkship, tired and honest to the end. She heard boots above, heard the order read, heard the sub-basement door barred from outside with iron dropping into sockets. The tally party was ordered up. She was ordered to remain. The lamp she carried became the only light.

She stood among shelves with her lamp and understood she was sealed in. Stone walls. Iron door. No second stair. The tablet lay against skin. The log lay hidden in her coat. The book she needed sat two shelves over, bound in dark cloth, year-marked. Dust hung in lamplight. Her own breath sounded too loud. She counted it to keep it even, seventeen to the minute, the way she counted before fights.

She tried the door. Barred. She tried shouting. Nobody answered in a voice she knew.

Yuan spoke, present, flat with fear.

“I am here. I see the door. I cannot open.”

She set the lamp down, took the book from its shelf with steady hands, opened it to the reassignment page. There it was. Her brother's line. *Assessed, reassigned, unreconciled.* And below, the signature, small, fair, in a hand nobody at Qinghe would know. She stared until the characters burned.

Boots returned above. Voices. The furnace crew ordered clear. The Hall's men, not Qiu's, she understood later. The distinction would matter for the rest of his life.

She could not be got out by anybody. No friend below. No rope. No second door. Du Hong above with no authority. Qiu above with authority that stopped at the Hall's order.

Yuan said the words and meant them.

“I speak plain. I tell the cost. I offer the way.”

She held the lamp higher. “Told me once through the hand it took more. You told me partial. Tell me whole now.”

He told her whole, present tense, each sentence short, because long sentences would have hidden it.

“I act through your hand. I move your body. I spend what settles in me. I pay from your foundation. I take the top. I do not take a little. I take the order you built. I cannot tell how much. I cannot make it partial. I cannot undo. I may kill you.”

Silence in stone. Lamp smoke rising. Her breath loud.

She made him say the rest first, because consent with facts was the only kind this book contained.

“Asked what the top meant,” she said, past tense, steady. “You said stages. You said order. Say it so I heard it.”

“I take what is newest. I take what you learned last. I take strength and the knowing with it. I leave what is underneath. I leave you weaker.”

“Asked if Settlement fell,” she said.

“It falls. I live. You pay for my living.”

“Asked if you could carry me without taking,” she said.

“I cannot. I wish otherwise. I cannot.”

She stood with the book in her coat now, bound tight, and the lamp burning down, and the iron door holding, and understood the price was not the room, it was her body. Stage nine. The drill. The seal she never got. The Nine-Step habit. All of it on the table.

She said yes.

“Agreed,” she said aloud, past tense, final. “You told me whole. I heard it. Do it.”

It was the irreversible one. She could not undo it and neither could he. The first time she consented to the tax having been told the price. She would not do it again until Volume 16.

Above, iron shifted. Heat breathed through stone. The furnace floor waited overhead, and the way out was through her.

She lifted the lamp and turned toward the door with the book bound against her ribs, ready to be spent.
