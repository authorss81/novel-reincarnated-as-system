# The Tablet

The outer yard on a weekday morning was busy with work that pretended not to watch.

Ning Yan crossed it with the tablet against her skin and Feng’s copies in her lining, stage eight hidden under a plain coat, and every bucket carried past was a night-watch check she had not yet failed. Her goal was entire and frightening. Decide what to do with the only object in the world that proved the furnace column was not bookkeeping.

Resistance was the choice itself. Showing it meant admitting she went to a sealed place, which was a capital matter. Not showing it meant carrying a piece of a person forever, alone, with no one to read it.

She went to the dormitory corner where she could be unwatched for an hour, sat with her back to the wall, and took the tablet out inside her coat where no eye could land. Warm. Skin-like. Giving under her thumb. She had described its texture once in the kiln and would remember it in her thirties, standing where a god used to be, because it was the only thing the layer ever let out.

She tried the voice.

She laid her hand flat on the tablet through the coat and thought, *Touch it with me.*

Yuan went still, then bright with fear and want.

“I can try. I hold through you. I do not promise.”

She felt him lean toward her hand, toward the designation that bound them, toward the channel that rose every time he used it. Warmth gathered at her palm, strange and doubled, as if another hand lay over hers without weight.

He held it. Through her, with her, for exactly as long as the designation held. The tablet seemed to lighten. Her palm tingled. For a breath she felt what he felt, nineteen years without picking anything up, and then picking up, through borrowed skin.

“I touch,” he whispered in her head, awed. “I pick up. I hold for the first time in nineteen years.”

Joy went through her so sharp it was grief. He had no hands, no body, no qi, and now through her hand he held a piece of fired person and wept without eyes.

Then the cost landed.

He spoke her name in his head, then another name, his mother’s name, said out loud in the present tense because he carried it for six years as a thing he could still say, his mother dead six years, the last private detail he ever gave her.

“I name her. I love her. I remember—”

Silence. A tearing stillness. The touch took the name itself, not the grief, not the fact of the death, the name. Afterwards he could not say what she was called, and he never said it again, and he never said he forgot. His Settlement spiked on the touch, hot and hard, coherence bought with memory.

She felt it go. She felt him reach for the name and find the room where it was dark.

She pulled her hand back. “Enough,” she said aloud, to the empty dormitory corner. “You do not do it twice.”

“I obey. I stop. I stay.” His voice came smaller, present, missing a tooth it did not know was gone.

She sat shaking with the tablet in her lap and understood what he could do and what it took. He could act through her. It was slower and it cost her more and he had been saving it, because using it burned Settlement and memory both, and the second burn was the one that mattered.

She did not let him do it twice. She read the tablet herself, tilting it to window light, tracing the small brushed lines on its face, a name that was not her brother’s, a date, a disposition in the same three words. Then she took out paper, brush, ink, and wrote it out in her log, in her own hand, in characters a Bureau clerk could not read quickly, cramped laundry-hand shorthand mixed with tower marks.

She started the log. The log was the first thing she had ever done entirely for herself, not for quota, not for a quest, not for proof to show an elder. A book of accounts in her own hand, kept for the day she could use it.

When she finished she tied the log inside her lining, put the tablet back against her skin, and sat with the weight of both. Nothing grew. Stage eight held. The work was paper, and paper was heavier than stages.

Yuan spoke once more, thin.

“I am here. I am yours. I continue.”

She touched her coat where the tablet lay. She had the weapon for the whole volume, a copy the Elder Hall could not seize because they could not read it fast, and a cost neither of them would name.

She went up the mountain with the tablet and found Elder Qiu waiting for her at the terrace steps, sixty-one, kind-faced, hands folded, looking at her the way a man looks at a ledger line he has already decided to settle.

She stopped with her hand on her lining and her heart beating seventeen to the minute.

The next question had found her first.
