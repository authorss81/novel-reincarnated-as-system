# The Shape of the Silence

She went up all nine steps on the ninth day at the second hour, with her left hand on the rail and her right arm in the sling, and it took her four minutes, and she had gone up them at a run in the autumn with forty-one volumes on her back.

There was snow on the terrace. Not much and not white.

The ash had been falling for nine days and it did not settle, and what came down on the ninth night was snow, and the snow went through eleven days of ash on its way and arrived grey, so that the whole of the Nine Steps Terrace had a grey dusting on it and the rail had a line of grey and white along the top of it that would be gone by morning.

There were two days left in the fall. She had been counting since the fourth morning and she would keep counting until it stopped, and then she would know what a man in her head looked like on the tenth day of an ordinary winter.

She stood at the ninth step and looked down over Qinghe.

The Assembly Hall had a lamp on a pole and two braziers at the north end and a stack of squared timber along its south wall, and from up here the three walls and the gap looked like a mouth with the teeth taken out. It would not be built again. She had heard that on the second day, from a steward, in the voice of a man telling her the price of firewood.

Nine days.

Her left hand was cold on the rail and the token was in her coat and she could feel it through the cloth, a flat iron thing with three names cut into the back of it, and she had carried it for four days and had used it for nothing and had not decided whether to keep it.

“You have been carrying that for four days,” he said.

“I have.”

“You have not decided.”

“No.”

“Why are you up here.”

“Because I am going to have to decide something and I would rather do it in the open,” said Ning Yan.

## What He Had

“All right,” he said. “Then I will tell you what I have, because you are going to ask and because I have been waiting since the fourth morning to be asked and I have been working out how to do it without making you ask.”

She did not move.

“The designation is not a reward,” he said. “That is the first thing and it is the thing nobody has ever told you. A reward is given. A designation is a *writ*. The layer honours a Chosen One's quests, which means that the layer can be induced to do a thing it does not otherwise do, by one person, on request. Nobody else inside the bound can make the layer do anything at all. That is what I do. That is the entire of what I am.”

“You have said that.”

“I have said the shape of it. Here is the part I have not said.” He was quiet for a moment. “I have been inside the designation for six years and for five of them there was nothing there.”

“There was a space and a shape
There was a space and a shape and no name on it, and the whole of my existence was standing in an empty room waiting for somebody to need me, and I never once looked at what the room was made of, because I was nineteen and dead and glad to be anything.”

“And now.”

“Now there is a name on it, and the layer has a name for me as well, and in two days I have been able to look at things in here that I have never been able to look at before, because a designation with an occupant in it is a different kind of object from a designation with nothing in it.”

He gave her three things. He gave them without decoration, in order, the way he gave everything, and she understood that he had practised them.

“The first is that there have been others. Eleven of them, before you. The layer keeps them the way it keeps everything, which is that it does not keep them at all — there is no roll, no list, no place where a person can go and read them. But there are gaps in the shape of the room, and I have been able to look at them since the fourth morning, and there are eleven of them, and they are spaced at irregular intervals across about six hundred years.”

“Six hundred years,” said Ning Yan.

“Eleven in six hundred years. Which is not often. Which is not one a generation. Which is not how you would run a programme that was looking for the right person, and is exactly how you would run one that fires when it has to.”

He stopped.

“I cannot see what happened to any of them,” he said. “Not one. I can see that the designation was filled and that it stopped being filled, and there is a seam at each one, and the seam is the same seam as the one where you come through, and I cannot see past it.”

[The bound has held eleven designations before this one. The bound does not report what became of them.]

“The second thing,” he said, “is that a designation can be set aside. Not ended — *set aside*. There is a form of it in the machinery and I have been able to see the shape of it since the fourth morning, and it does not require my agreement and it does not require hers. It requires somebody to make a request, and the request does not have to come from inside the bound.”

That was the one that got into her stomach.

“So somebody out there can put me down.”

“Somebody out there can put you down, and would not have to tell you, and the layer would report the change the way it reports everything, which is as a sentence, and the sentence would not have your name in it.”

“Who is out there.”

“I do not know,” he said. “That is the third thing and it is the whole of what I have. In two days I have found the shape of an office and I cannot find the name of it. There is a place where a decision about you would be made and it has a name on it and the name is the one thing I cannot read anywhere in this, and I have looked for it for two days and a night.”

She stood on the ninth step with grey snow going into the shoulder of her coat and looked at a Hall with no roof and did not say anything for a while.

## The Limit

“Not tonight,” said Ning Yan.

He stopped.

It was not a dramatic silence. He simply was not there in the next sentence, and then he was there again, and she felt the shape of a conversation that had been going one way change in about a quarter of a second.

“You have not finished.”

“No.”

“There are four things after the one you have just said and I have read them and one of them is about the eight hundred jin.”

“Not tonight.”

“Ning Yan—”

“Stop,” said Ning Yan.

He stopped.

She had never said that to him. She had refused him a quest in the third night of the winter and she had refused him a hand in the middle of a stair in a records room and she had told him to go quiet in a cart, and none of those had been this, because none of those had been a thing she wanted while she was saying it.

“You have been in me for four months,” she said, “and you have spent me four times and you have decided one thing about me without me and told me about it afterwards, and I have not forgiven that and I am not going to.”

“And in the last nine days
And in the last nine days you have told me more in one sitting on a step than you told me in the first ten weeks, and I have sat here and listened to all of it, and I can feel the shape of what you are doing, and you are doing it because I am about to be examined and you want me to have the whole of it before that happens.”

“Yes.”

“I am not going to have it tonight,” she said. “You have spent enough of me tonight.”

He did not say anything.

“You are not going to tell me why, or how much more there is, or what the four things after that one were.”

“No,” he said. “I am not.”

“Good,” said Ning Yan. “Then it works.”

She turned round and looked at the top of the stair with her left hand on the rail, and the grey went down past her into the yard where the washing crew were out at the trough at the second hour because they were always out at the trough at the second hour, and the nine of them had grey in the backs of their coats and grey on their arms and they were working.

“You have never once done that in four months,” she said.

“No,” he said. “You are the first person who has ever been able to stop me and the reason you are able to is that I cannot leave.”

“That is not the reason.”

“No,” he said. “It is not the reason. Do not get used to it.”

## Keeping

She sat down on the eighth step because her legs had started to argue about the right arm, and she put the token out on the stone beside her knee and looked at it, and then she did the thing she had come up here to do.

“Right,” she said. “Here is what I am going to do with a life that has just been made visible, and I am doing it now because it is four months overdue and because you are the only instrument in the world I do not have to check.”

“That is a compliment.”

“It is a job description. Listen to it.”

She told him three things, in order, in the voice she used for reading tallies off a wash board to eleven women who did not want to hear them.

“One. I am keeping you. Not forgiving you. Employed. If you are going to be a voice in my head for the rest of this then I am going to say what the work is, out loud, in a room, where you can be held to it.”

“Two. The work is my brother. *Assessed, reassigned, unreconciled*, above the authority of a furnace, in a hand nobody at Qinghe recognises, reassigned at thirteen and alive, because he is alive — I know he is alive, he was on the Warden's post with a white stone on it and that post is a list and a list does not carry dead names.”

“Ning Wu in a book, in ink
Ning Wu in a book, in ink, where somebody has to look at it. That is the whole of the work. It is not a favour and it is not forgiveness and it is the only thing I have ever asked anybody for.”

“Three. You do not decide anything about me again without telling me first. Not after. Not as a kindness. Not because it works and you can see that it works.”

He was quiet.

“That is a rule and I have never made one before and I am making it now and you will hate it.”

“I will hate it,” he said.

“Good.”

She picked up the token and turned it over, and the three names on the back of it were cut shallow and had been cut by somebody who was tired.

“And I am going to use your name once,” she said.

He went completely still, which is not a thing a voice can do.

“You said not to use it lightly.”

“I am using it lightly.”

“Then do not use it at all.”

“Yuan Sheng,” said Ning Yan.

He did not answer for four seconds, which was the longest answer she had ever had off him.

“I said once,” she said, “because a man who is only ever spoken to as a number has nobody to come back to, and I have been a number in six books in four months, and I am not going to let it be six. That is the whole reason. Use it when it is useful and not before.”

“That was not light.”

“No,” she said. “It was not. I was wrong about it. I will do it again and I will be wrong about it again, and that is a habit you are going to have to live inside, and I am sorry.”

He made a sound that in a body would have been almost a laugh.

“I am going to live inside it for as long as you keep me,” he said.

“You are. That is the arrangement now.”

She got up off the eighth step with her left hand and went down four steps and stopped, because the whole of the inside of her had gone quiet.

Not the way it had gone quiet in a cart, and not the way it had gone quiet for four seconds on a floor in front of nine hundred people.

It had gone the way a room goes when the person in it leaves it and does not shut the door.

She stood on the fifth step of the Nine Steps Terrace in grey snow with an iron token in her hand and waited.

Four hours, it turned out. She did not know that then, and she spent the first hour making sure there was nothing wrong with her, because there was not: the ash was going to stop in two days, there was a signature on a document in four county books, there was a word on her brother's record that she had bought, and somewhere in the machinery of the world a room had just gone over to the side of a question she could not hear.
