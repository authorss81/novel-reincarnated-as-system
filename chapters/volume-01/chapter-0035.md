# The Furnace Floor

Inside the barred records room the air changed before the door moved.

Ning Yan stood with the book bound in her coat and the lamp low, stage nine steady, consent given, waiting for the spend. Her goal was simple and held her upright. She needed to get out alive with the book.

Resistance was the Hall's men, not Qiu. She heard them above on the furnace floor, boots, orders in Bureau cadence, iron tools. They were going to open the sub-basement by force and take paper and girl together. Qiu was not down there and did not know this was happening. That fact mattered for the rest of his life.

The door shuddered. Dust fell from stone. Somewhere above a furnace mouth roared. The lamp flame leaned sideways with the change in air, and her shadow threw itself across the shelves of old paper that would burn in a breath if flame touched it.

Yuan spoke, present, braced.

“I begin. I hold you. I spend.”

Warmth gathered at her palm, doubled, strange, the through-hand lean she knew from the dormitory, stronger now, frightening. Her hand lightened. Her breath quickened. Settlement rose like water behind a dam.

Then other boots came fast, alone, down the furnace stair. A voice she knew, raised, past tense, furious in a way kindness rarely sounded.

“Stopped there. You held your order. This floor was mine.”

Qiu. Come down on his own authority, without escort, without permission from the Hall's session. She heard him place himself between the Hall's men and the records room door, heard the shift of bodies, heard the argument begin.

“He ordered them back,” the shift leader said later. “He told them the room held tally girls and old paper, and if they wanted it they went through him.”

She stood inside stone and had to decide whether to let him. The man she came to destroy, saving her from his own institution. Letting him meant owing him. Not letting him meant shouting through iron to send him away, which would kill them both.

She let him. She put her palm flat to the door and said, low, past tense, so only stone heard, “Stayed. You stood. I allowed it.”

And she did not forgive him, and she did not tell him why, and both of those were choices.

The argument above turned. The Hall's man read the scheduling order. Qiu answered with forty years of furnace authority, with names, with quota numbers, with the weight of a department head who knew every pipe. He lost. Deliberately. She heard it in the way his voice gave ground, point by point, yielding the door while holding the men back minutes, each minute bought with standing.

He was not a coward and he was not a martyr. He was a department head who decided to lose this argument, on purpose, to give the girl below time.

Yuan spoke, awed, present.

“He stands. He loses. I honour.”

The door shuddered again. Iron screamed. Heat poured through the widening gap, furnace floor light, orange and white, roaring. Hands reached. She moved, book tight, lamp abandoned, through-hand burning up her arm now, foundation shifting like earth before a slide.

She went toward heat with Qiu's voice overhead shouting for lamps, for water, for room, covering her exit with procedure, and she understood the reversal. Rescue came complicated beyond comfort, from the man whose floor fired people, who remembered five names, who let the sixth go upward.

She climbed out of the sub-basement into roar and smoke with the book in her coat and did not look at his face. Looking would have made it forgiveness, and it was not.

The spend was about to happen and she did not know it yet, only that warmth turned to fire and the floor tilted and somebody caught her before iron did.
