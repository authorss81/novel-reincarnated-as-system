# What It Cost

The laundry room two days later held cold and the smell of lye.

Ning Yan lay on a pallet behind the tubs with the book in her coat and could not get the coat open. Her goal was small and entire. She needed to read the page.

Resistance was her own body. She could not hold the brush. She could not get her feet under her. Her hands shook too badly to work string. Breath came shallow, twenty-four to the minute, catching at the centre of her chest where something had cracked all the way through. The pallet straw bit through thin cloth. Lye burned her nose. Every swallow hurt. When Du Hong found her, hidden by the crew boy's word, he opened the coat for her, gently, the way people handled the hurt, and did not speak at what he saw.

A second scar ran from left shoulder to hip, thin, red, crossing the old one. Permanent, in public skin.

She sat with help and read the page anyway.

Body-Forging stage four. Not eight. Four. Her aperture had cracked the whole way through and would not heal. Nothing inside the bound repaired apertures. The four months from the Verge were nothing next to what this took. The missing knowledge was different and worse. It was not four months of practice. It was everything she learned between stage four and stage eight, and the Nine-Step went with it, habit and all, except fragments that surfaced like drowning hands.

The Foundation tax was not a share. It was a withdrawal, and it took what was on top. The newest strength. The latest knowing. The order built last. She understood it with her body the way she understood stage and scar, and the understanding would hold for the rest of the book.

Yuan spoke, present, low with grief and coherence.

“I am here. I am loud. I am more than half settled. I pay with you. I remain.”

His Settlement spiked to over half. She felt it as weight and heat, coherence bought with her foundation. He was louder now, continuous, readable, and the loudness frightened them both.

She looked at the page in her lap. The reassignment line. Her brother's three words. *Assessed, reassigned, unreconciled.* And the signature below, small, fair, Bureau hand, in strokes nobody at Qinghe recognised. Feng would recognise it in Volume 5. Here it was only unknown, which was worse.

She did not shout. She asked him, quite calmly, what she used to be, and he told her, and she said what was hers.

“Were nine,” she said, past tense, even. “You told me. Three days past. Stage nine on the flats.”

“I tell true. You stand at nine. You burn bright. You fall.”

“Am four now,” she said. She touched the new scar through cloth. “That was mine.”

Silence. Lye smell. Cold through shutters. Du Hong weeping quietly by the tubs, trying not to show it.

“I hold it. I carry the debt. I do not excuse.”

She folded the page copy into the log with hands that would not steady, in her poor hand that clerks could not read quickly, rubbish-camouflage holding. The book itself she wrapped for Feng. The tablet lay against skin, never comfortable, worn throughout, remembered, not described again.

She was permanently weaker for the rest of the book and every volume would say so. Stage nine to stage four in one spend. Aperture cracked through. Second scar. Settlement over half. Signature unknown.

The Assembly stood nineteen days off and she was at stage four with a cracked aperture and could not ascend without a seal, and there was no seal for her.

She lay back on the pallet with the log against her ribs and the cost read calm, and asked the only question left.

“Kept the book,” she murmured, past tense, to the voice that spent her. “What do we do with a body that will not hold what it held?”

[Through-hand expenditure is logged. The foundation pays. The aperture breaks.]
