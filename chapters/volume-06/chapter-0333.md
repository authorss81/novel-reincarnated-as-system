# The Clearing She Bought

The head at the low drain on the far side of the elms is about a hundred and ten bricks, a foot and a half of footing on the low side, and two days of the board’s four men.

She had it done in two days on the seventh of a month and there was one box against the wall of the open end of the building waiting for her, and she carried nine holds in her left hand into that yard on the morning of the ninth and carried eight out of it, and she did not find that out at the drain.

She found out at the gate.

## What Came Up Through The Base

Nothing came up, and nothing had come up since the twenty-fourth of the last month, and she did not stand in a gateway being disappointed about it.

Her base stayed quiet all day. The ring kept burning two miles west and there was no place in her where anything went out through the ground, and on the ninth of a month she took a box of dressed stone off a wall nine miles east of a mill with four fingers and a thumb on her right hand and eight holds on her left.

She had been two days in a ditch a hundred yards from a bar that would take the weight of her without her asking it to.

What she had not had was a reason to know whether the hold had gone. The cold had been the thing that told her anything had happened at all, and it had been gone since the twenty-fourth of the last month, and she had spent three days on this job in the middle of that not knowing.

## What She Checked, In The Order She Always Checked

The right arm, which opens half the time.

The aperture, which is cracked through and has been since a stage in a year she has never had to name.

The four fingers and the thumb.

The left hand.

She did all of that at about the seventh hour on the ninth of a month at the yard gate, standing up, in the ordinary order, with a box standing against the wall behind her and the man behind the stack about nine feet off going on with his own afternoon.

Then she put her left hand on the rim of the box and closed it, twice, hard, the way you close it on a gate post in a wind.

Eight.

She had nine at the fifth hour that morning and she had watched her own hand do this about four thousand times in four years, and it had not occurred to her until that afternoon that a hand could lose one of a thing and look exactly the same doing it.

## The Part That Is Arithmetic

Nine of eleven at the first of a month.

Eight of eleven at the ninth.

That is the whole of the arithmetic and it takes as long as a man ties a gate shut, standing up.

What it meant was not a number. What it meant was that every hold she has left is a thing that can be spent once, and she had known that since she was nine years old and had never spent one, and in five months on that ground she had spent three and was holding eight.

Six lines on the board at the yard on the ninth of a month.

Eight holds in her left hand.

And that is not a ratio. That is two different things and she stood at the gate and made herself finish the sum in the ordinary voice, in daylight, to nobody, because if she did not say it out loud it would come out as something else later on.

A listing is not priced by what she can hold. A listing is two days and a box, and the box is what the work was made of, and the box is the same box whether her hand has eight holds in it or nine or eleven, and the days are the days the ground wants.

The board does not go down.

That had been true since the third of a month back and she had said it out loud at a ditch and been glad of it, and she was still glad of it standing at this gate on the ninth of a month with eight holds and a finished job behind her.

She had bought a clearing she could not have cleared before, and she had paid for it with the thing that took the fire and then took the answering, and now it had taken a hand.

## What She Wanted To Know

She wanted to know whether eight holds would clear the next line off that board, and the answer was no, and she worked that out about nine minutes later standing in the same gateway and it was not a complicated sum.

A head at a low drain is about a hundred and ten bricks under one arm at a time, on a footing, in a bank, with a bar.

She had done it in two days and she had been slower on the second day than the first, and she had not written any of that down anywhere because there was nothing to write it in.

She was going to do the next one slower than that. And the one after that, slower again. And five more lines were coming down from the same place over the next two months, and after that the yard goes quiet until the season turns, and the whole arrangement worked on the fact that a woman with four fingers and one arm at half could put her hand on a thing and shut it.

She had shut eight of eleven of them and there were six lines on a board three miles east and she was eighteen years old.

## What She Did About It

She went back to the bench where the listing man was squaring a board and she asked him one question, and she asked it in daylight in the ordinary voice and she asked it exactly once.

“How many of these are there at once.”

He looked at the board for a while.

“Six,” he said. “There are six lines on it and there will be six on it when you come back, and there are five more coming down from the same place over the next two months, and after that it goes quiet until the season turns.”

“That is eleven.”

“That is eleven,” he said, “and I am not going to tell you what eleven is to a hand, because I do not know what it is and I have been wrong about a thing once this month.”

She had heard him refuse to guess at something above him once before and had believed him both times.

“All right.”

“I will tell you what I will tell you,” he said, and he put the chalk down on the bench and squared the board with his thumb. “You took two lines off this board in a month and you cleared both of them and I have had a yard that did not lose a stone. Every woman who has ever taken a line off this board cleared the one she took. That is true and it is not going to stop being true because your hand has changed.”

## The Plain Part

He was right.

He was right and he was telling her about the yard and not about her, and there was not one other thing in the world she could have said to him that would have been better, and she stood at the bench and listened to a man tell her, correctly and without any kindness in it at all, that the world was going to be exactly the same size for her as it had been for the last one.

That is what it costs to have eight holds where you had eleven.

Not the strength. The size of the thing in front of you.

She walked west with the box she could not carry on eight and carried it on three loads over two days, and she carried the first load about a mile before she put it down and stood in the road and found out that her hand had told her the weight of it in a way it had told her every day of four years, and had told her it correctly.
