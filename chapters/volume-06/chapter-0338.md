# The Room With Doors

It is not the same place as the last time.

That is the first thing he is certain of, standing in it, and he checks it the way he checks everything now, from the outside in, letting the terms come to him instead of going to them.

The last time there had been lines going out in all directions and entries hung on them the way washing is hung, and a board at the far end, and a hand with a paste pot that did not know what it carried. That is a field. That is the layer’s own reading, and it is the whole width of a person seen from above by something that has no eyes.

This is a room.

There is a floor. There is a table. There is a chair pushed back from the table at an angle that a man pushed it back at and did not push it in again. There are two doors, and both of them are shut, and one of them has a handle on it.

There is a window at the far end with the shutters open and nothing on the other side of the window, which he notes, because everything in this place is arranged to look like a building and is not one.

## The Hand On The Table

A hand with a brush.

It is not the same hand. The hand on the board at the far end of the last place was a keeper’s, and it was going to paste a strip with wet paste and it did not know what the strip carried, and it went home along a track afterwards with an empty paste pot.

This hand is writing.

It is writing with a brush and it is slow, and it is doing what he has watched people do in rooms with a floor above them for four years: it is making a thing on a sheet that will afterwards be correct.

On the sheet there are two columns.

The left-hand column is a running column of days. Five months of them, dates down the margin, hours against them, a season marked at each end, and at the top of the column a name he can read.

NING YAN. OF LOWWATER, and it is at the top of a column in a hand that has never once been her own.

He has never seen that name written.

He has never seen that name written. He has read it four thousand times in a layer and he has never once seen a person put it down on anything.

Under that column there are two more lines, and he looks at both of them, and the first of them is hers again and it says material and it has a place against it and a rule underneath with nothing at the end of it.

The second of them is his.

## What It Says About Him

He reads it the way he reads everything, standing still, letting the terms come.

A mark at the head of the column that is not a name. Four figures. A rule.

And then, under the rule, the word.

HELD.

Not *entered*. Not *in the line*. Not *readable from outside*.

HELD.

A column drifts without something to hold it. That is what a holding is, and every book on nine days of road has one for every column in it, and a column with a holding under it is a column that somebody can ask about and get an answer to, because a man who asks about a person is asking about the person the person is held for.

He has been a line since the seventeenth of a month five months back.

A line is a thing that gets found.

A holding is a thing that can be asked for.

And the difference between the two is that nobody has to walk to a road to ask for one.

## The Terms On The Table

The hand finishes a figure and turns the sheet about a quarter round, the way a man turns a thing when he has finished with one part of it and is coming to another, and it lays four fingers on the margin.

The terms come up off the sheet into the room the way terms do, which is all at once and without anybody deciding.

*What it gets.* Her hours and the material against them are held. They do not stop when she stops standing where they are. They continue on the reach after the reach, and they are carried in a book that shuts at the end of a month and is read against other books that shut at the end of a month, and they will go on being read whether she is at a gate or at a ditch or nine miles off on flat ground with a bar on her shoulder.

And the fire. The ring in a shed at a mill that she has to walk two miles to light at the fourth hour of the morning, every morning, for as long as she is the kind of person who walks two miles to light a fire. That will hold by itself. That will not stop because she is somewhere else, and she will not have to be at that shed at the fourth hour for one hour of one day for the rest of her life.

*What it costs.*

He looks at the four figures and the rule and the space under the rule where the price is not written, because it is not written, because prices are not written on the thing that is being paid.

The room does not put it on the table either. The room puts the terms up and waits the way a room waits.

He works it out himself, and it takes him about a minute, and he has been working things out like that since the night he woke up as a voice.

## What Is Left Of Him

There is one thing.

He has been behind it since the seventeenth of a month five months back. It is not a secret and it is not a hiding place; it is the last piece of ground in him that is not on a surface anybody can put a sheet on.

He has been *unnamed*.

He has been a voice in a layer and a line on a board and a thing a room could read, and he has never been a word in a sentence. He has been the whole of what nobody could point at. Five months of standing behind a thing that an office cannot name, on a ground where offices read everything else, and it was the only piece of him that was not findable, and he has never once used it and he has never once thought about it in terms of use.

They do not need to find him.

They need to ask for him.

And you cannot ask for a thing by the name it has refused. If the room cannot write a word for him, the room cannot put that word on a strip and send it up a road and have it come down somebody else’s road with a place at the end of it. A holding has to be held by name.

That is the price and he was told it last night in a shed by a woman who put her palm flat on a strip of paper and told him the price before the word, which is what she has done every time for four years and which is the only arrangement either of them has ever been able to keep.

If he is held, he is named.

If he is named, he is addressable.

If he is addressable, then there is an office in this country that has a piece of paper with a word on it that points at him, and any hand that comes down a road with that word on it will find him, and there is no such thing as a road that does not go where that word goes.

## The Second Part Of It

He looks for the morning.

He has looked for it since the seventeenth of a month five months back and he has looked for it at the rate of about once a day, which is not often enough to stop it hurting and often enough to know it is still gone, and it is still gone.

He is nineteen and he died badly on the wrong side of a sect that was about to take his core, and the last coherent thing he managed was an apology, and he still has the apology, whole, exactly as he left it.

There is no morning under it. There has not been one for five months. Nothing in this room is going to give it back and he is not going to find out again by looking, because looking is a thing you can do twice before it becomes a way of not doing anything else.

So the price is what it is, and it is not the morning. It is not the apology.

It is that after this hour he will not be able to stand in one place on this ground and be nothing that any office on it has a word for.

## What He Says Before He Does It

He has about nine seconds and he uses them, because he is a man who says the price back to the person paying it and there has never once been a time he did not.

“The fire holds without you,” he says. “That is what you are buying. Not your days — your days already hold, they are in a book one flight above a counter. The fire. The four hours of every night you have spent walking two miles to keep a thing you cannot leave.”

He has never been in a shed. He has been in her the way a lamp is in a room, and there is no part of this he cannot see from the inside, and what he sees is a woman at the second hour of the twenty-second of a month on boards in the dark with a piece of cloth folded in three against her ribs.

“And what you are paying for it is me,” he says. “I am not going to be anywhere after this. I have wanted to be somewhere I cannot be reached for about four years and I have got it now and it is the worst thing I have ever bought.”

He is in the room. There are two doors and both of them are shut.

“Being read means being found,” he says. “I said that once, in a room, to a man, about two months ago, and it was the truest thing anybody had ever said to her about the country she is standing in, and it was mine.”

“Being held means being sent for. And being sent for is a thing that happens to men in rooms with doors.”

## The Spend

He goes forward the only way he can go forward, which is to turn his attention toward the line and let it take him.

It comes out of her.

He is not there and he has not been there since before she crossed, and the arrangement is that he reaches her through the reach and the reach is hers and she gives it or she does not, and what he takes is taken out of the ground she is standing on and not out of her hands.

He feels it leave her at about the second hour of the twenty-second of a month in a shed two miles west of a yard, and it is not heat and it is not cold. It is the thing under her base — the part of her that keeps a fire lit in a room she has to walk to — and he takes it, and it does not come back, and there is nothing in the taking that anybody would call an injury.

A shed at a mill, two miles west of a yard, three miles east of a gate.

The ring was banked to a third with a strip of dry cake and four lumps of bought clay in it, and there is no hand in that shed and no hand has been in that shed since the second hour.

The fire comes up anyway.

It comes up against the brick and stands there and fills a corner of the shed to about nine feet, and it will hold at that until the clay in it is gone, and then it will go down and get up again when a woman puts something into it, and it will go on doing both of those things for as long as there is anything in that mill to burn.

## What Is Taken Of Him

It comes after, the way a cost comes after a stage, in the order a thing has to be moved in.

He waits for the morning. It is not there and he does not expect it and that is not the cost.

Then the ground closes.

The last piece of him that was not on a surface anybody can put a sheet on — the unnamed state, the five months of standing behind a word he refused, the only part of this he has that no office on nine days of road had a word for — closes over like water closing over a stone.

It does not hurt the way the morning had hurt. It hurts the way a hand hurts when it has been holding something for four years and puts it down.

And then he feels the other half of it, which is the part he had thought about and had not looked at directly, which is this: he is now *somewhere*.

For five months he has been the only thing on nine days of road with no address. He has been the thing you could not send for, and that was not a liberty and it was not a hiding place and it was the last thing he had that was his, and he has just sold it at the price of a woman’s walk.

There is an office in this country with a word on a sheet that points at him.

There is a strip with two columns on it and four lines at the bottom, and the third line says a holding is to be presented in person in a room, and the room is not named on the strip because the room is on the copy, and the copy is somewhere above a counter he has never stood in.

And in about a month the doors of that room will shut, and if there is nothing in it by then, something will be sent.

## What He Says After

The hand at the table goes on writing. It does not look up. It was never going to look up; a hand that writes a column does not know there is a man in it, and that has been true of every hand that has ever written him.

He has been entered once before, on a board at the far end of a place that was not a room, by a keeper with a paste pot who went home along a track and never thought about it again.

This time there is a floor and two doors and one of them has a handle on it, and a man at a table who has just written the word HELD in a hand that is the same hand in four hundred figures, and that man is going to go on up a road to a room when this sheet is finished, and he is going to carry it up in his own hands.

“I am held,” he says.

Nothing comes back. The layer has said nothing since the first hour and is not going to say anything now.

“You do not get to have me twice,” he says, “and you had the other one free, and you gave it away for a fire in a shed, and I am not sorry. I want that said in the room.”

## What Is Left In The Room

The row does not come down past him here. There is no row and there is no field and nothing is hung on a line. There is a table and a chair pushed back at an angle and a hand with a brush going over four figures a second time to see that they are right.

Then the hand stops.

It stops for about a second and a half, the way a hand stops when it has found that the thing it is copying is not only its own column, and it turns the sheet about a quarter round again and looks at what is under the days, where two rules have been ruled in a book that shuts on the thirtieth, and neither of the two has anything at the other end of it.

The hand has found the two lines with no end on them. The hand will deal with them later, the way a hand deals with everything, in the ordinary course, in the room above, where it is copied and read against other things and nothing comes back down.

It picks the brush up again and finishes the figure.

The room is a room. The doors are shut. Two miles west of a yard, at the second hour of the twenty-second of a month, a woman has just got off the boards and is standing with her palm flat on a cold stone by a door that tells her nothing, and she will find out in about four days what she has bought, and she will find it out from the fire, and she will not be able to give it back to him or take it back from herself.

That is the whole of the hour, and it cannot be undone, and nothing in this room was done to him.
