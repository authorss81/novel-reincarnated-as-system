# Lift, Set Early, Move By Hand

There was a rhythm in the far gully that had nothing to do with the size of the stone and she had it in her body before she had put the bar over her shoulder, and it came up out of four years at a bank nine days west where a woman with a scar across the back of both hands had put her down on the light stone and said four words at her and then let her find out the rest by working.

Lift with the furnace. Set early. Move by hand.

## What It Was

The middle bank was lighter stone and tighter footing and the men there worked a rhythm a woman had to learn by watching, and it was not strength. It was that you never waited.

You did not decide where a stone went and then lift it. You took the bar and got under the stone and you lifted and the stone came, and you set it down a foot before the place it was going, and you went back and moved it by hand the last foot with your arm open and your fingers flat on it.

That was the whole of it. A loaded reach that would be heavy if you let it be heavy and was not heavy at all if you never once let it be heavy, and a breath that went to five going up and came back to four with the stone on the crest.

It had been in her body since she was fourteen, and there had been nothing on nine days of road that weighed anything until a footing came off a board about eight days before, and she had known for about a fortnight that it was going and had asked for the footing at the far gully out loud in a doorway at the fourth hour for exactly that reason.

## The First Day On The Middle

She got about four feet of the eleven in on the first day and it took the whole of the day, and the whole of what the day was made of was the four feet.

The stone came up out of the face in pieces about the size of two flat loaves and she got the bar under each of them and lifted and the stone came, and she set it down a foot before the place it was going and went back for the last foot and moved it by hand with her arm open and her fingers flat on it, and the next bar went under the next stone before the last stone had stopped moving.

That is the whole of it and it is not strength.

A man who waits for a piece to come out of a bank before he thinks about where it goes is working twice as hard and getting the same result, and a man who sets it down and then stands and looks at it has stopped for about a minute, and there were eleven feet of that bank.

## The Second Day

She got about seven feet of the eleven done on the second day and put nine pieces back into the bank in a line she did not have to think about, and the ninth piece went in on the crest with about as much noise as a hen turning over, and she stood with her hand on it and counted her breath.

Four. Four. Five.

Five at the third count going up and four on the top with the bar off her shoulder and her arm down at her side, and it had been that way since the sixteenth of a month a fortnight before and it was not going to be anything else.

## What She Felt About It

She was not afraid and she was not tired in the way she usually was tired, and the thing she felt, standing on the crest of a four-foot bank at about the seventh hour with nine dressed pieces in a bank and the bar in her left hand, was closer to pleasure than anything she had felt on that ground in five months.

She could not remember the last time she had felt it and she was fairly sure nobody on nine days of road had seen it, because there was a rule about how a woman stands on a bank and she was standing the way she had been taught and the rule did not have a word in it for this.

Nobody on nine days of road was paying her for this.

That was the other half of it and she stood on the crest and let the whole of it arrive. She was not being paid by the reach at the gate, where a third of a reach was one and a half coppers and the reach went down again at whatever she took next. She was being paid by the job, and the job did not get smaller when she did, and the job was measured by eleven feet of bank and not by her arm.

And in about two months the board would be bare.

And in about four days this would be the last footing on nine days of road she was ever going to be able to do, and she did not know that yet and would find it out on the twenty-third of a month on open ground with the bar lying on the grass, and what she knew on the crest of that bank was only the pleasure and a bad feeling in her about the pleasure.

## The Box, And What Was In It

The board paid in what the job was made of, and this job was made of the bank it was a cut in, so the box was the stone and the clay that came up out of it together, and she got it down at the crest at about the sixth hour with about four hours of light left and about nine pieces in it that she could not lift and had to roll to the edge one at a time.

A box is about four days of the ring. That had been the figure since the summer and it had not changed and it was the only number on nine days of road that had been going up instead of down.

She stood at the top of that bank at the seventh hour with the bar across her knees and her breath at four and her left hand closed on nothing.

## The Man At The Box

She got the box down at about the sixth hour on the third day and the man behind the stack was at the cart with the boards already on it.

He looked at the bank and not at her.

“That is good work,” he said. “That is the way it wants to go back in and nobody on this ground has asked me that about anything in eleven years.”

“That is not work,” she said. “That is a bar in one hand and four fingers on the other.”

“That is what good work is,” he said, and got the bar up onto the cart bed and leaned it against the side.

## What She Wanted In The Dark

She wanted the footing to be the heavy one. She wanted the stone at the top of the bank to be the heaviest stone on that ground and her to be the only person on nine days of road who could put it back, and she wanted to be asked, in daylight, by a man with a bench, to put it back.

What she got was two days and a box and a gate that paid a third of a reach, and the pleasure went out of it somewhere between the sixth hour and the seventh and did not come back, and what was left was the ordinary arithmetic she had been doing since the eighth of the ninth of the month before last.

Eight holds. Five counts. A third of a reach.

Six of the eleven were somebody else’s before the season turned, and the board would not find out that she had run out.
