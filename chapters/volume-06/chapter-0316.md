# It Came Twice

The cold came up through the base twice more on the eighth and the ninth of a month, and the second time it sat in her for a long time, and she had nowhere to put herself while it did.

She was at the gate both days and she worked half a reach both days and she did not tell Pei anything about it, and at the seventh hour on the ninth she sat down on the stone kerb at his gate with the bar across her knees and waited for it to be finished.

It was finished at about nine minutes.

## It Is Not Fear

That was the first thing she wanted to put down plain, and it took her two days to be sure of it.

It is not fear.

She had been under a bank four minutes in the dark in the last month of the last month with nobody within a mile who would have come, and she knew exactly what fear was and where it sat in her, and this was not sitting there.

This came up from underneath and went through her and did not stay, and the part of her that was watching it was entirely calm and entirely awake and counting.

Under the gate post, with the light off the field and the boy gone inside:

“It comes off a listed thing when the listed thing is finished,” he said, “and I want a line, and I tell you what it is for before I spend it.”

“Take it.”

## What It Is For

That was the rule and she had watched him break it in other directions for four years and she waited for it, sitting on a stone kerb with her bar across her knees, and what she got instead of the rule was a line.

One line, in a record an office reads, that the work at the lane crossing was cleared by the person whose days are in the book on the floor above a counter two miles and four hundred yards west. Nothing else on it. No figure and no rate and no date on it.

It goes up with the rest of them and it cannot be un-put.

She asked him the thing she had needed to ask and the answer was the answer it had been every time.

There was no room on that ground where a job taken off a board got into a book with her name on it. The man at the yard hung it on a board. The board was a board.

That is the whole of what he can do and she had known that for four years and had never once had a reason to use it, and the reason was a head and an apron at a lane crossing that she had laid with one arm under a man’s board and been paid for in a box that was standing against a wall with nothing written on it.

He did not say anything else. The arrangement was that he said the thing before and not after, and she had been getting the same arrangement out of him for four years, and she sat on the kerb for about nine minutes after it with her hands on the bar and did not ask him a second question about it.

## The Price Of A Line

That was the morning of the tenth and she stood on the flat about a mile east of the mill with the bar on her shoulder and the cold coming up under her, and the price of one line is not money.

The price of one line is that from the fourth hour of the tenth of a month there is no work of hers in the world that nobody can read.

She had given up the truth of her own days in a room with a brush and four other people in it, and that had been about herself. This was different in the way a second thing is different from a first thing, and she worked out how it was different in about four minutes standing in the road, because it is one thing to have what happened to you written down and it is another thing entirely to have what you did written down, and the second one does not stop at the end of the month.

Her column had a thirtieth in it and then it shut.

This does not have a thirtieth.

## Saying It

She said it out loud on the flat, in the ordinary voice, to nobody, because that is the arrangement and has been for four years.

“Do it,” she said.

She stood there and said the next thing, which was nothing, and the cold came up under her and went through her and did not stay, and after it there was a place in her that was different in the way a place is different when something has been taken out of it and nothing has been put back.

It did not hurt.

That was the part she had not been ready for on the tenth of a month at about the fifth hour on flat ground with a bar on her shoulder. It did not hurt, and it was permanent, and there was nothing in nine days of road that could put a line back out of a record.

## The Walk West

She went west that afternoon and got to the yard at about the seventh hour and stood in front of the board.

There was nothing on it. Her line had been on it for five days and it had been taken down and put in a box at the open end and the box was in the row with nine that had the yard’s mark on the corner of them, and this one had nothing on it at all, and the board had three lines on it that had not been there the last time she had stood in front of it.

She read all three and took none of them, which is a thing she had never done before and which took her about four minutes standing in a gateway with the light going.

She had taken two off that board in a fortnight and both of them had cost her the ring, and she stood there and found out that she could not say which of the three would cost her what, and that not being able to say it was going to be the condition for as long as she took work off walls on that ground.

That is the price of a line too. She said it out loud in the gateway, in the ordinary voice, to three lines of somebody else’s handwriting:

*You do not get to choose what it takes. You get to choose whether you take it.*

She went home past a wheel that had not turned since before she came, and the ring was still out, and the shed was cold, and she slept in it anyway.

## The Second Thing

The second thing was not about her at all and she had not expected it and it arrived on the eleventh at about the fourth hour, standing in the yard at the material shed past the mill with the man behind the stack, and it went into the ring.

That was how she found out.

She went west on the eleventh because the ring was cold and she had a third of a charge in the corner of the shed behind a third of a sack and she wanted to put the third of it in.

It was out.

Not banked. Out. Cold the way the stone kerb outside was cold, and the brick of it grey and the clay in the seam grey, and there was no heat anywhere in it at all, not at the back of it and not in the ring of it and not at the seam where a fire had been banked three times in five months.

She stood in the shed with her hand an inch off it and she could not feel a thing.

## What It Came Out Of

She got that out in about four minutes, sitting on the boards with her back against the wall and the shed door open on the yard.

It came up through the base and it went out through the same base, and the ring is what is under her base, and the ring is the only thing under her base in the world.

So: she had spent a line out of herself, and the furnace of her own had gone out at the same moment, because the furnace of her own is the part of her that the line came out of.

She got up off the boards and stood over it with her arm hanging and did not put her hand on it at all.

A ring that goes out costs nine coppers to light again, and nine coppers at two and a half a day is four days at a gate, and she had two and a half coppers in the front of her coat and no way on that ground of making four days into nine in less than four days.

That is the whole of the arrangement. She did it in the ordinary voice in her own head, in a shed, on the eleventh of a month:

*You clear a listed thing, and it settles, and it is worth a line, and the line is the fire.*
