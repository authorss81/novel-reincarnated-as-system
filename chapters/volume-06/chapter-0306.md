# Is There More

She did not answer it on the twentieth and she did not answer it on the twenty-first, and she went down into Pei’s drain at the fourth hour on both of those days and cleared more than the gate pays for, and that is the whole of the two days.

## The Space Beside It

On the morning of the twentieth she went west at the second hour, because she wanted to see whether the strip was still on the bench.

It was not on the bench.

It was in the book.

It had been put into the book face down at the place where the month sits when there is nothing to write about it, and to the right of it, in the space the house rule keeps for whatever the strip turns out to be, there was a clean square of nothing.

She stood at the counter and looked at it for about as long as a person looks at a thing they have been putting off.

A strip comes off a cart. A strip is read. The answer is written. If there is no answer, the strip goes into the book anyway, because a strip that came a long way does not go back down, and the space beside it stays a space until the ordinary week comes round and a hand is on the pen.

She had known that on the eighteenth and had gone home and worked it out anyway, because knowing a thing and having it sit in front of you on the right-hand page of a book four hundred yards away are two different sizes of problem.

The square of nothing in that book was about the size of four fingers.

She did not ask the man behind the counter anything. Her hand went down flat on the counter on the side she could reach it with, and she said, “The book is open to a blank.”

“It is,” he said.

“All right.”

She went out into the sun with nothing bought and nothing said and two miles of flat road in front of her.

## Three Sentences In Her Own Head

She got them out somewhere about the second mile, walking, and she has never put any of them in writing anywhere and she was not going to.

Say yes and be a running column.

Say no and be a finished person in a book for ever.

Say nothing and let the space be filled by a hand that has never seen her.

That is the whole of what the choice was and she did not add a fourth and she did not need to.

The third one was the one she wanted, and the reason she could not have it was not loyalty to anybody and not fear of anybody.

It was that the space was going to be filled.

And what it was going to be filled with was not a woman who had stopped. It was going to be filled with a shape — a reach that had gone quiet, or a column that had ended, or a house that had shut, whichever of those the man with the pen found most natural on the ordinary week.

And then it would be true.

That was the part she could not get past for two whole days. Everything else about that ground could go ahead and decide something about her, because an office deciding a thing about a woman is what an office is for, and she had known that since the second hour of the seventeenth of a month.

But there was exactly one thing she had ever had that belonged to her alone.

Her days had been true.

That was five months of days in a column she had entered herself, with twelve gaps in it that she had entered herself and explained to nobody, and it was the only object in the world that nobody had ever been able to write a word on.

And the whole of the third option was handing that to a pen she would never see.

## The Ring Going Down

The ring went down on the night of the nineteenth and it went down further on the twentieth, and she banked it lower both nights because there was nothing to bank it with.

A banked fire is not a fire.

She had known that since the first time she had tried it, about four months back, in a yard four hundred yards past the post, when she had put three handfuls of ash on a ring and told herself it would hold until the light and had watched it not hold.

What it did instead was sit there giving off about enough to warm a hand and no more, and a hand is not a body, and a body that has been warm for four years and is not warm now is a body that has been told something by arithmetic.

She did not open the ring. She had nothing to open it with. She sat on the boards at the back of the shed with her hand about a span above the bricks and she felt the heat come off the clay and she counted the difference between that and the heat that came off it in the morning of the sixteenth, and the difference was the whole of what she had spent in two days.

The first time she had lit it properly she had done it out of wages at five coppers a day, a day at a time, for five months.

The second time she had done it out of what was left in her fist after bread and material.

The third time was going to have to be out of something else, and she had not yet worked out what, and there were three options in that too and she had not counted them.

## The Thing Under The Ring

There was a thing she had known since the nineteenth of a month four months back and had never once said out loud, and two nights of a banked ring had brought it up out of her the way water comes up out of a floor.

The ground under the ring does not give anything back.

Not a little. Not over five months and three firings and two days out of a column of her own and twenty-nine coppers on a promise. Nothing at all, and not slowly, and not in a way a person could write down and call it a return.

She had gone out and dug it out twice and spread it on a flat stone and looked at it and put it back, and she had done it on the seventh of this month with her own hands in the dark, and it had come down through the light in a flat grey fall and had not settled.

She had not said it out loud on the seventh and she was not going to say it out loud in a shed on the twentieth either, and it was not going to go in her head either, because saying it in her head was the same as saying it out loud with more witnesses.

She said, instead, on the boards, with her hand a span above the bricks: “It is not holding.”

That was the entire sentence and it was about the clay.

## More Than The Gate Pays For

On the twentieth she opened a reach and a half.

On the twenty-first she opened a reach and about half of another one, which is more than she had opened on any day since she started at that gate, including the day she did a whole one in eight feet of wet stone with a man standing on the bank who was not watching her.

She did not go slowly and she did not do it wrong.

She did it the way a woman with a question hanging on her and a ring going down does the one thing that is in front of her, which is to work at it harder than it needs, and the gate pays by the reach and paid her by the reach, and the extra half of a reach on the twenty-first was in no book and bought nothing and belonged to a man whose field had a drain in it that would not have gone on being blocked if she had stopped at the reach.

She told herself on the twenty-first at about the seventh hour that this was the sensible way to spend two days she could not spend any other way, and that was true, and it was also about a third of the reason.

## What She Was Afraid Of

The frightened thing was new and she noticed it was new.

It was not the strip and it was not the book and it was not the man behind the counter, all of whom were inside four hundred yards and could be walked to.

It was a room.

She had been afraid of a great many things in four years. She had been afraid in a hall with no roof and on a bad flat at the far end of a reach and of a word in an announcement in a hall with no roof when she was seventeen, and every one of those had been a place with a floor she was standing on and a door she could see.

She had never been afraid of a room she had not stood in.

There was one above that counter. She had never seen it and could not have drawn it and did not know what was in it, and she had worked out in the last two days that whatever was in it had read her.

## On The Boards

On the second night, in the shed, with the ring banked as low as it goes:

“Three days on a bench with a space beside it,” he said. “That is the shape of a thing being settled by people who are not settling it.”

She put her coat over her knees and did not answer him.

She had built five months on the fact that nothing within nine days of road could be proved about her, and she was about to hand over the proof herself, and there was not one person on that ground she could have handed it to instead.
