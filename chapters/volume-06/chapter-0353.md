# The Weight Goes To The Bar

The low side of Pei’s field is a drain and a drain at four feet deep is a loaded reach, and she had known it was a loaded reach every morning for five months and had gone in at the seventh hour on the twenty-fourth of a month with the bar on her shoulder and the gate open behind her.

She got about nine feet in and the ground came up to a bar’s length under her boots.

She could dig.

She had never once doubted that she could dig and she dug for about an hour and a quarter with the bar in her left hand and the arm that was open doing nothing, and she put the footings out along the near lip where a man puts them so the bank will stand, and every one of those footings went out on the flat with a hand on it.

## What A Reach Is

A reach is not a length. That was the thing she had had in her body for five months and had never once said out loud to anybody.

Five coppers a reach is five coppers for what comes out of that cut and goes over that bank.

And what came out of it went up a bank of four feet, and a piece going up a bank of four feet goes up on the bar with a hand under it, and she had gone up four feet of bank about nine hundred times with the bar in one hand and the stone under the other.

On the twenty-fourth of a month she stood at the bottom of a drain with the bar in her left hand and a footing about the size of a flat loaf under her right arm and she could not get the bar under it.

The bar went in. The bar came up. And the stone went back into the hole where it came from, because there was nothing else for it to do.

## What Pei Did

He came out at about the eighth hour with his own bar and did not look at her face and did not say one word about it, which she noticed, and then he said four.

“That is a fifth of a reach,” he said. “You took nine feet out of it and you got about four feet of it over the bank before noon and the rest of it is in the drain and it is not costing me anything to have it in the drain.”

He set the bar down against the fence.

“Five coppers a reach. A fifth of a reach is one. I am not taking it out of what you do, I am taking it out of what you take over the bank, and the man before you took a whole one every morning and he had two arms.”

He picked the bar up again.

“I have never paid anybody less than what they carried out and I am not starting on you.”

## The Store, Five Miles Off

She went to the store at about the eighth hour on the twenty-sixth with two coppers and came back with bread for four days, which was all she had, and the storekeeper put the four days in a twist of paper and did not say anything about the price.

Two coppers had been bread for seven days for five months. It had been bread for six days since the eighteenth of this month, when the reach went down to a third, and it was going to be bread for five from the twenty-eighth, and she had not asked for it to be any of those things.

There is a store five miles off and a gate three miles off in the other direction and a yard two miles off and the whole of her life on nine days of road was between three points and one copper a day.

## The Sum

One copper a day.

She did it standing at the gate post with the light going off the field, and it took about as long as a man ties a gate shut.

Bread was two a day at the far-side store and she had been eating it about five days in seven and on the other two she had had clay and nothing, which was not food.

One copper a day is five coppers in seven days and bread at two a day is fourteen.

## What She Still Had

The ring in the shed at the mill was lit and had been lit for a fortnight by itself, and there was about a box and a half of clay in the corner of that shed behind it, and the clay was worth three coppers a third of a sack, and the box and a half was about two days of the ring.

The three lines still on the board at the works were cut and ditch and head, and all three of them were measured by length, and she could take all three of them, and she would be paid by what the job was made of, which for a ditch line is the clay out of it.

And the bar was the reason she could not take a stone line, and there was no stone line on the board, and there was not going to be one before the season turned.

## The Afternoon

She worked the second hour to the sixth on the twenty-seventh and the seventh to the ninth on the twenty-eighth and she got a fifth of a reach out of each of them.

She got it up the bank a piece at a time with a hand on each piece and no bar under any of them, and it took her three times as long as it had taken her on the seventeenth of this month when a third of a reach had been one and a half coppers.

On the evening of the twenty-seventh she sat on the boards in the shed with the ring burning nine feet away and did the whole of it out in her head without meaning to, because she had been doing the whole of it out in her head every evening for five months.

Five stages had taken a turn at the end of a bar stroke and the feeling out of both hands and one hold and a longer hour for every line she wrote and a count of breath that did not come back to four where the work was heaviest. Six stages had taken the heavy end and the ramp and a whole day’s digging.

The seventh had taken the bar.

There was nothing on the other side of it. That was the thing she could not get round. She had been going down through them at about a stage a month since the eighth of a month and the stage she was on did not seem to have anything to take, because she could still dig and she could still put a stone down and she could still do a ditch line in four days with a bar in one hand.

It had taken the bar. It had not taken anything she could see because the bar was the only thing in the arrangement that had been hiding the rest of it.

## The Carter

He came for the box off the footing at the far gully at about the sixth hour on the twenty-sixth and put the boards on the cart and roped it and got up on the bed with the bar.

He had been doing this run for nineteen years and he had done it about four hundred times and he was not in any hurry.

“There is a line out west of here about nine miles,” he said, without being asked and without looking at her. “I have driven past the end of it four hundred times and I have never once been let onto it.”

He got the shafts down.

“Men go out on it and come back. They are paid by the day and their families are on it, and in nineteen years I have watched that road from this side and not one of them has ever come off it with anything in his hands.”

He turned the cart about on the flat.

“I do not know what is at the top of it and I am not the man who would find out.”

## What She Wanted

She wanted four days back.

That was the whole of what she wanted on the three-mile walk back with the bar on her shoulder and the shed in front of her, and it was the first time in a long time that she had wanted a thing she could name in four words.

Four days is what four men did the footing in and it took her two, and she had got one copper a day where she had had a day and a half, and she was going to have to stand in a gateway on some morning in about six weeks and look at a stone line and leave it on the wall.
