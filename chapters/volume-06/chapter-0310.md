# Half A Reach

The twenty-fifth came clear and she went down Pei’s bank at the fourth hour with the bar in her left hand and the sun still on the field above her and nobody within two hundred yards of the cut in any direction.

She did it in the middle of a reach because she had been doing it in the middle of a reach for four years and there was no other place on her.

The furnace behind her had been banked since the twenty-first and there was nothing to bank it with, and she had not thought about the furnace once since the twenty-first, and she had been going down into that cut every fourth hour with a bar in her left hand.

That was the whole of the arrangement and it took about as long as a breath goes in and out once.

## The Fourth Hour

The stage came up out of her the way the three before it had come.

There was no announcement. There has not been an announcement past the last working point in four months and there was not going to be one on the twenty-fifth of this month, because there is nobody inside to be told and there is no county book on that ground to take anything down, and the sound of a stage is a sound and a sound has to go somewhere.

It went into the ground under Pei’s field and about a quarter of a mile into the air and that was the whole of it.

She stood at the bottom of the cut in the open with her right hand open on her knee and waited for about four minutes, and nothing came back, and nothing has come back since the second hour of the seventeenth of a month, when eleven men two hundred yards behind her had all of them stopped working at the same moment.

[Ning Yan, past the line, servant-born, under no ceiling any county holds. Golden Core, stage four, is held.]

The layer said it once in the far-side house form and stopped, the way it had said the three before it and stopped, and it has never in four years explained or advised anything and it did not begin on the twenty-fifth.

## The Flat Of The Second Finger

Then the hand told her.

It was at about the fourth hour and it was not a feeling and it was not a warning. It was an instrument that had been going one way for four months changing into an instrument that went another way, and she had been watching it do it the way you watch a thing that is not going to stop.

The flat of a second finger went from longer to longest.

She took the bar out of her left hand and put her right hand flat on the side of the cut and moved the second finger along the stone, once, about four inches, and then put it back.

It was the longest the flat of that finger had ever been, and it stayed that way, and there was nothing in nine days of road that would ever shorten it again.

She worked out what it meant before she got out of the cut, standing up, with the silt going down her sleeve.

Every line she writes anywhere now costs her the hour it is written in twice over.

There was a room one flight above a counter with a brush in it in it at that moment and about nine sheets squared up, and there would be more, and there is not one sheet of paper in nine days of road she cannot get to. Bread at a shop. A line in a book four hundred yards behind her. A gate.

And the yard work she could still price one-handed was half of what it had been when she started down the bank that morning.

## Half A Reach

She came up out of the cut at about the fifth hour and started again from the top of the reach she had been on and she knew what she was doing about a third of the way through it.

That is the part of it that is worth putting down plain, because it is the part that is not frightening and should be.

There was nothing slower anywhere. Not in the leg, not in the back, not in the left arm holding a bar, not in the count of breath, which went at four everywhere it had gone at four that morning and would go at four everywhere after this as well.

A furnace of her own at stage four spends a thing out of the hands and a thing out of the written hour and it does not take anything else. She had had it explained to her by a man with a brush four years ago in a hall with no roof, with his hands moving, and the hands were the last part of it to be explained.

She got half a reach done by the seventh hour and came up out of the cut and stood at the gate with her right arm hanging the way it hangs and her left hand closing on the bar, and the other half of the reach was bank and silt and standing water and she was not going to open it today.

## Two And A Half

He paid her out of the fold of cloth in the inside of the door frame into her own hand with the count done by him out loud.

Two and a half.

That was a reach and a little over at seven, a reach and a little over at five, and half a reach at five, and the three of them were in her hand inside about nine days and she had got there by taking four stages that no county in nine had ever heard of.

She put it in the front of her coat where she kept it and did not do the sum.

## What Was Different About It

She did not have the fear she had at the third.

At the third, in the middle of a reach at the last working point, she had stood still for about four minutes and had not been able to account for a single thing that had happened, and it had taken eleven men two hundred yards behind her to go back to work.

This was not that.

What it was instead was a flat and quite unalarming recognition, standing in a drain bank on the twenty-fifth of a month, that the stage had gone through her and had cost her exactly what she had already spent.

Every stage had done that. That was the arrangement. She had bought three of them out of five months of five coppers a day and had known it every time, and this one had cost her the flat of a second finger going the length of a small man’s finger and nothing else at all that she could put a hand on.

She had gone into that reach at the fourth hour of the twenty-fifth able to price a day’s work with one hand.

She came out of it able to price half of one.

And there was nobody in the world she could tell.

## The Rest Of The Day

She did not open the other half of that reach and she went back up the bank at about the seventh hour and stood at the gate with her right arm hanging and her left hand closed on the bar.

Behind her, about a quarter of a mile east, there was a shed with about nine different things standing on the ground in it and a man who did not come out from behind the stack for a woman, and in three days she was going to stand in the yard of that shed with what a reach and a half used to be able to buy, and find out what a charge of clay and brick costs at a size one arm can carry.

She did not think about that at the gate.

She thought about the flat of a second finger, which was the longest it had ever been, and about the fact that it was going to be that long every day for the rest of her life, and about the fact that in a room one flight above a counter there was a brush and a stack and about nine sheets squared up and a hand that would take eleven more of them off that stack.

And then she went back down the bank at the eighth hour and levered about a foot of silt off the top of the reach she had left half done, and went home to a shed with a ring in it that had not been lit since the twenty-first.
