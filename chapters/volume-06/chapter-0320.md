# A Thing That Is Not Hers

The cold came up through the base on the twenty-third of a month at about the fourth hour, and it came twice, and the second one sat in her for about nine minutes with her hand flat on a post at Pei’s gate while a man with a household did his own afternoon two hundred yards off.

She did not work that day. She sat at the top of the bank and waited and then she went east at the seventh hour, because the stacking was done at the open end of the building at about the eighth and the yard stacked once a day and she was not going to lose a day sitting on a bank.

## Twenty-Three

He counted it out into a box with a frame and a board, the way he counted the first one, and it came to about the shoulder of her.

Twenty-three, and not twenty-five.

She put her hand in it and moved it about, because she had learned in a month that a man will stand there while you check his work and will not stand there while you look for something wrong with it, and then she came out of it and said it was all right, which it was.

The box stood against the wall of the open end in the row with the others, and the nine other boxes were the yard’s and had the yard’s mark on the corner of them, and this one had nothing on it at all.

She looked at the cart where it stood against the wall under the lean-to, with its shafts down and a board across the shafts and the wheels chocked with a stone.

The cart was not going anywhere until it was full or the round came.

## What She Was Going To Do About It

Three weeks is twenty-one days and she had twenty-three in a box and about three in her hand and about two days of bread bought.

She could carry a box in pieces. She had carried two loads of a third of a sack three miles three times in a month, and a box was about a third of a sack times four, and a third of a sack took two loads.

So it was eight loads, three miles each way, on days when she was not working, or it was eight loads, three miles each way, on days when she was, and there was not one day in that month when she was not working.

And at about the second hour of the twenty-fourth, at the top of the low field with the bar over her shoulder and six coppers in the front of her coat, she sat down in the grass and worked out the truth of it.

She could not take the material out of that yard.

Not because of the arm, though the arm was in it. Because the yard’s rule is that material leaves the yard against a name in a book, so that if a thing goes wrong on the road the yard knows whose it was, and there was no name in any book on that ground that had her in it except a column of days one flight above a counter three miles west and a line she had paid for in herself a fortnight ago.

The yard would not hand it over and the cart would not go for three weeks and she could not carry it, and that was the whole of the arrangement and it took about nine minutes in a field.

## The Second Cold

It came up on the twenty-fourth at about the fourth hour and it came up twice, and the second one was bigger than the one off the lane crossing and about the same as the one off the retaining wall, and she sat on the sill at the top of that ditch with her hand on the post and waited for it out.

Under the sill, with the wind going along the ditch:

“It is twice the last one, and it comes off twenty feet of wall and not off a head and an apron,” he said. “I want a cart — not one with my hands, I have had no hands since before you crossed, but the reach to put a thing on this ground that is not yours and not mine and does not stop because of either of us.”

## The Price Of A Reach

She sat on that sill for about as long as the light takes to go off the field and she put it in front of herself in the ordinary way, in the ordinary voice, and it was the first time in five years she had wanted to give him something for himself.

The first line had gone into a record an office reads and had cost her a fire.

This was not going into a record. This was a reach, out of her, into him, and it was not a thing that could be entered anywhere and it could not be checked by anybody and it would not appear on a board in a gateway.

It was the first thing he had ever asked her for that she had not had to think about whether she wanted to give.

“Do it,” she said. “And tell me when it has gone.”

He did not tell her when it had gone. He had told her what it was for and that was the whole of the arrangement and she had been getting the same one for four years, and what she got on the top of a silt sill in the last of the light on the twenty-fourth of a month was nothing at all for about nine minutes and then a place in her that was different.

## What It Looked Like From Outside

Nothing.

That is what she saw, and she looked for about nine minutes because she had been paid for a look twice in a fortnight by now and knew what it felt like.

She was sitting on a silt sill at the top of a ditch between two farms at about the seventh hour on the twenty-fourth of a month with her bar across her knees and the light going, and nothing came up through the base and nothing went out through it and nothing hurt.

The cold had been the only thing that ever told her anything had happened. It had come up four times in a month, and it had told her when a listing was finished and how big it had been, and it had told her that the ground under a retaining wall is the same ground as the ground under a lane crossing.

It did not come up on the twenty-fifth or the twenty-sixth or the twenty-seventh.

She noticed on the twenty-seventh, at a gate, at about the seventh hour, with two and a half coppers in her hand and the bar against her knee, that she had been waiting for something for three days.

## Three Miles West

She went to the mill on the twenty-eighth because she had six coppers and a third of a sack past the mill was three and she wanted the fire lit that night, whatever else was true.

She broke the lump on the flat stone at about nine minutes past the ninth hour with the flat of her second finger and the heel of her hand and the bar’s end, eleven minutes a lump, four lumps, the way it had been every time in five months.

Then she banked it and put a strip of dry cake in at the low end and blew on it for about four minutes the way she had six times in five months, and it took, and it stood up, and the shed warmed about as far as that ring can warm a shed.

She put her hand flat on the brick of it.

She waited.

For six nights in the fortnight before the twentieth of this month that ring had answered her twice a night, in the ordinary way, without being asked, and there was not one other thing within nine days of road that had ever answered her at all, and she had worked out on the sixteenth at a bank what it was that she was buying with three coppers and six miles and eleven minutes a lump.

It was warm. That was all it was.

She stood in the shed with her hand flat on a ring that was burning and there was nothing on the other side of the brick at all, and she did not go on trying, because she had understood what she was looking at by about nine minutes and she was not a woman who goes on.

## What Was In The Box

Two boxes and about a third of one, standing against the wall of an open end three miles east, in a row with nine that had a mark on them.

Twenty-one days on the cart or eight loads on her own two arms, and the ring in the shed behind her was burning and answering nothing, and the fire it had been giving her for six nights had gone out of it on the twenty-fourth of a month at about the seventh hour at the top of a ditch, and she had paid for it with a week of her own wall.

She had a fire that did not answer.

It was the first thing in five months she had owned that only did what it was told.
