# The Book On The Thirtieth

The thirtieth of a month came up and she did her half reach at Pei’s gate as usual, and then she went east with the bar on her shoulder, because a thirtieth is the one day in that ground on which a book shuts and there was nothing else anybody would be doing.

The tray was on the bench in the mill yard at about the eighth hour with the cord in the man’s hand and the book in it showing at the edge, and he was doing what he had done twice since the first of a month before this one.

She stood about nine feet off and watched him put the cord on.

## What A Thirtieth Is

A book that shuts clean on the thirtieth is a finished book.

It goes in a tray and the tray goes on the first cart up the road to the counting house, and everything that goes up that road gets read against everything else that went up it, and nothing comes back down. She had that in five sentences on the eighth of a month in a shed, from a man working at a bench between two trays, and it had not changed and was not going to.

Four trays since she had walked out past a wheel that had not turned.

The first had gone up in the month after she came past the mill, with two months of days in it and the word above the hours that said she was good. The second had gone on the thirtieth of the month before that one. The third had gone on the thirtieth of the month before this one. The fourth was on the bench in front of her with a cord going round it and a knot in the man’s teeth.

She had been in four trays and she would be in one every month for as long as she kept working, and there was not one thing on nine days of road that could take her out of them, and she had known that since the third of a month in this yard in a gateway.

What was new was that she was about to be in one from the inside.

## What She Took Back Up The Road

She left the book on the bench at about the eighth hour and watched the man put the knot in the cord, and then she went up the road to the counting house and stood at the counter on the side she could reach with a whole hand.

There was one thing she had wanted to ask since the twenty-eighth of the month before this one and she asked it once, in the ordinary voice, in daylight, with a man behind the counter and six of his men at their cups.

“Where does my material go.”

He put his pen down flat.

“Here,” he said.

## What That Meant

Not here. Not the counter. Here, the book.

“It has to be here,” he said. “Everything that is a fact about a person is in that one, and that is the one that shuts on the thirtieth, and the one that keeps it does not shut and never has and never will.”

He turned the seasonal book about a quarter round and put his thumb on it.

“A day ends. A day has a beginning and an end and the end of it is on the thirtieth and that is the whole of what this book is. Material does not end. There is always more of it owed against a ground and more of it paid on it, and that book has been open since before I came here and it will be open when I am gone. That is what it is for.”

“So mine goes in that one.”

“Copies of yours go in this one,” he said. “Everything the other office holds about you is brought across and set here, because this is the book that shuts and it is the only book anybody reads that has your name at the top of it. It is done at the end of the month and it is done by hand and it is done the same way every month.”

He picked the pen up.

“You did not ask me a question about your days,” he said, “and I am not going to tell you about your days. But you asked me where your material goes and it goes here, and it goes here because that is the only place on nine days of road where anything about you can be shut at the end of a month, and because a book that shuts is the only kind anybody upstairs has ever been able to ask about.”

## What She Wanted To Ask Next

She had a second question and it was a good one and she had it ready.

She wanted to ask him what happens to a book that has got a line in it with nothing at the other end of it.

She did not ask it.

She had asked one question a month in this building and got it, and on the thirtieth of the month before this one she had stood on a step and worked out that asking a second question tells a man the first one mattered, and she had spent four years learning what that costs a person who has to write it down.

So she stood at the counter and looked at about nine feet of room and asked the question in her own head instead, in the ordinary voice, and had the answer before the pen went back down on the desk.

It shuts the line, because that is what a book that shuts does to everything in it. And the other office has one that has never shut and never will, and it copies the other direction at the end of every month, and in about four months the thing comes down again with something on it and it will be hers.

## What She Saw

The seasonal book was open on the counter at the ninth hour and there were about nine men in the room and nobody was standing between her and it.

It was open at the back, not at the front, and the pages before the one it was open at were the ones she would have wanted.

She did not look at it.

She had stood on a step a month before and worked out that her column was the only thing in the world she had ever been able to prove about herself, and that she had given that up in a room one flight above this one, and she was not going to go back into the same counter and get a look at it for free while six men were at their cups.

That was a decision she made standing up, in daylight, in about nine seconds, and it cost her something, and it was the right cost.

## The Simple Part About One Person

She walked back up the road to the mill in the dark with the bar on her shoulder and thought about a woman of eighteen who had spent five months being unable to do one single thing about her brother and had gotten a line.

A line is not much. She had worked that out standing at that counter a month before and had not enjoyed the working of it.

And it was in a book that shut on the thirtieth, and it had been for five months, and this one had been corded up in the yard at the eighth hour tonight and would go up the road on the first cart in the morning, and there would be one every month after that for as long as she cleared a single listed thing on nine days of road.

She got to the mill at about the eleventh hour and the ring was burning in the corner of the shed with the yard empty, and she put her bag down against the wall and stood in the doorway looking at it.

It had been lit at the fourth hour by nobody, four days now, and it would be lit at the fourth hour tomorrow by nobody, and the cart would go up the road at about the sixth hour in the morning with her in it, and she had not asked for any of it and all three of the things had happened anyway.
