# The Thing That Answers

She started it on the sixth of a month at about the seventh hour with the box against the wall of the open end and the bar in her left hand and about twenty-three pieces in it, and she got nine of them onto the flat ground by the gate in about an hour.

That is about two fifths of the box in an hour with one arm.

She stood in the gateway at about the ninth hour with nine dressed pieces laid out in a row on flat stone and looked at the row and looked at the box and did the whole of it again in her head, and the whole of it came out at about eight loads and a little better than two days a load, because she worked the gate every morning of it, and sixteen days of it, three miles each way, on a ground she would be standing on every day of the rest of the month.

Three weeks on the cart or sixteen days on her own hands. The man at the gate had not said a figure and she had not asked him for one because she had worked it out on the sixth of a month with nine pieces on the ground.

## Two Days Of It

She went back on the seventh and the eighth and did not get any faster.

On the seventh she got eleven pieces out and dropped one of them on the flat stone and it chipped a corner off and she left the chipped one where it was because a chipped block is not a block.

On the eighth she carried the first load about four hundred yards and had to put it down in the grass and go back for the rest, and then carried it the four hundred yards again with two pieces under one arm and one in her hand, and the one in her hand was the one she could not feel, and she put it down on top of the other two at the yard gate and knew that it had not been a good carry and would not have known if it had been a bad one.

That was the thing about it. Not that it was slow. That on the sixth, the seventh and the eighth of a month, on flat ground in the light, with nothing at stake but nine pieces of dressed stone, she could not tell one carry from another.

## What The Material Is For

She stood at her own gate with her hand shut and got to it about nine minutes later, and it took four lines and she had most of it by the seventh and the rest by the eighth.

It is for the ring.

A third of a sack is three coppers and it is about a day’s ring, and she buys one every fourth day. Twenty-three pieces of brick and clay and stone out of two boxes is about eight days of the ring, and eight days of the ring is twenty-four coppers, and twenty-four coppers at two and a half a day is ten days at a gate.

Twenty-four coppers is about a month of the ring, and in five months she had never once been a month ahead of a fire.

And she could not take it out of the yard, and she could not buy the twenty-four coppers out of one hand, and she could not put it in the ring, and there was a fire burning in a shed three miles west that had not answered her once since the twenty-fourth of the last month.

## The Second Thing The Material Is For

That was not the whole of it and she had known it since the sixteenth of the last month at a bank above Pei’s low side and had not wanted to look at it since.

She is not cold any more.

She had noticed that on the first of a month at about the fourth hour and she had been carrying it about for a week the way a person carries a thing they have not looked at. Her hands were warm and her hands were her own instrument for four years and they had nothing in them, and she was out on that flat ground every night of the rest of the month.

She could not buy the feeling back. She had asked for it lying on the ground at the second hour of the fifth of a month and there was nothing on nine days of road that sold it and no board in a gateway that listed it and no office she could reach that had ever heard of it.

So she was going to buy the thing that had gone out with it, which was a fire in a corner of a shed that had gone out in her hands on the first of a month, and she was going to buy it with sixteen days of carrying or three weeks of waiting, out of a box with no mark on it, standing against a wall.

## The Third Thing

The fire she was buying was not the heat either.

The heat had been nothing since the eighth of the first month of the last month. She had said that out loud on the fifteenth of a month in a shed and it had been true.

She had got down to the bottom of it on the sixth of a month standing in a gateway with nine dressed pieces on the ground, and it came out in one sentence and she said it out loud, in the ordinary voice, to nine pieces of stone:

“The material is for the thing that answers.”

That was what she had been buying since the eighth of the first month of the last month with two and a half a day at a gate and six miles a fire. Not heat. Answer.

And on the eighth of a month she stood in a gateway and found out that the thing that answers had been taken out of her on purpose, by her, in front of her, for a reach, at the top of a ditch between two farms, and that it had not come back when she lit it again, and that there was no material in the world that would put it back.

## What That Left

Nine pieces on the flat stone by the gate.

A box of twenty-three against the wall of an open end, with no mark on it.

Three weeks on a cart that did not go when she wanted it to, or sixteen days of two arms and a bar on a ground she was standing on every day anyway.

And a fire three miles west that was burning and would go on burning and would answer nothing, and she was not going to be able to do one single thing about that, on that ground or on any other.

She looked at it in the ordinary order, standing at her own gate in the last of the light on the eighth of a month, four fingers and a thumb that could not feel a stone and a bar over her left shoulder and a board in a gateway with no name on it anywhere.

There was one thing on that ground that could move a box out of a yard and put it in a shed three miles off.

It was not her and it was not the man behind the stack and it was not the bar.

She went and slept on the boards in the corner of the shed at the mill with the fire in it going and saying nothing, and she was going to stand at that yard’s gate at about the ninth hour on the ninth of a month and ask about it out loud, in daylight, in the ordinary voice, to a man who had told her the truth twice and had been right both times.
