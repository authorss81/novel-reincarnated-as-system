# She Asked At The Gate

“Ning Yan,” she said. “Of Lowwater. I am asking to be put in the materials account of the office that sets this board.”

The man who kept the list did not move and did not look at her arm and did not ask her to say it again.

He put his hand flat on the bench and looked at the board on the wall inside the gate, which was up with nothing on it, and then at the cart with its shafts down and a stone under each wheel.

“Say the last part again,” he said.

“I am asking to be put in the materials account of the office that sets this board.”

“That is the whole of it and that is the whole of it correctly,” he said. “Most of them say it as a wage and I have to take it back and make it a materials, and they get in a temper about it, and it takes four days longer.”

## What It Is

It is not a hiring.

That was the first thing he said and he said it looking at her and not at the board.

“There is no day-book for you. There is no rate and no day and no stub and nothing torn off anything. You do not stand on this ground at the fourth hour and you do not get paid at a gate at the seventh. If the office lists a thing and you clear it, what the thing was made of comes to your account and you take it where you are standing.”

“And if I do not clear it.”

“Then it is not listed to you and nothing comes to your account and you go back to the gate at the half-mile and clear half a reach for two and a half, the same as you do now.”

## How It Is Entered

He got a board off under the bench. It was a strip of the same stuff as the one on the wall and about as wide as a hand, and it had a line ruled down it and a line across near the bottom, and it was cut at the bottom edge with a knife, which meant there had been more of it at one time.

He wrote on it in ink, standing up, at about the eighth hour of the twenty-sixth of a month, in a gateway nine miles east of a mill whose wheel had not turned since before she came.

He did not ask her anything while he wrote it. He wrote what he had been given to write, the way he had written the two boxes and the twenty-three and the line with nothing at the other end, and he did not read it out and he did not hold it up.

When he had done he turned it round and put it on the bench on its face, which is what a man does with a thing that is going somewhere and is not his.

He did not look at her while he did it and that was the part she watched.

## The Word For It

“It is a vouching,” he said. “That is the word and there is no other one and you have been vouched once already in a building with a stove in it and neither of us had anything to do with that one.”

“I have.”

“It is the same act twice and it is not the same thing twice,” he said. “That one was about your days. This one is about material. And material is copied further than days are, because days stay on the ground they happened on and material goes to every yard between here and wherever it stops.”

## The Price, Four Times

You cannot take it back.

Not by walking a season’s road the other way. Not by leaving. Not by telling a man at a gate that you did not mean it.

It is in a book and the book is copied, and the copies go to a floor she has never been in, in a hand she has never seen, and there is nobody in nine days of road who can take her out of it.

He said those four as four separate sentences, standing at a bench, in the ordinary voice, and he was not being hard and he was not being kind.

“I am telling you it now because you are standing here and not in four weeks,” he said. “And there is nobody who will be able to tell you it in four weeks, and I have watched that happen to other people and I have never once been able to do anything about it for them afterwards.”

## What She Said

“Yes.”

That was the whole of it. There was no speech and there was nothing to be gained out of the way she said it and she said it in daylight at about nine feet from a bench with a strip of cloth lying face down on it.

“You want to be sure of the thing you are asking for,” he said, and he was not hurrying her and he was not stopping her. “Because after this there is not one hour you get to keep, and I have had people refuse because of that and change their minds four days later, and I cannot take it off a board for four days.”

“I am sure,” she said. “I have been sure for three days.”

## What Is Different From The Ninth

On the ninth of a month she had asked whether a woman who is not on the books of that ground was allowed to ask to be put on one.

This was the other thing and she said it out loud and it is the whole of the difference and there is no part of it that is a trick.

On the ninth she had asked a question about herself and had got an answer in daylight from a man who was right.

On the twenty-sixth she had asked for a thing, in her own name, out loud, in the light, at a gate, the way she had said it at every trestle she had ever stood at, and a man with ink on the side of his right hand had written it down on a strip of cloth because that is what the strip is for and because she had asked.

Nobody made her a speech about it. Nobody argued her out of it and nobody argued her into it and nobody in that gateway had any interest in the outcome except that the thing they had been put in front of them was the thing they had been built to do.

## Where It Goes

It goes up.

That is the whole of the answer to a question she had been asking for six weeks and had got no answer to on any ground between a mill and a hedge: the strip goes where strips go, and it does not stop at the building it was written in, and the answer to a strip does not go back down into the room it came from.

He told her that in about four sentences with his hands on the bench.

“Nobody will tell you anything about that and I am not going to, because I do not know and I am not going to guess at a thing that is above me,” he said. “You will not see it. You will not be able to get at it in a year. And in about four months it will come down again with something on it and it will be yours, and it will be priced by the job and not by your arm, and you will not be able to give that back either.”

## What It Closes

She stood in the gateway and did the four things at once, standing up, in about a minute, in the light.

There was a yard on this ground that had been giving her work for a month with nothing on any board about who she was, and that was finished at the eighth hour of the twenty-sixth of a month.

There was a reach that had been the price of her for four years and would be the price of her for ever, and there was now one other way of being paid on nine days of road and it was not wages and it was not the reach and it followed her.

There was a cart with a stone under each wheel that a man was going to lose because of a week of her wall, and about nine minutes ago she had told him not to lose it on account of her, and there was now a name on a strip that was going up that had her in it, and he would either get his cart back or he would not.

And there was a man two miles and four hundred yards west who had a column with her name at the top of it, and a man nine miles east with a strip of cloth face down on a bench, and she was going to be in both of them, and neither of them could see the other, and she would not see either.

Leaving the reach no longer took her out of the books.

## What He Said In The Yard

She got about nine feet down the made road before she stopped and put her hand on a gate post and stood there.

“You said it out loud,” he said.

She stood at the post with the bar on her shoulder and the light going off the field and did not answer him out loud, and she did not tell him to stop.

The strip was on a bench behind her, face down, going up, in a hand that was not hers, into a floor she had never been in, and there was nothing on nine days of road she could do about it and nothing she would have done differently and she had said it in daylight at a gate about eight minutes ago and she was eighteen years old and it was the first time in five months she had asked for anything.

She walked west. The elm line came onto her left and the ground came up flat out of the far side of it and there was a wheel three miles on against a wall with its arms still where they had been.

She went on past it.
