# Five And Not Four

A third of a reach is one and a half coppers, and she worked a third of a reach at Pei’s gate on the seventeenth of a month and again on the eighteenth, and for the second morning running she was paid less than half of it, which had not happened in two months.

## The Sum

She did it at the gate post at about the seventh hour on the eighteenth with the bar across her knees and two and a half in the fold of the inside of the door frame for the last time.

Five coppers a reach. Half a reach was two and a half. A third of a reach is one and a half.

That is the whole of it and it took as long as a man ties a gate shut, on a gate post, in the ordinary voice, to nobody, with the light going off the field.

The reach had been hers at a whole, then at a half, and it was at a third, and it would be at a quarter of one at whatever she took next, and a quarter of one is one and a quarter, and there is no version of a gate on nine days of road that pays a quarter of a reach.

There is a works three miles east with a board on the wall in a gateway, and the board pays by the job, and the job does not get smaller when she does. She had worked that out on the eighteenth of a month a month before and had been glad of it, and she was still glad of it standing at a gate post with a third of a reach in her hand.

## What A Third Of A Reach Is In Bread

Two and a half a day had been bread and had been most of what she spent, and there had been enough of it left over on about two days in seven to buy a third of a sack of clay.

One and a half a day was not bread.

One and a half a day bought bread and about a third of what was left over on two days in seven, and a third of a sack of clay is three coppers and a third of a sack is about a day’s ring.

So the fire she had been given for nothing at the fourth hour of the morning on the twenty-second of a month had turned an arrangement that had been exactly balanced for five months into an arrangement that did not pay.

She stood at the gate post and worked that out and then she stood there some more.

The fire was not saving her anything. The fire was doing the one thing on nine days of road that she had bought for a week of her own wall and a cart that went on the wrong day, and it was doing it for nothing now.

And she was going to go to the shed at the second hour and put a third of a sack into it that she could not afford, because she was not going to have a cold ring in a shed two miles west while a fire burned in it.

And then the arithmetic turned round on her the way it had at a ditch a month before, and she saw the other half of it, which was that she had not been paying for the fire at all in the last four days. She had been paying for the clay, and the clay was what was going.

## The Listing

She went east at about the seventh hour on the eighteenth and took the next line off the board.

Footing at the low side of the long ditch, four days, one box. A four-foot cut with about nine feet of footing gone under it and clay under the clay, and the whole of it had to come out and go back in before the water came.

Four days is what four men do it in.

## The Second Day

The footing was in it. She got about nine feet in on the first day and about two-thirds of the length on the second and she was working down off the cut at about the fourth hour with a bar and about sixty pounds of dressed stone under her right arm.

The ground she was standing on was the last eighteen inches of the low side and it had been undercut by about a foot and a half over two days and it was wet clay with a skin of dry stone on it.

It went.

Not a slide. A go — the whole eighteen inches at once, under her right foot, with her weight on it and sixty pounds on her arm and the cut on her left.

She got about four feet down onto the bottom of the ditch and she kept the load off the side she was lying on and she did not put her arm in anything.

## Getting Out Of It

Four feet down on wet clay, with sixty pounds of dressed stone on one arm and a bar lying across the ditch above her and a four-foot cut on her left with nine feet of clay in it that had been standing up since the beginning of the week.

She was there about eleven minutes.

There was nobody within nine days of road who would have come, and the reason there was nobody was that there was nobody, and she had been in two feet of standing clay in a drain five months ago at the sixth hour and had come out of that one with a bar.

This was a different kind of hole and a worse one, because in this one she had a load.

She got out with a foot on the near face at the ninth minute and the load under her arm the whole way and the bar where she had left it, and she lay on the bank above for about four minutes and did the four things in the order she always did them.

The right arm was open and stayed open. The four fingers and the thumb were there. The aperture was cracked through and had been for a year. The left hand closed.

Everything she had was still in her and she was lying in a wet ditch in the second hour of a listing with a day gone.

## The Day She Lost

She lost the whole of the second day and she got about six feet of the nine done in the remaining four hours, and she was a day behind the board at about the seventh hour of the second day and she finished it at about the sixth hour of the fifth day.

Five days where the board had said four.

## What The Man Said About It

She was in the gateway at about the seventh hour of the fifth day with one box against the wall behind her and eight holds in her left hand and a dirt bank down the right side of her face, and the man who kept the list was at the bench with the board off and his ink.

He did not look at the dirt.

“Five days,” he said.

“Yes.”

“That is the yard’s line and the yard’s figure is what four men do it in,” he said, “and it is not a figure about you and it was never going to be. You are owed one box and you have got one box. If you put it back on the board, it waits for the next one and I have had a gully on that board for eleven years that never got done because two different women took it off and neither of them finished it.”

He put his thumb on the line he had written and left it there.

“You did not put it back on,” he said. “That is the whole of what the day cost you and you worked it out yourself before you got here, so I am not going to say it again at length.”

## What She Wanted On The Walk Back

She wanted four days back and she was not going to get them and she had known that at about the third hour of the second day when she was lying on the bottom of a wet ditch with a load she could not put down.

What she wanted on the three-mile walk back at about the seventh hour in the last of the light was one thing that she had wanted for about three weeks and had not asked for out loud, and it was this.

There were four lines on that board when she left it.

The gate was a third of a reach now and the reach goes down again at whatever she takes next. She had nine holds at the first of a month and eight at the ninth, and a count of breath on a loaded reach that went to five at the third count and does not come back.

She has cleared five listed things on that ground in seven weeks and there are four left, and five more come down from the same place over the next two months, and then the yard goes quiet until the season turns.

And the thing she wanted was for the board to have a line on it that her hand could not do.

It was not going to get one. The board did not know what her hand was and it was not going to find out, and there were nine men in that yard who would take the next line off the wall beside her and not one of them would look at her arm, and in about two months the sixth of the eleven would be somebody else’s.

In her own head, at about the eighth hour, on the made road, with the elm line on her left:

“I have four left and I am eighteen,” she said.

The road went on west for about a mile and did not answer, and she had not expected it to, and that was the arrangement.
