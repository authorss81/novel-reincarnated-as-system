# The Fire Without Her

She did not get up at the fourth hour of the twenty-sixth of a month.

That was the whole of it and it took her about nine seconds to notice on the boards with the ring nine feet away behind her and the shed warm for as far as a ring makes a shed warm.

She had been getting up at the fourth hour for five months. She had got up at the fourth hour on nights when there was nothing in the ring and nothing coming and nothing to bank it with, and she had got up at it on nights when she had put a strip of dry cake in at the low end for four minutes of her own breath, and she had got up at it on nights when the wind was coming under the door and going through her coat.

She lay on the boards and looked at the corner of the shed where the corner of it was visible and she could see that the stone at the foot of the ring was the colour that stone is when there is a fire against it.

She did not get up.

## At The Second Hour Of The Twenty-Seventh

She went in at about the second hour with the bar on her shoulder and put her hand flat on the brick, and it was hot, and it was up against the corner of the shed where she had banked it to a third and put three handfuls of ash on it on the nineteenth of a month.

It had come up on its own and it had come up full.

She had put the last lump of bought clay into that ring on the seventh of a month and had carried the box off a listing nine miles east on two days in three loads, and there had been about two days of it in the corner of the shed behind a third of a sack ever since, and the ring had been burning at a third since the nineteenth.

There was nothing in it that she had done and no hand anywhere near it.

## What She Did About It

She put her palm flat on the brick and left it there for about as long as it takes to write a date.

Her hand could not tell her. That had been true since the first of a month and it was true now: the brick was hot and her palm was warm and there was no difference between the two of them anywhere in her, and the instrument she had used for four years to find out whether a thing was safe to pick up had been gone from her hands for over three weeks.

So she did the only thing there was.

She took her hand off the brick and put the back of her wrist against it instead, which is how a person finds out how hot a thing is when a hand has stopped working, and the back of her wrist said hot, in about a second and a half, and she took it away and stood in the middle of the shed with her arm up in the air for a moment like a woman at a wash.

## The Test

A ring with a woman in the arrangement holds while she is standing there.

She had known that since the eighth of the first month of the last month and had known it in the specific way she knew things, which was by putting a hand on a thing every day for five months and finding out.

So she tested it, in daylight, over two days, the only way a person can test a thing like that.

On the twenty-seventh she walked east to the works at the seventh hour and stood in a gateway about nine feet off a bench and worked half a reach at Pei’s gate in the morning and was three miles off the mill from the fifth hour of the morning until the seventh hour of the evening.

On the twenty-eighth she did it again and went out to the far side of the elms and came back at about the eighth hour with a box in three loads and her left hand not closing on all of it.

Both nights the fire was up against the corner of the shed when she came into it, and both nights she stood in the doorway and looked at it and did not touch it.

## What That Meant

She worked it out on the boards on the night of the twenty-eighth with her back to the wall and the ring going nine feet away and the door shut against the wind.

Her hours hold without her. That was the term on the right-hand column of a strip about as wide as a hand and she had known it since the twentieth of a month and had not known what it looked like.

It looked like that.

The days in the book one flight above a counter went on being days after she stopped standing at a gate. The material in a second office nine miles east went on being material after she stopped clearing listings.

And a fire in a shed two miles west of a yard went on burning whether she was standing in it or not. She had bought that with a hold she had already spent and with the whole of what he had left, and she had not known the price of it, because he had not told her there would be one.

He had told her there would be a price. She had asked him for it and he had given it to her in four sentences at the second hour of a night and she had said *all right*.

She sat on the boards and made herself put the whole of it in one line, in the ordinary voice, at about the third hour of the twenty-eighth of a month, because she had learned in five months that a thing she did not say out loud came out of her sideways later.

He had bought her the only thing on nine days of road she had never been able to buy.

## The Plain Part

She was eighteen years old and she had walked two miles every night of five months to keep a fire in a shed and she was going to stop doing it, and she was going to be warmer and freer and further off that ground than she had ever been in her life, and the price of it was a man in a room with two shut doors who had stood in it and said a word out loud and could not unsay it.

She did not forgive him. She had not forgiven him for four years and she was not going to start over a fire.

She did not tell him to stop. She had never told him to stop and she had thought about telling him to stop perhaps nine times in five months and had not done it, and she was not going to do it now, and the reason was the same as the reason she had never thanked him for anything and it had nothing to do with him.

## What She Did On The Twenty-Ninth

She got up at the fourth hour out of the habit of a woman who had had it for five months, and she got dressed, and she went out into the yard in the dark with the bar in her left hand.

The mill yard was empty and the wheel was against the far wall with its arms still where they had been, and the ring was burning in the corner of the shed, and there was a stack of trays against the far wall under boards and sacking roped twice about as high as a man.

She stood in the middle of that yard for about a minute with the cold coming off the flat and going through her coat, and her hands were warm, and the fire was up against the corner of a shed with nobody in it and nobody near it, and she was the coldest thing in that yard and the only thing in it that had to be.

She went back in and slept until the light.

That was the whole of what happened in the twenty-four hours before the twenty-ninth of a month, and she did not tell anybody anything about it, and there was not one person within nine days of road who could have told that anything had changed.
