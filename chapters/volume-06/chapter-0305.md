# The Strip Off The Cart

On the eighteenth she went west at the second hour because a gate takes a yard of drain whether or not anybody has heard anything, and she had been working at the gate since the fourteenth, and because two miles of flat road is the only thinking there is on that ground.

She went into the room and the bag was on the bench.

It was the same bag. Canvas, a leather patch at one corner, sewn on with a shoe lace, and the man behind the counter had his hand in it up to the wrist going through what was in it a piece at a time, and there were six men at their cups and the stove was going.

He had a book open flat under his other palm and he was putting pieces of paper into it from the bag and going past her without looking at her, which was correct, because she was a person who came and went and had never once asked to be looked at.

Then he stopped.

He put the last piece of paper down on the bench and turned it round so that it faced her, and he put his finger on it, and he said: “That is about your book.”

## The Three Words

It was a strip of cloth about a foot long and two inches wide.

It was the cloth the keeper’s board was made of. She had been looking at that cloth every day for five months from four hundred yards away, on a board at a rise, where a strip of it went up every nine or ten days whether or not there was anything in it. It was cut to a shape, and this was cut to the same shape, and there was a difference of about an inch on one end.

Down the length of it were three columns ruled in the same small hand she had been reading on books for five months.

There was a line ruled across near the bottom of it.

And in that line, in the middle column, three words.

*Is there more.*

There was no name on it. There was no heading on it she could read and no seal and nothing at the top of it at all, and the three columns above the ruled line were empty, and there was nothing written in the two columns either side of the words.

That is all it was. It was about a book. It was in the shape of a box and the question was sitting in the place where the totals of a reach go, and she understood while she was standing at the counter looking down at it that it was not about her at all in the way she had feared in the first half of the second mile.

It was about a reach. Somebody had read a number and the number had not been all the number there was, and they had written three words on a piece of cloth to ask for the rest.

And the reach it was about was hers, because she was the only person inside nine days of road whose hours were going up a stair for the first time.

## The Cloth She Had Been Looking At For Five Months

She had seen that cloth for five months and had never once asked about it, and she was standing at a counter asking about a piece of it.

At the rise past the post there was a board on two stakes, and on the board was a strip of cloth the length of a forearm, and the cloth was the same cloth. A man of about fifty-five filled it off a cart every nine or ten days out of a book he kept under his arm, and he did it whether there was anything in it or not, and it had been the same board and the same man and the same cloth for the whole of the five months she had been on that ground.

She had kept four hundred yards between herself and that board since the seventeenth of a month two months back.

That had not been fear, exactly. It had been the ordinary discipline of a person who had been announced inside a bound four years and had learned that the only safe distance from anything that writes you down is more than a walking pace.

Four hundred yards had been enough against a board.

It had not been enough against a cart.

## What She Could Have Said

There were three things she could have said at that counter on the eighteenth and she went over all three of them at the fifth hour that evening, standing at the top of Pei’s bank with the bar across her shoulders, and all three of them ended in the same place.

She could have said there was no more.

That was a lie, and she had put twelve gaps in a season’s column herself and entered every one of them, and she was not going to write a false day into a book to save herself the trouble of a true one.

She could have said nothing at all and gone away.

That put a hand she had never seen into a space in a book she could not reach, and the hand would be writing about her, and it would not be her.

Or she could have said there was more.

And then the three words would go into that counter’s room, in a book about a room, with a date against it, and the man behind the counter would have had to write it and he would have had to decide where, and there was nowhere in that room for it, and if he made a place for it then his room was in it.

Which meant somebody would have had to carry it up.

So she had stood at that counter and said *where did this come off*, and got four words, and left the three words lying on a bench in a room that had no way of answering them.

That was the eighteenth, and the strip was still on the bench when she went, and the six men had not looked at any of it.

## Which Way It Runs

She looked at the two empty columns on either side of the three words and worked out the rest of it in about the time it takes to draw breath.

The answer does not go back down into this book.

She could see the shape of that without being told it. There was a counter, and a room, and a rule at the side of the page about a book sent up, and a man who wrote one line a month about his room and nothing more, and if she had stood at that counter and said the answer out loud he would have had to write it somewhere, and there was nowhere in that room it belonged, because the thing being asked about was not in that room.

It was upstairs. It had been upstairs since the first.

So the only way that a sentence said at this counter could ever get to whoever had written the three words was to go up the stair, and the only way that happened was if somebody carried it up.

And a man does not carry a thing up a stair on his own initiative, because that is not what a counter is for, and because carrying it up would put his room in it.

She stood at the counter and understood the whole of that ground in about a second, and the whole of it was that it ran one way, and she was standing on it.

## What She Said

“Where did this come off.”

“Off the cart,” he said. “Off the top of the bag.”

“All right.”

That was all she said to it. She put her hand flat on the counter on the side she could reach it with, the way she had done five times in five months, and she said nothing else at all, and the six men went on with their cups.

The strip was still on the bench in front of her with the three words in it and the two columns empty, and she did not put her hand on it.

## The Nineteenth

She worked the reach on the nineteenth and she worked it well, and then she went back up the bank a hundred yards and stood in the field above it looking west down four hundred yards of flat ground at a post with a board on it that she had kept her distance from since the seventeenth of a month two months back.

The board had a strip of cloth on it. She could not read it from where she was and she was not going to walk over and try.

She stood there and worked at it.

A question is not a person stopping.

A question on a piece of cloth that came down off a cart and asked for three words back is a form with a space in it, and a space in a book gets filled, because that is what a space in a book is for, and it gets filled by whichever hand is holding the pen when the ordinary week comes round.

And the thing that will be written in that space if she says nothing at all is not a woman who has stopped.

It is a woman who is not there.

That was the whole of the nineteenth and it took about as long as a reach of drain.

## The First Fear

For five months everything that had ever happened to her had happened to her.

That was not a boast and it was not loneliness. It was a fact with a shape, and the shape was that she had been the only person inside nine days of road who knew any of it, and she had got very good at being the only person who knew.

And now there was a piece of cloth with three words on it, and the three words were about her, and they were nine days of road from anybody who had ever met her or could be found by her or would be told anything by her, and it had come down off a cart into a room with a stove in it on its own.

Somebody she would never meet had written a question about her and had sent it off with a book, and it had taken eight days and had cost a strip of cloth and about four words of a cart driver’s day, and it had not been a large thing to do.

That was what frightened her on the nineteenth, standing in a field with the drain running below her and the post four hundred yards away.

It was not that anybody wanted anything from her.

It was that it had been easy for somebody to ask.
