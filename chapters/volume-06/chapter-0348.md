# The Room With A Date In It

She took the strip out on the fifteenth of a month at about the fourth hour in the doorway of the shed and held it in both hands with the wind off the flat coming under the door and the ring burning nine feet away behind her where she had not been and had not needed to be for fourteen days.

Two columns. Five months of her days on the left with the word above the hours that said good, and the material line and the holding line under it, and the right-hand column the whole length of the strip with four figures she could not read and a rule.

And four lines at the foot of it, which she had read about forty times and had never once been able to read as anything except a thing that had been written at her.

She turned it round and put her finger on the fourth one.

THE DOORS OF THAT ROOM SHUT ON THE TWENTY-EIGHTH OF A MONTH.

## Which Twenty-Eighth

She stood in the doorway and worked it out on her fingers, which is what she did when there was nothing to write on and there had not been anything to write on since she was a child.

The strip had come down on the seventeenth of a month, which is four weeks back. The seventeenth, and the twenty-eighth of that month was eleven days after it, and that one went past seventeen days ago and she had been on the ground for every hour of it.

And so the next one was this one.

She counted it on the flat of her second finger against the door frame the way she wrote every line she had ever written, and the flat of that finger told her nothing about the number because it had not told her anything since the first of the month a fortnight before the twenty-second of the month before.

She had thirteen days.

## What She Was Going To Do With Thirteen Days

Nine days of road.

That was the whole of it and it was not a thing she could argue with or work out or shorten, because it was not distance on a map. It was a strip of country with lit houses and farms and a gate on it and a works, and men paid by the day in it, and offices in it, and a building at the end of it with a counter in the ground floor and a floor over the counter where things got read against other things.

She had never been west of that road. She had stood at the bottom of a stair in it for nine seconds once and had not gone up it, and the stair above the floor over the counter went on out of the light and she had looked at it and never taken it.

Nine days there and nine days back, on bread, at four fingers, with a bar that took two hands and a body with a third of a reach left in it.

And at the end of the nine days a room she could not name, in which a man would be produced or not produced, and the whole of what she would have for it would be the ordinary knowledge of what a holding is.

## The First Day On The Footing

She went down into it at the seventh hour with the bar and got about five feet in along the face and stood at the bottom of it with clay to the knee on the high side and nine feet of cut above her and the wind coming down the open end of it.

A four-foot cut is a small thing to a man who has been in one for eight hours.

She had been in a four-foot cut every morning for five months at the low side of a field, and the thing about a cut is not the depth. The thing about a cut is that the ground under the spade is where it has to come off, and there is no bar under a footing in a cut — a footing is the bank you put back, and you put it back with your hands and with a bar and in the order the bank will stand in.

She got five feet in and looked along it.

Eleven feet to do. Two days on the board. Four men, and she was one of them for those two days, and there were nine men in that yard who could have taken it and had not wanted it because the bank at the far gully is stone and not clay and it does not go back the way clay goes back.

She worked until the light went and came up out of it at the far end where the ground came up to the lip, and she lost about an hour of that day standing in the bottom working out whether eleven feet of stone bank could be put back at all by a woman with four fingers and a thumb and an arm that was open about half of the time.

It could. She had done two days of it in four years and had never lost a whole hour to thinking about it, and she lost this one because for the first time in about six months the thing she was standing in front of was not the only thing.

## The Shed At The Second Hour

She told him at the second hour on the sixteenth, sitting on the boards with the strip flat under her hand, the way she had told him every price of everything for four years — the number first, then the thing.

“The doors shut on the twenty-eighth of this month,” she said. “It is twelve days off and it is nine days of road and I am not going to get there, and I have known that since the seventeenth of a month, and I have not said it out loud until now because saying it does not change the road.”

He took about a minute.

“I was in a room like that,” he said. “There was a table and a man with a brush, and he did not look up when he finished the figure.”

He did not say anything else and he did not offer to carry it for her and he did not ask her what she was going to do.

## What She Did With The Rest Of The Day

She went out at the sixth hour and took the bar and walked three miles east to the far gully and stood on the high side of the cut and looked down into it for about ten minutes without going in.

The cut was four feet deep. The bank on the low side was about eleven feet of stone and the stone at the top of it was the heavy stone, and there was about two days on the board for it and a box at the end of it, and it was the last thing on nine days of road that anybody was going to pay her by what it weighed.

She went down into it at the seventh hour and got about five feet in on the first day and lost about an hour of it standing in the bottom working out that she was going to have to move stone to put stone back, which was the one thing a footing is.

She had eight holds in her left hand and four fingers and a thumb on her right, and the right arm was open about half of the time and had been for four years, and the bar in her left hand and a dressed piece under her right arm was the arrangement she had carried about nine hundred times.

It was still good. She went up the low side eleven times before the light went and it was still good, and her breath went to five at the third count going up and came back to four with the load set down on the crest, and it did that every one of the eleven times.

That was the whole of what she had, and it was thirteen days old and going.
