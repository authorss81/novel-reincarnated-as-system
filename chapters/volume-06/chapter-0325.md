# The Cart Turned Round

The thirteenth of a month came up and she was in the gateway at about the ninth hour with twenty-three pieces of dressed stone standing against the wall behind her, and she had not asked for anything and had not decided about anything and had not slept properly on the eleventh.

The cart was against the wall with its shafts down and a board across the shafts and a stone under each wheel.

It was not going anywhere that day.

That was a fact about that yard on that morning, in the way that the wheel of a mill is a fact about that yard every morning of the year.

## The Ninth Hour

The man behind the stack came out and got the shafts up and put his shoulder against the near one and turned the cart about nine inches, and she stood in the gateway with the bar on her shoulder and watched him do it.

Then he called the man who kept the list.

Then the man who kept the list came out with the ink on the side of his hand and looked at the cart and looked at the box against the wall and came and took the board down off the bench and squared it and wrote a line on it.

She watched him write it from about nine feet off and it took about as long as he took to write anything.

There was nothing against it.

That is what she stood there and understood, at about the ninth hour of the thirteenth of a month, standing in a gateway with a bar over her left shoulder: there was a line on the board of a stone yard with the yard’s own mark at the end of it and no name at the other end of it, and the man who kept the list wrote it and did not stop halfway through and did not ask her anything.

## What He Said About It

He put the chalk down and looked at the line and then at her.

“That is out,” he said, “and there is nothing against it.”

“Yes.”

“That is a hole,” he said. “I do not have a name for it and I have had this board eleven years and it has not had one. When the round comes round in about eleven days somebody will ask me what that line is and I will have to say a box went out on a cart that was not going out.”

He said it in the ordinary voice, to a woman standing in his gateway, and he did not ask her where it had come from and he did not ask her whether it was hers and he did not ask her twice.

Then he squared the board up on the bench and went back behind it.

## The Box Going On

They put it on the cart at about the tenth hour, the whole of it, twenty-three pieces, in about nine minutes, and they roped it twice, and the ropes went over the load and under the shafts and were tied by the man behind the stack who did not speak at all during any of it.

The cart came out of the yard and turned left on the made road and went west.

The mill is west. The yard is east of the mill by three miles and the road between them is a made road with a hedge on the north side of it and it goes through about half a mile of open ground and then a line of elms.

She got up on the near shaft with her bar and rode it in.

## Where It Came From

She worked it out on the road between the elms and the open ground, sitting on a shaft with her feet on the shafts and her back against the load, and it took her about nine minutes and she did it alone.

The round is a thing on a board.

She had watched it for ten days and had never once looked at it, because a round is the yard’s business and the yard’s business is a cart and a stone and a man. There was a line somewhere that said when the cart went and what it took and where it put it down, and the yard wrote it and the yard kept it and the yard did what the line said.

It was a line.

It was the same as a column. It was a line about hours and a cart, kept by men, read by men, and it said what a thing goes out on and where it goes and when, and about nineteen days ago it had been put there and it had been put there out of a woman who had spent a week of her own wall.

On the twenty-fourth of a month at about the seventh hour, at the top of a ditch between two farms, with her hand on a post, he had said he wanted the reach to put a thing on this ground that was not hers and not his and did not stop because of either of them.

He had told her what it was for before he spent it. That was the arrangement and he had never once broken it in four years.

She had had nineteen days since the twenty-fourth to stop him and she had not known it was coming and she was not going to be able to say that she had known.

## What She Said To Him

The cart came onto the flat at about the eleventh hour and the elm line went onto the left of it and there was nothing on that ground within about half a mile that could hear two people on a cart.

She sat on a shaft with the bar across her knees and the load behind her and said:

“I moved the cart.”

“That is what it is for,” she said.

Neither of those had been said out loud. She said them in her own head, in the ordinary voice, sitting on a shaft, and he was not there and the arrangement was not that he heard things she did not say.

The cart went on.

## The Yard Behind Them

The listing man was standing at his board when the cart came out of the yard and he was not watching it go.

That was the part she thought about afterwards, on the road, and it was the part that made it sit wrong. A man whose cart has gone against his own line at about the tenth hour of the middle of the day stands at the gate and watches it go, because that is the only thing there is to do with it.

He had gone back to the bench. He had his hands on the board and he was looking at a line with nothing against it, and about half a mile of made road went between them by the time she could not see him at all.

A hole in a book with a man’s name at the end of it and nothing at the other end, and eleven days to find out about it, and he had put it there himself at the ninth hour of the thirteenth of a month with a woman standing in his gateway and had not asked her a single question.

She had not hired anybody. She had not promised anybody anything. She had not bought a cart or a round or a man. She had taken three lines off a board in a month and carried one block into a ditch with her own hands and lost it.

And a thing had come apart in a country she had been in for five months on the strength of a week of her own wall, and a man with ink on the side of his hand was going to have to answer for it in about eleven days with a hole in his own account and a board he had kept for eleven years.

That was the price of it and it was not on her and it was not on him and it was on a person with a yard.
