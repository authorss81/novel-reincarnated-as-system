# Six Miles To The Fire

The ditch between the two farms was nine feet wide at the top and about eleven at the bottom and the wall on the far side had gone under about twenty feet of it, and she stood at the top of it at about the fifth hour on the fifteenth of a month with a line in her coat and looked at the whole of the job in one look.

Four days was the yard’s figure and the yard’s figure was not a guess. It was the figure of what four men would do to it with two barrows.

## On The Fifteenth

She bought a third of a sack at the shed past the mill at the eighth hour on the fifteenth for three coppers out of the five she had in the front of her coat, and carried it west in two loads with a bar under it because one arm is one arm.

That shed is about a mile east of the shed with the ring in it, and she carries it that mile twice over, every week she buys it. She broke the sack up on the flat stone by the door at about nine minutes past the ninth hour, standing over it in the dark, eleven minutes a lump and about four lumps, with the flat of a second finger against a piece of bought clay and the heel of her hand behind that.

She banked the ring at about the tenth hour and lit it low with a strip of dry cake and about four minutes of her own breath, and it came up and held, and the shed was warm for about as far as a ring that size makes a shed warm.

Two miles and eleven minutes a lump and three coppers, to get one fire.

That is what a ring costs to keep lit when she buys it.

## Six Miles

It is not six miles when she buys it.

The works is three miles east of the mill and the mill is where the ring is, and a box of what a job was made of comes out of that works at three miles from the only thing on that ground it is for, and the works does not deliver and has no cart going for another three weeks, and a woman with four fingers and an arm that is open half the time moves a box three miles in about eight loads over as many days.

Three miles there and three miles back, on a ground she is standing on anyway, on days she is not working, and there are not any.

So a box off a listing costs six miles before it costs a coppers, and a sack off a shed costs two.

And a listing does not cost coppers at all. That is the whole of it, and it took her until about the ninth hour on the fifteenth of a month, standing on flat stone by a door with the ring lit behind her, to finish the sum she had started on the third of a month in a gateway with nothing on it.

## What It Is Actually For

She sat down on the boards with her back against the wall and her right hand open on her knee and worked out what she was buying, properly, for the first time in five months.

The heat was nothing. She had known the heat was nothing since the eighth of the first month and had gone on paying for it anyway, because a ring is a ring and a cold ring is a ring not doing anything.

The heat is about as far as a hand goes and then it stops, and a person on flat ground at the second hour of a night is not warmed by a ring in a shed she has to walk to.

She was cold the way a stone is cold, all the way through, all the time, and had been since she was eleven years old, and the ring did not touch that and never had.

What it did was answer.

She put her hand flat on the brick of it and it was the first thing in five months that had answered her. Not a man with a brush and not a farmer at a gate and not a clerk with a strip of cloth and not a man with a bar coming out from behind a stack, and not anything anybody had done for her, because it did not do anything for her. It was there when she lit it and it was there at four hours and it did not want anything.

That is what the whole of it costs: eleven minutes a lump, six miles a fire, three coppers, and a woman breaking up bought clay in the dark because there is no other way to get it into that ring at a size she can lift.

## The Sentence That Goes With It

A woman who has a thing that answers her cannot be anywhere without it.

She had worked that out on the sixteenth at about the fifth hour on a bank above Pei’s low side with the bar across her knees, and she had been doing the version of it that said *a thing that answers a person is a thing a person can be found at* since the sixteenth of the last month, and the second version is the same sentence with nothing taken out of it.

She had a person’s name at the top of a column in a book one flight above a counter two miles west, and a word above the hours, and a fire in a shed that answered her when she lit it, and she was the easiest thing on nine days of road to find and she had known it since the sixteenth of the last month.

She did not put the fire out.

That is the arrangement and there was no part of her that wanted to.

## The Rule She Worked Out

On the seventeenth and the eighteenth she worked half a reach at Pei’s gate both days and did the arithmetic both days and it came out the same both days, and on the evening of the eighteenth she had it whole and she was standing at the top of a ditch with a bar on her shoulder.

Two and a half a day, at five coppers a reach, and half a reach is what she could open.

Four days of that is ten, and a charge is nine, and three coppers a fourth day is what the ring actually costs, and a box of what the work was made of is four days of the ring.

Every stage she has taken in four years has taken a piece of that number, and the piece she took at the last one was half of it, and the number is the reach and the reach is what her body can do on a day and her body gets stronger and the reach goes the other way.

The gate pays by the reach.

The yard pays by the job.

## What That Is Worth

She stood at the top of that ditch and made herself say it out loud, in the ordinary voice, because it was the kind of thing that would go out of her head by the morning if she let it.

“For four years the only price on anything has been what I can lift.”

She said it to a ditch between two farms at about the seventh hour on the eighteenth of a month and the wind took about half of it.

The board at the yard did not ask what she could open on a day. It said four days and it said two boxes, and the two boxes were two boxes whether she opened them with one hand or two or none, and the four days were the four days the ground wanted and not the four days she could manage.

That is the whole difference between five months of a gate and a board on a wall, and it is the first work in four years whose price has not fallen when she got stronger.

## What She Was Going To Do About It

She was going to clear the retaining wall at the ditch, and it was four days of the yard’s four and not her four, and it was two boxes, and it was eight days of the ring.

And she would still work Pei’s gate, because the gate is the only coppers on that ground and bread is not made out of boxes, and she would work the gate for bread and the yard for fire and put the two together and be a woman with two prices on her instead of one.

She worked out the second half of it standing at that ditch at about the seventh hour on the eighteenth with the light going off the water in it, and it is the only calculation she made in five months that came out better than she had expected.

Clearing a listing pays in fire. Clearing a listing also costs a fire, out of her, once, at the moment it is finished.

A box is four days of the ring. The settling at the fourth hour takes the fire that is in the ring that day, which is between nothing and four days of it, depending on where in the four she stands.

So a listing that pays one box costs her worst case four days of fire and gives her four days of fire, and the rest of the box is not a trade with her at all. It is the money. It is the part of the yard’s payment that does not come out of her foundation.

And she cannot settle anything off the gate, because the gate is not listed and a gate does not settle anything, and the man at Pei’s gate has been paying her five coppers a reach for a month and has never once caused anything to come up through the base of anything.

That is the whole arrangement and there is no man in it. It is arithmetic, and she did it in about four minutes at the top of a ditch with the light off it.

Then she went down into that ditch at the fourth hour on the nineteenth with the bar in her left hand and about four feet of her own weight to shift before the light came up.
