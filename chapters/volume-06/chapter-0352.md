# It Takes Two Hands Now

She was standing on the open ground about a mile and a quarter east of the mill at about the fourth hour of the twenty-third of a month with the bar lying on the grass in front of her boots and nothing within about half a mile.

She had been standing there for nine minutes and what she had been doing for the nine minutes was counting her breath, because she had built the fortnight for it and was not going to waste a minute of it on being frightened.

Four. Four. Four.

Four on a flat with the bar on the grass. Five on a loaded reach, and it did not come back to four there and it was never going to.

## The Stage

She did it standing up.

It came the way the seven before it had come. There was not a living soul on that ground to be told and there was no county book standing on it to take anything down, and the sound of a stage is a sound and a sound has to go somewhere. It went into the ground under that grass and a quarter of a mile into the air, and a dog on a farm about a mile off did not bark.

[Ning Yan, past the line, servant-born, under no ceiling any county holds. Golden Core, stage eight, is held.]

It said its one sentence and stopped, the way the seven before it had said it and stopped, and it has never told her a thing.

## What It Took

She put her hand flat on the bar where it lay.

It came up.

She took four dressed pieces out of a box against the wall of the shed at the mill and put them on the crest of that bank and went up the near side with the bar in her left hand and four dressed pieces under her right arm, the way she had gone up it four hundred times.

At the third count her breath went to five.

She set the pieces down on the crest and stood over them and counted, and it was four, and she picked them up again and went down the far side and up the near side, and at the crest she stopped.

She was on the top of the bank with the load on her arm and the bar in her left hand and she needed to put them down, and she could not get the bar to go anywhere.

She stood there trying for as long as it takes a man to write a word down.

## The Arithmetic Of Hands

She had the whole of it in her body from a bank nine days west and it was four words long, and she could not get any part of it past the fourth word today.

The bar goes under the stone before the stone comes. The stone comes up on the bar and the last foot of it goes by hand, with the arm open and the fingers flat on it, and it does not stop anywhere in between.

That is one hand on the bar and one hand on the stone.

She had eight holds in the left one. She had four fingers and a thumb on the right one and the right arm open half the time and had been for four years, and she could hold a stone with it for about two minutes at a time and no longer.

A bar and a stone. Two hands.

She did not have two hands.

## The Plain Part

She put the pieces down on the crest of the bank and stood there and let it arrive, and then she said it out loud on open ground where there was not a living thing within half a mile, which she had not planned to do and did anyway.

She had said two sentences to herself out loud on that ground in seven months and neither of them had been about a bar.

“I can carry a bar.”

She lifted it and it came up and she could walk with it and she could turn with it and she could put it down.

“I cannot carry a stone and a bar.”

That was the whole of it.

She could not argue with the shape of it. The bar she had learned to carry one-handed at a bank nine days west, from a woman with a scar across the back of both hands, had asked her for two hands on the fourth hour of a morning on open ground, and she did not have two hands, and the heavy end was not coming back.

## What Closed

The heavy end closed to her at about the fifth hour of the twenty-third of a month on four feet of bank past the last hedge, and it is closed for good.

Not the ramp. The ramp was never hers and she had asked about it in a gateway a day before and had been told no by a man who was right.

Everything else. A footing is a bank you put back and the bank at the far gully is stone. A block is a block. Any work on nine days of road that is measured by what comes out of the ground in pieces rather than in length is measured by weight, and weight is carried with a bar and a hand, and she has one hand.

Nothing on that board pays her for that, and nothing on that board could be put her on.

She had asked for a line the board could not give her.

She got one, and it was not on the board.

## The Fifth Hour Again

She went back down off the bank at about the fifth hour and walked the mile and a quarter to the shed with the bar over her shoulder, and she went in and put her hand flat on the flat stone by the door out of the habit of a woman who had done it four thousand times, and the stone was cold and her hand was warm and it told her nothing.

That was the only moment of the morning that had not gone according to the rest of it, and she had expected that. She stood in the doorway and let the stone tell her nothing at all, and then she went out again.

## What She Did With The Morning

She went back to the shed and got the bar and the four pieces and carried them down off the bank and along the flat to the gully, and she put them on the gully floor one at a time with her hand on each one and no bar under any of them, and she did nine pieces that way before the light got long, and it took her until about the ninth hour to do what she had done in about forty minutes the day before.

That was the price and it was a price in hours and not in anything anybody could read.

## The Want

She had wanted this for eleven days.

She wanted it because she was eighteen and there were four lines left on a board and nine men in a yard who would take the next one off the wall beside her, and because her body was a smaller number than it had been a month ago, and the ground did not know that and never would.

And on the twenty-third of a month she got a bar that takes two hands, and a gate that pays by the reach, and a reach she now opens at a third because she cannot lift out what she digs at the depth a reach is priced at, and a strip folded in three against her ribs with five days left in it.

She wanted to put the bar down on the grass and scream, and she did not, because there was not a living thing within half a mile and the dog on the farm a mile off had not barked.
