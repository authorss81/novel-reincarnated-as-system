# Five Counts

She took it at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of a month out past the last hedge, on open ground about a mile and a quarter east of the mill, with the bar laid down on the grass and nothing within about half a mile that had ever heard anything.

She had been standing on that ground for nine minutes with the bar under her hand before she did it, and what she was doing for the nine minutes was counting her breath, because she had built the last fourteen days for it and was not going to waste them.

Four. Four. Four.

Four on a loaded reach at the third count, five days out of five, and four unloaded, and she had known the answer before she started and had wanted it counted anyway.

## The Stage

She did it standing up.

It came the way the six before it had come and there was no announcement and there was not going to be one.

There was nobody inside to be told and no county book on that ground to take anything down, and the sound of a stage is a sound and a sound has to go somewhere. It went into the ground under that hedge and a quarter of a mile into the air, and a dog on a farm a mile off did not bark, and it was over at about four minutes.

It was over at about four minutes.

[Ning Yan, past the line, servant-born, under no ceiling any county holds. Golden Core, stage seven, is held.]

It said it once, in the far-side house form, and then it was finished, and it did not explain and it did not advise and it did not begin.

## What It Took

She picked the bar up off the grass.

That was ordinary. The bar came up the way it came up.

She went back to the mill, which is a mile and a quarter, and got four dressed pieces out of a box against the wall of the shed, and carried them to the flat with the bar in her left hand, and went up a bank of four feet at the second gully, three miles east of a gate.

## Five

Her breath went to five on the loaded reach.

Not everywhere. Not at the second count and not on the way down and not at four in the morning in a shed and not on the flat and not standing at a bar with nothing on it. On the loaded reach, at the third count, going up four feet of bank with four dressed pieces under one arm and the bar in her left hand, her breath went to five and stayed at five for the rest of the climb.

She set the pieces down on the crest of that bank and stood over them and counted.

Four.

She lifted them again and went down the far side of the gully and up the near side, and it went to five at the third count again, at the same place, the way a thing goes to the same place every time.

Four when it was down. Five when it was up.

## The Part That Does Not Come Back

She did it four more times in about an hour, with four pieces and with five, and the place where the count went to five did not move, and when the pieces were on the gully floor and her hand was empty and her arm was at her side, it came back to four and stayed there.

It will go to five on a loaded reach for the rest of her life. It will not come back to four there again.

That is the whole of it and it is not a figure about a furnace and it is not on her face. It is the difference between a body that can carry a load and decide where to put it down, and a body that can carry a load, and the difference is one count of breath on four feet of bank and it came out of a stage she took on open ground at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of a month with nobody within half a mile.

## The Plain Part

The breath was short on every ground in the world before this.

Four counts, everywhere, on a bank or on flat or at a gate or in a ditch at the second hour of a night. She had carried four counts through nine days of road and four hundred yards of somebody else’s ordinary country and a ditch that came in on her at the sixth hour, and she had counted it since she was nine years old and she had never once been asked what it was for.

It was for carrying a load and deciding where to put it down.

And on the sixteenth of a month she put four pieces down on a gully floor at about the sixth hour and her arm was open and her right hand had four fingers and a thumb on it and her left hand had eight holds where it had had eleven, and she had five counts of breath on the bank and four on the ground, and she was eighteen years old.

## What She Did About The Knowing

She did not go and tell anybody, because there was nobody, and she did not write it down, because there was nowhere, and she did not go back to the works and take a line off the board, because it was the fourth hour of the morning of the sixteenth and the board was a mile and a half off.

She went and got four dressed pieces out of a box against the wall and she took them and the bar and she walked the three miles east to the second gully and she went up the bank eleven times.

Five on a loaded reach at the third count. Four with the load down. Four with five pieces on her arm at the first count and five at the third, and she put them on the gully floor both times rather than find out what the fourth piece does.

She had worked that out on the second of a month a fortnight earlier when she did the same thing with the same bank and had got five on the second load, and she had not known what it was then and had put it in a shed with herself about it. Now she knew what it was and she went up the bank eleven times anyway.

Not to find the place. To be certain of it.

## What She Was Going To Do About It

She went down off the flat at about the seventh hour with the bar on her shoulder and got to Pei’s gate in time to have it counted.

The low side of Pei’s field is a drain and a drain at four feet deep is a loaded reach, and she had known it was a loaded reach every morning for five months and had counted four going into it every morning for five months, and it was going to be five from that morning on.

She stood at the gate post for about as long as it takes a man to lift a box and worked out the number.

Five coppers a reach.

Half a reach was what her body could open on a day and it had been half a reach for two months and it was going to stop being half a reach at about the seventh hour of the sixteenth of a month.

She went in at the seventh hour and opened the drain and it took her until about the eighth hour to get as far as she used to get by the eighth, and the count went to five about nine feet in, and she worked the rest of it at five, and Pei came out at about the eighth hour with the bar and his boy on the fence with a stick and he did not say one word about it.
