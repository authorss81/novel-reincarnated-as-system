# Warm Hands

The thirtieth of a month came up and she worked half a reach at Pei’s gate and took two and a half out of the fold in the inside of the door frame, and she did the lane-to-yard walk at the seventh hour to find out how many days a box of twenty-three lasts a ring that is burning and answering nothing.

Nine days.

She had known it would be nine days and she had wanted it to be a different number on flat ground at a gate in the last of the light, and that was the whole of what she had wanted it for.

## At The Mill

She went west on the last night of the month and slept on the boards in the corner of the shed with the ring banked low against the wall behind a third of a sack, and she was at the fourth hour of the first of a month standing in the yard with the ring out on the flat stone by the door and lit low and the shed door open behind her.

She had carried it out on purpose. It had not been on the stone at the fourth hour for any reason in five months, because a furnace of her own that wants watching is not a furnace of her own.

The wheel was against the far wall with its arms still where they had been.

The stack of trays was against the wall under the lean-to, roped twice, with boards and sacking thrown over it, about as high as a man, and there was more of it under the second lean-to, and there was a third stack further along.

Every reach on that ground sent one a month. Her column was in one of them and she did not know which one and was not going to look.

Inside the shed a man with the shoulders of a man who has carried trays for thirty years was working at a bench with a rule and a paste-pot, and he did not come out, and she had been sleeping on his floor since the eighth of the first month of the last month and he had never once come out for her.

## At The Fourth Hour

She did it standing, in the yard, with the bar laid down on the stone.

The stage came up out of her the way the four before it had come, and there was no announcement, and there was not going to be one, and there had not been one at the last working point in five months and there was not going to be one on the first of a month in a mill yard with a stack of closed books against the wall.

Nobody inside to be told. No county book on that ground to take anything down.

The sound of a stage is a sound and a sound has to go somewhere.

It went into the ground under that yard, which was stone and about two feet of old fill under the stone, and into the air above it about a quarter of a mile, and there was a dog on a farm a mile off that did not bark, and nothing came back.

She stood in the open and waited for it to be over and it was over at about four minutes and nothing came back then either.

[Ning Yan, past the line, servant-born, under no ceiling any county holds. Golden Core, stage five, is held.]

The layer said it once in the far-side house form and stopped. It had never explained and it had never advised and it did not begin.

## What She Noticed

She noticed the hands first.

That was about nine minutes after, standing on the flat stone by the door with the ring burning a foot away, and she put the palm of her right hand flat down on the stone and left it there.

The stone was cold.

She took her hand off and turned it over and looked at four fingers and a thumb with the flat of the second one longer than it had ever been, and she held it up in front of her face with the light behind it and it was not cold.

She put it back on the stone.

Cold.

She put her left hand on the bar where it was lying on the stone and it was cold and the bar was cold and the yard was cold and the wind off the flat came in under the shed door and went through her the way it had gone through her every day of four years.

Her hands were not cold.

## Short

That is all it was.

She stood in a mill yard at about the fourth hour of the first of a month and found out that the cold had gone out of her hands, and she did not have a name for what had happened and she did not look for one.

The stone was cold and the bar was cold and the ring was hot and she was cold everywhere except both hands, and the cold everywhere had been the ordinary condition of her life since she was eleven years old.

She had noticed the stone being cold about four thousand times in her life. She noticed it that morning because it was strange to put a hand on something and have it be one thing when she was another.

## The Pan And The Knife

She took the pan and the knife out of the corner where they had been since the ninth of the first month of the last month and carried them to the step and put the blade of the knife on the flat stone with her right hand, and left it about the length of a breath in and a half, and picked it up.

She did that three times and each time she put her hand on it flat and it was the same thing underneath, and there was nothing in her hand that told her which it was.

It had been the instrument she had for four years. She had used it to tell a cold night from a wet one on a floor of boards nine days of road behind her, and she had used it to tell a lump of clay she had just broken from one she had not, and she had used it on the flat stone by that door a hundred times with the flat of a second finger to find out whether the ground was ready to bank a ring on.

The instrument was still there and it was not connected to anything.

## The Wind

The wind came off the flat at about the fifth hour and it was the same wind that had come off the flat on the eighth of the last month and on the second of this month and every morning since she had walked out past a wheel that had not turned.

She stood in the yard with her back to it and let it come and it went through her coat and through her shirt and stopped at her, the way it always had, and her hands stayed where they were.

That was the whole of what she had on the first of a month at about the fifth hour, standing in front of a stack of trays with boards and sacking thrown over it in a yard a stone’s throw from a wheel that had not turned since before she came.

The fire behind her was burning and it was answering nothing. The stack against the wall was nine books thick and one of them was her days and she had come and gone past it nine times in a month without looking at it. Her right hand was open on her side with four fingers and a thumb on it and she was eighteen years old and she had taken five stages in four years and no county in nine days of road had heard of any of them.

And her hands were warm, and she did not know yet what that was, and she stood in the yard a long time before she went in to bank it.
