# She Says No At The Counter

The twenty-eighth of a month was two days off and the book on the counter had a page fanned open in it about two inches from the back, and she stood on the side of it she could reach with a whole hand on the twenty-sixth at about the ninth hour with about nine men in the room and a stove at the back and the strip folded in three against her ribs.

The man behind the counter turned it a quarter round so she could see where her name was.

“Your line came back down the road on the twenty-second and it has been read,” he said, “and it was not mine to read and it is not mine to send back, and it has been in my room for four days and I have not been able to do one thing with it.”

He put his thumb on the margin.

“I have a counter and a book and six men and a stove, and I have a line in my book that has to be produced in a room on the twenty-eighth, and the room is not named on the strip and it is not in this building. That has been the case since the seventeenth of a month two months back. It is the twenty-eighth.”

He took his thumb off.

“There is one name under it. I have never seen the name and I have never asked what it is and I am not going to. You are the only person on nine days of road that has got a road to that building, and I am asking you one question, in this room, in front of these six, and I will write down what you say either way and I will write down the word I hear.”

## The Question

“Are you going to carry the holder in.”

He said it in the ordinary voice and he did not put any weight on it at all, and it went round the room and came back off the walls, and about nine men who had been at their cups for an hour looked at a woman standing at the end of a counter.

## What She Said

She looked at the page with her name on it for about as long as it takes to write a date.

“Ning Yan, of Lowwater,” she said. “No.”

The room did not make a sound.

She had said it in the ordinary voice at the ninth hour of the twenty-sixth of a month in a room with a stove in it, and she had said her name first, the way she had said it at a trestle and at a gate and at a bench in a gateway.

“The holder is not presented,” she said. “I am not carrying him to that room. And I will not stand in one and say that I have.”

## Why That Is The Worst One To Give

A holding is what a column leans on so it does not drift. Every book on nine days of road had one for every column in it and every one of them was one name and not two.

She had refused the only thing in three weeks that would have made it survivable, and in the three weeks before it a man had refused the same arrangement at the other end of it, out loud, in his own words, on the boards of a shed at the second hour of the fourth of a month, and had not been asked for anything for it.

And now there was one name on a line in a book nine days of road away, and the name could not be produced, and the doors of the room shut on the twenty-eighth at some hour she was never going to be told.

## Why She Went Up There

She had been carrying that fourth line at her own ribs for ten weeks, and six weeks ago she had stood eleven feet off a man with a notebook in ten inches of wet grass and been told that there were four rooms on the ground he had been in and two of them shut on the last day of a month, and had not been told what one of them looked like and had not been able to ask.

And there was a date on it, and the date was two days off, and she had been going up that road to say no to somebody whether it was the right thing to do or not, and she would have gone up there if the board at the works had had four lines on it and there had been a season coming.

That was the part she looked at afterwards, on the three-mile walk back, in the dark, with nothing folded in three against her ribs.

She had not gone up there to protect the line. She had gone up there because there was a date on it and it was two days off and there was nobody else on nine days of road who could say anything about it.

## What He Wrote

He put the pen down flat and looked at it for a moment and then picked it up and wrote at the left of the page in the margin, against the day, in the ordinary hand.

“Ning Yan, of Lowwater. The holder is not presented. Refused at the counter. The twenty-sixth.”

Fifteen words, and he read them back to himself once without saying them out loud before he put the pen down, because that is what a man does with a thing he has just made permanent.

He did not write why. He said out loud that he was not going to write why, because the man behind a counter does not write down what a person says about a thing he cannot see, and because a reason written in a margin is a thing that gets read out to a room.

## What Was In The Book After

Three things in that book with nothing at the other end of the rule.

The materials account against the mill, from the twenty-sixth of a month four months back.

The holding, from a strip that came down in the middle of a month with words at the bottom of it.

And now a refusal, with a day against it and a name at the left of it, that no man on nine days of road can do one single thing about.

He looked at the page for about a minute.

“It will not shut now,” he said. “It has not shut since the first of a month and I have sent it up four times as it is, and it has not been a trouble to anybody, because a book that does not shut still goes up on the first cart and it goes as it is.”

He put the pen down.

“And it goes up on the first cart tonight with a refusal in it that was not in it on the first of a month, and you have done that yourself, and there is no hand in this building that can take it out.”

## The Plain Part

She is eighteen years old.

Five months ago she had a gate and a furnace and no name on anything past the last working point, and everything on nine days of road that touched her had been right, and the whole of what she had built was a third of a reach and a shed with a fire in it.

She has a running column one flight above a counter in a hand she has never seen. She has a materials account nine miles east that has been copied upward out of its own office in a hand she has never seen. She has a holding with one name on it under a line she cannot read, and a refusal in the same book, in her own name, with a day against it, that cannot be taken out by anybody.

From that hour the country past the line has to deal with her where she stands or not at all.

## On The Way Down The Road

She walked back down the mill road in the dark with the bar over her shoulder and nothing against her ribs that was folded in three.

The strip was gone. She had taken it out at the counter and put it on the bench and he had put it in with the book, because the strip and the refusal are the same thing and he was right about that too, and she had not asked him to and he had not waited to be asked.

She was two days out from a date that was in a room nine days of road away, and the room would shut with nothing in it that she had carried, and there was no road on nine days of road that would bring her anything back from it.

She got to the shed at about the eleventh hour and put the bar down against the wall and stood in the doorway with the ring burning nine feet away in the corner where she had not been and had not needed to be for three months.

It was the driest and the quietest thing on nine days of road and it was hers.
