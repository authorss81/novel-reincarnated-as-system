# What Her Hand Could Not Say

She had been five days on it by the second of a month and it had not improved by the fifth and she was not expecting it to.

The cold had not gone out of her the way a thing goes out of a room. The cold was still there, in the yard, in the bar, in the stone by the door, in the air off the flat coming in under the shed door at about the ninth hour every night and going through her coat and her shirt and stopping at her.

It had gone out of her hands.

She was not cold. Her hands were not cold. They were the temperature of a thing that has been near a fire for an hour, which is a different thing, and there was a difference and she had not been able to find the edge of it in five days.

## The Instrument

A body that cannot feel cold cannot tell what cold is for.

That is the whole of it and it took her two days to get it down to that and it is not a small thing and it is not on her body.

She had used the flat of her hand for four years as an instrument. She had put it on a stone floor to find out whether the floor was ready to bank a ring on. She had put it on a lump of clay to find out whether the lump was wet, because a wet lump breaks wrong and a dry one breaks in the hand. She had put it on a bar at the top of a drain at the second hour of a night to find out how deep the water was going to be.

All of that had been one instrument and it had been in her hand since she was eleven years old, and it had never once been the same instrument as the one on her ribs, and it had been the only honest one she had ever owned.

On the third of a month, at about the sixth hour, at the open end of a building about three miles east of a mill, she put her palm flat on a dressed block that had been out in the yard all night and it told her the temperature of her own hand.

## Frost

That was the part that was going to kill somebody and it came on the fourth.

Frost comes off stone between about the second hour and the fourth and it makes the top of a stone slick, and a slick stone comes out of your hand when you have it at the wrong angle, and every stone-mason on nine days of road puts a hand on a stone before he picks it up.

She put her hand on nine stones on the morning of the fourth of a month and got nine of the same answer and went on working.

## Going Down

The block was about as long as her arm and as thick as her two hands and it was at the wrong angle on the flat stone where she had put it, and she had a bar under it and her right hand on the upper edge and her weight coming down through her left knee.

Her hand told her nothing.

It was not that it told her the wrong thing. There was no wrong thing in it. There was no answer at all, from any part of it, in the way that there is no answer when you put your hand on a thing you have not touched in an hour.

The block came off the stone.

It went across her thigh and it was moving and it was heavier than she had thought, and the bar’s end was under it and caught it at about the edge of her knee and held it about a quarter of a second longer than she needed and stopped it, and she came off it onto her left hip on the flat stone of the yard with the bar under the block and about four inches of leg in between.

She lay on the stone of that yard for about four minutes and did not move.

## What She Checked

She did it in the order she did it in after the bank, in the last month of the last month, in the wet grass at the seventh hour.

The four fingers. They were four fingers and a thumb and they were warm and they closed on the bar when she told them to.

The arm. It was open. It had been open before she went down and it was open after and she got it back about nine minutes later.

The nine holds out of eleven in the left hand. She went through them against the edge of the stone one at a time, slowly, in the yard, in the light.

The count of breath. Four. Four everywhere, at rest and after, exactly as it had been at the fourth hour of the first of a month.

Nothing broke in her that she has not had back once.

## The Human Sentence

She lay on the stone in a yard for about four minutes after that and thought one sentence and it was not a stage and it was not a price and it was not in anybody’s book.

A woman with four fingers and no cold in her hand is going to pick up a stone one day that she should not have picked up, and there is nobody within nine days of road who is going to be there when she does.

That was it. There was no second thing under it.

There was a man behind a stack in that building who would have heard it and who would have come out, and she looked at the open end of the building while she lay on the stone, and he was behind the stack, and there was about four feet of brick between his hands and her, and he would have heard the block hit the bar and he had not come out.

## The Thing She Cannot Do Any More

The night after the fourth she was on flat ground about a mile east of the yard with nothing lit within three miles and it was the kind of night that had put her under a bank a month ago in a different way.

She lay down at the ninth hour and waited to find out whether the night was going to be one of the bad ones, the way she had done every night of four years, by the instrument in her hand.

The instrument was warm.

She lay on flat ground until about the second hour with her hand open on her knee and it stayed warm and the wind came off the flat and went through her and the ground came up through her and neither of them came out at her hands.

That is what she had lost. It was not a finger and it was not a stage and it was not a price on a board in a gateway.

She could not tell by feel whether a night would hurt her.

## What She Wanted

She wanted the feeling back in four fingers and a thumb.

Not the index. That had gone on the twenty-first of a month four months back in a drain a long way from here and nothing had given it to her and nothing had offered to. She did not want that one and had not wanted that one for two months.

She wanted to be able to put her hand on a stone in the dark and be told something.

She lay on flat ground on the fifth of a month at about the second hour and wanted it the way she had wanted a thing she could carry in one trip on the twenty-eighth of the last month, which is to say entirely, and with no shape in it, and with no plan attached to it at all, and she knew before the light came up that there was not going to be one.

The first of a month had taken the feeling out of her hands and it had not put anything anywhere else, and she was going to be out on that flat ground every night for as long as she stayed on it, and she was going to have to get up in the dark every morning and put a hand on a stone to find out whether it was a good one.

She got up at the fourth hour and put her hand on a flat stone by the yard gate.

It told her the temperature of her own hand.
