# Copied Upward In A Fine Hand

She was on the open ground past the last hedge at about the fourth hour of the twenty-fifth of a month with the bar on the grass and nothing within about half a mile, and she had done the arithmetic on the way out and it had come out wrong twice.

Golden Core eight had been on the twenty-third of a month a month back.

Thirty-two days. Not thirty-seven.

She stood there with that in her and did not know what to make of it, because the last five stages had all come at between thirty-six and thirty-eight days and there was nothing in her that had any idea what a stage wanted, and a shorter interval was not a thing she had been given any way of reading.

She put her hand down flat on her own knee, and counted, and it was thirty-two, and she left it at that and did the other thing.

## The Stage

It came the way it always came, out of the ground she was standing on and not out of her, and there was not a living soul on that flat to be told and nothing within half a mile with a book to take anything down.

[Ning Yan, past the line, servant-born, under no ceiling any county holds. Golden Core, stage nine, is held.]

It said it once and then it was finished, and it gave her nothing to read and nothing to do about it.

## What It Took

Not her hands and not her arm and not a hold and not a count of breath.

She picked the bar up and it came up, and she stood there and waited for something in her to go that had gone every other time, and nothing went, and she stood out on that grass for about two minutes waiting for the bill.

The bill came at about the seventh hour and it came in the shed and it was not on her body at all.

## The Furnace, Finished

The ring had been burning in the corner of that shed by itself since the second hour of the twenty-second of a month three months back, out of clay she had carried in three loads with a hand that could not close on all of it.

She put three handfuls of ash on it at the seventh hour.

It did not go up. It came down about a finger’s width and it sat there and it did not need her and it did not come up either, and she stood in the doorway with her back to the wind and watched a fire that she had bought with a hold and with the whole of what was left in a man behave like a fire in a room.

Then she broke a lump off a box against the wall and put it in at the low end and the fire came up against the brick and stood there about nine feet high and it was hers.

That was the last of the six, and the whole of what finishing a realm past the world’s ceiling turned out to be was a fire in a shed at a mill that no office had built and two offices had vouched for, and a woman who could not hold the bar she carried it in.

## The Copying Floor, Second Hour

She went up the mill road at the second hour with the question she had brought from the eleventh of a month, and the man with the brush had his sleeves turned up and the top step cleared, and the two sheets were still on it.

“Ask it,” he said.

“I am not asking it.”

“Then it has waited nine days for nothing,” he said, “and you can stand there and not ask it, and I will go back to the sheets, and that is the ordinary course and not an injury to either of us.”

She asked it.

“Where did that second sheet come from.”

## What He Said

He came down four steps and stood at the bottom and did not touch either sheet.

“That one is not mine,” he said. “I have never seen it before and I did not write it and it is not in the counter’s hand and it is not in the hand I write in. It came down on the bundle on the twenty-second and it was in the bundle already written, and it goes back up on the first cart with whatever I do to it.”

He put his thumb on the margin of the sheet with one column on it, the one with her name at the top.

“This one is yours and I copy it out against the other book once a month because the two of them were put across from each other on the twenty-third of a month back. That is the cross-reference and it is done in the ordinary course and I have been copying books in this building for eleven years.”

He took the thumb off.

“I copy down,” he said. “Everything comes up that stair and it gets read against everything else that went up it. That is the whole of this floor. I have never in eleven years copied a thing upward out of this room and I am not the man who could.”

## The Finding

She stood at the bottom of that stair and looked at the two columns on the sheet that was not his.

The left-hand column was days. Dates down the margin and hours against them and a season marked at each end, and the word above the hours that said good, in a hand she could read from four feet off.

And under that column, at the foot of it, two more lines.

The first of them had her name at the left of it and the word material under that and the mill written against it and a rule under the whole of it and nothing at the other end of the rule.

That was the vouching she had asked for at a gate in daylight in her own name, on a strip of cloth with a ruled line and a line cut across near the bottom with a knife.

The second of them had a mark at the head of it that was not a name, and four figures, and a rule, and nothing at the other end of that either.

That was his.

And both of them were in the hand she could not read.

## The Four Sentences

He was standing four feet off with his sleeves turned up and he said the rest of it without being asked, in the ordinary voice, the way he said everything, and in four sentences.

“There is a line out west of this building about nine miles,” he said. “It is kept, and what is on it is paid for by the day out of a place I have never seen, and every month-end the ordinary traffic of it comes past this floor on a cart and is read against other things.”

He turned the brush over in his hand and did not use it.

“You are not the first person on this floor to be a person a road went past, and you will not be the last, and I have been the man who copies it for eleven years.”

## What It Meant For One Person

She had asked a man with a bench at a gate in daylight, on the twenty-sixth of a month four months back, to be put in a materials account against the mill.

She had done it because a furnace eats three coppers a third of a sack and she had a third of a reach a day at a gate, and she had said her name out loud in the yard and asked for a thing and been given it, and she had known at the time that it could not be taken back and she had not asked how far it could go.

It had gone four hundred yards further than she had asked for it to.

She is a person two offices will vouch for. She found out on the last step of six stages she had taken in seven months that the second of those two vouchings was carried up out of the office that keeps it and written down again in a hand she has never seen, by somebody she will never meet, in a room nine days of road away, and sent back down her own road so that a man with a brush would set her against herself.

And she cannot be un-entered twice over. There is no going back to being a woman who works off a wall with no name on anything, and there is no going back to being entered once, and the only thing she gained by asking in daylight was that she is the one standing here finding out, instead of a person who finds out from a strip about three weeks after it happens.

## The Line He Was On

He wiped the nib on the rag and squared the sheet up against the table edge and did not look at her while he did it.

“The second one is going back up on the first cart whatever I do to it,” he said. “I am going to set your column against it, because that is what I do with a sheet that has arrived, and in about three weeks it will have been up that road and down it twice. That is not me and it is not the counter and it is not the man who wrote it. It is what a road is.”

Then he went up the stair and got on with it, because it was the twenty-fifth and the copy was the copy and it was done in the ordinary course at the end of every month and nothing in the room had changed.

She stood at the bottom of the stair with the stove at her back and about nine men at their cups, and the second sheet lay on the top step with her name on it in two places and in a hand she had never been able to read.

The doors of that room shut on the twenty-eighth.
