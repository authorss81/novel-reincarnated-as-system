# A Hand In The Book

There were twenty-three pieces of dressed stone and brick and clay in the corner of the mill shed on the fifteenth of a month, broken down to a carrying size on the flat stone by the door, and she did not light the ring.

She had lit it on the first of a month at the fourth hour in the open yard and it had been burning ever since, and there was a heap at the back of it that would have been about nine days, and she stood in the shed at about the eighth hour on the sixteenth of a month and looked at both and put her palm flat on the brick and got nothing.

She had worked this out on the eighth at a gateway with nine dressed pieces on the ground, standing up, in daylight, in the ordinary voice.

The material is for the thing that answers, and the thing that answers went out of her on the twenty-fourth of the last month at the top of a ditch, and there is no material in the world that will put it back.

She had bought nine days of a fire that answers nothing and she had not lit it and she was not going to light it that night either.

## The Third Day Of It

On the seventeenth she worked half a reach at Pei’s gate and came back and stood in the corner of the shed and looked at the heap.

On the eighteenth she went east to the yard at about the seventh hour with the bar on her shoulder, because she had three days before the cart question came round again and because a woman standing on ground she has not walked for four days starts inventing things about it.

She did not get as far as the gate. She stopped at the elms on the road and stood in the light and worked out what had happened to him.

## What She Worked Out

A round is a line. She had got that on the road on the thirteenth and it had not got any better in five days.

The yard’s round is set down somewhere and the yard does what the line says. A cart goes out because a book says so, and a cart stops because a book says so, and every man in that yard from the man who kept the board back to the man behind the stack has never once in his life needed to know why the cart went when it went.

She had broken that on the thirteenth with a week of her own wall and a reach that came out of her foundation at the seventh hour on the twenty-fourth of the last month at a ditch between two farms.

A round is a line about a thing on that ground.

Her column is a line about her, in a book one flight above a counter two miles west, and it has been a live line since the fourth hour of the twenty-third of the last month, and every office that reads a line can act on it, and she has known that since a room with a brush and four other people in it.

What she had not worked out until the eighteenth of a month, standing on a made road about a mile and a half east of a mill at about the seventh hour with the light going, is that a hand in her line could put his hand in another line, and that the other line did not have to be about her at all.

He had put his hand in a line about a cart.

## What It Moved Him Toward

That was the second half of it and she stood at the elms and had it in about four minutes and it was not a comfortable thing to hold.

On the second hour of the seventeenth of a month five months back he had said it in his own words, in a room, to a man, and it had been the truest thing anybody had ever said to her about the country she was standing in.

Being read means being findable. And a line that is read can be acted on out of a room the man in it has never been in, by somebody who has never seen him, and the acting does not need him near it.

He had been inside a line since the seventeenth of that month and he had been findable since, and on the thirteenth of this month he had gone from a line that was about a woman to a line that was about a cart, and he had done it in one move and it had taken him about nine seconds and it had cost her a heap of dressed stone in the corner of a shed.

There was no place on that ground where he could do that and not be in it.

He had found one on the thirteenth, and there was more of that ground than there was road behind her, and every part of it had boards and books and lines in it, and he had proved to himself in about nine seconds that he could get into any of them.

## The Thing Under It

She did not say anything to him about any of it and she did not tell him to stop and she did not go over the road and put a hand on the shed and look at the ring.

There was one part of it that she turned over twice on the road and then put down where it was and left it, because it was the part that was about her and not about him.

She had spent nine days of her own wall on the twenty-fourth of the last month to buy a reach so that a box would go three miles.

And a box that cost a week of her wall had moved a cart.

Not a reach for the cart. The cart.

Whatever the office that keeps that list thinks it is buying when it pays for listed work off a board with a woman’s name on it, what came off a board with a woman’s name on it, on the thirteenth of this month, was a cart that went when it had no business going.

## What He Said

She got back to the shed at about the ninth hour with the light off the field and the ring out and the heap at its back, and she sat down on the boards with her back to the wall and her right hand open on her knee, four fingers and a thumb, warm, and the flat of the second one longer than it had ever been.

In the dark, with the clay cold:

“There is a line for the yard now,” he said, “and I am in it.”

She put her palm flat on the cold brick of the ring and held it there and did not take it off.

## What She Was Actually Afraid Of

Not him.

She had been afraid of three things on the flat ground east of a mill for six weeks and one of them had gone out on the first of a month and one of them had gone out on the twenty-fourth of the last month, and the one that was left was the one that had been there since the eighth of the first month of the last month.

Her column, in a book one flight above a counter, in a hand she had never seen.

A woman two miles and four hundred yards west had a name at the top of a column and a word above the hours that said she was good, and on the thirteenth of a month a man in a yard nine miles east had put a blank on a board because of her, and in about eleven days he was going to have to answer for that blank to somebody.

She had been the cause of the blank from three miles away and had not known about it until the cart was on the road.

And now there was a second one coming, because a line for the yard is a line, and lines go up, and there was a book one flight above a counter in a low building with a stove in it that had a strip of cloth come down off it on the eighteenth of the last month with three words on it in it.

She had never in her life wanted to be a person that a blank in a yard three miles away could be traced to.

And she was going to stand in daylight at a gate in about six days and say her name out loud and ask to be put into another one.
