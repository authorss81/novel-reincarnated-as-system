# The Ceiling Of A Realm

She went at the ring for four days and it came up a finger’s width and stopped, four days running, and on the fourth of them she stopped doing it on purpose, which was the first time in seven years she had ever stopped doing a thing on purpose.

The rule is the rule and it has not moved since a shed in a winter a long way from here: the furnace will not light itself. A stage is a room and a second pair of hands and a number of hours, and she has one body and four fingers and a thumb and an arm that is open half the time and eight of eleven holds, and there is no version of a stage that is one pair of hands.

She had asked once in her life, and she had asked a woman of sixty-one, at the fourth hour, in a shed at a mill, four months and eleven days back.

It took eleven days to come. It came as two hours of six days from a woman who is sixty-one and who walked eleven miles to give them, and everything she has to show for eight months of it is a ring of clay and a name in a book she cannot get out of.

There is nobody else.

She did the arithmetic four times in four days on the boards of the shed and it came out the same four times, and the arithmetic is not hard. There is no floor between here and the mill with a second pair of hands in it that anybody has offered her, there has not been one since the second of a month, and there is not going to be one in eighty-four days, because for eighty-four days nobody is paying for anything at all.

So she did not ask.

That is everything she did between the thirteenth and the seventeenth of a month, and it took four days, and she was proud of it for about a day and a half and then it turned into something else and stayed something else for the rest of her life.

## The Sixteenth

She went up the lane and through the gate at the third hour of the sixteenth because there was nothing else to do with an afternoon, which has been the reason for most of the things she has done for two years and is going to be the reason for most of the things she does for the next two.

Xie Changyin was in the long room at a table with her hands behind her back, reading, at sixty-one, in the last hour before the lamps.

“Your strip went east on the first with nothing on it,” she said, without looking up.

“I know.”

“I did not put it there. That is the ordinary end of a month and it is not a thing anybody has to be told.” She turned her head about a quarter of an way. “You have not come about the offer in a hundred and ten days and I have carried it every one of them and I am not going to say a word about it.”

“All right.”

“I am going to say one word about it,” Xie Changyin said, “and then I am going to stop, and it is this. On the twelfth I am going to have to tell you that the terms are final, because after the twelfth I go up the road for the season and there is a woman in this building who will offer them and she is thirty-one and she is very good at it and I have watched her do it eleven times.”

She stopped there, which was more than she had said in a hundred and ten days, and she went back to the sheet.

“I will be here on the twelfth,” Ning Yan said.

“I know you will,” Xie Changyin said. “You said a date out loud to a stranger in a doorway four months back and it took you eleven miles and three days and a turnstile, and there is not one person in this building who doubts you. That is not a compliment. That is me telling you that I have stopped arranging my season around you.”

## The Eighteenth, At The Fourth Hour

She was in the long room.

About forty people were at tables with a lamp on every one of them and the shutters up and the sun coming in off the lime wash, and she was standing at the end of the counter with her right arm open at her side, and she had a figure in her head, one thousand one hundred and nine days and six hours, and she had been carrying it for twenty-three days and had not put it down.

It started at the fourth hour and it did not ask her.

That is the first thing and she has never been able to get it into a sentence since. Every other stage she had ever taken had a shape: a room, a ring, a lump of clay, a woman holding a rim with two hands, an announcement going out into the ground and the ground not caring. Every one of them had a moment where she agreed to it.

There was no moment. There was a fourth hour in a room with forty people in it and a lamp burning and the shutters up, and the small open place low behind her fourth rib, quiet since the twenty-first of a month four months back, opened.

Not a crack. It came open the way a door comes open.

## The Part She Could Not Do Anything About

She did not have a choice about it and she wants that written down somewhere, because it is the thing she has never been able to make anybody understand, and because it is the only time in her life she has been in a thing she had not agreed to.

She tried to stop it.

That is not a figure of speech. She put her hand flat on the wood of the counter and she set her feet and she did the thing she has done eleven times in seven years when a thing has started that she does not want to be in the middle of, which is to say no out loud, in the ordinary voice, and mean it.

“No,” she said.

It did not go anywhere. Nothing in the world failed. No book would not shut, no door opened, no person in the room looked up, and the no went out of her mouth and into the air of a room with forty people in it and came back off the lime wash and did nothing at all.

She can still say no. She just cannot make the no take.

She had learned that in a shed at a mill four months ago, on the twenty-ninth of a month, on a made road at the ninth hour with eleven days between the saying and the happening, and she had gone eleven miles and three days on a body with one arm at half to find out that agreeing to a thing and being able to get out of a thing are two entirely different powers and she has only got one of them.

And it started anyway. She did not agree to it and she could not have stopped it and she stood there in a room for about two minutes being the strongest person in it while the top of something came up through her like water through a floor.

That is the whole of what the layer said. One sentence, into the ground, the way it has said nine things in seven years, and it did not say anything after it, and it has never once explained or advised or begun.

Then nothing.

And that was the part that frightened her.

She was standing at the end of a counter with her hand flat on the wood and her breath gone and forty people reading. Two years of silence from that thing, since a furnace on nine days of road, and it had nothing to say now.

Not one word. It said the sentence and it stopped, and she has been listening for something in her own head for seven years and there was nothing in there, and there was nothing in there now, and she stood in a room with nothing behind her for the first time in her life.

She got out.

She did not decide to. She found herself at the door and then across the counter room and then out into the road in the sun with the bar left inside on a bench where she had put it, and she went about nine feet past the gate and she lay down in the dirt on her back with her right arm open and the small place behind her fourth rib going like a hand working a lump of clay under water.

The man at the gate sat on his stool with his cup in his hand and looked at her for a while and then he looked away down the road, the way gate men do, and he did not come over, and he did not call after her, and that is his rule and his own decision both.

## What Came Out Of It

She lay in the road for about a quarter of an hour and then she got up and went and sat with her back against the lime wash, and she worked her way down the list the way she has worked her way down every list since she was fourteen.

Her right arm was open at the side where it has been open half the time since a shed two years back. Her breath ran four counts. Four fingers and a thumb on the right hand, and the grip on a bar at the heavy end was closed to her for good and was still closed. Eight of eleven holds. Eighteen years old.

The aperture was cracked through and it was cracked through and permanent and it was the same. The small open place low behind her fourth rib was not the same, and that was the one thing she could not make into a line on a board.

It had been quiet since the twenty-first of a month four months back. It was not quiet now.

She put her left hand flat on the lime wash and pressed and could not feel it and that was ordinary. She put her hand on the place under her ribs and it was there, and it was open, and it was not going to close, and she sat against a wall in the sun at the fifth hour of the eighteenth of a month and understood that was the price.

Nothing closed it. There was nothing above her to close it with.

## There Is Nothing Above Her

That is what the ninth stage is and she worked it out on the back of a hand over four days, because there is nobody she can say it to and she says everything out loud exactly once.

Every technique she has ever owned runs out at nine. Every resource she is able to hold runs out at nine. There is no tenth and there is no place to go and no hand anywhere on nine days of road, or past the turn, or on any road at all, that can take her one further.

She is at the top of a realm at eighteen years old.

And the way out of a realm, when a person is at the top of it, is a document. She has known that since she was fourteen, the way a person knows where the door of a room is, and she has never once gone and looked at the door, because there was no reason to and because everything below it was still moving.

There was a piece of paper against her ribs. It had been against her ribs for four years. It said Foundation the first stage on the front of it and it had seven conditions referenced on the inside of it and a claim at the foot, and she had put it there herself with her own hands, and she had never opened it, not once, not in a shed in a winter and not at a yard and not on nine days of road.

She put her hand over it through her coat at the fourth hour of the twenty-first of a month with her back against a lime wall, and she sat there with her hand on it for a long time.

She did not open it.

That is the part she will not be able to explain to anybody later, and she has been sitting with it since the eighteenth, and she knows exactly why, and the reason is not fear and it is not that it might be a bad paper.

It is that she is going to sit in a room with a lamp in it on the first of a month and have a document read out loud to her by somebody qualified to read it, in daylight, on the ordinary condition, with a book open at the end of it.

She is not going to be read to again. That is all. There is a piece of paper against her ribs and it is the only door in the world and she is the only person alive who can open it, and she has just worked out that she would rather not know what is in it than have it read to her in a room.

She is eighteen. She is at the top of a realm. She has five coppers in a fold stitched shut and no bread since the twenty-eighth of a month two months back and a bar with a strap she bound out of her own coat lining and four fingers and a thumb and an arm open half the time and eight of eleven holds and a place behind her fourth rib that is not going to close.

And the last thing the world ever did to her arrived on its own, in a room, on a morning when an institution was being honest with her for the first time in four months, and she did not ask for it and she could not have said no that would have taken.

She has been a person who was useful to three different things for seven years and she has spent four months saying no to all three of them and the ninth stage turned up anyway, in daylight, with forty people in the room, at the fourth hour of the eighteenth of a month, four days before she went to hear a form read to her.

That is what it is like to be a resource. She had known it in her body for seven years and she had never once had it in front of her as a morning.
