# The Eight Coppers She Is Owed

She was on a bank on the made road east of the turn at the seventh hour on the twenty-seventh of a month and there was a team standing in the mud with the carts loaded and a woman of twenty sitting on the tail board of the near one with a whinny whip folded twice and going past at the speed of a loaded cart.

She had come out with nothing in her hands.

“I want to know what eleven miles east costs.”

Wei Ran got off the tail board.

“Eight for a person,” she said, “and nine for a load, and you have no load.”

She did not look at her while she said it. She said it the way she says everything, which is the way a person says a number they have said four hundred times, and she was not being unkind and she was not performing anything.

“And three days there and three days back,” she said, “and that is a road and not a season and I have not got a rate for standing still and I am not going to invent one tonight.”

## The Arithmetic

“I have twenty-one,” Ning Yan said.

“All right.”

“And I am owed eight.”

Wei Ran did not move and did not look up.

“You are,” she said.

“From you. Since the twenty-third of a month three months back. Nine hundred feet of cart track in two days at four coppers a day and I have not got the eight.”

“I know what you have not got. I have had them in my pocket for three months and I have not offered you once, because the first time I offered you, your hand went out about the length of your own arm and stopped in the air over a bench with the four on it, and the second time you did it with your whole weight on it and it went eleven inches and stopped.”

“All right.”

“And you have never once said one word to me about why, and I have never once asked, and that was two months ago and it is the same today.”

## What She Had And Had Not

“You have twenty-one coppers,” Wei Ran said, “and none of them are any use to me, and I am not saying that to be clever and I am not saying it to be kind.”

She turned the whip over once.

“You cannot be paid. I worked that out on the twenty-third of a month by watching you not pick up four coppers off a bench, and everything since has confirmed it, and you have never once told me and I have never once asked you, and I have got a team of four and a rate and a bank and I have run this road for six years and I have never in six years had a person who could not take money off me.”

“And the rate is more than I have.”

“The rate is more than you have,” Wei Ran said. “That is the plain way of it and I am not going to soften it. You have nothing. You have twenty-one in a fold in a door frame and eight on my book and neither of them is money to you, and a rate is a number that a person has, and you have not got one.”

## The Eight

She picked the whip up off her knee.

“I am going to take the eight and I am going to say the number out loud so that you know exactly what you are getting and you can argue with it.”

“All right.”

“Eight coppers. Eleven miles. Three days there, and if you come back, three days back. The twenty-one in your fold are not going anywhere and are not a price and are not in this, and if a man on that road asks you on the way what you paid for the walk, you tell him eight, and it will be the first time in three months that anybody has got a correct answer out of either of us.”

Ning Yan stood in the mud on a bank.

“Take the eight.”

“I am going to take it now and I am going to write it off against the fare, and it is not going into your hand and it is not going into my pocket. It is going on a line and the line is closing today.”

She took the pencil out of the side of the cart and put a mark on a board against the name of a yard four miles west, and she did it in four seconds and she did not say a number to him while she did it and he did not ask her for one.

She put the pencil away.

“That is the whole of the trade and there is nothing in it that is not a trade.”

## The Rate Of The Return

“One more figure and then I will let you get on with it,” Wei Ran said. “A man with nothing on him going east is twenty-nine. A man coming back is twenty-nine. And a man who goes east and does not come back is nothing to me at all, which is the arrangement and I do not make exceptions to it for anybody.”

“That is a bad rate.”

“It is the only rate in the trade that anybody quotes me on twice,” she said. “Everything else on this road is round. Loads, days, carts, teams. A person is a figure and a figure is either paid or it is not, and I have never once had anybody tell me they were half of one.”

She got the whip down off the tail board and turned it once.

“You will want to know if I have done this before with somebody who does not come back. I have. It was nine years ago and it was a man going east, and he did not come back, and I did not go after him and I did not tell anybody.”

“All right.”

“I have thought about it every year since and I have never once worked out whether I did the correct thing. I have worked out that I did the only thing there was to do, because the alternative was to leave a bank half on a road and go after a man with no money, and the bank would have been under water in a month.”

## What She Said Going

She got up on the tail board and sat down on it properly with her feet off the mud.

“You are going up the made road at the sixth hour tomorrow and the road goes four miles and then it does not, and I have never walked it and I am not going to.”

“No.”

“But there is a made road on past the mill and it is somebody else’s ground after that and everybody on it will want money you do not have and are not going to get. So: eat before you leave and do not stop for anything you can carry, and when it gets to the point where a man says a number to you, say the number back to him and do not agree to anything in the first hour.”

“All right.”

“I am not doing this for you and there is nothing in it for me either except that a carter who does not get paid does not go, and I am going on the twenty-ninth whatever happens, and I have said that out loud so that you do not have to think about it.”

The four men and the three carts went west.

## Eleven Days Written Out

She wrote it out at the first of a month, in the ordinary hand, in a column, the way she writes columns on those boards.

The second, the third, the fifth, the seventh and the ninth she would not be in a hundred-foot room with nine men in it.

The fourth, the sixth, the eighth, the tenth and the eleventh she would be at the rail at the sixth hour and at the gate at the seventh hour and four miles east of it by the ninth, which is a day a shed does not cost her anything because the shed is where she is going back to at the tenth hour.

The twelfth she would be somewhere on a made road.

The thirteenth she would be at the east end of that road with a woman of sixty-one in the ordinary voice.

And that was eleven days, and she counted them twice on the boards, and then she wrote one more line underneath.

“There is nothing at that floor that will be worse without me in it and nothing better with me in it, and the only thing in the whole of those nine days that changes is that nine men will not have a thing to read out loud to, and that is the first time in seven months that the only thing missing from a day of mine is a use.”

## What She Gave Up For It

It was eleven days.

That was the arithmetic and she did it on the boards on the first of a month with the bar under her right hand, and it was the shortest number in the whole of it and it took her the longest.

Two miles up the made road from that shed there is a hundred-foot room with nine men in it and a bench in oak four inches thick and eleven dressed stones standing along the wall with their faces out, and four times in the last two months there have been men in that room reading sheets to her and her reading them back, and every one of those four times had a use in it that was not a coin.

She had no reason to be at the lifting floor on the second, or the third, or the fifth, or the seventh, or the ninth, and she was not going to be there for eleven days, and the man of fifty-one who runs that floor was not going to be told why, because he had said in a room with nine men in it that he had not got a term for what she was to that floor and he was not going to invent one.

Nine men who had all worked out where she stands, and a sheet with two columns on it, and a floor a block short.

She put a lump out of the box in at the low end of the ring and it came up a finger’s width and stopped, which is what it has done every night since the spring and is the whole of what the shed is for.

## The Sixth Hour Of The Second

She went east at the sixth hour of the second of a month with a bar over her right shoulder and a cloth round the middle of it because the heavy end has been closed to her since before she came past the last working point and is not going to open again for anybody.

The made road goes four miles from that shed to a mill whose wheel has not turned since before she came, and then it keeps going for another seven, and she had never walked any of it and there was nothing on the boards about it.

She had thirty-five coppers in the fold in the inside of the door frame and bread for two days and eight coppers that had gone on a line against a fare four days ago, which were not hers and were not anybody’s.

And at about the seventh mile the ground changed and she worked that out without anything happening.
