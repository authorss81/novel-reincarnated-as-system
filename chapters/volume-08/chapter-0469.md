# A Sheet Under A Lamp

It came in on the eighth of a month at about the fourth hour, on a cart with eleven other things on it, and it went onto the bench in the long room and it was tied with a string and a slip of paper through the tie with a date on it, which is how everything comes in.

It is the fourth one in ten months and the finest of the four.

They had it four days before they brought it to her.

## Four Days Of A Sheet Nobody Can Read

She watched all of it and she did not say one word for four days, and the not saying was the hardest thing she has ever sat in a room and done.

The man with the brush took it off the bench and looked at the top third of it and put it down and took it up again.

The woman with the thumb gone at the first joint read it at the fifth hour and then read it again at the seventh, which she does not do. The man of sixty-one came down the room with the book under his arm and did not open it, and put his palm flat on the boards once and took his hand away, which he does at a table he does not trust.

And the woman of twenty-two came and stood at the bench and looked at it for a long time without touching it.

“It is a bad hand,” she said, to Ning Yan, at the third day, in the ordinary voice, the way a person asks for a thing they need. “You can tell. You did not want to tell me last time and you could.”

“No,” Ning Yan said.

“You could.”

“Yes,” Ning Yan said. “That is the ordinary condition and it has not changed.”

## What She Had Never Told Anyone

And then the man with the brush said, in the middle of a long room with a lamp on every table and about forty people within nine feet of it, the sentence that put a figure in front of her for the rest of her life.

“We have four days on it,” he said. “It goes east on the twenty-second with a string on it and somebody past the turn reads it in a month, and if it goes with nothing done to it that is a month of somebody’s work not done. Will you read it.”

She could have said no.

She has said no once in a room with a lamp in it, four months back, at the eighth hour, in front of a man of forty-three, and it went up the same road every answer has gone up, and there is a question on a floor with nothing at the other end of the rule, and she gave that up on purpose and it cost her everything she wanted and she was right.

She said no again. That was all it cost this time and she was braced for four days of a bill coming in and it did not come.

What she did instead was tell them the thing she has never told anybody.

“I know why you cannot read it,” she said.

The room did not stop. About forty people were reading and about forty people went on reading and three of them put down nothing, because nothing was ever put down in that room.

“You are going to tell me there is a hand in the world that I cannot read,” the man with the brush said, “and I have heard that sentence four times in forty years and it has been wrong four times.”

“It has not been wrong here,” Ning Yan said. “It has been wrong for every hand on this road that has ever come down it, and I am the only person on nine days of road who can read this one, and I have been able to read it for a very long time.”

“Then how do you know it is fine.”

“Because I have spent two years refusing to have one read to me,” Ning Yan said. “You cannot refuse a thing you have not recognised.”

She put her right arm open at her side, which is what it does.

“That is all of it. I knew what it looked like before anybody ever offered to read it to me and I said no anyway. And then one day four of you stood round a sheet in this room and asked me in the ordinary voice whether I would read it, and I did, and a name went into a book.”

She stopped there.

Nobody in the room said anything at all for about nine seconds.

## The Thing That Is Hers

What she did not say, and what she had been not saying since the eighteenth of a month four months back in a room with a lamp in it, is the part that is hers.

There is a sheet about her. There has been one for a long time.

It comes about nine times a year down the mill road in a hand nobody in this building can read, it has a name at the head of it, and the man behind the counter read it four times on a night four months ago and did not sleep. She was four miles east at a yard at the ninth hour of that night and did not know one word of it.

She has never seen it. She has never been told one word of it. She said no in a room and the no went up the road and there is now a question on a floor with nothing at the other end of the rule, and she put it there herself, and she is the only person alive who knows that the cost of that no was not only money and a finger and half an arm.

It was also the whole reason a woman of sixty-one walked eleven miles into a shed at a mill.

If she had let it be read to her in a room, in daylight, with a man behind a counter, then there would have been an answer, and the answer would have gone east on the first, and no stranger would ever have had to quote two lines and a date back to a voice in a furnace to find out what was going on.

Her no was right. She knows that. She has known it every day for four months and she has never once been able to make it feel like a thing she was allowed to have been right about.

## Xie Changyin In The Middle Of It

The woman of sixty-one had been standing four feet off through all of it with her hands behind her back.

“You have told this room that you can read a hand it cannot read,” she said.

“Yes.”

“You have told it why you can, which is that you refused one, and that is true, and I have listened to it, and I am not going to pretend it is a small thing to be in a room with.” She turned her head about a quarter of the way. “What you have not told it is why you will not read this one.”

“No.”

“Why not.”

“Because I cannot be paid for it and because I said no once already and I am not going to be a person who says things twice,” Ning Yan said. “That is the whole of my reasons and both of them are bad and I know it.”

“They are not bad,” Xie Changyin said. “They are the same two reasons I would have given. I am going to tell you what the room is going to do about it, because you have told it something it has not had before and it is going to want to do something about it, and the thing it is going to want to do is ask you again.”

“Is it.”

“It is going to want to ask you again on the twentieth and on the twenty-third and on the twenty-eighth, and it is not going to, and I am telling you that so that you can put it down and stop carrying it.”

## The Sheet Goes East

It went east on the twenty-second with a string on it and a date on the string and four days of somebody’s work on it that nobody on nine days of road had read.

She was at the gate when the cart went. She had not come up that day.

The cart went at the seventh hour with nine bundles on it and the boy on the front and the tarpaulin over four of them and not over the fifth, because the fifth was a sheet with a string on it and a string does not lie flat.

She watched it go past the turnstile and out along the long road, and she stood there with her arms behind her back and worked out, out loud, at the gate, in the ordinary voice, where a person stands who does not want to be seen doing arithmetic about a cart.

Past the turn a month. Past the turn again if the first one does not read it. It is the fourth one in ten months and the other three came back on a cart that does not go east with a note on them, which is a whole other thing and which she has been sitting on since the second of a month and has not put anywhere.

A woman of twenty-two stood behind her at the gate and said nothing, and she did not turn round, and the woman said nothing anyway, and that was the last of the fourth day and the last of that piece of it.

She wanted to read it.

That was all. She wanted to read it more than she has wanted to do anything in nine months, and she could have done it in four seconds, and there was a cart going east within the hour and a month on the far side of it.

And she has not got a way to read one without being in it.

That is what two years and a room with a lamp in it bought her. There is no instrument on this ground for a person who can read a hand and does not want to be entered by it, and she looked for one for four days at the end of a lane with a bar against a wall and a woman of fifty asking her about carts, and there is not one.
