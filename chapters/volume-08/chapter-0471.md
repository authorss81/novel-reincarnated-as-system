# The Version Where He Is There

It was the twelfth of a month and she had five days to decide and she used four of them standing in a room, and the thing that made it hard was not the room.

A sheet came in on the twentieth with a string on it and it was not the finest hand any of them had seen. It was simply a hand, and it was not one they had, and four people stood round a bench and read the top of it for a day and a half.

The man with the brush had the ordinary reason ready before anybody asked him for it.

“There is a girl on it,” he said. “Twenty-two. And there is a hand on it that is not one I have ever read in this building. So either it goes east on the twenty-eighth with a string on it and somebody past the turn has her in a month, or it sits on my bench for eleven months and nobody ever finds out.”

“You are asking her,” Xie Changyin said.

“I am putting the ordinary facts of the room on the table,” the man with the brush said. “She is the only person at this table who can read it. That is not an argument. That is a shape.”

“No,” Ning Yan said. “It is not.”

“You have not read it.”

“I have not read it.”

## The Thing She Was Not Allowed To Say Out Loud

The woman of twenty-two was at the bench. She had been at the bench twice since the ninth of a month five months back and she had not once asked, and she did not ask now.

And Ning Yan stood there with two true sentences in her mouth and she could not get either one out.

The first was: “It will be read past the turn in a month.” That is the ordinary trade, it is what the cart is for, it is what the man with the brush is paid for, and the woman of twenty-two knows all of that and has known it longer than Ning Yan has.

The second was: “I have read one of these in this room and a name went into a book and I did not know that was going to happen.”

She said neither. She said, “Put a string on it and send it east,” and the man with the brush said, “That is not what I asked you.”

“No,” Ning Yan said. “It is what I am able to do and you may put the two side by side in your own head and draw a line under whichever of them you like.”

## The Part Where She Was Not Going To Use Him

And then, in the middle of a long low room with a lamp on every table and about thirty people within nine feet of her, with the woman of twenty-two at a bench and a man with a brush and a woman of sixty-one with her hands behind her back four feet off her shoulder, something came up out of the floor in her.

It is not a sound. It is a thing that arrives the way a thing arrives when a person who is very strong is standing in a room and is thinking about a hand on a sheet.

She could have read that sheet. She could have read it in four seconds with her right arm open at her side and her breath running four counts, and she could have put the woman’s whole life on the table in front of everybody in the room, and the room would have written it down and the room would have been right to.

And there is a way of doing that which does not require her to touch the paper.

She has watched it work three times in seven weeks. It goes out of the ground under her feet and it does not come back.

It is not a message and it is not a voice and it does not go up a road to a woman of sixty-one. It goes into a list in the ground, and the list carries a piece of work, and any hand anywhere that goes looking for work will find it. A person who is standing in the room she is in right now could walk out of it at the seventh hour, go up the long road, and come back in a week with the whole of a fine hand in her head.

Nothing comes out of her body. Nothing comes out of her hands, or her arm, or a hold, or a count of breath. It comes out of the place behind her fourth rib that is still open, and it does not come back.

She stood there and felt it standing there ready to be used, and she wanted it, which is the part she has not been able to write down anywhere.

She wanted it for the woman of twenty-two. That is the honest shape of it. A girl of twenty-two with a hand on a sheet about her and a bad night in nine months, and there is a way of giving her the whole of it in a week, and it will not cost her one thing she can name, and she is the strongest person in every room she walks into.

And it would have been exactly what he did to a man at a gate.

## The Refusal

“No,” he said, out loud, in the present tense, in the room.

Four people in that room heard a woman of twenty-two say no to a man with a brush, which is what it sounded like, and four people were correct.

“I could put it where your hand already is,” he said. “You would read it in four seconds and it would go east in a week instead of a month and nobody would pay for it.”

He stopped.

“You already worked out that part yourself,” he said, “and you have worked it out four times, and you worked it out four times by going back and finding a man in a ditch, so I am not going to say it again in a room.”

“Then say the rest of it.”

“The rest of it is that a hand only works where somebody’s hand already is.” He said it at the same speed he says everything.

“I put it in front of you, and you read it, and that is a good afternoon and a girl of twenty-two finds out in a week instead of a month. And then somebody puts the next one in front of me, and somebody else’s hand is in a room, and a man at a gate takes a bar off a wall because a painted board said so.”

“That is not an argument, that is a reason.”

“It is not a reason either. It is a fact and I have known it since the sixth of a month and I could not do anything about it until the thirteenth.”

He said that in the present tense, with four people in the room, out loud, which is the first time in seven years he has said a thing about himself where anybody was standing.

## Why This One Is Not The Other One

She stood at the end of a counter and let him have it, and then she made herself say the thing out loud because she says everything out loud exactly once and this was going to be the only time she got to.

“You refused this once before,” she said. “In a shed at a mill, eight months ago. You gave a reason then and I have thought about it every day since.”

“I remember what I said.”

“You said that a second use is a second time to be sent for, and that the first one has not finished, and that you would not be told otherwise at the time.”

“Yes.”

“That is not the reason you have just given,” Ning Yan said. “That one was about me. This one is not about me at all.”

“No,” he said. “That one was about you and this one is about the shape of it, and it does not protect you.”

“Does it protect anybody.”

“No,” he said. “That is the finding and I have had it since the sixth of a month and it has not once made me feel better.”

## The Cost Of Refusing

She stood there and worked it out while thirty people read and a woman of twenty-two waited at a bench.

Nothing came out of the ground. Nothing came out of her.

A spend is a thing that comes out of her foundation and goes into somebody else’s work, and it is the only thing he has ever had that is his, and it has been seven weeks and six days since the last one and it is the only thing he has left that is his and it is still sitting there.

He refused it and it cost her nothing at all.

That was the first refusal in seven years that came out clean, and she had been braced for a figure for four days, and she stood in a room with nothing taken out of her and found that it was worse than being taken from.

He has never once refused for free. The other two were refusals in a shed at a mill and they cost her a cold place and a thing she could not name for two days afterward.

This one cost the pair of them nothing. It came out of him and not out of her, and she stood there and worked out that the reason was obvious and she had not seen it, which is that a refusal is the one act in this entire business that is his and not hers. He has two of them left in him and he has just spent one on a girl of twenty-two he has never met.

That is what he has left. She has been carrying a bill for seven years and she has just found out that the two things in this world that are his are a refusal and a silence, and he has used one of them in a room full of strangers about a person who is not either of them.

## The Cart Went On The Twenty-Eighth

It went east on the twenty-eighth with a string on it and a date on the string, and it had four days of a woman’s life on it in a hand that nobody in that building could read.

A month past the turn. A month past the turn again if the first one does not take it.

The woman of twenty-two came to the gate at the seventh hour to watch the cart go, which she has not done before, and she stood four feet off the post with her arms folded and her hand somewhere near the middle of herself without knowing it was there.

“I will know in a month,” she said, to nobody in particular.

“You might,” Ning Yan said. “Or you might not, and it will not be because of anything anybody did.”

“That is not a thing anybody says.”

“It is the only thing I have got this month,” Ning Yan said, and did not say the rest of it, and the woman of twenty-two did not ask, and the cart went up the long road and out past the turn with a string on it and did not come back.

She stood at the gate and wanted it to be wrong.

That is everything she took away from the twenty-eighth. It is not a small thing and it is not a thing she can put down anywhere, because she has one part in fifty-three of a number and no instrument on nine days of road for checking anything at all, and a man in her head has just spent one of two things that are his on a girl of twenty-two who is going to go on not knowing until she knows some other way.
