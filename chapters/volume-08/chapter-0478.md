# Forty-One Lines On A Rail

She walked west on the twenty-second because there is nothing else to do with a morning and there was nothing else to do with thirteen of them.

Thirteen miles: two of lane, eleven of made road, and a store five miles past the mill where the bread is. She did it in three days because she was carrying a bar she cannot hold at the heavy end over a body with one arm at half, and because there was no hurry at all, which is the first time in eight years that has been true of any journey she has made.

She bought a loaf at the store on the second day. Two coppers, five down to three.

She bought it because she wanted it. She has not wanted bread since she was fourteen years old and she has had five coppers and no bread since the twenty-eighth of a month two months back and a body that does not want anything, and she stood in a store with a bar against the wall and put two coppers on a plank bench for a thing she does not need.

That is everything she bought with it and she is not going to be able to explain it to anybody, and she ate about half of it on the second afternoon sitting on a stone at the side of the road with the bar across her knees, and it was the best thing she has put in her mouth in four years.

## The Shed

The roof cord held. The boards were the boards. The ring was at the low end with the clay in it that had gone down an inch and dried and gone down another inch over four months, and she put a new lump in at the fourth hour on the twenty-fifth because it is the only thing in the shed that has ever asked anything of her.

The door does not shut properly and she did not fix it. There is a wind under it that comes in at about a foot a second off flat ground and she has slept behind that door for two years.

## Eleven Days Of Nothing

She did not say his name for eleven days.

That has never happened. In seven years there are eleven times she has said it out loud into an empty room and got something back, and about thirty where she got nothing, and every single one of the thirty was inside a dozen days of a spend, and this was not inside a dozen days of a spend. This was eighty-four days after it.

She did not count. That was all of it, and she noticed it on the eighth and did not say anything about it then either.

She slept. She went down to the rail on the first and again on the fourth and both times she stood at it and read it and took nothing off it, and both times she came back up and did not say anything out loud to anybody, because there was nobody within nine hundred yards of her on either occasion except the water at the trough.

## The Sixth Hour Of The Seventh

There is a rail outside the lifting floor with a board on it, and the board was full at twenty-nine lines when she came to this ground two years ago and every one of the lines was measured by the weight of what came out of the ground or by the length of it, and she can take none of them, and she never could.

There are forty-one lines on it now.

She stood at the rail at the sixth hour on the seventh of the month with the bar against her leg and read all forty-one of them down and then read all forty-one of them down again, because that is what she does, and she got them the same twice.

Loads by the load. Dressed stone by the yard. A chain that wants oil. Nine men and a frame that is four hundred years of oak and comes down the wrong way. A leg, on a man of thirty-four, on the fifteenth of a month. And a heading in her own hand, put over a second column on a floor nine days of road west, read to her out loud by a man of forty in the middle of his own sentence.

Forty-one lines, in a hand she cannot read, on a board that is full, on flat ground four miles east of a mill whose wheel has not turned since before she came.

## Eleven Minutes

She stood there and read them the second time and then she did not do anything for a while, and the sun came up off flat ground the way it does.

And then she noticed that she had been standing there eleven minutes and nothing had happened.

Not the boy from the stair. Not the man behind the counter, who has asked her a question about a rate three times in eight years and put four coppers on the wood once and swept them back when her hand stopped in the air over them. Not the man with the brush, who has asked to see a slip three times.

A month ago four people on that floor asked her a question a month and wrote the answer down in the ordinary hand. And a woman of fifty asked her about carts every day for two months, and Ning Yan answered her, and the woman of fifty said nothing wrong with it.

None of them has come.

She stood at a rail and did the arithmetic and it took nine seconds and it came out where arithmetic always comes out: there is not one thing on nine days of road that wants anything from her at all. Not a sheet. Not a hand. Not a fine hand. Not a cart, not a bench, not a chain, not a counter, not a painted board, not a bundle of paper going east on the first with her column on it.

A strip came back down the mill road on a cart that does not go east because a room had read her column and put it in a book. That column has come out of that book. What is in the book now is a line that is not a name, and the date against the line is the third of a month, and a man of sixty-one wrote it in the ordinary hand and there is no hand in that building that takes it out.

She bought her own anonymity with her own usefulness.

She said it out loud at a rail at the sixth hour on the seventh of a month, alone, in the ordinary voice, and the wind took about half of it.

## Eighteen, At The Top Of A Realm

Three coppers in the fold in the inside of a coat, stitched shut, and bread for a day in a piece of paper.

Four fingers and a thumb on the right hand. The right arm open half the time. Eight of eleven holds. Short breath at four counts everywhere, including inside a loaded reach. A small place low behind her fourth rib that came open on the eighteenth of a month two months back and is not going to close, and there is nothing above her to close it with.

Nascent Soul nine. The ninth of nine. Every technique she owns runs out here and every resource she is able to hold runs out here, and there is no tenth, and there is no place to go, and no hand on nine days of road or past the turn or on any road at all that can take her one step further.

She is eighteen years old at a rail on flat ground, at the top of a realm, with three coppers and no season and no wages and nothing above her.

And a piece of paper against her ribs that says Foundation the first stage on the front of it, that she has carried for four years, that she put there herself with her own hands, and that she has still never opened.

She put her hand over it through her coat.

She has had eleven days of not saying a name and she has had eleven days of a number in her head that she has not put on one board or another, and she is not going to open it today, and she knows exactly what she is going to do about it and she is not going to say that out loud either.

## The Shed With Boards On The Floor

She went up at the seventh hour and let the door stand where it stands and lay down on the boards with her boots on, because she was too tired to take them off and she has done that eleven times.

She lay there a while with her hands behind her head and the light going off the boards and the wind coming in under the door at about a foot a second.

“Yuan.”

That is all. She has said it out loud about eleven times in seven years and got something back four times, and it has always come inside a dozen days of a spend. This is the twelfth.

Nothing came back.

She lay there and waited, which she did for nine minutes, and then she worked out what she already knew, out loud, on the boards, because she says everything out loud exactly once.

He has had nothing to spend for eighty-four days and there is nothing left to answer with.

A spend is the only thing he has ever had that is his. It comes out of the ground under her feet and it is the only thing in seven years that has ever made him move, and she asked him to put one down on the thirteenth of a month two months back in a shed with the door shut, and he put it down, and he has been in there ever since.

He is not gone. She can feel him in there the way she has felt him for seven years, in the room behind the one she is in, with nothing moving in it.

She lay there on the boards with that, and she did not make it smaller and she did not make it bigger, and then she waited for another nine minutes, and nothing came back for that either.

And she waited anyway.

That is the last day of this month and she is not going to count it and she is not going to put a mark on a board.

She lay on the boards at the end of the seventh day of a month with three coppers in a fold and a bar against a wall and a door that does not shut properly, waiting for a man who has nothing to say, and she was not going to be anywhere else.

The wind came in under the door at about a foot a second. The boards were cold. There was nothing above her in the world and there was one door in it and she had not opened it.

She waited anyway.
