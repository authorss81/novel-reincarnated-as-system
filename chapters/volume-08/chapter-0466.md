# One Thousand One Hundred And Nine

He was there at the fourth hour on the twenty-sixth, which was eleven days after she had shut the door and thirteen days after he had put the fourth thing down and said so.

He was there the way he is always there. That is the thing about it that she has never got over and has never once been able to put anywhere: there is no arriving. There is a man in her head and then there is a voice in the ordinary voice, and the distance between those two things is not a distance.

He did not ask what she wanted and he did not wait to be asked twice.

“One thousand one hundred and nine days and six hours,” he said. “That is the whole of it. From the eighth of the month it started in to the twenty-sixth of this one, and it is not a rate, it is a sum, and I have not given it to you before because you did not ask me for it.”

## Two Figures And Not One

She had come ready for one.

“Twenty-one days and four hours,” he said, “is what I put where a hand would find it. That is three times on this ground and I told you about one of the three.”

He stopped there. He did not add to it and he did not explain it and he did not put a word on any part of it, and she waited ten seconds for something to arrive the way things arrive from him, and nothing arrived.

“You did not ask me,” he said.

That was all the other half was.

She stood in the shed with her boots on the boards and four sheets on a bench outside and the bar against the wall and she put the two figures in the order they had been given and looked at them.

That is the shape of the man. Seven years and he gives a person all of it and the part of it she was angry about, in that order, and then hands her a sentence about the fact that she did not ask, and does not put the word “kindness” anywhere near it, and does not take it away off any of the seven years either.

He never once said it was a kindness.

She had been waiting two years for him to say it. That was the thing she found standing there with a number in her hands: she had built a great deal of the last two years on the expectation that a man would eventually come and name the thing he had been doing to her, and he had not, and a thing that is never named is not a decision anybody made.

## What She Did Not Say

She did not say thank you, because there was nothing to thank him for and she had learned that on a bank nine days of road west with a bar in her hands.

She did not say it was fair, because she did not know that.

She did not say she forgave him and she did not say she did not, and both of those were available to her at that moment and she took neither, because a woman who has been given a figure does not get to have an opinion about it in the same breath.

She said: “Say the first one again.”

He said it again. That was all he did, and he did it in the same words and the same order and the same voice, and it came out of him the second time exactly as it had come out of him the first time, which told her something she had not known, which was that the figure was not something he had worked out the night before to give her.

It had been sitting there the whole time. She had spent seven weeks working out a way to make a man with nothing spend something, and he had had this in him for seven years, and the reason he had not given it was that she had not asked, and the reason he had not given it was that she had not asked.

## The Part She Could Check

She spent the rest of the day on the arithmetic, because it was the only thing in a shed at the end of a lane she had ever been any good at.

One thousand one hundred and nine days and six hours, and the six hours standing there at the end of it like a man who has come home from somewhere and put his hand on the door frame.

She did it three times on the boards with the point out of the bar and got the same three times, which is not a thing she needs to do three times and did because she had not had a number in a long time and wanted the feel of one going in.

Twenty-one days and four hours is the part she has.

Five days and two hours out of the ground under her on the sixth of a month, told to her before it happened. Two loads off the low end of the frame on the fifteenth, which is Zheng’s line and a leg wrapped and eleven days, and which she has never once been told the price of. And forty yards of dressed stone on the twenty-first, laid on a painted board at a gate for a man who did not know it was there, and which is the one that did not come back.

That is three. That is everything he put where a hand would find it in seven years, or near enough, and the total is twenty-one days and four hours, and the whole is one thousand one hundred and nine and six.

She sat on the boards with her back against the door and worked out the proportion, because she is a person who works out proportions.

One in fifty-three.

## The Thing She Was Not Going To Do

She had a piece of charcoal off the low bank that a man had dropped at the end of the cut, and she got out the point of the bar and she put the point on the board and she wrote the figure.

One thousand one hundred and nine days and six hours.

It went onto the wood in her own hand, in the ordinary way, in a shed where the light came in under the door because the door does not shut properly.

Then she sat looking at it and she worked out the reason she had not wanted to write it, and it was not the reason she had expected.

She could not write a false line. That has been true for a very long time — a line she puts in ink in her own hand is a thing she means, and there is no hand she can pay to enter one for her, and she has leaned on that the way other people lean on a wall.

It had not done her one thing’s worth of good in a shed at the end of a lane.

Because this was not false. This was the truest line she has ever written, and she had put it on a board in a shed two miles past a building, and every true line she has ever put anywhere on this ground has gone up a road in a bundle and come back with something done to somebody. A column is not a record. A column is a request.

She got the point of the bar and she went over the figure and scraped it off the board, all of it, down to the wood, and it took a while because charcoal on a board that has been walked on for four hundred years does not lift, and she had to go at it with the corner of the blade and then with her thumbnail, four fingers and a thumb.

She put the dust on the floor with the side of her boot and the board went back to being a board with eighty-four marks on one end and one at the other, put there on the eighteenth by a woman who was keeping something for herself.

## The Six Days

The twenty-seventh she carried bundles. The twenty-eighth she was at the sixth hour on the bank and the woman of fifty came out of the far room with the day’s sheet in her hand and gave her the figure at the head of it, and Ning Yan matched it against the crossing sheet and it was right, and the woman wrote nothing anywhere and said nothing at all.

On the twenty-ninth she went four feet off the post at the crossing, because there was a reason to, which was that there were six lines of chalk on a painted board and she wanted to know whether there were still six.

There were six. Nobody had taken one off. She had thought, for about a day and a half, that there might be a seventh, and there was not, and the reason there was not was that a boy of fifteen comes down the lane for carts and a boy comes for carts and does not stop at a chair.

The chair under the low roof of the hut had a cloth over it and the cloth had been moved by the wind. The hut had a light in it in the middle of the afternoon, which it had not had on the nineteenth.

She stood there in the rain for a while and then she went back down the lane, because there was nothing else to do with an afternoon and because she had worked out, somewhere around the fourth hundred yard, that a woman who counts things to find out what is happening to her is going to end up counting herself, and that she did not want to find out.

That night in the shed she did the last piece of the arithmetic, and the last piece was the one that was going to keep her for four days.

She had twenty-one days and four hours in her hand and one thousand one hundred and nine and six, and the gap between the two numbers was one thousand and eighty-eight days and two hours.

She did not have anything to do with those either. Not yet. She had a figure with a hole in it and she was not going to spend four days in a shed being angry about the shape of a hole, because she had a floor to be on at the sixth hour in the morning and a man with a leg wrapped going thirty times the length of a cut.
