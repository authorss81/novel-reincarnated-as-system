# The Price Of Not Being Found

She did not have anybody to ask about it and she got on with that, because it is a thing she has been getting on with since a yard on nine days of road at the age of eleven.

The Hunter would have told her.

That is the shape of it and she is not going to pretend to herself that it did not cross her mind on the twelfth, standing at the end of the cut with nothing on the bench. There was a man she had met at nine feet off who had been asked two questions by people on the ground and had answered both of them in the ordinary voice, and one of those two questions was exactly this one, and he is not here.

Both of his pages are used and she is not going to go and find him.

So she did it the only way she knows how, which is to stand at the edge of a thing and count it.

## What She Counted

Here is everything that has ever come for her in eight years, and she put them in a row on the boards of the shed at the end of the lane on the twelfth of a month with the bar against the wall.

A girl of eleven behind a furnace, entered as good at, because a hand she could read wrote it down and she did not know that was happening.

A strip that came back down the mill road on a cart that does not go east, with a date on it and nothing else, because a room had read a line of hers and put it in a book.

A woman of sixty-one walking eleven miles into a shed at a mill, saying two lines and a date out loud to a person in her own head, in a doorway, in daylight.

A woman of twenty-two asking a question in a room with a lamp in it, four people round a sheet, and a reading that went into the best book on nine days of road in four hundred and seven names.

A man at a gate putting his thumb under a line of chalk on a painted board four feet off a post and taking a bar off a wall.

Five things. Eight years.

And she went down the row one at a time, which took her until the light went, and what she found out was not one thing. It was three, and the third one was the one she had not expected.

## Finding One

Every single one of those five came out of a document.

There is not one door on nine days of road into Ning Yan’s life. There is one door and it is made of paper, and it has a hand on it, and the hand is either in an office nine days of road west or on a table two hundred yards from where she sleeps.

She got that in about nine minutes and then she sat with it for two hours.

That is not a discovery about the Order. That is a discovery about herself and it is eight years old and she is the only person alive who could have made it. She has been looking for a road in for eight years. She has wanted one badly enough to walk four miles with a leaded thing strapped flat against her ribs and not sleep, to take a stage a month early because a man offered, to stand in a yard and say her name out loud twice.

There is no road in. There has only ever been a piece of paper with a name at the head of it, and she has spent eight years working out how to be the kind of person paper comes for.

## Finding Two

The second one came on the thirteenth and it took her nine days to finish it and she finished it standing in the road at the crossing with the chain across it, at four feet off a post, which is where a person stands to read a painted board.

A person who is not read for four days is harder to find than a person who is read for four years.

She did not get that from anybody. She got it out of a gate she has stood at nine times.

She stood there and put the two facts on either side of her and looked at them.

The man at that gate did not go and find her. He went and read a painted board, because a painted board was on his post, and that is where his hands are every morning of his life, and it had been there before she came and it will be there after. The sill came down off that board because a voice put forty yards of dressed stone in front of a man whose hand was already on the wall.

Take the paper away and the hand does not stop. The hand is nineteen years old and it is in four trades and a wife and a girl of nine and eleven years, and it does not go and look.

That is what unread buys. It does not buy safety and it does not buy quiet and it does not buy a woman of fifty’s sheets. It buys the fact that there is no longer any paper in the world with her name at the head of it going anywhere.

And the cost of that is in the same row, in the same ink, in the same ordinary hand, by a man of sixty-one who has written four hundred and seven:

Nobody knows she is on this ground except the people she has stood in front of.

## Finding Three, Which Is The One She Was Not Ready For

She worked the last of it out on the fourteenth at about the seventh hour with four coppers in a fold stitched shut and no bread since the twenty-eighth of a month two months back, and she worked it out standing up, in the ordinary voice, to a lane.

She is eighteen years old and she is at the top of a realm, and that is not the finding.

There is no wages and there is no season. There is a rate of four coppers a page in a building two hundred yards west that her hand cannot close on and never could. There is a piece of paper against her ribs that says Foundation the first stage on the front of it and has never been opened.

There is no institution on nine days of road that is going to pay her for the only thing she is good at, and there is no longer any institution on nine days of road that has heard of her.

And there is a woman of fifty with a floor and a boy with a chain and a hundred and forty feet of lime wash full of people who need things read.

That is the shape of what she bought.

She has bought her own anonymity with her own usefulness. She said it out loud under a hedge four days ago in the rain and the rain took it, and she is saying it again now because there is nobody within nine miles to hear it and she is not going to be a person who only says a true thing once.

And it is the best thing that has happened to her in two years.

That is the part she has not got a face for. She is eighteen years old and it is the seventh best day of her life and it is the best one and she is standing in a lane outside a mill whose wheel has not turned since before she came, alone, and it is unbearable, and she chose it, and it is hers, and she cannot say so out loud to anybody including him.

## The List On The Boards

She got out the point of the bar on the fifteenth and wrote it on the board, which she has not done since she scraped a figure off the same board on the twenty-seventh of a month.

Five coppers. No bread since the twenty-eighth of a month two months back. Four fingers and a thumb. The right arm open half the time. Eight of eleven holds. Short breath at four counts everywhere including inside a loaded reach.

A place behind the fourth rib that came open on the eighteenth of a month and is not going to close. Nascent Soul nine, and nothing above it anywhere. A rate of four coppers a page that her hand cannot take. A piece of paper against her ribs that says Foundation the first stage and has never been opened.

And one line at the bottom, well away from the rest, where she could not count it with anything.

The third of this month. Declined, at the fourth hour, in a room, with the shutters up. Her name on it. Read back to her before it went in.

She did not write what she was going to do about it because she does not know yet, and she has looked at it for two days and there is no version of it that is not somebody else’s arithmetic, and the last time she let a sheet about herself be read to her in a room it cost her the only thing she wanted for two years.

## What She Wants To Do About The Paper

And then, because she cannot help it, because she is eighteen and she has been at the top of a realm for twenty-six days, she said the next one out loud as well.

There is a document against her ribs. It is the only door in the world. It has been against her ribs for four years and she put it there herself and she has never once opened it, and she knows more about what is inside it than she knows about most things, because she has had four years and eleven months to think about it and has not once opened it.

And she is at the top of a realm, and there is nothing above her, and the only way up that any hand in this world has ever written down is on that paper.

She wants to open it.

That is the want and it has been the want since the eighteenth of a month. It goes on the board at the bottom, under the third of a month.

She did not do it, and the reason she did not do it is not fear, and she has worked the reason out twice and it comes out the same both times. She is not going to say it out loud a third time.
