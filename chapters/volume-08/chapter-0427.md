# The Sheet At The End Of The Arm

The lifting floor is a hundred feet long with a wide door standing open at the near end, and the bench runs the whole length of the right-hand wall in oak four inches thick, and there are eleven dressed stones standing on edge along the left wall with their faces out.

Nine men work there. Eight of them were between her and the door.

She came in at the seventh hour on the nineteenth with a bar over her right shoulder because she always has one, and a man about nineteen came in nine steps behind her with a lamp in a hand and nothing else on him at all, and he was the one Xie Changyin had sent.

## What She Came To Say

She said it standing at the near end of the bench in the ordinary voice in a room with nine men in it, and she said it to Geng and not to the room.

“I want the lifting floor’s own work read by somebody qualified.”

Geng came off the frame with the chain in his hand.

“What for.”

“It is not for me and it is not about me and there is a woman of sixty-one at the shed who will tell you the rest of it. It is not the question I offered to ask her and it is not going to be. I am spending a question on this instead and I am spending it here, and I would like it done before the end of the fourth day.”

Nine men in that room had worked out where she stood in the time it takes to pick up a bar. None of them said anything, because nothing had been said that had to be said back.

## The Sheet With Two Columns On It

It was on the bench, squared to the edge of it, in Geng’s own hand, in pencil, nine men and nine days of work, and it had two columns with the same rule ruled under both of them.

The left one was the day the work was done. The right one was the day it was checked. On every line on that sheet except one, the two were the same day.

The second column had been empty for nineteen years and it had a heading over it that a man of about forty had written in his own hand on the thirteenth of a month, and it had four lines in it now and it was not empty.

He squared it and turned it round and put it on the bench in front of the young man with the lamp.

“All of it,” Geng said. “Nine days. And I am telling you before you start that there is a woman standing in this room who is the reason there are two columns on it and I am not going to talk about that while I am working.”

## Nine Days Of Figures

The young man was about nineteen and he had a good eye and no standing, and he read the figures the way she reads a board, which is fast and by the shape before the words.

He did it at the bench, out loud, in the ordinary voice, with nine men standing in the room listening to a stranger do a thing none of them had ever had done on that floor.

He went through nine days of dressed stone weight and face and length and frame, and he did the sums, and he read the dates back against the two columns, and he stopped twice to ask Geng a question about a figure that was not in the sum and was written in the margin, and Geng answered both of them without being asked twice.

It took about two hours and eleven minutes.

Nobody in the room told him anything.

## What He Said At The End

He put the sheet down, squared it to the edge of the bench, and straightened up and looked at Geng, and he said it in the ordinary voice, in a room with nine men in it.

“There is nothing wrong with any of it.”

Geng did not move.

“I have been over the weights and the faces and the lengths and the dates and the margin, and I have done it against the column that was empty for nineteen years and the four lines that are in it, and there is nothing wrong with any of it and I cannot find anything wrong with any of it.”

“You have read it once.”

“I have read it once and I have read it the way it is read, which is down the left and across to the right and then the check, and it is right on every line and the two dates are the right two dates.”

He turned round and looked at the eight men behind her.

“In nineteen years this floor has had its own work read out loud by a man standing on it,” he said, “and that is the correct arrangement and I am not saying a word against it. And nobody has ever stood outside this floor and read it, and I have just done it, and it is right.”

## The Hour Before The End

It took two hours and eleven minutes and the young man did it standing at the bench with the lamp on it and the sheet turned sideways, and he did it in the order a floor reads a sheet in, which is down the left column and across to the right one and then the margin.

Geng did not sit down once. He stood at the far end of that bench for the whole of it with his hands behind his back and he let a stranger do the thing he has done himself four thousand lines of without stopping.

Eight men stopped what they were doing three separate times in the first hour and did not pretend they had not.

At about the fortieth minute she went and stood at the near end of the bench with her left thumb on the second column, because she could not help it.

The line she put her thumb on was the one she had stopped a man in the middle of thirty-six days before. The day on the left of it is the eighteenth of a month and the day on the right of it is the twenty-ninth, and there is a heading over that column because of her.

## What A Floor Does With A Name

Geng took a pencil off the bench.

He wrote the name at the foot of the sheet, in his own hand, under the last line, and he wrote it the way he writes four thousand lines of stone weight, which is without any weight at all.

He did not say thank you.

There were nine men in the room and none of them said thank you and none of them said anything at all, and the young man from the east end of the road did not look like a man who had expected it.

Then Geng turned round and said, “Are you recruited.”

“No,” the young man said.

“Are you on a roll.”

“No.”

“Then what are you,” Geng said.

“A reader,” the man said, “and I am not one of anything and I have not signed anything and I have not asked to sign anything, and I will go back down the four miles at the third hour tomorrow.”

“All right,” Geng said, and that was the end of that.

## The Plain Part About One Person

She spent the only question she had on a stranger, and she did not spend it on the one she had offered to ask at the shed, and that one is still hers and is still unasked.

That was the whole of what she did with it. She had carried it since the tenth of a month and she had walked four miles up a made road to put it on a sheet belonging to a man of fifty-one.

And in exchange a young man who is not on any book anywhere had stood in the biggest room she has ever stood in and said out loud, in front of nine men, that nothing was wrong with the lifting floor’s work for the first time in nineteen years.

And then Geng had written his name at the foot of the sheet because that is what a floor does with a name.

She had got a stranger’s good name into a room of nine men who had all worked out where she stands, and she had done it by saying one sentence at the near end of a bench, and there was nothing at all in it for her.

She did not know what to do with her face.

## The Stone

The dressed stone was still four feet off its frame.

It was standing on the low side with a cloth over it and the near end of it clear of the low side and the joint of the floor under it had a sand line broken in it where it had gone, and it was not going back into that frame, and this floor could not put it back and this floor did not want to.

She looked at it for a while.

No man in that room offered to shift it and not one of them said a word about it, and nine men walked around a cloth over a stone the way they walk around anything that is where it is now.

## The Second Column

It was a heading a man of about forty had written over a rule he had kept empty for nineteen years, on the thirteenth of a month, because a woman of eighteen had read a sheet out loud and stopped him in the middle of it.

And there were four lines in it now, and there was a name at the foot of the sheet under them in a different hand, and it had not been explained to anybody in that room and it had not needed to be.

A floor that runs a second column is a floor that will be asked to run it again. She stood at the near end of that bench and worked that out in four seconds and she had been wrong the first time she ever read one and she was not going to be wrong about this one.

## The Door

She got to the door and stopped with her hand on the frame, the way a person does in a room they have decided to leave before anybody has said anything.

Geng said it from the frame, without raising his voice, to the room and not to her.

“That floor was a block short before she came and it is two blocks short now.”

She waited.

“And there is no term on any sheet in this building for a floor that is two blocks short because a woman was in the room twice,” Geng said. “I have been thirteen minutes on it since she came off the bench and I have not got it and I am not going to invent it tonight, and the day I invent it is the day I put a wrong figure in a book.”

He put the pencil in the groove.

“Come back when somebody on this ground has put a name in a book against you, and do not come back for me. The second column is not a favour and it is not going to be.”
