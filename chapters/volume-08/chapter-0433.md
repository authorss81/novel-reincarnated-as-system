# What He Did Not Say

The nineteenth and the twentieth of a month passed the way most days in that shed pass, which is at the sixth hour and at the second hour and on nothing else.

She went down to the rail at the sixth hour and read the board and could take none of it. She dug the run at the gate and got a copper. 

She walked four miles up a made road to a hundred-foot room with nine men in it and read a floor sheet out loud, and when she got to the line about a bell that had already gone she put her left hand flat on the bench and nothing came up, and the man of fifty-one said nothing about it and two of the nine said nothing about it.

She said nothing to anyone about the twenty-ninth.

## Eleven Times To The Boards

She had been to the boards eleven times in twenty-one days and every one of the eleven had been with something she had decided.

She had been argued with twice.

The first time was about the strip against her ribs and the second was about the ring of clay and what a furnace owes to the person who stands in it.

Both of those had gone badly and both of them had gone in about nine seconds, and she had walked out of that shed at the fourth hour of the twelfth of a month with a bar over her right shoulder, having been told no by a man she cannot see for a reason of his own that she would not have thought of.

She went to the boards on the night of the twenty-first at the second hour with both of those replies ready.

She did not get to use either of them.

## What He Said

He did not take any time about it, which is the thing she has learned to look for.

“I heard her at the seventh hour of the second day,” he said, “and I have been waiting three days for you to say it out loud and you have not said it out loud until now.”

That was all he said about that.

Then he said:

“The road is eleven miles and there are eight days and you have a bar you cannot hold at the heavy end and one arm that stands open half the time, and if you are going to be at that gate at the sixth hour on the twenty-ninth you are going to arrive with whatever you have got on the twenty-ninth.”

“I know.”

“There is a thing I can do about that,” he said, “and I am telling you the price of it before you ask me for it, because that is what I do and I have done it three times and you have noticed every time.”

“What is the price.”

“Out of you,” he said. “Out of the ground under your feet and not out of your hands, four days and six hours of it, and it does not come back, and I am not going to be able to tell you afterward what it took because nothing in this world will ever tell you what it took.”

## Why He Asked

“Why are you telling me.”

“Because you are going to ask me why in four seconds,” he said, “and I would rather answer it than watch you work it out.”

She lay on the boards with the boards under her back and the strip against her ribs and did not say anything for eleven seconds.

“No.”

“No.”

“You told me the price and then you said no.”

“I told you the price first and then I said no. The order is the whole of it. That is what you have been taught to look for in every man on nine days of road since the eighth of a month four months back and I am telling you the order so you do not have to go looking for it.”

## The Reason He Gave

He gave it in three sentences and he gave it in the present tense and he did not explain it and he did not argue it.

“A second use is a second time to be sent for.”

“The first one has not finished.”

“You have a want in this and I have watched you carry it since a strip came down a road and it is a real one and I am not going to tell you that you are wrong to want it.”

He did not say anything for a while.

“You asked me why, and that is the reason, and I am not going to argue it again and I am not going to be asked it again by anybody including you.”

“All right.”

“I am telling you now that I am not going to argue it again so that in about four months, when there is nothing left on this ground that will hold a road under anybody, you will not have to spend a morning working out whether I have changed my mind.”

## What She Had Ready And Did Not Use

There were four things ready and they were ready before she got to the shed.

The first was *I am not going to ask you for anything*. It was the true one and it would have been a kindness, and she had been carrying it for seven days.

The second was *then take it out of my own ground and not tell me how much*, and it had been ready since the twelfth of a month, and she had walked out of that shed that night with it half formed.

The other two were arguments about what eleven hundred years of a line on a sheet is for, and both of them had been in her mouth at the seventh hour of the thirteenth of a month in front of nine men.

All four of them went down into the boards with the clay and she did not say one of them out loud.

## The Nineteenth And The Twentieth Were Long

Two days is nothing. She has had worse than two days at that second hour in a shed at a mill and she has done it about four hundred times.

The reason those two days were long is that she had something to say and had been keeping it, which is a thing she has not had in twenty-one days. She has learned since the eighth of a month four months back that a person who stops talking to the voice in their head gets worse at it, and not better.

So she went down to the rail on the nineteenth and could take nothing off it, and she went up the made road on the twentieth and read a floor sheet out loud and came to the line about a bell that had already gone and had nothing for it, and there were nine men in that room and eight of them were between her and the door.

And at the seventh hour on the twentieth she stood at the near end of that bench with her left hand flat on the oak, in front of all of them, out of habit, in case.

Nothing came up. It had not come up once in two days and she had stopped trying by the fortieth minute.

She went down the made road four miles in the dark with a bar over her right shoulder and got to the shed at the tenth hour and lay on the boards with her hands behind her head and said his name out loud once into an empty shed, the way she has said it forty times in seven months and has never once got anything back.

She got nothing back that night either, and she lay there and worked out that a thing you are saving is worth more than a thing you have already said, and that she had gone eleven days without saying a word of it and had not known until the eighteenth that it was worth anything at all.

## What She Worked Out On The Boards

He had told her a thing costs her something before she asked, for the third time in twenty-one days, and he had told her no before she asked, and he had said the words *the first one has not finished* in a shed at the second hour of a month on the same ground where he had said them to her at the second hour of the twelfth, and he had not changed a word and had not explained one word further.

And she lay there and worked out the thing, which was that she had agreed to walk eleven miles on a date with a man who had told her, in his own words, that getting her ready for it costs her something he will not name and cannot argue about.

And that she would have gone anyway.

She put that out on the boards at about the third hour and she said it out loud twice, the way she says everything that has to be carried.

“There are two versions of the week I am going to have.”

“One of them is that I arrive on the twenty-ninth at the sixth hour with whatever I have got, and I have got that.”

“The other one is that I arrive on the twenty-ninth at the sixth hour with less of me than I have now and nobody will ever be able to tell me which.”

She lay there for a while.

“I picked one on purpose, and I picked it in a doorway in daylight, and I am not going to be at this shed on the twentieth saying the other one.”

## The Second Hour Of The Twenty-First

She got up and put a lump out of the box in at the low end of the ring.

It came up a finger’s width and stopped, the same as it has come up on four hundred nights in this shed, and she had stopped counting them.

She stood there and looked at it, because it had not come up at all for two days, and there had been eleven days of it before that at the width of a finger and not a quarter more, and she had not put one of those eleven into her own account until now.

Something in her body that had been shut since the thirteenth of a month came open with it.

It came open four inches inside her left side, low, behind the fourth rib, and it did not hurt and it did not move and it was there. And the short breath that is four counts everywhere except a loaded reach, where it is five and does not come back to four, came back to four inside a loaded reach for the first time since a dressed stone went the length of her own arm in front of nine men.

She lay down on the boards with her hands behind her head and waited for it to go back to five, and it did not go back to five.

There was nothing in that shed but her and the bar and the strip and the ring, and she was at the second hour of the twenty-first of a month with eight days to the twenty-ninth, and the day opened on the twenty-second.
