# A Man With Nothing Left

The first day after is the worst, and she had expected that, and being right about it did not help at all.

She lay on the boards from the light and she had nothing to do. That is all of it. She has had nothing to do since she was fourteen and she has never once found it unbearable, because nothing to do on this ground has always meant a rail with lines on it or a board she cannot take a line off or a floor that does not ask her for anything, and every one of those has been a kind of luck.

This was a different thing. This was a man in her own head who had been spending something, and had stopped, and there was nothing on the other side of him.

She lay there with her elbows out and waited for him to say something, the way she has waited eleven times in seven years for him to say the next thing.

He did not say anything.

That is what a spend is. It is the only thing he has ever had that is his, and it comes out of her ground and goes into somebody else's work, and it is the one thing in seven years that has ever made him move, and she has asked him to put it down in a shed with the door shut.

He put it down and now there is a man with nothing to spend and he is in her head for the rest of her life.

## Two Days

On the sixteenth she got up and carried bundles.

The floor runs the same as it ran on the ninth and it ran on the ninth when a man was in the chair at the crossing and a woman was in a nine-foot room at the back, and it runs the same now because a floor is not a thing that changes for one man and it was never going to be.

She carried bundles down the frame and up and she squared edges and she did not spill one. The woman of fifty was at the door of the far bank at the sixth hour and the day sheet went on the table and she went out of the room, and that was all of what the floor had to say about it.

At the ninth hour Ning Yan put the sheet down square and said, “Eight carts in and nine lines out, and the ninth is the same two loads twice, and there is a blank on the right against the ninth and that is because the cart came after the shut.”

“Nothing wrong with it,” the woman said.

“No,” Ning Yan said. “Nothing wrong with it.”

## Zheng

Zheng was on the frame both days with his leg wrapped and a bar over his shoulder, and she watched him from the bank and did not go and stand on the boards, because she had said what she was going to say to the woman of fifty in a doorway and she was not going to go and say it a second time to a man of thirty-four on a frame while nine men were working within nine feet of him.

He was up and down the length of it thirty times before the shut and he did not lean on the near rail at the top the way some of them do, and at the ninth hour he came off and went to stand on his own with his hands behind his back for a minute before he went to eat.

He looked across the cut at her once and she looked back and neither of them said anything, and that was all that passed between them, and he has a wife and a girl of nine and a rate in a book he cannot read and two figures against one line of his own work and he is going to be carrying that frame for another eleven years.

She wanted to tell him. She did not have a way to say it that would not put a lie in front of him, because there is no version of “a voice put forty yards of sill on a board” that a man at a frame can do anything with except be frightened by, and being frightened by it would cost him his sleep and he has eight days of the leg left to sleep through.

That is the arithmetic she did on the bank at the ninth hour on the sixteenth and it came out where arithmetic always comes out.

## What She Wanted

Here is what she wanted on the sixteenth and the seventeenth and she is going to write it down in the only place available, which is here.

She wanted him to be angry.

He has had a habit since before she found him and the habit is the worst thing about him and it is the reason a man went into a ditch and then lay down on a bed with his hands crossed. He leaves things out. He has been leaving them out for as long as she has known him and the leaving has a shape and she has known the shape for a long time.

She shut the door and she gave him a book with thirteen lines in it and a man in a hut and the words “you have spent three times and you told me once”, and the correct response — the only response a person has who has been let off by the person they did it to — is to say something. Anything. Half a sentence. A number she did not ask for.

He gave her nothing. He gave her the truth about the fourth one and about listings and about there being no line in the ground with him on it, and then he said nothing at all, and he has said nothing for two days.

She is eighteen years old and she has just won something and there is nobody to feel it with and the winning is a silence in her own head.

## The Rain

It rained on both days, thin and west, and she sat out under the trackside hedge at the crossing in the afternoon because there was nothing else, and the chain was across the gate and there was nobody lifting it.

The gate did not need lifting. That is what she worked out on the second afternoon, sitting in the rain four feet off a post: a gate is a gate, and a chain across a lane with nobody at it is a chain across a lane, and carts go round or wait or come back tomorrow.

Nothing is lost. Nothing is broken. The chair is empty and the board is on the post, and a boy of fifteen comes down the lane for carts, and a boy comes for carts, and does not stop at the chair.

That is how a place gets by for four days after a man dies in it. She sat in the rain and worked that out and it was the most ordinary thing she has ever learned and it was the worst thing she has ever learned.

The woman in the grey shawl came out to the crossing on the second afternoon with a cloth over the chair and stood there a moment and went back in. She did not shut the gate. She did not take the board down.

The board is the gate man's office. That is what it is on this ground — it is not a memorial and it is not a warning and it is not evidence of anything. It is five lines of chalk in a hand she cannot read, and it will be six before the month turns, and whoever chalks the sixth will be standing on this exact spot, because that is where a person stands to chalk a board.

## What She Is Carrying

The bar's strap is thinning at the shoulder point. She noticed it on the seventeenth morning and stood in the shed and looked at it and worked out she has to rebind it, and she has no twine and no money and she has the hem of her own coat lining and four fingers and a thumb and her teeth.

She unpicked four inches off the hem with the point of the blade from the bar and rolled it and bound the shoulder of the strap with it in a figure of eight, and it held her weight, and she did not need anybody to stand in a shed with her for six days.

Five coppers in the fold in the inside of her coat, stitched shut. No bread since the twenty-eighth of the month before last, which is the last of the two loaves she bought on the twenty-sixth and carried back in a bag. Water in her skin and the trough at seven miles and the seventh mile is thirteen miles from where she is sleeping.

She is eighteen. She is at a stage nothing on this ground can name and has been for two months, and the low place behind her fourth rib has been quiet since the twenty-first of a month three months back, and her breath runs four counts everywhere including inside a loaded reach, and her arm is open half the time, and there are eight of eleven holds in a hand with four fingers and a thumb on it.

None of it is a problem today.

That is the sentence. She is eighteen years old, she is the strongest person in every room she walks into, and none of it is a problem today, and it was not a problem yesterday either, and it was not a problem on the ninth of a month when a man went into a ditch in front of her.

At dusk on the seventeenth she got in and lay on the boards and said his name out loud, once, into a shed that smelled of standing water.

Nothing came back.

He is not gone. She can feel him in there the way she has felt him for seven years, in the room behind the one she is in, with nothing moving in it.

She lay there a long time with that and then she stopped talking and went to sleep, which was the arrangement, and which was going to be the arrangement for a long time.
