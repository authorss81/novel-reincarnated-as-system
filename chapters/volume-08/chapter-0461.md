# Twice For One Thing

She counted them over three days and she got thirteen.

Six books on that shelf and she had only ever opened four of them, and the two behind were older and thinner and ran back past four years into a period of the floor's life nobody had mentioned to her.

Twelve in four years, including the sill at the crossing, and one more in the two books behind it, and that one was from the fifteenth of a month.

She had the numbers before she had the story.

There was a line on a sheet dated the fifteenth of a month and it was a load off a frame at the low end of the bank, two of them, by the weight, the two that go up and not down, and it had a day in the left-hand column four days before the day the floor heard about it.

Four days. She read that line so many times over three days that she could have drawn it.

## The Second One

On the eighth she asked the woman of fifty one question in the room at the back and got a straight answer, which is the thing this floor does and is the reason she is standing in it.

“A load off the low end, two of them, on the fifteenth. Who took it.”

“Zheng,” the woman said. “He is on the frame this morning. He is the one with the leg.”

Nine feet wide and thirty-four years old, from the third of a month, and a woman of fifty said his name without a pause, which told her more about that floor than four books had.

“Two of them by the weight, up, and not down,” Ning Yan said. “What does that mean.”

“They are the two that go up,” the woman said. “You do not get them off the cart unless the bed is where it should be. Two days a month the bed at the low end is packed a foot high from what came down in the wet, and the men go up and clear it, and it is four times the work of the frame for the length of it.”

“How long does a man clear it.”

“A day,” the woman said. “A man and two helpers and a day.”

## What She Did Not Say

She stood at the table and worked out the rest of it and she did not say any of it, because there was a man of thirty-four on the frame eleven feet away and she is the only person on this ground who can do arithmetic on a page and she was not going to do it on him while he stood there.

The load had been cleared on the fifteenth. The floor had found out on the nineteenth, four days later.

And the right-hand column for that line had two figures against it.

Two figures, both the same, four days apart, against one line of work.

That is the whole of what the second man did not ask about and never will.

## Finding Him

She waited for him at the bank at the sixth hour on the ninth and did not go up onto the boards.

He came down off the frame at the sixth with his leg wrapped and a bar over his shoulder and sat on the low wall where she was sitting, two feet off, the way a man sits who has been on his feet since the fourth hour and has found the one place on nine days of road with room on it.

He looked at her. He did not ask.

“You cleared the low end on the fifteenth,” she said.

“I did.”

“Two of them, by the weight.”

“Two of them, by the weight,” he said. “It was packed a foot high and the bed had not been dressed since the month before. I had two helpers and it took us the day.”

## Two Figures

“Were you paid twice,” Ning Yan said.

He looked at her for a while. He is a man who has been paid for eleven years and has never once had a stranger ask him about it.

“No,” he said. “I was paid once.”

“Then the sheet has two figures against your line.”

“Then the sheet has two figures against my line,” he said, “and it has been sitting there since the nineteenth day of a month and I have been in this room every ninth hour for eleven years and I have never once looked at it, because a man does not go and read the book to see what it says about his own work.”

She looked at him.

That is a man who is correct. That is a man with a rate in a book he cannot read and a trade he can do in the dark and no reason in the world to have ever wondered what it says.

“You have not asked why,” she said.

“No.”

“Eleven years.”

“Eleven years on a frame,” he said. “Eight of them here.”

## Why She Believed Him

She believed him because she did the same thing on a shelf four days ago and has done it since she was eleven years old.

She has stood in front of a forty-year-old book and a forty-two-year-old book and a shelf of folders with dates on the strings and she has never once gone and read a book to see what it says about her, because a book is a thing that keeps things and she is not going to be one of the things it keeps.

Two of them. Two people on nine days of road east of a turn, standing in two rooms, not reading two books about themselves, and neither of them has any reason at all, and the cost of it is four days and a man on a bed with his hands crossed.

She had that on the wall with the two hands and it did not improve.

## His Mouth, In Five Pieces

She stayed on the wall and did not ask anything else about the sheet and that was a thing she decided in her own chest and it cost her.

“How is the leg.”

“It is a leg,” Zheng said. “It does what it is told on a good day.”

She looked at the wrap and did not say anything about it.

“A yard of sill at the crossing is a yard of sill,” he said, out of nowhere, and then stopped and looked at her as if he had heard himself.

She did not tell him she had heard it at the foot of the same post four days ago from a different man.

“My wife does the numbers at home,” Zheng said. “She does them in a book of her own and she has never once been wrong in it and she does not read this one because this one is not ours to read.”

“Is it not.”

“It is not ours to read,” he said. “She has never asked to. That is her business and she has not asked.”

They sat on the wall for a while with nine men going over a cut behind them.

“I have a wife,” he said, “and a girl of nine who counts in her head better than I do, and a trade, and a rate, and I am not going to be asked about a book by a woman I met on a wall.”

“I am not going to ask you about it again,” Ning Yan said.

“All right,” he said. “That is fair.”

## Four Days From The Third To The Eighth

That is when she worked it out.

She stood up off the wall and looked out over the cut at the low end of the bank where the bed was packed a foot high and had not been dressed since the month before, and she did it standing up with her back to the bed and she did it once and she did not have to do it twice.

The second figure against his line went in on the third of a month. The floor closed its books on the third and the books have a rule and the rule closes them, and a figure went into the right-hand column on the third against a piece of work done on the fifteenth.

She found it on the eighth.

Four days from the third to the eighth.

And a man was found at a crossing on the fourth day of a month, in a hut four steps off the ground, with his boots off and his coat on and nothing done to him by anybody, and the second figure went into the book on the day before they found him.

She put her left hand flat on the low wall and left it there.

He had carried one sill. Then he had carried the same forty yards of the same sill twelve days later. She had watched him do the first one from forty feet and had not moved, because nobody had asked her.

And a man on her own frame had carried a bed a foot high on the fifteenth and then been paid for it twice on the third, and both payments were in the ordinary book in the ordinary hand.

Nobody had ever come to take one of them back, and he had never once asked, and he had a wife who counts in a book of her own and a girl of nine and a rate and eleven years on a frame and no reason in the world to look.

She took her hand off the wall.

She went out and up the lane to the crossing and stood four feet off the post in the rain with her bar over her shoulder, and looked at the painted board with five lines of chalk on it and a chair standing empty on a step, and she could not read one word of it, and it was not going to change.
