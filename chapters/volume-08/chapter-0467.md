# Seventeen Hours Out Of Every Hundred

It took four days and she did it on the boards of the shed at the end of the lane with the bar against the wall and the four sheets coming and going on the bench outside, and it is the only arithmetic she has ever done twice over.

One thousand one hundred and nine days and six hours.

She started there because it was the number and finished there three times, and then she went underneath it, which is the only place worth going.

Twenty-six thousand six hundred and twenty-two hours.

## What A Day Is Good For

A day is a unit she has spent her whole life counting in, and for sixty minutes she let it be one, and then she made herself go and look at what it actually was doing in the sum.

A day is a way of saying twenty-four hours when what you mean is that the twenty-four hours go on whether or not anybody spends them.

Nobody spent these.

That was the whole of it, and she had been carrying the wrong end of that for four days without knowing she was carrying it. He had not given her a number of days in the sense she counts days. He had given her a number of days in the sense that a day is the only piece of himself he has ever been able to put on a table, because she thinks in days, and he has spent seven years learning what she thinks in.

So she took the unit away and the number stayed where it was and it got worse.

## Seventeen Out Of Every Hundred

She is eighteen years old.

She is eighteen years old and she has been alive for about six thousand five hundred days, and she worked that out on the boards out loud, because she does arithmetic out loud, and it came to roughly one day for every three she had slept.

One thousand one hundred and nine days out of six thousand five hundred.

She worked the percentage out three ways and got seventeen out of every hundred. Then she put her hand flat on the board and looked at it, and then she said all of it out loud into an empty shed at the end of a lane on the second of a month, in the ordinary voice, because she has said things out loud to empty sheds eleven times now and there is nobody to stop her.

Seventeen hours out of every hundred. A little over three years and eleven days out of the inside of a life she is eighteen years into.

And here is the part that she had not been ready for, and it came on the second and it stayed with her until the fourth: she could not point at one of them.

Not one. She has been beaten by men with rules in their hands, she has had a finger taken off at the far end of a bad flat, she has had half an arm open since the twenty-second of a month two years back and it has never closed, and every single one of those she can put a place to — a date, an hour, a ditch, a piece of dressed stone going the length of her own arm in front of nine men.

Three years and eleven days, and not one day in it.

Because it did not come out of a day. She knew that in her body and had known it for seven years and had never once had it in front of her as a figure, and a thing you know in your body and a thing you can add up are two different kinds of knowing, and the second one is the one that gets you.

## One In Fifty-Three

On the third she did the other half.

Twenty-one days and four hours is what he put where a hand would find it.

Five days and two hours on the sixth of a month, told to her before it happened, in a shed, in the dark, at the fifth hour. Two loads off the low end of the frame on the fifteenth, which is Zheng, and which she has never been told the price of and never will be. And forty yards of dressed stone on the twenty-first, laid on a painted board at a gate for a man who did not know it was there.

Three times. Twenty-one days and four hours. One thousand one hundred and nine days and six.

One in fifty-three.

She sat with that for a while and then she said it out loud, because saying it out loud is how she finds out whether a thing is true.

She has spent seven weeks and a board and a column and a woman of fifty and a man of thirty-four and four hundred yards of cart track she did not walk learning how to catch a man doing one thing in fifty-three.

That is what she has. That is the entire return on two years of reading sheets out loud in rooms for nothing, and she is the only instrument on this ground that can do it, and she found it, and she is going to be able to keep finding the next one, and the next one is one in fifty-three as well.

She put both hands behind her back, which is what she does when she is holding something she does not want to drop, and there was nothing in them.

## Saying It Out Loud To A Woman Who Counts Carts

She said the percentage out loud at the sixth hour on the third of a month, to nobody, on the bank at the east end of the cut, because it was the only figure she had that was not somebody else’s and she had been carrying it around for a day.

The woman of fifty was in the doorway of the far room with the day’s sheet in her hand and she heard it.

“Seventeen out of a hundred of what,” she said.

“Of a life,” Ning Yan said.

The woman of fifty looked at her for a moment over the sheet.

“That is a thing a woman says at twenty-six,” she said, “and it is a thing women in their forties say at a mill, and I have heard both and I have never once heard a man say it, and I have decided I am not going to ask you what you are seventeen out of a hundred of, because you will tell me the truth and it will be a number, and then I will be a person who knows a number about you.”

“All right,” Ning Yan said.

“It is not all right,” the woman said. “It is a plain thing and I have run a floor for nineteen years. Bring the sheets in when the light goes.”

## The Hole

One thousand and eighty-eight days and two hours.

That is the figure she has got no instrument for, and she gave it the third day and most of a night, and she came at it the only way she has ever come at anything, which is by listing what she knows and refusing to put a name on anything she cannot back up.

Here is what she knows.

A strip came back down the mill road in a cart that does not go east, dated the eighteenth, with nothing on it but a date, and something at the east end of a road had kept it and sent it back, and that is a finding.

A woman of sixty-one walked eleven miles into a shed at a mill and said two lines and a date and an ordinary voice out loud to a person in her own head, and did it in a doorway and did not ask for anything, and a person who is findable has been found.

Two lines and a date are the most dangerous things on nine days of road and he handed them to a stranger in daylight, and she has not worked out why, and she has had five months.

She has been read from outside this ground without her more than once and she has been addressed as a thing that can be sent for, and answered, and she knows all three of those words and has never once been able to put them in a column with a date against them.

And a man at a gate laid forty yards of dressed stone twice in twelve days and lay down on a bed on the fourth with his boots off and nothing on him.

None of those four things cost twenty-one days and four hours. She checked them twice. She has been in this business of figures for two years and she can add two columns and take one off, and four of the biggest days of her life came out of her at a price she could carry in a cart.

The other one thousand and eighty-eight is the standing.

And she got to the end of it on the fourth of a month at about the fourth hour, sitting on the boards with the door not shutting, and she said it out loud once, and then she sat there for a while with what she had said.

It is not for the work.

That is what came out of a shed at the end of a lane after four days.

The twenty-one days and four hours are the times he did a thing, and they are one in fifty-three, and the rest of it is not the price of any of the four times he has spent anything. It is the price of a person being the sort of person a place can put its hand on.

It is the price of a voice in a furnace that somebody, once, from outside, could find.

She did not say it any further than that and she is not going to. There was a door in it with a room behind the door and she got to the door in four days and she is eighteen years old and she has been wrong about rooms before.

She did not find out who set the price.

She has looked at that for four days and there is nothing in her that can see that far and there is nobody on nine days of road who could tell her. The only two people in seven years who have ever had anything to do with setting it are a woman of sixty-one who does not know her own price is being discussed, and a man in her head who has never once in his life claimed to have decided anything.

That is the honest finding of four days of arithmetic and she wrote it nowhere, because a column is a request.

## The Second Column

And then she got up off the boards and did the thing she had actually gone down there for, which had been sitting at the bottom of the four days the whole time like a stone under a post.

She wanted somewhere to put two dates.

The eighth of a month seven years back. The twenty-sixth of this one. That is the pair, and the pair is the shape of everything she has been reading since she was sixteen years old on a third floor of a counting house, and it has a name on this ground and it is a heading a man of forty wrote over a ruled cell nineteen years ago because she read a sheet out loud and stopped him in the middle of a sentence.

The day it happened, and the day anybody found out.

She had it. She had both halves in her own hands, checked twice, and there was not one book on nine days of road with a column for the pair, and there was not one person on nine days of road who was going to write a heading over one for her, and she had spent the whole of the four days being told a figure by a man who cannot write anything at all.

The day a thing happened to her was the eighth of a month seven years back, in a furnace on nine days of road, when she was a girl with four fingers and a thumb and nothing, and she was found.

The day anybody found out was the sixth of a month four months back, in a doorway, over a strip that came back down a mill road on a cart that does not go east.

Four days apart.

She stood in the shed and looked at a door that does not shut properly, and she worked out that she had been in a room with the second column in it on the second of a month two hundred yards west of where she was standing.

In that room there is a man of sixty-one who keeps a book in a hand, and a man with a brush, and a counter with a rate painted behind it. Not one of them has ever been asked, in their lives, to write down what it costs a person to be found.

She went up the lane at the sixth hour on the fourth of a month with the bar over her left shoulder and four coppers and no bread in her, and she did not know what she was going to ask for when she got there.

She knew the shape of it. That was the difference, all of it, and she had two years of being on the other side of it to know what the other side looked like from the inside.
