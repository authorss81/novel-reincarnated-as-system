# The Question She Had Not Thought To Ask

She worked it out between the fourteenth and the eighteenth without asking anybody anything, because she is good at that and it has not let her down in two years and there was nothing on nine days of road she could have asked it to anyway.

A reader is called in when a hand cannot be read.

That is the whole of the trade and Xie Changyin had said it in a doorway in nine words without once trying to make it sound like more than that.

## Four People Who Did Not Know

A boy of sixteen asked her about a cart because a sheet had come down the mill road about her and he had been sent to find out what a cart does.

A man of thirty asked her about a dressed stone because a dressed stone had gone the length of her own arm and he wanted to know which way it had gone.

A woman with a stove asked her what day the season turned because a floor needed a date.

A man behind a counter asked her whether there was anybody inside nine days of road who could read the hand a thing was in, because he had a question on his seasonal book with nothing at the other end of the rule and he had run out of other people.

Four people. Four times in a month. Four calls to a woman who can read, by people who had no idea in the world that they were making one.

She wrote the four of them out on the boards on the fifteenth, one under the other, with the date against each, in the ordinary hand of a woman who has been writing months out on those boards since she was eleven.

She looked at the four of them for a while.

Not one of those four people knew that a trade existed.

## Four Hundred And Six

And the four people who knew were four hundred and six names in a book eleven miles east of here, in a hand, and Xie Changyin had said the figure out loud in a doorway without any reason to make it sound like anything.

Four hundred and six people who knew what a reader was for.

One book out of that number had something in it that said she could do it, and the other four hundred and five did not, and there was exactly one way for that to happen, which is that somebody at that end had been told about a woman at a gate on a ground where no hand can read a line.

Somebody worked that out before she did.

She worked that out at the second hour of the sixteenth of a month, on the boards, in a shed with nothing in it, and then she got up and dug a lump out of the box and put it in at the low end of the ring, and it came up a finger’s width and stopped.

## The Thing She Did Not Have A Word For

And here is the part of the sixteenth of a month she has not written down anywhere and is not going to put in a book.

The four people came to her in a month, and she had spent two years being the one person inside nine days of road who could look at a thing and say what it said, and it had been worth nothing to her and had cost her a finger and half an arm, and she had got so good at the not being paid that she had stopped noticing it.

And then a woman of sixty-one had stood at her door and said *a person who reads a hand that other people cannot read*, and meant it about her, out loud, in daylight, with no force and nothing owed.

She felt something at the fourth hour of the sixteenth of a month standing in a yard at a turn with a bar in her right hand.

It was not fear. She knows what fear is and it is a thing about the arm.

It was not hope either. She has not had that since she was seventeen and she has not missed it.

It was a thing about being the only one. Four people a month had been asking her about carts and stones and dates, and she had been the only person on nine days of road standing in each of those rooms who could do it, and she had never once been in a room where that was the reason anybody wanted her in it.

Four hours of it. That is all she has ever had of anything that anybody wanted her for, and it went off like a cup going cold and she did not have a word for it and she was not going to get one.

## What She Could Not Ask

There is a man on the made road east of the turn with a notebook shut under his arm who has been in the same place every day for six months, and she has asked him four times in seven months and it is the only handle there has ever been on him, and he has never once asked her a question.

She could have asked him. That was the thing she worked out on the sixteenth and it took her most of a morning.

He never lies. He never threatens her, never touches her, never comes into her rooms, never pays for force, and has never once asked her anything. That is the whole of him and it is not nothing.

And she was not going to spend a thing like that on a woman she had met three days ago, and she was not going to walk four miles to a made road and stand nine feet off a man to ask him what a trade eleven miles east of here was, and she was not going to ask him anything at all, because the one handle she had was the only one there was and she was not going to spend it on this.

So she did it with arithmetic instead, on the boards, with nobody.

## Five Lines On The Boards

She got the order of it out on the boards on the sixteenth and it took her most of a night and it came out in five lines.

A hand cannot be read.

Somebody has to go and find a person who can read it.

That person says what it says, out loud, to whoever asked, in the ordinary voice.

That goes into a book.

And somebody, at some point, has to be a name at the head of a line.

She wrote the five of them down and then wrote the last one under the other four with a space between, and stood there looking at it.

Because a person who reads a hand for somebody else is a person in a room with a person, and a person in a room with a person is a person somebody can ask about later.

That is the whole of it. There is nothing else in that trade she can see, and there is nothing else anybody on nine days of road can see, because nobody on nine days of road has ever been inside a room where a hand is read.

She has been inside one. She was in one at the seventh hour of the nineteenth of a month with the door shut and a lamp on it, and six words came out of her in it, and she has not been in one since.

And that room is the reason. Not the answer and not the refusal and not the six words. The room. She had been in one, and the ground had found out.

## Four Names And One Book

She went back over the four of them on the seventeenth and wrote them out again with the dates, and then she did the thing four names does not let anybody do, which is to subtract.

One book out of four hundred and six has a thing in it that says a woman at a gate on that ground can read a hand nobody else on it can read.

There is no entry she has seen and no entry she knows of. But a man does not put a thing like that in a book because he thinks about it. He puts it in because somebody came to him about it.

Somebody had come to him about her.

She lay there with that for a while, and the boards under her back were cold and the ring was cold and there was nothing in that shed she could have used to find out anything at all. The whole of what she knew was that in eleven hundred years of practice on this ground nobody had ever come to anybody about a woman who reads, because nobody had ever needed to.

And now somebody had.

## The Second Hour Of The Seventeenth

She lay on the boards and said it out loud.

“There are four people inside nine days of road who would come and find me if a hand could not be read, and three of them have a copy of something I said out loud in a room and the fourth one keeps a book that shuts.”

“And there are four hundred and six names in a book that says who knows that is a trade.”

“And somebody at that end has put something about me in one of those four hundred and six, and I have not been there and I have not said a word to anybody, and the only thing I have done is refuse to hear one thing in a room.”

That was the sentence. She lay there with it for a while.

She had refused it in daylight with nothing owed and no force, and it had cost her everything she had wanted, and it had been the best decision she had made in two years, and that was true and she was not going to take any of it back and she was not going to be told by anybody that it had cost her anything, because nothing had failed and nothing had been written and no book would not shut.

And she could not see one single step between it and a woman of sixty-one standing at her door four days later asking whether she read a hand.

## What She Said Out Loud On The Eighteenth

The woman was not there until the seventh hour on the eighteenth. She came up the four miles in daylight with her boots mud to the ankle and she stood in the doorway with the folded sheet inside her coat and did not come in.

Ning Yan said it in the ordinary voice, at seven feet, in daylight.

“I would like to ask you one thing.”

“You may.”

“It is about one person and it is not about one thing about myself.”

“That is allowed,” Xie Changyin said. “That is the only shape of it that is worth asking. One thing about one person, and it will be done in a room with a lamp in it, and I will answer it in the ordinary voice and it goes nowhere.”

She came three feet into the yard.

“I am going to tell you what I have been doing for three days, because you have told me the only two things anybody has asked me for in two years and you have told me both of them inside four days, and that is not nothing.”

“I have not asked you for anything.”

“You have asked me for an hour and you have asked me for four days and you have told me the price of a page on nine days of road,” Xie Changyin said. “That is three.”
