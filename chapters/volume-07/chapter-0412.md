# No Last Day On It

The fourth of a month beginning 1,034 was the fourth hour of a day at the works and she was at Pei’s gate with the bar in her right hand at the seventh hour and the run going down to the far end of it, and there were eleven men on the bank above the gate because the drain was the second run and the second run was against the twenty-fourth and the twenty-fourth was ten days behind her and they were cutting it a second length.

She had dug four hundred yards of that drain in seven months and she could not take a line off the board for any part of it, and on the fourth she had seven coppers and a line at the counter cost ten.

## The Seventh Hour

It came up out of the ground between her boots.

It went down into the trench she had cut four days ago and it came up through the bottom of it, and eleven men on the bank above saw it come up through the floor of a drain run, and there was no wind, and the bar was in her right hand and four fingers and a thumb were on it and it did not move.

[Ning Yan, past the line, servant-born, under no ceiling any county holds. Nascent Soul, stage six, is held.]

It said its one sentence and stopped.

A man on the bank said something about the ground and two others looked at her and one of them looked at the ring, because she has been going up a finger’s width every night at the low end of a ring in a shed for five months and the people who work above that gate have heard about it from a carter and from no other source.

It took three seconds.

## The Eighth Hour

She went up the mill road.

She did not go to say anything. She has never announced a stage to anybody in her life and the sound of one goes into the ground past the last working point, and what happened on the seventh was that eleven men on a bank had seen it come up through a trench, and that was not something she could take back and was not something she was going to tell anybody about.

The man behind the counter had the seasonal book shut on the counter and his hand flat on it.

“Sit down,” he said.

“There is no bench.”

“There is a bench by the stove. Sit down or do not, I have not got time to argue about it.”

She stood.

## What He Had Done That Morning

He turned the book round and showed her the page and it was the third of a month, set up and open, with eleven lines on it and eleven names at the head of them in the ordinary hand, and about a third of the way down there was a name she knew.

He had got a pen out and put it back twice.

“There is your name,” he said, “from the twenty-second, and I have opened that book at the head of the season on a day when I do not usually open it, and I have put a pen to it twice.”

“Yes.”

“The first time I got as far as the first day and I wrote it and I could not get past it.”

He put his thumb on the page.

“A line is a name, a first day and a last day, and a figure against it, and I told you the three things on the third of a month at the seventh hour and I did not tell you about the figure because I did not think there would be a morning like this one before the end of the year.”

## The Figure

“A figure is a number of hours,” he said. “You go down a page and you put the hours against every day and at the end of the last day you put a total and the total is a number and the number is what a person is paid. That is the whole of what a figure is and I have written about four thousand of them.”

He closed the book half an inch and opened it again, which he has never done.

“I could not write one for you.”

“Why not.”

He looked up at her and he did not say it in a hurry, and he had already worked it out on the third at the fourth hour with a pencil and a slip of paper, and she could see the slip of paper on the counter under his hand with four things on it in pencil.

“Because there is nothing to write it against,” he said. “You are past nine. There is nothing above nine on that book, and there is nothing above nine on any book in this building, and above nine the hours are not written down against a day. They go on as a running thing, on a line that has got no last day against it, and there is no figure at the end of it, because there is no end of it to put a figure against.”

## The Plain Part About One Person

She is eighteen years old and a man behind a counter has told her in a room with a stove in it that the reason she cannot be paid is that she is too far up for a page.

Not that she is lazy. Not that she has not got a name. Not that she has not got a first day, and she has got a first day and he has written it down twice.

A column carrying a person past nine has no last day on it and no figure against it, so no office on nine days of road can enter her hours as wages.

And it is not the ten coppers. She had seven and she is owed four for a line of her own on the third floor and she would have found the other three inside a week, and she had been telling herself the money was the wall for seven months, and it was the money and it was not the money.

## What He Said With His Hand Off The Book

He took his hand off the book and put the pen in the drawer and shut the drawer, which is the end of a morning at that counter.

“I am going to say three things and then you can go up the hill.”

“All right.”

“One. I cannot open a line on this counter that has no figure on it, and I would not know what to pay you out of if I did.”

“Two. There are four other counters between here and the sea and I have heard about three of them and I have never been to any of them and I am not going to tell you they would do it.”

“Three.”

He stopped for four seconds.

“I am not going to tell you to go and try somewhere else, and neither is anybody else on this floor, because you have been here seven months and there are four other places within nine days of road and you have been to all of them, and you did not go to them for money. You went to them because you wanted a line.”

## The Counter And The Stove

He said the last of it standing up.

“I have been behind this counter eleven years and I have opened a thousand lines and every single one of them had a last day on it and a figure at the end of it, and I have never once had to think about either.

“You are the first person who has stood at this counter and not had one of them, and I have been sitting here since the fourth hour working out whether I could do it, and I cannot, and I would rather say so at the eighth hour than have you go away and come back in the spring.””

“Yes.”

“You can still have a line on the third floor for four coppers and I am not going to take that away from you either, and that is a different thing and I am not going to pretend to you that it is the same.”

## What The Board Said About It

There is not a line on the works board that is not measured off the ground, and there has not been one since the spring before the wheel stopped turning, and that is not a rule anybody made on that rail. It is what a board is.

She stood at that rail on the fourth of a month at the ninth hour and worked out that she had been standing at it for seven months waiting for a board to be full, and that when it was full there was nothing on it that wanted her, and that she would go on going to it every morning at the sixth hour because there is nowhere else to go at the sixth hour.

That is not an employment. It is a place she goes.

## The Board At The Rail

She went down the mill road and up the road to the works and stood at the rail at the ninth hour.

There were thirty-four lines on it and every one of them was measured by the weight or measured by the length, and she could take none of them, and there had not been a full board on that rail since the spring before the wheel stopped turning.

She read all thirty-four down and got them the same twice.

And that was the whole of it, and it was the seventh month of the first year of her being on that ground, and the bare board had been a season and this is the rest of the run.

## What She Said At The Second Hour

She lay on the boards at the second hour of the fifth with the bar under her right hand and said it out loud, because there was not a living soul in that shed.

“There are thirty-four lines on a board at a rail and there are four people within nine days of road who will pay me and every one of them pays by the weight or by the length or by the hour, and that is the end of them.”

She lay there for a while.

“And a man behind a counter told me yesterday at the eighth hour in a room with a stove in it that I am past the end of a page, and he is right, and I would have got the other three inside a week and it was never the ten.”

She put a lump of clay in at the low end of the ring. It came up the width of a finger and stopped.

“And on the seventh hour yesterday it came up out of the ground between my boots in a trench I had cut four days ago, in front of eleven men on a bank, and one of them looked at the ring.”

She lay there.

“So the pay question is shut, and I shut it, and there is not a person in any of it to be angry at, and it is going to be shut for the rest of it.”
