# A Cup Across A Bench

The twenty-sixth of the month beginning 974 was a yard day and she was at the turn four miles east at the sixth hour with nothing in her hands, and there were nine men on the north end and a second course going on over the sill that had gone down at the end of the month before, and it was holding, and a man checked it every morning and had never once mentioned it to anybody.

She was standing at the north end of the bed at a quarter past the sixth hour with her right arm open and her left hand in her coat.

## The Seventh Hour

It came up out of the ground under the low side of that yard.

It went under the clay stack, which is forty feet long and four feet high and has been there since before she came, and it came up through the strip of plank that a man had laid down on the hard standing over the place where his boot had been on the twenty-second of a month, and it did not move the clay and it did not move the plank.

It came up through the bed of a dressed stone nine feet off her and it stopped at the height of her knee.

[Ning Yan, past the line, servant-born, under no ceiling any county holds. Nascent Soul, stage five, is held.]

It said its one sentence and stopped. Two men on the north end were looking the other way and a third was nine feet off her and did not look up for four seconds.

## What She Felt In The Four Seconds Before It Came

That was the thing and she worked it out at the boards at the second hour two days later and it was true both times.

The fourth stage came on the seventeenth of a month nine days ago in the middle of a morning with nine men on that yard and two of them nine feet off her, and she had not had a hand in it and had not asked it for anything.

This one came at a quarter past the sixth hour on a yard day when she had not come for anything, and she was the smallest thing in that yard and the ninth of nine men and the only one there who could not hold the plank, the wedge, the chain or the end of the bar.

There was a stage underneath it this time of a person three or four stages above her, and that one has a floor that goes down eleven steps with a turn in the landing and keeps forty books open on a table the length of nine men.

The layer said its one sentence and stopped, and it did not change, and she did not go and find out anything about it.

## The Office End, The Eighth Hour

The woman in the office end came out with a cup in it.

She has been doing that since the middle of a month two months back. It is a cup of something hot out of a pot in an office end at a yard at the turn, and she puts it into her hands without stopping and goes back in, and she has never once asked her name, and she is not going to be asked.

That was the thirteenth time in seven weeks.

She came out and put the hand round the cup and held it out and Ning Yan took it, the way her hand takes a thing.

And her hand did not do it.

## What Happened

Not a paralysis. Not a burn and not a cold and not anything she had felt before.

Her hand went toward the cup and stopped about four inches short of it and stayed there, and she could feel the whole shape of the cup and the heat coming off it and the two inches of empty air between her fingers and the rim, and it stayed there for two seconds, and then it came back down to her side.

The woman in the office end stood in the doorway with the cup out.

“Take it,” she said.

She did not take it.

## The Cup And The Column

She cannot be given anything.

A gift that a person puts into her hand enters the column, and the column can be read, and a thing that has been given is a thing that an office can read her having been given. She can still be owed. She can still refuse to pay.

It is not a limb and it is not a hold and it does not touch her body at all, and it is the first time in five months that something has happened to her that she could not have told anybody about from the outside.

## What Was Said About It

The woman in the office end did not shout and she did not put it down on the ground.

She stood in the doorway for three seconds and then she said, in the ordinary voice:

“All right.”

And then she went back inside, and there was a cup of something hot standing in the open air four inches from a woman’s right hand, and there were nine men on the north end of that yard and a second course going on over a sill that was holding, and forty feet of clay stack, and it is not a thing that happens on nine days of road.

She stood there for a minute and a quarter and did not look at anybody.

Then she put the cup on the edge of the bench with the office end against it, which is where things go at that yard, and the cup was hot for four minutes and then it was not hot.

She watched it stop being hot. She was eighteen years old and she had watched nine hundred cups of hot things go cold over five months in a shed and in a yard and in a room with forty books on a table in it, and it was the first one she had ever been the person who did not take.

## The Plain Part About One Person

Seven weeks and thirteen cups, from a woman with a pot in an office end who has never asked her name and has never put one word about her into a book anywhere on nine days of road.

That was the entire sum of what had ever been handed to her on that ground without a price on it, and it cost her nothing for seven weeks, and it went nowhere, and on the eighth hour of the twenty-sixth of a month she found out that she could not have it.

She had wanted it. That is the part she kept coming back to on the walk back. She had wanted it every one of the thirteen times and she had not once thought where it went.

And a thing she is given is a thing a column can be read having. That is not a thing she worked out. It arrived in two seconds at the fourth hour of the third of a month and it is a rule and it is not going to soften.

## The Twenty-Seventh To The Thirtieth

Three days in seven and she went on going three days in seven.

The second course went on over the whole of the sill and a man with a rule in his hand checked it every morning and said nothing about it, and the three stones in the loose at the low side are under four feet of clay and two hundred yards of dressed stone now and are not going to be looked at again.

The gate paid a copper a day. The store was five miles west. The works board had eight lines on it and a strip from that yard went up the mill road with the bundle on the first at the sixth hour with a cord round the tray.

The third floor was on the six, the eight, the eleven and the fourteen and she read columns out loud at the edge of a table in the ordinary voice for four hours a month with her hands warm the whole of it.

And on the twenty-ninth, at the fourth hour, a man at a counter put a pen down flat in front of six men in a room with a stove in it and said that a line had been read upward on that ground for the first time, and that it had been read upward off a woman at a gate, and that the second line about her was in the seasonal book with a day against it.

He had the two halves of it and he had them the wrong way round, and she was four miles east at the seventh hour with a bar in her right hand and she was not there to tell him, and she worked it out on the boards at the second hour and got the same answer twice.

What went up the road was an answer. What came down it was a question, and the question was about a woman at a gate. He had it as one thing and it was two, and the second one cost her, and he will be right about the fact of it for the rest of his life.

## The First Of The Next Month

On the first of a month at the sixth hour she stood in a gateway a hundred yards short of the counting house and watched a cart go up the mill road with a tray on it and a cord round the tray.

There was a name in it. There had been a name in it on every first of a month for two months, and there is going to be one in it for a long time yet, and it goes up and it comes back down on the ninth, and a man with a brush copies it into a book on the floor below and cannot read the hand it was written in.

She is going to be at a yard at a turn on the ninth at the sixth hour, because it is three days in seven.

Four coppers in the fold in the inside of the door frame. Bread for three days. A gate, a bar, four fingers and a thumb, and an arm that does not close.

## What Was Still Open On The First Of A Month

There was a man on the made road east of the turn with a notebook shut under his arm who had wanted something from her since the spring before last and had never been refused it and never been given it, and he was still on that side of the post for the rest of the run, and the people who live on that line still did not know that she existed.

There was a floor above the third floor and a floor above the counting house and a room two miles up the mill road with a date in it that she had never seen shut, and she had gone up nine steps with a turn in the landing four times a month for seven weeks and had not climbed a single one of those stairs.

There was a hand on this ground that could read a line written in a hand she has never seen, and one person whose trade it was, and she had not been in a room with him and was not going to be.

There was a word that two people inside nine days of road knew and that was written down nowhere.

There were eleven people at a place nine days of road west whose business it was and not hers, and eleven hundred people at a stone that had never been spoken of in seven months.

And there was a strip that had gone east on the end-of-month cart three months back, and it had not come back on the cart that goes up with the empty trays, and no hand on nine days of road had ever sent anything east, and she carried the other half of it folded against her ribs every day and could not read the word at the head of it.

## The Seventeenth

She lay on the boards at the second hour of the seventeenth and put her hands behind her head and they were cold, and the ring was cold, and she said out loud because the second hour is for that.

“There is a page at a yard with two holders required against a date and there is a strip in a coat with two lines at the top of it in a hand I have never seen, and a book at a counter with my name at the head of a line and a day against it, and a stair book going up a road once a month with a name in it that I cannot read back.”

“And I cannot be given anything, so there is nothing left that somebody can hand me that does not end up in a column.”

She lay there.

“And that is the second time in about a month that I have worked out what I am for and found out it is a page.”

It came up the width of a finger and stopped, and she put a lump of clay in at the low end of the ring and went to sleep.

The cup was still standing on the edge of the bench at the yard at the turn with the office end against it, and there are seven weeks of them behind that one, and there is going to be another on that bench inside three days, and her hand is going to do the thing it did at the quarter past the sixth hour on the twenty-sixth.

She had not worked that out at the bench, because the rule was not new to her and a rule is not worked out twice.

Until the eighth hour of the twenty-sixth, the not being able to take anything was a thing about four fingers and an arm that does not close.

This is not about her hands at all.
