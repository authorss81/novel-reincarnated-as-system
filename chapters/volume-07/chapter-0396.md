# The Cord Round The Tray

The twenty-eighth of the month beginning 944 is the day the low side of the ground at the yard at the turn goes, and a sill course that has not been laid by then is not laid until the next dry ground, and the next dry ground is nine months off.

The line came off the works board at the fourth hour on the twenty-eighth, checked by the man with the rule in his hand and two of his men, and rubbed off with his own thumb.

She was three miles west of the turn at the sixth hour with a bar in her right hand when it came off, and she worked it out on the way and got it right, and by the time she got to the gate the bar was in the loose end of a run and there was nothing on the works board about the south end of any yard at all.

Twenty-nine stones instead of thirty-one. Two hundred and ten feet of bed instead of three hundred. And four feet of dressed stone in a hole on the low side with three stones stacked round it and eighteen inches of offcut on top of them.

## The Second Hour Of The Twenty-Eighth

She was on the boards at the second hour and there was not a living soul in the room, and a thing said at the second hour on the boards goes into no book at all.

“Four days and two hours.”

That is what came out of that ground on the seventeenth of a month at the eighth hour, in front of nine men, and there were nine men on that ground three days in seven for eleven days, and it came up to a pair of hands in the loose and not into the ordinary run of a building.

She said it out loud to the boards and then she said it out loud again, which is what a person does when they are about to spend a thing and want to hear what it sounds like twice.

“Four days and two hours. Off a sill line at the south end of a yard at the turn that came off a board on the twenty-eighth, and not off a death, and that yard will not pay anybody for it and the man with the rule in his hand is not going to know that I have said it out loud.”

“That is the price and I am going to spend it on nothing, and there is nothing on nine days of road to spend it on and nothing that will put it back.”

She put the boards under her and lay on them.

## What It Went Out Of

It went out of the ground beneath her feet, into a shed at a mill whose wheel has not turned since before she came, and it did not come back, and nothing in that going took a finger or an arm or a hold or a count of breath.

On the twenty-ninth she could not get it to come up in the shed and there was not another living thing in the shed and she had known that was going to happen.

On the first of a month at the seventh hour, at the counting house, with the man behind the counter and eight other men in the room, it did not come up.

On the second it did not come up in the yard at the turn with nine men on it and the woman in the office end and a cup of something hot put into her hands.

On the sixth, at the fourth hour, in a cold room at the top of nine steps with forty books open on it, a woman of forty-five put the back of her own hand flat on the table and it was cold.

“Wood’s cold,” Rao said.

“Yes.”

“It has been cold at this end every day since before I came on it,” she said, and turned the page, and did not look up.

## The Plain Part About One Person

She is eighteen years old and she has been warm in half the rooms she has stood in since the second of a month six weeks back, and she has just spent four days and two hours on nothing, and she does not know yet whether she has lost it or lent it.

Four days. Her hands went cold in a cold room that they had been warm in nineteen times, and the woman who owns that room noticed, said one sentence about the wood, and turned the page.

She could not tell her why and she was not going to try, because telling her why would mean telling a person in a room with forty books open on it that she has four days and two hours on a sill line at a yard four miles east, and that goes into a book.

## The Thirtieth

Eight coppers went west on the thirtieth of a month and there was bread for five days, and she had six in the fold in the inside of the door frame afterwards, and she did the sum twice on the boards and got six both times.

Fifteen at the eighth of that month. Twenty-three days at the gate at a copper a day, which is twenty-three in. Four trips west at eight, which is thirty-two out. Fifteen and twenty-three and thirty-two is six, and there is a length three days in seven at a yard four miles east that pays in a cup of something hot and no money at all.

She did that sum again in the morning before she picked up the bar, and it came out the same.

## What Was On The Cart That Was Hers And Was Not Hers

There were two things on that tray that had her in them and she could see neither.

One was a page out of a yard book at a turn, with a hole at the south end of it and a dressed stone in the hole, and a sill line ninety feet shorter than it had been on the morning of the seventeenth, and a margin with nothing written in it, because a man of forty with a rule in his hand and twenty-two years behind him did not have a term for any of it and would not invent one.

The other was a book the size of a school book with the months on it and a cord round it, going up the mill road for the second time with one line in it that had a word at the left of it where every other line had one word.

And there was a third thing she had not thought about, and worked out standing in the gateway with a bar over her shoulder.

That page from the yard had gone up from the yard to the counting house first, and the counting house had put it in with everything else that came into that building in thirty days, and it had not been read, and nothing had been asked what a hole in a yard was doing inside a bundle.

The page had changed hands twice and had been in two buildings without a reader, and it was going up a road to a floor where a woman of forty-five keeps everything that comes into that building.

## One Thing He Said

“You have spent it,” he said.

The boards were under her and it was the second hour and there was not a living soul in the room.

“You have spent it on nothing, and you did the right thing then, and there is no reason in the world to do anything else now. Four days and two hours out of the ground under you and it will never be entered in any book and you cannot get it back.”

“And there is a page on a cart with a hole in it.”

That was all of it. He told her nothing she had to do, and she was not going to do anything that night.

## The First Of A Month

The first of the month beginning 974 was a day off at the end of a month and it was five days to the end of the sill line’s grace, and nothing on that ground had come down a road since the fourth hour of the twenty-eighth.

At the sixth hour she was in a gateway a hundred yards short of the counting house, on the mill road, where she has stood four times in two months and never once sat down.

The cart came up the mill road at a quarter past the sixth hour with the tray on it and a cord round the tray.

Everything that has come into that building in thirty days goes up that road on the first at the sixth hour with a cord round it. The stair book goes with it. The seasonal book goes with it. The sheets from the copying floor go with it, and the pages a yard four miles east sends up the same road on the same cart because that is the cart.

And the man behind the counter cords it and does not know what is on it and does not ask, because a man behind a counter who asks what is on a tray has found out that it matters to him.

She watched it go past the elms.

There was a name in it. A woman of forty-five wrote it at the fourth hour on the twenty-eighth of a month six weeks ago, at the left of a line, in the ordinary hand.

It had gone up that road on the first before, and it was going up it now again, and it would come back down on the ninth and be copied out into a book on the floor below by a man with a brush who cannot read a hand she has never seen.

And the yard book of a yard at the turn was on that cart, and the page of it with a hole at the south end was on that cart, and there was nothing in the margin of it.

She stood in the gateway and watched it go and she did not walk after it.

## What She Felt About It Standing There

She wanted to run after it. That is the whole of what she wanted and she had it for four seconds, at the second gateway past the elms, with a bar over her right shoulder.

A strip goes up a road and the road is four miles and it takes a cart an hour and a half. She has four fingers and a thumb on her right hand and it does not close and she is eighteen and she can run four miles.

She did not do it and she worked out on the way back why, and the reason was that she would have been standing in a gateway with her name in her coat watching a tray go, and there is no version of a man seeing that which is about a tray.

## The First Of A Month, The Ninth Hour

She went down the mill road and out to the gate and dug a run at the eighth hour and got a copper, and the fire did not come up that night in the shed and she had expected it not to and it was different from expecting it.

That was the whole of the twenty-nine days and the four days and two hours.

There is a sill course at the south end of a yard at the turn, two hundred and ten feet of it, twenty-nine stones, laid square on four feet of bed, and four feet of dressed stone in a hole that four men in nine have walked round two hundred and ten times in eleven days.

And there is no line on nine days of road anywhere with her name on it, and a yard book is going up a mill road with a hole in it and no margin, and it will be read by somebody, and she cannot stop it and cannot read it and cannot be in two places.
