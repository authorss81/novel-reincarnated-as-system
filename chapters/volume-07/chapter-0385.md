# Ten Coppers For A Day

The sixteenth of a month, at the third hour, at the edge of the table with her strip in her left hand for an hour, and her hands warm inside her sleeves from ten minutes after she got there until about twenty minutes before she left.

She had worked out that on the road between the shed and that table they were cold, and in that room they were not. She had worked that out in about ten days and she was not going to work it out again.

It means going up that stair four times a month for the rest of her life for the use of two warm hands, and there is no line anywhere on that floor that has her on it.

## The Counter, The Same Morning

She went down the eleven steps and stood at the end of the counter, and the man behind it was writing, and there were nine men in the room and the stove going and the seasonal book lying shut on the end of the counter where it had been lying shut since the twenty-sixth of a month six months back.

He finished what he was writing and put the pen in the groove.

“You have come up that road four times in nine days,” he said, “and you have not asked me anything and you have not wanted anything, and that has been more use to me than anything anybody has done in this room for a month.”

“I have something now.”

“Then say it at the counter like a person at a counter,” he said, “and not like a person standing four feet off it.”

## What He Offered

He did not sit down and he did not stop doing anything, which is the way a man at a counter makes an offer that is not an offer.

“There is a line of yours in that book already,” he said. “I wrote it. You know that, because you stood in this room and said it out loud with nine men in it and I put it down and it is the twenty-sixth against it, and it is in here and it shuts and it will shut with that in it as long as this building stands.”

He put his thumb on the shut book.

“There is no more of you in here than that. Seven months and a day at a gate and a fifth of a reach, and none of it mine, and none of it in my hand, because a man at a gate puts what he paid in his own book and not in mine.”

“That is right.”

“If you want a line of your own in that book,” he said, “then it has to be a line somebody says at this counter and a man behind this counter writes down, because that is what the book is. It is what people said in this room. I do not have a way of putting a person’s handwriting in here and I have never wanted one.”

## The Price Of It

“How much is it.”

“Ten coppers.”

That was more than she had in the fold and she had worked that out before she got up the mill road, so she said the number and not the answer.

“I have seven.”

“Yes,” he said. “Ten is a day at this counter. It has been ten since before I came behind it and it is not because a line is hard. It is because a man at this counter who writes a thing down for a person has stopped doing his own work for a day, and there are nine men in this room and four of them are waiting on me and one of them is waiting on the stair, and that is what a day is.”

He put the pen down flat.

“You have not asked me whether I would do it for nothing,” he said. “Do not ask me that. There is one thing in this building that is not paid and it is my own time and I do not sell it to people who have not got anything else to give me, and that would be a favour, and a favour in this room is a thing that gets written down differently.”

## The Whole Of It In About A Minute

She stood at that counter for a minute and worked the whole of it out, which she could do standing up, because it was money and not a room.

Ten coppers is ten days of the gate. She gets one copper a day. She had seven, so she was three days short, and she could have had the ten in three days, and if she had had the ten she would have had no bread, because bread for five days is eight and she gets one a day.

So the ten is not a thing she cannot pay.

The ten is a thing she has never had in one fold in her hand in seven months, and if she had it she would have to choose between a line in a book she cannot reach and five days of not walking to a gate, and a person who does that is a person who has decided to stop being able to work.

And then the other half of it, which is the half that was never about money.

## What There Would Have Been Before The Eleventh

She worked that out in the same minute and it was the whole of why she was standing at that counter on the sixteenth of a month at the fourth hour with her hands cold.

Before the eleventh of that month there were three places inside nine days of road where a person could have paid four coppers to have a line put into a book in the ordinary hand that was not true.

There is one on the floor above that counter and one in a gateway at the works and one at a table at a yard four miles east. Every one of them does it. Every one of them does it for money and each of them thinks nothing about it, because a line is a line and a book is a book.

She could have paid four coppers at any of them. She has had four coppers in her hand eleven times in seven months.

From the eleventh of that month the list is empty.

Not hers, because her hand will not do it. Not anybody else’s, because the four coppers and the wanting live in the same place in a person and she has never had the wanting in the same place as the four coppers.

And the whole of that is hers, and it lives in her head alone.

## The Plain Part About One Person

She is eighteen years old and the only thing she can no longer do is write down something that is not true, and she cannot tell it to anybody on nine days of road, and there is no place on nine days of road where being able to write down something that is not true is a thing anybody is charging money for.

A false thing said out loud is still a thing she can do, and she has done four of them in seven months, and one of the four is in a book six miles from here that shuts.

She can still lie to the boards at the second hour with the flat of her second finger, and the boards are not a book and nothing reads them, and that is the whole of what is left to her out of every kind of writing there has ever been in her life.

There is a false line in that book at that counter with her name at the left of it and the twenty-sixth against it, and it is the only false line about her inside nine days of road, and it is hers, and she is not going to take it out and there is no hand in that building that can take it out.

She wanted the line. She has wanted a line of her own in a book since the twenty-sixth of a month six months back, when she gave this man one instead. It was four coppers and she has seven and she has wanted it since before she came past this mill.

## What She Said At The Counter

She said: “Ten is a day at this counter and a day at this counter is ten days at Pei’s gate, and I do not have ten.”

“No,” he said.

“I am not going to have it on a day when I do not have bread.”

“No,” he said, and picked the pen up, and went back to what he had been doing.

She did not say no to anything. He had offered her a thing and she had given him the two numbers it took and he had agreed with both of them and the offer is on the counter at that room in the ordinary course of a counter and has not been withdrawn.

She went down the mill road and out to the gate and got a fifth of a reach at three feet at the near end and three and a half at the low end, and it came up easy, and she put the clay in the barrow, and there are seven barrow-loads against that wall now and an eighth standing on its handles.

She did the gate on the seventeenth, the eighteenth and the nineteenth, and she went up the mill road on the eighteenth at the third hour and sat at the edge of the table for an hour with her strip in her left hand and twenty books open in front of her, and she read four columns out loud because a woman of forty-five asked her to, and her hands were warm for all of it.

And on the nineteenth, at the tenth hour, she stood in the gateway at the works and read the board from the bottom up and the ditch line was still second from the bottom, by the length, two weeks, and the man who kept the list had put a word against it in the ruled margin at the left of the board, in his own hand, where the word was a date.

And on the way back she worked out that the whole of it is that she would have to have ten coppers in her hand on one afternoon, and there is no way on nine days of road of having ten coppers on one afternoon, and that the only person who has ever put ten coppers in her hand in seven months is a man behind a counter who charges ten coppers for a day of his own work and is right about the price of it.
