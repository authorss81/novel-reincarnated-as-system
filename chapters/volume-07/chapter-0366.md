# What Did Not Come Back

She was at the yard gate of the works at about the seventh hour on the third of a month with nothing in her hands, because the east cart had gone out of that yard at a quarter past the sixth hour with a strip and a stone on its tail-board and she wanted to see whether it came back in at the same hour.

It did not come back at the seventh hour. It did not come back at the eighth or the ninth.

She stood at that gate from the seventh hour to about half past nine and then she went and did a fifth of a reach and got one copper and put the clay in the ring.

## The Fourth

The east cart came in at about the fourth hour on the fourth, behind a load of timber that had been standing in a yard on the made road since before she came past the mill.

It came in empty behind it.

She was at the gate post when it came and she looked at the tail-board from nine feet off and the tail-board was bare, and the timber was off, and the chains were back on the cart bed where they lived.

She stood there and looked at it for about as long as it takes to count nine.

It had gone out of a yard three miles from a gate with a strip on its tail-board and it had come back in empty, and a carter does not leave a strip on the tail-board of a cart at the far end of nine miles. He had put it somewhere, or it had been taken off it, and either way the cart had come back.

So the strip had not come back, and the cart had, and those were two different facts and the difference between them was the whole of the thing she could learn that day.

## What She Could Learn

She worked it out standing at the gate post in the sun, and it came out in about four lines.

A strip that goes up the mill road comes back down it on the ninth, with the empty trays, because that cart goes there and comes here and it has nothing else to do.

A strip that goes east does not come back on that cart, because that cart does not go east.

That was it. That was the whole mechanism, and it had been available to her at the fourth hour on the second of a month and she had got it from a man with a chain over his cart bed while she was asking him about his road.

There was no room in it and no trick in it and nothing anybody had to want of her. A reading had left the only room on nine days of road that could fetch anybody for it, and it had gone where the reading goes to be agreed about, and the room it left did not have a door back from that direction.

## The Four Days Between

She worked the gate on the fourth, the fifth, the sixth and the seventh at a fifth of a reach and one copper a day, and she went to the store on the fifth with eight coppers and came back with bread for five days, and she put a third of a sack of clay into the ring on the evening of the sixth and the ring came up at the low end about an hour later and stood there nine feet high.

She went east three miles to the works every second day and read a board with a cover on it.

She did not go up the mill road. She had one question a month in her and she had spent it on the twenty-eighth, and she had not worked out what to do about a strip going east by standing in a gateway asking men about carts, and the strip against her ribs was the last piece of paper on that ground with her name at the top of it.

## The Ninth

The cart came down the mill road at about the eleventh hour on the ninth with the shafts down and four empty trays stacked on the bed and a stone on the tail-board, and she was nine feet off on the near side of the track because she had come up the road to see whether anything came down it.

There was nothing on it.

The trays were empty. Four of them, nested, the way empty trays are nested, and a rope coiled on the near wheel and nothing else on that cart at all, and she stood there and watched it come and go and go past the elms west.

## The Plain Part About One Person

She is eighteen years old.

Six days ago a strip about as wide as a hand had gone east out of a yard with a stone on it, and it had her name at the top of it, and there was a word at the head of the four lines in the middle of it that she had known for six days and had never once been able to read.

It was somewhere about eleven miles east of the gate post she was standing at, on made road, past a turn, with a man who had been turned back at the end of it four hundred times.

And she could not get one word about it out of anybody inside nine days of road: the counter could not read the hand, the man with the brush could not read the hand, and the two people in the world who could read that hand were not in a building she could walk to and were not going to write to her.

She had been entered twice in six months by men in gateways in daylight, both times on her own asking, and she had never once been able to read what either of them had entered her into.

And now the entry had left the room.

## What She Wanted

She wanted to walk four miles east up a made road and keep walking.

That was the want and it came up about eleven times between the ninth hour and the eleventh hour on the ninth, and she turned it over the way she turned everything over, which is by putting it down and picking it up and looking at it.

Nine miles east was the long road. Past the turn the made road went on for five miles and then it became the long road, and the long road ends at a place where a man with a chain has been turned back four hundred times, and everything her body had said about itself in the last six months said the same thing about the first four.

A fifth of a reach. One copper. Four fingers and a thumb. Eight holds. An arm open half the time.

She could walk nine miles. She had done nine miles of road inside that boundary nine days west and she had done it at fourteen and again at sixteen and she could do it now in a day and be in a gateway by the ninth hour.

And there was nothing at the end of it she could do when she got there, and nothing she could do when she got back, and she would have spent two days of a gate on the going.

She went west at the eleventh hour and opened a fifth of a reach by about the first hour in the morning because she could not sleep and a gate is a thing that can be done at the first hour.

## The Season Turning

She heard it from the man who kept the list, in a gateway, on the eleventh, at about the ninth hour.

He had the cover off the board and the battens across and he was taking the battens down one at a time with his coat off, and the board behind them was not bare.

“Season turns,” he said.

She stood in the gateway and looked at it.

Four columns and a ruled space at the left and lines running into every column, and she could see from the step how many, and it was more than had ever been on it at once in the seven months she had been coming to this gateway.

She had been walking three miles every second day to look at a board with a cover on it.
