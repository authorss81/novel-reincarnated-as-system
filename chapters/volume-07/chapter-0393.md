# Two Men Short

The fifteenth of a month was a yard day and she was at the turn four miles east at the sixth hour with nothing in her hands and the strip in her coat.

The bed at the south end had gone from eleven feet to forty overnight, and the clay was stacked along it in a barrow row, and there were two men on the plank working the bar and a third with his boot on the bed.

The stone had not moved.

She stood at nine feet off for an hour and a quarter and she counted.

Four times they got the bar under and four times they got it up half an inch, and the fourth time the far end of the bar came up out of the stone’s own bed and the stone rode half an inch on the clay and came back and took a scrape out of the chalk line with it.

The man with the rule in his hand watched all four from twenty feet off with his arms folded and said nothing at all, which was the correct thing to do.

## What She Worked Out Standing There

A dressed stone of that size is not a thing a person lifts. It is about what four men weigh and it goes on a bar and it goes on a chain and it is set square with a plank and a wedge and it is stopped with a boot.

She has four fingers and a thumb on her right hand and it does not close. The heavy end of every bar on that ground has been closed to her since the twenty-eighth of a month four months back.

She cannot hold the plank. She cannot hold the wedge. She cannot hold the end of the chain and she would not be asked to, and there are nine men there and she is the ninth and the smallest, and she has been told twice what she can do and twice what she cannot.

She worked out that she could not do any part of that sill course.

That is what she worked out. She worked out that there is no line on nine days of road she can hold and no board line she can be put on and nothing at a yard at a turn that pays for a woman who cannot lift.

And she stood there for another quarter of an hour anyway, because she wanted to see it set.

## The Fifteenth, At The Office End

The woman in the office end came out with the cup and put it into her hands and went back in, and that was the whole of what happened between the two of them.

At the office door the man with the rule in his hand was standing with a chalk in his hand looking at the board propped against the wall.

He said it to the board and she heard it.

“South end is forty feet of bed and I have got nine men and the sill course wants four and I have got four and the four are all on the bed.”

He put the chalk in his pocket.

“Twenty-eighth. The low side goes at the end of a month and it will not carry a sill after the twenty-eighth and there is nothing I can do about that and I have told all nine of them twice.”

## The Sixteenth

The sixteenth was a gate day and she was out at the sixth hour with the bar in her right hand and four fingers and a thumb on it, and she dug the run in two lengths, four feet for the first six and three and a half for the rest of it, and the bar went in where the ground was hard and it came up easy.

She came off the gate at the eighth hour and went back to the turn, which is four miles east and a bad idea at the ninth hour in the heat of a day, and she stood at the south end for an hour.

The bed was at ninety feet and the stone had not moved and the bar had been put under it five times.

At the tenth hour one of the two men on the bar went to move a plank and the other one took his boot off the bed and put his weight down on the stone, the way a man does when he is working alone and has not stopped for a day and a half.

The bar went.

It did not go far. It went six inches and came down off the near edge of the stone with the chain on it and a sound in the yard that made four men turn round.

The stone came down an inch onto the bed and stopped, because there is clay under it and there has to be clay under it, and it stopped with the far end resting on the plank and the near end four inches down into the bed.

And the man who had taken his boot off the bed had his leg under the near end of it.

## What Was In The Yard

It is nine seconds.

A dressed stone on a bed of clay does not slide and it does not roll. It sits where it is put down, and what it does next is nothing at all unless something makes it do something.

Four men got to it. One of them took hold of a chain and two of them got the end of the bar, and the fourth one got down on his knees on the near side with his hands on the stone, and they did not lift it.

They waited, and the man on the ground got his leg out, and he got it out in four seconds, and then he sat on the plank at the side of the bed with both hands on the leg and did not say anything.

There was nothing broken in him. She could see that from nine feet off and so could everybody else in that yard.

She had one thought and it took her two seconds and it was this: if that bar goes again, somebody is going to be under it again, and there are two men short, and it is going to happen again before the twenty-eighth.

And then she had a second thought, which was that a stone set down once, with four inches of the bed driven out from under it by somebody’s weight, is not going to go back into a bed that was dug for it.

The far end is riding on a plank. The near end has a lip on it. That stone will not lie square, and it will be a lip for the twenty-nine stones that come after it.

## The Man With The Rule In His Hand

He came down the yard after the others had gone back to the plank and he looked at the stone and then at the leg of the man on the plank and then at the scraped chalk line.

“I am going to say three things and I am going to say them out loud so that nine men hear them,” he said.

“All right.”

“This stone is off the line and it is not going back on it. I can see that and any man on this yard can see that, and we have four feet of bed and a sill course and twenty-nine stones after it, and I am going to cut the bed wider at this end and take the corner out and lay the sill across it.”

“That is right,” she said.

“That is not what I am going to say next,” he said. “I am going to say that this yard has never had a stone come down off the line, and it is going to happen again before the twenty-eighth if I do not put two more men on it, and I have not got two more men.”

He put the chalk back in his pocket.

“And I am going to say that you were standing nine feet off and you did not come in, and that was correct, and I would say it to anybody.”

## The Plain Part About One Person

She wanted to say something.

That is the whole of what she wanted, for about half a minute, standing in a yard four miles east of a shed with her strip against her ribs and four fingers and a thumb on her right hand.

Not to be thanked. Not to be given a line. To say one ordinary sentence out loud to a man about a stone in a bed, because he had said three sentences out loud to her and one of them was about her.

She did not say it. She had no version of the sentence that would not have gone into a yard with nine men on it and she would be in that yard for the rest of this sill course and after it.

She is eighteen years old and she has been standing nine feet off things for five months and this is the first time that nine feet has been the correct distance.

## What She Took Back Up The Road

She walked four miles back with the bar over her right shoulder because a bar carries on one hand and she carries it on that one, and she got to the shed at the seventh hour and put the clay in at the low end of the ring and it came up the width of a finger and stopped.

She lay on the boards at the second hour and said it out loud, because it was the second hour and there was not a living soul in the room, and a thing said at the second hour on the boards goes into no book at all.

“There is a stone at the south end of a yard that is off the line and has a lip on it, and twenty-nine stones behind it, and a date on the twenty-eighth, and two men short.”

“And I have one thing I have never done on purpose and no way of finding out whether I can do it again.”

“And I am going to find out tomorrow, and there will be nine men on that yard, and every one of them will watch me do it, and the man with the rule in his hand will have to write down whatever it turns out to be.”

That was the whole of it. She went to sleep with the bar under her hand and the seventeenth was tomorrow and she was going to be at that yard at the sixth hour.

## One Thing He Said

“You are going to put it in a room,” he said.

She had been on the boards about a minute with her eyes shut.

She did not answer, because there was nothing to answer and because he had not asked her anything, and because he had bought nothing.

“Nine men, a date, and a stone that is off the line, and a man with a rule in his hand who will have to put all of it somewhere. You have never once put anything anywhere on purpose. That is the whole of what is different tomorrow and you already know it.”
