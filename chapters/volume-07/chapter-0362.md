# What A Month Comes To

A month is thirty days and a gate pays five coppers a reach and she could open a fifth of a reach in a day and a fifth of a reach is one copper, so a month came to thirty coppers and she had known that since the twenty-third of a month about two months back and had not wanted to do it standing up.

She did it standing up on the twenty-seventh, at about the seventh hour, at the bank on the low side of Pei’s field with the drain open four feet below her and the iron gone back in the gate house before dawn.

Thirty coppers.

Bread is two a day and she eats it five days in seven, and the store is five miles west on the far side of the gate, and a third of a sack of clay is three coppers, and a box is four days of the ring.

Thirty coppers in thirty days. Ten days of the month with bread in it is twenty coppers. A third of a sack is three. That is twenty-three, and seven coppers were the whole of what a month left over, and she had half a box of clay against the wall of the shed and four days of the ring in it.

Seven coppers was the margin. Seven coppers was what she had ever had, in six months on that ground, at the end of a month, with nothing coming.

She stood on the bank with the drain open under her and worked it out a second time and got the same seven, and then she worked it out a third time standing the other way up, and the third time she did not get seven.

The third time she got nothing, because she had forgotten the strip and the cart in it and the two other pieces of paper that came off a bundle every nine or ten days, and there was no price on any of that and there was no way to get one.

## The Store

She went to the store on the twenty-seventh afternoon, five miles west, with a basket, and the woman who kept it did not ask her anything and put four loaves in it and took eight coppers out of the fold in her apron.

“Five days,” she said.

“Five days,” the woman said.

It was a store with a floor and a counter and a cold room behind it, and there was about nine of the same sort of things in it that there had been six months back, and the woman had kept it open through all of it and had never once put her hand flat on a cold stone and told it nothing.

She carried the bread back and put it under the boards where it kept and came out at about the seventh hour and went east three miles to the works, because that is what she did now.

## The Clay

There was half a box against the wall of the shed and it was the shape of what she had carried in, and a box was four days of the ring, and the ring had been burning by itself since the second hour of the twenty-second of a month about four months back.

She stood in the doorway with the bread under the boards behind her and did the third sum a fourth time.

Twenty-three coppers of thirty. Seven over. And three coppers of that seven was a third of a sack, and she could carry a third of a sack in the left arm with the arm open, and she had done it about eleven times.

Seven coppers over for a month, and the ring wanted three of them, and that left four, and four coppers is two days of bread in a month of thirty.

She stood in the doorway with her hand on the frame and let that arrive, and then she went and got the bar out from against the fence where it had stood since the twenty-third of a month about two months back and put it down in the middle of the floor of the shed where it was in her way.

She had carried it out of that yard on her shoulder every morning for five months and she had put it down against a fence and left it, and there was a stage on the second of a month about three weeks back that had taken the whole of it out of her hands.

A bar takes two hands.

She had eight holds in the left one and four fingers and a thumb on the right, and the right arm was open about half of the time and had been for four years, and she could hold a dressed piece with it for about two minutes at a time.

## The Gateway, The Twenty-Eighth

She went to the works at about the ninth hour with the board cover on the wall and the man who kept the list inside, and she asked him a question she had not asked in six months.

“Is the board the only place work comes from.”

He put the rag down on the bench.

“No,” he said. “But it is the only place it comes from that anybody stands at, and the other places are the yards, and a yard is nine days or four miles or however far it is, and a yard does not put a line on a board for a woman it does not have on its books.”

He picked the rag up again.

“You have been in this gateway six months and you have taken four lines off that wall and you are not on the books of this yard or any other, and I have not put you on them and I am not going to, because you do not want to be on them and because a book you are on is a book somebody reads.”

“That is two reasons.”

“That is three reasons,” he said. “The third one is that you would be on it and you would be paid at the day and the day would be a day, and you have not asked me for a day and I am not going to hand you one out of a gateway because you are standing in it looking thin.”

She stood there.

“I am asking what fills it,” she said.

“The season,” he said. “It turns and it comes back and it comes back full, and every line on it will be measured either by weight or by length, and it has been that way since I came here eleven years ago, and I am not going to change how this yard measures work to fit one woman’s hand.”

## What She Took Back

She walked the three miles west with the iron gone back in the gate house and nothing in her hands and the last of the light going over the flat.

The season turns and the board fills. That was nine days off, or about nine, and she had been told on the fifteenth of a month about two months back that it came back full, and she had believed him then and she believed him now.

And when it came back full it would come back full of the same thing that had been on it for seven weeks, and she had taken four lines off that wall in six months and the only one of the four that had ever weighed anything was a footing at a far gully that four men would not take because the bank under it is stone.

There were nine men in that yard and none of them could not take a footing.

So the board was coming back full, and every line on it was going to be a line her right hand could not take, and there was going to be no wages in it for her, and the gate was going to go on paying one copper a day, and the ring in the corner of that shed was going to eat three coppers out of every seven she had over at the end of a month.

## At The Second Hour

She got to the shed at about the eleventh hour and lay down on the boards with the ring burning nine feet off and said it out loud because it was the second hour and there was not a living soul in the shed.

“Thirty coppers. Twenty-three of them spoken for before I have had a day.”

He took his time about it, the way he did.

“Thirty days,” he said. “You have counted them three times and you counted the paper wrong on one of them.”

She lay there in the dark and worked it out again and he was right, and it was the third sum that was wrong and not the seventh copper, and the third sum was wrong because she had left out a thing that was not money and had never been money and had a date on it.

She did not say anything else that night, and neither did he, and the fire was nine feet high in the corner of a shed that no office on nine days of road had ever built, and it did not need her at any hour of any day, and she had seven coppers over at the end of a month.
