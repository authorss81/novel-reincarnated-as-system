# A Foot And A Half Down

The second of a month she went to the yard at the turn at the sixth hour for nothing.

She had no line there and she had no work and there was no reason on nine days of road for a woman of eighteen to walk four miles east to stand beside a hole in the ground, and she did it anyway because the thing in the hole had been there for seventeen days and she had not looked at it since the twenty-eighth.

It had three stones stacked round it and eighteen inches of offcut on top of them and a square of plank eighteen inches across laid down on the hard standing two feet from it.

The square of plank was on the ground because a man had put his boot in a place and something had come up through it, and he had not said one word about that to anybody in three weeks.

She stood over the stone for four minutes and she did the thing she had come to do.

She put it there on purpose, with her hand four feet above the stone and her arm open, and she wanted it to go back. She wanted the bed, the lip, the hole and the stone all to be the way they had been at the seventh hour on the seventeenth of a month before nine men were watching.

It went four feet.

It went sideways and a foot and a half down and it went into the loose ground three feet from the bed, and it went into it a foot, and it is now in a second hole in the low side of a yard at the turn with three stones stacked round it and no plank laid down over anything.

## What The Yard Said About It

There were four men at the north end and none of them had seen it and the man with the rule in his hand was at the office end with his back to the low side, writing on the board that goes out on the first.

She did not tell him.

She stood there a minute working out whether to tell him, and she worked it out at forty seconds and the answer was that there is no version of telling a man at an office end on the second of a month that is not a page.

And then a man on the bed turned round and looked at the low side and said to the man next to him, without any particular weight in it:

“They’ve put another one in there.”

And the man next to him said, “Aye. That’s the third.”

And they went back to the bed.

## The Plain Part About One Person

She is eighteen years old and she tried to undo a thing she had done on purpose and it made a second hole.

She had gone four miles for it. She had stood over it for four minutes. She had put her hand above a stone at the low side of a yard at the sixth hour of the second of a month and asked for it to go back where it was, and it went four feet the way it goes, which is a line it picks and not a line she gives it.

Two holes. Four hundred pounds of dressed stone, one in each, and five feet between them, and two and a half feet of stone showing in the top of the second one.

And four men know that there are three stones in the low side of that yard and one of them came out of the bed two weeks ago and the two that came before that were nine feet of offcut and a barrow of clay and a chain and a wedge and forty feet of plank, and none of them has ever been spoken about.

## The Third Of A Month

The third was a yard day and she was at the turn at the sixth hour and there were four stones in the low side of that yard and nine men on the bed and the man with the rule in his hand came down at the seventh hour and stood at the low side for a while.

He did not look at her and he did not say anything to her and he counted the four of them, twice, with his mouth, the way a man counts when he is deciding whether he has been right about a number.

Then he went up the yard and took a barrow of clay off the stack and tipped two barrow-loads round the second hole and stamped it down with his heel, and did it without speaking, and went back to the office end.

That is not in the yard book because there is no rule in a yard book about clay.

She stood there and watched him and felt something she has not felt about any of this ground in five months, and it was not pride and it was not fear.

It was that she had made four holes in a yard and a man had filled one of them in with his own hands and his own barrow at seven in the morning without asking her a single question, and that was the whole of the pay.

## The Sixth Of A Month, The Fourth Hour

The fire came back in a yard at the turn at the sixth hour on the sixth, under the edge of a plank, and she felt it come up through nine inches of her boot and she put her foot down flat on it on purpose to stop it, and it came up through the plank at the other side four inches instead.

Nothing was burned. There were nine men on the bed and none of them was near the office end at that hour.

She was at the mill road at the third hour, on her way up, and she was warm from ten minutes after she got to the table, and she was warm all through an hour and three quarters with a woman of forty-five four feet off the other end of it reading out of a book.

And at the fourth hour she put the back of her left hand flat on the table, which is a thing she has done two hundred times, and it was warm against cold wood, and she took it away again.

Rao did not look up and did not ask and turned the page.

## The Fourth Of A Month

The fourth was a gate day and she dug the run in two lengths with the bar going in where the ground was hard, four feet for the first six and three and a half for the rest of it, and Pei came out at the eighth hour and stood at the end of it.

“You have dug it in the same two lengths you have dug it in for a month.”

“Yes.”

“It is the right shape for that ground and it is not going to be four feet all the way, and I told you twice.”

“I know you told me twice.”

He went inside and she stood in the gateway for two minutes with the iron in her hand and a copper in the fold, and there were four coppers in the fold after that, and she had bought bread on the second of a month and had five days of it left.

Then she went half a mile east on the made road to a low bank where a man keeps a board and she read it from the bottom up.

Eight lines. Two hundred yards of dressed stone by the load and forty feet of bed at the north end of the same yard at the turn and a date in the margin against the north end.

And on the tenth, three days off, the second course goes on top of the first one.

She read that four times and did the arithmetic on her fingers in the dust at the rail, and it came out the same every time, which is that there is a second course going on top of two hundred yards of course that has been down for eleven days with four stones in the low side of the yard.

## The Seventh, At The Boards

She worked it out on the seventh, on the boards, at the second hour, out loud, because it had to be said somewhere.

“Four days and two hours went out of the ground under me on the twenty-eighth of a month and it came back on the sixth.”

“And a thing I do with my hand goes four feet and does not go back, and it is the same fire underneath both of them, and I do not know what to do with that and there is nobody within nine days of road to ask.”

She lay there.

“There is one difference and it is the only one. On the fifth of a month I spent four days and two hours on nothing and the fire went off and came back on its own. On the second of a month I put my hand over a stone and asked for a hole to close and it did not close and it opened a second one.”

“Which means the money is not what moves it. It moves when I ask.”

## What She Felt About It

She was frightened of that for a week and then she stopped being frightened of it and started being something else, which she did not have a word for and did not look for.

A woman of forty-five four feet off her at the other end of a table had said on the sixth at the fourth hour, in the ordinary voice, “that is the longest one you have ever sat through”, and had gone back to the page.

And a man with a rule in his hand had filled a hole in with two barrow-loads of clay and stamped it down with his heel.

And at the fifth hour on the seventh she stood in a gateway at a post a hundred yards short of the counting house and watched two carts go up the mill road with nothing on either of them, because the tray goes on the first and it was two days off.

On the ninth it would come back down with her name in it, in a hand she cannot read, in a book that a man with a brush copies into another book, and she would not be in the building and she would not be on the road and she would not be able to do one thing about it.

She had asked a woman of forty-five for four hours a month of her own company six weeks ago and got it, and given her own name to get it, and she had not once thought about what it is like to want to know.

Now she wanted to know. That was the want on the seventh of a month at the fifth hour, standing in a gateway at a post, and she had a bar over her right shoulder and eight coppers in the fold and two days of road between her and the answer.

She worked out that on the ninth the tray comes down at the sixth hour and she would be at the gate at the sixth hour, because the gate pays a copper and there is nothing else that pays.

And she worked out that on the ninth she would be three miles west of the counting house with a bar in her hand, four fingers and a thumb on it, and that every person on nine days of road is at a gate at the sixth hour of a gate day and would be in a hole with a bar in it.

That is the whole of what she had on the seventh.
