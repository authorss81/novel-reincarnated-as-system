# What Six Months Costs

A reading that has not been answered does not sit in a room.

That is the whole of the twenty-fifth of a month beginning 1,034 and she worked it out standing at the end of a drain run at Pei’s gate at the ninth hour with the bar in her right hand, and she had worked out four things in four days and the fourth one was the only one that was doing any work.

Eight of the nine that come down that road every year are lists of places and dates.

One was a line about her, dated the eighteenth, and on the nineteenth of a month at the seventh hour in a room with a lamp in it she said six words in the ordinary voice, and the six words went up the same road every answer has gone up since before that mill was cast.

And there is no answer on it anywhere on nine days of road, and there never will be, and that is not a fault and it is not a thing anybody did to her.

## The Twenty-Fifth

The boy of sixteen came up the nine steps at the fourth hour on the twenty-fifth with a roll under his arm the way he comes, and he put it on the middle of the table and stopped, which he has never done.

He is sixteen and he has been on that floor two years and he does not know what a column is.

He said, in the ordinary voice and without looking at her:

“Was there a cart on the mill road on the eighteenth.”

“No.”

“Any cart.”

“Two went up at the sixth hour with a tray on one of them. There was one going down in the afternoon with nothing on it.”

The boy wrote nothing down and he went down the stair and she heard him go.

She sat at the near end of that table for four minutes afterwards and worked out what had just happened, and she got it the first time, which almost never happens.

## What The Floor Did About It

By the third of the next month there were four people on that floor who asked her a question and wrote the answer down, and none of the four had ever asked her anything in seven months.

The boy asked about carts and the road and the ground at the turn. A man of thirty who set the yard pages against the book underneath asked her whether she had seen a dressed stone go into a cart and which way it had gone. The woman who keeps the stove came up nine steps she has no business on and asked her what day the season turned.

And the man behind the counter, on the twenty-ninth, at the seventh hour, in the room with the stove in it, opened the seasonal book at the page with her name against the twenty-second and said:

“There is a thing on this floor that is about you and there is no answer on it.”

“I know.”

“I am not going to ask you what it says.”

“All right.”

“I am going to ask you one thing and you are not going to be able to answer it, and I would rather ask you in a room than have you find out in a week that somebody asked it of you at a gate.” He put his finger on the page. “Is there anybody inside nine days of road who could read the hand it is in.”

She did not answer that one.

She stood at a counter and looked at a man and said nothing at all for four seconds, and he did not repeat it, and he shut the book.

## The Plain Part About One Person

She is eighteen years old and she would rather be entered.

That is the part that took her four days and she has said it out loud to a living person.

Being entered is one line in a book with a day against it, written by a man with a pen, in the ordinary hand, about a woman at a gate, and it is over and it is shut and she cannot get it out and she knows exactly what it says.

Being a person the floor has taken to thinking might know something is a boy of sixteen asking her about carts on a stair, four hours a month, forever, with nothing written down anywhere at the end of that floor.

She traded one line she could have measured for a standing arrangement she cannot, and she did it in a room with a lamp in it on the nineteenth of a month, and it was worth it, and she is not going to be able to say how she knows.

## The First Of The Month

The first of the month beginning 1,064 went up the mill road at the sixth hour with a tray on it and a cord round the tray, and she stood a hundred yards short of the counting house and watched it go for the third time in four months.

There was a name in it and there had been a name in it on every first of a month since the twenty-eighth of a month two months back, and it comes back down on the ninth and a man with a brush copies it into a book on the floor below that shuts.

She was going to be at a yard at a turn on the ninth at the sixth hour, because it is three days in seven.

And on that page, when it comes back down on the ninth, there will be a fourth line in chalk in a yard-book hand, four words, about something a woman at a gate said out loud at the sixth hour on the twenty-seventh of a month, and a date in an ordinary hand on a slip of paper that a woman of forty-five put in beside a column herself.

## The Second, On The Road

The second of the month he was on the made road east of the turn at the ninth hour with the notebook shut under his arm and nine feet off, and she was coming back from a yard with nothing in her hands, and he had been there before she came round the hedge and he stayed nine feet off and he did not open the notebook.

He buys nothing. He has never bought anything.

She has asked him four times in seven months and it is the only handle there has ever been on him.

“Can an office send for a person who is not standing on the ground.”

She said it in the ordinary voice, standing nine feet off, with the strip against her ribs.

He did not make her wait.

“Yes,” he said.

## What It Costs

He kept the notebook under his arm.

“It does not need you to be anywhere. That is the whole of it and it is the only part most people find difficult. It needs a line with your name at the head of it and a rule under it, and then the line carries, and where you are standing is not a part of it.”

“Then what is a part of it.”

“The answer at the other end.” He looked at the road and not at her. “Somebody has to answer it, and the answer goes up, and then the question comes back down as a thing about you, and it does not wait for you to be finished.”

“I do not understand that.”

“You are not carrying anything at the moment,” he said. “That is what I mean. A man with a bar in his hand and a dressed stone on him at the wrong angle cannot answer a question at the same time, and the answer does not care what his hands are doing, and it will be somewhere in his head for six months before he hears about it.”

He shifted the notebook.

“That is what it costs the person being sent for. Not the road and not a room. The six months.”

## What She Said To That

She stood on a made road nine feet off a man with a shut notebook and said the thing she had been carrying since the nineteenth of a month.

“There is one already.”

“All right.”

“It came down on the eighteenth. It is about me. I said no to hearing it and the no went up and there is no answer on it and there never will be one.”

He did not write it down, because he never writes anything she says down, and there is a notebook under his arm and it is hers on no page in it.

“Then you already know what it costs,” he said.

## The Third

She was at the rail at the sixth hour of the third of a month and there were forty-nine lines on it, and she read all forty-nine down and got them the same twice, and she could take none of them and there has been a full board on that rail for a month.

Two miles up the made road there was a man of forty-three in a room with a lamp in it, and a sheet in the groove with two other sheets in it, and a name at the head of one of them that he had read four times and would never read again.

And in a room at the other end of the floor below this one a question was standing that had nothing at the other end of the rule, and she had put it there on purpose, and there was no way on nine days of road to un-ask it, and the man with the brush had begun to ask her about carts.

She was richer than she had been at the end of the month before and she could not spend one copper of it on one single thing on this ground.

There is a line out of that door and a road on the other side of it and nine days of walking, and she had crossed it on foot once before she came and would not walk it again, and she was not going to walk it now to be somewhere that is not here, and she has never in seven months wanted to be anywhere else.

She shouldered the bar and went east four miles to a shed at a mill whose wheel has not turned since before she came, and she lay on the boards at the second hour and said four sentences out loud, and one of them was that she had found out what it costs to say no, and it is six months, and she got it for nothing.
