# The Place An Answer Goes

It is not the room with the two shut doors.

He notes that within four seconds of standing in it, which is what he does with everywhere, because he has been inside the layer twice in his life and both times he started by working out what kind of place he was in before he let anything arrive.

The first time was a field. Lines going out in all directions from a centre, entries hung on them the way washing is hung, and a board at the far end with a hand and a paste pot at it. That was the layer looking at a person from above, and the whole width of it.

The second time was a room with a floor, a table, a chair pushed back at an angle, two shut doors and one handle, and a window with nothing on the other side of it.

This is not either of those.

## The Place It Is

It is a narrow room with a low ceiling and no furniture in it, and there is one thing in it, and the thing is a strip of paper lying flat on the floor nine feet from where he is standing.

There is light. He cannot tell where the light is coming from and he has looked for it, and there is no source and no shadow, and the strip on the floor is lit the way a page is lit on a table by a window that is not there.

There is no door in it that he can find and he has been in it two minutes.

The strip is not old. He knows that the way he knows a column: the edges of it are square and the fibre is light, and it is not a strip that has been in a coat for five months. It came down at the eighth hour of the eighteenth of a month and it is the same kind of paper as the one at the end of the first minute.

There are two lines on it in a hand he has never seen.

He reads them twice and does not move for the second reading, and the two lines say what everything he can see says: that a line has not been carried to a room, and that a holder is required against it.

## What He Does About It

Being held is a thing that can be answered now and he has known that for four months and has not answered anything, because there was nothing to answer.

This has been brought to him. That is the whole of what a send is, and it is not a thing that arrives like weather, and it is not a hand on a shoulder, and no man has walked into any room he is standing in.

It simply is here, and it has been here for two minutes, and it is the first time anything on nine days of road has come to him instead of going out of him.

He could leave it.

That is the honest shape of it and he stands there with it for nine seconds. He could turn his attention off the line and this would stop being a room he is standing in, because that is what happened the other two times, and nothing on this ground has ever made him do a thing.

He turns toward it.

## The Two Things He Goes Looking For

He looks for the morning first, because he has looked for it every day since the seventeenth of a month five months back and he has never stopped and he is not going to be a different kind of man tonight.

There is no morning under it.

There has not been one for five months and there is not going to be one in a narrow room with a strip of paper on the floor, and he made his peace with that a long time ago and it has not become easier and it has not become worse.

Then he goes and looks at the other thing, which is the unnamed state, the five months of standing behind a word he refused, the last part of him that no office on nine days of road had a word for.

It is not here either.

It closed over four months ago and it does not open because there is a question on a piece of paper, and that is correct, and there is nothing in this room that is going to give either of them back, and he is not going to look at them again tonight.

## The Answer

He goes down onto the floor of it, which is not a thing he has to do, and he puts his hand flat on the strip the way you put your hand flat on a page.

And he answers it.

He does not answer it in words. Words go on strips and strips go up roads and roads go to rooms, and there is one road out of this that has never once been used on this ground by anybody, and he is going to use it.

He answers in the only direction a line on this ground has never been read.

Up.

## What It Is

Being read means being found. He said that once, in a room, to a man, and it was the truest thing anybody had ever said to her about the country she was standing in, and it was his.

Being held means being sent for, and a send means a man in a room with doors, and he has been sendable for four months and has not had one.

This is the third thing and it is not either of the other two and it is worse than the second.

Being answerable is not being findable. Being answerable is not being sendable. Being answerable is that there is a question with his name against it and he has put his hand flat on it and said yes, and a thing that is answered can be read back. A finding can be denied. A sending can be refused once. An answer cannot be refused at all, because an answer is the only one of the three that is a thing anybody can quote.

He sits on the floor of a room that is not a room with his hand on a strip of paper nine feet away, and he works that out properly, because he is a man who says the price back to the person paying it.

## What It Goes Out Of

Out of the ground she is standing on.

He felt it leave her the other time and he took it out of her hands and out of a shed and he knows exactly what it costs and how it is paid. This is not that.

This is out of the ground under a pair of feet at the second hour on the nineteenth of a month, in a shed at a mill whose wheel has not turned since before either of them was born, and it goes out of the boards and not out of her.

He does not take it out of her and he could not. There is nothing in him that reaches into a person directly and there has not been since he died, and what happened the one time was a man at a counter with his hand flat on the floor of a room that was hot.

He thinks about that for a minute and a half, on the floor of a room that is not a room.

There was a fire there. Four days and two hours of it, gone out of the ground on the twenty-eighth of a month, and he felt that as a drain, the way a man feels a floor go cold under him, and he did not know where it had gone or whose it had been and he did not ask.

And he is going to take more of it tonight, and he is not going to be able to tell her, and she is going to find out about it on her own, the way she finds out about everything.

“She is eighteen. She will work out what a floor feels like and she will not be able to ask anybody inside nine days of road and she will put it down to a sill line.”

He does not like that and he is going to do it anyway, because the alternative is that the answer is not given, and she has been standing at a counter for five months refusing to carry him into rooms and has never once been asked what she would rather.

## The Twentieth, And Then The Twenty-First

It arrived on the twentieth. It had been on that floor since the strip went up the mill road on the nineteenth with the ordinary post, and it sat there a whole day and a night and nobody read it, because everything on nine days of road reads downward and a strip lying on a floor is the most ordinary object there is.

He turned toward it on the twentieth. The answer itself took the rest of that night and most of the next day.

The two lines on the strip went up. Not on a cart and not on a post and not on a tray with a cord round it, and not to the room that wanted a holder, and not to a floor with nine steps and a turn in the landing.

They went up to the place that sends strips down the mill road at the eighth hour of a day that is not the first and not the ninth, and that place had never been read to in the whole of eleven hundred years of practice on this ground.

Everything on nine days of road has been read downward for as long as there has been anything to read. A column is read out of a room. A strip is sent down a road. A holding is presented in a room. A room is shut on a date and the doors close and the line is still in a book a long way from where anybody could see it.

He read one upward and it moved.

## What He Says In His Own Room Afterwards

He is back where he is, which is nowhere that has a floor with boards on it and a door with a handle on it.

“She is going to find out.”

That is the part he turns over. He has spent five months inside a woman who could not be surprised by a person, and the surprise does not come back and will not come back, and it is not a thing anybody gives her twice, and he has not been able to look at her directly in five months except through the reach.

And he has just done a thing on nine days of road that is going to come back down a road and into a building she walks into four times a month, and it is going to come down as a question, and she is going to have to answer it, and an answer cannot be refused.

He does not know what the question will be. That is the part he did not choose.

“I chose the answering. I did not choose the answer. There is a difference and it is the only part of this I am going to have to live inside of for the rest of it.”

He is answerable. He is not findable and he is not sendable and those two are things he had and this one is new.

And an answer is a thing anybody can quote. He will be asked about it by somebody he has never met, at some point, by somebody who has only got the two lines and the date and the ordinary voice.

He sits inside that for a while and does not try to get out of it.

## What He Does Not Do

He does not think about the date of the morning he died, because it is not there and looking does not bring it and there is nothing in this that will bring it.

He does not think about the account, because he has not thought about the account in five months and he is not going to start tonight on the worst night of his life.

He does not tell her. She cannot hear him and he is not going to pretend to her that he can, and there is no part of this she has to be told about and no version of her being told about it that would be any use to her at all.

And he does not do it again, because one is what he has and two would be a habit, and the first thing that anybody on this ground does with a habit is put a line in front of it.
