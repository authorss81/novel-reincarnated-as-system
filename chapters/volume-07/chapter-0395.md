# Nine Men Going Round A Hole

The sill line starts at the north corner of the south end on the eighteenth of a month and it goes two hundred and ten feet, and there is a hole at the far end of it about four feet across with a dressed stone in it that has been in the loose two inches.

It is not in the way of the work. That is the thing. A man can walk round it in two steps and four men have walked round it two hundred and ten feet of bed and twenty-nine stones and have not once been stopped by it.

It is a hole in a yard where nine men work, four feet across, and it has been like that since the eighth hour of the seventeenth of a month.

## The Eight Days

She was at that yard three days in seven on the eighteenth, the nineteenth, the twentieth, the twenty-second, the twenty-third, the twenty-fifth and the twenty-sixth, and she dug nothing and carried nothing and was on the site for two and a half hours each of those seven days.

A man had set three stones down round that hole on the first morning and had stacked the offcuts on the outside of them, and the hole was no longer a hole you could put a foot into, and it was still a hole.

She used it four more times in eight days and it went four feet each time and it did not come back.

## The Nineteenth, Near The Office End

The first of the four was on the nineteenth at the ninth hour, and it was not a stone and it was not a mistake.

Two of the nine had put a stack of offcuts down on the bed itself, nine feet of it, to clear the plank, and they were going to shift it at the end of the day, and it had been on the bed for two days and it was in the way of the twentieth.

She stood at nine feet off and wanted it off the bed.

It went four feet and it went into the low side and it went into the loose beside the dressed stone and it went a foot and a half down, and it is in the way of nothing and the offcuts are off the bed.

A man looked at it and then at her and neither of them said one word about it, and then the man moved the offcuts off the bed himself with his hands, four feet of them at a time, and did not look at her again.

## The Plain Part About One Person

She is eighteen years old and she has a thing she can do on purpose now and it costs her four feet of whatever it is on.

On the nineteenth it was nine feet of offcuts that two men had stacked in the wrong place on purpose and were going to shift anyway. On the twenty-first it was a barrow of clay that had been tipped at the wrong end. On the twenty-third it was a wedge and a chain. On the twenty-fifth it was forty feet of plank.

None of those four was the stone in the hole. That stone has not moved and is not going to move and there is no version of this in which it moves, because she has not put her hand anywhere near it and she is not going to.

She had wanted, on four days out of seven, to move a thing four feet. And it moved four feet, and it went the way it goes, and she has to look at where it went before she puts anything down.

## The Twenty-Second, At The Sixth Hour

It came up under a boot at the sixth hour on the twenty-second and the man came off it and sat down on the plank.

It had come up four times in eight days off the ends of planks and off the rim of a barrow and under a boot. It had been coming up on that ground for six weeks with nothing else in the room and it had stood at the width of a finger and stopped.

Now it came up in a yard with nine men on it and went through the sole of a man’s boot two inches and he took the boot off and looked at the inside of it.

He did not say anything about it. He turned it over twice, looked at the sole, and put it back on, and worked for an hour and a half, and at the end of the day he went and got a piece of plank eighteen inches square and put it down on the ground where he had been standing.

He did not tell anybody and she did not tell anybody and she went and stood four feet off the whole of that.

She wanted to be able to turn it off and she found out at the fourth hour that she could not. She has been in a shed every night for five months with nothing in it and it stands at a finger’s width. She has been in a room with a woman of forty-five in it for two weeks and it comes up.

And she is eighteen and she has just worked out that the one thing her own body has ever done for her without being asked has an address on it, and the address is wherever she is standing.

And at the sixth hour in a yard four miles east of a shed, with nine men on it, it came up through the sole of a man’s boot and she can do nothing at all about where it comes up.

## The Man With The Rule In His Hand

He came down to the low side on the twenty-fifth at the fourth hour and stood where she was standing and looked at the stone in the hole for a while.

“I have been keeping this yard twenty-two years,” he said. “I have had men in it who could not count and men in it who could not hold a thing and one man in it who could do neither and was the best worker I ever had, and I put all three of them on lines and I was right about every one of them.”

He turned round and looked up the yard at the bed, which was two hundred and ten feet of it and twenty-nine stones and two hundred yards of the rest of it.

“And I have got a page in the yard book with a hole at the south end of it and a dressed stone in the hole and a line that is ninety feet shorter than it was on the morning of the seventeenth, and there is nothing to write in the margin of it.”

“All right.”

“I am going to write it on the first,” he said. “That is the day the book goes up the mill road with everything else in this yard, because it goes up on a first and I have never in twenty-two years had a page go up a day late because of me. And it comes back down on the ninth.”

He put the chalk in his pocket and looked at the hole again.

“And there is nothing on this ground I can put in the margin of that page which will explain it, and I am not going to invent a term for it, because a yard book is not the place to invent a term and there are nine men on this site who would have to learn it.”

## What She Wanted To Say

She wanted to say: “write my name on it.”

That is what she wanted, on a yard four miles east of a shed, at the fourth hour of the twenty-fifth of a month, standing next to a hole in the ground with a dressed stone in it, and she did not say it and she had known she was not going to say it before she got there.

And the reason she was not going to say it was that it would have been a gift, and a gift at that yard goes into a page, and the page goes up the mill road on the first, and she has spent six weeks learning what happens to a thing on that page. She worked that out on the nineteenth and she worked it out again on the twenty-fifth and she got the same answer both times.

There is no way on nine days of road of being in a book and not being able to be found in it. That is not a lesson anybody taught her. That is what the stair book on the top floor of a building two miles down the mill road has been doing since the twenty-eighth of a month six weeks ago.

## The Twenty-Sixth

It came off on the twenty-eighth and she was not there for that, because it was a gate day and she was three miles west of the turn with a bar in her right hand at the sixth hour.

What she had on the twenty-sixth, at the yard, was forty feet of plank out of the way of a bed and a man who had put a square of plank down where he had been standing and had never said anything about it.

And the twenty-eighth was two days away and she had no way of being in two places.

She walked four miles back up the made road with the strip against her ribs and she worked out on the hedge that she had made something happen four times in eight days in a yard full of nine men, and that a man with a rule in his hand was going to write a page about it on the first, and that the page was going to go up the mill road.

That was the first thing she had ever put anywhere on purpose, and she had not decided to put it anywhere, and the man who would have to write it down was telling her a week in advance.

## What She Felt About It

She felt proud for two hundred yards on the made road, and then she felt sick, and the sick lasted the whole four miles.

Proud was because four men in nine had got out of her way in eight days without a word being said. Sick was because a man of forty with a rule in his hand and twenty-two years behind him was going to sit down on the first with a page he had no term for.

He had not asked her for anything. He had not asked her to stay out of it. He had not asked her anything at all, and he was right on all three.

And she was going to walk past that yard again on the eighteenth and the nineteenth and the twentieth, because it was three days in seven, and there was nothing else to do with any of it.
