# Asking To Take A Name Out

The twenty-fourth of a month is not a day of the four she goes up nine steps for, and she went up anyway at the third hour with the strip in her left hand, and a woman of forty-five was at the far end of a table with forty books open on it and did not comment on it.

She stood at the near end and said what she had come to say, out loud, in the ordinary voice, in about nine seconds.

“Rao. Take my name out of the stair book.”

The pencil went down flat.

## What She Said Then

“There is a name at the left of a line in that book and it has been there since the twenty-eighth of a month six weeks back and it went up the mill road on the first and came down on the ninth and was copied out into a book on the floor below by a man with a brush, and there are now two books on nine days of road with my name in them and both of them shut.”

“Yes.”

“I would like it out of both of them and I cannot pay for it and I am not asking you to do it for nothing. I am asking you because I have known you for six weeks and you have never once said a thing to me that was not correct.”

Rao did not pick the pencil up.

“Both,” she said.

“Both.”

“No,” she said. “It is in one of them and it cannot come out of the other one either, and I am going to tell you why in four sentences and then I am going to read a book, because it is the fourth hour and I have a set to get through.”

She put her hand flat on the table.

“Those books went off this floor on the first of a month at the sixth hour and they came back on the ninth and they went round the floor below a man with a brush on the same morning, and every page in them was out of this room for eight days and I do not know what was done to them.”

“They came back.”

“They came back and they had been somewhere, and the only person on nine days of road who has ever had a page of mine in his hands without me is the man who cords the tray, and he does not read what is on it and I do not ask him what he does with it.”

She turned the page over.

“I told you on the twenty-eighth of a month that there was no hand in this building that could take it out. I was not being kind to you and I am not being kind to you now. I am telling you the shape of it.”

## The Plain Part About One Person

She is eighteen years old and the first thing she ever asked anybody for was a woman’s name out loud in a room and it was granted in eleven words.

The second thing she asked anybody for was to have the first one taken back, and it was not granted, and it was not refused either, and there is a difference and she cannot tell which one she is holding.

She did not say thank you and she did not say anything else. She went down nine steps and eleven and out of a door and along the mill road and she did not sit on the bench outside because she had not been outside, she had been inside.

## What She Said On The Way Down The Road

“It is not hers to take out.”

That is what she worked out at the rail two hundred yards down, at about the fourth hour, standing in the wrong place on the road where anyone coming down would not see her.

She had asked a woman out loud to do a thing the woman does not have, and the woman had told her the shape of it in four sentences, and had not been unkind, and would not be thanked and would not be honoured.

She wanted to be angry and she could not get at it, because the woman had been exactly right about every part of it and so had the man on the floor below, so had the man behind the counter, and so had a man of forty with a rule in his hand who had filled a hole with his own barrow.

## The Twenty-Fourth At The Works

She read the board at the rail at the fifth hour and it had eight lines on it and the two lines at the foot of the strip from the yard at the turn were still on it, and the man with the rule in his hand had written his margin note under them on the twenty-first, in his own hand, exactly the way he said he would.

Three men had read that margin.

She worked that out standing at the rail. Three men at a yard had read a margin note on a page that said two holders were required against the seventeenth of a month and that there was one holder on that ground, and one of them was thirty years old and had worked out on his own in six weeks that a woman from a gate had moved a stone.

## The Twenty-Fourth, At The Gate

She dug the run at the gate at the seventh hour on the twenty-fourth and filled in the four feet at the near end and did three and a half for the rest of it, and Pei came out at the ninth hour and stood at the end of it the way he stands.

“You have not been up the mill road on a day that is not one of those four,” he said.

“No.”

“I do not know that you have a rule about those four and I do not know where I got the four from,” he said. “I have got a counter and a gate and one thing I am allowed to say, and I said it on the sixteenth of a month two months back and I am not going to say it again.”

He went inside.

That was the whole of it. A man behind a gate, in daylight, being correct about a thing that was none of his business, and she had walked three miles in one direction and nine in the other in a day, and there was a cup at the other end of one of them that she had not wanted.

## The Twenty-Fifth, At The Yard

She was at the yard at the turn at the sixth hour on the twenty-fifth because it was three days in seven and she does not stop going.

The fire came up under a barrow wheel at the seventh hour and the man who was holding the handles let go of it, which he had not done in six weeks, and stood and looked at the wheel, and then at her, and then went and got a piece of plank and put it under the wheel.

He said nothing to her about it.

The woman in the office end came out at the eighth hour with a cup of something hot in it and put it into her hands and went back in without stopping.

That was the twelfth time in seven weeks and she had not asked her name and had never once been asked for one, and the cup was hot, and there was nothing at all wrong with the cup.

## What She Felt About The Yard

She stood at the low side with the empty thing in her hands and she worked out the first thing, which was that this yard had told its own story since the eighth hour of the seventeenth of a month and had never once said her name out loud.

Three stones in the loose. A page with a hole in it. Two holders required. One holder on this ground.

The cup came out of an office end the way it has come out eleven times. A thing handed to her in the open at a yard where nine men work is not a thing four people have to agree about later.

It is a cup of something hot. It is not in a book and it is not on a page, and there is no hand on nine days of road that could read her holding a cup of hot water in a yard at a turn four miles east of a shed.

It is the only thing anybody has given her in seven months that has cost nothing at all and gone nowhere.

And she stood there and found out that she did not want it, and that was new, and she had not worked out what it was yet.

## The Last Of It

She put the empty thing down on the edge of the bench at the office end at the eighth hour and walked four miles back up the made road with the strip against her ribs and got to the shed at the tenth and dug a lump out of the box.

It came up the width of a finger and stopped.

She lay on the boards at the second hour and said the three sentences out loud, because there was not a living soul in the room and a thing said at the second hour goes into no book at all.

“I have been told, in a room, that a name of mine cannot be taken out of two books on nine days of road and that there is no hand in the building that can do it, and she said it in four sentences and she was right about all four.”

“And there is a second line in a book at a counter with my name at the head of it and a day against it, and the thing that gave it came from the ground under my own feet and I never knew it was going to.”

“And there is a page at a yard with three stones in it and two holders required against a date, and three men have read the margin.”

Then she lay there for a while and said the fourth one, which was the one she had not worked out how to say.

“And there was a cup of something hot put into my hands this morning by a woman who has never asked me anything and put nothing anywhere, and I did not want it, and I have not worked out yet what that is.”

She got up at the fourth hour and went to the gate and dug the run and got a copper, and the fire did not come up that night and she had expected that and it was different from expecting it.

That was the whole of the twenty-fourth and the twenty-fifth.

It was nine days after the strongest thing anybody on nine days of road has done to her in two years, and she could not put it in a book, and would not have wanted to.
