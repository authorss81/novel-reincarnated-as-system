# Three Carts Going East

The twenty-second of a month beginning 1,004 was the day three carts and a team came down the made road east of the turn at the sixth hour and stopped on the bank nine hundred feet from the yard, and she was at that bank at the seventh with a bar over her right shoulder because the board had a line on it that said that ground and she could take neither the weight of it nor the length of it.

The woman at the head of the team was twenty and had a whinny whip folded twice and going past at the speed a loaded cart goes past, and she slowed it about as much as a cart slows when a person is standing in a cut with a bar in her hand.

“You are on the cart track,” she said.

“Yes.”

“It is not cut.”

“No.”

## What Was On That Bank

Nine hundred feet of it between the yard and the low place where the clay comes off, and the wheels go in up to the axle in a wet month, and a cart that goes in at the second hour does not come out that day.

Wei Ran got off and looked at the ground and then at the four men she had and then at the four men she had.

“It is nine hundred feet and I have got three carts going out on the twenty-eighth and I am not going to be the one that is still on this bank on the twenty-ninth,” she said. “It is four coppers a day for a man with a bar and it is two days and I will pay it at the end of each one.”

That is a rate and she gave it before she was asked for it, which is what a trade does.

“Yes,” Ning Yan said.

“I did not ask if you would. I am telling you what it costs so that you know what it is worth while you are standing there, and you can go east along the road if it is not.”

## Two Days

She cut it in two days.

The twenty-second from the seventh hour to the ninth and the twenty-third from the sixth hour to the third hour of the next day, with the bar in her right hand and four fingers and a thumb on it, and she got it down to four feet for the first two hundred and a half and three and a half for the rest with a step down between them the width of her foot, because the ground at the low end is soft and always has been.

Two hundred yards of that was the same problem she had solved at Pei’s gate in the eighth of a month four months back and it took her an hour and a quarter and an hour and it is the same copper and it is two hours instead of two and a half.

She gave up the gate on both days. That is two coppers she did not earn and did not have to, and she worked it out at the bank on the first evening and then worked out again on the second, and both times the answer was the same and both times she stood there anyway.

## What It Cost In Copper

She worked it out on the bank at the eighth hour on the twenty-second with the bar in her right hand and four fingers and a thumb on it, because she had been at that yard three days in seven for eight weeks for nothing and had never once cost herself a copper by going there.

Two coppers. That is what two days at that bank cost her at Pei’s gate, and she stood on a bank nine hundred feet east of the turn and worked out that she had given up two coppers to try to make eight, and that a woman with a team of three and a whinny whip folded twice had put four a day on a plank bench in front of her.

She finished the cut anyway.

That is the part she could not get round on the walk back. She had known at the eleventh foot that the hand was not going to take it, and she had known it again at the ninth hour of the twenty-second, and she had gone back down to that bank at the sixth hour on the twenty-third and put her whole weight on it and it had gone eleven inches and stopped, and she had done the second day.

A rule is not worked out twice. She found that out in nine days. A rule can be worked out twice and the second time you do the day anyway.

## The Bench

The bench at the loading end of that bank is a plank bench about the length of nine men with a post at one end of it and nothing on the near side, and things get put on it.

On the evening of the twenty-second, at the ninth hour, Wei Ran put four coppers on it and pushed them to the far end and squared them up with the side of her hand.

“For the twenty-second,” she said. “That is the rate I said and it is the rate I would pay anybody.”

Ning Yan put her right hand out.

It stopped.

Not a cramp and not a slow thing. Her hand went out about the length of her own arm and it stopped in the air over the bench with the four coins on it a foot beyond her fingertips, and she could see the edge of the near one and the shine on the top of it, and it stayed there for about two seconds and came back down.

## The Second Try

She did not say anything and she did not stop moving. She went at it again on the twenty-third at the third hour with the same four coppers on the same bench and the same post at the end of it, and she put her hand out the second time with the whole of her weight on it and it went eleven inches and stopped.

Wei Ran was sitting on the far side of the bench with her feet in the mud and her elbows on her knees.

“That is the second day,” she said. “You have cut nine hundred feet and I am going to the yard on the twenty-eighth and you are not going to be paid for the twenty-second either if this is what is happening, and I am not going to carry it around in a pocket for a week.”

“All right.”

“I am not asking you why. I have three carts and a team and a bank to get off, and it is not my trade to know why a person cannot pick up four coppers.”

She picked the four up off the bench and put them in her own pocket and wrote nothing down and did not say anything else about it.

## What She Said Before She Went

She stood up off the far side of the bench and put the whip in her hand and said four sentences, and she said them at the same speed she said everything else.

“The rate is four a day and I do not move it for anybody, so do not waste a day asking me to move it. I have had a team of four for six years and I have never once had this, and I am not going to have it twice to find out what it is.”

“And if you can work me for nothing, I will use you for nothing, and that is a worse rate for you than the four is, because you will do it for as long as I have ground.”

She got on the cart.

“And I am not saying that to be kind. I am saying it so that you know exactly where you stand at the yard, because everybody at that yard will tell you different things and they will all of them be right.”

## The Plain Part About One Person

She is eighteen years old and she is not hungry and has not been hungry since before she came past the last working point, and she cut nine hundred feet of cart track in two days for eight coppers and she has not got the eight.

A cup of something hot had gone cold on a bench at a yard four miles east eleven days ago, and she had watched it stop being hot and had not been able to say why it had happened to her.

This was money. This was not a cup.

She sat down on the end of the bench on the twenty-third at the fourth hour and put her right hand flat on the plank and looked at it, four fingers and a thumb, and the arm standing open at her side, and it was the same hand and it had dug nine hundred feet of track.

She could not ask anybody inside nine days of road, and she was not going to find out by asking, and a rule is not worked out twice.

## The Twenty-Fourth And After

She was at the rail at the sixth hour on the twenty-fourth with one copper in the fold, because the twenty-second and the twenty-third had been at a bank nine hundred feet east of the turn and there was no gate copper in either of those days.

There were thirty-one lines on it. The cart track was still on it with the twenty-eighth against it and it had not been checked off, and the man with a rule in his hand had been down to the bank twice that week and had not said a word to her about any of it, which was the correct thing for him to have done and which she had thought about more than she had meant to.

She dug the run at the gate on the twenty-fourth and the twenty-fifth and the twenty-sixth, three coppers, and the twenty-seventh was the day after and there would be four of them at the sixth hour when she got to the rail, and the board had gone to thirty-four lines and the drain line at the works was still on it with the twenty-fourth against it and the day was the twenty-seventh.

It had gone three days past.

She stood at the rail and looked at a date on a board that was three days behind her and worked out, standing up, that it was not going to be cleared, because it was nine hundred feet and she had been two miles west buying bread and at a bank nine hundred feet east with a trade’s team on the two days it would have taken her to cut it.

And then she went and dug the run at the gate, because the gate is the copper and the copper is what she has.

Three carts went out of that bank on the twenty-eighth and did not go in.
