# A Bed With A Lip On It

The seventeenth of a month she was at the yard at the turn at the sixth hour with the strip in her coat and nothing in her hands, and there were nine men on the south end and two of them nine feet off her.

There is no reason for a woman of eighteen to be at a yard four miles east of a shed at the sixth hour. She had been at that yard thirty-one times in six weeks and there is no line on that ground with her name on it, and the man with a rule in his hand had said twice that she would not be paid anything for any part of that sill course, and he had said it out loud so that nine men heard it.

She stood at the near end of the bed with her right arm open and her left hand in her coat.

## The Seventh Hour

It came up out of the ground between her feet and the barrow of clay.

It went under the bed four feet and along it and it came up through a screed of broken stone and old clay that has been under that yard since before she came, and the nine men on it did not stop and did not look up, and the bar did not move and the stone did not move and the chalk line did not move.

[Ning Yan, past the line, servant-born, under no ceiling any county holds. Nascent Soul, stage four, is held.]

It took four seconds and it went into the ground and it stopped saying anything.

## The Last Four Words

She stood there and listened to the end of it, the way she has listened to the end of every one of them since the twenty-second of a month five months back.

“Under no ceiling any county holds.”

She has been past the last working point of a line of work for five months and there is no county within nine days of road and there has not been one since before she came, and she had been carrying those four words the way she carried her strip, as a thing that was true and belonged to somebody else.

It had stopped belonging to somebody else somewhere around a yard at a turn on the sixth of a month six weeks back, when a man with a rule in his hand looked at her arm for two seconds and worked out where she went among nine men.

There are men inside nine days of road who are three and four stages above her and one of them is in a building two miles down the mill road and reads forty thousand columns and cannot lift a chair.

The layer said its one sentence and stopped, and it did not change, and she did not go and find out anything about it.

## The Eighth Hour

They went for it at the eighth hour with three men, because two of the nine had gone down to the low side ten minutes before and the man with the rule in his hand had not stopped the work to wait.

A bar under the near end. A chain round the far. A plank under the far end, because it is riding on one. A boot on the bed.

She was standing eleven feet off, which is where she stands.

The bar went up half an inch and stopped, and the man on the chain took up the slack and pulled, and the stone came half an inch and stopped, and the man with the rule said “wait” in the ordinary voice and every one of them stood still with their arms out.

And then the man on the plank shifted his boot to get his weight over and the far end of the plank went two inches and the stone dropped an inch onto the bed and the bar came out from under it sideways and the chain went slack.

It did not go far.

It went six inches and came down an inch and stopped, with the far end of the stone resting on the plank and the near end four inches down into the bed, and the man who had put his weight on the plank had his leg under it.

## Nine Seconds

Four men got to it.

One took the chain and two got the bar and the fourth went down on his knees on the near side. They did not lift it. They waited.

The man on the ground got his leg out in four seconds and sat down on the plank at the side of the bed with both hands on it, and there was nothing broken in him that anybody in that yard could see, and eight seconds after that he stood up and walked to the water bucket and put his hands in it.

She was eleven feet off and she had two seconds.

## What She Did

She put it there.

That is the whole of it and it took no longer than deciding to. There was no wind and no fire and there was not a hand in that yard on the ground. She had been carrying the thing since the twenty-second of a month four months back and she had done it once by accident on a flat past the last hedge with four men nine feet off, and it had gone the length of her own arm and stopped and been set down clean.

She put it there on purpose in a yard with nine men on it at the eighth hour of the seventeenth of a month, and she wanted it to go up, because the man on the ground had his leg under it.

## What It Did

It went.

It went the length of her own arm and it stopped.

That is four feet, and that is all it has ever done, and it does not go where it is told. She wanted it to go up and it went sideways and nine inches down, into the open ground on the low side of the yard, where the clay has been scraped off for the bed and there are three feet of loose and then nothing.

The stone came down on that ground and went into it two inches and stopped, and it was not in the bed and it was not on the line and it was four feet from where thirty-one dressed stones have to be.

Nine men at a yard four miles east of a mill whose wheel has not turned saw a dressed stone that two men and a bar and a chain and a boot had failed to shift for two days go four feet in nine seconds in a direction none of the nine of them had asked for.

Her short breath went to five counts and stayed there for a minute and then came back to four, and it comes back to four the rest of the time and it has not come back to four inside a loaded reach since the twenty-second of a month five months back.

## What He Said About It

The man with the rule in his hand did not shout and he did not ask her anything.

He walked down the yard to the low side and stood three feet off the stone and looked at the hole it had made in the loose, and then at the bed, and then at the chalk line, and then at the eleven feet of bed that had been cut for it.

Then he walked back up the yard to the office end and took a piece of chalk out of his pocket and cut ninety feet off the end of that sill line, because a sill course that cannot start at the south corner does not start ninety feet in.

He did not tell her. He did it, and she watched him do it, and it was a change to a date and a line and a count of thirty-one stones on a board three miles west of where she was standing.

And then he came back and put the chalk in his pocket and said one sentence to nine men, and then to her.

“That is going in the yard book,” he said, “and the yard book goes up the mill road on the first and comes down on the ninth, and it is not my book and it does not stay in that yard, and there is not a column on this ground for what is written at the foot of a page.”

## The Plain Part About One Person

She is eighteen years old and she has just made something happen in a room with nine men in it, and four seconds later the man on the ground was sitting on a plank with his hands on his leg, and she cannot do it twice on the same terms and there is nobody on nine days of road she can ask whether it works differently.

A thing she reaches cannot be put back where it was.

The ground it crossed stays crossed. The bed it came out of has a lip on it and the near end of the bed is four inches down and there is a hole in the loose on the low side with a dressed stone in it, and the sill line is ninety feet shorter than it was this morning and there are twenty-nine stones on it instead of thirty-one.

And she had wanted it to go up.

## What She Felt About It Going Down The Road

She felt sick at the office end, at two hundred yards, and she stopped and waited for it to be over, and it was not nerves. She had wanted a thing to happen and it had happened and it had gone the wrong way, and there is no part of her that can put it back, and she had known that at the eleventh foot before she put it there.

She is eighteen and the first thing she has ever done to the world on purpose went sideways into a hole because that is what it does.

The man with the rule in his hand had told her in seven months what she could do and what she could not and he had been right both times, and he was still right now, standing in a yard with his chalk in his pocket having just cut ninety feet off his own line.

She wanted it back. She wanted it back the whole of the four miles and she worked out on the hedge that she could ask for it, in the ordinary voice, standing at the low side of a yard with nine men on it.

And she worked out on the hedge that the asking would move it four feet in a line it picked.

## What She Said At The Second Hour

She lay on the boards at the second hour with the bar under her hand and she said the three sentences out loud, because it was the second hour and there was not a living soul in the room.

“The bed at the south end is ninety feet shorter than it was this morning and two stones are out of it and one of them is in a hole on the low side with three feet of loose under it and two inches of stone in it.”

“Twenty-nine stones instead of thirty-one, and the twenty-eighth is eleven days off, and a man with a rule in his hand cut his own line for it and did not tell me.”

“A thing I reach cannot go back where it was.”

She put her arm out straight on the boards and it stayed out.

“And it goes sideways, and I cannot make it go up, and I did not know that until this afternoon and now I do.”
