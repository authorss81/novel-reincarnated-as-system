# Batch 0001 Summary (Volume 3, ch 119–132)

Fourteen chapters, **37,177 words** (~2,656/chapter). **VOLUME 3 OPENS.** Examination and exposure.

She lives licensed for the first time in her life. The licence's whole scope line — *paid work outside the crew* — turns out to be worth exactly a hire: a board behind a well in Lowwater, chalk, six loads to a water channel at twelve coppers the load, paid by a man with a board who has written nine hundred names on it in eleven years and never asks what anybody is. **It is the first time in her life she has been paid by somebody who did not already own her**, and she finds out she wants it.

**The lane is also the cost.** On the fourth day a foreman calls her *the licensed one* — a description, not a name, and cheaper than a name and it travels further than a name, and there is no office in nine counties that can strike it. On the fifth the mat comes off her shoulder in public because the right arm is out, and she gets it back in four minutes in front of forty people, and the man at the board says her deduction out loud so that everyone hears it came off the licensed one and off nobody else. And the county hour is at the third hour and the lane is nineteen minutes from the square, so **every county hour costs four loads** — half a day's pay, every day, for as long as the road is open.

**He can see her arm go before she can.** Standing Voice, near her, costing nothing — he simply reads her, and he tells her, because telling her is not deciding. She finds out that the whole project of testing her hand twice a day has been quietly removed and that there is nothing she can do with being told. **No price is named and nothing is foreshadowed toward ch 149.**

**Ch 122 — she answers the Hunter instead of lying to him.** He asks one question, in a market, at the ninth hour, in front of a queue: *who certifies your hours.* Not what she is, not where she sleeps, not whether she is the woman on the fourth register. She had four moves available, including saying nothing at all and walking on, and she used none of them, and she told a stranger with clean hands the one thing nobody outside the filing had said out loud in a market. **Irreversible.** Relief, immediately curdled: a man who reads paper is worse than a man who reads bodies, because he is slower and never has to hurry.

**Ch 123 — the girl of twenty-four.** Fourteen days of list, and one question a carrier asked in a lane that she could not answer, in the town she calls the safest thing in. Ning Yan says one true sentence out loud, once, in a wash house, and is not thanked and does not thank her. The county's written request for the furnace schedule goes up the quarter's return sheet on the girl's own hour, entered by her, in the crate on day 28. She is alive and on the page and untouchable.

**Ch 124 — the second application.** A relicensing is the same three legs again with an hours record continuous from the day the tithe book opened. **Condition two is the door and the annex is the way round it**: hours certified onto the Qinghe furnace schedule by the Hall's clerk are furnace hours by certification and not by place, and no office has ever had to write that down. The annex puts her hire hours into the quota book, and the number that has been level since the spring stops being level. **The claim comes with the new stamp** — Sun He's ch 115 precedent, entered last, required.

**Ch 125 — the Hall.** She asks for the seal and gets *nine*, not Foundation, and the only thing Lu Kang has said since the spring: a member at nine is inside their own schedule and examinable there by any office with a stamp, and that is worth three marks. **They are not refusing.** A door that is not shut is worse than a refusal, because three men saying nothing is a position they have practised since the sixth day of the winter. And the leg order inverts in front of her: they will not seal a sheet that says nine while her hours are in two places at once. **Clearance first.**

**Ch 126 — the batch midpoint.** At the third hour, eight feet from the counter, a man puts **coin on open wood** for a certified copy of the Qinghe furnace schedule, keeps the receipt in his own hand, folds it square against the counter edge, and reads the whole thing at the public counter. He stops on the stage column — one, two, three, and a gap, and five. He says two true things to a young clerk in the voice of a man making an observation in a room: **the next stage must be announced or the paper fails its own terms**, and **a licensee capped at five cannot be examined past five**. The clerk half agrees with him out loud. Sun He hears both and says *he is right about the stage column* without stopping writing. Nobody does anything wrong. He buys one thing in this batch and it is a sheet of paper.

**Ch 127 — under a misspelling.** A strip of sacking on the door of a lodging house above the tanner's, in the hand of a woman who cannot read, written from a mouth: *Chou Zhyan*. The doubled consonant is a clerk's mistake made from memory, and she has watched the Hall copyist make the opposite one for a year. **She does not go up the stairs.** Sun He wrote it up on the twenty-fourth and is required to tell the maintaining members. He has been on Feng's fourth stair five times and has never once asked for anything.

**Ch 128 — the batch's irreversible decision, and it is a decision not to act.** Three men will seal. The price is the second book, out loud, in a corridor: *if I use it, the seal is bought, and a bought signature is a purchase and not a sponsorship.* They say **then don't**. She doesn't. Nothing about the quiet book is lodged, copied or spoken anywhere in ch 119–178. **The seal was bought with silence**, and what she can never get back is the not-knowing — she can never again stand in that corridor without knowing they know she knows.

**Ch 129 — the crate.** Day 28, on the day and not the day before. The Hall clerk writes her name in the wrong order on the certification and Lu Kang reads it and does not correct it. She works out that the county reads a crate in the order it is stacked, does not tell a sixty-one-year-old keeper how to do his job, and asks for the schedule on top and a list in her own hand on the very top of that. A boy of seventeen runs one finger down four months of her column and finds nothing. **Feng coughs on the stair and does not offer to carry anything.** She will not put a paper crate on a cart.

**Ch 130 — the clearance, first.** Shen Xiu asks his one question in a year: *yours alone, or the crew's.* She says hers alone. He will not weight a figure for one arm, he will not close a season early, and he makes her write the asked figure herself in her own hand — **fifty-eight hours in the seven weeks that are left** — and then he signs the second clearance in one stroke without looking up. **Something close to respect, which she does not forgive and does not convert into anything.**

**Ch 131 — three marks on one sheet.** Pei An reads it standing. Deng Chao reads it out. Lu Kang asks four questions about the paper and none about her intentions, and then says, in a corridor, in front of two men and a clerk, that this is the hardest signature of his life and he is putting it down anyway, because the alternative is a member handed over with her schedule in a hand and **this Hall is a book that has been opened**. She writes her ceiling for the third time in the book — nine, in her own hand — and re-enters her own defect with a new sentence under it: *the defect restores nothing*. She thanks nobody, because they have not helped her.

**Ch 132 — relicensed.** Sun He stands up and reads the application in order to a room, and seven conditions with it: the sixth new one is that the annex hours return with the quarter on the same crate in the same order; **the claim is the seventh and the last thing on the paper**, entered because he is required to. He does not say *you may pick up your licence* this time, and she notices. The Hunter is eight feet back at the counter filling in a form and she does not look at him, because looking would be the first thing either of them had done that was not ordinary. **Channel: about three weeks.** Six at the bunk in the spring; three now; and she does not tell him to stop.
