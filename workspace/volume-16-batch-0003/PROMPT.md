## The male lead — he is nearly off the page and must not be

**Five standing requirements for ch 931–945, and they are not optional:**

1. **He speaks in every one of the fifteen**, and in at least six he carries an exchange rather than a line. **The
   refusal is spoken and he is a voice in a wall, and the whole of the batch is him trying to say one thing and
   failing at it twice.** A reader must be able to point at the sentence in any chapter that tells them what this man
   wants today, and it must not be the same sentence twice.
2. **He is answerable and he is not good at this.** Wrong, further away than she can reach, honest past his own
   interest, and visibly bad at the one thing he came back for. **He is not the reader's protector and nobody on the
   page is going to tell her what to do.**
3. **He acts at least three times: the first attempt and the stopping of it (934), the catching of the second (938),
   and the apology itself (944), which costs him the only thing he has left.**
4. **No eleventh frame scene**, and **no bracket that is his voice.** The layer is not him.
5. **Every sentence he speaks is in the present tense**, and no other character ever takes the present-tense cue as
   hers. That is the reader's signal for who is talking and it does not break. **After ch 945 he is in nothing.**

**Ch 934, 938 and 944 are specified here rather than left to a card.** Attempt one has a figure in it and he stops.
Attempt two has none and is still an entry and he catches it. Attempt three is the apology, for every decision he made
on her behalf, said as decisions, in the present tense, with no number on it anywhere, and it is not very good and it
does not stop.

## Prose, and the house style

**Numbers are words. Paragraphs short (two to four sentences; break over ~90 words; never over ~120). Reader inside
her hearing. Short sentences at the moment something is understood; long ones elsewhere. No more than three
consecutive documentary paragraphs; one simple sentence about one person after machinery. Every chapter ends on
question, danger, discovery, decision, reversal, or emotional consequence — never bed, arithmetic, or neutral action.
Felt emotion plus concrete want every chapter. Vary emotional temperature: this batch has to carry a man failing at a
sentence for four chapters without the reader tiring of him, and the way to do that is to change the temperature
inside the failure, not to make the failure louder. No procedure loop: every chapter transforms the larger situation.**

**Length, on method B (`grep -v '^#' <file> | wc -w`, per file, summed): no chapter under 1,450 and none over 2,700;
the batch of fifteen lands between 26,000 and 28,000.** Batch 0002 landed at 22,436 on a first drafting and had to be
expanded by about a quarter with scene material inside the existing cards. **Build to the band as you go. A chapter at
1,600 is not finished; it is missing the scene around the beat.**

**Do not re-explain known principles, and do not rebuild what an earlier repair took out.** Concretely:

- **Openings must vary.** At most four of these fifteen chapters may open on the morning figures, and those four must
  not be consecutive.
- **`She said` must not open more than about one paragraph in six.**
- **Do not write** `And here is the whole of what…`, `said plain`, `I am naming it because…`,
  `X finishes nothing, and I am saying so out loud`, `That is the record and there is nothing else in it`,
  `that was the day`, or `whether she climbed or not`. **Each is dead.**
- **Do not use two to four-word comfort lines as his whole contribution to any chapter.**
- **Name the feeling in the scene, not in a slot.** She may name it out loud; she may not open a paragraph with a
  feeling and a comparison to yesterday's.
- **No two chapters end on the same shape**, and no chapter ends on a line that restates its own opening.
