## Midpoint, climax, action, irreversibility

- **Batch midpoint (~ch 245–246):** she understands in plain terms that the far-side board holding a figure no county can answer for is now the book that matters — and that every hour she works past the line widens the figure. Decision, not speech.
- **Batch climax (~ch 251–252):** the far-side office confirms in the ordinary voice that the seventh condition stands actionable on their side — and names the price of acting, which she does not pay here. The pull is the draft that is not yet written.
- **Action and deadline:** at least one scene with physical risk or a real deadline — far-side wage-work at the top of what she is with four fingers on one hand (ground, weather, a bar, no rescue), and the far-side pay-day as a real deadline no hours can move.
- **At least one irreversible decision in the batch, hers, costing something real and closing an option** (e.g. hiring on at the far-side trestle for the season in the column that is hers — from that hour she is kept, and cannot be un-entered). Name it on its card.
