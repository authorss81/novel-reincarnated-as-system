## Where the batch opens — from the page, not from a plan

**Day 2,781, the eighth of the month beginning 2,774: the morning after ch 840.** A dark room at the top of
eight flights and eight hundred and eighty steps, four miles up a mountain from a square. A dead lamp in a
cage on a shelf at the far end of a twenty-two-pace table, out two hundred and forty-seven days. Three pieces
of wood face up about a foot in from the near end and a fourth about a foot further along. Her own strip of
pale wood **put back under the table on the seventh of this month, face up, at the near end of the leg.** A bar
nine feet nine against the wall on the left, unlifted four hundred and thirty-seven days, dust broken at one
end. A bundle of papers under the far end, corded twice and not knotted, not opened. A boy asleep on the
fourth landing with oil on his hands.

**Ch 840 does not end on a question to him and ch 841 must not open on one.** She said the six terms out
loud one at a time in the dark, held the second one alone for a hundred and forty counts meaning the stair,
and **said she was not going up there to tell him**. She is not speaking to him and she has been for a night.
Ch 841 opens on **a woman who got nothing back from a page she sent down nine hundred and four steps forty
days ago and has decided not to wait again**, and on the first question she has never once asked in nineteen
years: **where do the announcements come from.**

**Six beats are SPENT at ch 840 and must not be staged again**, all of them on the page at 840. The six terms,
one at a time, and what the second one means. The strip of pale wood going back under the table and why. That
she cannot be alone in the room any more, named once at ch 828 and not to be named again. The two refusals
and the readability, and the finding that they do not cancel. The bar and the four hundred and thirty-six
days. The first thing she ever sent cannot be called back and she would not call it back. **A recap is not a
dramatic restaging; if a card wants one of these, the card is wrong, not the page.**

**She is at IMMORTAL VESSEL 9**, taken ch 828 on day 2,732 at sixty-six days, **the ninth and there is no
tenth in this book.** Nineteen years old. Three fingers and a thumb. Right arm open since day 2,294 — four
hundred and eighty-seven days at day 2,781 — and not going to close. Seven of eleven holds. Short breath four
counts everywhere, seven on a rise, nine standing still. The small open place behind the fourth rib, the size
of a coin. **One thousand two hundred and twenty-two days lighter at `D − 1,559`, not one going back.** Time
in the room `D − 2,301` = 480. Not hungry. Nib and ink against her fourth rib. A book of eight leaves with
**four blank leaves still blank and one written on and sent down at ch 838.** A bag at the bottom of the first
four flights with dish, clay, ring, key and nine inches of hand-made paper.

**Yuan:** Court, third rung. Readable by the Scribe since ch 810 and not addressed. **He said yes once in
Volume 14, at ch 812, away from her hearing, and she does not know, and no card in this batch tells her.** No
figure of what is left of him on any page and none may appear. Every sentence he speaks is present tense.
**No frame scene in this batch.**

## The clock this batch runs on — read this before any card

**Pressure without a date is fifteen variations on *she states arithmetic and she goes up a stair*. This batch
has a deadline and it is a person's lamp.**

**The light is his and not hers.** Three lamps in rotation, one carried up eight hundred and eighty steps on
his own account, no oil in that building and none for nine years, none on that mountain, and nothing to
replace it with. **Every lamp she lights on this stair is a lamp he does not light at the bottom of it.**
She asks him for it at ch 844 and it takes about four seconds and it is the first thing anybody in that
building has asked him for in nineteen years.

**The date is the room's own date.** A first of a month: **2,804**, and it is in this batch, and she is nine
flights and a stair above the room it belongs to. The bundles come up nine hundred and four steps, forty of
them, four to a minute, and a hand goes along the table afterwards, and there is nobody there.

**And there is a second date that is not a figure.** The oil ends when it ends. **No burn rate, no
cups-per-day, no hour the lamp runs out and no exhaustion date exists on the page and none may be invented.**
That defect has been repaired in this run three times, the last at the Volume 14 Batch 0004 review, and
reintroducing it is the single worst thing this batch can do. His count is his and hers is hers and **neither
is converted into the other.** What is true is only that a lamp has to be carried up by hand and when it is
out it is out.

## What the batch must do

1. **Open on the not-waiting, not on a mystery, and put the whole question in her mouth by the end of ch
   842.** Where do the announcements come from. She has been reading the incoming end since she was seven
   years old and has never asked, and the reason she has never asked is on the page and is not mystery but
   arithmetic: the layer is not a thing anybody has a maker of.
2. **Find the stair above the room at ch 843 and say why she is not counting it.** She counts the landings
   and not the steps and says out loud, standing, in the ordinary voice, that a landing is a thing she can be
   wrong about and a stair is not, and that she is going up it for the last time and does not want to arrive
   holding a number. **No card states a step count for it. No card may.**
3. **Ask him for the lamp at ch 844** and let it cost her something. He is not bound, not entered, not sent,
   and nobody has asked him for one drop in nineteen years.
4. **Land the cells at ch 848, the midpoint, and let it take the volume out from under her.** The third
   landing: a table twenty-two paces long, a cage lamp out, forty boards under the middle, a bundle corded
   twice, **and at the far side, between the twentieth pace and the end, a place of the length of a forearm
   and the breadth of a hand that nobody has ever sat in.** The finding is one sentence: **her room is not a
   room, it is one of hundreds, and the only reason hers was different is that somebody was in it.**
5. **Put the first of a month at ch 850 and put her nine landings above it.** Forty boards up nine hundred
   and four steps, a hand along the table, and nobody there. **The room carries on without her and that is a
   cost, not a relief.**
6. **Climax at ch 853: the stair does not stop at nine.** Nine landings counted and it keeps going, with
   physical risk on the page — one lamp, an arm open at the elbow, no rail she has ever counted, a landing
   she cannot see the end of. **This is the volume's image: fifteen volumes of a world that counts to nine
   and stops, and a building that does not stop.**
7. **Land the irreversible step at ch 855: she leaves the room.** The six terms said out loud on a stair for
   the first time in nineteen years, standing, in the ordinary voice, and she goes up, **and she does not come
   back down in this volume.** She takes the lamp and her own arithmetic and nothing else, and she leaves the
   room exactly as it is.

## Prose, and the house style this book actually writes in

**Numbers are words. Paragraphs are short. The chapter opens on the figures, standing, in the ordinary voice,
and the reason comes after.** She says things out loud, standing up, and the reader is inside her hearing
rather than outside it. Short sentences at a revelation and long ones everywhere else. **No more than three
consecutive paragraphs of documentary register, and one simple sentence about one person after every stretch
of machinery.** She names what she is feeling before she argues with it, because she does that, and because
it keeps a cold from being mistaken for fear.

**Every chapter ends on a question, a danger, a discovery, a decision, a reversal or an emotional
consequence.** None of them ends on her going to bed, on a chapter of arithmetic, or on a neutral action.

**His sentences are the only ones in the present tense and he is not more than a paragraph or two apart.** He
is not funny here. He is a man who has been left alone in a head for a night.
