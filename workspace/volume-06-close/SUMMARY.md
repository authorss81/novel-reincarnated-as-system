## 1. The volume's goal, and how it was paid

`outline/series.md` §9 fixed it:

> *In this volume, Ning Yan must survive what is on the other side, or the crossing was a death and not an escape.*

**It is paid, and it is paid in the chapters.**

The far side is ordinary farmed country with offices in it — hedged ground, lit houses, a mill whose wheel has
not turned since before she came, a stone-and-brick works with a board on the wall — **and not one of the offices
in it is an enemy.** No card in the volume creates a villain, no card names an office, and the outline's own note
that the pressure cannot be stated without a person to be the problem was honoured by having no person be the
problem. **The pressure is that being legible has a price list and she is the one paying it**, and it is stated
in prices rather than in speeches: a gate at two and a half coppers a day falling to one, bread at two a day, a
box of clay is four days of the ring, a board that goes bare and a yard that goes quiet until the season turns.

She is entered twice — once as good at the seventeenth at a counter (ch 304) and once for materials at a gate in
daylight (ch 329) — **and on the last step of six stages she found out what the second of those two vouchings was
copied into**, which was carried upward out of the office that keeps it and written again in a hand she has never
seen, by somebody she will never meet, and sent back down her own road (ch 357). **She cannot be un-entered twice
over.**

She refused a sending out loud, in daylight, with her name said, in a room with a stove and about nine men in it,
and a man behind a counter entered it in his own seasonal book against the day and did not write why
(ch 358). **It cannot be undone and no hand in that building can take it out.**

She broke the closed-season box circuit at ch 344 — a man with a brush who could not take the lines out and
would not shut a book with a knot — and the bill arrived at ch 359, on a stone under a keeper's ladder at a post,
nine feet off a road, where **no office that can read that fine hand is ever going to know it is.**

And she reached Nascent Soul 1 **by hand and not by sound**, on a bank past the mill on the twenty-second, and a
stone she had never touched went the length of her own arm and stopped and was set down clean, and four men nine
feet off did not look up, **and an office wrote it down afterward in a hand she has never seen, because the hand
can read what happened.** She cannot read the hand.

**The cost of surviving is stated in the chapters and not in a plan**, in five places: the heavy end closed for
good (ch 352); the gate down to a fifth of a reach and one copper (ch 353); a bare board and a quiet yard (ch
356); a refusal she cannot take back (ch 358); and a reach that happened once and does not come back (ch 360).

## 5. What was broken and what was refused, and that neither is the other's

- **Broken: the closed-season box circuit, ch 344.** A man with a brush, in a room, with a person: *A book that
  does not shut is still a book, and a book that does not shut goes up on the first cart in the morning, and it
  goes as it is. That is not a rule anybody has made. That is what a book is.*
- **Refused: the sending, ch 358.** Out loud, in daylight, with her name said, entered in the office's own book.
  *NING YAN, OF LOWWATER. THE HOLDER IS NOT PRESENTED. REFUSED AT THE COUNTER. THE TWENTY-SIXTH.*
- **They are different kinds of act and neither chapter states the other.** The vouching was a **yes given in
  daylight for materials**; the refusal is a **no given in daylight for herself**. Ch 329 contains no refusal;
  ch 358 contains no vouching. Ch 358's one backward reach is to ch 355 — the second-holder refusal, which is a
  third act and is named as such.
- **Ch 359 is the bill and not the breaking.** The two chapters do not say the other.

**Volume 6 is the first volume of *Breaking*, and it broke one thing and refused one thing. Nothing opened the
Wall and no Order appeared — the outline's note that both are three-volume work was honoured.**

## 8. The protagonist, answered in writing

`outline/series.md` §2 makes this a standing requirement on every volume from Volume 5 on.

**What he actually did across Volume 6, and what each of it cost.**

- **Ch 325.** He moved a cart's turning through her column so a box would go on a week early instead of waiting a
  season. *I moved the cart* — six words, present tense, on a cart on a flat where nothing within half a mile
  could hear. **The cost is a hole in a yard's own account**, which a correct man with ink on the side of his hand
  will have to answer for in eleven days, **and a man who now knows in about nine seconds that he can get into
  any line on that ground, and there is no place on it where he cannot.**
- **Ch 338.** He let an office's hand address him as the holder of her column so her furnace hours would hold
  without her standing at a shed at the fourth hour. **The price was named in advance**, in her mouth the night
  before: **his unnamed state, and with it the ability ever again to be nowhere an office can address.** He says
  the price back before he pays it, in his own words, and then says what it moved him toward: *I am not going to
  be anywhere after this. I have wanted to be somewhere I cannot be reached for about four years and I have got
  it now and it is the worst thing I have ever bought.*
- **Ch 355.** He was asked for a second holder and **he said no out loud**, in the first word. **It was not spent.**
  **And the price of refusing was that he was refusing a thing that would have made her safer — she said it and
  he agreed.**

**Every one of those is a decision by the book's protagonist, and every one of them was paid for out of her.**

**What is now true of him that was not true at ch 298.** He is **readable without her, and therefore findable**
(ch 272, carried and not given back). He is **held, and therefore sendable** (ch 338). He has **refused once, out
loud, for a reason of his own that is better than hers, and has said so**. **Being sent for is now a thing that
can happen to him in principle, and Volume 6 deliberately neither resolves it nor has it happen off-page** — the
Batch 0004 budget records that his own condition since ch 338 is neither resolved nor spoken of anywhere in
ch 346–360.

**The closing position is the one the ending needs: a man the reader has seen in a room, in his own frame,
twice** — ch 272 in Volume 5 and ch 338 in Volume 6 — **and the prerequisite `outline/ending.md` §1 needs before
the final volume opens exists on the page**: he is coherent, addressable, findable, and legible to something that
has his name on a sheet. **Nothing in Volume 7's planning may assume more of him than Volume 6 earned.**

**What may not happen, and did not.** This close has not re-framed ch 299–360, has not re-titled the volume, has
not introduced a new final enemy, and has taken nothing `outline/ending.md` owns — the bargain, the fall, Feng's
death, the Anchor, the Sisterhood and the Scribe. The year of non-forgiveness is not retired, he is not forgiven,
and she never tells him to stop. `grep` for `Scribe`, `Anchor`, `Sisterhood` and `bargain` across the volume
returns **zero** for all four.
