## Batch goal, midpoint, escalation, climax, irreversible step

**Goal:** take the fourth stage past the ceiling, take the book off the stone, and be given the day the list closes.

**Pressure: being counted.** Nothing in it is a recruiting, a seduction, a keeping that is a wage-book, a debt, a
family paper, an ash, a ration, a hiring, a crossing, an audit, an examination, a hunt, a signature, a confinement,
or a second demand in a room. **It is also not Volume 9's *being kept*, and no two consecutive batches run the same
pressure.**

**Escalation:** she walks west with an open question · **Severance 4 at ch 556 and she is missed by a count** · she
goes to the bench and the turn and watches nine years of people not turning back · **she takes the book off the stone
at ch 563 and it cannot be put back the way it was** · she asks her second question and gets the rule again · **he
names the closing day at ch 569–570 and every asking narrows from there.**

**Midpoint, ch 563:** **she takes her book off the flat stone and cannot put it back the way it was.** Not because
somebody makes her. Because a keeping that has been asked about is not kept the same way twice, and once it is in her
own hands again the stone is a stone.

**Climax, ch 569–570:** **he names the closing day — day 1,802, the nineteenth of the month beginning 1,784 — in
daylight, because she asked.** He does not refuse. **The reversal is that he answers, and the answer is a day and not
a name: her asking set the closing, and the list was open only while nobody looked at it.**

**Irreversible step, ch 563:** **she moves the book out of unasked keeping, and the stone no longer holds it.**

**Physical risk or real deadline, pinned:** **four miles to the bench on the store road and four miles back on the
telling count, to a turn nine years of people have turned at and never turned back from** — plus the standing one,
carried and never re-derived: **an arm open four days in five and a body lighter than it was on the eighth of a
month, and no place to lie down in between the asking and the answer.**

**What she has to work with:** a bar nine feet nine, one good arm, a road gang at a loaf a day and a half, a boy of
fifteen who will walk two miles because she asked him to, and three terms.
