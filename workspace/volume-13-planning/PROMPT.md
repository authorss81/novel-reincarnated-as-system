## The question this volume is planned out of, and out of nothing else

> She has gone up a stair that a dead clerk's lamp lit and carried her own total up it in her own hand, and the announcement that has gone into the ground nine times went up this time, and it went up carrying a shape, and the shape is the shape of a woman who cannot write any more. She is at the top of the stair and she is the first person in twelve volumes that anything above the line has heard of. **The question is not whether she is heard. It is that being heard is what the levy was for, and she has paid for it in a figure that is now in a book, and whatever is up there knows exactly what she is and what it cost her to be it, and it does not know her.**

**And this phase does not preview what Volume 13 may show her: not the size of anything, not a person above the second stair, not a rule, not a title, not a name, not a crowd, and not the moral answer, which belongs to Volumes 15–16.** Being small is a thing the reader has to see her be, not be told about. The outline must earn every person, rule and scale on the page — nothing above the second stair exists on any page yet, and the plan may not invent it by fiat: each batch card must find it through what she does with the instruments she has (counting, a hand that cannot write a false line, a record kept out loud, two coppers she cannot count, five blank leaves).
