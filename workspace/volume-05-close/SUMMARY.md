## The volume in one paragraph

She crossed in Volume 4 and spent five months in somebody's ordinary country turning stone at five coppers a
day, keeping a column of hours that is hers in a wage-book kept by people who are not a county. In that time
she answered a claim that had stood since she was sixteen, closed a family account that had been open since
before she was born, put ten years of her brother's life where somebody has to look at it, and took three stages
of a realm the world has no ceiling for — **and not one county in nine heard a single one of them, because a
county book has no column for a thing done past its own boundary.** The far side paid for every one of those in
books it keeps by hand. And it also entered her. An office four hundred yards past the last working point wrote
the word *good* against her name on the seventeenth of a month, and told her it could not be taken out, and she
went back the next morning and worked the ordinary end anyway. **The season ended on the thirtieth at a date in
another man's hand. Three working days in that month were never paid and never will be. She shook the ash off
her coat, and it fell the way ash falls, and did not settle, and she left it where it fell. Then she walked out
past the cart turn and stopped half a mile short of a mill whose wheel has not turned since before she came,
and did not go past it.**

## The protagonist, answered in writing

**`outline/series.md` §2 names Yuan, male, as the protagonist and `state/book-metadata.md` records Gender:
Male.** The male-led requirement holds at series level. The question Volume 5 had to answer is different, and
the Volume 4 close put it in writing: *a man the reader has never seen in a room cannot carry the fall that
`outline/ending.md` fixes for him.* Volume 4 delivered two more volumes of a voice, off-panel, rationed.

**Volume 5 delivered the correction, and it is on the page.**

**What he did across Volume 5, chapter by chapter, and what each of it cost.**

- **ch 256.** He took a share of a settled listed furnace-tending and **told her the price before he moved it.**
  The price was two of the eleven holds in her left hand. *The share went the way shares go, intercepted and
  spent… and it did not come back.*
- **ch 258.** He stood in her own order — *You are ordering. And I am here* — and the layer said the one thing
  it had said since ch 237, and **the turn went out of her bar stroke, permanently.**
- **ch 272 — *The Man the Board Keeps*.** Away from her hearing, in the layer's own reading-space, where
  something walks a row once a day and looks at him: **he let a far-side keeper's hand enter him as a readable
  line so that her hours would hold past the line without her.** The price was on the table before the line
  went in: **his unentered state, and the date of the morning he died.** *He keeps the apology. He loses the
  day.* He wants her hours specifically, because that run is the only thing standing between eight hundred jin
  and a man she left standing in a county that could not pay it — and he wants, for the first time in four
  years, **to be the instrument instead of the thing instrumented.**
- **ch 281.** He is on the page as a line under a lump of paste, and she keeps four hundred yards from the post
  on purpose, and will not go and look at it.
- **ch 290.** He took a second share on the season's last listed run closed, held it six days, and **named the
  price before he moved it**: one line in the record the offices read, saying the balance carried forward
  against her is discharged and nothing is carried against her name. The price is her foundation, and **not one
  person alive has ever been able to tell her how much, including him.** She did not say no.
- **ch 296.** **He spent it, and the family account closed, and it cost her something she cannot name.**

**Every one of those is a decision by the book's protagonist. Four were paid for out of her body; the last two
were paid for out of her in ways that leave nothing to point at.**

**What is now true of him that was not true at ch 238.** **He is readable from outside the bound without her,
and therefore findable** — and the chapter that made him findable said on the page, in his own frame, in his
own words, what that means:

> Being read means being findable. A line on a board can be looked at by an office, and an office that looks at
> a line sends for the line, and being sent for is a thing that happens to men in rooms with doors.

`outline/ending.md` §1 requires exactly this before the final volume opens — the Court stage, the Scribe
finding him, and the fall. **The prerequisite exists on the page, in a sustained scene, in his own frame, away
from her hearing.**

**What Volume 6's planning may not assume.** Nothing more than this. He is not stronger, not restored, not
located, not protected, and not forgiven. **He has lost the date of the morning he died and will not have it
back.** He has no hands, no road, and no way of ever being anywhere she is not — he says so himself at ch 290.
**The Ledger is no longer hers to order, and there is nothing she can order instead.**

**What may not happen, and did not happen here.** The close did not re-frame ch 239–298, did not re-title the
volume, introduced no new final enemy, and took nothing the ending owns — **the bargain, the fall, Feng's death,
the Anchor, the Sisterhood and the Scribe are untouched, and the Scribe belongs to Volume 15.** The year of
