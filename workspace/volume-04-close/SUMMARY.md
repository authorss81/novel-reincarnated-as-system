## 2. The four repairs, made in the chapters

Nothing else was touched. **The planned plot, the sixty titles, the chapter order, the card beats, the four
pinned stage days (386, 412, 440, 462), the pinned attendance day (389), both recorded deviations (ch 225
opening on the fifteenth, ch 228 running 412–414) and every ending stay where they were.**

**Repair 1 — ch 224, the volume's only named weekday.** *I would do the whole of it again on Friday* → **I
would do the whole of it again on any working morning.** The rule is no weekday anywhere in ch 179–238.
**The repair costs nothing**: nothing in the plot turns on the day of the week, the chapter's own argument is
that he would repeat himself on an ordinary day and not in a mood, and *a working morning* is the idiom the
volume already uses eight times. Grep for all seven weekday names now returns zero across the volume.

**Repair 2 — ch 232, a pre-echo of the right index finger, three chapters early.** *…and she had four
fingers' worth of nothing on the other one that she had not noticed yet because nothing had been asked of that
hand* → **…and every part of her that had gone wrong since the thaw had gone wrong on that side.** The
volume's rule is that nothing before ch 235 foreshadows the finger, and this sentence used the ch 235 figure
about a hand that was whole.

**Repair 3 — ch 179, the construction ch 235 needs, spent fifty-six chapters early.** *…because that is the
finger that can still do a turn at the end of a stroke* → **…because a coin will go across the flat of that
one and not across a fingertip.** The reason she used that finger is preserved; the phrase is not, and ch 235
now owns it.

**Repair 4 — ch 220, the fifth stage on the wrong day of the month.** *…and it came on the nineteenth of this
month* and *The stage came on the nineteenth* → **…and it came on the seventeenth of this month** and **The
stage came at the fourth hour**, with *It came in the same week* → **It came earlier in that same day**, and
the two *two days* recast as **the rest of the day** and **the hours between the two of them**.

**Why this one mattered more than the other three.** The ninth-stage table fixes Foundation 5 at **day 360**,
and **day 360 is the seventeenth of a month** under the rule the book has used since ch 134
(`((day − 134) mod 30) + 1`). The chapter put the stage on the nineteenth, which is **day 362 and an interval
of twenty-four days**, contradicting three things at once: the pinned day, the twenty-two days the chapter
itself measures from the twenty-fifth of the month before, and the **twenty-six** days to Foundation 6 at day
386 — 386 minus 362 is twenty-four, not twenty-six. The strike was never in doubt: the chapter's own heading
is *The Seventeenth*, the strike is at the second hour of the seventeenth, and **nine later chapters (221,
228, 230, 231, 232 ×3, 234, 236) date it to the seventeenth.** The strike stays; the stage moves to meet it,
four hours earlier in the same day. **The chapter's load-bearing argument — that the two things are not
connected, and that being certain of that is worse than not knowing — is preserved and in fact sharpens**,
because two events four hours apart invite a connection more strongly than two events two days apart.

## 7. The protagonist, answered in writing

**`outline/series.md` §2 names Yuan, male, as the protagonist and `state/book-metadata.md` records Gender:
Male, so the male-led requirement holds at series level. That was never the question. The question is what
Volume 4 did with him inside his own book.**

**What he actually did across ch 179–238.**

He **found a hand that wrote a date against a claim.** Eight days out of ten, price said before the word: Luo
Ping, thirty-nine, sixteen years a schedule-man, with a wife and a boy of eleven living past the county's
boundary — and she had to carry that and could not use it.

He **held a standing for a crossing and was paid for it out of a base that was not finished.** One day out of
three, stated at ch 231 before the word, taken at ch 233, held under a day, put down when the crossing was
over, and spent out of a foundation one stage short of done.

He **intercepted a share of a settled job and spent it.** The job was the blind run. The trial cleared. The
Merit came on a finished thing and not on a dead man. He took a share and spent it, and it did not come back,
and it cost her the right index finger.

He **restored his own life.** About six weeks at the last page, in the only unit the book uses for it.

And he **read one name he had said four times he could not read, in one breath, and stopped.**

**Every one of those is a decision by the book's protagonist. Every one of them was paid for by the woman, in
hours, in a stage, or in a finger.**

**Is that enough? No — and the finding is stated plainly rather than softened.**

**Sixty chapters of close third on a woman who is not the protagonist, in which the protagonist is a voice,
off-panel, rationed to four marks and then to one, is a structural debt and not a stylistic one.** The
male-led requirement is met at series level and will be met at every volume level, because Yuan is the man the
whole book is about — the layer, the cost, the thing that pays, the thing that is rationed. But **Volume 4 is
the second consecutive volume in which he has no scene, no body, no room and no other character to speak to,
and the debt is now two volumes of sixteen old.** The Batch 0004 planning review carried the question in
`state/open-threads.md` and **nothing in the drafting of any of the four batches settled it.**

**What Volume 5 does about it, decided here, before Volume 5 opens.**

The options the series outline permits are all still open: a chapter or a run of chapters inside Volume 5's
allocation where he is on the page; a structural change of frame; or a decision that Volume 5's shape is
itself the correction. **Volume 5 takes the third — the shape of Volume 5 is the correction — inside Volume
5's own sixty chapters rather than by re-framing Volume 4.** As a requirement the Volume 5 planner must meet:

- **Volume 5 must put Yuan on the page in his own right for at least one sustained scene** — not more rationed
  sentences, not a description of him, not a dream. **A scene where he acts, or is acted on, away from her
  hearing.** He has never been in a room she was not in; there is now a country on the far side of the line
  where a man who keeps a board asks no questions, and a claim that has landed in the first office able to
  act on it, and the machinery of the layer is the only thing in the world that has ever moved him.
- **The cost of that scene is not optional and is not hers to pay again.** He has already paid the finger and
  the foundation. Whatever Volume 5 puts him in, **it takes something that is his and that the ending still
  needs** — because `outline/ending.md` is fixed that he refuses the bargain and takes the fall and is gone
  before ch 1000, **and a man the reader has never seen in a room cannot carry a fall.**
- **What Volume 5 may not do:** re-frame ch 179–238, re-title the volume, introduce a new final enemy, or
  take anything Volume 5 owns — **the family account, Ning Wu's decade, the four months erased in Chapter 3,
  the bargain and the fall. The Scribe is fixed in `outline/ending.md` and belongs to Volume 15**, and this
  decision does not touch it.
- **What Volume 5 must carry unchanged:** close third on Ning Yan for every external scene; **every sentence
  Yuan speaks in the present tense, and no other character ever in a marked present frame**; the fifteen
  continuity facts above; and the standing that his Ledger is **hers to order** and she has not ordered it.

**What it costs:** the volume's first instinct will be to put him on the page as a resource again — more
sentences, a bigger ration, a longer channel. **That is not the correction.** The correction is a scene in
which he is a person with his own situation and is not being spent. **It costs Volume 5 the easiest shape it
had, and it is worth it, because the alternative is a book with no protagonist in it from ch 179 onward.**
