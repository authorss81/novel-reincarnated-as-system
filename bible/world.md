# World

## The shape of it

There is a great deal of world and almost all of it is believed to be the world. This is the first fact a reader of the series has to hold, and it is the fact the whole book leans on.

Nine counties, one mountain range, three sect territories, and a sea to the south that nobody crosses because there is nothing on the other side of it that they have heard of. Cultivation has been practised for eleven hundred years inside these borders. There are four realms anyone can name and one realm they argue about at night.

The boundary is called the **Wall**. No one inside the mortal world thinks of it as a wall. They think of it as the horizon, or as the shape of the world, or as a matter of doctrine. A monk in the far west will tell you honestly that there is nothing beyond the water. He is not lying. He has never had cause to doubt.

## Layer by layer, revealed across sixteen volumes

1. **Physical survival and local work** (Vol 1–2). Ashwood Verge, the quota, the furnace, the kiln. What it is to be poor and skilled at something nobody values.
2. **Factions, families, professions** (Vol 1–4). Sects and servant-hands, the Lantern order and their rice paper, the Bureau of Records and its clerks, the inner elder hall and its quarrels over quota.
3. **Regional history and competing explanations** (Vol 4–6). The Founding of the Bound, the Nine Cracks, the Wars of Quota, three mutually incompatible accounts of why cultivation stopped at the fourth realm inside the border.
4. **The origin and cost of the power system** (Vol 6–11). Ascension is intercepted. Announcements are receipts. The Wall is maintained deliberately and by people with names and families.
5. **Cross-regional politics and trade** (Vol 6–14). The real realm is not a wilderness. It is a continent of sects with budgets, borders, and the same politics at ten times the scale.
6. **The final historical and moral conflict** (Vol 11–16). What the walls protect, who pays for them, and whether a merciful lie stays merciful when it lasts nine hundred years.

## The world inside the world: Qinghe

Qinghe Sect is a third-rank sect with eleven hundred disciples, of whom nine hundred are servant-born hands who do not cultivate at all. It sits on the Nine Steps Terrace above Lowwater, and it runs a furnace.

Its economy is simple and everybody knows it. Each winter the Bureau posts a quota. Each winter the sect fills it. Things with bounties, and people without sponsors, both fill it.

The hierarchy is not written down anywhere. That is the point of it. A servant-hand's child may cultivate, and if they cultivate well enough they become an outer disciple, and once they are an outer disciple they are, for tax purposes, sect property.

## Daily texture

Nine Steps Terrace in snow. Furnace smoke that tastes of iron at the back of the throat and does not go away for weeks. Rice paper, and the particular sound of a brush used by someone with a cough. The ash of the Verge going into the eyes and into the lungs for years. Lamp oil. The sound a cultivation advancement makes, which is not what people think it sounds like: it is a small unpleasant crack, like a knuckle, and it is loud enough to be heard through a wall.

## Who holds what

- **The Bureau of Records** — mortal clerks who register harvests, quotas and ascensions on rice paper and copy it upward to something they never name.
- **The Lantern order** — lamp-menders and bone-readers who follow the announcement layer's residue across the counties and read out what the layer said last night. They are almost all illiterate, which makes them extremely good at the one skill the Bureau cannot train.
- **The sects** — nine in the Nine Counties, of which Qinghe is the third rank. Sects compete for quota and do not compete for anything else, because everything else is quarrelled over by the elder hall and settled in rooms.
- **The furnace** — an institution, not a building, although Qinghe has one. The furnace takes a person whose cultivation has stalled and a person whose cultivation has failed and makes the first pay for the second. It is legal inside the bound. It has been legal for six hundred years.
- **The Wall** — not known as an object until Volume 4. Before that it is a shape in the sky, a boundary in a map, and a story about a man who walked into it.

## Rules of the world that hold everywhere

- Cultivation advances in numbered stages. Stage nine of a realm is where most people stop, and it is a real ceiling rather than a social one, though nobody inside the bound believes that.
- Ascending past Condensing into Foundation is announced. Everyone within the bound hears it. This is treated as good news.
- Death by harvest leaves an unsettled account. It follows the family for three generations unless someone with authority writes it off.
- No one inside the bound has ever met anyone who came back from beyond the Wall. This is presented in the text as a fact about the world and not as a fact about the Wall.
- **The world has no named weeks and no named weekdays.** A day is counted as an hour of the day and a day of a month, and a month is thirty days, so **month-day = `((day − 134) mod 30) + 1`**, with day 134 the first of a month. People say *four days up and four days down*, *the third of the month*, *the second hour of the afternoon*, *the end of the thaw*, *a month from now* — and they do not say Monday. **No narration in this book names a weekday, and none may.** This is written into canon here because the rule was previously inferred from Volume 4's practice and enforced at one line while eight standing instances survived in Volumes 1 and 2; all eight were repaired at the Volume 4 close review (ch 40, 47, 104, 109, 111 ×2, 114) and a grep of `chapters/` for all seven weekday names now returns zero across ch 1–238.

## The reveal discipline

The Wall is never denied and never explained. When it is first named, it is named by someone who is embarrassed to say it. The reader should understand, roughly eight chapters before the characters do, that the mortality rate on a Vege run is not weather.

## Weather and season

Winter is the quota season and the volume's pressure. The Verge burns in summer and freezes in winter, and a winter run is the dangerous one, because most of what lives out there does not hunt in summer. Ash falls for eleven days after a Cracking and does not settle.

## What must never appear

No modern technology. No firearms. No printed press. No other Earth. No martial arts terminology imported from outside the setting — the setting's vocabulary is in `bible/terminology.md` and is used consistently. **No named weekday, in any register and from any mouth** — see *Rules of the world that hold everywhere*.
