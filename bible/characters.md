# Characters

All primary protagonists are male. The male lead of this novel is **Yuan**, who has no body and is the intelligence, the want, and the arc. **Ning Yan** is a fully-drawn co-lead, not a device; she carries the physical plot and every physical cost.

## Yuan — the System

- Male. Nineteen when he died; indeterminate otherwise. He counts time in announcements and gets the count wrong under stress.
- His name before he died was **Yuan Sheng**. He hated it. He learns in Volume 11 that it was not a coincidence that the layer keeps the name.
- Servant-born, like Ning Yan. Furnace-man's son at a different sect, harvested at nineteen when a senior disciple's Condensing went wrong.
- **Appearance:** none. He is described by what he cannot do — no hands, no body, no voice anyone else can hear, no way to stand in the rain. Never given a face in narration.
- **Voice:** present tense, short, dry, and funnier than he means to be. He makes jokes at the wrong moment and knows he does it. He is thirty percent more competent than he lets on and hides it.
- **External want:** a Chosen One strong enough that he can hold a shape. He wants *more*. He wants the numbers to be his.
- **Internal need:** to stop using her. To accept that being carried is a thing that can happen to him.
- **Flaw:** he treats people as entries. He was a clerk's clerk and he believes that honest recording is a form of love. He lies to her by omission and always calls the omission a kindness. By Volume 14 he is issuing quests to other people and has stopped noticing the day he started.
- **Fear:** that she is right about what he is.
- **Unresolved shame:** his last coherent breath was an apology to the men cutting him open. He has not yet apologised to anyone in a way that counted.
- **Where he ends:** refusing a bargain that gives him everything, in the body he never had, and paying for it with the only thing he owns.

## Ning Yan — the Chosen One

- Female. Seventeen at the start of Volume 1; eighteen in Volume 3. The book does not romanticise her age and nothing in Volumes 1 and 2 is a romance.
- Servant-born at Qinghe Sect. Mother **Ning Xue**, a laundry-hand, dead of wet lungs at fourteen. Father **Ning Bo**, a furnace-man, taken for a stalled ascension when she was eleven. Brother **Ning Wu**, taken for the kiln at thirteen, never returned, and the reason she is who she is.
- **Want:** her brother's name in a book, in ink, where someone has to look at it.
- **Need:** to accept that being rescued is not the same as being owned.
- **Flaw:** she trusts competence over people. If a thing is capable and has not lied to her yet, she follows it. This is how Yuan gets a foothold and how it very nearly kills her in Volume 3.
- **Skill:** she reads shoulders. Before a fight she knows who has decided to lose on purpose. This is a servant's skill and it wins her fights.
- **Fighting style:** the Ashwood Nine-Step, from a laundry manual's back page because a laundry-hand's son copied it down. Holds heat and holds qi past comfort, which is a furnace-child's inheritance and reads as stubbornness.
- **Where she ends:** standing where a god used to be, with nothing in her head but a silence that has a shape.

## The antagonist ladder

Each is a real argument, not an obstacle. Each acts offstage, loses at cost to themselves, and returns changed.

### Qiu Zhulang — Elder of the Furnace, Volume 1

Sixty-one. Kind to servants' children in a general, unfocused way. Believes the bound is the world, the Wall is the sky, and everything terrible inside it is the price of keeping the rest of it safe. He has run the furnace for forty years and has never once been asked what he is allowed to think, and has never once wondered. Loses Volume 1 politically, not physically, and survives to be a recurring, diminished, and entirely sincere man for the rest of the book.

### Bai Zhu — the Hunter, Volume 3

A field agent of the Warden's office, sent to find and dismantle an unauthorised System. Polite, patient, and genuinely good at the job. He does not lie to her, at any point, and it is much worse. He believes he is doing a mercy and can be made to argue for the walls on his own.

### The Wall — and the sects that profit from it, Volume 6

The antagonist is a piece of geography defended by people who live on it. Not evil. Just load-bearing.

### Shuyin — Warden, Volume 9

Nine hundred years. Has kept the walls so the real realm does not notice. Keeps a list of every ascension the bound has swallowed. Some of the names on it are his friends. He is the most tired person in the series and the closest thing it has to an honest man.

### The Order of the Recruited, Volume 12

Wants Ning Yan as an instrument and has the shape of one. Not a villain so much as a job description with a budget.

### The Scribe, Volumes 15 and 16

The intelligence above Yuan, and by its own account the reason the walls exist. In Volume 15 it is a distant and courteous authority. In Volume 16 it offers terms, in good faith, and the terms are exactly what Yuan said he wanted.

## Supporting cast

**Ning Wu** — the brother. Thirteen when taken. Does not die offstage; he is on the Warden's list for a decade and Volume 10 is about finding him. He has had a life she does not know about and does not forgive her for missing it.

**Du Hong** — eighteen, outer disciple, first real ally inside the sect. Ambitious, generous, bad at lying. Survives Volume 1. Dies in Volume 2 in a way Ning Yan caused.

**Mei Jiu** — fourth-rank inner disciple, her rival. Not a bad person. Wants the same thing she wants and got it earlier, and it has not gone well for her either.

**Shen Xiu** — Team Elder of the Verge run. A tired man executing a quota, not a villain. The instrument of the Volume 1 midpoint.

**Feng Lushi** — Keeper of the Lantern-Register at Lowwater. A mortal clerk with a consumptive cough, twenty-two years of writing down announcements. He is the only person in Volume 1 who believes her. He writes it down. In Volume 3 he takes a Lung offered by the Bureau and she has to deal with the man who believed her.

**Wei Ran** — mortal-born, ascends improperly in Volume 3, the first person she meets who went through without permission. Slow-burn line begins in Volume 4 when she is twenty. Goes off page.

**Wen the Lamp-mender** — of the Lantern order. One genuinely surprising encounter per appearance. Carries people across the Verge for reasons she does not give.

## Relationship milestones

1. Volume 1 — she speaks to a voice and decides to test it instead of running from it.
2. Volume 1 — she finds the cost without being told, and keeps talking to him anyway.
3. Volume 2 — he lies about what she is and she works it out alone and does not forgive him for a year.
4. Volume 3 — she chooses to be hunted rather than be handed over.
5. Volume 5 — he is the reason her family account is settled and her brother is reachable. He has done something good and it cost her something she cannot name.
6. Volume 8 — he starts issuing quests to other people and she makes him stop.
7. Volume 11 — she learns what he was. She does not leave.
8. Volume 15 — the Scribe addresses him directly and she hears it happen.
9. Volume 16 — the last thing he says to her is an apology that is not an entry.

## What nobody does

No harem. No power-up that arrives free. No mentor who explains the rules on request. No antagonist monologuing through a fight. No deus ex machina from Yuan: every quest costs her something, and she can refuse him.
